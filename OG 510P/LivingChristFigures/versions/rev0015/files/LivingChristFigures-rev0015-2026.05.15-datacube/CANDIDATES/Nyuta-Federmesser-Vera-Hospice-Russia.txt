Nyuta (Anna) Federmesser / VERA Hospice Charity Foundation / Lighthouse children's hospice — and her mother Vera Millionschikova (First Moscow Hospice)

Status: PROVISIONAL KEEP WITH HEAVY STATE-PROXIMITY FLAG; OFFICE-CANON FOR PAIN-RELIEF-AT-DYING
Location: Moscow and other Russian regions; First Moscow Hospice (1994), Lighthouse children's hospice (opened October 2019)
Office: Pain-relief threshold / palliative dignity / opioid-access at end of life

Work:
Vera Millionschikova (1942–2010) helped open the First Moscow Hospice in 1994 with British journalist Viktor Zorza; ran it for sixteen years. Her daughter Anna "Nyuta" Federmesser founded VERA Hospice Charity Fund in 2006, co-founded the "House with the Lighthouse" children's hospice (opened 2019), and from 2016 has directed Moscow's State Center for Palliative Care. VERA supports the Moscow Center, eight Moscow hospices, and over sixty regional Russian hospices, plus families with incurable children. The work has materially expanded opioid availability for terminally ill patients — by some estimates, opioid availability for chronic pain in Russia increased roughly tenfold between adoption of the 2016 action plan and 2020, including legislative changes informally referred to as "Apanasenko's law" after the suicide of a retired naval officer denied adequate pain relief.

Why it belongs / might belong:
The cube needed a non-US/UK entry on the threshold of "dying without adequate pain relief." In Russia, doctors have feared prescribing under Article 228.2 of the Criminal Code, and untreated pain has driven suicides in terminal patients. The office is morphine, prescription, refill, home delivery, and the refusal to let a dying person be punished for the state's drug-policy anxiety. It is the closest pair the cube has to the migrant-grave office, but inverted: not what is done to the stranger dead, but what is withheld from a dying person while still alive.

Flags:
This entry tests the DOOR-ETHICS questions hard. Federmesser joined the Moscow government in 2016, publicly endorsed Sergei Sobyanin (a Putin ally) for re-election in 2018, joined the All-Russia People's Front (a Putin-led political organisation), and has used direct access to Putin to plead for opioid reform. Critics within Russia's liberal milieu have argued she has let an honest name be used by an authoritarian government; she has argued that patients in pain cannot wait for politics. After the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, the political costs of state cooperation in Russia rose sharply; the cube notes this without resolving it. The office and its outcomes (pain relieved) are real; the entanglement is also real. Also: a separate St. Petersburg children's hospice run by Archpriest Alexander Tkachenko represents a parallel religious-origin door — flagged similarly to Bahay Nazareth.
