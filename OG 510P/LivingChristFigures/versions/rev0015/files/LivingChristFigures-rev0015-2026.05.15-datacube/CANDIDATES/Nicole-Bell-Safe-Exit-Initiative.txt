Nicole Bell Peña / Living in Freedom Together lineage / Safe Exit Initiative successor office / Her Way Home — Worcester

Status: PROVISIONAL KEEP; LINEAGE-UPDATED rev0015; PERSON-AND-SUCCESSOR-OFFICE ENTRY WITH IDEOLOGY, FUNDING, GOVERNANCE, AND SAFEGUARDING FLAGS
Location: Worcester, Massachusetts, USA
Office: Safe-exit threshold / survivor-led harm reduction, housing, and noncompliance-tolerant accompaniment for women impacted by commercial sexual exploitation, violence, substance use disorder, homelessness, and criminalisation

Work:
This entry is corrected in rev0015. The rev0010 file framed Nicole Bell as founder/director of Safe Exit Initiative, formerly Living in Freedom Together. Fresh searching shows the lineage is more complicated. Bell founded Living in Freedom Together (LIFT) in 2014; local reporting says she was no longer CEO by May 2023, and that LIFT rebranded as Safe Exit Initiative later in 2023 under successor leadership. Safe Exit Initiative continues the office through Jana’s Place, legal clinic work, HARBOR drop-in / harm reduction support, survivor-led trauma-responsive services, and related programs. Separately, Bell now appears publicly as Nikki Bell Peña, co-founder and co-executive director of Her Way Home with Jillian Keans. Her Way Home describes itself as building pathways home that honour autonomy, choice, and self-determination, meeting women where they are, reducing barriers to care, and refusing compliance, perfection, or silence as the price of healing.

Why it belongs / might belong:
The office remains strong but should no longer be written as a simple founder-equals-current-organisation story. Its threshold is the place where a woman leaving or surviving exploitation, homelessness, violence, or addiction is usually asked to prove readiness, gratitude, sobriety, compliance, or narrative purity before help becomes available. The better version of the office says: come as you are; safety does not require perfection.

Flags:
This is a lineage correction, not a demotion. It does, however, move the entry out of easy founder-hagiography. The cube records three distinct things: Bell’s founding role and later Her Way Home work; Safe Exit Initiative as continuing successor office under newer leadership; and the contested ideology of exit-oriented anti-prostitution work. Sex-trade politics remain contested between abolitionist, Equality Model, decriminalisation, harm-reduction, and sex-worker-rights frameworks. The cube should not let “survivor-led” cancel governance questions, funding instability, staff safety, participant autonomy, or whether services remain available to people who need harm reduction while continuing in the sex trade. The Worcester Guardian report notes funding strain and the closure of overnight HARBOR shelter services; CauseIQ later lists HARBOR as a drop-in / hub and Jana’s Place as a 16-bed recovery home. Current service capacity should be refreshed again before any public-facing use.
