Lalish rebaptism office / Yazidi Spiritual Council — Baba Sheikh Khurto Hajji Ismail (d. 2020) and Khidher Domle, with successor Hazim Tahsin Beg

Status: OFFICE-CANON; INSTITUTION-CANDIDATE WITH NAMED ADVOCATES; KEEP WITH INTERNAL-LIMITS, CHILD-EXCLUSION, AND PURIFICATION-LANGUAGE FLAGS
Location: Lalish temple, Sheikhan district, Duhok Governorate, Kurdistan Region of Iraq; office serves Yazidi survivors of the 2014 ISIS Sinjar genocide and continuing crisis
Office: Doctrinal-return-after-captivity / religious community readmission of forcibly converted and sexually enslaved survivors / anti-erasure inside the survivor's own community

Work:
In late August / September 2014, weeks after ISIS attacked Sinjar and began the abduction and sexual enslavement of Yazidi women and girls, the supreme Yazidi spiritual leader Baba Sheikh (Khurto Hajji Ismail) issued a decree — reportedly after consultation with the Yazidi activist Khidher Domle, media director at the University of Dohuk — declaring that survivors of ISIS captivity remained Yazidi, were "angels" and "victims," and were to be welcomed back into the community without honour-killing or excommunication. Survivors are received at Lalish, the holiest temple of the Yazidi faith, where they are baptised again with water from the temple's holy spring. By 2016 elders estimated roughly 500 women had been received in the first two years; the office continues today. Baba Sheikh died in October 2020; the council under his successor Hazim Tahsin Beg has continued the welcome.

Why it belongs / might belong:
This is the cube's first explicitly theological threshold office. Before 2014, Yazidi tradition would have excommunicated or killed women who had been raped, forcibly converted, or had sexual relations outside the faith. The September 2014 decree was a doctrinal change of a kind rarely seen in any minority religious community in the time scale of a genocide. The threshold is the gate of Lalish: the survivor crosses it and is told, by the highest spiritual authority, that the ferman (massacre/decree) has not taken her name. The cube has held legal-civil thresholds (Tuenjai Deetes) and forensic-after-death thresholds (Sonia Bermúdez); the Yazidi case is religious-communal.

Flags:
The welcome is partial. In April 2019 the Spiritual Council under Baba Sheikh issued a decree widely read as accepting children born of rape, then three days later "clarified" that the decree did not apply to those children — leaving Yazidi mothers and their children stranded, particularly in eastern Syria. The cube holds this limit honestly: doctrinal mercy is not unlimited, and the very framework that welcomed mothers excluded their children. Some observers, including survivors themselves, have noted that the "purification" framing of rebaptism risks implying that the woman, not the perpetrator, needed cleansing; the cube records this critique without dismissing the spiritual leaders' own framing. Some published reporting on Lalish deliberately did not show survivors' faces or names; where the source has chosen that discretion, the cube does not republish quotes or images in contexts that strip their original framing.
