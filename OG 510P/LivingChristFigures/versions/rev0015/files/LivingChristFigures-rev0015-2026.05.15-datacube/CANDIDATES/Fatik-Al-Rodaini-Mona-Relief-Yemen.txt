Fatik Al-Rodaini / Mona Relief (Yemen Organization for Humanitarian Relief and Development) — Yemen

Status: KEEP WITH WAR-CONTEXT, BLOCKADE, AND FUNDER-DIVERSITY FLAGS; OFFICE-CANON FOR THE YEMENI VARIANT OF COLLAPSED-STATE-NEIGHBORHOOD-KITCHEN
Location: Sanaa-based, distributing across Yemeni governorates including Sanaa, Marib, Manakha, and other areas accessible despite the Saudi-led coalition blockade and the war between SAF/RSF-equivalent factions
Office: Collapsed-state-neighborhood-kitchen in a blockaded country / locally-purchased food aid delivered under active bombing / sustenance with no border-crossing supply chain

Work:
Mona Relief was founded in 2015 by Fatik Al-Rodaini, a Sanaa-born journalist with a degree from Sanaa University, after the Saudi-led coalition began its bombing campaign in March 2015. The name comes from the woman who made the first donation that funded the initial food delivery; from a Twitter-organised "#20ForYemen" campaign that raised $4,200 in 10 days enough to feed roughly 150 families for a month, the operation grew. By 2017 Mona Relief had distributed over 7 million meals to internally displaced persons. The team — primarily Yemeni journalists and activists turned humanitarians — buys all food locally (the only way to circumvent the air, land, and naval blockade) and delivers in active conflict zones. The operation includes monthly Iftar deliveries during Ramadan and, notably, regular food packages to Yemen's tiny remaining Jewish minority in Sanaa since 2016. Roughly 100% of donations are reported to fund aid; 96% in remote areas, with 4% for transportation, packing, and volunteer lodging.

Why it belongs / might belong:
The cube needed Yemen specifically. Mona Relief is the better-documented Yemeni parallel to Sudan's Emergency Response Rooms — civilian-led, locally-sourced, operating where international NGOs cannot reach, surviving the war without becoming the war. Where the Sudan ERRs are decentralised resistance-committee descendants, Mona Relief is a single-coordinator network rooted in pre-war Yemeni journalistic networks; both share the COLLAPSED-STATE-NEIGHBORHOOD-KITCHEN office.

Flags:
Fatik Al-Rodaini's pre-2015 career included roles inside the office of former President Ali Abdullah Saleh's electronic media (2007-2011, 2012-2015), as well as translator work for the UN-sponsored National Dialogue Conference (2013-2014) and the UN envoy's office (2014). Yemen's professional class is small, and pre-war institutional ties do not by themselves disqualify present war-context work, but the cube records the entanglement rather than smoothing it over. Mona Relief has accepted funding from a wide range of small-donor channels (Patreon, IndieGoGo, GoFundMe) and partnership grants (Swaziland, the UK Darul Atfaal charity) because the blockade has restricted normal institutional channels. Al-Rodaini has stated publicly that "Saudi money talks" and that Western governments are co-responsible for civilian deaths in Yemen; the cube records this as his stated position. Mona Relief volunteers have been targeted while delivering aid; the lethality of the work is real, the office is not safe theatre.
