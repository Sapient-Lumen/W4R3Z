Khulumani Support Group — South Africa — unfinished TRC, reparations, missing-dead return, and survivor-led truth after official closure

Status: OFFICE-CANON; NETWORK-CANDIDATE; KEEP WITH STATE-FAILURE, REPARATIONS-LITIGATION, AND MOVEMENT-LONGEVITY FLAGS
Location: South Africa — national survivor/family network, with roots in TRC-era testimony and post-TRC reparations struggle
Office: Unfinished-TRC threshold / reparations witness / family-led insistence that truth, remains, and recognition are not closed files

Work:
Khulumani Support Group was formed in 1995 by survivors and families of victims of apartheid-era political conflict as the Truth and Reconciliation Commission process was beginning. Its work has included testimony support, survivor membership, truth-and-memory work, reparations advocacy, litigation and coalition work, and pressure for prosecutions and investigations where amnesty did not resolve accountability. In 2026 Khulumani told the Khampepe Commission that decades of survivor-led community work had documented more than 120,000 victim statements of apartheid-era gross human-rights violations, including families of the dead and disappeared as well as survivors of detention, torture, displacement, and political violence. Earlier missing-persons work shows the office in its starkest form: exhumation, identification, and return of remains to families whose hopes of recovering loved ones had repeatedly been raised and dashed.

Why it belongs / might belong:
The rev0013 gap list named South African TRC successors. Khulumani belongs because the threshold was not the televised TRC hearing; it was the long afterlife in which people remained outside recognition, reparations, prosecution, and the return of remains. The office is the refusal to let “reconciliation” close the door while families still have no body, no acknowledgement, no reparative support, or no prosecutorial truth.

Flags:
The cube should not confuse the moral clarity of survivor-led repair with simplicity. Khulumani is not an apolitical care charity; it is a movement against state delay and corporate/state impunity. It also carries the dangers of any long-lived social movement: leadership continuity, representativeness, litigation strategy, donor proximity, and the possibility that suffering becomes administratively instrumentalised. The office is nevertheless a strong match for threshold mercy because it keeps people at the door of recognition after the state declared the ritual complete.
