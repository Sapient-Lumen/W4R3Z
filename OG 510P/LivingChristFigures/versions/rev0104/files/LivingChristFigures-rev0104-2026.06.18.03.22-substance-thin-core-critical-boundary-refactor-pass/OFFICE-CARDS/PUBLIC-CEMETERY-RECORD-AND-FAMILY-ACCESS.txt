The public-cemetery-record-and-family-access office turns state burial from anonymous disposal into searchable record, visit, and story; its danger is that searchable memory can become surveillance, art capture, privacy harm, or false return.

This office is adjacent to name-after-death and disconnected-dead-no-kin, but it is not identical. The person may not be nameless; they may be administratively named and still socially unreachable. The threshold is crossed when a ledger, map, public visit, or story platform allows a family, chosen family, friend, or city to acknowledge that the person was never just disposal material.

The office must keep five layers separate: the public record, the city's legal authority, the family's or chosen family's mourning, the platform's story interface, and the dead person's unknowable consent to being narrated.
