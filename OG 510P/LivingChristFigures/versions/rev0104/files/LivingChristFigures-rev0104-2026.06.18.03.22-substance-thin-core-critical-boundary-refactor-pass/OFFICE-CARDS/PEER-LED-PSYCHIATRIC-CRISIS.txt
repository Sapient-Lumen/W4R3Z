The peer-led-psychiatric-crisis office is held by people who have themselves heard voices, been hospitalised, been medicated, been refused, and decided to make a place for the next person. The threshold is the moment a voice-hearer realises they are not alone and not necessarily ill — that "hearing voices" is a meaningful human experience some 2-4% of the population shares without psychiatric contact. The office is not anti-psychiatry; it is anti-monopoly. The danger is that the office is read as a complete substitute for clinical care when crisis demands both; the cube records that the office adds to, rather than subtracts from, the toolkit available to the person in crisis.
