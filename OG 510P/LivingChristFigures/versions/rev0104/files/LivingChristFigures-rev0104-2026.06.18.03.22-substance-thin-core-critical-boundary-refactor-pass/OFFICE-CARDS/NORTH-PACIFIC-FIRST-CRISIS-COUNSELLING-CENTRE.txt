OFFICE CARD: NORTH PACIFIC FIRST CRISIS COUNSELLING CENTRE
REVISION ADDED: rev0019

SHAPE:
A first or near-first specialist crisis-counselling centre in a small-island / atoll / scattered-island context. It may be a counselling centre before it is a shelter, a phone before it is a building, a trained “safe counsellor” before it is a fully resourced system.

CANDIDATES / LINEAGE:
- Chuuk Women’s Council / Tongen Inepwineu Counselling Centre / APIMAR SafeNet (rev0019).
- WUTMI / Weto In Mour in Majuro and Ebeye (rev0019).
- Kiribati Women and Children Support Centre (rev0019).
- Fiji Women’s Crisis Centre / PWNAVAW as regional training backbone (rev0018).

WHY IT IS A CHRIST-FIGURE OFFICE:
The first door is fragile. It carries disclosure, shame, safety planning, police/court/clinic referrals, and family pressure before there is a thick local infrastructure. Its mercy is not spectacle; it is the survivor hearing, “You can tell us, and we know what to do next.”

DOOR-ETHICS QUESTIONS:
1. Is “first” being used to honour scarcity rather than market the organisation?
2. Does counselling connect to actual referral options, safety, and follow-up?
3. Are counsellors supervised, protected from burnout, and trained for confidentiality?
4. Can outer-island and remote survivors reach the service?
5. Does the cube distinguish service availability from donor-funded project language?

IMAGE / LANGUAGE RULE:
No survivor images or identifying details. Do not turn “first crisis centre” into triumphalist branding. Record capacity, gaps, and fragility.


REV0020 APPEND:
Pohnpei and Kosrae additions are intentionally not called crisis counselling centres. They are law-implementation and government-staff capacity entries. This prevents the Chuuk TICC / WUTMI/WIM crisis-centre line from being overextended to every FSM state.
