REASONING NOTE — rev0020

Reason for the revision:
rev0019 created a capacity/current-status distinction around helplines, first crisis centres, referral networks, training pools, and frameworks. rev0020 applies the same discipline to legal frameworks. The searches turned up laws, court pages, MoUs, working groups, legal-capacity reports, and training. Those matter, but none equals a survivor-accessible door by itself.

Why these additions were admitted:
- Palau is admitted because the inter-ministerial MoU, health-sector training, data/evidence support, and Family Protection Act implementation line show a first-response/evidence-chain office rather than just a statute.
- Nauru is admitted because the DVFPA, Family Protection Coordination Committee, safety/protection-order procedures, service-provider training, and safe-house / DV-unit references create a law-to-door line. It is also the strongest caution entry because public court language around compulsory counselling and family repair could pressure reconciliation.
- Nauru counselling/social-work capacity is admitted separately as a capacity precursor, not a service.
- FSM Pohnpei/Kosrae Family Protection Adviser is admitted as law-implementation connective work: strengthening services, collaboration, advocacy, and policy reform.
- Pohnpei DHSA staff training is admitted as pre-referral capacity only; it is deliberately not called a crisis service.
- Niue is admitted as negative space because the evidence says no counselling services for survivors of violence at the time of review and later research focuses on family-space dynamics rather than formal service provision.
- The RWG is admitted as a regional law-to-practice backbone.
- UNDP/MLSC Justice Pathways is admitted as a legal-capacity map/reference candidate, not as a service or legal advice.

Why rule 17 was added:
The legal-framework cluster exposed a new error risk: the cube could accidentally count a law, order, MoU, adviser, or framework as mercy without asking whether survivors can cross it safely. Rule 17 also responds to compulsory counselling / family-preservation language by requiring explicit coercion/reconciliation flags.

Main caution:
Most sources in this revision are official, regional, court, development, or research sources. They are adequate to map public institutional shape and gaps, but they do not prove current accessible services. The next pass should seek direct current local service information, legal-aid routes, enforcement evidence, and survivor-safe critique.
