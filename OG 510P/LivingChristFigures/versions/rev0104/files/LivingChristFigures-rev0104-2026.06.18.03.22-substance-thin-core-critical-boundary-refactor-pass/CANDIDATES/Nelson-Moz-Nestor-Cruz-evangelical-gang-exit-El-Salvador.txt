Pastor Nelson Moz / Eben-Ezer Baptist Church (El Salvador) and Pastor Nestor Cruz / Hungry Church (El Salvador) — religious exit-ramp from gang life

Status: KEEP WITH STATE-OF-EMERGENCY ENDANGERMENT, EVANGELICAL-FRAMING, AND CONVERSION-AS-PRECONDITION FLAGS; OFFICE-CANON FOR THE NON-US PARALLEL TO HOMEBOY INDUSTRIES — DOCUMENTED PERSON-BENCHMARK CANON ALSO INCLUDES GREG BOYLE / HOMEBOY INDUSTRIES
Location: San Salvador and elsewhere in El Salvador; particularly evangelical churches operating in former MS-13 and Barrio 18 territories
Office: Religious exit-ramp / reentry-from-gang via faith community / the rare departure from a gang the gang will permit

Work:
In El Salvador and Honduras gangs (maras) generally do not let members "retire"; departure is punished by death. The two near-universal exceptions are leaving the country and converting to an evangelical Christian church. Pastor Nelson Moz has led Eben-Ezer Baptist church in San Salvador for over two decades, working with former gang members and conducting at least three police raids' worth of interrogation during worship services during the post-2022 state of emergency. Pastor Nestor Cruz founded Hungry Church for former gang members deported from the United States — including himself, an MS-13-affiliated former youth offender deported to El Salvador after over 12 years in US prison; Hungry Church provides housing, microbusiness work (eggs, pupusas), and from 2018 carpentry and auto-mechanics training partly funded through the Salvadoran Lutheran Church / ELCA Lutheran Disaster Response. Research by José Miguel Cruz (FIU) finds over half of surveyed Salvadoran gang members identify as evangelical; church identity replaces gang identity. Companion canon: Greg Boyle / Homeboy Industries (Los Angeles, 1988) — the world's largest gang-intervention and reentry programme, kept as PERSON-BENCHMARK because of fame and US location, but the office shape is the same.

Why it belongs / might belong:
The rev0012 SearchTrail named "prison reentry beyond the US" specifically. Salvadoran and Honduran evangelical reentry offices are the most documented non-US case of the gang/prison reentry office and the most endangered: pastors operating these offices have been threatened by police and arrested under President Bukele's March 2022 state of emergency. Even gang leaders historically respected the church-as-exit; the state of emergency has criminalised the only exit that worked. The office is the church door and the willingness of a pastor to keep it open under accusation of being "a pastor for gangsters."

Flags:
The evangelical exit ramp requires conversion: gangs monitor former members for visible church attendance, sobriety, and avoidance of crime, and the gang will kill a member who "fails as a Christian." The cube notes this without endorsing the evangelical-only framing — Catholic reintegration has historically had less buy-in from gang leaders, and other reform pathways exist but at scale only the evangelical model has functioned. Under Bukele's state of emergency (renewed continuously since March 2022), pastors and former gang members in rehabilitation have been arbitrarily arrested; the office is operating under direct state opposition. Some sources withhold pastors' real names for safety (Newsweek's Honduras source uses "Pastor Alwin Flores," a pseudonym; one America Magazine source from SPASS / Servicio Social Pasionista asked to remain anonymous). The cube uses names that are already public — Nelson Moz, Nestor Cruz, Luis Gonzalez, the academic José Miguel Cruz — and respects the discretion of sources who chose otherwise. The same applies to former gang members: the cube uses the names the sources use (Wilfredo of Eben-Ezer, José Rolando Arévalo, Montano in NPR's reporting) and does not retroactively un-anonymise where reporting did not.
