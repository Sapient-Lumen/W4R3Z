NAME: Niue GBV service-gap marker / family-tree mapping intervention
LOCATION: Niue
OFFICE: Negative-space office: no/limited formal survivor-service door; family-space disclosure/accountability research
STATUS: GAP-MARKER; NEGATIVE-SPACE ENTRY; NOT A SERVICE DIRECTORY; SMALL-COMMUNITY PRIVACY CAUTION HOLDS; PACIFIC-GBV-SERVICE-CAPACITY-QUARANTINE rev0090

SUMMARY:
Niue is admitted as a negative-space entry rather than a service entry. A Pacific Data Hub review of counselling services in the Pacific reported that, among 14 Pacific Island countries reviewed, Niue reported no counselling services for survivors of violence and most other Pacific Island countries had only limited counselling services. A 2021 Pacific Health Dialog article on family-tree mapping and GBV in Niue says there is a lack of research and attention on GBV in Niue and examines how to work with the family space toward eliminating violence and promoting healthy relationships. Its family-tree mapping approach is framed as a way to illuminate disclosure, accountability, education, and talanoa/gossip while protecting participant privacy.

WHY THIS FITS THE CUBE:
The cube needs to record absence as well as presence. Where there is no formal survivor door, family-space research can help name hidden dynamics and privacy risks, but it is not itself a crisis service. This keeps the cube from pretending that every country has an office just because the search wants one.

OFFICE SHAPE:
- Service-gap marker.
- Family-tree mapping as research/intervention approach.
- Disclosure, accountability, education, and privacy in small-family/community space.
- No live-referral claim.

EVIDENCE:
1. Pacific Data Hub counselling review page: Niue reported no counselling services for survivors of violence in the 14-country review.
2. Pacific Health Dialog article: Niue has lack of research and attention on GBV; family-tree mapping pilots a privacy-protective way to examine family-space dynamics around disclosure and accountability.
3. UN Women Niue brief/search results separately support concern about absence of safe-house/counselling services, but the cube prefers the directly accessible Pacific Data Hub and journal pages for this revision.

DOOR-ETHICS FLAGS:
- Do not convert research into a service.
- In small communities, disclosure can identify survivors even without names; privacy is central.
- Family-based interventions can help or trap; accountability must not become pressure to reconcile or stay silent.
