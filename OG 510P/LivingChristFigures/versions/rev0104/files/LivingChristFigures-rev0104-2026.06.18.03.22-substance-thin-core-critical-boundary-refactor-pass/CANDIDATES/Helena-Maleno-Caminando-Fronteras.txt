Helena Maleno / Caminando Fronteras — phone, ledger, and family-search office at the Western Euro-African border

Status: OFFICE-CANON; PERSON-BENCHMARK, NOT UNKNOWN CANON; REVERIFIED rev0047 WITH MIGRANT-DEATH NO-ROUTE BOUNDARY
Location: Morocco / Spain / Western Euro-African border; Alboran, Algerian, Canary Islands, Strait of Gibraltar, overland, and related routes
Office: Distress-call witness / 24-hour sea-danger hotline / counter-forensic ledger / family search for the sea-dead and disappeared / border anti-erasure

Work:
Caminando Fronteras says its networking with migrant people protects the right to life and that it operates a telephone hotline 24 hours a day, 365 days a year for people whose lives are in danger at sea. It says this work has helped save more than 197,000 people on Western Euro-African sea routes. Its Right to Life monitoring reports document people dead or disappeared during migration to Spain and identify failures of rescue, delayed or absent search-and-rescue activation, state non-coordination, border externalisation, longer routes, and precarious boats as risk factors. The 2025 report records 3,090 people dead or disappeared between 1 January and 15 December 2025, including 192 women and 437 children/adolescents, across 303 tragedies and 70 boats that disappeared without trace. The same work treats deaths and disappearances as a family, justice, and memory question, not merely a border statistic.

Why it belongs / might belong:
The office is a phone and a ledger against water. It does not make the migrant locatable by ownership; it makes the state’s non-response locatable by evidence. The phone answers the immediate threshold — danger at sea — while the monitoring and family-search work answer the second threshold: after disappearance, when the person is at risk of becoming a number, an unidentified body, or no record at all.

Rev0047 boundary addendum — migrant-death monitoring is not a public route record:
Current Caminando Fronteras and IOM material strengthens the death-monitoring and undercount side of this entry, but it also sharpens the redaction risk. Route categories, missing-boat counts, distress-call context, family-search links, and incident datasets are held as counter-forensic/accountability evidence, not as public instructions. The cube may say, at high level, that the office combines distress witness, death/disappearance monitoring, family-memory pressure, and state-accountability pressure. It must not reproduce contact prompts, route/path details, coordinates, boat traces, distress-call material, family leads, individual names, images, nationality/age clusters tied to incidents, or any wording that lets a reader reconstruct a crossing or search path.

Flags:
Helena Maleno is a public activist figure with awards, recognition, and criminalisation history; this is not an unknown/non-powerful entry. The cube keeps it because the office is too important to omit, not because fame is proof. This entry carries high child-image and grief-image risk. The cube must not reproduce images of boats, bodies, families, or children as emotional currency. It must also avoid treating death counts as clean; the counts are counter-forensic work under conditions of undercount, disappearance, and political denial.
