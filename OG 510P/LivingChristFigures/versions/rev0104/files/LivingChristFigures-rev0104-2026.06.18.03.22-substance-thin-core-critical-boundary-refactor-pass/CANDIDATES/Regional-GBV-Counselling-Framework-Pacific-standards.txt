NAME: Regional GBV Counselling Framework / RWG and Pacific Women’s Network to End Violence Against Women
LOCATION: Pacific regional framework; launch in Suva, Fiji
OFFICE: Minimum standards for safe, ethical, survivor-centred GBV counselling across Pacific countries
STATUS: OFFICE-CANON / FRAMEWORK-CANDIDATE; IMPLEMENTATION-GAP FLAG; PACIFIC-GBV-SERVICE-CAPACITY-QUARANTINE rev0090

SUMMARY:
In February 2026, the Regional Working Group on the Implementation of Domestic Violence / Family Protection Legislation, together with the Pacific Women’s Network to End Violence Against Women, launched the Regional Gender Based Violence Counselling Framework during GESI week at the Pacific Islands Forum Secretariat. Reporting describes it as Pacific-led and Pacific-owned guidance to strengthen counselling standards for GBV survivors. The framework was developed through collaboration between governments, crisis centres, counsellors, and civil society organisations, with consultations in Kiribati, Samoa, and Papua New Guinea. It establishes minimum standards for ethical, safe, survivor-centred counselling practice and guidance on confidentiality, safeguarding, professional conduct, and accountability for service providers from government counsellors to women’s crisis centres and faith-based organisations.

WHY THIS FITS THE CUBE:
A survivor-centred door can fail even when a law exists. This framework is a threshold-underlay: not a rescuer, but a shared minimum grammar for the person who answers the disclosure, protects confidentiality, avoids blame, and knows the limits of their role.

OFFICE SHAPE:
- Minimum standards for GBV counselling.
- Confidentiality, safeguarding, conduct, and accountability guidance.
- Regional government/CSO/crisis-centre collaboration.
- Tool for countries adapting or establishing national counselling standards.
- Link between domestic-violence law and real survivor-centred services.

EVIDENCE:
1. PINA / SPC report: framework launched 12 February 2026; Pacific-led/Pacific-owned; developed through regional collaboration and consultations; establishes minimum standards.
2. Report states that prior to this work only Tonga, Solomon Islands, and Kiribati had national GBV counselling standards.
3. Pacific Women Lead 2024–25 progress-report page: confirms ongoing monitoring/evaluation and regional gender-equality implementation context, though framework implementation still needs country-level follow-up.

DOOR-ETHICS FLAGS:
- A framework is not a service; implementation and accountability must be checked country by country.
- Standards must not become gatekeeping that excludes informal survivor helpers where no formal service exists.
- Faith-based and government providers need explicit confidentiality, autonomy, and survivor-choice checks.
- Future pass: locate the full framework, national adoption plans, and survivor/practitioner feedback after implementation begins.
