REFRESH NOTE — rev0022
SUBJECT: Nauru mandatory counselling — open question answered; caution sharpened a second time

rev0021 verified the s27 court-reporting / s28 counsellor-registration
mechanism and left this open: "whether separate, voluntary, trauma-informed,
safety-led counselling is available as an alternative."

rev0022 verification outcome (primary text, Nauru District Court Bench Book
ch.17, Domestic Violence and Family Protection Act 2017, read in full):

- s 26(1): on granting any protection order the court MUST order the parties
  "either jointly or severally" into MANDATORY counselling; non-attendance
  without reasonable cause is contempt, up to one month imprisonment.
- s 27: counsellor reports to the court fortnightly or as directed (re-confirmed).
- s 19(1)(d): a permanent protection order may be granted where a counsellor's
  report "concludes that the parties cannot reconcile" — a reconciliation
  assessment as a statutory gateway to lasting protection.
- s 3 objectives expressly include "family mediation" and to "foster peaceful
  and lasting domestic relationships."

Answer to the open question: the framework provides NO separate, voluntary,
non-court-reported statutory counselling alternative. The only statutory
counselling is compulsory, court-reported, and may be joint, with
reconciliation written into both the purpose clause and the permanent-order
test. This is the configuration rule 17 exists to flag.

Status change:
- Before (rev0021): documented structural risk (court-reported counselling).
- After  (rev0022): documented structural risk, sharper — mandatory,
  joint-or-several, contempt-enforced counselling; reconciliation-conclusion
  gateway; no statutory voluntary alternative.
- Still open: whether this pressures survivors in practice; whether any
  NON-statutory confidential counselling exists outside the Act; survivor-side
  evidence; current capacity. No survivor-reported source found. Indirect
  corroboration only: 2023 US State Dept report — apology/reconciliation plays
  an informal role in Nauru proceedings "sometimes due to communal pressure."

No over-correction:
This is a confirmation about legal/court structure, not survivor testimony,
and NOT a finding that Nauru's entry should be removed. The Bench Book also
documents protective provisions, recorded so the entry is not distorted: closed
hearings (s44), publication restriction with an offence (s45), application by
phone/electronic/in person/in writing (s23), same-day interim orders not
requiring either party present (s18), safety orders without victim consent
where protection grounds are met (s13 — protective AND a survivor-control
limit). The entry stays, admitted deliberately in rev0020 WITH this caution.
The flag hardens; the safety question remains open.

Carry-forward:
- existence/accessibility of any non-statutory voluntary confidential counselling;
- survivor-side / independent-evaluation evidence on counselling practice;
- protection-order enforcement in a small, dense kin-network;
- legal aid, safe accommodation adequacy, transport, privacy, retaliation
  safeguards.

Door-ethics note:
Confirming that a counselling mechanism is coercive by design was done from
public legal/judicial-training text only, with no shelter, route, intake,
staff, helpline-tree, or survivor detail recorded (rule 15).
