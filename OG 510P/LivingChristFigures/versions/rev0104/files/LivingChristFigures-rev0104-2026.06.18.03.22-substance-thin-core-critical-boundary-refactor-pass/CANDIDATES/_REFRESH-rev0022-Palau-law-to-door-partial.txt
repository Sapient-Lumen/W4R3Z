REFRESH NOTE — rev0022
SUBJECT: Palau law-to-door entry — verification outcome: HOLDS, partially operational, capacity unverified

rev0020 admitted Palau as a law-to-door / health-first-responder /
evidence-chain entry and flagged "law/MoU/training not equal service;
confidentiality and consent; data not surveillance; cultural/religious leader
precondition risk." rev0021's START-HERE named Palau current-service
verification as a top carry-forward.

rev0022 verification outcome:

Operational reality (welcome, with limits attached in the same breath):
- Protection orders directly from court without going first to police; free of
  charge; 24/7 emergency line; court officials assist applications; court can
  assist locating safe temporary accommodation (Palau Judiciary, via Island
  Times report of the Judiciary's own statement).
- Victims of Crime Office provides counselling and temporary housing for
  victims via NGO partnership (UN CRPD review of Palau, March 2025).
- FPA coordinator exists; Human Trafficking Office and a Temporary Shelter
  established (Palau Beijing+25 review). The shelter is recorded in the
  trafficking-office context only; no general DV-shelter capacity is inferred
  and no shelter location/operational detail is recorded (rule 15).

Limits, un-smoothed (rule 16):
- 2016 UN Country Team: despite the Act, protection measures — shelters and
  survivor services — were not actually in place.
- March 2025 CRPD review: continuing implementation gaps — law-enforcement
  training gap on the Act, ad-hoc disability/sign-language access, data gaps;
  State to examine gaps over 6-12 months. Framework-in-motion, not verified
  steady-state capacity.
- Pacific Women "Five Years On" review existed because coordination and
  availability of survivor services needed strengthening.

Rule 17 reading (accurate contrast; NOT a softening of Nauru):
Palau's FPA 2012 (RPPL 8-51) directs court-ordered "domestic violence
intervention" at the convicted/ordered person with proof-of-compliance —
perpetrator-directed, structurally unlike Nauru's survivor-binding s26
mandatory counselling. Customary restitution does not extinguish criminal
liability. Palau's own ownership-tension is the no-drop policy: protective
against pressured withdrawal AND a removal of survivor control (door-ethics
Q2). Recorded as both.

Status:
- Before (rev0020): PROVISIONAL KEEP; law-to-door implementation entry.
- After  (rev0022): status unchanged; verified as a real law-to-door line with
  partial operational substance AND unverified current capacity independently
  found gap-laden as of March 2025. Not upgraded to a verified
  survivor-accessible steady-state service. rev0020 flags stand.

Carry-forward:
- verified current capacity/staffing of survivor counselling and temporary
  housing beyond treaty-body summary;
- state/outer-area access; disability and language access formalisation;
- the no-drop policy's effect on survivor choice;
- enforcement, legal aid, transport, privacy/retaliation safeguards.

Door-ethics note:
Verification used public institutional and treaty-body sources only; no
shelter, route, intake, staff, helpline-tree, or survivor detail recorded.
