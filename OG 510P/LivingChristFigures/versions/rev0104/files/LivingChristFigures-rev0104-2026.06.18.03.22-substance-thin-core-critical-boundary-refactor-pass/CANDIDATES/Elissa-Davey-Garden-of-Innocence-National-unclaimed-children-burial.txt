NAME: Elissa Davey / Garden of Innocence National — abandoned and unidentified children burial office
LOCATION: United States; originated in San Diego, California; affiliated/local garden network
OFFICE: Unclaimed-child burial, naming, and communal witness after death
STATUS: PROVISIONAL KEEP; OFFICE-CANON CANDIDATE FOR UNCLAIMED-CHILD BURIAL; POSTHUMOUS-CONSENT AND CATEGORY-BOUNDARY LOCKED rev0087

SUMMARY:
Garden of Innocence presents itself as a nonprofit that provides dignified burials for abandoned and unidentified children. Elissa Davey is the founder associated with the San Diego origin line and the national replication effort. The office is not rescue before death. It is the threshold after death where a child with no claimant, no name in public memory, or no family able or willing to arrange burial would otherwise pass into disposal logic.

WHY THIS FITS THE CUBE:
The cube already has name-after-death, migrant-grave, disconnected-dead, and return-of-the-disappeared offices. This entry adds a sharper and more dangerous subtype: the child whose first stable public care may arrive only after death. The mercy-shape is grave, name, service, handmade burial goods, escort, and community witness. The danger-shape is equally real: a dead child cannot consent to ritual, naming, imagery, donor story, religious framing, or category assignment. Therefore this candidate is admitted only with explicit posthumous-consent and category-boundary debt.

OFFICE SHAPE:
- Claiming or receiving abandoned, unidentified, or unclaimed children from public/coroner/medical-examiner/cemetery systems.
- Providing dignified burial or interment rather than unmarked disposal.
- Naming the child when no name is available, while treating names as load-bearing memorial data rather than decorative prose.
- Mobilizing volunteers, cemeteries, funeral labor, caskets/urns, blankets, ritual leadership, music, and community witnesses.
- Replicating through local gardens that appear to be community-staffed and locally controlled, with a national identity and shared manual/history.

EVIDENCE:
1. Garden of Innocence's own public mission page states that it provides dignified burials for abandoned and unidentified children.
2. Points of Light identifies Elissa Davey as founder and describes work with coroners' offices and cemeteries to claim bodies of abandoned and unidentified children and provide burial.
3. A 2024 Southern Cross report on San Diego's twenty-fifth anniversary says the San Diego garden had held nondenominational graveside services for more than 250 children and describes the first 1999 burial line.
4. The Garden of Innocence "Our Gardens" page lists multiple local gardens and describes a sister relationship with the St. Louis Garden of Innocents.
5. Orange County Cemetery District separately describes its Garden of Innocence services for abandoned and unidentified deceased children in Orange County.
6. St. Louis Garden of Innocents is a related but distinct organization for unclaimed children/infants in medical-examiner custody; it is not collapsed into the same candidate, but it helps define the office subtype and the name-collision risk.
7. Sacramento News & Review's 2013 critical reporting is retained as a boundary source: it challenged local usage of the "abandoned children" frame and raised stillbirth/fetal-remains and parental-knowledge concerns. The cube treats that not as disqualification but as mandatory evidence debt.

8. California Health and Safety Code fetal-death registration provisions are retained as official context for keeping fetal death, registration, disposition, and public memorial language distinct.
9. California Health and Safety Code disposition-control section is retained as official context for ordered authority over disposition and reasonable-inquiry boundaries; it does not authorize any case-level conclusion.
10. California State Auditor fetal-death registration audit report is retained as public-agency context for fetal-death registration delays and family-process burden, strengthening the no-assumption rule.

DOOR-ETHICS FLAGS:
- A burial can be dignified and still overclaim consent, category, or representation.
- "Abandoned," "unidentified," "unclaimed," "stillborn," "indigent," and "family unable to arrange burial" are not interchangeable categories.
- Public ceremony, names, photos, poems, and religious framing may serve memory, but they also create posthumous narrative ownership.
- A child name should not be repeated in cube prose unless the name is necessary to the claim being audited.
- The cube must not publish live contact details or imply this is a referral path for bereaved families or public agencies.

CURRENT CAPACITY / SAFETY:
Public institutional shape is supported. Current capacity is not verified garden-by-garden. Some sources list public locations and contact points, but the cube should not replicate them as operational guidance. `live_referral_safe=false` remains mandatory.

REV0087 CATEGORY / FAMILY-CONSENT BOUNDARY AUDIT:
Rev0087 does not resolve the factual category of any child and does not convert burial into proof of family abandonment, family consent, or child consent. Instead it locks the boundary that public prose must keep abandoned, unidentified, unclaimed, stillborn, indigent, family unable to arrange burial, and family unreachable after reasonable inquiry as separate categories. Official California legal context treats fetal-death registration/disposition and authority over disposition as procedural questions, not as charity-story vocabulary. The California State Auditor's 2024 fetal-death registration report adds process-risk context: delays and complex handoffs can burden families, so administrative disposition timing cannot safely be rewritten as family choice or abandonment. The Sacramento critical source remains adjacent because it raises the exact mismatch risk: local public charity framing may not match stillbirth/fetal-remains and parental-knowledge facts.

Rev0087 therefore changes the candidate from a merely open debt state into a no-case gate: no child name, assigned name, image, poem, public ceremony detail, cemetery/location cue, contact path, service prompt, family narrative, legal instruction, or case-level category claim may be exported. Official and cemetery pages may support only institutional shape after manual review. Legal/auditor sources may support only category-boundary and family-authority caution; they are not legal advice and do not prove anything about any individual family.

BOUNDARY NOTE:
This entry is intentionally boundary-first. The mercy is obvious enough to attract easy hagiography. The hard question is whether the office can give a child a name and grave without turning the child's death into a proof of the helper's purity, a donor object, or a religious/public ceremony imposed after the fact. The cube admits the office because the threshold is real; it refuses to close the evidence debt because the threshold is also dangerous.

REV0104 CRITICAL-DEBT DISPOSITION:
Rev0104 converts the two remaining critical evidence debts for this candidate into permanent boundary controls. This is a completion decision, not a new public finding. The cube should not keep asking future reviewers to resolve child category, family knowledge, family consent, abandonment, incapacity, refusal, or posthumous consent by collecting more case-level material. That path is too likely to reproduce the extraction the entry is meant to prevent.

Permitted inference after rev0104: public and official sources can support only a high-level institutional office shape: dignified burial/interment and community witness for children described by sources as abandoned, unidentified, unclaimed, or otherwise not claimed through ordinary systems. Forbidden inference after rev0104: that any particular child was abandoned by family, that family had or lacked knowledge, that family consented or refused, that public naming equals consent, that ceremony equals representation, or that a live service/referral path is safe to publish.

The candidate therefore remains provisionally kept as an office-canon example, but only under a no-case, no-image, no-name, no-contact, no-location, no-ceremony-detail, no-family-narrative export rule.
