Reasoning note — rev0039

After rev0038, the cube had proven that border counterpressure could be promoted without becoming a map. The next harder case was the Pacific survivor-service cluster: the most current sources are often exactly the sources that are unsafe for a public non-referral cube, because they contain phone, intake, location, and shelter/refuge surfaces.

The solution in rev0039 is not to pretend current service pages do not matter. It is to split the source class. Regional funding and counselling-standards sources can be promoted because they make public prose more constrained: they emphasize survivor-centred practice, confidentiality, safeguarding, professional conduct, accountability, and the gap between law/funding and lived access. Contact-rich primary/service/directory pages remain held or blocked.

This pass deliberately releases no public claim. Its behavioral value is that a funding announcement and a standards framework now function as brakes against brochure-style crisis-centre prose, not as permission to publish a referral directory.
