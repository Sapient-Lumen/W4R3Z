Finding 1 — Nauru: the open counselling question is ANSWERED; the caution
SHARPENS a second time. Status moves from "documented court-reporting risk"
(rev0021) to "documented mandatory-joint-or-several, contempt-enforced,
reconciliation-gated counselling with no statutory voluntary alternative."
------------------------------------------------------------------------
rev0021 verified s27 (counsellor reports to the court fortnightly) and s28
(Director of Women registers counsellors), and left open: "whether separate,
voluntary, trauma-informed, safety-led counselling is available as an
alternative." rev0022 read the primary judicial-training text in full (Nauru
District Court Bench Book, ch.17, Domestic Violence and Family Protection Act
2017). The full text shows s27 is the milder half of the mechanism:

- s 26(1): when a protection order is granted, the court MUST make a
  supplementary order directing the parties, "either jointly or severally,"
  to attend MANDATORY counselling. Failure to attend without reasonable cause
  is contempt of court, punishable by imprisonment not exceeding one month.
  The counselling is therefore compulsory for the protected person too, can be
  joint with the respondent, and is attached to every protection order by
  statutory obligation, not judicial discretion.

- s 27: the counsellor must report to the court fortnightly or as directed
  (verified rev0021; re-confirmed).

- s 19(1)(d): a permanent protection order may be granted where "a report from
  a counsellor under Part 5 concludes that the parties cannot reconcile." A
  counsellor's reconciliation assessment is thus a statutory gateway condition
  for lasting protection.

- s 3 objectives expressly include "measures for rehabilitation and family
  mediation" and to "foster peaceful and lasting domestic relationships."

Answer to rev0021's open question: the framework itself provides NO separate,
voluntary, non-court-reported counselling route. The only statutory counselling
is mandatory, court-reported, and may be joint, with reconciliation written
into the Act's purpose clause and into the test for a permanent order. This is
precisely the configuration rule 17 names. Status of the caution:

- rev0020: suspected coercion/reconciliation risk (flagged).
- rev0021: documented structural risk from primary text (court-reporting).
- rev0022: documented structural risk, sharper - mandatory, joint-or-several,
  contempt-enforced counselling with a reconciliation-conclusion gateway and
  no statutory voluntary alternative.

Still open, and not closed by this pass: whether, in lived practice, this
pressures survivors to reconcile or stay silent; whether any NON-statutory
(NGO/health/voluntary) confidential counselling exists outside the Act in
Nauru; survivor-side testimony; current service capacity. No survivor-reported
source was found. Indirect corroboration only: the 2023 US State Department
human-rights report records that, although Nauru has no statutory traditional
reconciliation mechanism, apology and reconciliation play an informal role in
proceedings "sometimes due to communal pressure" - consistent-direction, not
survivor-reported, and recorded as such.

No over-correction. The Bench Book also documents genuinely protective
provisions, recorded here so the entry is not distorted in the harsh
direction: protection-order hearings are closed to the public (s44);
publication of proceedings is restricted with an offence for breach (s45); an
application may be made in person, in writing, electronically, or by telephone
(s23); an interim order is determined the same day and needs neither party
present (s18); safety orders may issue without the victim's consent where the
protection grounds are met (s13 - protective and, simultaneously, a
survivor-control limit; recorded as both). Nauru was admitted in rev0020
deliberately, as a law-to-door line WITH this caution attached. The entry
stays. The flag hardens again. The safety question remains open. This is the
cube paying its own debt, not refuting its own entry.

Finding 2 — Palau: law-to-door entry HOLDS and is PARTIALLY operational;
current-capacity adequacy remains UNVERIFIED and was independently found
gap-laden as recently as March 2025. No upgrade, no removal, no softening.
------------------------------------------------------------------------
rev0020 admitted Palau as a law-to-door / health-first-responder /
evidence-chain entry and flagged "law/MoU/training not equal service." rev0022
tested current-service status against primary and direct sources:

Operational reality found (recorded as welcome, with limits attached):
- Protection orders may be sought directly from the court without going first
  to police; orders are free of charge; a 24/7 emergency line is described;
  court officials assist with completing applications and the court can assist
  with locating safe temporary accommodation while an order is sought (Palau
  Judiciary, via Island Times report of the Judiciary's own statement).
- A Victims of Crime Office provides services including counselling and
  temporary housing for victims, in partnership with NGOs (UN CRPD review of
  Palau, Geneva, March 2025 - recent and treaty-body-sourced).
- An FPA coordinator exists; a Human Trafficking Office and a Temporary
  Shelter have been established (Palau Beijing+25 national review). Note: the
  named Temporary Shelter is recorded in the trafficking-office context; the
  cube does not infer a general, currently-staffed DV survivor shelter from
  this and does not record any shelter location or operational detail
  (rule 15).

Limits found, kept un-smoothed (rule 16):
- The 2016 UN Country Team found that, despite the Act, protection measures -
  specifically shelters and survivor services - were not actually in place.
- The March 2025 CRPD review records continuing implementation gaps: a lack of
  law-enforcement training on the Act itself, ad-hoc rather than formalised
  disability/sign-language access, and data gaps. The State undertook to
  examine gaps over the following 6-12 months - i.e. still framework-in-motion,
  not verified steady-state capacity.
- The Pacific Women "Five Years On" review existed precisely because
  coordination and availability of survivor services needed strengthening.

Rule 17 reading, recorded as accurate contrast and explicitly NOT as softening
Nauru: Palau's Family Protection Act 2012 (RPPL 8-51) directs court-ordered
"domestic violence intervention" at the convicted/ordered person, who must
provide proof of compliance - perpetrator-directed, structurally unlike
Nauru's survivor-binding s26 mandatory counselling. Palau law also states that
customary restitution does not extinguish criminal liability - an
anti-reconciliation-pressure feature. These distinguish Palau favourably on
the reconciliation axis; they do not soften the Nauru finding and are not
permitted to. Palau's own ownership-tension is its no-drop policy (once a
report is made it follows due process): protective against withdrawal under
pressure, and simultaneously a removal of survivor control over the process -
recorded as both, under door-ethics Q2 (can the person refuse the script?).

Status of the Palau entry:
- Before (rev0020): PROVISIONAL KEEP; law-to-door implementation entry.
- After (rev0022): unchanged status; verification-supported as a real
  law-to-door line with partial operational substance AND unverified current
  capacity independently found gap-laden as of March 2025. Not upgraded to a
  verified survivor-accessible steady-state service. The rev0020 flags stand.
