# Boundary Essay 003 — Outreach Is Not Housing

Status: seeded rev0028
Track: longform_failure_and_boundary_essays
Seed candidate: `cand_jim_withers_operation_safety_net_street_medicine_institute`
Seed office: `office_street_medicine_unsheltered_house_call`

Street medicine begins with a true accusation: the clinic door was built in the wrong place.

A person sleeping outside may have an infected wound, asthma, frostbite, psychosis, withdrawal, trauma, or pain, and the ordinary clinical system still waits behind paperwork, transport, a waiting room, a phone, an appointment, storage for belongings, trust it has not earned, and a body that can survive the trip. Street medicine answers by moving the clinical threshold outward. It carries care to the camp, the sidewalk, the bridge, the alley, the doorway, the place the person currently is.

That movement is mercy. It is also dangerous.

A street-medicine team learns where people sleep. It may record locations. It may coordinate with hospitals, shelters, outreach teams, public agencies, police-adjacent systems, data platforms, and housing lists. Every one of those bridges can help. Every one can also betray. A map that helps a nurse find a patient can help another system find the same person for removal. A warm handoff can become coerced routing. A photo can become spectacle. A chart can become exposure. A grant metric can turn return visits into proof that the system is working while the person remains outside.

The office therefore needs a hard sentence:

Outreach is not housing.

Care outside is necessary because the patient is outside. It must not become the reason the public tolerates the outside. Street medicine is not the city’s absolution. It is a witness against the city’s threshold failure.
