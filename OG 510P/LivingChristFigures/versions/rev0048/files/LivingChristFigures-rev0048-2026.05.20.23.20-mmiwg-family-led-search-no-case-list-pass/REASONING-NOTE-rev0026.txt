I chose Garden of Innocence / Elissa Davey because the cube already had mature death-and-memory offices, but lacked the sharply bounded subtype where a child is named and buried after abandonment, unidentified death, or unclaimed-remains custody. The addition is intentionally not a hagiographic pass.

The public evidence supports a real office-shape: the nonprofit's mission page, Points of Light founder profile, Southern Cross 25-year San Diego report, local cemetery page, and related St. Louis reporting all describe grave/name/ceremony/community-witness work. But the Sacramento News & Review critique creates the essential negative evidence: category assignment and family knowledge/consent cannot be inferred from a beautiful burial.

Therefore rev0026 adds rule 20, a new office card, a boundary essay seed, and high-priority evidence debt. The central sentence remains under pressure: the office is the threshold held open without owning the one who crosses it. Here, the one who crosses cannot refuse; that makes non-ownership harder, not easier.
