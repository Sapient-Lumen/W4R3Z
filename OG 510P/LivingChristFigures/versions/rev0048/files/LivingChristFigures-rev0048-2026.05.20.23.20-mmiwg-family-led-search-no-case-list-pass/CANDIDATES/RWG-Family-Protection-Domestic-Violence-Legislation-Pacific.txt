NAME: Regional Working Group on the Implementation of Family Protection / Domestic Violence Legislation (RWG)
LOCATION: Pacific regional; 6th annual meeting held in Aiwo, Nauru
OFFICE: Regional law-to-practice backbone for domestic-violence / family-protection legislation
STATUS: OFFICE-CANON / FRAMEWORK-CANDIDATE; LAW-TO-DOOR BACKBONE; RESOURCE-GAP AND DATA-SYSTEM FLAGS

SUMMARY:
The 6th Annual Meeting of the Regional Working Group on the Implementation of Family Protection / Domestic Violence Legislation was held in Nauru, 11–13 August 2025. The outcomes document says the meeting aimed to strengthen connections to regional frameworks, meetings, and tracking implementation of family-protection/domestic-violence legislation; reflect on the final evaluation of the previous three-year project; identify priority areas; and set steps for the coming year. The group agreed to accelerate coordination and multi-sectoral partnership, work toward TFGBV and illicit-drugs/DV subcommittees, address resourcing challenges, integrate lessons from evaluation, strengthen data systems, and launch the Regional GBV Counselling Framework in February 2026.

WHY THIS FITS THE CUBE:
The RWG is not a survivor-facing door. It is the regional hinge asking whether the law becomes practice: data, coordination, technical assistance, resourcing, legal/institutional strengthening, and counselling standards. It belongs as a framework candidate because rev0020 is about law-to-door work.

OFFICE SHAPE:
- Regional family-protection / domestic-violence legislation implementation forum.
- Technical assistance and Secretariat coordination.
- Data-systems and monitoring/evaluation push.
- Regional counselling framework launch commitment.
- Multi-sectoral partnership and resource mobilisation.

EVIDENCE:
1. 2025 RWG outcomes document: 6th annual meeting in Aiwo, Nauru; participants from Pacific governments/states; Nauru chair and Solomon Islands co-chair.
2. Same document: objectives include tracking implementation, final-evaluation reflection, priority areas, recommendations, and alignment with human-rights treaties and Pacific gender-equality developments.
3. Same document: agreements on multi-sectoral partnership, data systems, resourcing, subcommittees, and the Regional GBV Counselling Framework launch.

DOOR-ETHICS FLAGS:
- Regional framework language can hide whether survivors can access help locally.
- Data systems must protect survivor privacy and not centralise risk.
- Faith-based, police, community, and private-sector partnerships require survivor-choice and confidentiality checks.
- Resource mobilisation must not become proof of implementation.
