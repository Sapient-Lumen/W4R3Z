OFFICE: Legal-capacity review as a map between violence, law, and survivor justice
STATUS: FRAMEWORK-CANDIDATE; JUSTICE-ACCESS MAP; IMPLEMENTATION-GAP EVIDENCE; NOT LEGAL ADVICE / NOT SERVICE CAPACITY

SUMMARY:
UNDP’s June 2025 “Justice Pathways” publication offers a review of legislation and broader legal capacities to respond to violence against women and children across Palau, RMI, and FSM. The UNDP page describes it as a review of legislation and broader legal capacities; the PDF itself frames the work as foundation for future research and deeper analysis rather than legal advice. This is admitted as a map/reference candidate, not as a service and not as an endorsement of any one legal route.

WHY THIS FITS THE CUBE:
The rev0020 cluster needs a guardrail against treating laws as doors. A legal-capacity review can show what pathways exist and where gaps remain; it also reminds the cube to keep legal remedy, cultural response, service access, and survivor safety distinct.

OFFICE SHAPE:
- Legal-capacity review across FSM, Palau, and RMI.
- Violence-against-women-and-children response mapping.
- Cross-jurisdiction comparison.
- Future dialogue / deeper analysis rather than direct service.

EVIDENCE:
1. UNDP publication page: June 18, 2025 report reviews legislation and broader legal capacities to respond effectively to VAWC across Palau, RMI, and FSM.
2. UNDP page categorises the report under gender equality, sexual and gender-based violence, Goal 5, and Goal 16.
3. Justice Pathways PDF/search text indicates the report is not legal advice and should be critically engaged because legal/contextual information evolves.

DOOR-ETHICS FLAGS:
- Legal maps can help but cannot replace local legal advice, counsel, shelter, healthcare, safety planning, or survivor accompaniment.
- The cube should not recommend pathways; it records office shapes.
- Cross-country reports risk smoothing local differences and customary/community power dynamics.

REV0041 LAW-TO-DOOR UPDATE:
The UNDP Justice Pathways report is promoted only as legal-capacity, implementation-gap, and no-service-capacity boundary evidence. The UNDP publication page describes a June 2025 review of legislation and broader legal capacities to respond to violence against women and children across FSM, Palau, and RMI. The PDF records protective-order frameworks and strengths, but also implementation challenges, survivor barriers to legal remedies, limited public awareness, resource/training constraints, uneven state coverage in FSM, and survivor-consent risk surfaces in No Drop approaches.

REV0041 PUBLIC BOUNDARY:
A justice-pathways report is not counsel. It may shape internal understanding of law-to-door gaps, but it must not be used as legal advice, a route to court, a service-capacity statement, a referral directory, a guarantee of enforcement, or proof that survivor autonomy is protected.
