REFRESH NOTE — rev0021
SUBJECT: Nauru compulsory-counselling / court-reported counselling — verified and sharpened

rev0020 raised this as a flagged risk and asked future search for "the exact
legal basis, court practice, survivor-rights safeguards, alternatives to joint
counselling, and whether counselling can be trauma-informed, separate,
voluntary, and safety-led."

rev0021 verification outcome:
A primary judicial-training source (Nauru Bench Book, chapter on the Domestic
Violence and Family Protection Act 2017) confirms a statutory counselling
mechanism in which the counsellor reports to the court on a fortnightly basis
or as the court otherwise directs (s 27), with counsellors registered and
listed by the Director of Women (s 28). Counselling whose reports flow to the
court, in the context of protection-order proceedings, is structurally the
forum-for-pressure risk rule 17 names.

Status change:
- Before: suspected coercion/reconciliation risk (flagged).
- After: documented structural risk from primary legal/judicial text.
- Still open: whether, in practice, this pressures survivors to reconcile or
  stay silent; whether separate, voluntary, trauma-informed, safety-led
  counselling is available as an alternative; survivor-side evidence; current
  service capacity. No survivor-reported source was found in this pass.

Do not over-read this:
This is a confirmation about legal/court structure, not a survivor testimony
and not a finding that Nauru's office should be removed. The cube admitted
Nauru deliberately, as a law-to-door line WITH this caution attached. The
caution is now better evidenced, not resolved. The entry stays, the flag
hardens, the safety question remains open.

Carry-forward:
- survivor-side / independent-evaluation evidence on counselling practice;
- existence and accessibility of separate (non-joint), voluntary counselling;
- enforcement of protection orders in a small family-network context;
- legal aid, safe accommodation adequacy, transport, privacy, retaliation
  safeguards.
