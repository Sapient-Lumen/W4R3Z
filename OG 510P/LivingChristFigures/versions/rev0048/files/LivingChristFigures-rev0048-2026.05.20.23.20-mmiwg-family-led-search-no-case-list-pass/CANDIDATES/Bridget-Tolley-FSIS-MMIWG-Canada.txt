Bridget Tolley / Families of Sisters in Spirit (FSIS) — and the broader Indigenous-led MMIWG2S+ search and memorial office

Status: KEEP WITH FAMILY-OF-VICTIM AND POLITICAL-CONTROVERSY FLAGS; OFFICE-CANON FOR INDIGENOUS-LED SEARCH AND WITNESS; REVERIFIED-BOUNDARY-rev0048
Location: Canada (FSIS based in Ottawa, translocal in scope); office is continental across Canada, the United States, and beyond
Office: Search-for-the-disappeared / annual vigil / indigenous-led anti-erasure for missing and murdered Indigenous women, girls, and two-spirit people

Work:
Bridget Tolley, an Algonquin grandmother, co-founded Families of Sisters in Spirit in 2011 after her own mother Gladys Tolley was killed by a Sûreté du Québec police cruiser in 2001 in a death she and her family contest as inadequately investigated. FSIS organises an annual October 4 vigil on Parliament Hill and supports families through search, identification, inquest, and media work that an earlier generation of Indigenous women — including the Native Women's Association of Canada's Sisters in Spirit Campaign and Beverley Jacobs's 2004 Stolen Sisters report — opened. Related, distinct work includes Jaime Black's REDress Project (2010) and Red Dress Day (May 5), the Native Women's Association of Canada, and US-side organisations like the National Indigenous Women's Resource Center.

Why it belongs / might belong:
The cube's existing distress-call witness and migrant-grave offices stop where Indigenous-led search begins. This office is search and witness for women whose disappearance falls inside settler-colonial states that have systematically declined to look. The threshold is the moment the family realises that "missing" will not become "actively sought" without their own labour. The office is held open by people who have themselves been told to wait, go home, and stop asking.

Flags:
Many founders and organisers are family-of-victim; the office cannot be cleanly separated from grief, and the cube does not romanticise grief as virtue. There is real political controversy: state institutions have responded with apologies, inquiries, and the 2019 Reclaiming Power and Place national-inquiry report with 231 Calls for Justice, while Indigenous-led groups continue to point to slow implementation and to argue the term "genocide" applies. The cube follows the framing of Indigenous-led organisations rather than imposing external categories — including in how the dead are named. Bridget Tolley has spent two decades insisting publicly on her mother Gladys Tolley's name and circumstances of death (struck by a Sûreté du Québec police cruiser in 2001); the cube treats the naming her family has chosen as binding rather than overriding it with caution she did not ask for.

Rev0048 boundary addendum:
Current and recent sources strengthen the office but also sharpen the extraction danger. The National Inquiry page supports the national truths/Calls for Justice frame; CIRNAC's reporting tool separates actioned work from completion; AFN's 2025 progress press release preserves Indigenous counterpressure around underfunded, inconsistent, family-excluding responses; and Amnesty's Bridget Tolley interview supports family-led witness without turning every family story into reusable public material. The cube now treats MMIWG2S+ search, vigil, data, red-dress, progress-dashboard, and support-surface sources as no-case-list/no-vigil-map materials unless separately reviewed.
