NAME: Palau Family Protection Act Working Group / inter-ministerial GBV first-response line
LOCATION: Republic of Palau; Koror / national first-response system
OFFICE: Law-to-door GBV response: health, police, justice, statistics, and case-evidence integrity
STATUS: PROVISIONAL KEEP; LAW-TO-DOOR PARTIAL; HEALTH-FIRST-RESPONDER AND EVIDENCE-CHAIN FLAGS; PROTECTION ORDER IS NOT SERVICE CAPACITY; CURRENT CAPACITY UNVERIFIED

SUMMARY:
Palau’s entry is not a shelter entry. It is an implementation-and-first-response threshold: an amended inter-ministerial Memorandum of Understanding under the Family Protection Act, health-sector GBV training for clinicians, nurses and case managers, front-line data/evidence support, and a whole-of-government / whole-of-nation claim that GBV response cannot be solved by one ministry. UNFPA reported that the 2023 forum included the Ministry of Health and Human Services, Ministry of Justice, Police, Ministry of Education, parliament, a governor, cultural and religious leaders, and civil-society organisations. It also reported training for 21 clinicians/nurses/case managers and front-line data/evidence equipment such as tablets, file cabinets, and sexual-assault evidence collection supplies.

WHY THIS FITS THE CUBE:
The threshold here is the moment a survivor reaches an emergency room, police officer, case manager, statistician, or legal file and is not lost through silence, shame, poor evidence handling, or ministry handoff. The cube admits this as a law-to-door implementation candidate, not as proof of comprehensive survivor access.

OFFICE SHAPE:
- Family Protection Act implementation working line.
- Inter-ministerial GBV Memorandum of Understanding.
- Health-sector GBV first-response training.
- Evidence/file integrity support for first responders.
- Whole-of-nation / multi-sector coordination under a law-to-practice frame.

EVIDENCE:
1. UNFPA / Spotlight Initiative 2023: Palau endorsed an amended inter-ministerial MoU specifying division of labour for Family Protection Act implementation.
2. Same source: 21 clinicians, nurses, and case managers received health-sector GBV response training; eight front-line workers received violence-against-women data collection and analysis induction.
3. Same source: UNFPA provided equipment to support case-file/evidence integrity for law enforcement and health workers.
4. UNDP 2025 Justice Pathways report page: review of legislation and broader legal capacities to respond to VAWC across Palau, RMI, and FSM.

DOOR-ETHICS FLAGS:
- Law, MoU, training, and evidence equipment are not the same as a reachable survivor door.
- Health-sector first response must protect confidentiality, consent, survivor choice, and non-retaliation.
- Data collection must not become surveillance of survivors or exposure of case details.
- Cultural and religious leaders in the room require precondition checks: no pressure to reconcile, remain silent, or protect family reputation.

REV0041 LAW-TO-DOOR UPDATE:
The UNDP Justice Pathways page and PDF support Palau only as a legal-capacity / implementation-gap entry. The PDF describes Palau's Family Protection Act framework, protective-order mechanisms, and integrated legal approach, while also emphasizing implementation challenges, survivor barriers to legal remedies, public-awareness gaps, and law-enforcement training/resource constraints. The report's No Drop discussion is treated as a survivor-autonomy caution, not as proof of survivor choice or safety.

REV0041 PUBLIC BOUNDARY:
Do not convert Palau law/MoU/training/evidence-chain material into a crisis-service directory, legal advice, or current-capacity claim. No public prose may describe filing steps, imply protection-order enforcement, treat No Drop as survivor consent, expose evidence-chain details, or use whole-of-nation coordination as proof that a survivor can safely reach help now.
