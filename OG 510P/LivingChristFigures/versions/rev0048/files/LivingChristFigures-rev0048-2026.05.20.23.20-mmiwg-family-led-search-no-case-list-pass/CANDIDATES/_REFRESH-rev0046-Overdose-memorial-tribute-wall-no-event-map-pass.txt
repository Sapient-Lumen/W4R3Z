# rev0046 — Overdose memorial / tribute-wall no-event-map pass

CENTRAL SENTENCE: A tribute wall can remember a person without making the cube a second overdose record.

## Why this pass exists

The cube already had an overdose-memorial office through Sally J. Finn / International Overdose Awareness Day, but the source ecology has become more visibly extractive. Current IOAD surfaces include campaign-shape material, tribute-wall material, event registration and event-map material, and public participation paths. The positive office is real: overdose death should not become a moral verdict. The new boundary is also real: grief, stigma reduction, and prevention campaigning do not authorize the cube to reproduce names, photos, family tributes, death details, local event logistics, or service/training paths.

## Source decisions

- IOAD About the Campaign: existing source row updated for current campaign shape only. It supports August 31 remembrance/prevention framing, the founding/stewardship history and anniversary/global-scale description. It does not release tribute, event-map, organizer, training, capacity, or referral wording.
- IOAD Tributes page: held as redaction-risk intake. It proves that tribute-wall memory exists, but the cube must not copy names, relationships, stories, exact dates, cause/drug details, or family language.
- IOAD Events 2026 page: held as event-map and contact-surface intake. It shows event registration/map structure, but the cube must not copy event names, locations, times, organizer contacts, training/naloxone access details, or reader-directed attendance instructions.
- Penington Institute 2025 Annual Overdose Report: promoted only for aggregate mortality and prevention-pressure context.
- NIDA Words Matter: promoted only as stigma/person-first language boundary context.
- DEA Faces of Fentanyl page: held as government memorial caution. A public memorial can solicit photos/stories and claim broad reuse rights without making those materials safe for cube extraction.

## Blocked by rule 44

- tribute-wall names, relationships, family text, dates, biographies, photographs, and cause/drug details;
- event names, addresses, times, map markers, landmark details, organizer contacts, registration paths, and attendance instructions;
- memorial-exhibit submissions, photos, story text, and broad reuse/license claims treated as cube permission;
- naloxone/training/supply access paths, current service capacity, referral wording, medical advice, and war-on-drugs framing used as memorial proof.
