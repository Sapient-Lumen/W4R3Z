Tuenjai Deetes / Hill Area Development Foundation (HADF)

Status: PERSON-BENCHMARK / OFFICE-CANON WITH RECOGNITION AND STATE-PROXIMITY FLAGS
Location: Chiang Rai Province, northern Thailand
Office: Civil-identity threshold / stateless-to-named-citizen / paperwork as anti-erasure

Work:
Co-founded HADF in 1986 in Paka Sukjai village; from 1973 has worked with Akha, Lahu, Lisu, and other hill-tribe peoples on Thai-language education, sustainable agriculture, and the slow paperwork of birth registration, civil status, and citizenship application. Helped enact the 2008 Civil Registration Act amendment and a 2005 Cabinet Resolution that opened naturalisation paths for six groups; over 90,000 formerly stateless people in Thailand have acquired nationality since 2008. As of late 2025 work continues for elderly stateless residents (over 110,000) who have lived in Thailand more than 40 years and remain without ID.

Why it belongs / might belong:
The office is the opposite end of the cube's existing migrant-grave and name-after-death work: civil identity during life. Without registration, a child cannot enter school, see a hospital, open a bank account, marry, travel, or own property. The threshold is paperwork — boring, repetitive, decades-long — that determines whether a person ever becomes legally visible to any other system. This is "name-before-life" as severe an office as "name-after-death."

Flags:
Tuenjai is a former senator (Chiang Rai, 2000), former National Legislative Assembly member (2006), former National Human Rights Commissioner (2015–2019, resigned 2019 citing restrictions on the body's investigative powers — an integrity marker), and 2018 UNHCR Nansen Refugee Award Asia regional finalist. She is well recognised, royal-patronage framing appears in older sources, and her work has at times been within state legislative machinery. Not a fit for "unknown" canon. The office itself, however, is too important to omit. Note also that hill-tribe communities are not monolithic; HADF's framing of statelessness as primarily a documentation problem has been productive but is not the only frame indigenous communities use.
