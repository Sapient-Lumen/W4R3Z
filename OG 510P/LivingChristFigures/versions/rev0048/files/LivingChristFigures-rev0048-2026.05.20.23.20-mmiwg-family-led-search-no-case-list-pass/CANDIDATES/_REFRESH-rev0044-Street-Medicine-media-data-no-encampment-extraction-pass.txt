# rev0044 — Street medicine media/data no-encampment-extraction pass

CENTRAL SENTENCE: A house call to the street is not permission to map the street, film the patient, or train a system on their exposure.

## Why this pass exists

Rev0028 admitted street medicine with an outreach-is-not-housing boundary. Rev0044 returns because the live evidence environment has become more data-heavy: field maps, media images, AI-assisted intake, law-enforcement coordination, complaint systems, encampment identification, and public contact pages can make care easier to locate while making unsheltered people easier to expose.

## Source decisions

- Street Medicine Institute home page: already registered and re-reviewed for high-level field-building only. Its public map and membership/contact surfaces remain non-extractive.
- Pittsburgh Mercy Operation Safety Net current street-medicine/outreach page: already registered and re-reviewed as contact-rich current service description. It may support internal service-shape review but remains blocked from public contact, referral, team-composition, route, and capacity wording.
- Street Medicine Institute media guidance: promoted as a media-dignity boundary source. It supports the rule that patient trust, priorities, and dignity come before publicity; it does not release images, scenes, stories, or consent details.
- CalMatters 2026 AI/street-medicine reporting: promoted as clinical-AI/data-risk boundary evidence only. It supports privacy, bias, patient-outcome, and governance caution around AI-assisted street-medicine intake; it does not prove safety, harm, capacity, or suitability for any particular program.
- National Alliance to End Homelessness 2025 law-enforcement outreach page: promoted as law-enforcement/privacy boundary evidence. It supports the need to navigate privacy, safety of personal information, and harms of law-enforcement encounters; it does not prove that any local team has safe data-sharing practice.
- Guardian reporting on San Jose AI encampment detection: promoted as adjacent encampment-surveillance boundary evidence. It supports the no-map/no-object-detection caution; it does not describe Operation Safety Net or SMI practice.

## Public claim release

None. Rev0044 releases no public claim, contact field, referral instruction, clinical instruction, patient story, image, route, schedule, camp location, geodata, AI triage detail, law-enforcement handoff detail, or current-capacity wording.

## Blocked by rule 42

- patient names, faces, stories, images, clinical details, consent scenes, and identifiable encounters;
- sleeping locations, camp names, routes, outreach schedules, staff patterns, and geocoded or complaint-derived data;
- by-name lists, AI prompts or outputs, triage scripts, diagnosis suggestions, model-performance detail tied to unsheltered patients, and patient-data flows;
- law-enforcement handoff details, patrol/co-response routing, sweeps-adjacent intelligence, and encampment-object-detection logic;
- contact fields, live referral instructions, service-capacity claims, hospital-navigation instructions, and medical advice.
