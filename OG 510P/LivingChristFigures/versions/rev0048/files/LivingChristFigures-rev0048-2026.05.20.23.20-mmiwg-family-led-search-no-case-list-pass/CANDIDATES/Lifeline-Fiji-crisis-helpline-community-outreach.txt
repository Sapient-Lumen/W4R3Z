Lifeline Fiji — crisis helpline, community crisis support, and suicide-prevention outreach

Status: PROVISIONAL KEEP; DIRECT ISLAND-COUNTRY PACIFIC FIND; INSTITUTION-CANDIDATE; KEEP WITH CLINICAL-SAFETY, HELPLINE-CAPACITY, AND SAFE-MESSAGING FLAGS
Location: Fiji
Office: Island crisis-line and community outreach / phone, text, face-to-face listening, and psychological first aid before a person is alone with suicidal distress

Work:
Lifeline Fiji describes itself as a national charity providing Fijians in emotional distress with 24-hour crisis support and suicide-prevention services. Its service modes include phone, text messaging, online, face-to-face counselling, outreach, community-based crisis support / psychological first aid, public emotional-wellbeing work, and training in community, faith-based, and corporate sectors. Its contact page lists the 1543 suicide-prevention helpline and adjacent child, domestic-violence, grief-and-loss, and other helplines. The organisation also says its crisis intervention workers are volunteers and reports earlier call/assistance figures, including 2016, 2017, and 2018 data.

Why it belongs / might belong:
Rev0015 left a gap for direct island-country Pacific suicide-prevention/postvention offices outside Aotearoa diaspora and fund-mediated Pasifika work. Lifeline Fiji is a cleaner island-country entry. The office is not spectacle and not a campaign slogan; it is the phone answered before the night closes. The threshold is the interval between distress and death, and also the community-training layer that tries to make villages, churches, schools, and families less silent.

Flags:
Because this is suicide-prevention work, the cube must keep method details out and write according to safe-messaging practice. A helpline can be load-bearing and still fragile: future revisions should look for independent audit, call-volume currency, coverage outside Viti Levu, language access, referral capacity, volunteer support, and safeguarding. The cube should not treat the existence of a hotline as proof that crisis care is reachable equally by all Fijians.
