The street-medicine house-call-to-the-unsheltered office moves the clinic threshold to the person outside; its danger is that outreach can become surveillance, medicalized poverty management, or a substitute for housing.

This office begins when the clinic door is in the wrong place. The person is sick outside the systems that are nominally for them; the office crosses the institutional threshold instead of requiring the patient to cross first.

The office is care where the person is, not permission to turn where they are into a permanent acceptable condition. It must keep four things separate: clinical attention, patient consent, encampment knowledge, and housing justice.
