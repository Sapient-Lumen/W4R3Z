The unclaimed-child-burial-and-naming office answers the threshold after a child dies without a claimant able to carry burial, name, and public memory; its danger is that posthumous dignity can become posthumous ownership.

This office is close to name-after-death, but not identical. The child is not merely unnamed; the child may be unclaimed, unidentified, stillborn, abandoned, or caught in a public custody / medical examiner / cemetery gap. The office holds grave, name, ritual, and witness together. It must also keep four categories separate: who the child was, who had legal authority, who knew or did not know, and who now tells the story.
