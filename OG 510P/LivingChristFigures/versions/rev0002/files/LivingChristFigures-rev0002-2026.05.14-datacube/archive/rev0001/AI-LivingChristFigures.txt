WHAT THIS MEANS

The point of this cube is not hero worship. Hero worship is often a trick: it lets the spectator enjoy goodness without being claimed by it. The better question is: what pattern of life makes goodness visible even to a tired, suspicious mind?

In rev0001, the answer is presence.

The people kept here are not primarily miracle workers. They do not defeat death, poverty, disability, or abandonment in the heroic-movie sense. Their holiness, if the word may be used carefully, is that they stay where most people flee. Mohamed Bzeek cannot make terminal children live; he can make sure they die as sons and daughters. P. U. Thomas cannot cure the whole wound of poverty; he can feed the next line of patients. Desai Linggi cannot undo childhood abandonment; she can become a durable mothering presence. M. A. Hussain cannot prevent every disappearance; he can search and reunite. Anil Joseph cannot make disability socially convenient; he can make a home where the inconvenient are wanted.

That is a severe hope. It is better than optimism.

Optimism says the world is improving. Hope says that even where the world is not improving, love can still take a body, a schedule, a set of keys, a kitchen, a medicine bag, a phone call to police, a bed, a name, a burial, a hand.

A "living Christ figure" in literary terms is not someone impressive. It is someone whose life discloses the scandal that the least-valued body may be the center of meaning. This is why fame is dangerous here. The famous person can become the center. The needed reversal is that the abandoned child, the wandering patient, the dying foster child, the destitute elder, the mentally ill stranger, the disabled child become the center.

THE CORE INHERITANCE

A civilization inherits what it can still recognize as holy. If all it can recognize is success, then successful people will impersonate virtue. The corrective is to search for lives where service remains service even when no one important is watching. The best lives found here make a claim on the reader: goodness is not extinct; it is usually local, tired, administratively difficult, underfunded, and stubborn.

The datacube begins with the insistence that humans can be virtuous. Not pure. Not flawless. Virtuous: repeatedly formed toward the good under pressure.
