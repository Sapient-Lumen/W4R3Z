OFFICE CARD: GBV SAFENET REFERRAL MESH
REVISION ADDED: rev0019

SHAPE:
A survivor has to cross several thresholds: police, clinic, shelter, legal aid, social welfare, transport, counsellor, family pressure, court. A SafeNet or referral mesh coordinates those doors so the survivor is not abandoned between agencies.

CANDIDATES / LINEAGE:
- Kiribati SafeNet / OLSH crisis-centre line (rev0019).
- Chuuk APIMAR SafeNet linked to TICC / Chuuk Women’s Council (rev0019).
- Solomon Islands SAFENET linked to FSC and Christian Care Centre (rev0018).

WHY IT IS A CHRIST-FIGURE OFFICE:
Mercy here is routing without ownership. The office keeps the person from falling through the spaces between institutions.

DOOR-ETHICS QUESTIONS:
1. Does the network protect survivor choice, consent, and confidentiality at each referral?
2. Are police, faith-based, legal, medical, and NGO actors clearly distinguished?
3. Is there a real handoff, or only a list of agencies?
4. What happens for outer-island, disabled, undocumented, queer, child, or language-minority survivors?
5. Can the survivor refuse a referral without losing support?

IMAGE / LANGUAGE RULE:
No operational maps, private phone trees, or staff rosters. Use only public institutional shape.


REV0020 APPEND:
Law-to-door entries in Palau, Nauru, and FSM/Pohnpei-Kosrae show a nearby but distinct mesh: a legal-protection/referral mesh. Keep legal orders, police duties, health files, counselling, safe accommodation, and advocacy separate enough to ask where the survivor can consent or refuse.
