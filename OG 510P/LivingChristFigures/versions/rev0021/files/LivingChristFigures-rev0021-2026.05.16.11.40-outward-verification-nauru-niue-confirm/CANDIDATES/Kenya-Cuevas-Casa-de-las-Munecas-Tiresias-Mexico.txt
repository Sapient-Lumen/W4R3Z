Kenya Cuevas / Casa de las Muñecas Tiresias — Casa Hogar Paola Buenrostro (CDMX), Cuernavaca, and Apaxco shelters — with parallel offices Casa Frida (CDMX/Tapachula/Monterrey, founded 2020) and Casa Arcoíris (Tijuana)

Status: KEEP WITH FAMILY-OF-VICTIM, RECOGNITION, AND CULT-OF-PERSONALITY FLAGS; OFFICE-CANON FOR CHOSEN-FAMILY-FOR-TRANS-AND-QUEER-ADULTS
Location: Mexico — Casa Hogar Paola Buenrostro in CDMX (opened January 2020); second house Catherinne Danielle Márquez in Cuernavaca, Morelos (opened February 2022); third house Kaory Catarero Regalado in Apaxco, Estado de México (opened 2023). Parallel: Casa Frida (CDMX, Tapachula, Monterrey).
Office: Chosen-family-for-trans-and-queer-adults / shelter, sober living, prison reentry, and legal/identity advocacy for trans women, gay men, and queer migrants who survive the rejection of biological family and the violence of street-based sex work

Work:
On 30 September 2016 Paola Buenrostro, a trans woman engaged in sex work in CDMX's La Merced district, was murdered in front of her best friend Kenya Cuevas, also a trans woman and sex worker. Cuevas — who had lived through prison, addiction, HIV diagnosis, and the deportation of her partner — turned that night into the founding event of Casa de las Muñecas Tiresias. Casa Hogar Paola Buenrostro opened on 18 January 2020 as the first dedicated shelter for trans women in Mexico, taking in trans women left without housing as COVID-era hotel closures ended sex-work survival income. The network has since opened Casa Hogar Catherinne Danielle Márquez (Cuernavaca, February 2022) and Casa Hogar Kaory Catarero Regalado (Apaxco, 2023). The organisation enters Mexican prisons for legal-literacy workshops for trans women, picks them up on release, and integrates them into the shelter. Casa de las Muñecas served roughly 1,800 people in its first year (2018, pre-shelter work) and around 10,000 by 2020. Parallel offices recorded here for context: Casa Frida (CDMX, founded May 2020 by Lizbeth and Ian, with sites in Tapachula and Monterrey), serving 423 LGBTIQ+ refugees and 349 reintegrated persons by early 2024; Casa Arcoíris in Tijuana for LGBT+ migrants from Central and South America.

Why it belongs / might belong:
The rev0012 SearchTrail asked for trans chosen-family work that doesn't require naming minors. Casa Hogar Paola Buenrostro and its sister houses are exactly the threshold offices that fit: adults, named as their chosen identities, reconnected with peers and into a "doll family" (the muñecas of the organisation's name) when biological families and the Mexican state have failed them. The threshold is the moment the trans woman is released from prison, evicted from a hotel, or arrives in CDMX after fleeing a homicidal home country, and discovers that someone has built a place for her.

Flags:
The trans-feminicide rate in Mexico is the world's worst in absolute numbers; Latin America accounts for ~70% of global registered trans murders, with Mexico, Central America, and Brazil dominating the count. Kenya Cuevas has received significant national and international recognition, including a 2022 Robert F. Kennedy Human Rights Award; she is publicly visible in a way the cube's "unknown canon" preference does not match, but the office is too important to omit. Cuevas's leadership has at times been described by observers as personalistic; the cube notes that any successor question for the network is not yet resolved. The shelters are partly funded through CDMX government programmes and corporate donations; this is the standard state-proximity DOOR-ETHICS question. Mixed user reviews of Casa Frida (Google business listings; QList) note administrative friction in some cases — the cube records this without weighting it. The chosen-family names the network itself has built into its house names — Paola Buenrostro (CDMX, murdered September 2016), Catherinne Danielle Márquez (Cuernavaca house, opened February 2022), Kaory Catarero Regalado (Apaxco house, 2023) — are how the network insists its dead be remembered, and the cube uses those names accordingly.
