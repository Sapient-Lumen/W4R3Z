Transcend Australia — family support for trans, gender-diverse, and non-binary children and young people

Status: PROVISIONAL KEEP; NON-US PARENT/FAMILY-REORIENTATION FIND; NETWORK-CANDIDATE; KEEP WITH ADVOCACY-VISIBILITY, CHILD-IMAGE, AND POLITICISED-CARE FLAGS
Location: Australia, national
Office: Parent and family reorientation after a child comes out / peer support, evidence-based resources, and social connection so the child does not have to become the parent’s teacher alone

Work:
Transcend Australia presents itself as a community-led national charity working with families, parents, and caregivers of trans, gender-diverse, non-binary, and gender-questioning children, adolescents, and young people. Its history page says it was founded in 2012 by Rebekah Robertson OAM, mother of Georgie Stone OAM, and became Australia’s first national family support network of its kind. The organisation says it has supported thousands of families through peer support, evidence-based resources, social connection, programs, and services. In May 2026 it announced funding for a dedicated program for fathers and father-figures of trans, gender-diverse, and non-binary children and young people.

Why it belongs / might belong:
Rev0015 added Mama Dragons / Mama Bears but noted the gap outside US Christian/Mormon reorientation frames. Transcend is a cleaner non-US case. The office is the parent learning not to make the child carry the whole burden of explanation. The child’s threshold is not only medical, legal, or social; it is the family kitchen, the school meeting, the father who needs a place to become safe without demanding that the child wait forever.

Flags:
This office is advocacy-visible and operates inside a highly politicised field. The cube should not turn trans children into proof-texts for adult moral transformation, nor should it flatten clinical disagreements or legal disputes into a simple inspirational story. Images and named young people need the strongest DOOR-ETHICS reading. This candidate also requires careful distinction between parent-support work and gatekeeping: support that helps a parent affirm a child is different from a parent becoming a new authority over the child’s self-knowledge.
