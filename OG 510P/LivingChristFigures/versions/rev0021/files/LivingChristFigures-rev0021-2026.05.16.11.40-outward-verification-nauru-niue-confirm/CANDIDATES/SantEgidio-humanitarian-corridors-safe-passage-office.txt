Community of Sant’Egidio / FCEI / Tavola Valdese / ARCI — humanitarian corridors and safe-passage hosting

Status: OFFICE-CANON; INSTITUTION-CANDIDATE; KEEP WITH HIGH-VISIBILITY, FAITH-BASED, SELECTION, AND STATE-MOU FLAGS
Location: Italy and wider Europe; corridors from Lebanon, Libya, Afghanistan/Iran/Pakistan, the Horn of Africa, Greece, Gaza medical evacuation contexts, and other routes depending on protocol
Office: Humanitarian corridor / legal safe passage, visa, arrival welcome, housing, language, school, medical, and integration threshold

Work:
The Community of Sant’Egidio describes humanitarian corridors as self-financed projects developed with the Federation of Evangelical Churches in Italy, the Tavola Valdese, and agreements with the Italian government to identify vulnerable refugees, facilitate humanitarian visas with limited territorial validity, arrange legal and safe entry, and support reception and integration after arrival. Its May 2026 materials say more than 8,592 people had safely arrived since February 2016 through the broader corridors page, and a 15 May 2026 news item says 131 refugees evacuated from Libya arrived at Rome Fiumicino on 14 May through an initiative promoted by Sant’Egidio, FCEI, and ARCI in collaboration with UNHCR and Italian ministries; that same item says humanitarian corridors have helped more than 10,000 people reach Europe safely. Sant’Egidio also reported in August 2025 on Gaza medical-evacuation hospitality and family reunification support.

Why it belongs / might belong:
This completes the rev0015 scout idea on Sant’Egidio and connects to the same gap as private sponsorship. The office is a legal route where the ordinary death route has become normalised: list-making, visas, plane tickets, houses, language classes, school enrolment, hospital reception, and the slow work of becoming local. It is the opposite of the boat as destiny.

Flags:
This is high-visibility institutional mercy, not unknown work. It is faith-based, ecumenical, state-MOU-bound, and selection-dependent. A corridor can save lives while leaving many people outside the list, and the cube must not let successful arrivals hide the politics of who qualifies, who waits, who is refused, and who remains at sea or in detention. The evidence is organisation-forward, so future passes should add refugee-led and independent evaluations.

REV0017 REFRESH — SELECTION ETHICS:
Keep the candidate, but do not let official corridor language stand alone. A 2025 Sage article identifies selection of beneficiaries, personalization of worthiness, volunteer professionalism, paternalism, and power relations between host groups and refugees as central limits. Humanitarian corridors remain a safe-passage office only when the unselected, the refusing, and the hosted person who resists possession remain visible.
