NAME: Nauru Domestic Violence and Family Protection Act / Family Protection Coordination Committee / safety-order threshold
LOCATION: Republic of Nauru; national court, police, WASDA / service-provider line
OFFICE: Protection-order and safety-order door under scarce services
STATUS: PROVISIONAL KEEP; LAW-TO-DOOR IMPLEMENTATION ENTRY; RECONCILIATION / COMPULSORY-COUNSELLING CAUTION

SUMMARY:
Nauru’s Domestic Violence and Family Protection Act came into force in 2017 and established a national Family Protection Coordination Committee. Public judicial guidance describes safety orders through the police, protection-order applications through court, urgent court hearing and service by police, and possible application in person, by telephone, in writing, or electronically. DFAT’s 2017 account says the Act followed the Child Protection and Welfare Act and notes implementation-plan and service-provider training work, with personnel from Child Protection, Women’s Affairs, Safe House, Police Domestic Violence Unit, Education and Health oriented around child-protection case management and mapping of services/referral mechanisms.

WHY THIS FITS THE CUBE:
This is a law-to-door candidate: the survivor’s threshold is not the statute itself but the ability to ask for protection, have it heard urgently, have it served, and be connected to services without being pushed back into danger. The entry is admitted with a major caution: Nauru Judiciary’s public page says counselling is compulsory when a victim seeks a protection order and frames it around repairing relationships and keeping the family together. That language is a door-ethics red flag, not a neutral benefit.

OFFICE SHAPE:
- Domestic Violence and Family Protection Act implementation.
- Family Protection Coordination Committee.
- Police-issued safety orders and court-issued protection orders.
- Service-provider training and referral mapping.
- Safe House / Child Protection / Women’s Affairs / Police Domestic Violence Unit / Education / Health as public institutional line.

EVIDENCE:
1. Nauru Judiciary page: Act and Family Protection Coordination Committee; safety orders through police; protection-order applications via court; urgent hearing and police service; applications can be made by multiple means.
2. DFAT page: Act came into force 1 June 2017; RRRT support; implementation plan and service-provider training underway; orientation included Child Protection, Women’s Affairs, Safe House, Police Domestic Violence Unit, Education and Health.
3. Pacific Data Hub Nauru list: ongoing social-work/counselling qualification capacity and service-review materials remain relevant to the gap between legal framework and available services.

DOOR-ETHICS FLAGS:
- Compulsory counselling and “keep the family together” framing may coerce reconciliation or reduce survivor autonomy.
- A protection order without safe enforcement, transport, shelter, legal aid, income, and confidential support is not enough.
- Do not infer full current Safe House capacity from historical training/orientation source.
- Police response must be treated as both potential protection and potential risk.
