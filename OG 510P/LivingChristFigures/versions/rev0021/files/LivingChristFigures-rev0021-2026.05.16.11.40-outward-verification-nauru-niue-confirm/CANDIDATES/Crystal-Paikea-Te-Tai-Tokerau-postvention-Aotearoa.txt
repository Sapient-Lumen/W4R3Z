Crystal Paikea / Te Tai Tokerau suicide postvention team — and the broader kaupapa Māori postvention office, including the Fusion group (Te Tai Tokerau ki Muriwhenua)

Status: KEEP; OFFICE-CANON FOR INDIGENOUS-LED SUICIDE POSTVENTION; TEAM-CANDIDATE WITH NAMED LEAD
Location: Te Tai Tokerau / Northland, Aotearoa New Zealand; serving a population of about 179,000
Office: Suicide-postvention threshold / whānau-grounded support for those bereaved by whakamomori (suicide) / indigenous reframing of stigma

Work:
Crystal Paikea leads a Health New Zealand Te Whatu Ora team of three providing face-to-face postvention support across Te Tai Tokerau. The team uses tangi (mourning) practice, visits whānau (extended family) as manuhiri (visitors), follows kaupapa Māori method, and often works with families for years rather than weeks. They are explicitly non-clinical: their service exists alongside, and frequently in lieu of, government mental-health services that bereaved whānau have refused or been failed by. Wider companion offices include the Fusion interagency postvention group established in Te Tai Tokerau ki Muriwhenua in 2012 after 19 young people died in the region (case-studied by the Suicide Mortality Review Committee), and the Te Arawa postvention tool developed by Tepora Emery and Candy Cookson Cox following a spate of rangatahi (young people) suicides between 2008 and 2012. The framework draws on the Turamarama Declaration (2017) and the He Tapu te Oranga o ia Tangata Suicide Prevention Action Plan 2019-2024.

Why it belongs / might belong:
The cube has not yet held an office focused on the survivors of a death rather than the dying or the dead. Suicide postvention is distinct: it serves those bereaved by suicide, who are at substantially elevated risk of suicide themselves. The Māori framework also offers something the cube has not held — an indigenous tradition that pre-dates colonial stigma. Pre-colonial Māori conceptualised whakamate (suicide) as the result of whakamomori (overwhelming sadness) and kahupō (spiritual blindness), to be met with aroha (love) rather than shame. The postvention office is not importing a stigma-reduction frame; it is recovering one.

Flags:
The team operates inside Te Whatu Ora, a state health service; this is the inverse of the DOOR-ETHICS state-proximity question raised for Federmesser. Here a state agency hosts indigenous-led work; the office is good in spite of the host, not because of it. Reporting from the Northland youth suicide inquest (Coroner Tania Tetitaha, four weeks of evidence 2024) indicates that clinical mental-health services have repeatedly failed Māori rangatahi, which is part of why the kaupapa Māori team is needed. Suicide is the cube's most sensitive topic for a reason distinct from naming: the cube follows safe-messaging practice on method and detail (per IASP/WHO guidance), and does so as a writing practice rather than as a redaction.
