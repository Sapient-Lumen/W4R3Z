Sudan Emergency Response Rooms (ERRs) / غرف الطوارئ — and the nafir tradition that underlies them

Status: OFFICE-CANON; NETWORK-CANDIDATE, NOT SINGLE-FOUNDER CANON; KEEP WITH WAR-CONTEXT, DONOR-LEVERAGE, AND ANONYMITY FLAGS
Location: Sudan — all 18 states; particularly Khartoum, Omdurman, Darfur, and Gezira; coordinated through neighborhood-level units federated up to state-level coordination bodies
Office: Collapsed-state-neighborhood-kitchen / civilian-led survival threshold during armed conflict and famine

Work:
ERRs are decentralised, volunteer, civilian-led mutual-aid networks that emerged from the Neighbourhood Resistance Committees that helped force the end of the Bashir regime in 2019, mobilised first against COVID-19, and from April 2023 became the de facto humanitarian backbone of a country in civil war between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces. As of late 2024, ERRs had assisted over 11.5 million people; in Khartoum State alone roughly 162 base ERRs coordinate over 4,000 volunteers running community kitchens (takaya), neighbourhood clinics, evacuations, infrastructure repair, refuges for survivors of conflict-related rape, and Women's Response Rooms that adapt the traditional soondooq community-savings model. The whole architecture is rooted in nafir / nafeer — a long-standing Sudanese practice of community mobilisation in crisis. ERRs were nominated for the 2025 Nobel Peace Prize by the Peace Research Institute Oslo and awarded a 2025 Right Livelihood Award.

Why it belongs / might belong:
The cube needed Africa beyond Nigeria/Tanzania/Ghana, MENA, and a threshold office under conditions of state collapse. ERRs are all three. The threshold is from "neighbourhood with infrastructure" to "neighbourhood that the war wants empty" — the office is the kitchen that does not close, the clinic that keeps a generator running, the WhatsApp room (the literal meaning of "room" here) that arranges an evacuation across active front lines. It is the most explicit case the cube has yet held of mercy as a horizontal mutual practice rather than a vertical one — the helper is also being helped.

Flags:
By December 2024 around 160 ERR volunteers had been killed; many more detained, tortured, or "disappeared." Accusations of espionage are routinely used against ERR members by both warring sides. The US government declared a genocide ongoing in Sudan in January 2025; humanitarian description here cannot be politically neutral and the cube should resist the donor-comfort framing of "two equally to blame." ERRs themselves resist external attempts to recast them as a single charismatic organisation; the cube should preserve their decentralised, anonymity-protective character. International recognition (Nobel nomination, Right Livelihood, Mutual Aid Sudan Coalition fundraising) is now substantial — this is no longer unknown work — but the office is too important to omit, and ERRs are likely to outlive any named spokesperson.
