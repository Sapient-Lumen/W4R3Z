South Africa Missing Persons Task Team / TRC Unit / Gallows Exhumation and reburial work

Status: PROVISIONAL KEEP; STATE-INSTITUTION CANDIDATE; KEEP WITH REPARATIONS-DELAY, PROSECUTION-DELAY, AND FAMILY-CLOSURE FLAGS
Location: South Africa; apartheid-era graves, pauper graves, exhumation and reburial sites; linked regional exile-remains work
Office: Return of the disappeared dead / exhumation, identification, handover, reburial, and partial repair after state violence

Work:
South African History Online describes the Missing Persons Task Team as established in 2005 within the National Prosecuting Authority to locate graves of people killed under apartheid, exhume and identify remains, and enable reburial; it reported 138 missing persons uncovered as of April 2018, with the number increasing. In May 2025 the NPA reported that its Missing Persons Task Team, with the Department of Justice and Constitutional Development’s TRC Unit, traced and recovered the remains of anti-apartheid activists Benjamin Malesella Moloise and Abraham Zakhele Mngomezulu from pauper graves in Mamelodi, Pretoria, where they had been buried without family consent after execution for politically motivated offences. Justice Department remarks in 2025 linked this to the Gallows Exhumation Project, launched in March 2016 to exhume the remains of 83 politically executed activists, and stated that 76 executed persons’ remains had been exhumed and handed to families so far.

Why it belongs / might belong:
Rev0015 added Khulumani as unfinished TRC recognition; this adds the grave-return side of the same unfinished mercy. The office is not abstract reconciliation. It is the state, pressured by families and memory workers, admitting that a body hidden in a pauper grave still belongs to kin, name, and ceremony. The threshold is burial after decades of disappearance.

Flags:
This is state-linked work, not grassroots proximity. It sits beside unresolved prosecution delays, reparations disputes, and family litigation over apartheid-era crimes. The cube should not convert reburial ceremonies into closure theatre. The return of remains matters, but it does not settle the justice question. Future searching should connect this more carefully to family-led demands, FHR-supported litigation, and criticism of delayed TRC prosecutions.
