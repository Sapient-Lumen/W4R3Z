# Lojban-v157: following love through a whole argument

This early [compiled draft](files/lojban_love_translation_master_UPDATED_2026-01-26_v157.md) carries a philosophical passage on love through an opening proem, attributed epigraphs, a connected argument and its footnotes. It is useful for seeing the work as a whole: the same proposed vocabulary has to serve definitions, commands, metaphors, moral claims and qualifications, rather than succeed in one isolated sentence.

The file puts its conventions and lexical decisions before the translation. These are a key to the choices being attempted, not a separate dictionary detached from the prose. The main blocks retain citation names and footnote markers, so a reader can follow how the argument uses its quotations and where its qualifications belong.

## Three distinctions to follow

**Loving, lovingness and the concept of love.** The opening lexicon distinguishes `prami`, the predicate, from the proposed abstractions `lo nu prami` (an event or state of loving), `lo ka prami` (lovingness) and `lo si'o prami` (the concept). That distinction matters when the passage moves from someone loving another to saying what love is or personifying love in an epigraph. Read the Joyce block beside these choices: it uses the concept-of-love expression repeatedly, then nests an act of loving under `nelci`, the draft's “like” predicate. The repetition is part of the wordplay, not simply redundant terminology.

**Goodwill, liking and attraction.** The lexicon assigns different expressions to these, and expands goodwill into a desire construction whose content concerns another person. The Aquinas block uses that expanded construction. Later, the main argument explicitly contrasts loving with liking and says attraction is insufficient. Its point is not merely a richer emotional vocabulary: the passage is trying to distinguish a morally directed stance from an affective response that cannot simply be changed at will. The draft's claims about choice and control belong to the philosophical argument being translated; choosing different predicates does not prove them.

**Flourishing and justification.** The lexicon marks its eudaimonia rendering as tentative. In the later main paragraph, `sarcu` and `na banzu` carry the attempted contrast between love's being necessary and its not being sufficient for flourishing. The paragraph then distinguishes flourishing and moral luck from claims about justification. Footnote `eu` continues the difficult comparison involving morally flawed and virtuous agents. Keeping that footnote beside the main paragraph helps prevent “well-lived,” “happy” and “morally justified” from becoming one interchangeable success term. This is the source's proposed philosophical structure, not an independent verdict that its account is established.

## Translation choices beyond word replacement

The [conventions and lexicon](files/lojban_love_translation_master_UPDATED_2026-01-26_v157.md#conventions) preserve the project's bespoke bracket-tokens as parenthetical constructions rather than silently dropping them. Capitalized terms such as The Good, The Right and The Other have a separate list of proposed replacements. This lets a reader ask whether a paraphrase retains the intended technical role rather than assuming a familiar-looking expression settles it.

Metaphor also receives explicit attention. The lexicon distinguishes the emotional-self rendering of “heart” from the organ, and gives paraphrases for ready-to-hand and present-at-hand. The body uses metaphor markers around several difficult images and follows the argument from individual relationships to collective and cosmic claims. The choices are attempts to keep those images functioning within an argument, not evidence that a technical term has acquired a universally accepted Lojban equivalent.

The footnotes are substantive. For example, `tr` first rejects reducing love to trust or particular grounds for respect, then explicitly includes respect within love. Reading only the negations would erase that second claim. The small `af` note concerns care in word choice; the short `b` note preserves a playful “don't hurt me” interruption. Argument and register travel together.

## Where to start

1. Read **Conventions**, **Key lexicon decisions**, and **Canonicalization candidates** at the top of the [master file](files/lojban_love_translation_master_UPDATED_2026-01-26_v157.md).
2. Compare the Aquinas and Joyce epigraphs with that lexicon. They expose, respectively, a definition's proposed structure and the treatment of repeated words.
3. Continue into the main argument after the epigraphs, especially the loving/liking/attraction contrast and the flourishing/justification paragraph.
4. Follow the matching [footnotes](files/lojban_love_translation_master_UPDATED_2026-01-26_v157.md#footnotes-lojban), particularly `tr` and `eu`, before reducing the passage to a short thesis.

This is a continuous working draft, not a grammar-certified final edition. Its `<<ref …>>`, `<<footnotes …>>` and quotation delimiters are retained document markup; they should not all be mistaken for Lojban expressions. Proposed terms and paraphrases remain proposals. The guide explains their role without silently correcting the supplied text.

The [later Lojban rev3471 guide](../Lojban-rev3471/README.md) follows a more localized Aquinas-line audit of otherness, beneficiary and willing. Read this edition for the broader composition, then that one for the narrower revision. The [early Ithkuil guide](../Ithkuil-108/README.md) offers a parallel view through explicit semantic scaffolds; the [collection overview](../../README.md) keeps the two language histories distinct.
