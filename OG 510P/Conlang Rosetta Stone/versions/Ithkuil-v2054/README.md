# Ithkuil-v2054

## Keeping love and violence in the same sentence

This edition follows a small passage through a large amount of translation reasoning. Its subject is the Lacan quotation in which an avowal of love leads through “something more than you—the objet petit a” to mutilation. The project asks how to preserve that disturbing coexistence without turning the sentence into either a denial of love or a straightforward substitution of violence for it.

The [v2054 development pass](files/ithkuil_work/q_lacan_contrastive_scope_alö_final_predicate_cluster_pass_v2054.md) gives the source sentence, current working translation, detailed parse and English control glosses. Read those together: the unfamiliar surface is not the whole work. The explanations show which interpretation each grammatical choice is intended to carry.

## What a change from *alo* to *alö* is meant to do

The latest revision changes one vowel while retaining the same contrastive affix. According to the [contrastive-scope audit](files/ithkuil_work/_cube_lacan_contrastive_scope_audit_v2054.md), *alo* targeted the final maiming predicate alone; *alö* is intended to include its adjacent participant expressions as well. The contrast is thus over “I mutilate you,” not just over the event-kind of mutilation. The speaker as agent and the addressee as harmed patient belong inside the unsettling conclusion.

That is also why the project rejects alternatives meaning “on the contrary” or “instead.” Those would replace or cancel the opening love-avowal. Its chosen “but / nevertheless” keeps both claims active. Nor is contrast treated as a special kind of maiming: the project leaves it outside the predicate, as a relation between the avowal and the final act. Its scope is local to that final cluster, not the whole preceding explanation.

## Naming the “something” without naming the wrong thing

The middle passage poses a different problem. The project keeps *objet petit a* as an unchanged foreign technical phrase, while an Ithkuil host expression supplies its interpreted role. The [carrier audit](files/ithkuil_work/_cube_lacan_carrier_phrase_audit_v2045.md) distinguishes describing that host, naming it and merely mentioning the words of its name.

The [v2052 apposition audit](files/ithkuil_work/_cube_lacan_apposition_scope_audit_v2052.md) then moves the name next to the host, ahead of the comparison standard. Otherwise the nearest expression could suggest that “you,” rather than the surplus-something, was being named *objet petit a*. The repair has a cost: “than you” arrives later, after the foreign phrase's closing marker. The notes preserve that rhythm and attachment tradeoff rather than presenting clarification as free of consequences.

The [comparison-standard audit](files/ithkuil_work/_cube_lacan_comparison_standard_audit_v2051.md) makes another interpretive commitment visible. It expands the comparison toward “all-that-is-you,” but explicitly retains the simpler “you” as a fallback because the stronger reading may over-abstract the beloved.

## Read the current line with its remaining questions

Start with the v2054 development pass and its companion scope audit, then follow the particular decisions that interest you. Earlier files retain earlier active lines: the [v2043 branch record](files/ithkuil_work/_cube_lacan_branch_policy_new_ithkuil_recanonicalization_v2043.md) separates the restored New Ithkuil project from its archived Ithkuil-III experiment. They should not be combined indiscriminately into a single current translation.

The v2054 audit still leaves punctuation, the weight of the foreign-phrase boundary, the rendering of “because,” and integration into the broader love text open. This is a working bridge translation with inspectable reasoning, not a finished poetic line or an independently certified linguistic result.
