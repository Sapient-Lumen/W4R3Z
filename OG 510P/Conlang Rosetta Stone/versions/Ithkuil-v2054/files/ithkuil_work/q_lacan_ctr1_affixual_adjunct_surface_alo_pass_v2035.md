## Why the surface should be an affixual adjunct, not an internal suffix on `aixtřas`

The archive has been carrying `<CTR₁>` as if it were a freestanding bridge-word. That was useful while the clause structure was still schematic, but it is no longer precise enough. `CTR` is not a root in the current analysis. It is a `VXCS` affix:

```text
CTR = Adverbial Contrastiveness
CS  = -l
Degree 1 = but / yet / still / nevertheless / despite the aforementioned
```

Because the final predicate has now been stabilized as `aixtřas`, there are two available implementation paths:

1. reopen `aixtřas` and insert `CTR/1` internally as a Slot-V or Slot-VII affix;
2. keep `aixtřas` intact and realize `CTR/1` as an affixual adjunct immediately before it.

v2035 chooses option 2.

The reason is scope. The English *but* is not part of the lexical semantics of maiming. It does not say “contrastive-maim” as a new derived event-kind. It says that the entire final predication contrasts with the preceding avowal and the explanatory frame:

```text
but [I mutilate you]
```

So the operator should have scope over the final predicate as a whole, including its eventhood and clause force, not merely over the root `XTŘ`, the local `Ca`, or a derived maiming-stem. The affixual adjunct is the cleanest way to express that without disturbing the already-frozen `aixtřas` word.

---

## Derivation of `alo`

### 1. `CTR/1` gives the consonant and degree vowel

For the New Ithkuil-style `VXCS` affix system used in this archive:

```text
CS(CTR) = l
Type-1 Degree-1 VX = a
```

Therefore the standard Type-1 Degree-1 `CTR` affix is:

```text
a + l  →  al
```

This is the light circumstantial contrastive value, not a derivational or affix-scoping value.

### 2. Affixual-adjunct `VS = o` gives whole-formative scope

A single-affix affixual adjunct has the structure:

```text
VXCS + VS
```

The `VS` value determines how the adjunct scopes over the following formative. For this pass, the needed scope is the entire following formative as a whole:

```text
VS = o = affix has scope over the entire formative as a whole, including Valence, Mood/Case, Illocution/Validation, etc.
```

So:

```text
al + o  →  alo
```

Default penultimate stress is retained:

```text
alo
```

not:

```text
aló
```

because ultimate stress would unnecessarily impose concatenated-stem-only scope.

## Why `alo` attaches to `aixtřas`, not to the framed middle clause

Earlier passes had already oscillated over whether the contrastive material should attach to the framed explanation or to the final violent predicate. v2030–v2034 effectively settled the scope by placing the `SIT` frame before the final predicate and then stabilizing the final predicate:

```text
[FRAMED:SIT ... hü]  <CTR₁>  aixtřas lo že
```

v2035 now makes that overt:

```text
[FRAMED:SIT ... hü]  alo aixtřas lo že
```

This is important. The line’s central contrast is not:

```text
I love you, but [because I love in you something more than you]
```

The contrast is:

```text
I love you, but [I mutilate you]
```

The framed clause explains why the paradoxical turn happens; it is not itself the adversative endpoint.

So the affixual adjunct goes immediately before the final predicate:

```text
alo aixtřas
```

not before the frame:

```text
alo <FRAMED:SIT[...]>
```

and not after the predicate:

```text
aixtřas alo
```

The latter would only become licensed if the formative were otherwise sentence-final and we specifically wanted post-position; here the final referentials still follow.

---

## Why not `al` without `VS`

A tempting shorthand would be:

```text
al aixtřas
```

This is demoted. Without the overt `VS` scope vowel, the archive would not be clearly saying that contrastiveness scopes over the final formative as a whole. The surface `alo` is slightly longer, but it is safer and more canonical for this stage because it explicitly chooses whole-formative scope.

---

## Why not `alö`

`VS = ö` would give the affix scope over the whole formative plus adjacent adjuncts, including a modular adjunct. That is too wide here.

The final predicate currently has no adjacent modular adjunct that needs to be included in the contrastive scope. The target is simply:

```text
but [aixtřas]
```

So:

```text
alo = enough scope
alö = excessive scope
```

If a later pass adds a modular adjunct before `aixtřas`, `alö` can be reopened. For v2035, it overbuilds the structure.

---

## Why not `XOR/5`

The affix table offers another apparent “but” candidate:

```text
XOR/5 = but [= on the contrary, X]
```

This is rejected for the Lacan line.

`XOR/5` would push toward a contrariety or replacement reading:

```text
not the aforementioned, but rather X / on the contrary, X
```

But the source is not saying:

```text
I do not love you; on the contrary, I mutilate you.
```

It says:

```text
I love you, but ... I mutilate you.
```

The contradiction is psychoanalytic and ethical, not a logical exclusive-or. The opening love-avowal remains active, and the final violence emerges out of the impossible structure of that love. Therefore `CTR/1` remains better than `XOR/5`.

---

## Why not `CTR/2`

`CTR/2` gives a heavier “however / on the other hand / as an exception” flavor. That is viable as a paraphrase, but too rhetorically explicit for the current lead. The source’s bridge-word is the lighter:

```text
but
```

and earlier archive work already preferred degree 1 for exactly this reason. v2035 keeps that decision and only surfaces it.

---

## Interaction with the frame boundary

v2030 refused to force a TPF-family frame-ending `-n` onto the terminal carrier-end `hü` before the hostability problem was solved. v2035 does not solve that frame-ending problem, but it improves the boundary in a different way.

Before v2035, the line ended the frame and then showed an abstract placeholder:

```text
... hü ]> <CTR₁> aixtřas lo že
```

After v2035, the reader gets a real Ithkuil-looking onset for the post-frame predicate:

```text
... hü ]> alo aixtřas lo že
```

That helps signal that the framed clause has ended before the final predication starts, even though the explicit TPF-frame-end issue remains open.

In other words:

```text
hü   = still only the foreign/carrier phrase end
alo  = first overt post-frame bridge into the final predicate
```

Do not read `alo` as a frame-closing marker. It is not one. It is the adversative affixual adjunct of the final predicate.
