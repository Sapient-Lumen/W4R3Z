# Lacan `objet petit a` carrier-end boundary marker `hü` pass v2023 — 2026-05-21

## English target span

This checkpoint continues directly from v2022.

Target sentence:

> **I love you, but, because inexplicably I love in you something more than you—the objet petit a—I mutilate you.**

The continuation point created by v2022 was narrow but important:

```text
esäl- «objet petit a»  →  esälu’u «objet petit a»
```

The foreign carrier now had:

- Stem 2 / PRC lead `e`,
- carrier root `-S-`,
- `STA + CTE + EXS` Slot-IV `VR = ä`,
- default Slot-VI `CA = -l-`,
- and final Slot-IX **RLT** case, surfaced transparently as `u’u`.

So the preferred local semi-overt packet stood as:

```text
<T-OBJ + ŠH₃>{LVL:SUR CMP:<2m>} esälu’u «objet petit a»
```

v2022 deliberately left open the next boundary question:

> should the French phrase be ended by punctuation only, low tone, pause, an explicit `hü`-style marker, or some register wrapping strategy?

This checkpoint answers that boundary question.

## What this checkpoint now freezes

### 1. the next frontier is the end of the foreign phrase, not the beginning of a new host-nucleus derivation

Now that `esälu’u` is a full cased carrier, the immediate danger is no longer the carrier itself.
The danger is that the following foreign phrase is visually and phonologically open-ended.

The whole sentence continues after the French phrase:

```text
... esälu’u «objet petit a» > <CTR₁> <XTŘ₂> <1m> <2m>
```

In an actual running Ithkuil line, the listener or reader must be told where the foreign phrase stops and where Ithkuil resumes.
Punctuation can show that to an English reader, but punctuation is not a morphological decision.
The archive should therefore now use an Ithkuil boundary marker.

### 2. the official construction supports leaving the foreign phrase itself unchanged after a carrier

The archive should not try to translate the internal French expression `objet petit a` into Ithkuil at this point.
It should also not force an Ithkuil phonological spelling merely for neatness.

The relevant official mechanism is exactly the carrier-root mechanism: a proper noun or foreign word/phrase follows a carrier stem, while the carrier supplies the grammatical identity/case behavior that the foreign phrase itself cannot bear.

The archive has already chosen the full carrier, not a shortcut carrier adjunct:

```text
esälu’u
```

That means the French expression can remain as the foreign expression:

```text
objet petit a
```

with the carrier doing the Ithkuil grammatical work.

### 3. the phrase should now receive explicit carrier-end marking: `hü`

The official New Ithkuil adjunct system provides a carrier-end marker for the end of a term/phrase governed by a carrier stem or carrier adjunct.
For this checkpoint, the archive should freeze the explicit marker:

```text
hü
```

So the preferred local right-edge packet advances from:

```text
<T-OBJ + ŠH₃>{LVL:SUR CMP:<2m>} esälu’u «objet petit a»
```

to:

```text
<T-OBJ + ŠH₃>{LVL:SUR CMP:<2m>} esälu’u «objet petit a» hü
```

Read without archive guillemets, the actual running-material skeleton is:

```text
... esälu’u objet petit a hü ...
```

The guillemets remain editorial notation only.
They are useful in the archive because they make the unchanged foreign phrase visible.
They are not part of the spoken Ithkuil string.

### 4. `hü` should be preferred over punctuation-only marking

A dash, comma, bracket, or guillemet boundary is useful in the English-facing archive, but it does not solve the Ithkuil parsing problem.

The French phrase contains ordinary-looking syllables from the point of view of a listener.
If an Ithkuil formative follows immediately after it, the listener needs a cue that the foreign phrase has ended.
The carrier-end marker gives that cue.

So punctuation may stay in the archive for readability, but it should not be the only boundary device.

Preferred:

```text
esälu’u «objet petit a» hü
```

Not preferred as the final running convention:

```text
esälu’u «objet petit a»
```

### 5. `hü` should be preferred over low-tone-only marking in this written project

The official system also allows prosodic strategies, including marking the end by tone / final pronunciation rather than an explicit ending adjunct.
That may be elegant in speech.
It is less robust for this archive.

This project is a written, highly audited translation path.
The point of the current revision is to make the boundary recoverable from the text itself.
Therefore this archive should choose the overt marker.

The archive can still note the speech-side convention:

```text
esälu’u objet petit a [pause / boundary cue] hü
```

But the stored written line should include `hü`.

### 6. the marker should be **carrier-end**, not a new case-bearing word

The new `hü` does not replace the carrier’s case.
It does not take RLT.
It does not identify the host.
It does not belong to the comparison-bearing noun packet.

Its job is narrower:

```text
hü = end of the foreign term/phrase governed by the carrier
```

So the internal structure after this checkpoint is:

```text
[host nucleus] [cased carrier] [foreign phrase] [carrier-end]
```

or, with the current archive labels:

```text
<T-OBJ + ŠH₃>{LVL:SUR CMP:<2m>}  esälu’u  «objet petit a»  hü
```

The carrier remains the relational nominal formative.
The foreign phrase remains the unchanged French theoretical label.
The `hü` marker only closes the foreign phrase.

### 7. do not insert `he` or `hi` between the carrier and the French phrase

This checkpoint also rejects a tempting alternate strategy:

```text
esälu’u he «objet petit a» hü
```

or:

```text
esälu’u hi «objet petit a» hü
```

The reason is adjacency.
The carrier-root construction wants the foreign phrase governed by the carrier.
Inserting a register-initial adjunct between the cased carrier and the French phrase risks making the phrase look less immediately governed by the carrier.

Also, `hi` would overstate the construction if interpreted as a strict proper-name Specificative register.
`objet petit a` is better treated here as a foreign theoretical term / label in a dash-apposition, not as a proper personal or placename.

And `he` would make the boundary look like a parenthetical register strategy rather than the direct carrier-governed foreign phrase strategy already chosen by the archive.

So the cleanest next move is not:

```text
carrier + register-open + foreign phrase + carrier-end
```

but:

```text
carrier + foreign phrase + carrier-end
```

Hence:

```text
esälu’u «objet petit a» hü
```

### 8. do not replace the full carrier with a carrier adjunct shortcut

Another tempting strategy is to discard the full carrier and use a carrier adjunct before the French phrase.
That would be attractive if the archive only needed case-bearing support for an already obvious foreign name.

But this passage needs more than a generic shortcut.
The archive has already done real semantic work inside the full carrier:

- it chooses Stem 2 for an abstract entity,
- it chooses CTE because the foreign phrase functions as an identity-bearing label,
- it keeps ordinary default `CA`,
- and it gives the carrier RLT so that the label identifies/distinguishes the preceding host.

A shortcut would hide too much of that work.

So this checkpoint freezes:

```text
keep full carrier:  esälu’u
add carrier-end:    hü
```

not:

```text
replace with carrier adjunct + French phrase
```

### 9. update the preferred local right-edge notation

Before this checkpoint, the preferred local notation was:

```text
<T-OBJ + ŠH₃>{LVL:SUR CMP:<2m>} esälu’u «objet petit a»
```

After this checkpoint, it becomes:

```text
<T-OBJ + ŠH₃>{LVL:SUR CMP:<2m>} esälu’u «objet petit a» hü
```

This is the first version of the right-edge packet that is both:

1. cased on the Ithkuil carrier, and
2. explicitly closed after the foreign phrase.

### 10. update the sentence-level readable working line

The v2022 sentence-level readable line was:

```text
<RKW₁> <1m> <2m> ; <FRAMED:SIT hleňfó-évvroal- <2m:ITP> <1m> <T-OBJ + ŠH₃>{LVL:SUR CMP:<2m>} esälu’u «objet petit a»> <CTR₁> <XTŘ₂> <1m> <2m>
```

The v2023 sentence-level readable line should now be:

```text
<RKW₁> <1m> <2m> ; <FRAMED:SIT hleňfó-évvroal- <2m:ITP> <1m> <T-OBJ + ŠH₃>{LVL:SUR CMP:<2m>} esälu’u «objet petit a» hü> <CTR₁> <XTŘ₂> <1m> <2m>
```

This is still not final surface Ithkuil.
It is a more robust semi-overt working line.
The gain is the closure of the foreign technical phrase before the final adversative mutilation clause resumes.

### 11. updated smooth control-reread

The current control-reread can now be sharpened as:

> **I love you, but, given the puzzling fact that through/in you I am attached in love to a salient excess-object that exceeds you — namely, the foreign technical label _objet petit a_ — I maim/mutilate you.**

The phrase “namely, the foreign technical label” is not a new semantic addition to Lacan.
It is only the English-facing explanation of what `esälu’u ... hü` is doing structurally.

## What this checkpoint still does not claim

This checkpoint still does **not** claim:

- that the host nucleus `<T-OBJ + ŠH₃>{LVL:SUR CMP:<2m>}` has been overtly realized,
- that the French phrase has been phonologically Ithkuilized,
- that the foreign phrase must always be written with guillemets,
- that `hü` is part of the French phrase,
- that a register wrapper has been chosen,
- or that the whole Lacan sentence has reached final surface form.

The gain is narrower:

```text
esälu’u «objet petit a»  →  esälu’u «objet petit a» hü
```

## Provisional takeaway

The archive should now close the unchanged French theoretical phrase with explicit **carrier-end `hü`**.
The right-edge packet is therefore:

```text
<T-OBJ + ŠH₃>{LVL:SUR CMP:<2m>} esälu’u «objet petit a» hü
```

and the readable whole-sentence lead becomes:
