# Lacan final-clause ERG + detrimental-ABS referential realization pass v2031 — 2026-05-22

## English target span

This checkpoint continues directly from v2030.

Target sentence:

> **I love you, but, because inexplicably I love in you something more than you—the objet petit a—I mutilate you.**

The v2030 active line was:

```text
<RKW₁> <1m> <2m> ; <FRAMED:SIT[ hleňfó-évvroal- sou li hlušh-otilöehëi sue esälu’u «objet petit a» hü ]> <CTR₁> <XTŘ₂> <1m> <2m>
```

The continuation point explicitly named by v2030 was the final violent clause:

```text
<CTR₁> <XTŘ₂> <1m> <2m>
```

and, more narrowly, whether:

```text
<1m> in final mutilation clause = ERGATIVE agent?
<2m> in final mutilation clause = ABSOLUTIVE patient?
```

The v2030 note guessed:

```text
lo  = 1m/ERG
sa  = 2m/ABS
```

This pass keeps the `lo` half and corrects the second half.  In the current official case table, **ABS is `e`**, not `a`; `a` is the Thematic/Content case marker, often zero-markable.  So a neutral addressee-patient would be:

```text
se
```

not:

```text
sa
```

But this pass goes one step further: because the final predicate is explicitly harmful, the best current addressee-patient is not neutral `se` but detrimental-effect `že`.

## Official grammar pressure consulted

### 1. root pressure: `XTŘ₂` is exactly in the right lexical neighborhood

The official lexicon gives:

```text
-XTŘ-  ‘BATTERY / AGGRAVATED ASSAULT / MAIMING / TORTURE’
Stem 2: (be) a state/act of maiming another; to maim another
```

So the root/stem choice already supplies the predicate lane for source **mutilate / maim**.

This means the next participant decision can stay local: it does not need to reopen whether `XTŘ₂` is the correct lexical root.  It is.

### 2. case pressure: the final `I` is ERGATIVE

The final act is tangible, deliberate violence against another party.  New Ithkuil's ERGATIVE case marks the animate party or force that initiates/causes an act/event creating a tangible effect or change of state in a patient.

So the final speaker is not:

```text
li = 1m/AFF
```

as in the middle affective clause.

The speaker is the agent of an act of maiming:

```text
lo = l + o = 1m.NEU + ERG
```

where:

```text
l = neutral monadic speaker
 o = ERGATIVE case
```

This keeps the archive's three-I split intact:

```text
opening I       = lover/avower, still not surfaced here
middle I        = AFF experiencer of obsessive attachment  → li
final I         = ERG agent of maiming                     → lo
```

That third `I` must not be confused with the second.  Lacan's violence is not merely something the speaker undergoes; it is something the speaker does.

### 3. case pressure: the final `you` is ABSOLUTIVE

The addressee is the target/patient who undergoes the tangible effect or change of state caused by the maiming act.

So the base case must be:

```text
ABS = e
```

not DATIVE, THEMATIC, AFFECTIVE, or STIMULATIVE.

A neutral addressee-patient would therefore be:

```text
se = s + e = 2m.NEU + ABS
```

The v2030 candidate `sa` is now demoted as a useful placeholder but rejected as a final patient form.

### 4. effect pressure: the final `you` should be Detrimental, not Neutral

New Ithkuil referentials distinguish Neutral, Beneficial, and Detrimental Effect.  For the second-person monadic addressee, the relevant consonants are:

```text
s = 2m Neutral
š = 2m Beneficial
ž = 2m Detrimental
```

The final clause says:

```text
I mutilate you
```

not merely:

```text
I physically affect you
```

The harm is not an incidental inference; it is part of the lexical and ethical force of the line.  Therefore the best current addressee-patient is:

```text
že = ž + e = 2m.DETRIMENTAL + ABSOLUTIVE
```

This is the central freeze of v2031.

## What this checkpoint now freezes

### 1. final-clause speaker

Freeze:

```text
<1m:ERG> → lo
```

Do not use:

```text
li
```

because `li` is already doing the middle-clause job: first-person Affective experiencer.

Do not use:

```text
lu
```

because Inducive would make the speaker both agent and patient of the same tangible act, i.e. effectively self-maiming/self-directed action.  That is not the source.

Do not use:

```text
lö
```

because Effectuative would make the speaker the enabler/causal-chain initiator rather than the immediate agent of the assaultive act.  The source's final verb is direct: **I mutilate you**.

So:

```text
lo
```

is frozen.

### 2. final-clause addressee

Freeze:

```text
<2m:DET+ABS> → že
```

This is deliberately stronger than neutral:

```text
<2m:NEU+ABS> → se
```

The neutral form is grammatically possible, but it underuses the official referential-effect contrast precisely where the text is screaming for it.

The final addressee is not just the patient of an action.  The addressee is the harmed patient of an act of maiming.  So `že` now leads.

### 3. final participant packet

The final packet now becomes:

```text
<XTŘ₂> lo že
```

with the contrastive bridge retained as before:

```text
<CTR₁> <XTŘ₂> lo že
```

This is now the best current surface for:

> but / yet / in contrast, I maim you.

### 4. the frame boundary from v2030 remains unchanged

This pass does not reopen the carrier-end or case-frame boundary question.

The middle frame still ends analytically after:

```text
... esälu’u «objet petit a» hü
```

and the final violent predicate remains outside the frame:

```text
<FRAMED:SIT[ ... hü ]> <CTR₁> <XTŘ₂> lo že
```

This matters because `lo že` belongs to the main violent clause, not to the explanatory affective frame.

### 5. `že` also helps protect the rhetorical reversal

The source's opening is:

```text
I love you
```

The closing is:

```text
I mutilate you
```

The closing `you` should feel like the same addressee, but now caught under the detrimental aspect of the speaker's action.  Using `že` accomplishes that without needing to add a separate moralizing modifier.

So the sentence now has a cleaner rhetorical arc:

```text
<2m>       opening beloved/addressee, still not frozen here
sou        middle interpretative addressee: through/in you
sue        middle comparative addressee: than you
že         final harmed addressee-patient: you-to-your-detriment
```

That is a real gain: the second-person material is no longer merely duplicated; each occurrence has a distinct job.

## Rejected or demoted alternatives

### A. `lo se`

Grammatically clean, but too flat.

```text
lo se = I-ERG you.NEU-ABS
```

This would translate the core argument structure, but it would not exploit the official beneficial/detrimental referential contrast.  Since the predicate is maiming, the detrimental addressee is more faithful.

Demoted, not forbidden.

### B. `lo sa`

Rejected as the final patient realization.

The v2030 line floated `sa` as a likely candidate, but the current case table makes ABS `e`; `a` is the Thematic/Content lane.  `sa` therefore risks reading as addressee-as-content/theme rather than addressee-as-patient.

### C. `řo že`

Overmarked.

```text
ř = 1m Detrimental
```

A detrimental first-person ERG would make the agent itself the referent under detrimental effect, or at least force the utterance to say something about detriment to the speaker.  That is not impossible psychologically, but it is not the direct surface of this clause.

The harmed party is the addressee.  Keep the speaker neutral:

```text
lo
```

### D. `lo si` or `lo sou`

Rejected.

The final addressee is not undergoing a merely affective experience (`si`), nor serving as an interpretative context (`sou`).  Those cases belong elsewhere in the sentence.

The final addressee undergoes bodily harm:

```text
že
```

### E. using the root's `OBJ` specification instead of an overt ABS patient

Rejected for now.

`XTŘ` has an `OBJ` specification meaning a victim of battery/aggravated assault, but the source is a verbal clause: **I mutilate you**.  Turning the addressee into an `OBJ`-specified nominal victim would shift the expression toward identification of the victim rather than the act done to the patient.

For now, keep `XTŘ₂` as the maiming predicate and mark the addressee as ABS patient.
