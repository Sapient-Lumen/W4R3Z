## Official grammar pressure consulted

### 1. the `XTŘ` root is still the correct root

The official lexicon gives:

```text
-XTŘ-  BATTERY / AGGRAVATED ASSAULT / MAIMING / TORTURE
BSC Stem 2: (be) a state/act of maiming another; to maim another
```

That exactly covers the source verb **mutilate** at the level the archive needs here: maiming another, with a direct victim.

So this pass does **not** reopen the root choice.

Carry forward:

```text
Root = XTŘ
Stem = 2
```

### 2. keep **BSC Specification**, do not promote to CSV or OBJ

A subtle trap remains: the lexicon line itself labels the Stem-2 value under **BSC**.

For this root, the BSC Stem-2 definition already means:

```text
a state/act of maiming another; to maim another
```

That is closer to Lacan's **I mutilate you** than the other Specifications.

Rejected for this line:

```text
CSV = a physical act of battery / violent assault
OBJ = a victim of battery or aggravated assault
```

`CSV` is tempting because the final clause is plainly physical and violent, but it drifts toward the general act of battery/assault rather than preserving the Stem-2 lexical narrowing to maiming.  `OBJ` would nominalize or victim-center the predicate and duplicate work already handled by `že`.

So the predicate remains:

```text
BSC Stem 2 of XTŘ
```

The new change is not Specification.  The new change is **Function**.

### 3. the final predicate is **DYNAMIC**, not STATIVE

The final clause is not merely reporting that a state of maiming obtains.
It says:

```text
I mutilate you.
```

The speaker is already frozen as:

```text
lo = 1m/ERG
```

and the addressee is already frozen as:

```text
že = 2m.DETRIMENTAL/ABS
```

That participant packet makes the event a direct agent-patient act.  The speaker causes a tangible harmful change in the addressee.

So, even though the lexicon's BSC Stem-2 gloss includes **state/act**, the grammatical Function should select the actional side of that range:

```text
Function = DYN / DYNAMIC
```

This is the opposite of the middle clause, where the archive deliberately kept the speaker as `li` because the VVR₂ predicate is an affective state the speaker undergoes.  The final clause must not inherit that affective/stative grammar.

### 4. why **STATIVE** is rejected

A STATIVE Stem-2 `XTŘ` predicate would lean toward:

```text
there is / obtains a state-act of maiming another
```

or toward treating maiming as an event/state without foregrounding the direct performance of the act.

That would undercut the Lacanian shock.  The line's cruelty depends on the final `I` becoming an outward causal agent after the middle `I` has been marked as an affective experiencer.

So v2032 rejects:

```text
STA + XTŘ₂
```

as the lead final predicate.

### 5. why **MANIFESTIVE** and **DESCRIPTIVE** are rejected

The final predicate is not an identity assertion:

```text
X is a maiming / X manifests as maiming
```

and it is not an adjectival/descriptive presentation:

```text
X is maiming-like / mutilative
```

So:

```text
MNF = rejected
DSC = rejected
```

The only Function that matches the source and the v2031 participant roles is:

```text
DYN
```

### 6. Pattern remains Pattern 1

Nothing in the surrounding archive has established a Pattern-2 or Pattern-3 derivational shift for `XTŘ`.
The official lexicon entry itself is being read in its ordinary Pattern-1 lexical lane.

So carry forward:

```text
Pattern = 1
Stem    = 2
Function = DYNAMIC
```

### 7. the official Vr value is therefore `ai-`

New Ithkuil's Slot-IV `Vr` encodes Pattern + Stem + Function.
For:



```text

```

This is the central freeze of v2032.

## Rejected or demoted alternatives

### A. `extř-`

Rejected as the lead final predicate because `e-` would be the STATIVE Pattern-1 Stem-2 Vr value.  That would align too much with a state of maiming and too little with the source's direct action:

```text
I mutilate you.
```

### B. `ixtř-`

Rejected because `i-` would correspond to Dynamic Pattern-1 Stem-1, not the already-chosen Stem 2.

Stem 1 would move the line toward aggravated assault/battery, while the source demands the narrower **mutilate/maim** lane.

### C. `eixtř-`

Rejected because `ei-` would correspond to Dynamic Pattern-1 Stem-3, i.e. the torture lane.  Torture is adjacent but too durative/torment-oriented for this source word.  Lacan says **mutilate**, not **torture**.

### D. `uixtř-` or other MANIFESTIVE choices

Rejected because the final predicate is not identifying something as an instance/manifestation of maiming.  It is an act performed by the speaker.

### E. `XTŘ-CSV`

Demoted.  A CSV reading would be plausible if the target were simply:

```text
I physically assault you.
```

But the source's lexical burden is sharper:

```text
I mutilate you.
```

BSC Stem 2 already says that.

### F. `XTŘ-OBJ`

Rejected.  The final addressee has already been made the harmed absolutive patient:

```text
že
```

Using OBJ would pull the predicate toward **the victim** rather than **the act of maiming the victim**.
