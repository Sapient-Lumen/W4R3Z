## 1. Scope of this revision

v2053 active line:

```text
rkwalû li sëi ; evvralácboa swou li hlušh-otiröehëi hlu’u «objet petit a» hü swië ; alo extřudêi lo že
```

v2054 audits the contrastive hinge:

```text
alo extřudêi lo že
```

Earlier v2035 correctly chose the New Ithkuil `CTR/1` affixual-adjunct path for English “but / yet / nevertheless.” The question reopened here is narrower:

```text
Should the affixual adjunct’s scope vowel be VS=o or VS=ö?
```

v2054 changes:

```text
alo  →  alö
```

New active line:

```text
rkwalû li sëi ; evvralácboa swou li hlušh-otiröehëi hlu’u «objet petit a» hü swië ; alö extřudêi lo že
```

This is a surface change, but a scope change more than a lexical change. The adversative affix remains `CTR/1`.

---

## 2. Why v2035’s `CTR/1` choice remains right

The English source says:

```text
I love you, but ... I mutilate you.
```

It does **not** say:

```text
I do not love you; on the contrary, I mutilate you.
```

Nor does it say:

```text
I love you; instead of that, I mutilate you.
```

So the contrast is not XOR-like replacement. The love remains active, and the maiming is the paradoxical consequence/eruption that coexists with it. Therefore the archive keeps:

```text
CTR/1 = but / yet / still / nevertheless / despite the aforementioned
```

and continues to reject:

```text
XOR/5 = on the contrary
XOR/6 = but rather / instead
CTR/2 = however / on the other hand / exception-to-the-aforementioned
```

`CTR/1` is still the lightest correct adversative operator.

---

## 3. Why `VS=o` was no longer enough

v2035 derived:

```text
CTR/1 = al
VS=o  = whole following formative scope
al + o = alo
```

That was adequate when the final predicate was still being stabilized as a predicate-formative in isolation. But the active v2053 final clause is now not simply:

```text
extřudêi
```

It is the full predicate cluster:

```text
extřudêi lo že
```

where:

```text
extřudêi = the maiming predicate
lo       = speaker as ERG agent
že       = harmed addressee as ABS patient
```

The English “but” scopes over the whole clause-like event:

```text
but [I mutilate you]
```

not merely over the bare event-kind:

```text
but [mutilation-occurs] I / you
```

The difference is subtle but important in this passage. Lacan’s shock is not just that maiming is contrastive; it is that **I**, because of this love/desire structure, mutilate **you**. The contrastive hinge must include the participant structure.

---

## 4. Derivation of `alö`

The consonantal affix remains:

```text
CTR = -l
Degree 1 Type-1 VX = a
CTR/1 = al
```

v2054 changes only the affixual-adjunct scope vowel:

```text
VS=o  = scope over the following formative as a whole
VS=ö  = scope over the following formative as a whole plus adjacent adjuncts
```

Therefore:

```text
al + ö  →  alö
```

Working value:

```text
alö = nevertheless / but, with scope over the following final-predicate cluster
```

The revised final clause is:

```text
alö extřudêi lo že
```

Control reread:

```text
nevertheless / but [I maim you-to-your-detriment]
```

---

## 5. Why the adjacent referentials count for this scope decision

The final participant referentials are not decorative. They are the clause’s argument structure:

```text
lo = 1m/ERG, speaker as tangible agent
že = detrimental 2m/ABS, addressee as harmed patient
```

New Ithkuil’s syntax normally places referentials after the main verb to which they are arguments. That is exactly the active order:

```text
extřudêi lo že
```

Because `lo` and `že` are adjacent to the main verb and are its arguments, the contrastive affix should include them. `VS=ö` is the available affixual-adjunct scope value for the following formative together with adjacent adjunct material. That is why `alö` is safer than `alo` after v2049–v2053 stabilized the predicate cluster.

---

## 6. Why `alö` does not over-capture the middle frame

A real risk remains:

```text
... hü swië ; alö extřudêi lo že
```

Could `alö` wrongly scope backward over the middle frame?

v2054 says no, for three reasons.

### 6.1 The affixual adjunct precedes its target formative

The adjunct sits immediately before:

```text
extřudêi
```

not before:

```text
evvralácboa
```

and not before the whole sentence.

### 6.2 The frame has already closed semantically

The framed middle clause ends at the delayed comparison standard:

```text
... hü swië
```

The semicolon is still bridge punctuation, not grammar, but it matches the parse: the frame is the explanatory circumstance; the `alö` phrase is the adversative endpoint.

### 6.3 `VS=ö` is local, not sentence-global

`ö` widens the affixual adjunct from “following formative only” to “following formative plus adjacent adjuncts.” It does not turn the affix into a discourse-level register marker over everything before and after it. So `alö` includes `lo že`; it does not consume `evvralácboa ... swië`.

---

## 7. Why not move the contrastive adjunct before the frame?

Rejected:

```text
rkwalû li sëi ; alö evvralácboa ... ; extřudêi lo že
```

That would read closer to:

```text
I love you, but [because inexplicably I love in you something more than you...]
```

But the English adversative target is the final violent act, not the explanation. The middle frame is not the contradicted element; it is the psychoanalytic reason the contradiction becomes possible.

The intended structure remains:

```text
[I love you] ; [given inexplicable VVR₂ fixation through all-that-is-you toward the named surplus-object] ; but [I maim you]
```

---

## 8. Why not attach `CTR/1` internally to `extřudêi`

Rejected possible path:

```text
extř-...-l/1-...
```

Internal attachment would make contrastiveness a derivational property of the maiming formative. That over-lexicalizes the English “but.” The contrast is not a kind of maiming; it is a relation between propositions/clausal acts:

```text
love-avowal  ↔  maiming-act
```

So the affixual adjunct remains the right level of expression.

## 10. Updated literal control gloss

```text
I-romantically-love you-as-affective-stimulus; given, for no reason, that I experience infatuated fixation through all-that-is-you toward a salient fuzzy surplus-something, named “objet petit a,” more-than all-that-is-you; nevertheless I maim you-to-your-detriment.
```

A smoother bridge-gloss:

```text
I love you; because, inexplicably, I am infatuated through all-that-is-you with the salient surplus-something named “objet petit a,” beyond all-that-is-you; nevertheless, I mutilate you.
```
