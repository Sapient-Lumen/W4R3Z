## 2. The active decision

v2046 rebuilt the final maiming predicate as:

```text
extřudá
```

That form used:

```text
VK = á = ASR/OBS
```

So the sentence was quietly saying the final maiming assertion was grounded in present sensory knowledge / present sensory experience.

v2049 replaces that validation with:

```text
VK = êi = ASR/USP
```

so:

```text
extřudá   ASR/OBS: “I perceive/observe that I maim”
extřudêi  ASR/USP: “I assert that I maim,” validation deliberately unspecified
```

The predicate remains ASSERTIVE. This is not a hypothetical, question, directive, or imaginary proposition. The shift is narrower: the sentence stops overcommitting to observational evidentiality.

---

## 3. Why ASR/USP is better than ASR/OBS here

The Lacan sentence is not primarily a police report or a witnessed physical assault. It is a psychoanalytic-theoretical utterance whose violence is intentionally unstable: “I mutilate you” can be read as literal, psychic, symbolic, ethical, or desire-structural violence.

Therefore:

```text
extřudá
```

is too sensorily concrete for the running bridge line. It implies that the speaker’s basis is present observation or present sensory experience. That may be possible in a maximal literalization, but it is not the safest default for the quoted sentence.

The replacement:

```text
extřudêi
```

keeps the force of the assertion while letting the quotation's register bear its ambiguity. It says: the speaker asserts the maiming, but this pass does not decide whether the basis is direct observation, inference, psychoanalytic theory, memory, fantasy, or rhetorical/theoretical dramatization.

This is especially important because v2047 already made the first clause explicitly intuitive:

```text
rkwalû li sëi
```

That opening “I love you” is rightly subjective self-report. The final “I mutilate you,” however, is not simply another interior affective state. It is an asserted consequence or action whose evidential mode should not be forced into sensory observation unless the translation has chosen a fully literal violence register.

---

## 4. Why not ASR/INF

A plausible alternative is:

```text
extřudú
```

with ASR/INF, “I infer that I maim you.”

This has a strong Lacanian appeal: the final violence could be treated as an inference from the structure of desire and the object-cause. But it over-explains the quotation. Lacan's sentence presents the mutilation as a stark first-person act/consequence, not as a conclusion marked “therefore I infer that...”.

ASR/INF remains a commentary-register alternate, especially if the line is being translated as psychoanalytic analysis rather than as a dramatic utterance.

---

## 5. Why not ASR/IMA

Another possible alternate is:

```text
extřudô
```

with ASR/IMA, an unreal/imaginative/dreamlike assertion.

This also has surface appeal because the line can be shocking and theoretical. But ASR/IMA would demote the violence too far into non-truth-intended imagery. The source does not simply say “I imagine mutilating you” or “as if I mutilated you.” It asserts a real structural violence of love/desire.

So ASR/IMA is rejected for the active bridge line.

---

## 6. Why not DIR or DEC

The final clause is not an imperative:

```text
* Be mutilated by me.
```

and it is not a performative declaration that creates the state merely by utterance:

```text
* I hereby mutilate you.
```

The final predicate remains ASR, not DIR or DEC.

---

## 7. Effect and participant roles remain unchanged

v2049 does **not** weaken the violence by changing the participants.

The final predicate still uses:

```text
lo   1m/ERG: speaker as agent/initiator of a tangible effect
že   2m detrimental ABS patient: addressee as the harmed patient
```

So the line still says:

```text
I maim you-to-your-detriment
```

What changes is only the evidential basis of the assertion.
