# Lacan opening clause `RKW₁` affective-stimulative surface `rkwal li sëi` pass v2036 — 2026-05-23

## English target span

Continuing the Lacan sentence:

> I love you, but, because inexplicably I love in you something more than you—the objet petit a—I mutilate you.

v2035 stabilized the post-frame adversative bridge and final violent predicate:

```text
alo aixtřas lo že
```

and left the first clause still editorial:

```text
<RKW₁> <1m> <2m>
```

This pass goes back to that opening declaration and gives it its first honest surface packet.

```text
rkwal = RKW₁, STATIVE, OBL/default shell, DEL/default Ca

sëi   = 2m neutral referential in STM case: you as stimulus/trigger of the affective state
```

---

## Why this opening should not be agent-patient `lo se`

The old placeholder:

```text
<RKW₁> <1m> <2m>
```

could tempt a flattened English-style mapping:

```text
RKW₁ lo se
```

where `lo` would be a first-person ERGATIVE agent and `se` a second-person ABSOLUTIVE patient. v2036 rejects that.

The opening “I love you” is not yet an action in which I physically or tangibly do something to you. It is a declaration of an affective state. Even if the broader project defines love morally and volitionally elsewhere, this Lacan quotation is specifically staging an emotionally charged interpersonal declaration which is then fractured by the middle clause. For this local line, the grammar should therefore keep the opener in the affective-experiential case-frame:

```text
RKW₁  AFF:1m  STM:2m
```

not:

```text
RKW₁  ERG:1m  ABS:2m
```

This also keeps the final clause maximally sharp by contrast:

```text
rkwal li sëi       = I experience romantic love toward/through you as stimulus
alo aixtřas lo že  = but I physically maim you-to-your-detriment
```

The line’s ethical horror depends on that grammar split. If both clauses were forced into agent/patient transitivity, the sentence would become flatter: “I do-love you, but I do-maim you.” The source is stranger: an affective bond becomes structurally complicit in violence.

---

## Root choice: keep `RKW₁`, not `VVR₂`, not the project’s moral-goodwill root lane

v1916 and later passes already split the two English occurrences of “love”:

```text
opening love       = RKW₁
middle cathexis    = VVR₂
```

v2036 reaffirms that split and finally surfaces the opener accordingly.

The opening clause is the plain interpersonal avowal:

```text
I love you
```

It is not yet the Lacanian object-fixation:

```text
I love in you something more than you
```

So the opener should not import the middle clause’s `VVR₂` infatuation/obsessiveness root. Nor should it switch to the broader project’s ethical-goodwill lane, because the Lacan quotation is not primarily an Aquinas/Kant thesis about willing the good of the other. It is psychoanalytic, interpersonal, and paradoxical.

Thus:

```text
RKW₁ = opening interpersonal/romantic love declaration
VVR₂ = middle object-cathexic infatuation/obsessiveness
XTŘ₂ = final maiming
```

This gives the sentence a three-part semantic descent:

```text
romantic love  →  obsessive excess-object attachment  →  maiming
```

That is much better than homogenizing it as:

```text
love  →  love  →  harm
```

## Deriving `rkwal`

### 1. Root and stem

The root remains:

```text
-RKW-
```

The relevant stem remains:

```text
Stem 1 = feel(ing of) romantic love
```

This is exactly the opener’s lane: a direct interpersonal declaration, not filial love, institutional love, or abstract devotion.

### 2. Function: STATIVE, not DYNAMIC

For Pattern 1 Stem 1, the STATIVE `Vr` is:

```text
a-
```

The DYNAMIC counterpart would be:

```text
i-
```

A DYNAMIC opening would drift toward “I am doing/entering/enacting romantic love” or “I am actively loving” in a way the Lacan source does not force. The source’s first clause is simply the state/declaration:

```text
I love you
```

So v2036 keeps STATIVE:

```text
Vr = a-
```

### 3. Case shell: OBL/default `a`

Following the same archive convention used for `aixtřas`, the verbal formative receives the simple default/OBLIQUE case shell:

```text
Vc = a
```

This is not doing participant work. The participants are carried by the following referentials:

```text
li  sëi
```

The opener therefore becomes:

```text
a + rkw + a + Ca
```

### 4. `Ca`: keep default DEL/UNI/CSL/M/NRM as `-l`

The final violent predicate deliberately chose PRX and therefore `-s`:

```text
aixtřas
```

The opening love-state should not copy that choice. The source does not say:

```text
I am currently in a locally relevant stretch of loving you
```

It says the simpler avowal:

```text
I love you
```

So v2036 keeps the default whole-state view:

```text
Ca = -l = NRM + DEL + M + CSL + UNI
```

Derivation:

```text
a + rkw + a + l  →  arkwal
```

### 5. Initial `a-` deletion

The archive’s running surface should use the usual word-initial STATIVE Pattern-1 Stem-1 shortcut:

```text
arkwal  →  rkwal
```

The full unelided analysis form remains useful in morpheme charts:

```text
arkwal = a-rkw-a-l
```

but the running line should now show:

```text
rkwal
```

not:

```text
arkwal
```

unless a later pass decides that unusually slow, hyper-explicit morphological transparency matters more than ordinary surface economy.

## Deriving `li`

This is retained from v2029 but now reused in the opening clause.

```text
l  = neutral monadic speaker, “I”
i  = AFF case
```

Therefore:

```text
l + i  →  li
```

Semantics:

```text
li = I as the experiencer/undergoer of the affective state
```

This is the right role for the opening love-state.

The contrast with the final clause remains essential:

```text
li = I as affective experiencer
lo = I as tangible ERGATIVE agent
```

So the same English “I” now receives two different Ithkuil roles in the same sentence:

```text
rkwal li sëi       = I undergo love
aixtřas lo že      = I enact maiming
```

That is not stylistic noise; it is a central gain.

---

## Deriving `sëi`

For the opening addressee:

```text
s   = neutral monadic addressee, “you”
ëi  = STM case
```

Therefore:

```text
s + ëi  →  sëi
```

Semantics:

```text
sëi = you as the stimulus/trigger/focus of the unwilled affective response
```

This is more precise than ABSOLUTIVE `se`.

`se` would cast the beloved as a patient undergoing an action. But in the opening clause, the beloved is not maimed, struck, moved, physically altered, or even necessarily affected by the declaration. The beloved is the affective stimulus: the one in relation to whom the speaker undergoes romantic love.

This also creates a useful internal echo:

```text
sëi  = you as stimulus of opening romantic love
hlušh-otilöehëi = the salient something-more-than-you as stimulus of middle VVR₂ attachment
```

The grammar now shows Lacan’s displacement:

```text
first, you are the love-stimulus;
then, in/through you, the objet-petit-a packet becomes the deeper stimulus.
```

That is exactly the sentence’s psychoanalytic fracture.

---

## Why this does not erase the ethical project’s broader definition of love

The surrounding project defines love as goodwill, moral responsibility, commitment to the Right, etc. That broader construction remains intact elsewhere in the archive.

But the Lacan quotation is one of the adversarial test cases. It should not be forced into the project’s normative definition too early. Here the word “love” is being exposed as unstable: interpersonal love can be captured by a partial-object structure, and that capture can turn violent.

Therefore v2036 preserves the local Lacan-specific split:

```text
RKW₁ = the initial interpersonal declaration
VVR₂ = the psychoanalytic obsessive attachment inside the beloved
XTŘ₂ = the mutilating consequence
```

The result is not the project’s final theory of love; it is the project honestly translating a hostile witness.

---

## Why not make the opening addressee beneficial or detrimental

The referential table allows neutral, beneficial, and detrimental effects. The final clause uses the detrimental second-person referential:

```text
že
```

because the addressee is harmed in the maiming.

The opener should not use detrimental second-person:

```text
žëi
```

because the fact of being loved is not yet itself the harm. Nor should it use beneficial second-person:

```text
šëi
```

because Lacan’s sentence does not let the opening love appear straightforwardly beneficial. The safest choice is neutral:

```text
sëi
```

This allows the sentence itself to generate the harm later:

```text
sëi  →  že
```

rather than smuggling harm into the first clause before the paradox unfolds.

---

## Why not use DATIVE for “to you”

A superficially attractive option would be:

```text
sü
```

as if “I love you” meant “my love is directed to you.” This is rejected for now.

DATIVE would foreground direction, reception, or affectedness. The opening line needs a simpler affective analysis: the addressee is not the recipient of a transferred object called love; the addressee is the affective stimulus of a speaker’s romantic-love state.

So:

```text
RKW₁ li sëi
```

is better than:

```text
RKW₁ li sü
```

for this exact clause.

---

## Why not reuse middle-clause `STM:[hlušh-otilöehëi ...]` as the only object of love

One could argue that the opening “you” is only a mask for the later objet-petit-a object, so maybe the first clause should be translated as though the true stimulus is already the excess-object packet.

v2036 rejects that collapse.

The source sentence itself stages the sequence:

1. I love you.
2. But because I love in you something more than you...
3. I mutilate you.

If the first clause already points to the excess-object instead of the addressee, the sentence loses its self-correction. The opening must be allowed to say what it says on the surface:

```text
I love you.
```

Only then can the middle clause split the apparent object:

```text
not simply you, but in you something more than you.
```

Therefore the opener gets `sëi`, and the middle object remains `hlušh-otilöehëi ...`.
