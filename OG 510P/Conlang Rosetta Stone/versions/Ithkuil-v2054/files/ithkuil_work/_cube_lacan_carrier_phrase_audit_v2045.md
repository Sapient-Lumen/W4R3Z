---

## 1. Summary decision

The active New-Ithkuil Lacan spine now uses:

```text
hlu’u «objet petit a» hü
```

not:

```text
esälu’u «objet petit a» hü
```

The old form is demoted because the host nucleus already supplies the “abstract something” semantics. The foreign phrase now only needs a named/appositional bridge, and the official New-Ithkuil carrier-adjunct pattern supplies exactly that.

## 3. Carrier cube axes

### 3.1 Mechanism axis

| Candidate | Form | Status | Reason |
|---|---:|---|---|
| full carrier stem | `esälu’u` | retired from active line | Over-specifies the foreign phrase as its own full abstract carrier after the host already supplies the abstract object. |
| carrier adjunct | `hlu’u` | active | Case-only name/apposition wrapper; matches official carrier-adjunct naming example. |
| SPF register | `hi ...` | inactive | Would mark proper-name specificity too broadly and weaken the grammatical case relation. |
| NAM adjunct | `hn...` | inactive | Would mention the name as a name-token rather than refer to the entity named. |
| PHR adjunct | `hň...` | inactive | Too meta-phrasal; not needed for an already conventional foreign technical term. |

### 3.2 Case axis

| Candidate | Reading | Status |
|---|---|---|
| RLT | named/identified by the following foreign phrase; relative/appositive naming | active |
| ESS | in the role/name/guise of the following phrase | live alternate, not active |
| DSP | descriptive adjective-like relation | inactive; would over-adjectivize the technical term |
| THM | bare thematic mention of the term | inactive; too flat for apposition |

### 3.3 Boundary axis

| Candidate | Status | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| `hü` carrier-end | active | Multi-word foreign phrase followed immediately by normal Ithkuil material. |
| pause/low tone only | allowed but not active | Good for oral performance, weaker for project archive clarity. |
| TPF frame-end suffix | inactive | The issue here is carrier-governed phrase end, not general case-frame end. |

## 5. Foreign phrase policy

The project now distinguishes three layers:

```text
Ithkuil host semantics:  hlušh-otilöehëi sue
Ithkuil carrier wrapper: hlu’u ... hü
foreign technical term:  «objet petit a»
```

Policy:

1. Do not phonologize or translate `objet petit a` in the active bridge line.
2. Do not assign case directly to the French words.
3. Do not use NAM unless the line is about the words “objet petit a” as a name-token.
4. Use carrier-end `hü` whenever a multi-word foreign phrase is followed by additional Ithkuil material in the same sentence.
5. Treat RLT as the active appositional naming case unless a future version intentionally shifts to an ESS “under-the-role-of” interpretation.

## 6. Interaction with the object-packet cube

The object packet now has two internal zones:

```text
[host zone]      hlušh-otilöehëi sue
[name zone]      hlu’u «objet petit a» hü
```

The carrier adjunct belongs to the name zone. It should not be used to revise the case of the host. The host remains STM because it is the affective stimulus of the `VVR₂` frame head.

## 7. Risk register after v2045

### Risk A — carrier adjunct presupposes known identity/nature

The grammar says the carrier adjunct is used when the identity/nature of the following foreign word or name is already known to the addressee. Here this is acceptable because the preceding host zone supplies the semantic class: it is the salient excess-object functioning as stimulus. Still, this remains a live risk.

Mitigation: preserve the retired full-carrier alternative in the archive for a future more pedagogical version.

### Risk B — RLT vs ESS remains philosophically close

RLT is source-nearer for dash apposition and official naming examples. ESS is tempting for Lacanian role/function analysis.

Mitigation: keep ESS as a named alternate, not as active.

### Risk C — `hü` may be pragmatically optional

In oral performance, low tone or pause may suffice. In the archive, explicit `hü` prevents the following `alo` from being interpreted as part of the foreign phrase.

Mitigation: active line keeps `hü`; later literary line may omit it if punctuation/performance instructions carry the boundary.
