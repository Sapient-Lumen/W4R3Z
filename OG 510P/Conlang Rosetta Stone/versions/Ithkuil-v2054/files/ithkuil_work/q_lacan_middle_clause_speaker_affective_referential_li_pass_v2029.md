# Lacan middle-clause speaker AFFECTIVE Referential `li` pass v2029 — 2026-05-21

## English target span

This checkpoint continues directly from v2028.

Target sentence:

> **I love you, but, because inexplicably I love in you something more than you—the objet petit a—I mutilate you.**

The v2028 middle-clause local packet was:

```text
hleňfó-évvroal- sou <1m> hlušh-otilöehëi sue esälu’u «objet petit a» hü
```

where v2028 had just frozen:

```text
<2m:ITP>  →  sou
```

and retained the still-unresolved inner speaker:

```text
<1m>
```

This checkpoint freezes that placeholder.

## What this checkpoint now freezes

### 1. the middle-clause speaker is the **experiencer** of an affective state

The archive’s middle-clause predicate is not the same as the opening whole-person love predicate.
The opening clause remains the direct interpersonal claim:

```text
I love you
```

But the middle clause has been deliberately routed through `VVR₂`, whose local working value is:

```text
VVR₂ = feel(ing of) infatuation / obsessiveness
```

So the speaker in this subclause is not best analyzed as an ERGATIVE agent doing a deliberate outward action.
The speaker is the one undergoing the affective/obsessive state.
The host packet has already been frozen as the STIMULATIVE trigger of that state:

```text
hlušh-otilöehëi = the salient something-more-than-you as STM
```

The matching participant on the speaker side is therefore:

```text
AFF = AFFECTIVE case
```

Working semantic frame:

```text
[affective predicate VVR₂]
  sou                              through/in you as interpretative medium
  li                               I, as affective experiencer
  hlušh-otilöehëi ...              the stimulus/object-cause of the affective state
```

### 2. `AFF` is the official case for this exact role

The relevant official case contrast is:

```text
AFF = AFFECTIVE: the experiencer/undergoer of an unwilled affective experience
STM = STIMULATIVE: the stimulus/trigger of such an affective response
```

That pair is exactly what the archive now needs.
The previous pass fixed the object-side role:

```text
hlušh-otilöehëi = STM
```

This pass fixes the subject/experiencer-side role:

```text
<1m> = AFF
```

This also matches an official New Ithkuil example pattern, where `li` is glossed as `1m/NEU-AFF` in a sensory/experiential frame.
So the project no longer needs to leave the speaker abstract.

### 3. the referential consonant is the neutral monadic speaker `l`

The speaker is singular direct `I`, not an inclusive `we`, not a speaker-plus-addressee cluster, and not a third-person resumptive.
The correct referential category remains:

```text
1m = monadic speaker
```

For neutral Effect, the consonant is:

```text
l
```

This pass does not use the beneficial or detrimental first-person forms:

```text
r   1m beneficial
ř   1m detrimental
```

Those would overstate the speaker’s effect-status in this local participant slot.
The middle clause is morally and psychically dark, but the local case is merely identifying who undergoes the affective state.
The harm belongs to the final mutilation clause, not to the technical Effect value of the speaker referential here.

So:

```text
C1 = l
```

### 4. the case vowel is `i`, yielding `l + i = li`

For a simple one-referent Referential, the surface is:

```text
C1 + VC1
```

Here:

```text
C1  = l     1m neutral monadic speaker
VC1 = i     AFF / AFFECTIVE case
```

Therefore:

```text
<1m:AFF>  →  li
```

No epenthetic vowel is required.
No stacked second case is required.
No personal-reference root is required.
No accent is required.

The form is simply:

```text
li
```

### 5. why `lo` / ERGATIVE is rejected

A tempting English-surface reading is:

```text
I love X
```

and Western grammar would tend to call `I` the subject/agent.
But New Ithkuil case choice is semantic-role driven, not English-syntax driven.
Inside this Lacanian subclause, the predicate is not deliberate benevolent willing.
It has already been made into a state of infatuation/obsessiveness.

So this pass rejects:

```text
lo = 1m/ERG
```

because it would make the speaker the doer/initiator of the affective event.
That would collapse the archive’s carefully maintained distinction between:

```text
ordinary / whole-person love      RKW₁
fractured obsessive attachment    VVR₂
```

The speaker is still morally responsible at the sentence level, especially in the final mutilation clause.
But the middle-clause role itself is experiencer, not agent.

### 6. why `lu` / INDUCIVE is rejected

The first-person Inducive form `lu` is also wrong here.
It would suggest a reflexive agent acting on or initiating something upon themself.
That is not the local event structure.
The speaker is not doing the affective state to himself as an initiated reflexive act.
He is undergoing an affective state triggered by the excess-object.

So:

```text
lu = rejected for the middle `<1m>`
```

### 7. why a generic `la`-type placeholder is rejected

Leaving the speaker as a generic direct participant would lose the main grammatical gain of the last several passes.
The archive has now established a clean affective pair:

```text
li                    speaker as AFF experiencer
hlušh-otilöehëi        host as STM stimulus
```

A generic or thematic first-person role would make the speaker look merely present in the clause, rather than specifying the actual relationship to the predicate.
For this local packet, that would be less official, less explanatory, and less faithful to the Lacanian asymmetry.

### 8. why the speaker should not be omitted

New Ithkuil can often economize more than English, and some argument roles can be recoverable from context.
But omission is bad here.
The source repeats first-person subjecthood three times:

```text
I love you
because ... I love in you ...
I mutilate you
```

That repetition matters.
The speaker is simultaneously:

```text
lover in the opening clause
experiencer of obsessive attachment in the middle clause
agent of mutilation in the final clause
```

The whole sentence pivots on the way those three `I`-roles fail to align morally.
The middle `I` should therefore stay overt, but now as `li`.

### 9. why no specialized personal-reference root is needed

A specialized personal-reference root would let the archive specify the speaker as self, personhood, psyche, embodied subject, identity-niche, etc.
That may be useful later in other parts of the love essay, but it is too heavy for this slot.

The immediate grammatical question is simply:

```text
Who undergoes the affective state?
```

The official short Referential answers it cleanly:

```text
li
```

No lexical psychologizing of the speaker is necessary here.
The psychological content is already in the predicate `VVR₂` and the Lacanian host packet.

### 10. placement: retain `sou li`, not `li sou`

v2028 froze the interpretative addressee immediately after the predicate:

```text
hleňfó-évvroal- sou
```

That choice should remain.
The addressee is the interpretative medium through whom the obsessive attachment takes shape.
The speaker then follows as the experiencer of that affective state.

So the preferred ordering is:

```text
hleňfó-évvroal- sou li hlušh-otilöehëi ...
```

not:

```text
hleňfó-évvroal- li sou hlušh-otilöehëi ...
```

The chosen order preserves the source’s sequence and the archive’s current scoping:

```text
in/through you  →  I undergo/love obsessively  →  something more than you
```

### 11. updated middle-clause packet after v2029

The v2028 fragment was:

```text
hleňfó-évvroal- sou <1m> hlušh-otilöehëi sue esälu’u «objet petit a» hü
```

The v2029 fragment is:

```text
hleňfó-évvroal- sou li hlušh-otilöehëi sue esälu’u «objet petit a» hü
```

Component gloss:

```text
hleňfó-évvroal-    framed/predicative middle-clause opening packet already frozen earlier
sou                2m neutral Referential in Interpretative case: through/in you
li                 1m neutral Referential in Affective case: I, as experiencer/undergoer
hlušh-otilöehëi     salient something-more-than-you as STIMULATIVE host
sue                2m neutral Referential in Comparative case: than you
esälu’u            foreign-name carrier in Relative case
«objet petit a»    retained foreign Lacanian technical phrase
hü                 carrier-end boundary marker
```

Working English control-reread:

> **Because, inexplicably, through you I undergo obsessive attachment toward the salient something-more-than-you, namely the _objet petit a_.**

This is intentionally not identical to:

> **Because, inexplicably, I choose to love you well.**

The source sentence is using the second “love” in a fractured, destructive, Lacanian way.
That is why `li` is right here even if other parts of the larger essay require more agentive/volitional love morphology.

### 13. revised local semantic map

After v2029, the middle clause has a nearly complete overt argument frame:

```text
Predicate / frame:
  hleňfó-évvroal-     inexplicably/framedly feel infatuation-obsessiveness

Predicate-level arguments:
  sou                 through/in you, Interpretative
```

The core affective pairing is now visible:

```text
li  ↔  hlušh-otilöehëi

```

That is the main grammatical gain of this checkpoint.

## Rejected or demoted alternatives

### A. leave `<1m>` abstract

Rejected.
The case and referential evidence is now strong enough to surface the form.

### B. `lo` / ERGATIVE

Rejected.
Too agentive for the middle `VVR₂` affective state.

### C. `lu` / INDUCIVE

Rejected.
Too reflexive/initiating.

### D. beneficial or detrimental first-person Effect

Rejected.
The speaker’s moral and psychic condition is complex, but this slot only needs neutral reference to the experiencer.
Do not encode the final harm or the speaker’s pathology in the referential consonant.

### E. specialized personal-reference root

Demoted.
Too much lexical commitment for a role that a short official Referential expresses cleanly.

### F. omit the speaker

Rejected.
The three repeated `I` roles are rhetorically important and must remain visible.
