# Ithkuil-108: making a philosophical translation inspectable

This edition works through an English philosophical essay on love, its epigraphs and footnotes, asking what each sentence would have to mean before compressing it into Ithkuil. Its main achievement is an inspectable translation-in-development: source wording, proposed semantic structure, alternatives and unresolved choices appear together. The [running translation](files/ithkuil_project/ithkuil_translation.md) proceeds from the opening proem through definitions and larger-scale relationships to the selected footnotes.

The text is a working semantic scaffold, not continuous finished surface Ithkuil. Labels such as `AGT`, `OBJ`, `PUR` and `BEN` expose the proposed agent, object, purpose and beneficiary. `S«…»` keeps an unresolved technical expression visible rather than replacing it with an apparently exact word. These devices let a reader question the interpretation separately from the eventual wordform.

## What the translation has to decide

**Love is not one undifferentiated feeling.** The [glossary](files/ithkuil_project/ithkuil_glossary.md) and opening [style decisions](files/ithkuil_project/ithkuil_notes.md) organize ethical goodwill around the project's `NZ₂` notation, while reserving affective-love choices for passages that actually concern those states. In Aquinas's epigraph, A.1, the difficulty is not merely finding a word for “love”: the draft distinguishes intending the good, the other who benefits, and the stronger possibility of explicitly volitional willing. It preserves alternative purpose, object and beneficiary arrangements instead of treating them as equivalent.

**Metaphors require a choice of relation.** Proem 0.1.1 reads “defines so much” as central significance within a conversation, not dictionary definition. In 0.1.4, “Consider the fall a part of the leap,” the intended relation is considering something *as a component*, rather than saying it is merely *like* another thing. The proposed fall and leap vocabulary remains a metaphorical model, but the part–whole decision is already explicit. These are useful places to see how a translation can become more precise without pretending the English has only one possible reading.

**A small qualification can change the argument.** Proem 0.1.7 refuses to equate “nearly selfless” joy with full altruism: it explores reduced self-centeredness while retaining “nearly” and “at least.” Footnote C.6 distinguishes love's not being reducible to trust or respect from the narrower claim that respect and some aspects of identity's predictability are nevertheless part of love. Its three negated sketches are followed by an inclusion claim, and the draft still flags the adequacy of its reduction operator. Read those parts together; the negations alone lose the qualification.

**Scale and causal direction matter.** B.4.1 treats “within individuals” as a domain of moral-psychological discussion, not literal bodily location. B.4.3 separates love as an enabling cause from love as an emergent consequence of a synthesis of contraries. B.4.4 moves to collective agents and makes the stated means of unification explicit. The work thus tries to preserve the essay's movement between individual, collective and cosmic claims without translating all three into the same spatial construction.

## A useful reading route

1. Read the [supplied project introduction](files/ithkuil_project/README.md), then proem 0.1.1–0.1.7 in the [translation](files/ithkuil_project/ithkuil_translation.md). Compare each English line with its interpretation and alternatives.
2. Follow A.1 and C.6 for the difference between a philosophical commitment and a convenient lexical substitution. Consult the [glossary](files/ithkuil_project/ithkuil_glossary.md) alongside them.
3. Read B.4.1–B.4.4 for domain, purpose, causal direction and instrumental relationships.
4. Use the [notes](files/ithkuil_project/ithkuil_notes.md), [reference record](files/ithkuil_project/ithkuil_sources.md) and [legacy worklog](files/ithkuil_project/legacy_worklog.md) to reconstruct revisions, not as interchangeable statements of a single final grammar.

The retained apparatus contains unresolved disagreements. For example, the glossary treats `NZ₂` as its ethical-goodwill root, while a section headed “Important correction” in the reference record calls it a project macro. The working “fitting way/time/reason” decomposition also retains `CB₃` where later notes distinguish motive `CB₁` from incentive `CB₃`. Those discrepancies are part of this edition's development record, not silently resolved by this guide. The interpretive distinctions above describe what the project is trying to preserve; they do not certify every lexical or grammatical choice.

The [later Ithkuil v2054 entrance](../Ithkuil-v2054/README.md) follows a much more localized investigation of the Lacan passage, contrastive scope and technical naming. Return to the [Conlang Rosetta Stone overview](../../README.md) for the parallel Lojban work. The early edition is valuable on its own for showing the broader essay's semantic decomposition, not merely as an obsolete version of that later phrase audit.
