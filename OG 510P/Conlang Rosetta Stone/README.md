# Conlang Rosetta Stone

## Translation as an examination of love

These Ithkuil and Lojban projects do more than find replacement words for an English passage. They repeatedly expose decisions the English can leave partly implicit: whether love names goodwill or attachment, who counts as the other, what a contrast modifies, and how much of a philosophical term should be translated rather than carried by name.

The two languages belong together here because they give readers different views of that work. Their version numbers remain separate histories, not a single race toward a finished translation.

## A route through the supplied drafts

1. [The earlier Lojban compiled draft](versions/Lojban-v157/files/lojban_love_translation_master_UPDATED_2026-01-26_v157.md) is a useful first entrance: translation blocks sit beside conventions and lexical decisions. Read the explanatory apparatus as part of the argument about meaning.
2. [The early Ithkuil notes](versions/Ithkuil-108/files/ithkuil_project/ithkuil_notes.md) distinguish ethical goodwill from affective love and retain open lexical gaps. Corrections and placeholders are visible rather than made to disappear behind a polished final line.
3. [The later Lojban revision note](versions/Lojban-rev3471/files/README_rev3471.md) focuses on the Aquinas line about willing the good of the other. Its account of making the other explicitly distinct shows why two grammatical variables alone were not considered enough. The accompanying package preserves both drafts and the patch.
4. [The later Ithkuil contrastive-scope audit](versions/Ithkuil-v2054/files/ithkuil_work/_cube_lacan_contrastive_scope_audit_v2054.md) narrows to a Lacan passage. A small vowel change is explained as changing the scope of a contrast over a predicate and its participants. The interest is the reasoning about scope, not merely the unfamiliar surface of the final sentence.

## Two small changes with large interpretive consequences

### Who is “the other”?

The [rev3471 Aquinas patch](versions/Lojban-rev3471/files/DIFF_rev3470_to_rev3471.patch) gives a compact way into the Lojban work. The line attributed there to Aquinas identifies loving with willing another's good. Its earlier formulation had two variables, one for the lover and one for the beloved. The revision's point is that two variable names do not, merely by looking different, require two different referents. It adds an explicit otherness condition and places the statement under universal quantification.

This changes what the sentence commits itself to, not just its appearance. The translator wants a general account of loving someone other than oneself, rather than leaving non-identity to the reader's expectations. At the same time, the patch preserves choices that remain open to philosophical examination: it renders willing through choosing and keeps the good related to a beneficiary. Making the participants explicit does not settle whether choosing, wishing, intending and bringing about are interchangeable, or whether benefit captures everything the source intends by good.

The patch is especially useful because the new line, its phrase glossary and its explanation travel together. A reader can inspect the intended change without reconstructing it from the final sentence alone. Read it beside the [early compiled draft](versions/Lojban-v157/files/lojban_love_translation_master_UPDATED_2026-01-26_v157.md), whose lexicon separates love, liking, attraction, goodwill and commitment. The later revision is not proof that every earlier distinction has been resolved. It is one inspectable answer to a particular problem.

### What does “but” place in contrast?

The [Ithkuil Lacan audit](versions/Ithkuil-v2054/files/ithkuil_work/_cube_lacan_contrastive_scope_audit_v2054.md) makes an equally small-looking change: alo becomes alö. In the work's analysis, the contrast now covers the final predicate together with its adjacent participants, rather than the predicate alone. The report draws both possible groupings and states that its brackets are an explanatory control parse, not characters secretly present in the translated line.

The intended effect is literary as well as grammatical. The closing violence is added to the avowal of love, not substituted for it as though the speaker had withdrawn the avowal. The audit rejects a replacement-style contrast and parks a rhetorically stronger alternative. Those decisions preserve the disturbing coexistence the translator reads in the quotation. They do not resolve that tension into the broader essay's account of ethical goodwill, and they do not make the violence an expression of justified care.

Nor is wider scope simply better scope. The report keeps the change local to the final cluster, leaving the preceding explanatory frame outside it. Its remaining questions concern punctuation, the weight of carrying “objet petit a” by name, and whether the frame adequately renders the source's causal relation. A vowel-level revision can therefore be an achieved local decision while the sentence remains unfinished in other respects.

Together these cases show why the notes are worth reading alongside the translations. One revision asks which participants a proposition quantifies over; the other asks which part of a sentence an operator governs. Neither problem is solved by a more exotic vocabulary. The pleasure and difficulty of the collection lie in watching an interpretation become explicit enough to disagree with.

## How to read the uncertainty

A pinned local choice is not an independent linguistic certification. These are evolving translations with their own resource choices, interpretations and unresolved questions. The guide does not declare the Ithkuil or Lojban correct, collapse their philosophical readings into one meaning, or continue their revision protocols.

Read the source notes, glossaries and superseded alternatives as companions to the drafts. They make the work unusually inspectable: a reader can see where precision was sought, where paraphrase was accepted and where the project declined to pretend a term had been settled.
