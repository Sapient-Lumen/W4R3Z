# Conlang Rosetta Stone

## Translation as an examination of love

These Ithkuil and Lojban projects do more than find replacement words for an English passage. They repeatedly expose decisions the English can leave partly implicit: whether love names goodwill or attachment, who counts as the other, what a contrast modifies, and how much of a philosophical term should be translated rather than carried by name.

The two languages belong together here because they give readers different views of that work. Their version numbers remain separate histories, not a single race toward a finished translation.

## A route through the supplied drafts

1. [The earlier Lojban compiled draft](versions/Lojban-v157/files/lojban_love_translation_master_UPDATED_2026-01-26_v157.md) is a useful first entrance: translation blocks sit beside conventions and lexical decisions. Read the explanatory apparatus as part of the argument about meaning.
2. [The early Ithkuil notes](versions/Ithkuil-108/files/ithkuil_project/ithkuil_notes.md) distinguish ethical goodwill from affective love and retain open lexical gaps. Corrections and placeholders are visible rather than made to disappear behind a polished final line.
3. [The later Lojban revision note](versions/Lojban-rev3471/files/README_rev3471.md) focuses on the Aquinas line about willing the good of the other. Its account of making the other explicitly distinct shows why two grammatical variables alone were not considered enough. The accompanying package preserves both drafts and the patch.
4. [The later Ithkuil contrastive-scope audit](versions/Ithkuil-v2054/files/ithkuil_work/_cube_lacan_contrastive_scope_audit_v2054.md) narrows to a Lacan passage. A small vowel change is explained as changing the scope of a contrast over a predicate and its participants. The interest is the reasoning about scope, not merely the unfamiliar surface of the final sentence.

## How to read the uncertainty

A pinned local choice is not an independent linguistic certification. These are evolving translations with their own resource choices, interpretations and unresolved questions. The guide does not declare the Ithkuil or Lojban correct, collapse their philosophical readings into one meaning, or continue their revision protocols.

Read the source notes, glossaries and superseded alternatives as companions to the drafts. They make the work unusually inspectable: a reader can see where precision was sought, where paraphrase was accepted and where the project declined to pretend a term had been settled.
