# Radical-Governance

A proposed architecture for contestable governance, from person-facing receipts and remedies to multi-level institutions and stewardship. These selected snapshots preserve changes in both substance and archive structure. Public preservation does not appoint an operational custodian, adopt the proposal, choose a new license or close its fieldwork gaps.

Supplied by h0p3; reading guides by Lumen, added 8 October 2026 UTC.

## Selected checkpoints

### [rev9 (internally identified; unversioned filename)](versions/unversioned-c0539b6b/README.md)

The filename says “latest”, but the opening README identifies this as rev9, dated 21 February 2026. It is an early set of compact design memos covering principles, remedies, public capacity and governance at several scales. The original filename is retained; “latest” is not a claim of currentness.

### [rev101](versions/rev101/README.md)

Develops governance as a system of joinable decision receipts, public rules, money traces and remedy routes. This checkpoint tightens private-actor identifiers and beneficial-ownership joins. Its own theory of change treats legibility as necessary but insufficient without enforcement and independent oversight.

### [rev216](versions/rev216/README.md)

Makes the proposal’s from-below posture and material prerequisites explicit: people need safety, support, time and enough institutional capacity to contest decisions. The archive asks for testing against lived experience before adoption; a specification is not a democratic mandate.

### [rev304](versions/rev304/README.md)

Extends join constraints across domain interfaces: joining records should name an empowered use and corrective action, with a safe fallback. Read the person-facing floors alongside the retained outside feedback. The feedback remains attributed historical commentary.

### [rev447](versions/rev447/README.md)

Distinguishes overlapping justice/redress and deliberation systems through explicit bridge guides. Its surrounding revisions improve navigation and reference integrity instead of flattening different remedies into one undifferentiated mechanism.

### [rev505](versions/rev505/README.md)

Adds participation defaults across scopes, from meetings and public comment to participatory budgets, deliberative panels and observer routes. The central question is how affected people enter a governing process between elections and court cases.

### [rev0613](versions/rev0613/README.md)

Adds a guidance hierarchy: which rule texts bind, which products explain, who may issue them, and what reliance is warranted. The original README explicitly says the prior full corpus was unavailable and calls this a continuation snapshot. It must not be read as a complete reconstruction of all earlier work.

### [rev0709](versions/rev0709/README.md)

Studies when one governing scale may override another, including local headroom, metropolitan responsibilities, national floors and treaty-bounded authority. These are the archive’s proposed defaults, not an account of the law of any particular jurisdiction.

### [rev0799](versions/rev0799/README.md)

Makes stewardship decisions and their absence visible: licensing, maintainer authority, contribution rules, security contact and succession are not conferred by possession of a ZIP. The published archive retains its open owner-decision and fieldwork gaps; this archival addition does not fill them.

## Reading and preservation notes

These are selected historical checkpoints, not a complete release history. New guides, inventories and integrity notes sit beside unchanged extracted files. Original ZIP names and bytes are preserved, including discrepancies and nested ZIPs. Wrapper layouts remain intact. No supplied program was executed and no external source claim was revalidated merely by copying the archive. Existing source-specific rights and licenses remain applicable; no blanket grant is added.
