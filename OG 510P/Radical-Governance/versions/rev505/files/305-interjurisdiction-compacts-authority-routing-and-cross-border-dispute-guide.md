# 305 — Inter-Jurisdiction / Compacts / Authority Routing / Cross-Border Dispute Guide

**Purpose:** turn the archive’s inter-jurisdiction family into a readable stack so boundary change, compacts, overlapping sovereignty, shared services, authority routing, and cross-border disputes stop competing for the same conceptual role.

**Why this memo exists:** `17`, `19`, `182`, `197`, `219`, `221`, `114`, and `230` all surface for queries like “who is responsible when authority overlaps”, “when should governments cooperate instead of reorganize”, “how should shared services work across jurisdictions”, “how do you stop ping-pong between authorities”, or “what should happen when a dispute crosses borders”. They describe one family from different layers. The archive should name those handoffs explicitly rather than invite flattening. For the prior international-instrument-typing seam — when the real question is whether a cross-border instrument is legally binding at all — route to `322-executive-agreements-mous-political-commitments-and-international-instrument-typing-rails.md`. For the narrower treaty-domestication seam — when international agreement crosses into domestic legal effect — route to `321-treaty-ratification-domestic-effect-reservations-and-implementation-rails.md`.
For the post-commitment follow-through seam — when the question is how international review cycles, recommendations, and domestic owner matrices should work once an instrument exists — route to `323-international-reporting-peer-review-and-domestic-follow-through-rails.md`.
For the treaty-lifecycle seam — when the question is how an existing commitment changes over time through provisional application, amendment uptake, suspension, denunciation, withdrawal, or public status-ledger control — route to `324-treaty-lifecycle-change-provisional-application-amendment-suspension-and-withdrawal-rails.md`.
For the narrower adjudication / complaints seam — when the question is which international court, tribunal, human-rights court, or treaty-body complaint mechanism can hear a dispute or complaint, on what consent basis, with what admissibility rules, interim protection, and compliance follow-through — route to `325-international-adjudication-individual-communications-jurisdiction-interim-measures-and-compliance-rails.md`.

**Evidence anchors:** OECD’s current multi-level-governance framing still treats decentralisation as inseparable from coordination across levels and notes that it is often accompanied by inter-municipal cooperation, metropolitan governance, and regionalisation [BIB-OECD-MLG-HUB-2026]. OECD’s 2024 Lithuania shared-services work keeps the practical focus on legal, fiscal, and institutional frameworks for shared municipal service provision rather than assuming coordination will work by goodwill alone [BIB-OECD-IMC-LITHUANIA-2024]. The Council of Europe’s Madrid Convention still treats transfrontier co-operation as something parties should “facilitate and foster” and explicitly encourages agreements and arrangements necessary for that purpose [BIB-COE-TRANSFRONTIER-1980]. HCCH’s 2019 Judgments Convention materials continue to frame recognition and enforcement as legal-certainty and access-to-justice infrastructure for cross-border cases, with the status table showing the Convention in force since 1 September 2023 and continuing to expand [BIB-HCCH-JUDGMENTS-2019] [BIB-HCCH-JUDGMENTS-STATUS-2026].

## Canonical reading order

### 1. Start with `17-jurisdiction-formation-and-boundaries.md`
Use `17` when the question is:
- whether the map itself should change,
- whether a merger, split, annexation, or responsibility transfer is justified,
- whether spillovers require a new unit rather than a compact,
- or how to keep boundary changes from erasing obligations, records, or remedy paths.

`17` is the **boundary / formation / map-change front door**.

### 2. Move to `19-compacts-and-cooperative-governance.md`
Use `19` when you need:
- a default “in-between” layer for cooperation without building a new tier,
- compact clauses, ratification discipline, cost-sharing, and dispute ladders,
- shared-service or mutual-aid cooperation with visible obligations,
- or a reversible coordination instrument that is still contestable.

`19` is the **compact / cooperative-governance front door**.

### 3. Use `182-polycentric-governance-and-compacts.md`
Use `182` when the real issue is:
- the general design pattern of overlapping centers of decision-making,
- why polycentric architecture can outperform single hierarchy,
- how to think about compacts as the minimal coordination contract,
- or how to frame redundancy, experimentation, and spillover control without jumping straight to federal theory.

`182` is the **general polycentric-pattern / design-language neighbor**.

### 4. Use `197-polycentric-federalism-and-overlapping-sovereignty.md`
Use `197` when you need:
- a cross-scope constitutional design for shared sovereignty,
- explicit default ownership plus overlap rules,
- intergovernmental bargaining, preemption, equalisation, and backstop design,
- or a federalism spine that preserves rights floors while allowing variation.

`197` is the **multi-level / overlapping-sovereignty / federal-design specialization**.

### 5. Use `219-shared-services-and-federated-administration-rails.md`
Use `219` when the real problem is:
- back-office or service delivery shared across jurisdictions,
- operator accountability when delivery is centralized but authority remains local,
- exit packages, portability, and no-rights-cliff seam design,
- or how to stop shared capacity from becoming a shadow state.

`219` is the **shared-services / federated-administration operating specialization**.

### 6. Use `221-jurisdiction-graph-and-authority-routing.md`
Use `221` when you need:
- a public, computable map of who is responsible for what, where, and when,
- a single-responsible endpoint,
- published delegation / shared-service / appeal edges,
- or an authority router that can answer “which office owns this case right now?”.

`221` is the **authority-routing / SRE / graph substrate**.

### 7. Use `114-interjurisdictional-dispute-and-coordination.md`
Use `114` when the issue is:
- a live overlap or buck-passing conflict between authorities,
- interim continuity while governments argue,
- a bounded dispute ladder and binding decider,
- or preventing scope conflict from becoming person-facing service collapse.

`114` is the **inter-authority dispute-lane / anti-ping-pong specialization**.

### 8. Use `230-conflict-of-laws-and-cross-border-dispute-rails.md`
Use `230` when the real problem is:
- cross-border recognition / enforcement,
- forum choice and conflict-of-laws packets,
- continuity and translation at an international or cross-border legal seam,
- or how to keep cross-border disputes from becoming trap doors or weaponized jurisdiction.

`230` is the **cross-border recognition / conflict-of-laws / dispute specialization**.


### 9. Use `322-executive-agreements-mous-political-commitments-and-international-instrument-typing-rails.md`
Use `322` when the real problem is:
- whether a cross-border instrument is legally binding at all,
- whether it should be a treaty, executive agreement, exchange of notes, MOU, or political commitment,
- what status clause, publication path, and scrutiny basis should apply before signature,
- or how to stop labels like “arrangement” or “MOU” from masking legal or fiscal consequences.

`322` is the **international-instrument typing seam memo**.


### 10. Use `321-treaty-ratification-domestic-effect-reservations-and-implementation-rails.md`
Use `321` when the real problem is:
- how a treaty, protocol, or international agreement becomes domestically effective,
- whether the agreement has direct effect or needs transposition,
- how reservations, declarations, opt-outs, amendments, or withdrawals should be surfaced,
- or how treaty obligations should be cross-walked into domestic rules, agencies, budgets, and remedy paths.

`321` is the **treaty-domestication / domestic-effect seam memo**.

### 11. Use `325-international-adjudication-individual-communications-jurisdiction-interim-measures-and-compliance-rails.md`
Use `325` when the real problem is:
- which international or regional forum can hear a dispute or complaint,
- what declaration, clause, optional protocol, or other acceptance basis makes the case possible,
- how standing, exhaustion, time limits, or other admissibility rules work,
- whether interim measures or urgent action can prevent irreparable harm,
- or how judgments, orders, views, or advisory opinions are typed and tracked for compliance afterward.

`325` is the **international adjudication / complaint seam memo**.

## Neighbor map and confusion boundaries

### Route to `296-status-identity-portability-and-mobility-routing-guide.md` when:
- the main question is who counts across borders,
- legal status or proof has to carry across jurisdictions,
- migration-status decisions or credential recognition are central,
- or the person-facing seam is primarily one of status continuity rather than institutional coordination architecture.

`296` remains the **person-status / continuity neighbor**.

### Route to `302-fiscal-state-budget-revenue-and-transfers-routing-guide.md` when:
- the real issue is who funds what across levels,
- the seam is mainly equalisation, transfers, open budgets, or fiscal constitution,
- or coordination is secondary to money-power design.

`302` remains the **fiscal-state neighbor**.

### Route to `170-watershed-and-water-governance-compacts.md` or `218-global-commons-governance-clubs-treaties-and-mrv-rails.md` when:
- the problem is sector-specific transboundary governance,
- the question is really about watershed compacts, club goods, treaties, or MRV,
- or the family is being entered through ecological / global-commons governance rather than general inter-jurisdiction design.

These remain the **sectoral / global neighbors**.

### Route to `322-executive-agreements-mous-political-commitments-and-international-instrument-typing-rails.md` when:
- the first dispute is whether the instrument is binding at all,
- a title like “MOU” or “arrangement” is doing too much constitutional work,
- the problem is scrutiny-evasion through non-binding form,
- or the public needs a typed status declaration before anyone argues about domestic effect.

`322` remains the **binding-status / instrument-classification neighbor**.

### Route to `305` first when:
- the question is broadly about seams between authorities,
- multiple memos in this family all look plausible,
- or you need to know whether the right next move is boundary change, compacting, shared-service routing, dispute handling, or cross-border legal coordination.

## Why these memos stay separate

Do **not** merge this family into one giant federalism / compacts memo yet. The archive still benefits from separate retrieval hooks for:
- **boundary and map change** (`17`),
- **compact clause design and cooperative governance** (`19`),
- **general polycentric design language** (`182`),
- **cross-scope shared-sovereignty architecture** (`197`),
- **shared-service operations and exit packages** (`219`),
- **authority-routing substrate and single-responsible-endpoint logic** (`221`),
- **live inter-authority dispute handling** (`114`),
- **cross-border legal recognition and conflict-of-laws discipline** (`230`).

The bridge exists to stop readers from having to infer those boundaries, not to erase them.
