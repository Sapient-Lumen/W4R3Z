# 327 — Scope-Form Defaults: Councils, Cabinets, Secretariats, and Anti-Theater Rails

**Cross-stack note:** use `306-scope-design-ideal-government-and-reference-bundles-guide.md` for the canonical route across the scope-design family. This memo is the scale-to-institutional-form front door; `14` / `176` explain cross-scope logic, `285` gives the default bundle language, `328` handles the scale-sensitive legitimacy channel, `329` handles the scale-sensitive tempo / time-horizon choice, `330` handles the scale-sensitive epistemic / evidence-mode choice, `331` handles the scale-sensitive accountability / review-mode choice, `332` handles the scale-sensitive coercion / direct-force-holding choice, `333` handles the scale-sensitive finance / revenue-model choice, `336` handles the scale-sensitive constituency / who-counts choice, `309` handles chamber design, and `311` handles the narrower national / supranational executive-form choice once the scale-sensitive default has been identified.

**Purpose:** answer the design question that remains after scope assignment and minimum-bundle design: **what institutional shape should government usually take at each scope?**

**Why this memo exists:** `14`, `54`, `176`, and `285` explain where functions belong and what the minimum bundle is, but they do not fully answer the next practical question: whether the ideal government at a given scale should look like an assembly, council-manager government, compact board, legislature-cabinet system, collegial commission, or treaty-and-secretariat layer. `311` answers part of that question for national and some supranational executive forms, but the archive still needed one compact memo that says **scale should change not just what government does, but the shape of the offices and decision bodies themselves**.

**From-below:** this memo is a guardrail against prestige imitation. It helps stop places from importing grand constitutional theater — little presidents, shadow metros, symbolic unions, or planetary strongmen — when what people actually need is a legible operator, clear receipts, and a remedy path that matches the scale of the problem.

**Evidence anchors:** local democracy works best when it reflects local preferences while still avoiding fragmentation and local autocracy [BIB-IDEA-LOCAL-DEMOCRACY-2017]; metropolitan governance is most justified where services create region-wide benefits, externalities, redistribution needs, or scale economies [BIB-WB-METROGOV-2020]; regional government is strongest where municipalities are too small for spillovers and infrastructure but the national level is too far away from functional areas [BIB-OECD-REGIONAL-GOV-2022]; constitutional borrowing should be cautious and system-aware rather than theatrical or partial [BIB-IDEA-LEGISLATURES-2024]; national executive form should follow function, especially around government formation, heads of state, and the risks of presidential overreach [BIB-IDEA-GOV-FORMATION-PRIMER] [BIB-IDEA-NONEXEC-PRES-2017] [BIB-IDEA-ELECTING-PRESIDENTS-2019] [BIB-IDEA-REMOVAL-PRES-2022]; crisis-era multi-level governance still depends on clear roles and negotiated coordination across layers rather than simple commandism [BIB-UNDP-MLG-CRISIS-2025]; current global reform language still points toward renewed multilateral machinery, future-guardianship, and digital-commons coordination rather than a unitary world state [BIB-UN-PACT-FUTURE-2024] [BIB-UN-GDC] [BIB-UN-DFG].

---

## Core claim

The ideal government at each scope is **not** just a smaller or larger copy of the national state.

As scale changes, several things change with it:
- the ratio of local knowledge to general rule,
- the width of spillovers and externalities,
- the need for professional administration,
- the thickness of the legitimacy story,
- and the danger that symbolic offices outrun actual competence.

So the archive’s rule is:
**lower scopes should default to councils, stewards, and professional operators; intermediate scopes should default to compact bodies and bounded executives; national polities can sustain thicker representative and executive forms; supranational and global layers should usually gain strength through rules, verification, finance, and coordination — not theatrical personalization.**

---

## The anti-theater test

Before creating or reforming an office, ask four questions:

1. **Mandate test:** does this office actually control decisions commensurate with the legitimacy story attached to it?
2. **Operator test:** does the form make everyday delivery and handoff clearer, or merely add symbolic prestige?
3. **Failure test:** when the office fails, is the correction path visible without constitutional drama?
4. **Scale-fit test:** is this office appropriate to the spillovers, finance model, and rights floor at this scale — or is it borrowed from a different level for status reasons?

If the answer to any of those is "no", the design is probably theatrical rather than functional.

## Default forms by scope

### 0. Household / association / cooperative

**Default form:** a **chartered assembly with a rotating or elected steward**.

Use:
- membership meetings or equivalent consent forums,
- a treasurer / steward / secretary role that keeps the memory and the money legible,
- simple mediation before escalation into public law.

Choose a thicker board form only when the body holds substantial assets, runs continuous operations, or exercises durable delegated authority.

**Avoid:** paternal quasi-state behavior, hidden coercion, or durable officeholders with unchecked control over money, membership, or sanctions.

### 1. Micro-local (building / block / village / campus / frontline institution)

**Default form:** an **open assembly or neighborhood council plus a named steward**.

Why this fits:
- local knowledge is very high,
- problem detection matters more than constitutional thickness,
- and legitimacy comes from visibility, recallability, and short feedback loops more than from a personalized executive story [BIB-IDEA-LOCAL-DEMOCRACY-2017] [BIB-UNDRR-LOCAL-AUTHORITIES].

Use:
- open meetings or equivalent community forums,
- rotation, lot, or short-term election for council roles,
- one clearly named steward or tiny operating team for upkeep, alerts, and municipal handoff.

**Avoid:** a miniature presidency, standalone coercive office, or any structure that lets informality become unreviewable power.

### 2. Municipal (city / town / county-equivalent frontline state)

**Default form:** an **elected council plus professional executive administration**, usually in a council-manager or council-plus-chief-administrator pattern.

This is the archive default because municipal government is usually the everyday operator of services, permits, infrastructure, and local enforcement. That makes professional administration, service standards, and inspectable queues more important than constitutional theater [BIB-IDEA-LOCAL-DEMOCRACY-2017] [BIB-OECD-URBAN-POLICY-2024].

Use a stronger directly elected mayoral form only when:
- the city is large and politically legible enough to justify a clearer citywide mandate,
- intergovernmental bargaining requires a visible political principal,
- and oversight, budget scrutiny, and remedy lanes are strong enough to prevent personalized municipal rule.

**Avoid:** ceremonial presidencies, weak councils facing strong personal executives, or local constitutional mimicry that obscures who actually runs service delivery.

### 3. Metropolitan / corridor / watershed / functional region

**Default form:** a **compact board, metro assembly, or delegated authority paired with a professional network operator**.

This scale exists because people, infrastructure, labor markets, watersheds, and externalities cross municipal lines. The best-fit form is therefore usually not a second city hall, but a bounded coordination body for the functions that truly operate at metro scale [BIB-WB-METROGOV-2020] [BIB-OECD-URBAN-POLICY-2024].

Use direct election or a thicker representative structure only when the metro tier holds genuinely political powers such as broad taxation, major redistribution, region-wide land-use authority, or high-salience public safety powers.

Otherwise prefer:
- member-jurisdiction delegation,
- a clearly published competence map,
- formula finance,
- and a professional operator for transport, water, housing coordination, resilience, or other cross-border systems.

**Avoid:** shadow governments that accumulate broad practical power without a matching legitimacy story, or conversely, grand directly elected metro executives whose actual powers remain too thin to justify the symbolism.

### 4. Regional (state / province / land / autonomous territory)

**Default form:** an **elected assembly plus executive government with sector backstop capacity**, usually anchored in a legislature-executive relationship rather than a quasi-sovereign presidency.

The regional level is often best placed for public goods with local/regional externalities, service and infrastructure matching to functional areas, and co-ordination among municipalities, especially where municipalities are too fragmented to govern alone [BIB-OECD-REGIONAL-GOV-2022].

Default features:
- an elected legislature,
- a premier / cabinet / executive council or similarly reviewable executive,
- professional regional administration,
- administrative-law discipline and inspection capacity,
- and strong transfer / equalization visibility.

A second chamber is usually unnecessary unless there is a strong territorial, linguistic, plurinational, or asymmetric-autonomy reason for it. A directly elected governor can work, but the archive default is to resist subnational presidentialization unless the region is constitutionally thick, politically cohesive, and heavily checked.

**Avoid:** treating regions as either mere deconcentrated branch offices or mini nation-states with symbolic executives untethered from real administrative or fiscal design.

### 5. National

**Default form:** a **representative legislature plus responsible executive government**, with a **non-executive head of state or other bounded continuity node** where feasible.

This archive’s default national answer is therefore usually:
- parliamentary responsible government,
- or a premier-presidential variant where the government remains genuinely accountable to parliament,
- plus clear publication of investiture, dismissal, dissolution, caretaker, and reserve-power rules [BIB-IDEA-GOV-FORMATION-PRIMER] [BIB-IDEA-NONEXEC-PRES-2017] [BIB-IDEA-ELECTING-PRESIDENTS-2019].

Why this default exists:
- national government must combine rights floors, redistribution, legislation, cabinet government, administrative capacity, and constitutional continuity,
- and that mix usually benefits from keeping day-to-day government politically responsible to a representative chamber rather than vesting too much system identity in one directly elected officeholder.

A presidential form can still be justified, but only when the constitution truly wants a separately elected executive mandate and has the counterweights, removal rules, legislative independence, and emergency constraints to live with it [BIB-IDEA-REMOVAL-PRES-2022].

**Avoid:** president-parliamentary ambiguity, direct election for offices expected to remain symbolic, or borrowed fragments of foreign constitutions that break the coherence of the whole system [BIB-IDEA-LEGISLATURES-2024].

### 6. Supranational / treaty union / regional court space

**Default form:** a **dual-legitimacy structure plus collegial executive / secretariat and independent court or review body**.

In practice, that usually means some combination of:
- member-state chamber / council,
- parliamentary or assembly channel for citizens or peoples,
- collegial commission / secretariat / steering body,
- independent court, review, or arbitration lane,
- and narrow, enumerated competences.

This is the appropriate default because supranational governance usually exists to solve spillovers and common-rule problems, not to reproduce the whole dramaturgy of a nation-state at a higher altitude. Where competences are still conferred and limited, a collegial or commission-like executive is usually safer than a personalized presidency [BIB-WB-METROGOV-2020] [BIB-OECD-REGIONAL-GOV-2022].

**Avoid:** symbolic presidentialization of unions whose real legitimacy remains mixed, delegated, and competence-limited.

### 7. Global / planetary

**Default form:** a **treaty-and-regime complex with secretariats, scientific panels, financing facilities, registries, and review / verification bodies**.

The archive default is emphatically **not** a world president or ordinary world state.

At planetary scale, the strongest legitimate tools are usually:
- target setting,
- registries and public commitment tracking,
- scientific and statistical verification,
- pooled finance and burden-sharing,
- peer review and implementation follow-through,
- and carefully bounded adjudication or sanctions interfaces where they exist [BIB-UN-PACT-FUTURE-2024] [BIB-UN-GDC] [BIB-UN-DFG] [BIB-UNDP-NEW-WAYS-GOV-2025].

That design is weaker at symbolic command but stronger at coordinating plural polities without pretending that a single world executive could safely or legitimately govern all ordinary public life.

**Avoid:** “world-state” fantasy, personalized summit politics masquerading as accountable government, or coercive tools that outrun publication, review, and humanitarian safeguards.

## Three form-selection rules that travel across the ladder

### A. The more service-heavy the layer, the more it should prefer professional administration over constitutional theater

Daily operators need:
- clear administrative ownership,
- stable budgets and staff,
- inspection and complaint lanes,
- and lossless handoffs.

That is why municipal and many regional designs should default to strong professional administration even when elected politics remains central.

### B. The more spillover-driven the layer, the more it should prefer compact and collegial forms over singular executives

Metropolitan, watershed, treaty-union, and many global functions exist because several jurisdictions must coordinate. These are the natural home of:
- boards,
- commissions,
- secretariats,
- joint authorities,
- and verification or finance bodies,
not faux presidents.

### C. The thicker the legitimacy story, the thicker the accountability architecture must be

Direct election, charismatic mandate, reserve powers, emergency discretion, or large coercive budgets all require correspondingly stronger:
- chamber scrutiny,
- publication duties,
- remedy lanes,
- acting / succession rules,
- and removal / deadlock design.

Do not create a thick legitimacy story with a thin accountability architecture.
