# 301 — Digital Governance / Data / Interoperability / Algorithmic-Assurance Routing Guide

**Purpose:** turn the archive’s digital-governance / data / interoperability / algorithmic-assurance family into a readable stack so broad digital-public-systems design, personal-data-power limits, interoperability seams, automated-decision inventories, ex ante AI assessment, and runtime assurance stop competing for the same conceptual role.

**Why this memo exists:** `06`, `127`, `128`, `42`, `152`, `146`, and `191` all surface for queries like “responsible digital government”, “public-sector AI governance”, “algorithmic accountability”, “how data sharing should work”, “how government systems interoperate without rights cliffs”, “what must be disclosed about automated decisions”, or “how to keep AI from becoming silent administrative power”. They describe one family from different layers. The archive should name those handoffs explicitly rather than invite flattening.

**Evidence anchors:** OECD’s current digital-government framing and 2025 index keep digital maturity tied to whole-of-government coordination, service design, and treating data as a strategic asset rather than a by-product [BIB-OECD-DGI-2025] [BIB-OECD-DIGITAL-GOV-HUB-2026]; OECD’s 2025 public-sector AI work treats AI in government as a question of public-sector roles, data and information management, governance, and safeguards rather than only procurement [BIB-OECD-GOVERNING-WITH-AI-2025] [BIB-OECD-AI]; the Interoperable Europe Act and current implementation work frame interoperability as a public-sector governance obligation rather than a purely technical integration task [BIB-EC-INTEROPERABLE-EUROPE-ACT-2026] [BIB-EC-INTEROPERABLE-EUROPE-REPORT-2026]; UNESCO and NIST continue to keep human rights, meaningful oversight, and lifecycle risk management central to AI governance [BIB-UNESCO-AI-ETHICS-HUB-2026] [BIB-NIST-AIRMF].

## Canonical reading order

### 1. Start with `06-digital-and-algorithmic-governance.md`
Use `06` when the question is:
- how public digital systems or automated decision environments should be governed in the first place,
- how to keep digital service delivery from becoming surveillance, exclusion, or AI-only gatekeeping,
- how digital power stays contestable and non-digital alternatives stay real,
- or how digital government fits into the archive’s broader person-facing governance model.

`06` is the **broad digital-public-systems / automated-governance front door**.

### 2. Move to `127-data-governance-and-privacy-interfaces.md`
Use `127` when you need:
- purpose limitation, lawful basis, sharing logs, and correction paths,
- person-usable receipts for what data exists, who used it, and why,
- bounded retention, sharing, and redress rules for high-impact public data systems,
- or a way to turn opaque data handling into a governed interface instead of a backend mystery.

`127` is the **data-rights / purpose-limitation / sharing-interface front door**.

### 3. Use `128-interoperability-interfaces-and-standards.md`
Use `128` when the real issue is:
- how agencies, jurisdictions, or vendors exchange information without creating continuity cliffs,
- how interfaces become governable objects with conformance, change receipts, and dispute paths,
- how interoperability stays purpose-limited and contestable,
- or how digital coordination becomes a public obligation rather than an informal integration habit.

`128` is the **interoperability / seam-governance / conformance front door**.

### 4. Use `152-algorithmic-impact-assessment-and-public-ai-governance.md`
Use `152` when the question becomes:
- whether an automated or AI-assisted system should be used at all,
- what public impact assessment, risk tiering, mitigations, and monitoring commitments must exist before deployment,
- how algorithmic use is tied to legal authority, affected groups, and remedy,
- or how ex ante public AI governance becomes a joinable artifact rather than a PDF ritual.

`152` is the **ex ante impact-assessment / public-AI-governance front door**.

### 5. Move to `42-automated-decision-systems-and-model-registry.md`
Use `42` when you need:
- a discoverable inventory of automated decision systems and models,
- stable IDs, model lineage, decision surfaces, and links to releases or appeals,
- a minimum registry substrate for rights-affecting automation,
- or a way to answer whether a model was in play, at what version, and under whose authority.

`42` is the **ADS / model-inventory substrate memo**.

### 6. Use `146-ai-assurance-and-public-sector-ai-ops.md`
Use `146` when the issue is:
- how a governed AI system stays safe after go-live,
- how model swaps, threshold changes, incidents, and degraded behavior become receipted events,
- how procurement, runtime monitoring, rollback, and incident learning stay connected,
- or how public-sector AI becomes an operated assurance pipeline instead of a one-time review.

`146` is the **runtime assurance / AI-ops / no-silent-swap memo**.

### 7. Use `191-algorithmic-systems-registry-and-audit-rails.md`
Use `191` when you need:
- public discoverability, contestability, transparency tiers, and recallability for algorithmic systems,
- independent audit expectations, drift monitoring, post-incident disclosure, and re-authorization cadence,
- a stricter public registry-and-audit framing for production systems,
- or a way to keep algorithmic power inspectable after deployment.

`191` is the **public registry / contestability / audit-rails specialization**.

## Confusion boundaries

### `06` vs `127`
- `06` is the **broad governance front door** for digital public systems and automated authority.
- `127` is the **personal-data-power interface memo** for data rights, sharing, retention, and correction.

### `127` vs `33`
- `127` is the **interface-and-artifact memo** for data receipts, sharing logs, and correction lanes.
- `33-data-protection-and-personal-data-governance.md` remains the **broader privacy / due-process / lawful-processing neighbor** when the question is really foundational data-rights doctrine and enforcement.

### `127` vs `128`
- `127` governs **what data may be used, shared, retained, and corrected**.
- `128` governs **how systems connect across seams, publish interface cards, and stay conformant under change**.

### `42` vs `191`
- `42` is the **inventory substrate**: what ADS / models exist, how they are identified, and what minimum metadata they must publish.
- `191` is the **production public-registry / audit-rails specialization**: how deployed systems stay discoverable, monitored, contestable, and recallable.

### `152` vs `146`
- `152` asks **should this system be used, under what impact tier, with what mitigations and monitoring commitments?**
- `146` asks **how does the approved system stay governed after deployment through change control, assurance receipts, incidents, and rollback?**

### `146` vs `173`
- `146` is the **runtime assurance and AI-ops memo**.
- `173-ai-standards-and-regulatory-mapping.md` remains the **law / standards / conformance mapping neighbor** when the real issue is how legal duties, management-system standards, and operational controls join.

### `06` / `127` / `128` / `42` / `152` / `146` / `191` vs `287`
- This family governs **digital public systems, data rights, interoperability seams, automated-decision inventories, and AI assurance**.
- `287-dpi-identity-and-trust-family-map.md` remains the right neighbor when the problem is really **digital public infrastructure architecture, digital identity, trust frameworks, or credential utility design**.

### `127` / `152` / `146` vs `144`
- `127`, `152`, and `146` govern **service-facing data use and AI system discipline**.
- `144-data-stewardship-and-data-trusts.md` remains the right neighbor when the issue is **stewardship institutions for shared or high-impact datasets rather than a particular service interface or AI deployment**.

- Cite `06` for **broad digital-government guardrails, non-digital fallback, anti-surveillance design, AI-only gatekeeping risks, and the general governance frame for digital public systems**.
- Cite `127` for **purpose limitation, legal basis, sharing logs, retention, correction rights, and person-usable data-governance receipts**.
- Cite `128` for **interface cards, conformance, change receipts, schema/governance seams, and cross-agency / cross-jurisdiction interoperability discipline**.
- Cite `152` for **algorithmic impact assessments, risk tiers, pre-deployment mitigations, public AI governance packets, and monitoring commitments**.
- Cite `42` for **ADS / model registries, version lineage, system inventories, human-role declarations, and minimum disclosure substrate**.
- Cite `146` for **runtime monitoring, no-silent-swap rules, assurance cases, AI incidents, rollback, procurement clauses, and post-deployment learning loops**.
- Cite `191` for **public registry completeness, contestability, transparency tiers, drift disclosure, audit expectations, and recallability of production systems**.

## Safe merge rule for this cluster

Do **not** flatten this family into one general memo on “AI governance”, “digital transformation”, “data governance”, or “algorithmic accountability”.
The safer pattern is:
1. keep `06` as the broad digital-public-systems front door,
2. keep `127` as the data-rights / purpose-limitation interface memo,
3. keep `128` as the interoperability / seam-governance / conformance memo,
4. keep `152` as the ex ante impact-assessment / public-AI-governance front door,
5. keep `42` as the ADS / model-inventory substrate,
6. keep `146` as the runtime assurance / AI-ops specialization,
7. keep `191` as the public registry / contestability / audit-rails specialization,
8. keep `33`, `144`, `173`, and `287` as named privacy, stewardship, standards, and DPI neighbors,
9. and use this memo as the bridge that names the roles.

That preserves distinct retrieval hooks while sharply reducing reader confusion.
