**Purpose:** answer the concrete design question behind the archive’s scope work: *what should the government at each scope actually look like when it is doing its job well?*

**Why this memo exists:** `14-scope-ladder.md`, `103-scope-cards.md`, and `176-ideal-governance-by-scope-synthesis.md` explain assignment logic, scope duties, and escalation. This memo adds the next layer: the **default institutional bundle** that makes an “ideal government” real at each scope without pretending every polity should use one constitutional template.

**From-below:** the point is not to romanticize localism or celebrate distant expertise. The point is to make sure every scope has the thinnest bundle that can still owe a person something concrete: an answer, a reason, a record, a remedy, and a continuity path.

## What this memo means by an “ideal government”

At any scope, the ideal government is the one that combines eight things without excess:

1. **a legitimacy engine** people can understand,
2. **a selection design** that says how offices are filled,
3. **a tenure design** that says how authority renews or ends,
4. **an office-status design** that says whether serving is volunteer, stipended, delegated, part-time salaried, or full-time salaried,
5. **an operating core** that can actually deliver,
6. **a finance model** matched to the job,
7. **public artifacts** that make power inspectable,
8. **a remedy path** that survives handoffs and failure.

If one of those eight is missing, the scope is not really governed; it is only being *administered*, *performed*, or *endured*.

This memo therefore does **not** ask “centralize or decentralize?” It asks: **what is the minimum bundle that makes each scale competent, bounded, and contestable?**

## The bundle template (use at every scope)

For each scope, specify these eight fields before creating or reforming an institution:

- **Legitimacy:** who authorizes it, and by what recurring process?
- **Selection:** how are offices filled — election, rotation, delegation, appointment, or some explicit mix?
- **Tenure:** how long do offices last, how renewable are they, and where should staggering replace synchronized turnover?
- **Office status:** is the role volunteer, expense-reimbursed, lightly stipended, per-diem delegated, salaried part-time, or salaried full-time — and who becomes excluded if that answer is wrong?
- **Operator:** who runs the thing between elections, meetings, or treaty cycles?
- **Finance:** what revenue or transfer model keeps it from becoming symbolic?
- **Artifacts:** what must be published so outsiders can see responsibility, money, and decisions?
- **Remedy / migration:** what happens when it fails, exceeds scope, or harms someone?

That template is the archive’s safest answer to “what should the ideal government be?” because it travels across constitutions while remaining testable. ## Reference bundles by scope

### 0. Household / association / cooperative

**Ideal default:** a **chartered consent-based body** with explicit exit, shared-money transparency, and a lightweight dispute path.

- **Legitimacy:** membership, consent, and revocable delegation.
- **Selection:** elected or rotating officers from the membership, with lot or turn-taking acceptable for tiny roles.
- **Tenure:** short, reviewable terms with easy rotation; no office should quietly become permanent property.
- **Office status:** mostly volunteer service with expense reimbursement, plus light stipend where treasury or coordination burden would otherwise become exclusionary.
- **Operator:** elected or rotating steward / treasurer / coordinator.
- **Finance:** dues, shared contributions, or clearly bounded common funds.
- **Artifacts:** charter, membership rules, spending log, decision receipts.
- **Remedy / migration:** mediation, easy exit, and escalation out to public law when coercion, abuse, or rights violations appear.

**What it must not become:** a hidden quasi-state that claims binding authority without due process or escape.

### 1. Micro-local (building / block / village / campus / frontline institution)

**Ideal default:** a **commons council plus named steward**: small enough to know the place, formal enough to keep receipts.

- **Legitimacy:** open meetings, rotation or election, anti-capture rules, and visible convening rights.
- **Selection:** short-term election, rotation, or lot for steward roles; avoid permanent local bosses.
- **Tenure:** short, interruptible, and rotation-biased; the steward role should be challengeable before it hardens into local incumbency.
- **Office status:** volunteer or lightly stipended stewardship; paid part-time only where the burden is clearly continuous and no longer occasional civic duty.
- **Operator:** a steward or tiny operating team for immediate upkeep, alerts, and first response.
- **Finance:** micro-budgets, maintenance funds, or delegated neighborhood grants.
- **Artifacts:** who is responsible today; issue log; small-budget ledger; meeting / decision receipts; posted escalation routes.
- **Remedy / migration:** mandatory escalation to municipal services, ombuds, or emergency operators when local capacity or rights floors are exceeded.

**Ideal job:** detection, trust, maintenance, and rapid coordination where local knowledge dominates.

**Hard limit:** micro-local bodies should not hold final authority over identity status, detention, major exclusions, or other high-consequence rights decisions.

### 2. Municipal (city / town / county-equivalent frontline state)

**Ideal default:** an **elected council plus professional executive and independent complaint lane**.

- **Legitimacy:** local elections, open hearings, and bounded delegated rulemaking.
- **Selection:** directly elected council by default; executive selection matched to the real city model; professional operators appointed on merit.
- **Tenure:** medium fixed electoral cycles for representative offices, indefinite merit tenure for administration, and staggered terms for continuity-sensitive boards.
- **Office status:** mixed — representative offices may be part-time in smaller places, but the operating core should be salaried, and thick city leadership often becomes functionally full-time.
- **Operator:** city manager / mayoral administration / professional civil service with published service standards.
- **Finance:** own-source revenue plus formula transfers and equalization where needed.
- **Artifacts:** rule inventory, budget and contracts, queue/service dashboards, permitting and enforcement receipts, complaint and appeal registry.
- **Remedy / migration:** ombuds / tribunal access, regional mutual aid, and explicit transfer protocols when cases cross boundaries.

**Ideal job:** be the visible operator of everyday government: the place where a person can walk in, ask what happened, and get a reasoned next step.

**Hard limit:** municipalities must not become sealed containers that strand people at jurisdiction seams.

### 3. Metropolitan / corridor / watershed / functional region

**Ideal default:** a **compact authority or metro body** for cross-border systems, with a narrow mandate and high publication duties.

- **Legitimacy:** delegated authority from member jurisdictions, direct election where powers are broad, or a mixed board where powers are narrow but technical.
- **Selection:** member-jurisdiction delegates as the default, mixed boards where useful, and direct election only when metro power becomes thick enough to justify it.
- **Tenure:** delegated seats should usually track the originating office, while compact-critical technical or oversight seats should be staggered rather than wholly replaced at once.
- **Office status:** delegated or office-linked political roles plus salaried full-time administrative backstop; use stipends or per diems for bounded committee burdens rather than inventing a prestige political caste.
- **Operator:** a professional network operator for transit, water, airshed, grid, housing coordination, or resilience planning.
- **Finance:** formula contributions, earmarked taxes/fees, or shared capital plans.
- **Artifacts:** mandate card, competence map, budget formula, performance dashboard, compact text, cross-border dispute lane.
- **Remedy / migration:** no-wrong-door routing for cases that start in one municipality and end in another; upward escalation when rights floors or major externalities are in play.

**Ideal job:** govern the systems people actually move through, not the old borders printed on the map.

**Hard limit:** functional authorities should be narrow enough to avoid becoming unaccountable shadow governments.

### 4. Regional (state / province / land / autonomous territory)

**Ideal default:** a **legislature-executive pair with sector backstop capacity and administrative-law discipline**.

- **Legitimacy:** regional elections plus legally reviewable delegated authority.
- **Selection:** directly elected representative chamber, constitutionally typed executive selection, and appointed sectoral / regulatory execution.
- **Tenure:** medium fixed terms for representative politics, confidence-linked tenure for parliamentary executives, and longer or staggered terms for review / oversight offices.
- **Office status:** salaried representative and executive politics, with full-time professional administration and incompatibility rules strong enough that the office is not quietly subsidized by outside patrons.
- **Operator:** a regional civil service able to backstop weaker local governments and run scale-sensitive sectors.
- **Finance:** own taxes plus intergovernmental transfers / equalization.
- **Artifacts:** regional standards, waivers, capital plans, transfer formulas, emergency orders, inspection and enforcement records.
- **Remedy / migration:** administrative courts / review bodies, temporary takeovers or support compacts when lower levels fail, and restoration plans when emergency intervention ends.

**Ideal job:** absorb variation across municipalities while preserving rights floors, sector quality, and cross-city coordination.

**Hard limit:** regions should not hoard routine, high-context service decisions that belong closer to the ground.

### 5. National

**Ideal default:** a **constitutional state with rights floors, redistribution, professional administration, and backbone rails**.

- **Legitimacy:** constitution, elections, representation rules, and judicially reviewable public authority. Where chamber structure matters, keep the chamber / committee / confidence architecture explicit rather than implicit (`309-representative-chambers-committees-opposition-rights-and-confidence-architecture.md`).
- **Selection:** competitive elections for representative institutions, constitutionally typed executive formation, and appointment for bounded expert or neutral offices.
- **Tenure:** medium legislative cycles, stronger anti-entrenchment for singular executives than for representative chambers, and long staggered terms for independence-sensitive bodies.
- **Office status:** salaried, functionally full-time representative and executive office; salaried professional administration; no thick national public office dependent on private wealth.
- **Operator:** national civil service, ministries, regulators, audit institutions, statistics bodies, and backbone digital / fiscal operators.
- **Finance:** broad tax base, borrowing capacity, macro-fiscal tools, and social-insurance / equalization pools.
- **Artifacts:** law and delegation inventory, budgets and procurement, national statistics, election integrity artifacts, algorithm/system registries where used, portability rules, and public integrity disclosures.
- **Remedy / migration:** national courts, ombuds, inspectorates, and emergency backstop authority for lower-level breakdowns.

**Ideal job:** hold the rights floor, run redistribution and risk-pooling, and maintain the interoperability rails that let people survive movement across institutions and regions.

**Hard limit:** national government should standardize floors and backbone interfaces, not erase justified local variation. If the design question turns from bundle composition to executive form — parliamentary vs presidential vs semi-presidential vs collegial, or whether head of state and head of government should be separated — route to `311-executive-system-choice-parliamentary-presidential-semipresidential-and-collegial-rails.md`. If the narrower issue is how a distinct head-of-state office should be designed once that broader form is chosen — non-executive president vs constitutional monarch, countersignature, reserve powers, or succession — route to `313-head-of-state-design-non-executive-presidents-constitutional-monarchs-reserve-powers-and-succession-rails.md`. If the missing piece is the post-election interval itself — who gets the first formation attempt, what proves confidence, how minority support is disclosed, or when dissolution is allowed — route to `314-government-formation-investiture-windows-minority-governments-and-deadlock-breaking-rails.md`.

### 6. Supranational / treaty union / regional court space

**Ideal default:** an **enumerated-powers union** with clear subsidiarity tests, common standards, and review institutions strong enough to bind collective-action failures.

- **Legitimacy:** treaties plus member-state authorization, often with a mixed legitimacy structure (member governments + parliamentary or assembly representation + judicial review); if a parliamentary channel exists, its dual-legitimacy chamber design should be explicit (`309-representative-chambers-committees-opposition-rights-and-confidence-architecture.md`).
- **Selection:** compound selection — parliamentary election where citizens are directly represented, delegated state representation where member polities remain principals, and appointment / approval for bounded executive and judicial offices.
- **Tenure:** fixed medium cycles for directly representative institutions, bounded multi-year terms for commissions / colleges, and staggered tenure for courts and review bodies.
- **Office status:** directly representative and executive roles are usually salaried and full-time; delegated member-state seats remain office-linked; courts, agencies, and secretariats are salaried professional work.
- **Operator:** secretariat / commission / standards bodies / shared court / review agencies.
- **Finance:** assessed contributions, shared budget instruments, or narrow common revenues tied to limited competences.
- **Artifacts:** competence ledger, subsidiarity reviews, common rule dockets, compliance reports, mutual-recognition criteria, dispute decisions.
- **Remedy / migration:** treaty-law review, infringement / compliance procedures, and bounded refusal grounds where member autonomy remains protected.

**Ideal job:** solve cross-border coordination problems that states cannot solve alone without creating an unlimited remote sovereign.

**Hard limit:** supranational institutions should govern spillovers and shared standards, not micromanage local delivery.

### 7. Global / planetary

**Ideal default:** a **constraint-setting and verification layer**, not a world state.

- **Legitimacy:** treaty consent, broad state participation, scientific credibility, plural representation, and affected-population visibility.
- **Selection:** state delegations for assemblies and conferences, appointment for secretariats and expert panels, and advisory participation channels rather than fake global executive elections.
- **Tenure:** bounded multi-year terms for secretariat heads, visible rotation or staggered renewal for committees and panels, and no open-ended elite permanence disguised as continuity.
- **Office status:** political negotiation remains mostly state-funded or office-linked delegation, while secretariat and expert-support work is salaried international public service rather than volunteer diplomacy in disguise.
- **Operator:** secretariats, scientific panels, financing facilities, registries, inspectors, and implementation-support networks.
- **Finance:** assessed contributions, burden-sharing formulas, climate / risk funds, and pooled response mechanisms.
- **Artifacts:** targets, commitments registries, MRV outputs, compliance / exception notices, risk alerts, financing flows, affected-population complaint lanes where feasible.
- **Remedy / migration:** escalation through review, reputational pressure, club incentives, sanctions, funding conditions, and domestic incorporation rather than direct micromanagement.

**Ideal job:** set hard outer constraints for planetary risks and collective-action failures, verify behavior, and move resources toward under-capacity jurisdictions.

**Hard limit:** the global layer should rarely be the frontline operator; its legitimacy is strongest as a bounded constraint-and-verification architecture.

## Cross-cutting case: indigenous nations and non-territorial polities

Some legitimate governments do not fit neatly onto a territorial ladder. The ideal answer is **not** to force them into municipal or national templates. The ideal answer is to treat them as first-class governments or governing bodies with explicit interfaces:

- own charter / membership / representation rules,
- published jurisdiction and coercion ceilings,
- recognized compact paths with territorial governments,
- remedy and continuity duties for people caught between systems.

That is how the archive’s polycentric logic avoids both assimilation and romantic exemption.

---

## Three anti-patterns the ideal stack must avoid

### 1. Romantic localism
Giving micro-local or municipal bodies moral prestige without money, staff, or backstop capacity turns “community control” into abandonment.

### 2. Remote optimization
Giving higher levels clean dashboards but weak contestability creates elegant coordination without lived remedy.

### 3. Functional-authority sprawl
Solving every spillover with a new special district or authority creates invisible government unless mandates, budgets, and appeal lanes are brutally legible.

---

## Practical design rule

When designing or reforming any scope, ask:

1. **Is the legitimacy engine fit for the discretion this scope holds?**
2. **Is the operating core strong enough to deliver without improvisation?**
3. **Does the finance model match the spillover and redistribution burden?**
4. **Can an outsider inspect who decided, who paid, and who is responsible next?**
5. **Can a harmed person force review or continuity without restarting from zero?**

If the answer to any of those is “no,” the government at that scope is not yet ideal; it is still missing a bundle component.
