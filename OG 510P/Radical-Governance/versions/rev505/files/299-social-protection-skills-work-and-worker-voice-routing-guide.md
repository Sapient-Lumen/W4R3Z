# 299 — Social Protection / Skills / Work / Worker-Voice Routing Guide

**Purpose:** turn the archive’s social-protection / skills / work / worker-voice family into a readable stack so income security, capability formation, labor-market regulation, and countervailing power stop competing for the same conceptual role.

**Why this memo exists:** `64`, `68`, `69`, and `175` all surface for queries like “economic security”, “skills for work”, “school-to-work transitions”, “decent work”, “worker power”, “how to reduce precarity”, or “what institutions prevent labor markets from becoming domination machines”. They describe one family from different layers. The archive should name those handoffs explicitly rather than invite flattening.

**Evidence anchors:** social protection floors and just-transition coverage expansion [BIB-ILO-SOCIAL-PROTECTION-HUB-2026] [BIB-ILO-SPF-R202-2024]; education as a lifelong right and system-level capability project [BIB-UNESCO-EDUCATION-HUB-2026] [BIB-UNESCO-ED2030-2015]; integrated, stakeholder-engaged skills governance across education and labor-market institutions [BIB-OECD-SKILLS-OUTLOOK-2025] [BIB-OECD-ADULT-SKILLS-WORK-HUB-2026]; decent work, labor administration, and social dialogue as institutional pillars rather than side constraints [BIB-ILO-DECENT-WORK-SDG-HUB-2026] [BIB-ILO-LABOUR-ADMIN-HUB-2025] [BIB-ILO-SOCIAL-DIALOGUE-HUB-2025].

## Canonical reading order

### 1. Start with `64-social-protection-and-benefits-governance.md`
Use `64` when the question is:
- how a polity guarantees subsistence, income continuity, or minimum material security,
- how benefits eligibility, recertification, payment, suspension, and recovery become auditable,
- how administrative burden, delay, and proof burdens create poverty by procedure,
- or how shocks in work, family status, disability, or migration status should not instantly turn into destitution.

`64` is the **income-security / benefits-delivery front door**.

### 2. Move to `68-education-and-skills-governance.md`
Use `68` when you need:
- the institutional pipeline for enrollment, placement, safeguarding, assessment, credentials, and transitions,
- a governance frame for lifelong learning, reskilling, TVET, and school-to-work pathways,
- the rules that decide who gets access to learning supports and recognized qualifications,
- or a way to treat education and skills as capability-building infrastructure instead of only a social sector.

`68` is the **learning / capability-formation / credentialing front door**.

### 3. Use `69-labor-and-work-governance.md`
Use `69` when the real issue is:
- wages, hours, safety, misclassification, retaliation, or labor-law enforcement,
- how inspection, complaint, sanction, and remedy systems should actually work,
- how algorithmic management, scheduling, payroll, and employment-status decisions become contestable,
- or how the state disciplines labor markets so “decent work” becomes operational rather than rhetorical.

`69` is the **labor-standards / enforcement / employment-relationship front door**.

### 4. Use `175-worker-voice-sectoral-bargaining-codetermination.md`
Use `175` when the question becomes:
- how workers gain durable countervailing power instead of only ex-post complaint rights,
- how sectoral bargaining, works councils, codetermination, or cooperative pathways change market governance,
- how institutions should structure consultation, bargaining coverage, and protected workplace voice,
- or how a polity keeps labor-market governance from collapsing into unilateral employer discretion.

`175` is the **worker-voice / bargaining / codetermination institutional-carrier memo**.

## Confusion boundaries

### `64` vs `68`
- `64` is about **income continuity, eligibility, and benefits delivery**.
- `68` is about **learning access, educational placement, safeguarding, and credentialing**.

### `68` vs `69`
- `68` governs **capability formation and credential recognition before and alongside work**.
- `69` governs **employment relations, labor standards, inspection, and remedy inside the world of work**.

### `69` vs `175`
- `69` tells you **how labor-law rights, inspections, sanctions, and recoveries should run**.
- `175` tells you **which institutions create worker voice, bargaining coverage, and governance power before a case reaches enforcement**.

### `64` / `68` / `69` / `175` vs `123`
- `64`, `68`, `69`, and `175` are this cluster’s **social floor / capability / work / worker-voice stack**.
- `123-association-and-collective-power.md` remains the **broader cross-domain collective-power neighbor** when the question is general protected association, collective filing, or negotiation duty outside specifically labor-market institutions.

### `68` vs `203`
- `68` handles **education, assessment, and credential issuance inside the learning system**.
- `203-mutual-recognition-of-credentials-licenses-and-status.md` is the right neighbor when the real issue is **portability and recognition of already-issued credentials across borders or jurisdictions**.

### `64` / `68` / `69` vs `82` / `140`
- `64`, `68`, and `69` are **domain front doors**.
- `82-service-standards-and-minimum-service-guarantees.md` and `140-queues-and-prioritization-integrity.md` remain the **shared operating substrate** when the problem is really queues, time budgets, urgent-category handling, or service-level failure across domains.

## Retrieval guidance (what to cite when)

- Cite `64` for **benefits eligibility, payment continuity, recertification, suspension, recoupment, administrative burden, and interim protection against deprivation**.
- Cite `68` for **enrollment, placement, accommodations, discipline, safeguarding, assessments, lifelong-learning pathways, and credential-award / denial decisions**.
- Cite `69` for **wage-and-hour enforcement, workplace safety, misclassification, retaliation, inspection powers, sanctions, and algorithmic management in work settings**.
- Cite `175` for **sectoral bargaining design, works councils, codetermination, bargaining coverage, protected consultation rights, and institutional worker voice beyond individual complaints**.

## Safe merge rule for this cluster

Do **not** flatten this family into one general memo on “human capital”, “social policy”, or “future of work”.
The safer pattern is:
1. keep `64` as the income-security / benefits-delivery front door,
2. keep `68` as the learning / capability / credentialing front door,
3. keep `69` as the labor-standards / enforcement front door,
4. keep `175` as the worker-voice / bargaining / codetermination institutional-carrier specialization,
5. keep `123`, `203`, `82`, and `140` as named collective-power, portability, and operating neighbors,
6. and use this memo as the bridge that names the roles.

That preserves distinct retrieval hooks while sharply reducing reader confusion.
