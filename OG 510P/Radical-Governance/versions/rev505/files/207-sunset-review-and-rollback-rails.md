# Sunset, Review, and Rollback Rails for Laws & Regulations

**Goal:** keep rules *fit for purpose* by making review routine, sunsetting meaningful, and rollback possible when evidence shows harm or failure.

This memo defines a small, portable set of artifacts and triggers for **regulatory lifecycle governance**.

---

## 1) Why rules accumulate and rot

Rules degrade through:

- **drift:** world changes; rule doesn’t
- **implementation gaps:** rule exists on paper but not in practice
- **capture:** rule optimizes for incumbents
- **overlap:** multiple instruments regulate the same thing
- **emergency hangover:** temporary powers persist

OECD reviews repeatedly highlight the role of **ex post evaluation** and systematic review mechanisms (including sunset and evaluation clauses).

---

## 2) Core artifacts

### 2.1 Rule Register (RR)
A public index of binding rules with:

- owner institution
- purpose statement (one paragraph)
- scope + affected populations
- operational metrics (intended outcomes + burden indicators)
- review date and trigger conditions
- linked impact assessments and reviews

### 2.2 Post‑Implementation Review (PIR)
A standard review packet created after deployment:

- what outcomes happened vs predicted
- compliance and enforcement reality
- burden distribution
- unintended effects
- recommendation: keep / amend / repeal

(UK evaluation guidance can be used as a template for PIR discipline.)

### 2.3 Sunset Continuation Report (SCR)
If a rule has a sunset clause, continuation requires an SCR that:

- demonstrates continued necessity
- shows alternatives considered
- identifies what will be different (simplification or safeguards)
- proposes the next review date

Sunset clauses are often discussed as safeguards, but they can fail without real review and continuation discipline.

---

## 3) Trigger system (when review is mandatory)

A rule must be reviewed if any trigger fires:

- **T1:** metric failure (missed outcomes; high error rates)
- **T2:** burden spike (time/cost burden exceeds threshold)
- **T3:** rights signal (credible rights-impact complaints)
- **T4:** market structure shift (concentration, lock-in)
- **T5:** incident (harm event; scandal; audit finding)
- **T6:** emergency expiry window (automatic)

This matches “keeping rules fit for purpose” practices that embed review requirements into the law.

---

## 4) Rollback protocol (how to undo safely)

### 4.1 Rollback Receipt (RBR)
When a rollback is proposed, publish:

- what’s being rolled back (RR-ID)
- why (evidence summary, one page)
- rollback plan (what replaces it; how services continue)
- who bears transition costs
- redress plan for those harmed by the prior rule

### 4.2 Two rollback lanes

**Lane A: Normal repeal/amend**
- standard legislative process
- PIR + consultation
- transition plan

**Lane B: Fast rollback (high harm / clear failure)**
- temporary suspension with strict bounds
- immediate independent review scheduling
- mandatory publication of reasons
- automatic sunset on the suspension itself (to prevent abuse)

---

## 5) Post‑legislative scrutiny integration

Legislatures should maintain an explicit **post‑legislative scrutiny** schedule, prioritizing high-impact rules.

(Parliamentary guidance and scrutiny manuals provide operational rationale and patterns.)

---

## 6) Minimal implementation checklist

- [ ] Publish Rule Register (RR) with stable IDs
- [ ] Require PIR for major rules and programs
- [ ] Allow evaluation/sunset clauses by default for high-uncertainty policies
- [ ] Make SCR mandatory to continue any sunsetted rule
- [ ] Wire triggers to auto-start review
- [ ] Support fast rollback lane with bounded safeguards
- [ ] Publish all review/rollback artifacts by default
