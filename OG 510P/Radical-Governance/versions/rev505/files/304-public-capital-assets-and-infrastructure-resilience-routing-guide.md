# 304 — Public Capital / Assets / Infrastructure Resilience Routing Guide

**Purpose:** turn the archive’s public-capital / assets / infrastructure-resilience family into a readable stack so capital allocation, project integrity, asset maintenance, resilience compacts, and cyber-critical continuity stop competing for the same conceptual role.

**Why this memo exists:** `97`, `138`, `225`, `48`, `154`, and `59` all surface for queries like “how should governments govern public assets over their full life”, “how do you stop infrastructure from decaying into emergency repair politics”, “how should major projects, maintenance, and resilience fit together”, “where should asset registers sit in the governance stack”, or “how do infrastructure and cyber continuity relate without collapsing into one memo”. They describe one family from different layers. The archive should name those handoffs explicitly rather than invite flattening.

**Evidence anchors:** OECD’s current infrastructure-governance hub and 2025 Infrastructure Governance Indicators keep the frame on whole-of-life governance—plan, prioritise, fund, budget, deliver, operate, and monitor infrastructure assets—rather than just project approval [BIB-OECD-INFRA-GOV-HUB-2026] [BIB-OECD-INFRA-IGI-2025]. The IMF’s current infrastructure-governance / PIMA materials still treat planning, allocation, and implementation institutions as one assessable cycle, with climate-responsive infrastructure now embedded through C-PIMA [BIB-IMF-PIMA-HUB-2026] [BIB-IMF-PIMA-2025]. The World Bank’s current InfraGov 2.0 framing still treats infrastructure governance as a modular, actionable assessment problem rather than a one-off policy statement [BIB-WB-INFRAGOV-2-2025]. GFDRR’s current resilient-infrastructure work and 2025 annual report continue to center lifecycle risk reduction, maintenance, emergency preparedness, business continuity, and integration of resilience into large-scale infrastructure investment [BIB-GFDRR-RESILIENT-INFRA] [BIB-GFDRR-AR-2025-INFRA].

## Canonical reading order

### 1. Start with `97-public-investment-and-capital-project-governance.md`
Use `97` when the question is:
- how governments should choose, gate, pause, or cancel capital projects,
- how public investment should stay contestable over its lifecycle,
- how stage gates, option value, and long-lived obligations should work,
- or how capital planning fits the archive’s person-facing model of legitimacy.

`97` is the **broad capital-allocation / stage-gate / lifecycle-governance front door**.

### 2. Move to `138-public-investment-and-capital-projects-integrity.md`
Use `138` when you need:
- anti-capture discipline for large projects,
- change-order controls,
- benefit-drift detection,
- or a way to make megaproject optimism bias, scope creep, and contractor gaming legible.

`138` is the **large-project integrity / change-order / benefits-realization specialization**.

### 3. Use `225-public-assets-maintenance-and-capital-planning-rails.md`
Use `225` when the real issue is:
- deferred maintenance,
- lifecycle accounting,
- condition-based planning,
- maintenance backlogs as hidden liabilities,
- or keeping capex politics from crowding out operations and repair.

`225` is the **maintenance / lifecycle-planning / deferred-maintenance-liability specialization**.

### 4. Use `48-asset-and-infrastructure-register.md`
Use `48` when the real problem is:
- the asset substrate itself,
- stable identifiers and condition grades,
- owner/operator responsibility,
- dependency mapping,
- or joinable visibility across projects, contracts, incidents, and service obligations.

`48` is the **asset-identity / condition / join-key substrate**.

### 5. Use `154-critical-infrastructure-resilience-compacts.md`
Use `154` when you need:
- cross-scope resilience compacts,
- operator / regulator / emergency-management handoffs,
- minimum service floors under stress,
- mutual-aid activation logic,
- or interdependency-aware continuity design across energy, telecoms, water, transport, and health systems.

`154` is the **cross-scope resilience-compact / continuity-governance specialization**.

### 6. Use `59-critical-infrastructure-and-cyber-resilience-governance.md`
Use `59` when the problem is really:
- cyber / OT risk in critical services,
- outage reporting and follow-through,
- vendor accountability in essential infrastructure,
- or how to govern digital failure in systems people materially depend on.

`59` is the **cyber / OT / outage-governance specialization**.

## Neighbor map and confusion boundaries

### Route to `302-fiscal-state-budget-revenue-and-transfers-routing-guide.md` when:
- the real issue is public-finance architecture,
- the question is mainly about budgets, revenue, transfers, or fiscal transparency,
- or capital planning is secondary to the broader money-power design problem.

`302` remains the **fiscal-state neighbor**.

### Route to `294-climate-resilience-infrastructure-retreat-and-risk-finance-routing-guide.md` when:
- adaptation, catastrophe finance, retreat, or climate-risk governance is central,
- resilience is being asked as a climate-governance question rather than an asset-lifecycle question,
- or the real handoff is between protection-in-place, relocation, and catastrophe-transfer design.

`294` remains the **climate-resilience neighbor**.

### Route to `303-productive-state-utilities-market-structure-and-industrial-resilience-routing-guide.md` when:
- the issue is really utility regulation, market structure, sector strategy, or industrial resilience,
- infrastructure assets are important mainly because they sit inside essential sectors,
- or the key question is how states should govern strategic industries rather than how they should govern capital assets as such.

`303` remains the **productive-state / sector-governance neighbor**.

### Route to `13-regulation-utilities-and-soes.md` or `137-critical-infrastructure-and-utilities-integrity.md` when:
- the issue is utility obligations, tariff logic, concession discipline, or utility-integrity controls,
- rather than capital-planning and asset-lifecycle design across sectors.

These remain the **regulated-utilities neighbors**.

## Why these memos stay separate

Do **not** merge this family into one giant infrastructure memo yet. The archive still benefits from separate retrieval hooks for:
- **capital choice and stage-gate legitimacy** (`97`),
- **megaproject integrity and change-order discipline** (`138`),
- **maintenance and lifecycle liability** (`225`),
- **asset identity and dependency substrate** (`48`),
- **cross-scope resilience compacts and mutual aid** (`154`),
- **cyber / OT continuity and outage governance** (`59`).

The bridge exists to stop readers from having to infer those boundaries, not to erase them.
