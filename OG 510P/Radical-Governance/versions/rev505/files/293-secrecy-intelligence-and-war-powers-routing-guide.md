# 293 — Secrecy, Intelligence, and War Powers Routing Guide

**Purpose:** turn the archive’s secrecy / intelligence / war-powers cluster into a readable stack so ordinary secrecy discipline, intelligence oversight, external use-of-force governance, and adjacent access-to-documents / emergency-law questions stop competing for the same conceptual role.

**Why this memo exists:** `77`, `168`, and `222` all surface for “national security governance”, “classified power”, “intelligence oversight”, “secrecy abuse”, or “war powers” queries because they describe one family from different layers. Nearby `200` is the requester-side openness neighbor when the issue is withholding and review, and `23` / `286` are adjacent emergency-powers memos when the problem becomes a broader exception-law question. The archive should name those handoffs explicitly rather than invite flattening.

**Evidence anchors:** enduring legality and oversight baselines [BIB-UN-CHARTER] [BIB-ICCPR] [BIB-COE-NS-OVERSIGHT-2015] [BIB-VENICE-SECURITY-2007] [BIB-VENICE-SIGINT-2015]; current surveillance / democratic-oversight / armed-conflict framing [BIB-OHCHR-PRIVACY-REPORTS] [BIB-OSCE-SECURITY-OVERSIGHT-2025] [BIB-ICRC-IHL-CHALLENGES-2025].

## Canonical reading order

### 1. Start with `77-sensitive-information-and-secrecy-governance.md`
Use `77` when the question is:
- what secrecy is allowed to do at all,
- how withholding, classification, review, and declassification stay bounded,
- and how the state publishes contestable receipts even when payloads cannot be public.

`77` is the **canonical secrecy and withholding front door**.

### 2. Move to `168-intelligence-and-secrecy-governance.md`
Use `168` when you need:
- intelligence-service oversight,
- surveillance authorization and auditability,
- classified budgets, liaison risks, vendor capture, or oversight-with-access,
- or the specific governance problem of secret institutions becoming a parallel state.

`168` is the **intelligence and security-service specialization**.

### 3. Use `222-war-powers-and-external-use-of-force-governance.md`
Use `222` when the real issue is:
- external use of force,
- armed-conflict participation,
- security assistance with foreseeable abuse risk,
- or harm accounting and review around war-authority decisions.

`222` is the **external-force and war-powers specialization**.

### 4. Use `200-freedom-of-information-and-access-to-official-documents-rails.md` when the problem is requester-side access to documents
Use `200` when you need:
- ATI / FOI filing and routing,
- refusal receipts,
- public-document access clocks,
- or a contestable lane for ordinary access-to-documents disputes rather than the full design of secrecy governance.

`200` is the **requester-side openness and access-to-documents neighbor**.

### 5. Use `23-emergency-governance-and-exceptions.md` or `286-emergency-powers-stack-and-exit-governance-guide.md` when the problem becomes a broader exception-law question
Use `23` / `286` when you need:
- a general emergency-governance frame,
- derogation and renewal structure,
- exit and rollback discipline,
- or the question is no longer mainly secrecy/intelligence/war powers but emergency state power as such.

`23` is the **broad emergency-governance neighbor**, and `286` is the **cluster-routing bridge** for that adjacent emergency family.

## Confusion boundaries

### `77` vs `168`
- `77` is the **general secrecy-governance front door** for withholding, review, declassification, and contestable secrecy receipts.
- `168` is the **security-service specialization** for intelligence powers, surveillance, classified budgets, and oversight institutions with secure access.

### `168` vs `222`
- `168` governs **secret domestic / foreign intelligence institutions and intrusive collection authorities**.
- `222` governs **external-force decisions, continuing force authorizations, security assistance, and civilian-harm review**.

### `77` vs `200`
- `77` is about **how secrecy itself is bounded and reviewed**.
- `200` is about **how a person requests official documents, receives a refusal, and contests it**.

### `222` vs `23` / `286`
- `222` is the **war-powers and external-force specialization**.
- `23` / `286` govern the **broader emergency / exception family**, including non-war emergency powers and exit discipline.

### `168` / `222` vs `115`
- `168` and `222` govern **high-secrecy institutions and authorities**.
- `115-information-integrity-and-record-interfaces.md` remains the **record / provenance / correction substrate** that those domains still depend on.

## Retrieval guidance (what to cite when)

- Cite `77` for **withholding receipts, classification scope, declassification review, oversight access, and contestable secrecy-by-design**.
- Cite `168` for **intelligence oversight, surveillance authorization, classified budgets, vendor/liaison risk, and anti-parallel-state design**.
- Cite `222` for **war-authority receipts, renewal packets, civilian-harm accounting, security-assistance risk, and secrecy-disciplined external-force governance**.
- Cite `200` for **ATI / FOI filing, refusal routes, access clocks, proactive disclosure, and requester-side document access**.
- Cite `23` / `286` for **general exception-law structure, declarations, renewals, derogations, and exit discipline**.

## Safe merge rule for this cluster

Do **not** flatten this family into one memo on “national security governance”.
The safer pattern is:
1. keep `77` as the general secrecy-governance front door,
2. keep `168` as the intelligence / surveillance specialization,
3. keep `222` as the war-powers / external-force specialization,
4. keep `200` as the requester-side access-to-documents neighbor,
5. keep `23` / `286` as the adjacent emergency-powers family,
6. and use this memo as the bridge that names the roles.

That preserves distinct retrieval hooks while making the cluster substantially easier to enter.
