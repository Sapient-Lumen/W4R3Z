## When deliberation is the right tool
Use representative deliberation when:
- the problem has **high value conflict**, deep uncertainty, or long time horizons
- technical expertise matters but legitimacy is contested
- tradeoffs affect different groups unevenly (distributional politics)

Do **not** use it when:
- rights must be protected regardless of popularity (use courts/rights rails)
- the decision is purely operational with clear performance criteria
- the state cannot credibly commit to a response pathway

OECD’s guidance frames representative deliberative processes as a complement to representative institutions, with repeatable good‑practice principles.

## The non‑negotiables (anti‑theater invariants)
1) **Representative selection**: a credible civic lottery / stratification plan with publishable draw receipts.
2) **Balanced learning**: evidence is plural, contestable, and disclosed with provenance.
3) **Skilled facilitation**: independence + process integrity.
4) **Decision connection**: a binding, time‑bounded **Response Duty** that can be audited.
5) **Public accountability**: a minimal public log that enables evaluation and future replay.

(See also OECD good practice principles and institutionalisation options.)

## Minimal artifact set (keep it tight)
The point is *joinability*, not paperwork.

### 1) Assembly Charter (`CA-CHARTER-*`)
Publish:
- convening authority + legal basis
- **mandate** (question, scope, constraints, non‑negotiables)
- decision rights (advisory vs. binding; what triggers what)
- budget, staff, contractor roles, conflicts policy
- timeline + publication plan
- how recommendations will be translated into proposals

### 2) Selection & draw receipts (`CA-DR-*`)
- sampling frame and exclusions (with justification)
- stratification variables + targets
- randomness source and reproducible draw receipt

Use the selection integrity pattern in `119-selection-and-sortition-integrity.md`.

### 3) Witness & evidence packets (`CA-WPK-*`)
- list of witnesses (experts, stakeholders, affected people)
- written submissions + disclosure of interests
- a **balanced brief** that records viewpoints and uncertainty (not “one true narrative”)

### 4) Recommendations (`CA-REC-*`)
- recommendations in participants’ own words
- vote/consensus method and result
- minority reports (if any)
- explicit tradeoffs (who wins/loses; time horizon; risks)

### 5) Response duty & binding receipts (`CA-RESP-*`)
A public response must:
- accept / modify / reject each recommendation
- give reasons tied to evidence, constraints, and rights
- specify implementation plan OR the next decision gate
- include a review date (and rollback lane if harms appear)

This is the core anti-theater safeguard: **no charter without response rails**.

### 6) Evaluation & learning (`CA-EVAL-*`)
Publish:
- representativeness achieved vs target
- process quality survey summary (safe aggregate)
- implementation follow‑through status

OECD has published evaluation guidance for representative deliberative processes.

## Process blueprint (4 phases)
This is the smallest viable lifecycle.

1) **Frame**
- define the question, constraints, and what “success” means
- pre‑register what decisions can follow and who holds the pen

2) **Select**
- run the civic lottery with reproducible receipts
- offer accessibility supports (care, stipend, translation, disability access)

3) **Learn + deliberate**
- balanced brief + contested evidence handling
- facilitation that prevents domination and protects minority voice
- keep *internal* deliberation private if needed, but publish *external* inputs and outcomes

4) **Bind**
- response duty executed with clocked receipts
- implementation converted into change packets (`REL/CHG` discipline) and evaluated

Involve and other practitioners describe a common learning→deliberation→recommendation model and practical setup guidance.

## Failure modes & countermeasures (tight list)

### A) Capture via framing
**Symptom:** narrow mandate that pre‑chooses outcomes.
- Counter: require **alternatives set** + explicit constraints + public framing rationale.

### B) Capture via evidence curation
**Symptom:** stacked witness list; missing lived experience.
- Counter: plural witness selection; published submissions; disclosure of interests; a “contest lane” for the balanced brief.

### C) Performative consultation
**Symptom:** recommendations disappear.
- Counter: response duty with deadlines; follow‑through ledger hooks (`55-oversight-findings-and-response-register.md`).

### D) Legitimacy backlash
**Symptom:** “random people can’t decide this.”
- Counter: clarify decision rights; embed within legislative or executive gates; publish why this tool is used.

### E) Accessibility exclusion
**Symptom:** low-income / disabled / caregivers underrepresented.
- Counter: pay, care, access supports; accessibility as a hard requirement.

## Institutionalising deliberation (patterns)
- **Standing panel**: recurring citizen panel attached to a ministry/committee with clear triggers.
- **Triggerable assembly**: auto‑convene on threshold events (e.g., constitutional change, emergency review, major infrastructure siting).
- **Hybrid**: online inputs + representative deliberation, with a clean boundary between “input” and “decision”.

OECD’s work on new democratic institutions discusses options for institutionalising deliberative processes.
