# 326 — International Sanctions, Listings, Countermeasures, Humanitarian Exemptions, and Delisting Rails

**Purpose:** stop international coercive measures from collapsing into one opaque bucket by forcing sanctions, listings, delisting, exemptions, countermeasures, and domestic implementation into typed, reviewable lanes.

**Why this memo exists:** the archive now has seams for deciding **what kind of international instrument exists** (`322`), **when a treaty becomes domestically effective** (`321`), **how review cycles run** (`323`), **how commitments change over time** (`324`), and **which international forum can hear a complaint or dispute** (`325`). What it still lacked was the distinct coercion layer after breach, threat, or security designation: **who may impose international restrictive measures, on what authority, with what listing record, with what humanitarian carve-outs, with what delisting or exemption path, and under what limits countermeasures may be used without turning compliance pressure into lawless drift**.

**Evidence anchors:** the current United Nations Security Council materials are the clearest operational baseline because they make targeted-sanctions design public: the ISIL (Da'esh) & Al-Qaida regime page states that targeted sanctions there are an asset freeze, travel ban, and arms embargo; the list is publicly available in PDF / XML / HTML; narrative summaries of reasons for listing are published; and further list changes are posted immediately after Committee decisions [BIB-UNSC-1267-LIST-MATERIALS-2026]. The current Ombudsperson and Focal Point materials matter because they show that delisting and exemption are not optional afterthoughts: the Ombudsperson is independent and impartial, receives delisting requests for the ISIL / Al-Qaida list, works under the current Annex II procedure in resolution 2734 (2024), and the Committee has not yet overturned an Ombudsperson recommendation; the Focal Point exists for other Security Council sanctions lists and current Security Council pages also note the 2024 delisting-procedure update for non-1267 committees [BIB-UNSC-OMB] [BIB-UNSC-OMB-PROC] [BIB-UNSC-FOCAL-POINT-DELISTING-2026] [BIB-UNSC-DELISTING-UPDATE-2744-2024]. Humanitarian carve-outs need their own anchor because the Security Council’s current materials show that the resolution 2664 framework is a live operational control, with implementation notices and a continued humanitarian exemption for the ISIL / Al-Qaida regime affirmed in resolution 2761 (2024) [BIB-UNSC-1267-HUMANITARIAN-2024] [BIB-UNSC-2664-IAN-2024]. The International Law Commission’s Articles on State Responsibility supply the countermeasure baseline because countermeasures are allowed only to induce compliance, must be temporary and proportionate, may not affect the prohibition on force, fundamental human rights, or humanitarian obligations, and must generally be preceded by notice and an offer to negotiate [BIB-UN-ILC-STATE-RESPONSIBILITY-2001]. OHCHR’s current sanctions-and-humanitarian note is also a useful cautionary anchor because it warns that poorly designed humanitarian exemptions and over-compliance can seriously undermine emergency response in practice [BIB-OHCHR-SANCTIONS-HUMANITARIAN-2022].

## Core claim

A safe international-coercion architecture does **not** begin with “what punishment do we want?”
It begins with six prior public questions:
1. **What kind of measure is this — Security Council sanction, treaty-based restrictive measure, countermeasure, retorsion, export control, or domestic implementing act?**
2. **What legal basis authorizes it, and who owns the designation or non-compliance finding?**
3. **What public reasons, evidence classes, and narrative summaries are available without defeating legitimate confidentiality?**
4. **What delisting, exemption, or mistaken-identity lane exists from day one?**
5. **What humanitarian, basic-needs, due-process, and over-compliance protections prevent collateral governance failure?**
6. **What condition ends the measure — compliance, time expiry, delisting, court order, political decision, or regime termination?**

The archive should therefore stop using “sanctions” as though it were one thing.
A Security Council listing, a state countermeasure, a domestic procurement debarment, an export-control denial, an asset freeze with humanitarian exceptions, and a wartime coercive act are **not** interchangeable.

The design target is simple:
- **type the coercive instrument before it is imposed,**
- **publish a listing / reasons / exemption packet rather than leaving targets to infer the rule from frozen accounts,**
- **treat delisting and humanitarian carve-outs as core architecture, not reputational add-ons,**
- **keep countermeasures temporary, proportional, and compliance-oriented,**
- **and force domestic implementation plus private-sector screening behavior back under public accountability.**

## Design rules

### A. Type the coercive instrument before acting
Do not let governments say only “sanctions were imposed”.
A public record should say whether the measure is:
- a **UN Security Council listing / sanctions measure**,
- a **regional or treaty-system restrictive measure**,
- a **countermeasure responding to an internationally wrongful act**,
- a **retorsion / lawful unfriendly act**,
- a **domestic implementing measure carrying an international obligation into local law**,
- or some other category with its own authority and review path.

Different types trigger different legal limits, different remedies, and different termination conditions.

### B. Every designation needs a public reasons packet even where full evidence stays protected
For targeted listings, publish at least:
- the listing authority,
- the criteria invoked,
- a narrative summary of reasons,
- the date of effect,
- the measures applied,
- the contact or filing lane for delisting / correction / exemption,
- and the next review trigger.

The archive rejects “discoverability by bank failure” where the first notice is an account freeze.

### C. Delisting and mistaken-identity correction must exist from day one
A sanctions regime is incomplete if it can list but cannot quickly:
- correct false or mistaken identification,
- hear a delisting request,
- process basic-needs or travel exemptions,
- and show who decides.

The architecture should visibly distinguish:
- **Ombudsperson / independent review lanes**,
- **Focal-point / committee lanes**,
- and **domestic judicial or administrative review lanes**.

### D. Humanitarian exemptions must be operational, not rhetorical
A humanitarian carve-out is not serious unless real operators can tell:
- who qualifies,
- what transactions are covered,
- what notice or paperwork is required,
- how fast answers arrive,
- and what safe harbor exists against good-faith delivery.

Exemption text without implementation guidance just shifts risk onto banks, vendors, and aid actors.

### E. Countermeasures are for inducing compliance, not for permanent punishment
Countermeasures should be treated as a narrow lane with visible limits:
- they aim to induce compliance,
- they are temporary,
- they should permit resumption of the suspended obligation as far as possible,
- they must be proportionate,
- and they should generally follow notice plus an offer to negotiate.

Do not narrate indefinite coercion as though it were still a temporary countermeasure.

### F. Human rights, humanitarian floors, and force prohibitions do not disappear inside the word “countermeasure”
The archive treats countermeasure talk as invalid if it tries to bypass:
- the prohibition on the threat or use of force,
- obligations protecting fundamental human rights,
- humanitarian obligations prohibiting reprisals,
- or other peremptory norms.

### G. Adjudication and countermeasures must not run on parallel mythologies
Where a binding forum is seized in good faith, the archive prefers visible rules for suspension, narrowing, or termination of coercive measures rather than pretending adjudication and pressure campaigns do not interact.

### H. Domestic implementation must stay publicly owned
When international restrictive measures are implemented domestically, publish a joined packet showing:
- the international source,
- the domestic legal basis,
- the agencies and private actors expected to implement,
- the local contest path,
- the exemption / licensing path,
- and the termination or update trigger.

The archive rejects the governance pattern where private compliance vendors silently become the real law.

### I. Over-compliance is a governance defect, not merely market caution
If banks, platforms, universities, NGOs, or logistics providers systematically exceed what the legal instrument requires because the public rule is too vague or the exemption pathway is unusable, that is a design failure.
The fix is not only “be braver”; it is better publication, better safe harbors, and faster authoritative answers.

## Instrument families the archive should keep distinct

### 1. Security Council targeted sanctions and listings
Typical questions:
- What measures attach to the listing?
- Is there a public narrative summary?
- Is the path for delisting through an Ombudsperson, the Focal Point, or the committee itself?
- What exemptions are available?

### 2. Other Security Council sanctions-committee regimes
Typical questions:
- Which committee owns the list?
- Does the petitioner go through the Focal Point?
- Have the 2024 delisting-procedure updates taken effect for this regime yet?
- What exemptions and reporting duties apply?

### 3. State countermeasures and lawful responsive measures
Typical questions:
- What internationally wrongful act is being invoked?
- Has notice been given and negotiation offered?
- Is the measure proportionate and temporary?
- Would the measure interfere with human-rights, humanitarian, force, or dispute-settlement obligations?

### 4. Domestic implementation and licensing / exemption systems
Typical questions:
- What domestic law carries the international measure?
- Which ministry, regulator, customs body, or bank-supervision authority answers questions?
- How are humanitarian, educational, medical, or legal-service carve-outs handled?
- How quickly are corrections and licenses issued?

### 5. Screening, vendor, and over-compliance layers
Typical questions:
- Is a private intermediary adding non-public rules?
- Are institutions denying activity that is actually permitted?
- Is there a public safe-harbor or authoritative FAQ?
- Who owns correction when private filtering overshoots the law?

## Failure signatures

Watch for these recurring pathologies:
- **“Sanctions were imposed” with no typed authority or measure list.** → category-collapse failure.
- **The first notice is an account freeze or shipment rejection.** → discovery-by-harm failure.
- **Narrative summaries, exemption rules, or delisting lanes are absent or unusable.** → no-exit architecture failure.
- **A humanitarian exemption exists in theory, but operators still cannot tell whether food, medicine, cloud services, or legal fees are permitted.** → carve-out-on-paper failure.
- **A state labels a long-running pressure campaign a countermeasure without naming the breach, notice, proportionality logic, or end condition.** → pseudo-countermeasure failure.
- **Domestic agencies and private firms implement a restrictive measure more aggressively than the public instrument requires.** → shadow-compliance failure.
- **A listing survives even though the reasons packet, review trigger, or mistaken-identity correction path is stale.** → frozen-list inertia failure.
- **A coercive measure continues unchanged while a binding dispute forum is seized and active, with no public account of how the two lanes interact.** → split-track opacity failure.

## Scope-sensitive defaults

### National
- **Default:** every internationally sourced restrictive measure should publish one domestic implementation packet plus one local contest / exemption lane.
- **Prefer:** named licensing or exemption authority with fast service clocks for humanitarian and mistaken-identity cases.
- **Guardrail:** do not outsource rule interpretation to private banks or vendors without a public correction path.

### Supranational / treaty-union
- **Default:** separate union-level listing authority from member-state implementation obligations and from member-state judicial remedies.
- **Prefer:** one joined matrix for designation, publication, exemption, delisting, and national execution.
- **Guardrail:** mixed competence must not become mixed accountability.

### Global
- **Default:** list authority, narrative summary, exemption path, and delisting path should all be visible on the same public surface.
- **Prefer:** humanitarian carve-outs accompanied by implementation notices and current FAQs, not only resolution text.
- **Guardrail:** do not let “global security” become a reason to abandon due process or permanent-review discipline.

### Municipal / regional / functional authorities
- **Default:** they usually should not invent independent international-sanctions classifications, only implement or contest within their mandate.
- **Prefer:** local front-line operators receive a plain-language packet explaining what is prohibited, what is permitted, and how to escalate edge cases.
- **Guardrail:** subnational implementers should never have to infer international law from rumor or vendor black boxes.

## One-sentence design test

An international-coercion system is well designed when an ordinary outsider can tell, without specialist folklore, **what measure exists, on what authority, against whom, for what publicly stated reason, with what humanitarian and due-process protections, through what correction or delisting path, and under what condition it ends.**
