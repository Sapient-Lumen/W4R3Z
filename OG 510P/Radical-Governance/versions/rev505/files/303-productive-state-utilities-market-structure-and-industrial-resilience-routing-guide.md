# 303 — Productive State / Utilities / Market Structure / Industrial Resilience Routing Guide

**Purpose:** turn the archive’s productive-state / utilities / market-structure / industrial-resilience family into a readable stack so public-interest regulation, energy transition governance, competition enforcement, structural antimonopoly, and industrial-policy / supply-chain strategy stop competing for the same conceptual role.

**Why this memo exists:** `13`, `65`, `94`, `153`, and `167` all surface for queries like “how to govern essential sectors without capture”, “how to do industrial policy without creating a cartel state”, “how competition and utility regulation should divide labor”, “how to govern energy transition as both a public utility and a strategic industry question”, or “how resilience policy should avoid becoming disguised protectionism”. They describe one family from different layers. The archive should name those handoffs explicitly rather than invite flattening.

**Evidence anchors:** OECD’s 2025 competition-trends work treats competition governance as an active enforcement-capacity and market-studies problem rather than a settled doctrine [BIB-OECD-COMPETITION-TRENDS-2025]; the 2024 OECD SOE Guidelines frame SOE governance around professionalised ownership, integrity, resilience, and a level playing field rather than informal ministerial control [BIB-OECD-SOE-2024]; UNIDO’s current industrial-policy framing treats industrial strategy as targeted structural transformation that coordinates trade, innovation, labour, and investment rather than a subsidy wishlist [BIB-UNIDO-INDUSTRIAL-POLICY-HUB-2026]; OECD’s 2025 supply-chain resilience review argues for agile, adaptable, and aligned resilience rather than indiscriminate relocalisation [BIB-OECD-SUPPLY-RESILIENCE-2025]; and the IEA’s current energy-security framing explicitly joins oil, gas, electricity, clean-energy supply chains, cyber risk, and extreme-weather disruption into one evolving security problem [BIB-IEA-ENERGY-SECURITY-2025].

## Canonical reading order

### 1. Start with `13-regulation-utilities-and-soes.md`
Use `13` when the question is:
- how essential or high-externality sectors should be regulated in the public interest,
- how utility, concession, and SOE power should stay legible and contestable,
- how independent regulators, tariff logic, universal-service floors, and professionalised state ownership should fit together,
- or how to govern monopoly-like services without confusing regulation, ownership, and enforcement.

`13` is the **broad public-interest regulation / utilities / SOE front door**.

### 2. Move to `65-energy-and-decarbonization-governance.md`
Use `65` when you need:
- energy-transition decisions that stay auditable instead of becoming extraction by green branding,
- rate, shutoff, subsidy, permitting, reliability, and decarbonization choices joined into one governable chain,
- public-interest governance for electricity, fuels, clean-energy deployment, and affordability,
- or a sector-specific answer to how energy security, affordability, and decarbonization can be governed together.

`65` is the **energy-transition / essential-energy-systems specialization**.

### 3. Use `94-competition-and-market-power-governance.md`
Use `94` when the real issue is:
- cartel, collusion, market-study, merger, or abuse-of-dominance enforcement,
- competition assessment across rules, licenses, and procurement,
- procedural fairness for competition authorities and enforceable market-power oversight,
- or how to stop private coercion from becoming a shadow government.

`94` is the **broad competition-governance / enforcement / market-power front door**.

### 4. Move to `167-antimonopoly-and-market-structure-governance.md`
Use `167` when you need:
- structural remedies, separation, interoperability, portability, and access duties,
- platform or network-power governance where contestability must be engineered rather than assumed,
- gatekeeper obligations, essential-facility logic, or switching-cost governance,
- or a sharper answer to how market structure should be redesigned when ordinary competition tools are too slow or too narrow.

`167` is the **structural antimonopoly / contestability-engineering specialization**.

### 5. Use `153-industrial-policy-and-supply-chain-resilience.md`
Use `153` when the question becomes:
- how missions, stockpiles, domestic-content rules, subsidies, and export controls should be governed,
- how industrial strategy can build capability and resilience without degenerating into capture,
- how supply-chain dependence, strategic sectors, and resilience tools should be made auditable,
- or how to govern economic-security choices as versioned public decisions instead of permanent exception logic.

`153` is the **industrial-policy / capability-building / supply-chain-resilience specialization**.

## Confusion boundaries

### `13` vs `94`
- `13` is the **public-interest regulation / utilities / SOE memo** for concessions, tariffs, service floors, regulator design, and state ownership.
- `94` is the **competition-governance memo** for cartels, mergers, abuse, market studies, and general competition assessment.

### `94` vs `167`
- `94` governs **general competition enforcement and procedural fairness**.
- `167` governs **structural antimonopoly and engineered contestability** such as interoperability, portability, access duties, line-of-business restrictions, and separation.

### `13` vs `65`
- `13` is the **cross-sector regulation / utilities / SOE front door**.
- `65` is the **energy-sector specialization** for reliability, affordability, decarbonization, permits, and shutoff / subsidy governance.

### `65` vs `153`
- `65` governs **the energy transition as a sector-specific public-utility and decarbonization problem**.
- `153` governs **cross-sector industrial strategy and supply-chain resilience** using missions, stockpiles, subsidies, export controls, or strategic capability-building.

### `153` vs `302`
- `153` is the **mission / capability / resilience memo** for industrial-policy choices and supply-chain strategy.
- `302-fiscal-state-budget-revenue-and-transfers-routing-guide.md` remains the right neighbor when the issue is really **budget architecture, tax expenditures, subsidy legibility, procurement allocation, or transfer design** rather than productive strategy itself.

### `13` / `65` / `153` vs `137` / `59`
- `13`, `65`, and `153` govern **sector strategy, regulation, public-interest obligations, and resilience choices**.
- `137-critical-infrastructure-and-utilities-integrity.md` and `59-critical-infrastructure-and-cyber-resilience-governance.md` remain the right neighbors when the issue is really **asset integrity, outage/restoration accountability, cyber posture, or operational incident follow-through**.

### `13` / `94` / `167` / `153` vs `157`
- This family governs **regulated sectors, market structure, and productive strategy**.
- `157-monetary-and-financial-stability-governance.md` remains the right neighbor when the problem is really **money, credit, payment rails, prudential supervision, or crisis liquidity**.

- Cite `65` for **energy security / affordability / decarbonization tradeoffs, shutoff/subsidy/permitting governance, energy-transition accountability, and auditable mitigation pipelines**.
- Cite `94` for **competition authority process, merger/control logic, bid-rigging prevention, cartels, abuse of dominance, and general market-power enforcement**.
- Cite `167` for **contestability engineering, interoperability/portability remedies, access duties, switching-cost governance, structural separation, and gatekeeper-style obligations**.
- Cite `153` for **mission dockets, industrial-policy capture controls, strategic stockpiles, supply-chain-risk governance, domestic-content / reshoring tools, and resilience policy design**.

## Safe merge rule for this cluster

Do **not** flatten this family into one general memo on “industrial policy”, “competition policy”, “economic security”, or “regulated sectors”.
The safer pattern is:
1. keep `13` as the broad public-interest regulation / utilities / SOE front door,
2. keep `65` as the energy-transition and essential-energy specialization,
3. keep `94` as the broad competition-governance / enforcement front door,
4. keep `167` as the structural antimonopoly / contestability-engineering specialization,
5. keep `153` as the industrial-policy / supply-chain-resilience specialization,
6. keep `137`, `59`, `302`, and `157` as named infrastructure, cyber, fiscal, and monetary neighbors,
7. and use this memo as the bridge that names the roles.

That preserves distinct retrieval hooks while sharply reducing reader confusion.
