# 338 — Scope-Selection Defaults: Rotation, Elections, Delegates, Appointments, and No Prestige Elections

**Cross-stack note:** use `306-scope-design-ideal-government-and-reference-bundles-guide.md` for the canonical route across the scope-design family. This memo is the scale-to-selection-mode / office-filling front door; `14` / `176` explain the cross-scope logic, `285` gives the default bundle language, `327` handles the scale-sensitive institutional form, `328` handles the scale-sensitive legitimacy channel, `336` handles the scale-sensitive constituency / who-counts choice, `337` handles the scale-sensitive decision-rule / threshold-choice choice, and `307` / `147` / `56` / `119` / `141` / `228` / `297` / `309` / `311` / `312` / `314` handle the deeper elections / selection / appointments / chamber / executive / formation substrate once the scale-fit selection question has been identified.

**Purpose:** answer the design question that remains after scope assignment, bundle design, form choice, legitimacy choice, constituency choice, and decision-rule choice: **how should offices at this scale usually be filled?**

**Why this memo exists:** `14`, `176`, `285`, and `327-337` explain what each scale owns, what shape it should take, how it should be legitimated, who should count there, and how it should decide. But the archive still needed one compact memo that says **scale should also change how officeholders are selected** — rotation, direct election, indirect election, delegated representation, merit appointment, or mixed selection.

**From-below:** this memo is a guardrail against false democracy in both directions. It helps stop small or technical offices from borrowing theatrical direct elections they cannot honestly justify, and it helps stop powerful general-purpose offices from being buried in opaque appointment or intergovernmental bargaining.

**Evidence anchors:** the ICA still states that primary cooperatives are democratically controlled by members with equal voting rights and elected representatives accountable to the membership [BIB-ICA-COOP-IDENTITY-2026]; the European Charter of Local Self-Government still says the right to local self-government shall be exercised by councils or assemblies composed of members freely elected by secret ballot on the basis of direct, equal, universal suffrage, while allowing executive organs responsible to them [BIB-COE-LOCALSELF-ART3-2026]; OECD’s metropolitan-governance survey still reports that about 65% of metropolitan governance bodies are composed of elected local-government officials while only about 10% are directly elected, which is a useful anchor for delegated metro selection as the normal case rather than a democratic failure by itself [BIB-OECD-METROGOV-SURVEY-2024]; OECD’s regional-governance typology still treats decentralised regional governments as self-governing entities with elected bodies [BIB-OECD-REGIONAL-GOV-ELECTED-2022]; EU treaty law still combines direct election of the European Parliament with intergovernmental nomination and parliamentary election/approval for the Commission [BIB-EURLEX-TEU-ART14-2026] [BIB-EURLEX-TEU-ART17-2026]; and the UN Charter still frames the General Assembly as a body of member-state delegations while the Secretary-General is appointed by the General Assembly upon the recommendation of the Security Council [BIB-UN-CHARTER] [BIB-UN-CHARTER-ART97-2026].

## Core claim

Every scale needs a **default selection mode**.

Not because one office-filling method should dominate everything, but because institutions become performative or shadowy when they cannot say which roles should be directly elected, which should be delegated, which should be appointed for competence or neutrality, and which should rotate or be filled by lot.

As scale changes, the fitting selection mode also changes:
- the smaller and more person-near the office, the more selection can lean on **rotation, lot, or short-loop election**;
- the more general-purpose, coercive, redistributive, and person-facing the office, the more selection should lean on **competitive election**;
- the more compound the polity, the more office-filling should often lean on **delegated or mixed representation** rather than pretending there is one flat electorate;
- and the more technical, bounded, or verification-heavy the office, the more selection should lean on **appointment with merit, balance, and review** rather than prestige elections.

So the archive’s rule is:
**elect thick political authority; delegate compound intergovernmental authority; appoint bounded expert or neutral execution; rotate or lot the tiny offices that do not need permanent political class formation.**

---

## The anti-prestige-election / anti-shadow-appointment test

Before creating or reforming any office, ask seven questions:

1. **Mandate test:** does this office hold real general-purpose discretion over people, money, or force, or is it mainly an operating / coordinating post?
2. **Public-fit test:** is there one honest electorate here, or is the governed public really compound across jurisdictions, peoples, or member bodies?
3. **Execution test:** does the role need political authorization, technical competence, neutral administration, or some mix?
4. **Visibility test:** can outsiders tell who selected the officeholder, for how long, under what eligibility rules, and how replacement works?
5. **Inflation test:** would direct election create a thicker mandate story than the office can honestly carry?
6. **Shadow test:** would appointment let a powerful public office avoid meaningful public authorization or scrutiny?
7. **Recall / removal test:** is there a scale-fit way to correct failure — reelection, recall, replacement by the parent body, merit discipline, or fixed-term nonrenewal?

If the answer to several of these is “we will see” or “the office should be elected because elections feel democratic,” the design is likely suffering from **prestige-election theater, shadow appointment, or mandate inflation**.

## Default selection modes by scope

### 0. Household / association / cooperative

**Default selection mode:** **member election or rotation from the membership, with lot or turn-taking acceptable for tiny roles**.

Use:
- one member, one vote for representative or steward roles where the body is a member polity,
- rotation or turn-taking for low-stakes offices,
- and higher-formality election where money, sanctions, or durable delegation are involved.

At this scale the main danger is not weak public authorization but quiet oligarchy by founders, donors, or informal insiders.

### 1. Micro-local (building / block / village / campus / frontline institution)

**Default selection mode:** **short-term election, rotation, or lot for small offices; no permanent local bosses**.

Use:
- open nomination and visible selection,
- short terms and easy replacement,
- rotation or civic lottery for low-discretion steward roles,
- and direct election only for the few positions that really coordinate shared budgets, meetings, or common rules.

This scale works best when the officeholder remains challengeable by presence, not buffered by distant campaign machinery.

### 2. Municipal (city / town)

**Default selection mode:** **directly elected representative councils, with executive selection matched to the real executive model and merit appointment for professional administration**.

Use:
- direct election for the main representative body,
- direct election for a mayor only where the office actually carries a thick citywide governing mandate,
- council-selected or confidence-dependent executive leadership where the city uses a council-manager or assembly-led model,
- and merit-based appointment for clerks, finance officers, service executives, inspectors, and other roles that should not become partisan popularity contests.

The anti-pattern is electing every city operator just to signal democracy while destroying competence and accountability clarity.

### 2.5 Intermediate local administration (county / prefecture / province-lite backstop)

**Default selection mode:** **delegated or indirect selection for narrow backstop units; direct election only where the unit has broad political authority**.

Use:
- delegated boards or indirectly authorized leadership where the body mainly coordinates municipalities or runs shared administration,
- direct election for assemblies only where the unit genuinely makes broad distributive, legislative, or rights-affecting choices,
- and appointed professional administration for the operating core.

Do not wrap a mostly administrative backstop in fake high-politics selection rituals, but do not hide a real subnational polity inside appointment chains either.

### 3. Metropolitan / corridor / watershed / functional region

**Default selection mode:** **delegated representation from member jurisdictions as the normal case, upgraded to mixed or direct election only when metro power becomes thick enough**.

Use:
- member-jurisdiction delegates, often sitting by virtue of holding local elected office,
- mixed boards where some seats represent operators, users, or affected interests under published rules,
- and direct election only when the metro tier holds broad tax, land-use, policing, or redistributive powers that justify a distinct popular mandate.

This is the clearest no-prestige-election zone. Metro government often needs democratic legitimacy, but that legitimacy is frequently **compound and delegated**, not necessarily built around one directly elected figurehead.

### 4. Regional (state / province / land)

**Default selection mode:** **direct election for the legislature; executive selection through the regional constitutional model; appointment for sectoral and regulatory execution**.

Use:
- direct election for the representative chamber,
- parliamentary or assembly-accountable executive formation where the region is cabinet-style,
- direct election of the executive only where that constitutional choice is real and openly justified,
- and appointments with merit / independence rules for agencies, inspectorates, and technical backstop institutions.

Regions are usually too consequential for pure delegation and too broad for steward rotation.

### 5. National

**Default selection mode:** **competitive election for representative institutions, constitutionally typed executive formation, and appointment for bounded expert or neutral offices**.

Use:
- elections for the legislature and, where the constitution provides, head-of-state or head-of-government offices,
- party competition and chamber rules to turn votes into governing authority,
- merit or independence-based appointment for civil service, regulators, judges, central-bank roles, and other offices where neutrality, continuity, or specialized competence matters,
- and hearings / confirmation / countersignature / publication rules so appointments do not become shadow sovereignty.

The national anti-pattern comes in two forms: electing neutral offices that should be insulated, and appointing thick political authority that should answer through elections.

### 6. Supranational (treaty union / shared-rule space)

**Default selection mode:** **compound selection: direct election where citizens are represented directly, delegated state representation where member polities remain constitutional principals, and appointment/approval for bounded executive and regulatory offices**.

Use:
- direct election for the parliamentary channel where it exists,
- member-state delegates or ministers for the state channel,
- intergovernmental nomination plus parliamentary election / consent / hearings for commission, college, or court personnel,
- and explicit incompatibility and independence rules for quasi-executive or review bodies.

This scale should not pretend one selection path is enough. Its honesty comes from **mixed selection that mirrors mixed legitimacy**.

### 7. Global (planetary / treaty-complex level)

**Default selection mode:** **state delegation for negotiating and voting bodies, appointment for secretariats and expert panels, and advisory or review-facing participation channels for affected publics**.

Use:
- state delegations for assemblies, conferences, and treaty bodies,
- appointed secretariat leadership under published nomination and approval rules,
- competence-and-balance-based appointment for expert panels, inspectors, and review teams,
- and recurring public-observation, consultation, or complaint channels where global decisions have direct human effects.

The archive default here is emphatically **not** a fake global presidential election. Global legitimacy is usually strengthened by better delegation, transparency, expertise, and review discipline, not by theatrical world-office campaigns.

### Non-territorial & indigenous polities (cross-cutting)

**Default selection mode:** **selection methods rooted in the polity’s own legitimate traditions, bounded by external rights floors and legible compact interfaces**.

Use:
- member-, clan-, community-, or council-based selection where those forms remain reviewable and publicly legible,
- stronger formalization where the polity wields delegated public force or person-facing service authority,
- and published compact rules when internal selection affects neighboring governments or shared populations.

Do not force every polity into a party-list or mayoral template. But do require outsiders to see how authority is conferred and contested.

---

## Cross-scope defaults worth carrying everywhere

1. **Elect thick political authority.**
2. **Appoint bounded expert or neutral execution.**
3. **Delegate compound intergovernmental authority unless and until a stronger common mandate is honest.**
4. **Rotate or lot tiny low-discretion offices instead of growing a miniature permanent class.**
5. **Do not elect offices just because election looks prestigious.**
6. **Do not appoint offices just because appointment looks efficient.**
