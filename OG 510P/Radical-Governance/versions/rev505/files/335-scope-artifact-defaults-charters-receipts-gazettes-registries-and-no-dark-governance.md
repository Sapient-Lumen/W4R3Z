# 335 — Scope-Artifact Defaults: Charters, Receipts, Gazettes, Registries, and No Dark Governance

**Cross-stack note:** use `306-scope-design-ideal-government-and-reference-bundles-guide.md` for the canonical route across the scope-design family. This memo is the scale-to-public-surface / artifact-defaults front door; `14` / `176` explain cross-scope logic, `285` gives the default bundle language, `327` handles the scale-sensitive institutional form, `328` handles the scale-sensitive legitimacy channel, `329` handles the scale-sensitive tempo / time-horizon choice, `330` handles the scale-sensitive epistemic / evidence-mode choice, `331` handles the scale-sensitive accountability / review-mode choice, `332` handles the scale-sensitive coercion / direct-force-holding choice, `333` handles the scale-sensitive finance / revenue-model choice, `334` handles the scale-sensitive operator / staffing-pattern choice, and `71` / `31` / `115` / `200` handle the deeper interface / records / publication-integrity / access-to-documents substrate once the scale-fit artifact question has been identified.

**Purpose:** answer the design question that remains after scope assignment, bundle design, form choice, legitimacy choice, tempo choice, evidence choice, accountability choice, coercion choice, finance choice, and operator choice: **what public artifact surface should government usually present at each scope?**

**Why this memo exists:** `14`, `176`, `285`, and `327-334` explain what each scale owns, what shape it should take, how it should be legitimated, what clock it should run on, what evidence it should mainly govern with, how it should be checked, which scales should directly hold force, what finance model should dominate there, and what operator should usually run it. But the archive still needed one compact memo that says **scale should also change the dominant publication and record layer that makes power visible there at all**.

**From-below:** this memo is a guardrail against dark governance. It helps stop micro-local bodies from operating with no posted steward or escalation card, municipalities from promising services without catalogs, queues, or contract trails, metro bodies from coordinating shared systems without compact registries or dispute dockets, regional authorities from equalizing without territorial dashboards or formula publication, national governments from ruling without a gazette and public accounts spine, and supranational or global bodies from issuing commitments that never harden into official journals, treaty registries, stocktakes, or auditable follow-through.

**Evidence anchors:** OECD’s Recommendation on Open Government treats openness, transparency, accountability, and stakeholder participation as a whole-of-government design problem rather than a one-off portal exercise [BIB-OECD-OPEN-GOV-REC], and the OECD’s 2023 global assessment emphasizes that open-government practice is increasingly being mainstreamed across budgeting, procurement, justice, and the local level rather than staying an executive-side transparency gesture [BIB-OECD-OPEN-GOV-ASSESSMENT-2023]; the IMF still treats its Fiscal Transparency Code as the international standard for disclosing information about public finances [BIB-IMF-FISCAL-TRANSPARENCY-HUB-2026]; the Open Contracting Data Standard remains a practical baseline for publishing data and documents across the contracting lifecycle [BIB-OCDS]; Article 102 of the UN Charter still requires UN members to register treaties and international agreements with the Secretariat and for the Secretariat to publish them [BIB-UN-ARTICLE102-REGISTER-2026]; EUR-Lex’s Official Journal remains the authentic publication surface for EU legal acts [BIB-EURLEX-OJ-2026]; and the Paris Agreement’s Enhanced Transparency Framework plus the recurring global stocktake show that distant global governance is only credible when reporting, review, and stocktake artifacts exist on a fixed cadence [BIB-UNFCCC-BTR-2026] [BIB-UNFCCC-GST-2026].

---

## Core claim

Every scale needs a **dominant public surface**.

Not because publication by itself is enough, but because a government that leaves behind the wrong kind of artifact becomes hard to understand, hard to contest, and easy to mythologize.

As scale changes, the dominant artifact should also change:
- lower scopes should default to **short, person-legible, posted artifacts**,
- frontline municipal and intermediate layers should default to **service catalogs, queue/decision traces, budgets, and contract trails**,
- regional and national layers should default to **gazettes, statute books, territorial dashboards, public accounts, and audit surfaces**,
- supranational layers should default to **official journals, conferral maps, implementation scoreboards, and court / review dockets**,
- and global layers should default to **treaty registries, reporting cycles, stocktakes, verification outputs, and pooled-finance ledgers**.

So the archive’s rule is:
**the closer the scale is to the person, the shorter and more immediate the artifact should be; the farther the scale is from the person, the more the artifact burden should shift toward canonical publication, version control, comparability, and verification.**

---

## The anti-dark-governance / anti-document-sprawl test

Before creating or reforming a government at any scale, ask six questions:

1. **Findability test:** can a person tell who is responsible here *today* without expert help?
2. **Decision test:** does a consequential decision leave behind a receipt, reason, or docket entry rather than vanishing into conversation?
3. **Money test:** can outsiders see the budget, contract, transfer, or burden-sharing trail appropriate to the scale?
4. **Version test:** is there a canonical place where rules, commitments, or metrics are current, or only scattered presentations and PDFs?
5. **Contestability test:** can a person or member jurisdiction locate the complaint, appeal, or dispute lane from the publication surface itself?
6. **Scale-fit test:** is the artifact short enough for the scale closest to the person and rigorous enough for the scale farthest from them?

If the answer to several of these is “no”, the design is likely **dark governance, dashboard theater, or documentation sprawl without accountability**.

## Default artifact modes by scope

### 0. Household / association / cooperative

**Default artifact mode:** a **charter + membership / money ledger + decision receipt layer**.

Use:
- a short charter,
- a visible steward / treasurer list,
- meeting or vote receipts,
- and a common-fund ledger.

Increase documentary thickness only when the body holds material common assets, employs staff, or exercises durable delegated public authority.

**Avoid:** pseudo-constitutional sprawl, hidden rules, or common-money control without a ledger.

### 1. Micro-local (building / block / village / campus / frontline institution)

**Default artifact mode:** a **posted responsibility card plus issue log**.

This scale is strongest when it visibly publishes:
- who the steward or convening body is,
- where and when people can show up,
- current issues / incidents / maintenance status,
- decisions or meeting receipts,
- and the upward escalation path.

The default artifact here should usually be legible on a wall, noticeboard, phone screen, or one-sheet handout — not a remote portal maze.

**Avoid:** making people discover power through rumor, personal networks, or group-chat archaeology.

### 2. Municipal (city / town / county-equivalent frontline state)

**Default artifact mode:** a **service-and-rule publication spine**.

Municipal government should usually have:
- a service catalog and access journey surface,
- a local rule / bylaw / instrument inventory or gazette,
- queue, service-standard, and decision-receipt surfaces,
- budget and procurement publication,
- and complaint / appeal lane publication.

This is the scale where publication has to help a person do something concrete: show up, apply, understand a rule, contest a decision, track a queue, or inspect a contract [BIB-OECD-OPEN-GOV-ASSESSMENT-2023] [BIB-OCDS].

**Avoid:** treating a glossy city dashboard or strategy deck as a substitute for joinable service, rule, and contract artifacts.

### 3. Intermediate local administration (county / prefecture pattern)

**Default artifact mode:** a **standardized continuity and backstop surface**.

This layer should usually publish:
- service-territory and office maps,
- standardized forms / access routes across municipalities,
- cross-municipal queue and continuity indicators,
- delegated-administration funding and transfer publication,
- and boundary-spanning complaint / review lanes.

Its artifacts should reduce municipal seam harm rather than add another opaque layer.

**Avoid:** invisible administrative empires whose role becomes legible only when something goes wrong.

### 4. Metropolitan / corridor / watershed / functional region

**Default artifact mode:** a **compact registry plus shared-system dashboard**.

Metro and functional authorities should usually publish:
- the compact or legal basis that created the body,
- member maps and authority boundaries,
- board decisions and voting records,
- capital-plan and contract surfaces,
- network / corridor / watershed performance dashboards,
- and a dispute / escalation docket for member conflicts.

This is the scale where the artifact needs to prove that the body is real, competence-bounded, and tied to a shared system rather than a prestige shell.

**Avoid:** second-city-hall publication sprawl, or conversely, invisible compacting with no public map of who agreed to what.

### 5. Regional (state / province / land / autonomous territory)

**Default artifact mode:** a **territorial statute-and-equalization surface**.

Regional government should usually publish:
- regional laws / delegated instruments / planning documents,
- territorial indicators and equalization formulas,
- inspectorate and review outputs,
- cross-municipal infrastructure and resilience plans,
- and program / transfer dashboards that reveal where support and obligations actually flow.

At this scale, the publication surface must help people and municipalities see how territorial coordination, backstopping, and equalization are actually happening.

**Avoid:** symbolic regional sovereignty that over-produces speeches while under-publishing formulas, maps, and service consequences.

### 6. National

**Default artifact mode:** a **gazette + statute-book + public-accounts spine**.

National government should usually maintain:
- a canonical legal publication surface,
- budget, public-accounts, procurement, and audit publication,
- official-statistics release calendars and methods,
- registry surfaces for high-consequence systems such as ADS, sanctions, or licenses where relevant,
- and public records / ATI surfaces that make the state inspectable [BIB-IMF-FISCAL-TRANSPARENCY-HUB-2026] [BIB-OECD-OPEN-GOV-REC].

This is where publication needs version control and authenticity more than rhetorical accessibility alone.

**Avoid:** replacing canonical publication with ministerial press release culture, or letting official statistics, budget, and procurement live on disconnected islands.

### 7. Supranational (union / treaty-union / shared-rule space)

**Default artifact mode:** an **official-journal and implementation-scoreboard surface**.

This scale should usually publish:
- authentic legal acts in an official journal,
- conferral / competence maps,
- implementation scoreboards and notices,
- court / tribunal dockets and judgments,
- funding-instrument rules and allocations,
- and public state-of-compliance views [BIB-EURLEX-OJ-2026].

Because authority is more distant here, legitimacy depends heavily on publication discipline, version control, and visible competence boundaries.

**Avoid:** relying on summit communiqués, press pages, or policy decks where legal effect, implementation status, and contest routes are unclear.

### 8. Global / planetary

**Default artifact mode:** a **treaty-registry + reporting / stocktake / pooled-finance surface**.

Global governance should usually publish:
- treaty registrations and status changes,
- commitment and reservation registers,
- reporting cycles and submissions,
- verification and review outputs,
- stocktake and synthesis reports,
- pooled-finance accounts and burden-sharing formulas,
- and clear domestic follow-through links [BIB-UN-ARTICLE102-REGISTER-2026] [BIB-UNFCCC-BTR-2026] [BIB-UNFCCC-GST-2026].

This is the scale where the artifact often *is* the institution’s reality. If reporting, stocktake, verification, and finance traces do not exist, the commitment is often only ceremonial.

**Avoid:** donor-report kaleidoscopes, summit declarations without registries, or global dashboards that never resolve into member-specific obligations and follow-through.

## Cross-cutting design rules

### A. The closer the scope is to the person, the shorter and more situational the artifact should be

People should not need treaty-grade documentation to learn who is responsible for a blocked drain, a school entry decision, or a neighborhood hazard.

### B. The farther the scope is from the person, the more the artifact burden should shift to canonical publication and authenticity

Distant authority needs official journals, registries, revision control, and review cycles because ordinary social visibility is weaker there.

### C. Dashboards never substitute for receipts, dockets, or rule-in-force publication

Aggregates help observe systems; they do not replace a person-facing or member-facing trace of what was decided, by whom, and on what basis.

### D. Shared systems need compact artifacts, not just performance artifacts

Whenever several jurisdictions share a body, the public surface should show not just outcomes but the legal basis, members, boundaries, votes, contributions, and dispute path.

### E. Money artifacts should thicken as scale and pooling increase

Micro-local ledgers can stay simple; pooled national or global finance needs stronger accounts, audit, and burden-sharing visibility.

### F. Publication that fails accessibility, language access, or offline reach still counts as dark governance

An artifact that exists only for insiders or full-time portal users is not a real public surface.
