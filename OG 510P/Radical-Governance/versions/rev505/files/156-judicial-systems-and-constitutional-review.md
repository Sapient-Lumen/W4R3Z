# Judicial Systems & Constitutional Review (Legality That Actually Binds Power)

**Purpose:** make “rule of law” real in practice: decisions can be challenged, rights can be enforced, and state power is constrained **on time**—without requiring wealth, insider status, or heroic litigation.

**Person served:** anyone harmed by a public decision (or by inaction) who needs a fair, understandable, timely path to a binding remedy.

**From-below:** if the only “check” is expensive, slow, or discretionary, it is not a check. This memo specifies *service-level* and *integrity* rails for courts, tribunals, and constitutional review across scopes.

**Route note:** use `316-bill-finalization-assent-promulgation-publication-commencement-and-constitutional-referral-rails.md` when the issue is not constitutional-review architecture in general but the narrower checkpoint where a bill may be referred for review before promulgation or entry into force.

---

## 1) What a judiciary is for (functions, not symbols)

A capable judicial system provides five binding functions:

1. **Fair adjudication** of disputes (civil/criminal) with due process floors (see `[BIB-ICCPR]`, `[BIB-EU-CHARTER-A47]`).
2. **Administrative legality**: review of public decisions for legality, reason-giving, proportionality, and non-arbitrariness (see `[BIB-COE-GOODADMIN-2007]`, `[BIB-VENICE-ROL-2025]`).
3. **Rights enforcement** with *effective remedies* (not merely declaratory opinions) (see `[BIB-UN-REMEDY-60147]`).
4. **Constitutional review / ultra vires control**: stop actors from acting outside delegated authority; resolve competence conflicts in polycentric systems (see `[BIB-VENICE-ROL-2025]`, link to `197-polycentric-federalism-and-overlapping-sovereignty.md`).
5. **Precedent + predictability**: consistent application with reasoned divergence and accessible publication.

**Anti-pattern:** “independence” that becomes **unreviewable power**. Independence must be paired with accountability *for integrity and performance* (not for outcomes) (see `[BIB-UN-JUD-INDEP-1985]`, `[BIB-BANGALORE-2002]`).

## 2) Core invariants (non‑negotiable rails)

### Independence & impartiality
- **Budget + staffing sufficiency** as a *legal entitlement* of the judiciary, not discretionary goodwill (see `[BIB-UN-JUD-INDEP-1985]`).
- **Protected tenure + clear discipline** for misconduct (not political disagreement). Discipline must be due-processful, published, and reviewable (see `[BIB-BANGALORE-2002]`).

### Access to justice as a service guarantee
- **No-legalese intake** + assisted filing + multilingual support.
- **Fee ceilings/waivers** so inability to pay cannot block entry.
- **Default right to reasons** for any denial or delay.
- **Geographic + digital access** with *public terminals* and accessibility standards (link to `108-service-standards-and-time-budgets.md`).

### Timeliness (justice delayed is governance failure)
Adopt judicial time management as a first-class obligation (see `[BIB-CEPEJ-SATURN-2018]`):
- time budgets per case class,
- queue transparency (what is pending and why),
- escalation lanes when deadlines are missed.

### Open justice with safety and privacy
- **Presumption of open proceedings + published reasons**, with narrow, recorded exceptions for safety, minors, and sensitive evidence.
- **Redaction protocols** that are reviewable and auditable (link to `115-information-integrity-and-record-interfaces.md`).

### Remedies that bite
- courts must be empowered to order:
 - **injunctions / interim relief**,
 - **orders to produce records**,
 - **set-aside and remake** decisions,
 - **damages / restitution** when appropriate,
 - **structural remedies** for systemic violations (bounded by monitoring and sunset).

## 3) Institutional architecture by scope (minimal, composable)

### Micro-local (building → block → neighborhood)
- **Accessible community tribunals** for service disputes, housing, local admin decisions.
- **Mandatory reasons + appeal path** upward for rights or serious deprivation.

### City / county / region
- **Administrative review court** (or chamber) with fast lanes for permits, benefits, policing oversight decisions, procurement disputes.
- **Specialized problem-solving courts** only with *rights floors* and independent evaluation (avoid coercive “treatment” as punishment).

### State / national
- **Two-layer review**: (1) administrative legality; (2) constitutional / rights questions.
- **Constitutional court model** or **constitutional chamber** in a supreme court: pick one, but ensure clear jurisdiction to avoid forum shopping.
- **Public interest standing** for categories of harm that otherwise remain unchallenged (bounded by docket management).

### Cross-border / confederal / global regimes
**Routing note:** use `325-international-adjudication-individual-communications-jurisdiction-interim-measures-and-compliance-rails.md` when the real question is which international court, tribunal, or complaint body can hear a dispute or complaint, what consent or admissibility basis applies, what interim protection exists, or how compliance with outcomes is tracked afterward.

- **Treaty compliance tribunals** with:
 - transparent admissibility criteria,
 - interim measures for imminent harm,
 - remedies linked to funding/market access levers where possible.
- **Arbitration carve-outs**: prohibit mandatory private arbitration for core rights/public-law disputes.

## 4) Selection, promotion, and discipline (integrity without capture)

Tie judicial HR to the archive’s integrity spine:

- Use **appointments integrity** (see `113-appointments-and-tenure-integrity.md`) for:
 - merit + transparent criteria,
 - conflict-of-interest disclosure,
 - cooling-off restrictions to reduce revolving-door capture.

- Use **selection integrity** (see `119-selection-and-sortition-integrity.md`) where helpful:
 - citizen/peer panels for *process oversight* of appointments (not case outcomes),
 - random audits of disciplinary decisions to detect politicization.

**Hard rule:** no punishment for decisions, dissent, or lawful interpretation; only for misconduct and incapacity proven with due process (see `[BIB-UN-JUD-INDEP-1985]`, `[BIB-BANGALORE-2002]`).

## 5) Court operations as a control loop (measure what matters)

A judiciary should run a published *ops dashboard* that resists Goodharting:

- **Backlog age distribution** (not just totals).
- **Time-to-interim-relief** for urgent matters.
- **Adjournment reasons** and delay attribution (state, defense, court).
- **Self-represented success**: completion rates + comprehension tests.
- **Outcome integrity audits**: sample-check for bias patterns, reversals, and error clusters.

Design metrics with the archive’s evidence and Goodhart cautions (see `03-metrics-and-evidence.md`, `105-institutional-circuit-breakers.md`).

## 6) Attack surface & mitigations (threat-model the judiciary)

- **Docket capture:** strategic litigation to swamp capacity → mitigate with triage + fast lanes + abuse-of-process discipline that is reasoned and appealable.
- **Quiet defiance:** agencies ignore judgments → mitigate with enforcement powers + budget hooks + personal liability for contempt (careful; guard against political weaponization).
- **Opaque settlements:** “confidential” resolutions that hide policy → mitigate with publishable terms when public rights are implicated.
- **Data laundering:** sealed records used to avoid scrutiny → mitigate with minimal sealing + periodic review + sealing registries.
