# Migration & Mobility Governance (Status + Rights as an Auditable Pipeline)

**Cross-stack note:** use `296-status-identity-portability-and-mobility-routing-guide.md` for the canonical route across the status / identity / portability / mobility cluster. This memo is the migration / border-status specialization; `12` is the person-recognition front door; `125` is the civil-status / membership specialization; `109` is the portability / continuity specialization; `203` is the mutual-recognition specialization; `158` is the family-unity neighbor; `287` is the digital-implementation family neighbor.

**Purpose:** make mobility governance contestable and humane where discretion and secrecy otherwise dominate.

**Person served:** A person whose ability to live, work, or remain is decided by migration systems and who needs humane procedures, custody limits, and meaningful review.

**From-below:** This makes status decisions traceable so you can see what rights you have, why you were denied, and how to appeal without limbo.
**EXP pointer:** counters `EXP-02` (Waiting) and `EXP-05` (Fear) by requiring deadlines + safe contestation in status decisions (`98-persons-path-and-accessibility-invariants.md`).

**Material floor (one sentence):** assumes MVF staffing and assisted/offline access; in degraded mode, preserve salvage‑core obligations: receipting, time bounds, and continuity of records. (See `07` MVF; `80` Phase −1; `98`; `31`; `101-claude-rev142-normative-requirements.md` (NR-13).)

**Assistance & representation:** publish assisted/oral paths (incl. interpretation) and who can act on behalf of someone; name independent advocates where conflict risk is high (see `98`, `36`, `47`, `82`; `101-claude-rev142-normative-requirements.md` (NR-12, NR-17)).

**Authority:** status/custody decisions must point to a binding review lane (tribunal/court or equivalent) with interim protection options and duty-to-decide enforcement (`36`, `66`, `43`). (See `101-claude-rev142-normative-requirements.md` (NR-02, NR-09).)
**Retaliation safety:** safe filing, confidentiality/proxy representation, and separation from enforcement where needed; monitor chilling and coercive “withdrawal” patterns (`83`, `77`, `03`); include at least one degraded/offline safe channel (no personal device required) and publish `03` IPM‑4. (See `101-claude-rev142-normative-requirements.md` (NR-08).)

## Non‑refoulement, access to procedure, and “no silent removal”

**Hard boundary:** no person is removed to a place where they face threats to life/freedom (non‑refoulement), and the system must provide a *workable* way to raise that claim before removal. ([BIB-OHCHR-REFUGEE-CONVENTION-1951])

**No silent swap:** any *change* in the factual/legal basis for removal (destination, category, evidence set, credibility findings) must re‑open notice + response + review clocks; do not “roll” a person into a new track without explicit receipting. (Tie to `115` record interfaces; `111` binding; `105` circuit breakers.)

**Access-to-procedure invariants:** publish a minimal, status‑agnostic entry point (“I need protection / review”), with (a) interpreters, (b) disability‑accessible forms, (c) emergency safe‑contact channel, (d) protected assistance, (e) deadline clocks. ([BIB-UNHCR-HANDBOOK-2019])

## Detention discipline: last resort + reviewable necessity

**Default presumption:** migration detention is exceptional; use only when necessary and proportionate for a lawful purpose, for the shortest time, with effective judicial review and compensation for unlawful detention. ([BIB-UNHCR-DETENTION-GUIDELINES-2012]; [BIB-OHCHR-ICCPR])

**Alternatives without “shadow detention”:** if conditions are used, they must be the least‑restrictive set that achieves the purpose; conditions must be time‑bounded, reviewable, and not imposed where no restrictions are required. ([BIB-UNHCR-DETENTION-GUIDELINES-2012])

**Detention records as first‑class:** detention decisions must be reason‑coded, time‑stamped, and linked to (a) risk assessment, (b) less‑restrictive options considered, (c) counsel/advocate contact, (d) hearing schedule, (e) medical vulnerability flags. (Tie to `116` coercion/detention governance; `52` reason codes; `108` time budgets.)

## Firewalls: service access without enforcement bleed‑through

**Firewall principle:** basic services (health, schooling, emergency shelter, labor safety, reporting exploitation/violence) must be accessible without the service provider becoming a de‑facto enforcement front door. Make the boundary explicit in law/policy + interfaces: data minimization, purpose limitation, and publishable sharing rules + audit trails. (Anchor to `31` FOI/memory; `115` record interfaces; `139` risk assurance.) ([BIB-UN-GCM-2018])

**“Safe reporting” track:** create protected channels for reporting crimes and labor abuses that do not automatically trigger status enforcement actions; publish the guarantees, scope, and exceptions with reason codes. ([BIB-UN-GCM-2018])



**Mercy / interim protection:** where this interface can impose coercion, deprivation, or irreversible loss, it MUST define an auditable waiver/exception path (`85-waivers-variances-and-exceptions-discipline.md`) and an interim protection / stay rule when deadlines are missed, a timely challenge is pending, or a credible hardship claim is filed (`82-service-standards-and-minimum-service-guarantees.md`, `36-appeal-lanes-and-redress-registry.md`). (See `101-claude-rev142-normative-requirements.md` (NR-05, NR-16).)
**Time bounds & escalation:** disclose ack/decision deadlines + no-response rule (auto-escalation / interim protection) and publish tail waits for high-harm classes (default: `82-service-standards-and-minimum-service-guarantees.md`; receipt semantics: `31-records-foi-and-government-memory.md`). (See `101-claude-rev142-normative-requirements.md` (NR-05).)
**Notices & receipts:** rights‑affecting outcomes MUST generate a comprehension-tested Decision Receipt with the `31` minimum fields (incl. consequence triggers, as‑of basis, deadlines, and the binding follow‑through lane). (See `101-claude-rev142-normative-requirements.md` (NR-02, NR-15).)
**As-of & corrections:** decisions and receipts MUST state the as‑of basis (rules, data, releases) and MUST propagate corrections (reopen/undo downstream holds/penalties when upstream records change); do not strand people in stale status. (See `31-records-foi-and-government-memory.md`, `70-interoperability.md`; `101-claude-rev142-normative-requirements.md` (NR-07, NR-15).)

**Proof burdens:** publish required evidence + least-burdensome alternatives; disclose “once-only” retrieval of state-held facts; ensure adverse outcomes cite `RC-*` + a contestation lane (`47`, `44`, `52`, `36`). When no category fits, accept the filing and route to measurable “edge review” (authorized human adjudication + reasoned receipt) (see `47-...`, `82-...`, `12-...`). (See `101-claude-rev142-normative-requirements.md` (NR-06, NR-10).)
**Join constraints:** cross‑scope joins/IDs/data sharing MUST name an empowered use‑path + corrective action (per `70`), stay purpose‑limited/minimized, and provide a narrow fallback when joins are unsafe or missing (prefer person‑portable receipts/reference numbers). (See `70-interoperability.md`; `101-claude-rev142-normative-requirements.md` (NR-14).)

**Problem class:** movement across borders and jurisdictions is a high-rights, high-discretion interface that blends **identity**, **administrative justice**, **service access**, and (sometimes) **coercion**. Failure modes include: *decision-by-queue* (delay as denial), *opaque inadmissibility filters*, *identity gating*, *coercive “administrative” detention without effective remedy*, and *offshore/outsourced enforcement that evades accountability*.

**Design goal:** treat migration status decisions and mobility controls as an **auditable pipeline** (registration → eligibility/status determination → conditions/work authorization → service access → enforcement/return (if any) → remedy + learning) using existing join-keys.

**This memo is intentionally minimal.** It composes existing interfaces: rules (`RULE-*`), decision receipts (`DRR-*`), reason codes (`RC-*`), remedy lanes (`AL-*`), identity/eligibility systems (`IDN-*`), service journeys (`SRV-*`), enforcement/custody events (`ENF-*`), releases (`REL-*`), and oversight closure (`OFR-*`).

**Anchors:** refugee status determination guidance ([BIB-UNHCR-HANDBOOK-2019], [BIB-UNHCR-RSD-PS-2020]); migration cooperation principles ([BIB-UN-GCM-2018]); data on migration dynamics ([BIB-IOM-WMR-2024]).

## Named tensions (design must surface these)
- Border control/security vs human rights and non‑refoulement duties.
- Throughput/speed vs individualized assessment and due process (avoid “decision-by-queue”).
- Transparency/legibility vs evasion/retaliation risk and applicant privacy (see [TM-29]).
- Sovereignty vs international obligation (burden-sharing vs unilateral deterrence).
- Deterrence signaling vs humanitarian duty (policy posture can become coercive harm).

## A) Non-negotiable constraints (always-on)
1) **No “decision by queue”:** if a decision is required (status, work authorization, removal/return, detention review), it MUST have a **deadline**, a **no-response rule**, and a discoverable remedy lane (`AL-*`). Missed deadlines are governance incidents (pattern-auditable). For rights/subsistence‑linked cases, **waiting is harm**: define interim‑protection rules + a duty‑to‑account with a revised estimate (no silent limbo). (`36-...`, `08-...`, `31-...`, `82-...`) (See `101-claude-rev142-normative-requirements.md` (NR-05).)
2) **Rule traceability for gateways:** admissibility, eligibility, and evidence requirements MUST be expressible as `RULE-*` and queryable “as‑of” (no hidden rubrics or shifting checklists). (`39-...`, `25-...`)
3) **Receipts for status-affecting acts:** grants/denials/revocations/conditions/detentions/returns MUST emit `DRR-*` that cite controlling `RULE-*`, portable `RC-*`, any material evidence releases (`REL-*` where appropriate), and the remedy lane (`AL-*`). (`31-...`, `52-...`, `36-...`)
3a) **Comprehension + navigation duty:** receipts and notices MUST be understandable to the person affected (translation + disability access) and must tell them exactly what they can do next (who to contact, by when, what to submit, and whether help is available). (`98-persons-path-and-accessibility-invariants.md`, `31-...`, `08-...`)
3b) **Fear is a defect:** grievance/appeal channels MUST include anti-retaliation posture and safe reporting options; otherwise remedy is performative. (`98-persons-path-and-accessibility-invariants.md`, `08-...`, [TM-29])
3c) **Representation duty:** for minors and others who cannot realistically self-advocate, the system MUST trigger an independent advocate/guardian channel for high-stakes determinations. (`98-persons-path-and-accessibility-invariants.md`, `36-...`, `66-...`)
4) **Detention is exceptional and reviewable:** any deprivation of liberty requires an explicit basis, recorded time window, and scheduled review; custody events are logged as `ENF-*` and each custody authorization/extension emits a `DRR-*` with an appeal/review lane. (`43-...`, `05-...`, `66-...`)
5) **Outsourcing does not break accountability:** when enforcement, screening, shelter, or casework is delegated to contractors/NGOs or other jurisdictions, the same receipt + audit + remedy requirements apply via contract/compact clauses (`CON-*`/`CMP-*`) and independent follow-through (`OFR-*`). (`38-...`, `19-...`, `55-...`)
6) **Purpose limitation for data:** biometrics, watchlists, and case files require a bounded lawful basis; sharing and automated triage must be registered (`DPR-*`/`ADS-*`) and contestable. (`33-...`, `42-...`, `44-...`)

## B) Minimum Viable Mobility Governance Spine (MVMGS)

### B1) Public artifacts (what must be joinable)
- **Status decision receipts:** each status decision emits a `DRR-*` (grant/deny/revoke/renew/condition), with `RC-*` reasons and an `AL-*` lane.
- **Gateway rule inventory:** the PRR contains the admissibility/eligibility/evidence rules and any prioritization/triage policy (including “fast track” criteria). Publish “as‑of” access and changelogs. (`39-...`, `53-...`)
- **Service access map:** publish a simple crosswalk: status class → essential services access paths (`SRV-*`), including minimum access during pending status where required (health, shelter, schooling for minors, legal aid). (`47-...`, `64-...`, `57-...`)
- **Custody & enforcement logs:** custody events are logged in `ENF-*` and joined to the authorizing `DRR-*` (and to any contracts/compacts involved). Publish aggregate custody/return stats as `REL-*` with methods and revision logs. (`43-...`, `51-...`)
- **Return/removal pipeline receipts:** if return/removal is used, each order and its execution emits `DRR-*`/`ENF-*` artifacts with remedy lanes and non-refoulement safeguards.
- **Oversight closure:** material patterns (excessive delay, unlawful detention, high error/overturn rates, offshore harm) open `OFR-*` cases with duty‑to‑respond and closure verification (avoid “dashboard theater”; see [TM-33]). (`55-...`, `32-...`)

### B2) Decision typing (legitimacy routing)
- **Rule changes** that alter admissibility, evidence burdens, or detention/return powers are high-rights and require consultation logging (`ENG-*`) and a published impact note (`REL-*` methods) with a review/sunset trigger.
- **Individual determinations** are administrative but always receipt + remedy + correction channel.
- **Automation introductions** (risk scoring, triage, fraud flags, identity checks) must be registered (`ADS-*`), auditable, and appealable.

### B3) Multi-level boundary rules
Mobility governance often mixes scopes: local service delivery, national border/visa authority, supranational coordination, and global compacts.

Use the scope test (`54-...`) when:
- local units bear service burdens without aligned funding (unfunded mandate spillover),
- national enforcement practices create cross-border spillovers or rights risks,
- or interoperability (documents, readmission, asylum cooperation) requires shared standards.

Prefer compacts (`CMP-*`) for:
- reception and service funding formulas,
- shared casework standards + information-sharing limits,
- and return/readmission cooperation with rights-floor + oversight clauses.

## C) Typical failure modes to design against
- **Delay as denial (“queue weaponization”):** indefinite “pending” status, missed interviews, paper-only requests. Mitigation: publish queue metrics by service (`SRV-*`) and decision class; enforce no-response rules and urgent lanes.
- **Opaque inadmissibility filters:** hidden checklists, discretionary “risk” labels. Mitigation: PRR inventory of gateway rules, receipts for denials, and publish aggregate error/overturn rates.
- **Identity gating + correction backlog:** inability to correct identity records blocks status and services. Mitigation: define assurance tiers, publish correction timelines, and provide alternatives.
- **Administrative detention creep:** detention becomes default rather than exceptional. Mitigation: custody logs + scheduled review receipts + independent oversight cases.
- **Offshore/outsourced harm:** accountability gaps via private contractors or third-country processing. Mitigation: contract/compact receipt + audit + remedy clauses and mandatory independent follow-through (`OFR-*`).

## D) Reason code starter set (`RC-MIG-*`)
Use these *in addition to* `RC-PROC-*`, `RC-ELIG-*`, and (where coercion applies) `RC-ENF-*`.

- `RC-MIG-001` **Protection/status granted** — criteria met under as‑of `RULE-*` (cite key elements).
- `RC-MIG-002` **Status denied (criteria unmet)** — eligibility criteria not met (cite which element failed + evidence basis).
- `RC-MIG-003` **Inadmissible / barred** — statutory bar applies (cite the bar rule; name remedy lane and any waiver pathway).
- `RC-MIG-004` **Condition/work authorization decision** — authorization granted/denied/limited under a published rubric (cite rule + effective dates).
- `RC-MIG-005` **Detention/custody authorized or extended (time‑bounded)** — deprivation of liberty authorized/extended with review date and lane.
- `RC-MIG-006` **Return/removal order issued** — order issued under cited authority; MUST include remedy lane and documented safeguard checks.
