# Worked Example Authority Chains, Signatory Validity & Voidable Acts


**Purpose:** make the example system explicit about who can validly bind, when acting/delegated authority expires, and what should happen when the institution cannot prove the chain.

**Person served:** clerks, supervisors, advocates, judges, auditors, implementers, and reviewers dealing with decisions whose signatory line looks improvised, stale, or impossible to verify.

---

## Authority-chain checklist

| Question | What must be visible | Protective default if missing |
|---|---|---|
| who signed, in what role? | signatory role + authority schedule pointer | no execution until a valid role is shown |
| was the role acting or delegated? | delegation / acting receipt with scope and dates | shallow voidability lane + interim protection where harm is live |
| did the role have authority for this decision class? | decision-class map and limits | downgrade to nonbinding recommendation until corrected |
| can a reviewer reconstruct the chain as-of the act date? | as-of publication + revocation history | adverse inference against the institution on contested validity |

## Design rules
Current org charts are not enough. Temporary authority needs tighter scope and clearer expiry than ordinary authority. Invalid authority is not a paperwork footnote; where a person is already being bound, missing authority proof should trigger a stop, reopen, or voidability lane.
