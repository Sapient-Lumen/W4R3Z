# 296 — Status, Identity, Portability, and Mobility Routing Guide

**Purpose:** turn the archive’s status / mobility cluster into a readable stack so person recognition, civil status, cross-jurisdiction continuity, migration status, and mutual recognition of valid proof stop competing for the same conceptual role.

**Why this memo exists:** `12`, `125`, `109`, `67`, and `203` all surface for “identity”, “legal status”, “proof of who counts”, “cross-border mobility”, “recognition of credentials”, or “portability” queries because they describe one family from different layers. Nearby `158` is the family-unity / child-protection neighbor when status or mobility decisions separate caregivers and children. Nearby `287` is the digital-identity / DPI implementation family when the real question becomes wallets, trust frameworks, or credential ecosystem governance rather than status law or continuity rules. The archive should name those handoffs explicitly rather than invite flattening.

**Evidence anchors:** current legal-identity and civil-registration framing [BIB-UNICEF-BR] [BIB-UN-SDG-16-9] [BIB-UNHCR-STATELESS-HUB-2026]; current migration-governance framing [BIB-IOM-MGI-HUB-2026] [BIB-UN-GCM-2018]; current recognition-of-qualifications framing [BIB-UNESCO-GLOBAL-CONV-QUALS-HUB-2026].

## Canonical reading order

### 1. Start with `12-identity-and-recognition.md`
Use `12` when the question is:
- who counts as a recognized person in the system,
- how proofing, correction, and non-surveillance recognition should work,
- or how to avoid identity gates that erase, duplicate, or strand people.

`12` is the **canonical person-recognition front door**.

### 2. Move to `125-identity-membership-and-civil-status.md`
Use `125` when you need:
- civil-status categories, membership, residency, or eligibility-state design,
- status receipts, evidence ladders, non-reset transfers, and status change discipline,
- or the problem is not identity in the abstract but a concrete status that unlocks or denies rights.

`125` is the **civil-status / membership specialization**.

### 3. Use `109-portability-and-cross-jurisdiction-continuity.md`
Use `109` when the real issue is:
- movement between jurisdictions, agencies, providers, or programs,
- continuity of benefits, licenses, deadlines, or accommodations across a seam,
- or the system is making the person restart because a boundary changed.

`109` is the **portability and continuity specialization**.

### 4. Use `67-migration-and-mobility-governance.md`
Use `67` when you need:
- border, visa, asylum, status-determination, detention, return, or admission governance,
- humane procedure and remedy where mobility is controlled by high discretion,
- or the question is specifically about migration-status authority rather than generic continuity.

`67` is the **migration / border-status specialization**.

### 5. Use `203-mutual-recognition-of-credentials-licenses-and-status.md`
Use `203` when you need:
- recognition of licenses, qualifications, study periods, work authorization, or comparable status across jurisdictions,
- equivalency maps, recognition denials, provisional access, and anti-dossier design,
- or portability depends on formal recognition rules rather than simple record transfer.

`203` is the **mutual-recognition specialization**.

### 6. Use `158-child-family-separation-and-alternative-care-governance.md` when the real issue is family unity under status or mobility stress
Use `158` when you need:
- family-unity presumptions,
- separation / reunification clocks,
- kinship-first alternatives,
- or the status / mobility decision is really causing or legitimizing child-family separation.

`158` is the **family-unity / child-protection neighbor**.

### 7. Use `287-dpi-identity-and-trust-family-map.md` when the issue is digital identity infrastructure rather than status law or continuity
Use `287` when you need:
- digital-ID architecture,
- trust frameworks, federation, credential revocation, and verifier governance,
- or the practical question has shifted from *what status means* to *how the digital identity / credential stack is governed*.

`287` is the **digital-implementation family neighbor**.

## Confusion boundaries

### `12` vs `125`
- `12` is the **person-recognition front door** for identity, proofing, correction, and anti-erasure design.
- `125` is the **civil-status and membership specialization** for explicit statuses, effects, status receipts, and non-reset transfer rules.

### `125` vs `109`
- `125` governs **what a status is and how it changes**.
- `109` governs **what happens when a person crosses an institutional or jurisdictional seam and continuity must survive the move**.

### `125` vs `67`
- `125` is the general memo for **civil status, residency, citizenship, and eligibility as administrative power**.
- `67` is the specialized memo for **migration-status authority, protection access, detention limits, return, and mobility control**.

### `109` vs `203`
- `109` is about **carry-forward continuity** when the state already owes the person something and a boundary changed.
- `203` is about **formal recognition** when another jurisdiction’s proof, qualification, or status must be evaluated and accepted, refused, or provisionally honored.

### `67` vs `203`
- `67` governs **migration and border-status procedure**.
- `203` governs **whether a credential, qualification, or comparable status is recognized across jurisdictions once presented**.

### `12` / `125` vs `287`
- `12` and `125` govern **status meaning, rights effects, correction, and humane recognition**.
- `287` governs the **digital identity / credential / trust-framework implementation family** that may carry those statuses.

### `67` vs `158`
- `67` is about **migration-status decisions and mobility control**.
- `158` is about **family-unity, child safety, reunification, and anti-trauma design** when those status decisions cut through households.

## Retrieval guidance (what to cite when)

- Cite `12` for **identity recognition, anti-erasure design, correction, proof burdens, plural recognition, and surveillance-safe personhood infrastructure**.
- Cite `125` for **civil status, residency, membership, eligibility-state design, status receipts, and non-reset transfers after status change**.
- Cite `109` for **boundary seams, provisional continuity, carry-forward duties, transfer ownership, and anti-cliff design across jurisdictions or agencies**.
- Cite `67` for **admission, asylum/protection access, migration detention limits, return/removal procedure, and mobility governance under high discretion**.
- Cite `203` for **mutual recognition, equivalency maps, provisional access while recognition is pending, and anti-dossier recognition design**.
- Cite `158` when the problem is really **family unity, child separation, reunification, kinship-first care, and anti-trauma constraints on status or mobility systems**.
- Cite `287` when the real issue is **digital credentials, relying-party governance, trust frameworks, revocation, federation, and DPI implementation design**.

## Safe merge rule for this cluster

Do **not** flatten this family into one mega-memo on “identity and mobility governance”.
The safer pattern is:
1. keep `12` as the person-recognition front door,
2. keep `125` as the civil-status / membership specialization,
3. keep `109` as the portability and continuity specialization,
4. keep `67` as the migration / border-status specialization,
5. keep `203` as the mutual-recognition specialization,
6. keep `158` nearby as the family-unity / child-protection neighbor,
7. keep `287` nearby as the digital-implementation family neighbor,
8. and use this memo as the bridge that names the roles.

That preserves distinct retrieval hooks while making the cluster substantially easier to enter.
