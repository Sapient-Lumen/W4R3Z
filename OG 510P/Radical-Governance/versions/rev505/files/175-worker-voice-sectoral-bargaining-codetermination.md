# 175 — Worker voice institutions (sectoral bargaining, works councils, codetermination) as anti-capture rails

**Stack relation:** use `299-social-protection-skills-work-and-worker-voice-routing-guide.md` for the canonical route across the social-protection / skills / work / worker-voice family. This memo is the worker-voice / bargaining / codetermination institutional-carrier specialization; `64` is the income-security / benefits-delivery front door; `68` is the learning / capability / credentialing front door; `69` is the labor-standards / enforcement front door.

**Purpose:** Make *worker voice* a first-class governance layer that can counter employer monopsony, subcontracting opacity, and retaliation — without relying on heroics or one-off lawsuits.

**Person served:** A worker in a fragmented supply chain (temp agencies, contractors, platform labor) who needs **credible protection + bargaining capacity** that travels with them.

**From-below:** Worker voice is an **institutional power-balancer**: it creates *continuous* contestation channels over pay, scheduling, safety, and surveillance — not just after harm.

## Design stance
- Treat labor markets as **power-asymmetric** by default; “voluntary” channels are insufficient where retaliation and churn are common (`69-labor-and-work-governance.md`; `98-persons-path-and-accessibility-invariants.md`).
- Prefer *plural voice* (unions + councils + boards + ombuds + sectoral standards) to single points of failure (`104-governance-control-loops.md`; `105-institutional-circuit-breakers.md`).
- “Joinable” means: clear coverage rules, receipted decisions, time bounds, safe channels, and escalation paths (`172-administrative-justice-complaints-ombuds-mesh.md`; `82-service-standards-and-minimum-service-guarantees.md`).

## The menu (stackable modules)

### A) Sectoral bargaining / fair pay agreements / wage boards (macro-to-meso)
**What it does:** Sets **minimum floors** for pay and conditions across an occupation/sector to prevent “race to the bottom” contracting.
**Why it helps:** In fissured workplaces, firm-level bargaining misses contractors and temps; sectoral floors travel with workers. ILO surveys and comparative work emphasize collective bargaining’s role in wage-setting institutions and distributional outcomes. See [BIB-ILO-CB-REVIEW-2023].
**Implementation rails**
- **Coverage registry:** who is in-scope, how “sector” is defined, and how conflicts/overlaps are resolved (use the same mechanics as benefits eligibility: deterministic rules + appeal).
- **Initiation triggers:** thresholds (worker petition / union / employer association / minister) and **anti-veto backstops** if no employer party appears.
- **Public schedule + cost model:** publish expected impacts *and* uncertainty; treat forecasts as contestable (Goodhart guardrails: `00-README.md`; `03-metrics-and-evidence.md`).
- **Enforcement handshake:** inspectorate + payroll data + retaliation protections; remedies must be **fast and automatic** for common violations.

### B) Works councils (firm-level) with information + consultation rights (micro-to-firm)
**What it does:** Creates an elected worker body with rights to information and consultation, enabling day-to-day contestation over scheduling, safety, monitoring, and restructuring.
**Evidence signal:** Reviews of European codetermination note generally neutral to small-positive firm-performance effects and emphasize councils/board participation as mechanisms for voice and constraint; see [BIB-JAEGER-CODETERM-2021] (overview + evidence review).
**Implementation rails**
- **Protected communication:** council channels cannot be throttled by management IT.
- **Consultation clocks:** decisions require minimum notice windows; exceptions require written justification.
- **Shared fact base:** employer must provide standardized datasets (pay bands, hours, injury logs, monitoring tools in use) subject to privacy minimization (`144-data-stewardship-and-data-trusts.md`; `06-digital-and-algorithmic-governance.md`).
- **Retaliation tripwires:** expedited review with presumptions and interim relief.

### C) Board-level codetermination (firm-to-meso)
**What it does:** Seats worker representatives on supervisory boards (or equivalent) to shape strategy, risk appetite, and distribution.
**Reference point:** Germany’s codetermination framework is a canonical example of board-level representation requirements for large firms; see [BIB-DE-MITBEST-1976].
**Implementation rails**
- **Scope test:** apply by employee-count + control structure; prevent evasion via fragmentation.
- **Fiduciary clarity:** worker-board members owe duties to the enterprise and its lawful purpose; conflicts are handled explicitly (not via ambiguity).
- **Risk committee parity:** worker reps participate in committees affecting layoffs, surveillance, and safety.
- **Disclosure:** publish board votes on worker-relevant policies (redacted as needed).

### D) Worker cooperative / platform-coop pathways (micro-to-firm)
**What it does:** Provides an ownership/governance option where voice is structural.
**Rails:** procurement preferences, access to patient capital, technical support, and portability of benefits; avoid “co-op theater” by requiring audited governance and worker-majority control (`87-intermediate-local-administration.md`; `07-fiscal-and-budgetary-governance.md`).

## The joinable pipeline (how this becomes a system, not a slogan)

### 1) Coverage + portability
- Worker voice rights attach to the **person and the workplace**, not only to the “direct employer.”
- Require **joint liability** and chain-of-responsibility where control is exercised (tie to `153-industrial-policy-and-supply-chain-resilience.md`).

### 2) Safe channels + fast remedies
- Provide anonymous/assisted intake, interim protections, and time-bounded case handling (`31`; `66-justice-and-administrative-justice-governance.md`).
- Use “no wrong door”: councils/unions/inspectorate/ombuds can file and route cases (`172-administrative-justice-complaints-ombuds-mesh.md`).

### 3) Bargaining + budgeting handshake
- If sectoral standards raise costs, specify **how** the system absorbs them (prices, margins, subsidies, procurement terms) rather than pretending costs vanish (`108-service-standards-and-time-budgets.md`; `93-tax-and-revenue-administration.md`).

### 4) Anti-capture architecture
- **Multi-channel legitimacy:** elections + councils + lotteries + audits (avoid single captured chamber).
- **Transparency with privacy:** publish the *decisions* and the *rules*, not worker-identifiable datasets (`144`; `86-regulatory-experimentation-and-sandboxes.md`).

## Failure modes (and countermeasures)
- **Fragmentation evasion:** apply coverage via control tests; joint liability; consolidated reporting.
- **Retaliation chilling effect:** interim relief + presumption rules + dedicated retaliation prosecutors.
- **Paper councils:** minimum resource entitlements + enforceable information rights.
- **Council capture / factionalism:** term limits, conflict-of-interest rules, and external audit of election integrity.

## Minimal viable checklist (MVP)
1. Coverage registry + deterministic scope rules + appeal path.
2. Protected worker communication channels + retaliation tripwires.
3. Sectoral floor process with initiation triggers and anti-veto backstop.
4. Works-council consultation clocks + standardized datasets.
5. Board-level parity for large firms (or a functional equivalent) with committee access.
6. “No wrong door” routing to ombuds/inspectorate/courts + receipted time bounds.

## Where this plugs into the scope ladder
- Micro: workplace councils + safe channels
- Meso: sectoral bargaining + joint liability across contractors
- Macro: statutory floors, portability, macro stabilization policy
- Transnational: supply-chain due diligence + procurement standards (`153-industrial-policy-and-supply-chain-resilience.md`; `162-procurement-as-governance-lever-and-guardrails.md`)
