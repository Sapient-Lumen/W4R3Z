# 292 — Coercion, Custody, and Public-Safety Routing Guide

**Purpose:** turn the archive’s coercion / custody / public-safety cluster into a readable stack so minimum-violence governance, portable coercion protocol, policing-specific rails, custody/corrections governance, and non-carceral / restorative neighbors stop looking like accidental duplicates.

**Why this memo exists:** `05`, `116`, `233`, and `232` all surface for “policing governance”, “detention oversight”, “use of force”, “custody”, or “public safety” queries because they describe one family from different layers. Nearby `193` is the non-carceral / restorative neighbor that often belongs in the same design conversation when the real objective is to reduce coercion rather than merely regulate it harder. The newer scope-design seam `332` belongs nearby when the question is not how to govern coercion once it exists, but which scales should directly hold coercive powers at all. The archive should name those handoffs explicitly rather than invite flattening.

**Evidence anchors:** current law-enforcement and use-of-force framing [BIB-OHCHR-LAW-ENFORCEMENT] [BIB-UN-BPUFF] [BIB-UNODC-UOF-RB]; detention-prevention and independent visiting architecture [BIB-OPCAT] [BIB-UN-SPT-NPM]; prison management, rehabilitative environments, and non-custodial direction [BIB-MANDELA-RULES] [BIB-UNODC-PRISON-MATTERS-2025] [BIB-UNODC-KYOTO-MODEL-STRATEGIES-2025]; restorative and non-carceral path design [BIB-UNODC-RESTORATIVE-JUSTICE].

### 1. Start with `05-public-safety-and-coercion.md`
Use `05` when the question is:
- what coercive public power is for,
- how force, detention, reporting, and remedy stay bounded enough to remain publicly legitimate,
- and what minimum-violence governance requires across scopes.

`05` is the **canonical coercion and public-safety front door**.

### 2. Move to `116-coercion-use-of-force-and-detention-governance.md`
Use `116` when you need:
- the portable coercion protocol,
- force-event and detention-event receipts,
- independent serious-incident routing,
- or a reusable minimum packet for any institution that can use force or deprive liberty.

`116` is the **portable coercion protocol layer**.

### 3. Use `233-policing-and-use-of-force-governance-rails.md`
Use `233` when the real issue is:
- policing as an institution,
- patrol / stop / search / arrest governance,
- early-warning, training, dashboarding, or decertification,
- or public-order and law-enforcement operating rails rather than generic coercion alone.

`233` is the **policing specialization**.

### 4. Use `232-corrections-and-incarceration-governance.md`
Use `232` when you need:
- prison / jail / probation / parole governance,
- health, inspection, deaths-in-custody, segregation, contact, or reentry continuity,
- or facility-level accountability for people who cannot safely exit the institution.

`232` is the **custody and corrections specialization**.

### 5. Use `193-restorative-justice-and-conflict-resolution-rails.md` when the goal is to reduce coercion, not just govern it better
Use `193` when you need:
- diversion,
- restorative / mediated resolution,
- safe non-carceral defaults,
- or a repair-oriented lane that prevents harms from being unnecessarily routed into policing or custody systems.

`193` is the **non-carceral / restorative neighbor** for this cluster.

## Confusion boundaries

### `05` vs `116`
- `05` is the **broad minimum-violence governance front door** for coercion, detention, and contestability.
- `116` is the **tighter portable protocol** for force-event receipts, detention receipts, and independent serious-incident routing.

### `116` vs `233`
- `116` is the **generic coercion packet** usable across coercive institutions.
- `233` is the **policing-specific operating rails memo** for public-order practice, early warning, dashboarding, discipline, and decertification.

### `116` vs `232`
- `116` governs the **moment of force, arrest, or liberty restriction**.
- `232` governs the **ongoing custody environment** after a person is inside a prison, jail, probation, parole, or other incarceration-like system.

### `232` / `233` vs `193`
- `232` and `233` assume the state is still using **coercive criminal-justice institutions**.
- `193` is the **bounded non-carceral neighbor** for cases where safety, consent, and power conditions allow repair and resolution without escalating coercion.

### `232` vs `43`
- `232` is the **custody-governance memo** for conditions, safety, inspection, complaints, and continuity.
- `43-enforcement-and-custody-event-register.md` remains the **narrower event/register component** for joinable enforcement and custody records.

### `05` / `116` vs `66` / `156`
- `05` and `116` govern **coercive executive power and immediate safeguards**.
- `66-justice-and-administrative-justice-governance.md` and `156-judicial-systems-and-constitutional-review.md` govern **formal adjudication, review, and binding judicial remedy** once the question becomes court or tribunal architecture.

### `05` / `116` / `233` / `232` vs `332`
- `05`, `116`, `233`, and `232` explain **how coercive power, policing, and custody must be governed once a level holds them**.
- `332-scope-coercion-defaults-policing-detention-emergency-force-and-no-shadow-militias.md` answers the prior scope-design question of **which levels should directly hold those powers at all**.

## Safe merge rule for this cluster

Do **not** flatten this family into one mega-memo on “criminal justice governance”.
The safer pattern is:
1. keep `05` as the coercion and public-safety front door,
2. keep `116` as the portable coercion protocol,
3. keep `233` as the policing specialization,
4. keep `232` as the custody/corrections specialization,
5. keep `193` nearby as the non-carceral / restorative neighbor,
6. and use this memo as the bridge that names the roles.

That preserves distinct retrieval hooks while sharply reducing reader confusion.
