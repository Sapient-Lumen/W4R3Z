# 325 — International Adjudication, Individual Communications, Jurisdiction, Interim Measures, and Compliance Rails

**Purpose:** stop international dispute and complaint pathways from becoming diplomatic folklore by forcing forum choice, consent, admissibility, interim protection, outcome type, and compliance follow-through into one visible governance lane.

**Why this memo exists:** the archive now has a seam for deciding **what kind of international instrument exists at all** (`322`), another for the point where a treaty becomes — or does not become — domestically effective (`321`), another for recurring review and reporting cycles once the commitment exists (`323`), and another for how commitments change over time (`324`). What it still lacked was the distinct dispute-and-remedy layer after those commitments or institutions already exist: **which forum can actually hear a case, on what legal basis, under what admissibility rules, with what urgent-protection tools, and who owns compliance after an order, judgment, views, or friendly settlement is issued**.

**Evidence anchors:** the United Nations Charter and the International Court of Justice remain the baseline because Article 33 frames judicial settlement as one lawful path among peaceful-settlement tools, the ICJ’s contentious jurisdiction depends on state consent, the Court handles advisory opinions separately from contentious cases, and Article 94 of the Charter states that parties undertake to comply with ICJ decisions in cases to which they are parties [BIB-UN-CHARTER-ART33] [BIB-ICJ-CONTENTIOUS-JURISDICTION-2026] [BIB-ICJ-ADVISORY-JURISDICTION-2026] [BIB-UN-CHARTER-ART94-2026]. The Statute of the ICJ is also central because it gives the Court power to indicate provisional measures under Article 41 [BIB-ICJ-STATUTE]. ITLOS is a strong operability anchor because it makes provisional-measures practice, preliminary objections, and party compliance reporting legible, including in some settings before the final arbitral tribunal has been constituted [BIB-ITLOS-FAQ-2026]. OHCHR’s current complaint guidance is a strong human-rights-process anchor because it distinguishes individual communications, inter-state complaints, and inquiries; stresses that not all committees may hear complaints; links access to state acceptance of committee competence; and describes registration, written proceedings, interim measures, and follow-up to views and recommendations [BIB-OHCHR-INDIVIDUAL-COMMUNICATIONS-2026] [BIB-OHCHR-COMPLAINTS-TREATIES-2026]. The Council of Europe’s current ECHR materials are strong execution anchors because Rule 39 interim measures are reserved for imminent risk of irreparable harm, and final judgments plus friendly-settlement decisions move into a public execution process supervised by the Committee of Ministers with action plans, action reports, and final resolutions [BIB-ECHR-INTERIM-MEASURES-2026] [BIB-COE-ECHR-EXECUTION-2026].

---

## Core claim

A safe international-remedy architecture does **not** begin with the question “who is right?”
It begins with five prior public questions:
1. **What kind of forum is this — court, tribunal, complaint body, inquiry process, or advisory body?**
2. **What act of consent, declaration, optional protocol, compromissory clause, membership term, or jurisdictional trigger makes the forum available?**
3. **What admissibility or gateway rules apply before the merits can be heard?**
4. **What urgent interim protection exists if irreparable harm is imminent?**
5. **What exactly happens after the outcome — who must comply, who supervises, and what domestic measures follow?**

The archive should therefore stop using “international law remedy” as though it were one thing.
A treaty-body communication, an inter-state case before the ICJ, a regional human-rights judgment, an arbitral award, an advisory opinion, and a peer-review recommendation are **not** interchangeable outputs.

The design target is simple:
- **publish one forum-consent ledger before disputes are filed,**
- **type admissibility requirements instead of leaving them to specialist memory,**
- **keep an urgent interim-measures lane open where irreparable harm is plausible,**
- **type outcomes differently rather than treating all findings as “a judgment”,**
- **and assign domestic compliance ownership the same day an international outcome is issued.**

## The smallest good architecture

### 1. Forum consent ledger (`FCL-*`)
For every international forum the polity may use or appear before, publish one durable record stating:
- forum type: court / tribunal / human-rights court / treaty-body communications mechanism / inquiry process / advisory body,
- subject-matter scope,
- eligible applicants or parties,
- consent basis: treaty clause, optional-clause declaration, optional protocol, standalone declaration, special agreement, or membership term,
- reservations or limits,
- territorial or temporal scope,
- and the domestic authority that accepted the forum.

No ministry should need to reinvent the answer to “did we ever accept this forum’s jurisdiction?”

### 2. Jurisdiction and admissibility packet (`JAP-*`)
Before any filing or forum referral, publish a compact packet stating at minimum:
- who may bring the case,
- why this forum is available,
- whether the respondent accepted jurisdiction or complaint competence,
- key gateway questions: standing or victim status, exhaustion, timeliness, duplication / parallel-proceedings risk, ratione temporis, and scope of subject matter,
- and the likely preliminary objections.

The point is not to guess the merits early. It is to stop a case from being filed into a forum that clearly cannot hear it.

### 3. Interim protection lane (`IPL-*`)
For each forum that can issue provisional measures, interim measures, or urgent requests, publish:
- legal basis,
- trigger threshold,
- filing channel and out-of-hours contact path,
- evidence needed for urgency and irreparable harm,
- named domestic officials who can respond immediately,
- and what default domestic hold or preservation action follows while the forum decides.

Interim protection is a governance capability, not merely a litigation tactic.

### 4. Outcome-type register (`OTR-*`)
Every international case or complaint should end in a typed output record that distinguishes at least:
- **judgment / award**,
- **order on provisional or interim measures**,
- **decision on admissibility or jurisdiction**,
- **views / recommendations on an individual communication**,
- **friendly settlement / strike-out decision**,
- **inquiry findings**,
- or **advisory opinion**.

Do not let press offices flatten all of these into one word such as “ruling”. The domestic compliance path depends on the type.

### 5. Compliance and execution ledger (`CEL-*`)
For every international outcome, publish:
- binding status and legal basis,
- lead ministry or execution coordinator,
- any compensation or individual-relief obligations,
- any required law / regulation / guidance / practice changes,
- reporting deadlines,
- supervising body if any,
- public status of compliance,
- and links to domestic implementation artifacts.

This ledger should exist for treaty-body views and recommendations as well as for court judgments, but it must type them honestly rather than pretending they are identical.

### 6. Domestic handoff note (`DHN-*`)
Every `CEL-*` record should point onward to the next needed systems:
- `321` if domestic legal-effect mapping or implementing law changes,
- `323` if the outcome feeds future reporting or follow-up cycles,
- `08` if person-facing remedy lanes or compensation mechanisms must change,
- `156` if domestic courts must reopen cases, adjust precedent, or enforce rights differently,
- and the relevant substantive policy memos if operations, budgets, or services change.

The archive should reject “internationally decided, domestically ownerless”.

## Design rules that prevent the common failures

### A. Forum type comes first
Ask first whether the body is deciding a contentious dispute, reviewing an individual complaint, issuing an interim measure, conducting an inquiry, or giving an advisory opinion.
Do not import court-language into review bodies or advisory processes.

### B. Jurisdiction is never inferred from moral urgency alone
A bad fact pattern does not itself create jurisdiction.
The file must state the exact basis: optional clause, treaty compromissory clause, special agreement, optional protocol, declaration, or other accepted hook.

### C. Advisory opinions are not substitutes for contentious judgments
Advisory opinions may be normatively and politically weighty, but they are not the same as a judgment between parties in a contentious case.
Type them separately and assign follow-through accordingly.

### D. Admissibility rules are part of justice, not a technical afterthought
Standing, victim status, exhaustion, time limits, and preliminary objections should be surfaced early and publicly.
A remedy that exists only for people who already know the hidden gateway rules is not a serious remedy system.

### E. Interim-measures capacity must exist before the emergency
If a forum can order urgent protection, the polity needs a standing domestic duty path for same-day response, record preservation, non-removal / non-transfer holds, and ministerial notice.
Do not improvise the response after the order arrives.

### F. Outcome types must map to distinct compliance expectations
- A **judgment or award** usually triggers formal execution and, where needed, legislative or administrative change.
- An **interim-measures order** triggers immediate preservation duties even before the merits are decided.
- **Views and recommendations** from complaints bodies still require a visible government response and follow-up path, but should not be mislabeled as if they were the same instrument as a court judgment.
- A **friendly settlement** needs implementation tracking just as much as an adjudicated outcome.

### G. Compliance ownership starts on day one, not after political debate cools
The day an outcome is issued, one named domestic office should own:
- legal analysis,
- immediate protective steps,
- compensation / reopening / individual relief,
- general measures,
- and public status reporting.

### H. International and domestic dockets must stay joined
If a domestic proceeding is reopened, compensation is paid, a law is amended, or a ministerial practice is changed because of an international outcome, the `CEL-*` record should show it.
The archive rejects separate worlds of “foreign affairs” and “domestic implementation”.

### I. No-wrong-door filing support matters
People should not have to know, at intake, whether their path is domestic appeal, ombuds, treaty body, regional court, or inter-state claim.
A serious system gives them routing help without forcing them to master forum doctrine first.

## Forum families the archive should keep distinct

### 1. Inter-state adjudication
Typical questions:
- Did both states consent?
- Is the consent general, treaty-specific, or case-specific?
- Are provisional measures available?
- Who executes the judgment afterward?

### 2. Individual communications and human-rights complaints
Typical questions:
- Has the state accepted the body’s competence?
- Does the complainant have standing or victim status?
- Must domestic remedies be exhausted first?
- Are interim measures available to prevent irreparable harm?
- What follow-up process exists after views or recommendations are issued?

### 3. Regional human-rights courts
Typical questions:
- What admissibility rules and time limits apply?
- How do interim measures work?
- Who supervises execution?
- What individual and general measures must the state take?

### 4. Specialized tribunals and arbitration-linked forums
Typical questions:
- What jurisdiction clause or annex applies?
- Are preliminary objections or prima facie jurisdiction tests front-loaded?
- Can provisional measures be issued before the merits forum is fully constituted?
- How is compliance reported and tracked?

### 5. Advisory-opinion pathways
Typical questions:
- Which organ or agency may request the opinion?
- What legal and political effect should be expected afterward?
- Which domestic or international actors own the follow-through even without party-specific execution machinery?

## Failure signatures

Watch for these recurring pathologies:
- **“The forum exists, so of course it can hear this case.”** → consent-blind jurisdiction failure.
- **A complaint is filed without checking whether the state accepted the body’s competence.** → phantom-forum failure.
- **Interim measures exist on paper, but no one knows who can stop the deportation, transfer, demolition, seizure, or destruction before morning.** → urgency-without-ops failure.
- **A treaty-body view is treated as meaningless because it is “not a judgment”, or treated as identical to a court judgment without stating the actual follow-up basis.** → outcome-type collapse.
- **Friendly settlements disappear from public accountability because there was no “judgment”.** → settlement-opacity failure.
- **The foreign ministry receives the outcome, but line agencies, treasuries, prosecutors, and domestic courts never get a structured handoff.** → ownerless-compliance failure.
- **International and domestic case histories diverge, leaving nobody able to say whether reopening, compensation, or law reform actually happened.** → split-docket failure.
- **People discover only after filing that the real blocker was exhaustion, time limit, or standing.** → hidden-gateway failure.

## Scope-sensitive defaults

### National
- **Default:** maintain one public forum-consent ledger and one execution coordinator function.
- **Prefer:** the attorney-general / foreign-affairs / justice interfaces be pre-wired for urgent interim-measures response.
- **Guardrail:** do not leave compliance ownership to ad hoc cabinet bargaining after an international outcome lands.

### Supranational / regional
- **Default:** separate member-state obligations, union-level obligations, and court-supervised execution lanes.
- **Prefer:** one public matrix joining judgments, interim orders, action plans, and domestic implementation measures.
- **Guardrail:** complexity of multi-level governance must not become uncertainty about who owes compliance.

### Global / regime-complex
- **Default:** keep contentious adjudication, treaty-body complaints, inquiry mechanisms, and peer review visibly distinct.
- **Prefer:** one routing note that tells outsiders whether they are looking at a court, a committee, a review cycle, or a political organ.
- **Guardrail:** do not let the vocabulary of “compliance” hide the difference between binding judgments, urgent measures, views, and recommendations.

### Municipal / regional implementers
- **Default:** they should not need to parse treaty doctrine.
- **Prefer:** central government should translate international outcomes into plain operational instructions, funding notes, and rights-protection holds.
- **Guardrail:** no local frontline office should learn of an urgent international protection order from the news.

## One-sentence design test

An international-remedy system is well designed when an ordinary outsider can tell, without specialist folklore, **which forum can hear the dispute or complaint, on what consent or admissibility basis, what urgent protection exists, what kind of outcome may emerge, and who must visibly do what after that outcome is issued.**
