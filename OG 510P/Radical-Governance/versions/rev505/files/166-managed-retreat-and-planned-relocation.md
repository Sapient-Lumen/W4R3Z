# Managed Retreat & Planned Relocation (Governance Pattern)

**Cross-stack note:** use `294-climate-resilience-infrastructure-retreat-and-risk-finance-routing-guide.md` for the canonical route across the climate-resilience cluster. This memo is the managed-retreat / planned-relocation specialization; `63` is the broader climate adaptation / DRR front door; `154` is the critical-infrastructure resilience specialization when continuity depends on operator compacts; `169` is the risk-finance / catastrophe-transfer specialization; `170` is the watershed / water-governance neighbor when retreat is driven by basin-scale allocation or flood-risk governance.

**Purpose:** make relocation from hazard exposure (flood/fire/erosion/heat, cascading infrastructure failure) *lawful, dignified, and non‑predatory*.

**Person served:** someone being pressured (by markets, insurers, landlords, or government) to leave their home—or trapped in place by lack of options—who needs relocation decisions to be contestable, compensated, and community‑preserving.

**From-below:** reduces the most common harms of retreat: coerced “voluntary” exit, compensation games, community breakup, elite land capture, and indefinite displacement.

This memo is **scope-crossing**: micro-local delivery, municipal land use, regional housing + infrastructure, national fiscal capacity + rights floor, and (sometimes) international protection norms.

Anchors: [BIB-UN-GP-IDP-1998], [BIB-UNDRR-SENDAI], [BIB-IPCC-AR6-WGII], [BIB-GCC-MANAGED-RETREAT-TOOLKIT].

## Named tensions
- **Safety vs consent:** “move people for safety” becomes coercion without enforceable choice architecture.
- **Speed vs justice:** urgent hazards tempt shortcut procedures; shortcuts create permanent legitimacy loss.
- **Individual compensation vs community continuity:** programs often pay individuals while dissolving neighborhoods.
- **Risk reduction vs land capture:** post-buyout land becomes a speculative prize unless disposition is constrained.

## Threat model (minimum)
- **TM-RET-01 coerced voluntarism:** offers designed so refusal is irrational (insurance withdrawal, utility neglect, code enforcement targeting).
- **TM-RET-02 compensation gaming:** underassessment, opaque valuation, unequal add-ons, discrimination.
- **TM-RET-03 community fracture:** relocation disperses social support, care networks, and local economies.
- **TM-RET-04 eligibility cliff:** aid tied to narrow event declarations; slow-onset hazards excluded.
- **TM-RET-05 post-exit predation:** land-banking by insiders; resale flips; “green gentrification.”
- **TM-RET-06 indefinite displacement:** temporary housing becomes permanent limbo; rights/benefits decay.

## Pattern: the Relocation Compact (CMP-RET)
Treat planned relocation as a **compact** between affected people and the state (often multiple scopes). Publish it as `CMP-RET-*` with enforceable obligations.

**CMP-RET MUST specify:**
1. **Trigger + scope-test:** the hazard/risk basis and why this scope owns the decision (use `DRR-TYPE: SCOPE`; see `54-subsidiarity-and-scope-assignment-test.md`).
2. **Choice set (real options):** (a) stay with protections, (b) retrofit/raise, (c) relocate in-community, (d) relocate as a community unit elsewhere, (e) individual exit—with *no punitive degradation* of services for lawful non-selection.
3. **Compensation rules:** valuation method, timelines, appeal lane, and anti-discrimination checks.
4. **Community continuity plan:** options and funding to keep groups together (site planning, cohort moves, school continuity, care continuity).
5. **Land disposition + anti-speculation:** what happens to vacated land and how resale/leases are constrained (see below).
6. **Grievance + review:** fast appeals, independent review, and remedy authority (`ALR` + `DRR` joins; see `08-remedy-and-grievance.md`).
7. **Data minimization + safety:** publish enough for accountability without doxxing vulnerable households (`99-protective-legibility-and-adoption-dynamics.md`).

## Rights floor & displacement safeguards
Use the Guiding Principles on Internal Displacement as the **rights baseline** even for “domestic” relocation: non-discrimination, protection against arbitrary displacement, and duties during displacement and return/settlement ([BIB-UN-GP-IDP-1998], [BIB-UNHCR-GP-IDP-HUB], [BIB-IDMC-GP-IDP]).

**Operationalize as interface obligations:**
- Every relocation determination issues a **reasoned receipt** (`DRR-RET-*`) joining: risk basis, option set offered, compensation calc, and appeal lane.
- **No silent swap:** all program rule changes are versioned and published (see `51-release-registry.md`).
- **Time-bound temporary status:** if temporary displacement occurs, benefits/rights must not decay by administrative drift.

## Land disposition rails (anti-capture)
Vacated parcels are a governance hotspot.

Default rule: vacated land becomes one of:
- **risk-buffer commons** (floodplain, firebreak, erosion buffer) with enforced non-development;
- **community land trust / public trust holding** with resale constraints;
- **time-limited lease** for compatible uses (agroecology, parks) with clear reversion.

**Prohibitions (unless docketed + supermajority justification):**
- resale into private speculative markets;
- rezoning that converts retreat into profit extraction;
- “green amenity” projects that displace remaining nearby residents without mitigations.

(Connect to land rails in `150-land-commons-stewardship-anti-speculation.md` and `136-land-housing-and-commons-integrity.md`.)

## Financing & intergovernmental responsibilities
Sendai emphasizes disaster risk governance and “Build Back Better,” but relocation is often unfunded or misaligned across tiers ([BIB-UNDRR-SENDAI]).

**Finance design rules:**
- **Money matches mandate:** if national policy demands relocation, national funding must cover it (avoid unfunded mandates).
- **Predictable windows:** create standing programs not only disaster-declaration spikes.
- **Equalisation logic:** poor municipalities need automatic top-ups to avoid “stay in danger because you’re poor.”
- **Audit joins:** publish transfers + awards as joinable releases (`REL-*`) and map to outcomes (see `18-intergovernmental-finance.md`, `03-metrics-and-evidence.md`).

## Legitimacy architecture: consent without veto-traps
Relocation governance fails when it is either **majoritarian coercion** or **minority veto** that blocks safety.

**Use a dual channel:**
- **affected-person assemblies** (representative deliberation) to shape option sets and tradeoffs; plus
- **ballot/elected accountability** for fiscal commitments.

Require **duty-to-respond** and publish response quality (see `41-public-participation-and-deliberation-register.md`, [BIB-OECD-DEL-EVAL-2021]).

## Minimum metrics (keep ≤ 10)
- **Safety:** deaths/injuries avoided; exposure reduction.
- **Option reality:** share offered ≥2 viable options; take-up by option.
- **Equity:** compensation parity across protected classes; time-to-payment distribution.
- **Continuity:** % households relocating with chosen cohort/community; school/care continuity.
- **Stability:** housing stability at 1/3/5 years; debt burdens post-move.
- **Land outcomes:** % parcels in non-speculative holding; compliance with development bans.
- **Remedy:** appeal rates, reversal rates, time-to-decision; satisfaction with process.

## Where this belongs in the scope ladder
- **Micro-local/municipal:** case management, outreach, local land use, service continuity.
- **Regional:** housing supply, receiving-site planning, transit/jobs integration.
- **National:** rights floor, funding, anti-discrimination enforcement, insurance regulation.
- **Supranational/global:** shared norms, financing standards for development banks, climate risk framing ([BIB-IPCC-AR6-SYR-SPM], [BIB-CGDEV-PLANNED-RELOCATION-MDBS]).

## Implementation note
Start with a **pilot compact** for one neighborhood and publish the full joins (risk basis, option set, compensation rules, land disposition, appeal outcomes). Then scale only after the *remedy lane* proves it can actually correct mistakes.
