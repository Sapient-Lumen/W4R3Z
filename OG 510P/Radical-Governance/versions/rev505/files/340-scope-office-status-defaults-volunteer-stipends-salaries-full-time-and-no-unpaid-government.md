# 340 — Scope-Office-Status Defaults: Volunteer, Stipends, Salaries, Full-Time Work, and No Unpaid Government

**Cross-stack note:** use `306-scope-design-ideal-government-and-reference-bundles-guide.md` for the canonical route across the scope-design family. This memo is the scale-to-office-status / pay-status / time-commitment front door; `14` / `176` explain the cross-scope logic, `285` gives the default bundle language, `327` handles the scale-sensitive institutional form, `328` handles the scale-sensitive legitimacy channel, `338` handles the scale-sensitive selection / office-filling choice, `339` handles the scale-sensitive tenure / term-design choice, and `113` / `120` / `228` / `297` handle the deeper appointments / outside-income / board-governance / merit-and-professionalism substrate once the scale-fit office-status question has been identified.

**Purpose:** answer the design question that remains after scope assignment, bundle design, form choice, legitimacy choice, selection choice, and tenure choice: **what should office at this scale usually ask of a human life — volunteer duty, expense reimbursement, stipend, part-time salary, full-time salary, per-diem delegation, or a bounded professional civil-service role?**

**Why this memo exists:** `14`, `176`, `285`, and `327-339` explain what each scale owns, what shape it should take, how it should be legitimated, how it should decide, how offices should be filled, and how long they should last. But the archive still needed one compact memo that says **scale should also change office status** — how much time the office should consume, whether it should rely on unpaid labor, when reimbursement is enough, when stipend or salary is required, and when a delegated board seat should stay tied to another office rather than becoming a freestanding political career.

**From-below:** this memo is a guardrail against two opposite failures. It helps stop tiny local roles from becoming inaccessible because only people with spare money and time can serve. And it helps stop higher or compound layers from inventing unnecessary full-time political castes where delegated, bounded, or technical roles would do.

**Evidence anchors:** the European Charter of Local Self-Government is unusually explicit that the conditions of office of local elected representatives must provide for the free exercise of their functions and allow appropriate financial compensation for expenses, compensation for loss of earnings or remuneration for work done, and corresponding social welfare protection [BIB-COE-LOCALSELF-ART7-1985]; the same Charter also says local-government employee conditions should permit merit-based recruitment with adequate training opportunities, remuneration, and career prospects [BIB-COE-LOCALSELF-ART6-1985]; OECD’s metropolitan-governance evidence still points toward metro bodies composed mainly of elected local-government officials, which is a useful anchor for delegated / dual-hatted rather than standalone salaried metro politics [BIB-OECD-METROGOV-SURVEY-2024]; the European Parliament’s current official rules page still makes the opposite point for directly representative supranational office by publishing a common salary and work-related allowances for MEPs [BIB-EP-MEPS-SALARY-2026]; and the UN common system still pays Professional and higher-category staff on worldwide salary scales established by the General Assembly on the recommendation of ICSC, which is a clear public-law anchor for salaried international secretariat work rather than volunteer global administration [BIB-ICSC-SALARY-SCALES-2026].

---

## Core claim

Every scale needs a **default office-status design**.

Not because every role needs a salary, but because institutions become captured, exclusionary, or theatrical when they cannot say:
- whether ordinary people can afford to serve,
- whether the office is supposed to be volunteer, part-time, or full-time,
- when reimbursement is enough and when it is not,
- when a delegated seat should remain tied to another office,
- and when professional, salaried execution is required because the job is no longer occasional public duty but actual operating work.

As scale changes, the fitting office status also changes:
- the smaller, lower-discretion, and more trust-based the office, the more it can lean on **volunteer service, reimbursement, or light stipend**;
- the more the office is expected to carry routine operating burden, compliance exposure, or a standing service obligation, the more it should become **paid part-time or salaried**;
- the more compound and intergovernmental the body, the stronger the case for **delegated / dual-hatted seats** instead of a separate full-time political class;
- the more general-purpose and publicly political the office, the more it should usually become **functionally full-time and salaried** so wealth does not become a qualification for rule;
- and the more expert, administrative, or secretariat-like the role, the more clearly it should be **professional salaried work with merit, incompatibility, and outside-income rules**.

So the archive’s rule is:
**volunteer or lightly stipended where offices are tiny and occasional; reimbursed or paid part-time where the role asks real labor but not a whole career; delegated and often dual-hatted where a compound body mainly coordinates member governments; salaried and usually full-time where representative or executive authority is thick; and clearly salaried professional work where the role is administrative, expert, or secretariat execution.**

---

## The access / anti-capture test

Before creating or reforming any office, ask seven questions:

1. **Accessibility test:** can an ordinary worker, caregiver, disabled person, or lower-income resident realistically serve without private subsidy?
2. **Load test:** is the real burden occasional civic duty, steady part-time work, or a de facto full-time job in disguise?
3. **Dual-hat test:** does this office really need its own political class, or should it ride on a parent office or delegated mandate?
4. **Operating-work test:** is this role doing actual service delivery, enforcement, regulation, records, or technical support that should plainly be treated as paid labor?
5. **Capture test:** would unpaid or underpaid office quietly bias the pool toward landlords, retirees, employers, insiders, or donor-backed actors?
6. **Prestige test:** are we paying for real public burden, or manufacturing a symbolic office that exists mainly to flatter the scale?
7. **Outside-income test:** if the office is paid, are outside-employment and conflict rules clear enough that salary does not simply become an add-on to shadow influence?

If several answers are “we assume public spirit will solve it,” the design is likely drifting toward **wealth-gated office, shadow patronage, or fake part-time government**.

## Default office-status modes by scope

### 0. Household / association / cooperative

**Default office-status mode:** **volunteer service with expense reimbursement, and only light stipend where the burden becomes persistent or exclusionary.**

Use:
- unpaid member service for tiny and genuinely occasional roles,
- expense reimbursement for treasury, secretarial, or meeting burdens,
- and modest stipend once the role is absorbing enough time that only the already-comfortable can continue doing it.

The rule here is not “never pay.” It is “do not pretend a real job is still just goodwill.”

### 1. Micro-local (building / block / village / campus / frontline institution)

**Default office-status mode:** **volunteer or lightly stipended stewardship; paid part-time only where the burden is real and continuous.**

Use:
- volunteer rotation for tiny convening roles,
- reimbursement plus modest stipend where steward work includes repeated coordination, recordkeeping, incident logging, or maintenance follow-through,
- and paid part-time stewardship when the role is clearly no longer occasional.

The main anti-pattern here is unpaid local government that only retirees, property-holders, or machine insiders can actually sustain.

### 2. Municipal (city / town)

**Default office-status mode:** **mixed — representative politics may be part-time in smaller places, but the operating core must be salaried, and thick city leadership often becomes functionally full-time.**

Use:
- salaried professional managers, clerks, inspectors, and service operators as the default,
- representative compensation high enough that office is not wealth-gated,
- genuinely part-time council service only where the jurisdiction and workload remain small enough for that claim to stay honest,
- and clear conversion to full-time political office where the city’s budget, staff, emergency, and intergovernmental burden plainly require it.

No city should run complex frontline government by pretending the real work is still volunteerism.

### 2.5 Intermediate local administration (county / prefecture / province-lite backstop)

**Default office-status mode:** **delegated or mixed political leadership, paired with salaried full-time administrative backstop.**

Use:
- delegated or office-linked political roles where the layer mainly aggregates or coordinates municipalities,
- full-time salaried administrative staff where the layer runs courts support, social services, emergency coordination, records, or technical backstop,
- and stipend or per-diem logic for occasional committee or board participation rather than a prestige-political caste.

This layer often exists because continuity work is too heavy for volunteerism and too seam-heavy for pure localism.

### 3. Metropolitan / corridor / watershed / functional region

**Default office-status mode:** **delegated / dual-hatted political seats plus salaried operator staff.**

Use:
- member-jurisdiction representatives whose metro role is usually tied to another elected office,
- per-diem or bounded stipend for meeting-heavy delegated board work where appropriate,
- and a clearly salaried technical / executive operator for transit, water, planning, or other shared systems.

The metro anti-pattern is creating a separate fully salaried political class where coordination can instead be carried by delegated legitimacy and a professional operator.

### 4. Regional (state / province / land)

**Default office-status mode:** **salaried representative and executive politics, with professional full-time administration and bounded allowances rather than hidden patronage.**

Use:
- compensation and staffing that treat regional office as serious governing rather than ceremonial adjunct work,
- full-time salaried execution for ministries, regulators, and backstop services,
- and clear incompatibility / outside-income rules where authority, budget, and sector influence are substantial.

At this scale, pretending politics is still honorary often just hides class bias and lobby dependency.

### 5. National

**Default office-status mode:** **salaried, functionally full-time representative and executive office; salaried professional administration; no thick public office dependent on private wealth.**

Use:
- salaries and staff support that make national representative office realistically accessible beyond the rich,
- clear separation between political office, outside employment, lobbying, and business control,
- and salaried civil-service execution rather than patronage retainers.

The archive’s national rule is simple: **general-purpose state authority should not be an unpaid hobby.**

### 6. Supranational (treaty union / shared-rule space)

**Default office-status mode:** **directly representative or executive offices are salaried and full-time; delegated member-state seats usually remain office-linked; secretariat and court work is salaried professional work.**

Use:
- full-time salaried status for directly representative supranational members,
- delegated or home-system-funded status for member-state representatives whose legitimacy flows through national office,
- and professional salaried staffing for commissions, courts, agencies, and secretariats.

This scale usually needs a sharper split between **representation**, **delegation**, and **professional execution** than domestic politics does.

### 7. Global (planetary / treaty-complex level)

**Default office-status mode:** **state-funded or office-linked delegations for political negotiation; salaried secretariat and expert-support work for ongoing administration; stipend or honorarium only for bounded advisory participation.**

Use:
- member-state delegations for conferences and assemblies,
- salaried secretariats, inspectors, analysts, and implementation-support staff for continuous work,
- and bounded stipends / honoraria for advisory or review participation where needed for inclusion.

The global anti-pattern is twofold: unpaid elite diplomacy at one extreme, and a fake directly elected world political class at the other.

### Non-territorial & indigenous polities (cross-cutting)

**Default office-status mode:** **follow legitimate internal traditions where the burden is genuinely communal, but do not rely on unpaid insider labor once the office carries delegated public power, essential services, coercion, or major public funds.**

Use:
- voluntary or customary service where that remains live, legitimate, and non-exclusionary,
- reimbursement or stipend where participation burdens would otherwise narrow the officeholding pool,
- and salaried administrative or service roles where the polity is exercising ongoing public or compact-bound responsibilities.

Do not flatten every polity into the same salary grid. But do require honest disclosure when “tradition” is being used to hide inaccessible or uncompensated public labor.

## Cross-scope defaults worth carrying everywhere

1. **No thick public office should depend on private wealth.** If the job is real, payment or real time protection must also be real.
2. **Expense reimbursement is not the same as compensation.** Once the role reliably costs income, care time, or work hours, reimbursement alone is usually a fiction.
3. **Delegated compound bodies should usually dual-hat rather than spawn a new full-time political class.** Metro and intergovernmental coordination often need paid operators more than separately salaried politicians.
4. **Administrative and expert execution should usually be plainly salaried work.** Secret government often begins when real labor is disguised as volunteer civic duty.
5. **Citizen, sortition, and review participation may need stipends and supports even when not turned into offices.** Inclusion sometimes requires money, childcare, translation, travel, and time protection.
6. **Salary needs incompatibility and disclosure rules.** Paid office without outside-income and conflict discipline can still become bought government.
7. **Underpayment is a governance design choice, not a neutral budget fact.** It changes who can serve and who quietly governs.
