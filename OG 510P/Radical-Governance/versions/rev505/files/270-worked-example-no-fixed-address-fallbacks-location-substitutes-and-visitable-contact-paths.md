# Worked Example No-Fixed-Address Fallbacks, Location Substitutes, and Visitable Contact Paths

**Purpose:** make institutions usable for people whose lives are not organized around one stable residential address, without forcing them into invisibility or false-address workarounds.

**Person served:** someone staying with others, moving between shelters, living in a vehicle, using a P.O. box, relying on a trusted contact, or otherwise unable to supply one durable residential address in the format the system expects.

**From-below:** lack of a fixed address is not lack of a person, a contact path, or a legitimate claim on service.

---

## Use this memo when
- the person has no stable residential address but does have a reachable contact path;
- service, queue, or eligibility systems equate unstable address with no eligibility or no-contact status;
- field teams need a visitable location separate from a safe-contact or mailing path.

## Required artifacts
- **Location-substitute receipt** showing what the institution is using for contact, visit, mailing, service routing, and confidentiality.
- **Reachability packet** naming trusted contacts, shelters, service hubs, or scheduled meeting points.
- **No-fixed-address continuity rule** so address instability does not itself reset queue, identity, or service state.

## Safe defaults
- Reachability can be proven by more than one residential field.
- No-fixed-address status does not erase notice rights, queue rights, or service continuity.
- Where the institution cannot safely visit, it must not pretend the person was unreachable without a trace.
