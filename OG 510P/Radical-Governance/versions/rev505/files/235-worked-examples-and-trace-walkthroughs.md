# Worked Examples Casebook & Trace Walkthroughs

Treat `274-worked-example-to-domain-crosswalk.md` as the fast bridge back into the thematic memo stack when you know the case but need the architecture or rails.


**Purpose:** make the archive understandable through concrete, replayable cases that show what a person should be able to see, what the institution must emit, and what has to happen when something goes wrong.

**Person served:** readers who understand governance faster through lived cases than through abstractions, and people testing whether the archive would actually help on a bad day.

**From-below:** these examples exist so receipts, registers, queues, review clocks, stop rules, restoration proofs, and duty-to-respond rules cash out as something a person, advocate, clerk, journalist, auditor, or judge can actually follow.

**EXP pointer:** EXP-01 (Opacity), EXP-02 (Waiting), EXP-04 (Error), EXP-05 (Fear), EXP-07 (Threshold cliff), EXP-10 (Wrong door).

**Use note:** these are **worked traces**, not model laws. Adapt the artifacts to local law/institutions, but keep the accountability properties: visible decision owner, replayable basis, reachable remedy, deadline, interim protection where harm compounds, adverse inference when the institution cannot prove what it says happened, and follow-through that survives vendors, batches, and staff turnover.

1) **Situation:** what concrete event triggered power?
2) **Person path:** what should the affected person be able to learn and do quickly?
3) **Artifacts emitted:** what receipts, register entries, or continuity links must exist?
4) **Operator view:** what should a frontline worker or reviewer be able to inspect without guesswork?
5) **Oversight view:** what should an auditor, organizer, ombuds, journalist, or court be able to pattern-match?
6) **Smallest repair:** what is the minimum believable change that would materially improve the case right now?

Each case now has a stable **Worked Example ID** (`WX-*`) so domain memos can point to examples without repeating the whole story.

## WX-01 — Benefits cutoff after a risk score or income mismatch

**Situation:** a parent buying groceries discovers their cash-assistance payment did not arrive. A portal later shows “ineligible,” but the person cannot tell whether the problem is a documentation gap, a data mismatch, a threshold change, or a fraud score.

### What the person should be able to learn quickly
- what exactly changed, on what date, and whether the loss is temporary or ongoing;
- whether the issue is a rule change, a missing proof item, a data mismatch, or a metric/score;
- what can keep the household safe while review is pending; and
- who must answer by when, with a staffed/offline route if the person cannot navigate digitally.

### Minimum person path / trace
1) The adverse action emits a **Decision Receipt** (`DRR-*`) with the consequence, effective date, legal/rule basis (`RULE-*` as-of), reason code (`RC-*`), evidence class used, and the review lane (`31`, `36`, `39`, `52`).
2) If a metric, fraud score, or eligibility indicator materially affected the cutoff, the receipt also points to a **Metric Use Receipt** (`MUR-*`) naming the exact metric version (`MIC-*`), measured value/time window, uncertainty flags, and whether a human override occurred (`142`).
3) The person gets a **safe contestation path** with oral/offline filing, a deadline, help/representation options, and **interim protection** if missed timing or credible hardship would cause subsistence harm (`08`, `36`, `82`, `98`).
4) If the root cause is bad upstream data, the correction propagates append-only to dependent records/systems; the case is re-adjudicated and the propagation is visible (`31`, `53`, `70`, `127`).
5) If similar cases cluster, the pattern escalates into systemic redress and a program review rather than dying as isolated error correction (`76`, `28`, `133`).

### Smallest repair if the current regime is broken
Ship a paper-or-text **Decision Receipt** with a reason code, effective date, and staffed remedy lane first. Then add `MUR-*` only where metrics are materially shaping outcomes.

### Why this example matters
Without the `DRR/MUR/AL-*` chain, “computer says no” becomes unreviewable power.

## WX-02 — Disaster-aid queue transfer across county lines

**Situation:** after a flood, a household applies for temporary shelter and repair aid in County A, is told to evacuate to County B, and then discovers the move appears to reset their queue position and document review clock.

### What the person should be able to learn quickly
- whether their place in line and existing evidence traveled with them;
- which office now owns the case;
- what counts as enough proof in degraded conditions; and
- what happens if one county says “not ours” while the other says “start over.”

### Minimum person path / trace
1) The initial application creates a **Queue / Priority trace** that gives the person a stable reference number and records queue position class, priority basis, and review clock (`140`, `108`).
2) When the case crosses a boundary, the transfer emits a continuity artifact showing old owner, new owner, effective time, and a **non-reset default** for queue position and already-accepted evidence (`109`, `114`, `125`).
3) If state-held or already-verified facts exist, the household is not asked to rebuild the file from scratch; degraded-mode alternatives are published when normal documents are impossible to obtain (`37`, `44`, `127`).
4) If either county refuses ownership, the no-wrong-door rule routes the case through a coordination lane rather than forcing the person to shuttle manually between offices (`98`, `114`).
5) If shelter delay reaches a safety threshold, interim protection triggers automatically while the jurisdiction dispute is resolved (`82`, `112`, `63`).

### Smallest repair if the current regime is broken
Create a **transfer receipt** with non-reset queue continuity and a mandatory receiving-owner field before trying to harmonize every benefit rule.

### Why this example matters
Boundary changes are where humane policy often collapses into bureaucratic abandonment; continuity is not an administrative nicety but a survival property.

## WX-03 — Procurement award with a suspicious change order

**Situation:** a city awards a road contract to a bidder with a mediocre delivery record; six months later the contract price jumps by 28% through a change order justified as “unforeseen conditions.”

### Minimum person path / trace
1) The award decision publishes the decision basis, bid comparison logic, scoring or evaluation notes where allowed, and any conflict/integrity disclosures (`110`, `120`).
2) The contract itself is tied to a public money trace that lets a person follow budget line, contractor, amendments, and delivery milestones without reconstructing the whole file manually (`110`, `138`).
3) Any material amendment or change order emits a **change receipt** with reason, approver, scope delta, price delta, schedule delta, and a pointer to the original procurement basis (`110`, `118`, `138`).
4) If the justification is “unforeseen conditions,” the record distinguishes true new facts from design omission, under-scoping, or opportunistic renegotiation.
5) Repeat amendments, concentration, or delivery failures trigger audit / inspection review and possibly suspension or debarment consideration rather than being normalized as project management noise (`130`, `131`).

### Smallest repair if the current regime is broken
Version every material change order and tie it to the original award basis. If the public can only see the final contract value, capture is still hiding in the middle.

### Why this example matters
Money trails are often the clearest way to make elite discretion inspectable; without amendment traceability, capture hides after the ribbon-cutting.

## WX-04 — Detention or enforcement action with risk-scoring and delay risk

**Situation:** a person is detained after a stop and told there is a “risk flag” or “watchlist concern,” but family and counsel cannot tell whether the basis is identity confusion, a stale warrant, a risk tool, or a manual override.

### Minimum person path / trace
1) Any deprivation of liberty or coercive hold emits a custody / detention receipt with legal basis, time of hold, responsible unit, review clock, and contact/remedy information (`116`, `31`, `36`).
2) If a risk score, watchlist, or automated classification contributed materially, the person-side record includes the **use category**, version or source class, confidence/uncertainty flags, and a path to challenge identity mismatch or stale data (`142`, `127`).
3) The review clock is visible and enforceable; delay itself is contestable, with escalation and interim protections for health, counsel access, family notification, and translation (`82`, `98`, `116`).
4) Serious incidents, prolonged holds, or repeated false positives route automatically into independent review rather than staying inside the same command chain (`32`, `130`, `116`).
5) If a systemic pattern emerges—same watchlist source, same shift, same unit—the pattern becomes an audit/remediation object, not just a sequence of individual complaints (`76`, `130`, `133`).

### Smallest repair if the current regime is broken
Publish custody review clocks and a challengeable identity/error lane before attempting a perfect full-custody dashboard.

### Why this example matters
Coercive power is where hidden metrics become most dangerous; replayability is a liberty safeguard, not a nice-to-have.

## WX-05 — Utility outage and restoration prioritization after a storm

**Situation:** a storm knocks out power and water across several neighborhoods. Residents are told crews are restoring service “by priority,” but no one can tell whether priority reflects hospitals and safety-critical loads, political pressure, or neighborhood status.

### Minimum person path / trace
1) The outage is logged as a joinable **outage/degradation event** with affected area, start time, cause class, operator, and next review/update time (`137`, `59`).
2) If scarcity or restoration priority rules are activated, the operator publishes the priority class logic and any queue/priority receipts used to order restoration (`137`, `140`).
3) Safety-critical exceptions (dialysis, cooling, water, medical devices, shelters) create visible obligations for alternative service or emergency support rather than generic apology notices (`82`, `57`, `63`).
4) Material changes in restoration forecast emit versioned updates so prior promises remain inspectable (`53`, `118`).
5) Repeated outages or maintenance deferrals trigger asset-integrity and oversight review rather than being normalized as weather fate (`48`, `130`, `139`).

### Smallest repair if the current regime is broken
Version outage updates and publish restoration priority classes first. Perfect geospatial dashboards are optional; non-silent change histories are not.

### Why this example matters
Scarcity and restoration order are governance decisions wearing an engineering costume; if the priority logic is hidden, the hardship distribution is hidden too.

## WX-06 — School discipline or special-support placement driven by poor data joins

**Situation:** a student is excluded from class or routed into a restrictive support track because attendance, behavior, or testing records were merged incorrectly across schools or systems. The family is told the placement is “policy,” but cannot inspect the underlying record.

### Minimum person path / trace
1) Any exclusion, suspension, or restrictive placement emits a **Decision Receipt** with duration, legal/policy basis, record/evidence class used, and remedy lane (`31`, `36`, `68`).
2) If the decision relies on joined records, the family can see which upstream record category drove the outcome and how to correct it without triggering punitive delay (`31`, `70`, `127`).
3) If a score, risk band, or attendance threshold materially affected the decision, the case links to the metric/version used and its allowed purpose (`03`, `142`).
4) The student retains continuity protections: alternative instruction, schedule continuity, and non-reset review rights while contest is pending (`82`, `98`, `109`).
5) Repeated misplacement by school, subgroup, or data source escalates to systemic review rather than leaving each family to fight alone (`76`, `133`).

### Smallest repair if the current regime is broken
Make restrictive placement and suspension receipts mandatory, with a visible correction lane for underlying records, before trying to solve every education-data problem at once.

### Why this example matters
Children and families often encounter hidden administrative power through schools first; if data joins are contest-proof there, the lesson learned is obedience to error.

## WX-07 — Deliberation that actually changes a zoning or siting decision

**Situation:** a city runs a residents’ assembly on a rezoning and bus-depot siting plan. Residents participate, but they have seen “engagement” used before as theater for pre-decided outcomes.

### Minimum person path / trace
1) The process publishes its **scope of influence**, decision owner, response deadline, evidence-pack rules, and standing/safety rules before participation begins (`111`, `41`).
2) The final recommendations are logged as a deliberation record (`ENG-*`) with minority reports where relevant (`41`, `88`, `111`).
3) The binding authority answers recommendation-by-recommendation using the response taxonomy (**ADOPT / ADOPT-WITH-MODS / REJECT / DEFER / PARTITION**) in a stable **Decision Receipt** (`DRR-*`) rather than a generic summary note (`111`, `31`).

## WX-55 — False positive death, custody, departure, or sanctions match suspends a live person

**Situation:** a severe external or internal match marks a living person as dead, detained, removed, sanctioned, or otherwise disqualified, and downstream systems instantly act on it.

### Minimum person path / trace
1) treat the severe match as tentative until a second source or accountable human verification confirms it (`43`, `125`, `266`);
2) emit an immediate false-positive protection receipt and freeze downstream cutoffs, extractions, or enforcement while identity is contested (`252`, `253`, `266`);
3) require same-day override propagation to every known consumer of the severe state (`53`, `109`, `253`, `266`);
4) open an audit/replay incident for each false positive in a severe-match class (`130`, `257`, `266`).

### Smallest repair if the current regime is broken
Adopt a two-source severe-match rule plus a same-day kill switch and propagation proof.

### Why this example matters
Few bureaucratic errors are more violent than falsely treating a live person as legally gone or disqualified everywhere at once.

## WX-56 — Mailing, residence, safe-contact, or unit-level address mismatch routes notice or service to the wrong place

**Situation:** the institution has the right person but the wrong address role. A mailing address, residence address, safe-contact address, service location, or unit number gets conflated with another, and notice, inspection, delivery, routing, or territory checks happen at the wrong place.

### Minimum person path / trace
1) emit an address-role receipt that shows which location is being used for notice, residence, service, inspection, and jurisdiction (`47`, `98`, `109`, `267`);
2) preserve the last safe contact path while the address-role dispute is corrected (`82`, `98`, `267`);
3) where multi-unit ambiguity exists, require unit-level resolution before high-consequence action reaches the wrong door (`136`, `269`);
4) treat safe-contact divergence as a protective design feature, not a presumption of fraud (`77`, `267`).

### Smallest repair if the current regime is broken
Separate address roles in the person-facing receipt and block punitive action at the wrong unit or contact path.

### Why this example matters
Many institutions do not lose the person; they lose which place about that person is doing which job.

## WX-57 — District, territory, catchment, or coverage-boundary mismatch denies service or bounces the person between systems

**Situation:** a school, utility, clinic, shelter network, or other service says the person is out of area, out of district, or outside coverage because of a stale boundary file, a disputed map edge, or mismatched territory logic between systems.

### Minimum person path / trace
1) emit a boundary-basis receipt naming the map/version, governing rule, and appeal lane (`71`, `109`, `268`);
2) preserve continuity while the boundary seam is reviewed rather than bouncing the person between agencies or providers (`82`, `140`, `268`);
3) show whether the problem is map drift, address resolution, service-territory lag, or conflicting zone files (`109`, `128`, `268`);
4) audit repeated wrong-door and wrong-territory incidents by map version, provider, and geography (`03`, `130`, `268`).

### Smallest repair if the current regime is broken
Force every territory denial to cite the controlling map and keep a grace routing rule while the seam is contested.

### Why this example matters
A hidden boundary file can decide access just as harshly as an overt denial.

## WX-58 — Parcel, geocode, frontage, or unit mismatch triggers wrong-place action or wrong-place eligibility

**Situation:** the institution acts on the wrong parcel, wrong structure, wrong frontage, wrong unit, or low-confidence geocode and a person bears the consequence because the place-resolution layer never became visible.

### Minimum person path / trace
1) emit a place-resolution packet showing parcel, address, unit, geocode, and confidence class (`136`, `269`);
2) no high-consequence action should harden where the institution cannot distinguish parcel-level from unit-level responsibility (`82`, `136`, `269`);
3) keep a wrong-place challenge lane with an immediate protective hold where the risk is displacement, billing, citation, or service loss (`36`, `252`, `269`);
4) version map/geocoder inputs and audit repeated wrong-place incidents by source system and contractor (`53`, `128`, `130`, `269`).

### Smallest repair if the current regime is broken
Treat place resolution as a governed packet with a confidence threshold, not as invisible background machinery.

### Why this example matters
Wrong-place action is often described as a small data error when it is the whole reason the wrong person gets hit.

## WX-59 — No-fixed-address or alternate-contact-path person is treated as unreachable, ineligible, or outside service logic

**Situation:** a person staying in shelters, vehicles, multiple homes, temporary placements, or with rotating hosts can still be reachable and still need service, but the system equates unstable residential address with no-contact or no-eligibility.

### Minimum person path / trace
1) emit a location-substitute receipt showing what is being used for mailing, safe contact, visit, service routing, and confidentiality (`47`, `98`, `109`, `270`);
2) prove reachability through trusted contacts, service hubs, shelters, scheduled check-ins, or other visitable paths rather than requiring one stable residential field (`31`, `98`, `270`);
3) preserve queue, identity, and service continuity while address instability is resolved or re-verified (`82`, `109`, `140`, `270`);
4) treat “no fixed address” as a design condition the system must handle, not as evidence of abandonment (`249`, `250`, `270`).

### Smallest repair if the current regime is broken
Create a location-substitute and reachability packet, then stop using unstable residence as shorthand for no-person and no-contact.

### Why this example matters
Address instability is one of the easiest ways institutions quietly strip a person of notice, place, and standing all at once.
