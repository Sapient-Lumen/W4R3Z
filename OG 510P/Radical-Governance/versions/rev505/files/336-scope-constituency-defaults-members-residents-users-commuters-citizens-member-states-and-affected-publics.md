# 336 — Scope-Constituency Defaults: Members, Residents, Users, Commuters, Citizens, Member States, and Affected Publics

This memo is the scale-to-relevant-public / who-counts front door; `14` / `176` explain cross-scope logic, `285` gives the default bundle language, `327` handles the scale-sensitive institutional form, `328` handles the scale-sensitive legitimacy channel, `329` handles the scale-sensitive tempo / time-horizon choice, `330` handles the scale-sensitive epistemic / evidence-mode choice, `331` handles the scale-sensitive accountability / review-mode choice, `332` handles the scale-sensitive coercion / direct-force-holding choice, `333` handles the scale-sensitive finance / revenue-model choice, `334` handles the scale-sensitive operator / staffing-pattern choice, `335` handles the scale-sensitive public artifact surface, and `141` / `147` / `125` / `109` / `203` handle the deeper representation / membership / portability / recognition substrate once the scale-fit constituency question has been identified.

**Purpose:** answer the design question that remains after scope assignment, bundle design, form choice, legitimacy choice, tempo choice, evidence choice, accountability choice, coercion choice, finance choice, operator choice, and artifact choice: **who is the relevant public at this scale?**

**Why this memo exists:** `14`, `176`, `285`, and `327-335` explain what each scale owns, what shape it should take, how it should be legitimated, what clock it should run on, what evidence it should mainly govern with, how it should be checked, which scales should directly hold force, what finance model should dominate there, what operator should usually run it, and what public surface should usually make it visible. But the archive still needed one compact memo that says **scale should also change who mainly counts there by default** — members, residents, regular users, commuters, territorial populations, citizens, member states, directly affected people, or future generations.

**From-below:** this memo is a guardrail against constituency confusion. It helps stop neighborhood bodies from confusing property titles with the whole lived public, municipalities from ignoring regular users and commuters, metro bodies from pretending only member governments matter, national governments from collapsing service and rights standing into citizenship alone, supranational regimes from treating state consent as the only relevant public, and global governance from oscillating between a fake singular world demos and technocracy with no public at all.

**Evidence anchors:** the Council of Europe’s Charter framing still treats local democracy as the level closest to the citizen and ties local self-government to the right of citizens to participate in managing public affairs plus community autonomy [BIB-COE-LOCAL-SELF-GOV-2026] [BIB-COE-CHARTER-HOW-IT-WORKS-2026]; OECD’s functional-urban-area definition still treats commuting flows as constitutive of the real urban public rather than an afterthought outside administrative borders [BIB-OECD-FUA-2026]; Article 10 TEU still states that citizens are directly represented at Union level while Member States are represented in the European Council and Council, which is the clearest compact statement of supranational dual legitimacy in current positive law [BIB-EURLEX-TEU-ART10-2026]; the UN Charter still begins from “We the peoples” rather than a unitary world electorate [BIB-UN-CHARTER]; and the Pact for the Future / Declaration on Future Generations now makes future generations explicit subjects of present governance rather than rhetorical background only [BIB-UN-PACT-FUTURE-2024] [BIB-UN-DECL-FUTURE-GENERATIONS-2024].

## Core claim

Every scale needs a **default public**.

Not because every scope needs one simple electorate, but because institutions become confused and abusive when they cannot say who mainly authorizes them, who mainly bears their burdens, who mainly uses them, and who has standing to challenge them.

As scale changes, the relevant public also changes:
- lower scopes should default to **members, residents, occupants, and directly affected users in the place**,
- municipal and intermediate local scales should default to **residents plus regular service users and seam-crossers**,
- metropolitan and functional scales should default to **residents plus commuters, network users, and affected member jurisdictions**,
- regional scales should default to **territorial residents plus constituent local governments / communities**,
- national scales should distinguish **citizenship as the main constitutional authorization public** from **all persons / residents as the rights-and-service public**,
- supranational scales should default to **dual or compound publics: citizens / peoples and member states**,
- and global scales should default to **states, peoples, affected humanity, and future generations**, without pretending that one thick world demos already exists.

So the archive’s rule is:
**the farther power moves from the person, the less honest it is to pretend there is one simple constituency; the closer power is to the person, the less honest it is to ignore the people actually living with the consequence.**

---

## The anti-fake-demos / anti-wrong-public test

Before creating or reforming a government at any scale, ask six questions:

1. **Authorization test:** who mainly authorizes this body — members, residents, citizens, member jurisdictions, or treaty parties?
2. **Burden test:** who mainly bears its costs, constraints, or exclusions in practice?
3. **Use test:** who regularly depends on the service, system, territory, or commons being governed here?
4. **Standing test:** who must be able to complain, appeal, or trigger review even if they are not the voting public?
5. **Flow test:** does this function follow residence, membership, commuting, network use, watershed exposure, or humanity-wide externalities rather than a single neat border?
6. **Scale-fit test:** are you pretending there is a singular demos where there is really a compound public, or pretending there is no public where there is in fact a clearly affected one?

If the answer to several of these is “we do not know” or “only the formal electorate counts”, the design is likely suffering from **constituency confusion, fake demos, or no-voice technocracy**.

## Default constituency modes by scope

### 0. Household / association / cooperative

**Default constituency mode:** **members and directly affected dependents**.

Use:
- membership as the main authorization public,
- clear standing for co-residents / dependents materially affected by decisions,
- and explicit exit or external-law escalation when internal governance becomes coercive.

Increase constituency thickness only when the body holds shared assets, employs staff, or exercises delegated public power.

**Avoid:** pretending a private association speaks for a territorial public it does not actually represent.

### 1. Micro-local (building / block / village / campus / frontline institution)

**Default constituency mode:** **people in the place** — residents, occupants, regular users, and immediately adjacent affected persons.

This scale works best when it treats the relevant public as the people who actually live, move, study, work, or rely on the place, not merely title-holders or the loudest incumbents.

Use:
- open local voice for the people actually present,
- special care for tenants, students, shift workers, and others who use the place without owning it,
- and clear rules for when place-voice ends and municipal rights floors take over.

**Avoid:** property-only localism, closed chats as public reason, or steward capture by the most permanently present faction.

### 2. Municipal (city / town / county-equivalent frontline state)

**Default constituency mode:** **residents as the main political public, plus regular users as a standing-bearing service public**.

Municipal government is usually the everyday operator of services, streets, schools, clinics, licensing, and local enforcement. That means the people who count here are not only formal local electors, but also the persons who regularly depend on the city’s service surface, including many commuters, students, unhoused people, and cross-boundary users.

Use:
- residents for the main representative authorization story,
- service standing and consultation rights for non-resident regular users where city systems materially govern them,
- and explicit non-discrimination / access rules so municipal government does not become a club for formal insiders only.

**Avoid:** treating municipal citizenship or title-holding as the only relevant public when actual service dependence is broader.

### 3. Intermediate local administration (county / prefecture pattern)

**Default constituency mode:** **residents across the covered localities, seam-crossing service claimants, and member municipalities as represented units**.

This layer mainly exists to stabilize continuity across smaller municipalities. Its relevant public is therefore mixed: the people receiving boundary-spanning services plus the local units whose continuity it backstops.

Use:
- territorial residents for service entitlement,
- municipalities for intergovernmental representation,
- and no-wrong-door standing for people who started in one locality and got bounced into another.

**Avoid:** invisible administrative power that is legible only to insiders or mayors.

### 4. Metropolitan / corridor / watershed / functional region

**Default constituency mode:** **residents plus commuters, network users, and functionally affected jurisdictions / communities**.

Metro and functional authorities exist because the real public is wider than one municipal electorate. Labor markets, transit systems, watersheds, air basins, housing markets, and grids are used by people who cross local boundaries every day [BIB-OECD-FUA-2026].

Use:
- residents and commuters for urban-system design,
- upstream/downstream or corridor exposure for ecological and infrastructure systems,
- member-jurisdiction representation where the authority is delegated rather than directly elected,
- and direct elections only when the metro body holds broad powers that justify a thicker political public.

**Avoid:** city-core majoritarianism over the wider functional region, or delegated metro bodies that ignore the actual commuting / network public they govern.

### 5. Regional (state / province / land / autonomous territory)

**Default constituency mode:** **territorial residents plus constituent local governments / territorial communities**.

The regional level usually coordinates systems too wide for municipalities but still territorially grounded. Its relevant public therefore includes both persons living in the region and the municipalities / local communities whose capacities, equalization, and standards it structures.

Use:
- regional residents for democratic authorization,
- territorial communities and municipalities for structured voice in shared standards and transfers,
- and stronger recognition rules where the region is plurinational, linguistic, islanded, or asymmetrically autonomous.

**Avoid:** treating regions as either pure branch offices of the center or as culturally singular polities when the actual regional public is more mixed and territorially layered.

### 6. National

**Default constituency mode:** **citizens as the main constitutional authorization public; all persons and residents as the basic rights-and-service public**.

The national level is where the archive most sharply separates **authorization** from **standing**. National constitutions, elections, taxation, and sovereignty stories are usually citizen-centered. But the state’s basic duties around due process, emergency power, public administration, education, health, labor enforcement, and ordinary legal protection are not honestly describable as owed only to citizens.

Use:
- citizens for the main constitutional representation story,
- residents and all persons under jurisdiction for rights floors, administrative justice, and service standing appropriate to law,
- and explicit publication of where citizenship matters, where residence matters, and where personhood alone is enough to trigger protection.

**Avoid:** collapsing the whole state into a citizen-only club, or conversely dissolving constitutional accountability into pure service managerialism.

### 7. Supranational (treaty / union level)

**Default constituency mode:** **a compound public of citizens / peoples and member states**.

This is the archive default because supranational governance is usually too strong to rest only on state consent and too distant to pretend it has one ordinary national-style demos. The honest design answer is compound legitimacy and compound constituency: citizens or peoples directly represented in some channel, member states represented in another, and clear standing for persons materially affected by the regime [BIB-EURLEX-TEU-ART10-2026].

Use:
- citizen / people representation where common law or budget power is real,
- member-state representation where conferral and treaty responsibility matter,
- and rights / complaint standing for affected persons even where franchise remains nationally mediated.

**Avoid:** either pure diplomatic intergovernmentalism that leaves citizens unrepresented, or fake federal plebiscitary claims unsupported by conferral and public identification.

### 8. Global (planetary)

**Default constituency mode:** **states, peoples, affected humanity, and future generations — but rarely one thick demos**.

The global layer governs public goods and catastrophic externalities that reach beyond any one electorate. Its relevant public is therefore plural by default: states as legal parties, peoples as the moral and political horizon, directly affected populations where harms are uneven, and future generations where irreversibility is high [BIB-UN-CHARTER] [BIB-UN-PACT-FUTURE-2024] [BIB-UN-DECL-FUTURE-GENERATIONS-2024].

Use:
- states for treaty consent and implementation duties,
- peoples / humanity language for legitimacy boundaries and public justification,
- affected-public participation for domains with unequal burdens,
- Indigenous and local-community knowledge channels where planetary governance meets lived systems,
- and future-generation institutions / standing where the harm profile crosses generations.

**Avoid:** both world-state fantasy and elite-secretariat rule without affected-public pathways.

## Quick defaults table

| Scope | Default public | Main warning |
|---|---|---|
| Household / association | Members + directly affected dependents | Do not fake territorial publichood |
| Micro-local | People in the place | Do not reduce the public to owners / insiders |
| Municipal | Residents + regular users | Do not ignore service dependence across lines |
| Intermediate local | Residents + seam-crossers + municipalities | Do not become an invisible administrative empire |
| Metropolitan / functional | Residents + commuters + network users + affected jurisdictions | Do not govern a functional region as if one city alone were the public |
| Regional | Territorial residents + constituent communities / municipalities | Do not flatten territorial pluralism |
| National | Citizens for authorization; persons / residents for rights and services | Do not collapse the state into a citizen-only club |
| Supranational | Citizens / peoples + member states | Do not pretend one channel alone is enough |
| Global | States + peoples + affected humanity + future generations | Do not oscillate between fake demos and no public |

## Design rule

When in doubt, specify four constituency layers explicitly:
1. **authorization public** (who confers authority),
2. **service public** (who depends on delivery),
3. **burdened public** (who bears costs / restrictions / exposure),
4. **standing public** (who can trigger review and remedy).

At low scales, those four often overlap.
At distant scales, they usually do **not**.

That is why the right design goal is not “one constituency everywhere.”
It is **honest constituency mapping at the right scale**.
