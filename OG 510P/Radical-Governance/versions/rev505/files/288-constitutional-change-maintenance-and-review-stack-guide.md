# 288 — Constitutional Change, Maintenance, and Review Stack Guide

**Purpose:** turn the archive’s constitutional change cluster into a readable stack so amendment rails, standing maintenance, entrenchment discipline, and constitutional review observability stop competing for the same conceptual role.

**Why this memo exists:** `124`, `171`, `205`, and `206` all surface for “constitutional reform” questions because they describe one family from different layers: amendment-event discipline, recurring maintenance, entrenchment theory, and review/compliance visibility. The archive should name those handoffs explicitly rather than invite flattening.

**Evidence anchors:** constitutional amendment discipline and legitimacy of formal procedures [BIB-VENICE-CONST-AMEND-2010]; referendum clarity / fairness / declared thresholds [BIB-VENICE-REFERENDUM-2022]; checks and balances plus constitutional review as explicit contemporary rule-of-law benchmarks [BIB-VENICE-ROL-2025]; comparative amendment-design choices and recurring reform patterns [BIB-IDEA-CONST-AMEND-PRIMER] [BIB-IDEA-ANNUAL-CONST-2024].

## Canonical reading order

### 1. Start with `206-constitutional-change-and-amendment-rails.md`
Use `206` when the question is:
- how a constitutional change packet should be formed,
- what anti-bundling, cooling-off, certification, and Harder Path triggers should exist,
- and what the minimum viable amendment-event discipline looks like.

`206` is the **canonical front door for concrete constitutional change control**.

### 2. Move to `171-constitutional-maintenance-and-amendment-ops.md`
Use `171` when the issue becomes:
- how constitutional maintenance is kept out of crisis mode,
- how periodic review dockets, agenda-setting, and repair vs redesign tracks work,
- and how a polity keeps foundational change governable between headline amendment fights.

`171` is the **standing maintenance and review-operations layer**.

### 3. Use `124-constitutional-amendment-and-entrenchment.md`
Use `124` when the real question is:
- what entrenchment is for,
- how foundational change should differ by scope and risk tier,
- how anti-bundle and no-stealth rules protect legitimacy,
- and when “constitutional emergency” or identity-of-the-people fights require extra caution.

`124` is the **broader foundational-change and entrenchment memo**.

### 4. Use `205-constitutional-review-observability-and-precedent-ledgers.md`
Use `205` when the key issue is:
- how constitutional review becomes inspectable,
- how precedent, dissents, remedies, and compliance status are published,
- and how review avoids becoming a black box whose holdings are visible but whose implementation is not.

`205` is the **review-observability and compliance-loop memo**.

## Confusion boundaries

### `206` vs `171`
- `206` is about the **amendment event itself**: packets, thresholds, certification, anti-bundling, reversibility classes.
- `171` is about the **standing maintenance system**: scheduled review, agenda discipline, repair vs redesign, and amendment operations over time.

### `124` vs `206`
- `124` explains the **broader normative logic** of constitutional change, entrenchment, and scope-sensitive legitimacy.
- `206` specifies the **concrete operating rails** for a given amendment packet.

### `171` / `206` vs `205`
- `171` and `206` govern **how change is proposed, processed, and adopted**.
- `205` governs **how constitutional review and remedy execution are made visible after or during dispute**.

### `205` vs `156`
- `205` is about **observability, precedent ledgers, remedy tracking, and compliance visibility**.
- `156-judicial-systems-and-constitutional-review.md` remains the memo for **institutional design of constitutional review bodies themselves**.

## Retrieval guidance (what to cite when)

- Cite `206` for **change packets, anti-bundling, cooling-off, procedural certification, and Harder Path triggers**.
- Cite `171` for **scheduled review dockets, maintenance cadence, agenda-setting rights, and repair vs redesign tracks**.
- Cite `124` for **entrenchment theory, scope-sensitive constitutional change, anti-stealth rules, and foundational-change legitimacy**.
- Cite `205` for **precedent ledgers, decision cards, compliance plans, and constitutional-review observability**.
- Cite `156` when the real question is **court / chamber design, appointment design, or the institutional placement of constitutional review**.
- Cite `308` when the real question is **caretaker limits, acting authority, or continuity of office during political transition rather than constitutional amendment itself**. Use `314` when the narrower problem is how a government is actually formed after seats exist — investiture proof, minority support disclosure, or deadlock fallback.

## Safe merge rule for this cluster

Do **not** flatten this family into one mega-memo on “constitutional reform”.
The safer pattern is:
1. keep `206` as the amendment-event front door,
2. keep `171` as the maintenance and docket layer,
3. keep `124` as the broader entrenchment and foundational-change memo,
4. keep `205` as review/compliance observability,
5. keep `156` nearby as the institutional-design sibling,
6. and use this memo as the bridge that names the roles.

That preserves distinct retrieval hooks while sharply reducing reader confusion.
