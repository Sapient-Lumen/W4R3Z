# Rule of Law Operational Checklist (Minimal, Testable)

**Purpose:** turn "rule of law" from slogan into **operational checks** that any jurisdiction can run and publish without pretending the checklist is a single number.

**Key idea:** publish a **Rule-of-Law Self‑Assessment Packet (RoLSAP)** on a fixed cadence (e.g., yearly), with:
- *claims* (what is true),
- *evidence pointers* (where to verify),
- *exceptions* (where it breaks),
- *remediation plan* (what changes next).

This memo is designed to **compose** with existing archive rails:
- disclosure clocks / refusal receipts (`115`, `200`)
- audit & inspection integrity (`130`)
- dispute resolution & access to justice (`195`)
- emergency governance & exceptions (`23`)

---

## A. The RoLSAP packet (publishable artifact)

A RoLSAP is a **small bundle** (a few pages + links) containing:

1. **Scope + institutions map**
 - jurisdiction boundary, courts/tribunals, prosecutors, police, regulators
 - constitutional review venue(s) and standing rules

2. **Core chapters (checklist)**
 - *Legality* (rules exist, are knowable, and bind officials)
 - *Legal certainty* (stability, non‑retroactivity discipline)
 - *Prevention of abuse of powers* (anti‑capture gates, conflict rules, oversight)
 - *Equality before the law / non‑discrimination*
 - *Access to justice* (independent courts, effective remedies)
 - *Checks & balances* (separation, oversight, emergency constraints)
 - *Constitutional review* (effective review with published reasons)

 These chapter headings track the Venice Commission’s updated rule‑of‑law checklist structure and can be used as a **common schema** for publishing and comparison.

3. **Evidence pointer table**
 - for each claim: **stable pointer** to statutes, policy, case law, audit reports, metrics dashboards
 - include change‑control receipts (`118`) for the last period

4. **Exception register**
 - enumerated “known failures” (backlogs, enforcement gaps, selective prosecution signals, etc.)
 - **time‑boxed** with escalation owner and deadline

5. **Remediation plan**
 - top 3 risks
 - concrete fixes
 - next publish date

**Rule:** a RoLSAP must be *auditable by outsiders* using only the pointers provided (or refusal receipts explaining why not).

---

## B. Minimal chapter checks (tests, not essays)

### B1. Legality (rules bind officials)
- Every coercive action type has a **Mandate Card** + lawful authority pointer (`132`).
- All binding rules are discoverable (registry / gazette) and versioned (`118`).
- Significant discretionary programs publish **decision receipts** and appeal lanes (`195`).

### B2. Legal certainty
- Retroactive rulemaking is forbidden except under tightly enumerated conditions, with review.
- “Emergency” powers are time‑boxed, reviewable, and revocable (`23`).
- Individuals can obtain **clarification** and **correction** for official records (`127`).

### B3. Prevention of abuse of powers
- Conflict‑of‑interest rules are enforced with case files and sanctions ladder (`120`, `131`).
- Independent inspectors/auditors have protected budgets, access, and publication duties (`130`).
- Influence integrity: lobbying / gifts / revolving‑door disclosures are complete and timely (`120`).

### B4. Equality before the law
- Enforcement is measurable for **selectivity / disparate impact**; publish analysis and remediation.
- Non‑discrimination claims have a usable complaint path and meaningful remedies (`195`).

### B5. Access to justice
- Courts/tribunals meet **service SLOs** (time to hearing / decision) with backlog transparency (`193`).
- Legal aid coverage rules are public; denial requires a reason + appeal lane.
- Outcomes are published in a privacy‑safe way; precedent is searchable (see `205`).

### B6. Checks & balances
- Core oversight bodies cannot be removed or defunded without a public “removal packet” and review.
- Budgets, procurement, and appointments publish **decision + change receipts** (`179`, `118`).

### B7. Constitutional review
- Review exists, is reachable (standing rules), and publishes reasons.
- When review is blocked, an alternative remedy exists (e.g., ombuds or supra‑court channel).

## C. Publication and comparability

The RoLSAP should be published in:
- **human‑readable** format
- **machine‑readable index** (a small JSON listing the chapter claims + evidence pointers)

This makes rule‑of‑law governance comparable without relying on a single contested index.

---

## D. Failure modes and tripwires

Use these early warning signals:
- sudden rise in sealed decisions without published reasons
- unexplained prosecutor declination swings
- selective enforcement indicators
- emergency renewals without independent review
- growing court backlog without remediation plan

When tripwires trigger, require:
- public incident write‑up (cause, scope, remedial actions)
- independent review assignment
- deadline + follow‑up publication
