# 328 — Scope-Legitimacy Defaults: Voice, Elections, Delegation, Dual Legitimacy, and Verification

**Cross-stack note:** use `306-scope-design-ideal-government-and-reference-bundles-guide.md` for the canonical route across the scope-design family. This memo is the scale-to-legitimacy-channel front door; `14` / `176` explain the cross-scope logic, `285` gives the minimum-bundle language, `327` gives the institutional-form default, `329` gives the tempo / time-horizon default, `330` gives the epistemic / evidence-mode default, `331` gives the accountability / review-mode default, `332` gives the coercion / direct-force-holding default, `333` gives the finance / revenue-model default, `336` gives the constituency / who-counts default, `21` gives the broad legitimacy architecture, and `180` compares legitimacy engines in the abstract.

**Purpose:** answer the design question that remains after scope assignment, bundle design, and institutional-form choice: **what legitimacy channel should usually dominate at each scale?**

**Why this memo exists:** `14`, `54`, `176`, `285`, and `327` explain where functions belong, what minimum bundle a government needs, and what institutional shape usually fits the scale. `21` and `180` explain legitimacy in general and compare elections, deliberation, sortition, and recall. But the archive still lacked one compact memo answering the narrower practical question: **at a given scope, should legitimacy come mainly from assembly visibility, mass elections, delegated intergovernmental consent, dual legitimacy, or review-and-verification machinery?**

**From-below:** this memo is a guardrail against fake democracy in both directions. It helps stop small scales from being stripped of visible voice in the name of efficiency, and it helps stop large coordinating scales from borrowing plebiscitary symbolism that their real powers, publics, and remedy paths cannot honestly sustain.

**Evidence anchors:** local self-government standards still treat local authorities as bodies with political, administrative, and financial independence and require election through universal suffrage [BIB-COE-LOCAL-SELF-GOV-2026]; OECD’s 2024 trust survey and 2025 *Government at a Glance* updates keep stressing that political voice and efficacy are among the strongest drivers of trust [BIB-OECD-TRUST-2024] [BIB-OECD-GAAG-TRUST-VOICE-2025] [BIB-OECD-POLITICAL-EFFICACY-2025]; OECD’s current participation stocktake also shows that recent representative deliberative processes are concentrated at local and regional levels and are more often permanently institutionalized there [BIB-OECD-GAAG-DELIB-2025]; metropolitan governance remains strongest where functions create region-wide benefits, externalities, redistribution needs, or scale economies [BIB-WB-METROGOV-2020]; regional governance guidance keeps emphasizing territorial scale, co-production, multi-level governance, and integrity rather than one fixed constitutional template [BIB-OECD-REGIONAL-GOV-2022] [BIB-OECD-RDP-TOOLKIT-2026]; and current UN reform language still points toward multilateral compacts, digital-cooperation frameworks, and future-generations duties rather than a unitary global demos [BIB-UN-PACT-FUTURE-2024] [BIB-UN-GDC] [BIB-UN-DFG].

---

## Core claim

Every scale needs legitimacy, but **not every scale should use the same dominant legitimacy channel**.

The archive default is:
- **the smaller and closer the scale, the more legitimacy should come from visibility, participation, and recallability;**
- **the more general-purpose, coercive, and redistributive the scale, the more legitimacy should come from competitive elections plus rights and remedy;**
- **the more coordinating and cross-border the scale, the more legitimacy should be compound, delegated, and review-heavy rather than plebiscitary;**
- **the more planetary and treaty-based the scale, the more legitimacy should come from consent, verification, finance, public reason, and due-process review rather than theatrical electoral claims.**

So the question is never just “should there be elections?” It is:
**what channel can honestly authorize this scale’s powers, correct its failures, and match the public it actually governs?**

## The archive’s legitimacy-channel defaults by scope

### 0. Household / association / cooperative

**Default legitimacy channel:** **membership voice and consent**.

Use:
- an assembly or equivalent common forum,
- transparent voting or consensus rules,
- recallable stewards,
- and legible handling of money, membership, and sanctions.

At this scale, legitimacy mainly comes from **presence, consent, and easy reversibility**.

**Avoid:** permanent officeholders whose authority feels electoral but is not actually contestable.

### 1. Micro-local (building / block / village / campus / frontline institution)

**Default legitimacy channel:** **open participation plus short-loop accountability**.

At micro-local scale, legitimacy should come mainly from:
- open meetings or visible forums,
- rotation, lot, or short-term election for small governing roles,
- a named steward who can be challenged quickly,
- and an immediate escalation path when local power becomes coercive or exclusionary.

This is the scale where people most need to feel that they can **show up, understand what happened, and get an answer without a constitutional pilgrimage**. Direct elections can exist, but the stronger legitimacy asset is usually visible participation and short feedback loops, not a heavy electoral narrative [BIB-IDEA-LOCAL-DEMOCRACY-2017] [BIB-OECD-GAAG-DELIB-2025].

**Avoid:** miniature executive mandates that claim more authority than the institution can safely review.

### 2. Municipal (city / town / county-equivalent frontline state)

**Default legitimacy channel:** **representative elections plus administrative fairness**, with **bounded participatory and deliberative supplements**.

Municipal government is usually the first general-purpose public authority people actually experience. That makes competitive elections for the council or equivalent body the default legitimacy base. But municipal legitimacy is not exhausted by the ballot. It must be continuously proven through:
- equal treatment in service delivery,
- readable queues and permits,
- complaint and appeal lanes,
- and bounded participation for place-based budgeting and long-horizon conflicts [BIB-COE-LOCAL-SELF-GOV-2026] [BIB-OECD-TRUST-2024] [BIB-OECD-GAAG-DELIB-2025].

The archive therefore treats the municipality as a **hybrid legitimacy zone**: electoral at the top, administrative in daily operation, and selectively participatory where local knowledge and trust repair matter.

**Avoid:** turning municipal legitimacy into mayoral plebiscite theater when the real legitimacy work lies in service fairness, records, and remedy.

### 3. Metropolitan / corridor / watershed / functional region

**Default legitimacy channel:** **delegated and compound legitimacy**, upgraded to direct election only when metro power becomes thick enough to justify it.

Metropolitan bodies usually exist because several municipalities share spillovers, infrastructure, labor markets, watersheds, or risk. Their default legitimacy should therefore come from:
- delegated authority from member jurisdictions,
- published competence and finance rules,
- visible burden-sharing formulas,
- and a professional operator that can be inspected across the region [BIB-WB-METROGOV-2020].

A metro tier should move toward direct election or a thicker assembly only when it begins exercising genuinely political powers at metro scale — broad taxation, region-wide redistribution, major land-use authority, or high-salience public safety powers. Until then, the safer default is a **compound legitimacy story** rather than a symbolic metro plebiscite.

**Avoid:** directly electing a metro figurehead whose actual powers remain too thin or too delegated to justify the mandate-story attached to them.

### 4. Regional (state / province / land / autonomous territory)

**Default legitimacy channel:** **representative elections for a legislature plus territorially aware executive accountability**.

At regional scale, public power is usually too consequential to rely on delegation alone and too place-differentiated to be reduced to national uniformity. The default should therefore be:
- an elected regional assembly,
- executive government responsible to that assembly or otherwise strongly checked,
- and, where needed, territorial or plurinational accommodation that keeps the regional level from flattening internal diversity [BIB-OECD-REGIONAL-GOV-2022] [BIB-OECD-RDP-TOOLKIT-2026].

Deliberative institutions are especially useful here as a **quality gate** for spatially uneven trade-offs, constitutional asymmetry, and regional development bargains. But they should supplement elected authority rather than replace it.

**Avoid:** appointive-only regionalism where the region has real legislative, fiscal, or distributive power; and avoid subnational presidentialization when a legislature-executive accountability story would be more truthful.

### 5. National

**Default legitimacy channel:** **general elections and party competition**, constrained by **rights review, public reason, and remedy**.

National government holds the thickest ordinary public mandate: legislation, redistribution, coercion, emergency powers, and the standing architecture of rights. Its primary legitimacy channel should therefore be:
- regular competitive elections,
- representative parties and chambers,
- responsible executive formation,
- and enforceable legal constraints that keep victory from becoming domination [BIB-OECD-TRUST-2024] [BIB-OECD-POLITICAL-EFFICACY-2025].

Deliberative mini-publics, citizen assemblies, and referendums can all matter here, but mostly as **supplements, checks, or escalation paths**, not as replacements for representative government. National legitimacy cannot honestly be reduced to one charismatic office, one plebiscite, or one procedural event.

**Avoid:** treating direct election of a strong executive as a substitute for chamber scrutiny, party competition, or remedy.

### 6. Supranational / treaty union / regional court space

**Default legitimacy channel:** **dual legitimacy** — **member-state consent plus citizen/people representation**.

Supranational institutions govern across pre-existing polities. Their legitimacy must therefore be compound. The default channel is:
- a member-state chamber or council,
- a parliamentary / assembly channel for the people or peoples,
- conferral and subsidiarity rules,
- and an independent review or court path that prevents competence creep from becoming self-legitimating [BIB-OECD-MLG-HUB-2026] [BIB-WB-METROGOV-2020].

This means neither pure diplomacy nor pure transnational electoral symbolism is enough on its own. Once a union makes person-facing rules, the citizens/peoples channel must be real; but where competences remain conferred and shared, member-state consent remains part of the legitimacy story too.

**Avoid:** pretending there is one undivided demos when the system still relies on conferral, or pretending intergovernmental bargaining is sufficient once the regime starts issuing direct person-facing obligations.

### 7. Global / planetary

**Default legitimacy channel:** **state consent plus verification, finance, public reason, and affected-public voice**.

Global governance usually lacks a single general-purpose demos, a unified coercive apparatus, and an ordinary global executive. Its legitimacy should therefore come mainly from:
- treaty consent and public commitments,
- scientific and statistical verification,
- peer review and implementation follow-through,
- pooled finance and burden-sharing,
- due-process complaint and delisting paths where coercive measures exist,
- and recurring advisory channels for affected publics and future generations [BIB-UN-PACT-FUTURE-2024] [BIB-UN-GDC] [BIB-UN-DFG].

This is still democratic work, but it is **not** well served by pretending that a thin global electoral ritual would automatically legitimate ordinary world government. The archive default is therefore a public-goods and review architecture, not a world plebiscite.

**Avoid:** summit theater, symbolic global parliaments without consequence, or faux-executive offices whose mandate-story is far thicker than their accountability and remedy structure.

## Four rules that travel across the ladder

### A. Direct election should track real discretionary power

If an office is directly elected, it should usually control a meaningful general mandate that outsiders can see and contest.

Do **not** directly elect offices that mainly coordinate, certify, or implement decisions made elsewhere. That creates mandate inflation.

### B. Delegation is legitimate only when the delegators remain visible and the delegated powers stay bounded

Delegated metro, inter-municipal, or supranational authority is legitimate when:
- the conferral path is public,
- the competence map is specific,
- financing is legible,
- and exit / review / reform are real.

Invisible delegation is capture-friendly delegation.

### C. Deliberation should thicken legitimacy most where ordinary elections are weakest at surfacing trade-offs

Use deliberative mini-publics most aggressively where problems are:
- local but conflictual,
- regional and spatially uneven,
- long-horizon,
- or technically dense but politically salient [BIB-OECD-GAAG-DELIB-2025].

That is why the archive defaults to stronger deliberative add-ons at local and regional scales than at global scale.

### D. Verification becomes more important as authority becomes more distant and less electorally unified

At national scale, verification supports elections.
At supranational scale, verification supports dual legitimacy.
At global scale, verification is often part of legitimacy itself.

Do not ask distant regimes to borrow electoral symbolism when what they actually need is measurement, review, and consequence discipline.
