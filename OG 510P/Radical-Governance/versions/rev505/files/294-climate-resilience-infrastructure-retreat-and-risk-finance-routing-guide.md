# 294 — Climate Resilience, Infrastructure, Retreat, and Risk-Finance Routing Guide

**Purpose:** turn the archive’s climate-resilience cluster into a readable stack so adaptation / disaster-risk governance, infrastructure resilience, planned relocation, and catastrophe-finance design stop competing for the same conceptual role.

**Why this memo exists:** `63`, `154`, `166`, and `169` all surface for “climate resilience”, “disaster adaptation”, “retreat governance”, “resilient infrastructure”, “recovery finance”, or “catastrophe policy” queries because they describe one family from different layers. Nearby `170` is the watershed / water compact neighbor when the hazard is basin-scale and transboundary, and `11` is the ecological-limits neighbor when the real issue is commons budgets and standing. The archive should name those handoffs explicitly rather than invite flattening.

**Evidence anchors:** current disaster-risk and adaptation governance framing [BIB-UNDRR-DRR-STRATEGIES-2025] [BIB-UNDRR-GAR-2025] [BIB-UNFCCC-NAP-PROGRESS-2025]; infrastructure-lifecycle and financial-preparedness framing [BIB-GFDRR-RESILIENT-INFRA] [BIB-WB-DRF-2025]; human-mobility / planned-relocation framing [BIB-UNFCCC-TFD-HUMAN-MOBILITY] [BIB-UN-GP-IDP-1998] [BIB-UNDRR-SENDAI].

## Canonical reading order

### 1. Start with `63-climate-adaptation-and-disaster-risk-governance.md`
Use `63` when the question is:
- what climate adaptation and disaster-risk governance is for across scopes,
- how baselines, triggers, preparedness, recovery, and reconstruction fit together,
- and how resilience becomes an auditable planning-and-decision pipeline instead of a slogan.

`63` is the **canonical climate adaptation / DRR front door**.

### 2. Move to `154-critical-infrastructure-resilience-compacts.md`
Use `154` when you need:
- cross-scope operator compacts for energy, water, telecom, transport, health, or logistics,
- infrastructure interdependency mapping, continuity duties, mutual aid, and outage accountability,
- or lifecycle resilience for essential systems rather than the broader adaptation stack.

`154` is the **critical-infrastructure resilience specialization**.

### 3. Use `166-managed-retreat-and-planned-relocation.md`
Use `166` when the real issue is:
- movement out of hazard exposure,
- community-preserving relocation, compensation, land disposition, and anti-predation rails,
- or the question has shifted from “protect in place” to “what does dignified retreat require?”

`166` is the **managed-retreat / planned-relocation specialization**.

### 4. Use `169-resilience-finance-risk-pooling-and-catastrophe-rails.md`
Use `169` when you need:
- risk layering,
- contingency reserves, pooled finance, parametric triggers, catastrophe transfer, and rapid-disbursement design,
- or the core problem is how money arrives fast enough and cleanly enough to support resilience and recovery.

`169` is the **risk-finance and catastrophe-transfer specialization**.

### 5. Use `170-watershed-and-water-governance-compacts.md` when the real hazard problem is basin-scale, water-linked, or transboundary
Use `170` when you need:
- basin budgets,
- drought / flood trigger ladders,
- upstream / downstream allocation disputes,
- or natural-system governance at the river-basin / aquifer scale rather than the full climate-resilience family.

`170` is the **watershed / water compact neighbor**.

### 6. Use `11-commons-and-ecological-governance.md` when the issue is ecological ceilings, rights, or commons stewardship rather than adaptation operations
Use `11` when you need:
- ecological budgets and standing,
- environmental-rights and participation baselines,
- or broader commons / externalities governance beyond adaptation, retreat, infrastructure continuity, or disaster finance.

`11` is the **ecological-limits and commons-governance neighbor**.

## Confusion boundaries

### `63` vs `154`
- `63` is the **broad adaptation / DRR front door** for baselines, triggers, reconstruction, and resilience planning.
- `154` is the **infrastructure specialization** for operator compacts, interdependency mapping, emergency continuity, and maintenance / asset-management obligations.

### `63` vs `166`
- `63` governs **how risk is assessed, reduced, and acted on across the adaptation pipeline**.
- `166` governs **what happens when continued occupation becomes unsafe or unjustifiable and relocation must be designed lawfully and non-predatorily**.

### `63` vs `169`
- `63` is the **governance and planning frame** for adaptation and disaster-risk reduction.
- `169` is the **financial-preparedness and disbursement layer** for buffers, pools, trigger-linked payouts, and anti-looting rails.

### `154` vs `169`
- `154` governs **what resilient infrastructure operators and compact partners must do**.
- `169` governs **how resilience and recovery get financed, pre-committed, and disbursed**.

### `166` vs `170`
- `166` is about **people, households, communities, compensation, continuity, and land disposition under retreat / relocation**.
- `170` is about **basin-scale allocation, flows, monitoring, and dispute architecture** when the hazard is really a water-governance problem.

### `63` / `166` vs `11`
- `63` and `166` govern **adaptation operations and movement under risk**.
- `11` governs **ecological ceilings, commons stewardship, and environmental standing** even when no relocation program exists.

## Safe merge rule for this cluster

Do **not** flatten this family into one mega-memo on “climate resilience governance”.
The safer pattern is:
1. keep `63` as the climate adaptation / DRR front door,
2. keep `154` as the infrastructure-resilience specialization,
3. keep `166` as the managed-retreat / planned-relocation specialization,
4. keep `169` as the risk-finance / catastrophe-transfer specialization,
5. keep `170` nearby as the watershed / water-governance neighbor,
6. keep `11` nearby as the ecological-limits / commons-governance neighbor,
7. and use this memo as the bridge that names the roles.

That preserves distinct retrieval hooks while sharply reducing reader confusion.
