# 330 — Scope-Epistemic Defaults: Local Observation, Service Data, Territorial Indicators, Official Statistics, and MRV

**Cross-stack note:** use `306-scope-design-ideal-government-and-reference-bundles-guide.md` for the canonical route across the scope-design family. This memo is the scale-to-epistemic / evidence-mode front door; `14` / `176` explain the cross-scope logic, `285` gives the default bundle language, `327` handles the scale-sensitive institutional-form default, `328` handles the scale-sensitive legitimacy-channel default, `329` handles the scale-sensitive tempo / time-horizon default, `03` is the general metrics-and-evidence front door, `26` handles public-knowledge infrastructure, `184` handles official-statistics integrity, `202` handles evidence commons and scientific integrity, `218` handles global-commons MRV design, `331` handles the scale-sensitive accountability / review-mode choice, `332` handles the scale-sensitive coercion / direct-force-holding choice that determines who checks the evidence users, and `333` handles the scale-sensitive finance / revenue-model choice that determines who can sustain the evidence substrate, and `336` handles the scale-sensitive constituency / who-counts choice that determines which public the evidence must actually describe and answer to.

**Purpose:** answer the design question that remains after scope assignment, bundle design, institutional form, legitimacy choice, and tempo choice: **what kind of knowledge and evidence should government at each scale mainly govern with?**

**Why this memo exists:** `14`, `176`, `285`, `327`, `328`, and `329` explain where functions belong, what minimum bundle a government needs, what shape it should take, how it should be legitimated, and what tempo it should run on. `03`, `26`, `184`, and `202` explain metrics, public knowledge, official statistics, and evidence integrity in general. But the archive still lacked one compact memo answering the narrower practical question: **when should a scale rely mainly on local observation, frontline service data, harmonized territorial indicators, national official statistics, supranational benchmarking, or review-and-verification-heavy MRV machinery?**

**From-below:** this memo is a guardrail against two opposite failures: remote dashboard rule that cannot see lived harm, and local anecdotalism that cannot honestly govern macro, cross-border, or planetary systems. It keeps nearby harms visible without letting prestige metrics erase them, and it keeps distant coordination evidence-based without pretending that aggregate averages are the whole truth.

**Evidence anchors:** UNDP’s 2025 community-based resilience guidance keeps emphasizing area-based, community-facing capacities for sensing, adaptation, and recovery [BIB-UNDP-CBRB-2025]; OECD’s Local Data Portal now offers more than 100 local/subnational indicators across 10 domains using official statistics plus geospatial sources [BIB-OECD-LOCAL-DATA-PORTAL-2026]; OECD’s 2024 regions-and-cities overview stresses that governments need to engage communities and tailor solutions to territorial needs rather than govern only from national averages [BIB-OECD-RCAG-2024]; the UN Statistics Division’s current overview of the Fundamental Principles says official statistics must be professionally sound, transparent, impartial, and trusted as a global standard [BIB-UNSD-FPOS-2026]; and the UNFCCC’s current transparency materials frame global climate governance around recurring reporting, review, and fully operational ETF cycles rather than one-shot declarations [BIB-UNFCCC-TRANSPARENCY-2026] [BIB-UNFCCC-ETF-FAQ-2025].

---

## Core claim

Every scale needs evidence, but **not every scale should use the same dominant epistemic substrate**.

The archive default is:
- **the smaller and closer the scale, the more government should lean on direct observation, issue logs, and person-facing service signals;**
- **the more operational the scale, the more it should lean on administrative records, queue data, asset condition data, and neighborhood / parcel / network visibility;**
- **the more territorial and cross-jurisdictional the scale, the more it should lean on harmonized indicators, geospatial baselines, and comparable definitions;**
- **the more constitutional, redistributive, and macro-governing the scale, the more it should lean on independent official statistics, census and registry systems, and reproducible evaluation;**
- **the more cross-border and planetary the scale, the more it should lean on benchmarking, peer review, scientific assessment, treaty reporting, and verification-heavy MRV rather than local anecdote or political assertion.**

So the question is never just “what data do we have?” It is:
**what kind of evidence can honestly see the harms, support the decision, travel across the relevant boundaries, and still be challenged by the people it affects?**

---

## The anti-blindness / anti-omniscience test

Before choosing an evidence mode, ask five questions:

1. **Proximity test:** where will the harm be noticed first — by residents, frontline workers, local operators, or distant reviewers?
2. **Comparability test:** does this decision require data that can be compared across places, or mainly facts about one place?
3. **Independence test:** who must be insulated from operational self-interest for these facts to be trusted?
4. **Verification test:** which claims require third-party review, peer challenge, or formal MRV rather than self-report?
5. **Person-path test:** if the aggregate says one thing and the lived case says another, can the person contest the record without disappearing into the dashboard?

If the evidence mode is too abstract to see lived harm, it produces **blindness**.
If it claims more certainty or comparability than the evidence can actually bear, it produces **false omniscience**.

## The archive’s epistemic defaults by scope

### 0. Household / association / cooperative

**Default epistemic mode:** **direct observation, shared memory, and simple ledgers**.

Use:
- visible meetings,
- simple money and membership records,
- and low-cost notice that can be contested immediately.

At this scale, the strongest knowledge asset is proximity. The point is not analytics sophistication but making common facts legible before conflict hardens.

**Avoid:** importing elaborate scorecards or pseudo-professional metrics that outrun the group’s actual duties and review capacity.

### 1. Micro-local (building / block / village / campus / frontline institution)

**Default epistemic mode:** **situated observation plus lightweight issue logging**.

At micro-local scale, government should lean mainly on:
- direct observation of place conditions,
- recurring walkthroughs or check-ins,
- incident and maintenance logs,
- steward notes that can be inspected,
- and immediate escalation when local facts need municipal verification.

This is the scale where nearby people often see failure first. The goal is not massive quantification but **fast, shareable, contestable noticing** [BIB-UNDP-CBRB-2025].

**Avoid:** forcing neighborhood harms to wait for a citywide or national dataset before they count as real.

### 2. Municipal (city / town / county-equivalent frontline state)

**Default epistemic mode:** **frontline service data plus neighborhood observation plus local geospatial / asset visibility**.

Municipal government is the everyday operator. It should therefore lean mainly on:
- service queues, permits, inspections, complaints, and dispatch records,
- parcel / address / route / facility data,
- maintenance and asset-condition logs,
- neighborhood-level observation and local consultation,
- and bounded city observatory / open-data functions where capacity exists [BIB-OECD-LOCAL-DATA-PORTAL-2026] [BIB-OECD-RCAG-2024].

The municipal danger is not lack of data but **governing from citywide averages that erase block-level failure**.

**Avoid:** consultant dashboards that cannot be traced back to inspectable frontline records, place conditions, or remedy lanes.

### 3. Metropolitan / corridor / watershed / functional region

**Default epistemic mode:** **harmonized cross-jurisdiction operational data plus territorial indicators and shared network models**.

Metro governance should lean mainly on:
- shared definitions across municipalities,
- interoperable service and incident records,
- territorial indicators for housing, labor markets, mobility, watersheds, utilities, and exposure,
- geospatial baselines that can see cross-border systems,
- and common performance views for shared infrastructure [BIB-OECD-LOCAL-DATA-PORTAL-2026] [BIB-OECD-RCAG-2024].

This is the scale where separate local dashboards stop being enough. The problem is often not missing evidence but **non-joinable evidence**.

**Avoid:** treating metropolitan governance as if each municipality’s isolated metrics were still an adequate map of a shared system.

### 4. Regional (state / province / land / autonomous territory)

**Default epistemic mode:** **territorial statistics, sector indicators, spatial-equity mapping, and evaluation-backed planning**.

Regional government should lean mainly on:
- comparable subnational indicators,
- labor-market, education, health, housing, energy, basin, and land-use statistics,
- remote-sensing and geospatial overlays where territorial pattern matters,
- program evaluation and implementation tracking,
- and place-based diagnostics that can compare municipalities without flattening them into one average [BIB-OECD-RCAG-2024] [BIB-OECD-LOCAL-DATA-PORTAL-2026].

This is the scale where territorial mismatch becomes visible: capital-city metrics and national means stop being enough.

**Avoid:** making regional policy from national averages, or from one favored city’s data standing in for the whole region.

### 5. National

**Default epistemic mode:** **independent official statistics, census and registry backbones, administrative data integration, and reproducible evaluation**.

National government should lean mainly on:
- an independent official statistical system,
- census and major survey capacity,
- national accounts and population baselines,
- administrative registers joined with legal safeguards,
- scientific agencies and evaluation functions,
- and publication discipline strong enough that ministries cannot quietly replace official facts with political theater [BIB-UNSD-FPOS-2026].

At national scale, the epistemic danger is not just error but **capture**: ministries treating public data as a communications instrument rather than a public-good infrastructure.

**Avoid:** confusing ministerial KPIs, campaign talking points, or vendor analytics with the independent statistical and evaluative substrate required for rights floors, redistribution, and macro-governance.

### 6. Supranational (union / treaty-union / cross-border regime)

**Default epistemic mode:** **harmonized cross-national indicators, peer review, scoreboard discipline, and methodological transparency**.

Supranational governance should lean mainly on:
- cross-country definitions and metadata,
- comparable indicators with disclosed limits,
- peer review and implementation monitoring,
- interoperability notes that show where national systems do not line up,
- and public dashboards that do not pretend comparability where it does not exist.

This layer usually governs by coordination, conferral, and enforcement-through-review rather than direct street-level operation. Its evidence should therefore privilege **comparability and challengeable methodology** over local vividness alone.

**Avoid:** ranking states with non-comparable data, or using a union average to erase meaningful differences in member-state baselines and capacities.

### 7. Global / planetary

**Default epistemic mode:** **MRV, treaty reporting, scientific assessment, Earth-system observation, and bounded early-warning systems**.

Global governance should lean mainly on:
- measurement, reporting, and verification systems,
- recurring treaty and stocktake cycles,
- scientific assessments and observatories,
- independent review,
- global risk surveillance and early-warning infrastructure,
- and finance / compliance reporting that can be challenged publicly [BIB-UNFCCC-TRANSPARENCY-2026] [BIB-UNFCCC-ETF-FAQ-2025].

This scale cannot honestly rely on anecdote, nor can it honestly claim omniscient command. Its epistemic strength is **repeatable verification under public methods**, not theatrical certainty.

**Avoid:** summit-era dashboard theater, unverifiable target claims, or crisis narratives with no reporting, review, or method notes behind them.

## Cross-cutting design rules

### A. The first place harm appears should have a log

The scale that first sees failure must be able to record it in a way that can travel upward. Local observation that cannot enter a reviewable record is politically fragile.

### B. Higher scopes should aggregate without erasing lower-scope reality

National, supranational, and global evidence systems should standardize and compare, but they should not overwrite the lived and territorial signals that show where the aggregate is lying by omission.

### C. The more coercive or redistributive the use, the stronger the independence requirement

If evidence supports policing, detention, status decisions, taxation, transfers, sanctions, or large-scale regulation, the producing and validating institutions must be more independent than the operational users of the data.

### D. Comparability is a governance product, not a natural fact

Cross-place indicators require common definitions, metadata, versioning, and review. They do not become fair just because they fit on the same dashboard.

### E. Every evidence stack needs a person-side contradiction path

A person must be able to say: “your system says X, but my case shows Y.” Without that contradiction lane, the epistemic system turns into an anti-remedy shield.
