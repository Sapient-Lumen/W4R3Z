# 342 — Scope-Geometry Defaults: Membership Maps, Walksheds, Service Areas, Functional Urban Areas, and No Cartographic Theater

**Cross-stack note:** use `306-scope-design-ideal-government-and-reference-bundles-guide.md` for the canonical route across the scope-design family. This memo is the scale-to-geometry / boundary-fit / no-cartographic-theater front door; `14` / `176` explain the cross-scope logic, `54` handles the auditable assignment test, `17` handles deeper boundary / reorganisation procedure, `285` handles bundle composition, `327` handles institutional form, `336` handles who counts, `71` handles interface publication, and `15` / `16` / `19` / `109` / `128` / `164` / `197` handle the deeper functional-authority, metro, compact, continuity, standards, geospatial, and polycentric substrate once the geometry question has been identified.

**Purpose:** answer the design question that remains after scope assignment, bundle design, form choice, legitimacy choice, constituency choice, and decision-rule choice: **what kind of governing geometry should this scale usually use — membership map, walkshed, addressable municipal area, county catchment, functional urban area, territorial region, constitutional territory, member-territory overlay, or planetary constraint surface?**

**Why this memo exists:** `14`, `176`, `285`, and `327-341` explain what each scale owns, what shape it should take, who counts there, how it should decide, how offices should be filled, and what review path should exist. But the archive still needed one compact memo that says **scale should also change boundary logic** — when a place should be treated as a porous neighborhood instead of a miniature sovereign, when a city should be drawn as a service and address geography, when metropolitan governance should follow commuting and network footprints rather than inherited municipal lines, when regions should be durable territorial units rather than arbitrary slices, and when supranational or global governance should stop pretending to be one fused territorial state.

**From-below:** this memo is a guardrail against two opposite failures. It helps stop tiny institutions from acting like hard-border sovereigns when people’s lives flow across them every day. And it helps stop distant governance from drawing neat maps that ignore the real service, labor-market, ecological, and statistical seams that people actually live inside.

**Evidence anchors:** the European Charter of Local Self-Government still requires prior consultation of local communities before local-authority boundary changes, which is the right democratic floor for local reorganisation [BIB-COE-LOCALSELF-ART5]; OECD’s functional-urban-area method still defines metropolitan space as a city plus its commuting zone, and OECD’s 2025 urban-policy stocktake still urges governance beyond administrative perimeters where appropriate and across urban scales from neighbourhoods to megaregions [BIB-OECD-FUA-2026] [BIB-OECD-URBAN-POLICY-5Y-2025]; OECD’s geographical definitions still distinguish cities / FUAs from large and small territorial regions (TL2 / TL3), which is a useful reminder that metropolitan and regional geometries are not the same thing [BIB-OECD-GEOGRAPHICAL-DEFINITIONS-2026]; and the UN’s current geospatial-statistical framework still treats standardized location references, geocoding, and boundary files as foundational to integrating statistics and governance across scales [BIB-UN-GSGF-2025].

## Core claim

Every scale needs a **default geometry design**.

Not because every institution needs a new map, but because authority becomes symbolic, evasive, or overreaching when it cannot say:
- what unit it is actually governing,
- whether that unit is territorial, functional, ecological, or membership-based,
- how its edges relate to service delivery, data, and rights,
- what changes when people commute, move, or cross a seam,
- and how boundary change is consulted, published, and carried across records.

As scale changes, the fitting geometry also changes:
- the nearer and smaller the scale, the more the unit should track **lived place** rather than fantasy sovereignty;
- the denser and service-heavier the scale, the more the unit should become **addressable, contiguous, and joinable to service networks**;
- the more networked the function, the more geometry should track **commuting zones, corridors, basins, airsheds, grids, or other real externality footprints** rather than inherited municipal lines;
- the thicker the redistribution, legislation, and territorial planning burden, the more the geometry should become **durable subnational territory**;
- the more constitutional the authority, the more geometry should rely on **recognized territory with stable internal geography and explicit boundary-change rules**;
- and the more governance becomes treaty-based or planetary, the more geometry should rely on **member territories, competence overlays, and Earth-system constraint surfaces** rather than pretending to be one undifferentiated world district.

So the archive’s rule is:
**draw governance where the problem is real, publish the crosswalks when lines differ, and do not mistake inherited cartography for the right governing scale.**

## The no-cartographic-theater test

Before creating, merging, or reforming a unit, ask seven questions:

1. **Lived-footprint test:** does the proposed line follow where people actually live, move, or receive the service?
2. **Addressability test:** can decisions, notices, parcels, buildings, assets, and statistics be joined to the unit without guesswork?
3. **Externality test:** do the main harms or benefits outrun the line through commuting, water, air, transport, grid, or ecosystem links?
4. **Continuity test:** when the line changes, are equivalency tables, identifiers, service continuity, and record migration explicit?
5. **Consultation test:** if the line alters local democratic membership or burdens, were the affected communities actually consulted in time?
6. **Overlay test:** would a compact, district, or functional overlay solve the real problem better than inventing a new general-purpose tier?
7. **Anti-theater test:** is the geometry being chosen because it is substantively right, or because it looks administratively neat on a prestige map?

If several answers are “we kept the old line because it already exists,” the design is drifting toward **cartographic theater, scale mismatch, or administrative convenience mistaken for legitimacy.**

## Default geometry modes by scope

### 0. Household / association / cooperative

**Default geometry mode:** **membership map first, place only where the shared asset or dwelling actually requires it.**

Use:
- membership or occupancy as the governing edge for association decisions,
- asset or dwelling boundaries for common-property maintenance and internal safety,
- and explicit compact interfaces once the body starts affecting outsiders, workers, tenants, or neighboring publics.

### 1. Micro-local (building / block / village / campus / frontline institution)

**Default geometry mode:** **lived place, address cluster, walkshed, or steward-relevant site footprint — porous for coordination, clear for responsibility.**

Use:
- the building, block, campus, or obvious neighborhood catchment as the main unit,
- addressable entrances, assets, or steward areas for work assignment,
- and soft coordination edges where neighbors, regular users, or nearby impacts matter.

### 2. Municipal (city / town)

**Default geometry mode:** **contiguous local territory anchored in parcels, addresses, streets, utilities, and service obligations.**

Use:
- a legally clear local territory so taxation, zoning, services, and elections are legible,
- address / parcel / asset joins so person and property interfaces work reliably,
- and published annexation / merger / split rules with continuity for records and service access.

### 2.5 Intermediate local administration (county / prefecture / province-lite backstop)

**Default geometry mode:** **contiguous backstop catchment spanning several municipalities.**

Use:
- county / district-style geometry where records, courts, social administration, health backstops, or emergency coordination need one joinable field above the municipality,
- and line design that minimizes person-bouncing across common services.

### 3. Metropolitan / corridor / watershed / functional region

**Default geometry mode:** **functional overlay — city plus commuting zone, corridor, basin, grid, or other real spillover footprint.**

Use:
- functional urban areas for labor-market and commuting systems,
- watershed / airshed / coastal / grid footprints for ecological and infrastructure systems,
- and compact or authority overlays across the underlying municipalities rather than forced municipal consolidation by default.

### 4. Regional (state / province / land)

**Default geometry mode:** **durable subnational territorial region, sometimes supplemented by ecological or development overlays.**

Use:
- a stable large region for legislation, planning, redistribution, and territorial balancing,
- small-region statistical and administrative subdivisions beneath it,
- and overlay institutions where basin, mountain, island, corridor, or linguistic realities need something the base map cannot do alone.

### 5. National

**Default geometry mode:** **recognized constitutional territory with stable internal administrative and statistical geography.**

Use:
- constitutional territory for law, taxation, macro-stabilization, rights floors, and security,
- standardized internal geographies, identifiers, and geocoding so censuses, registries, elections, and services interoperate,
- and explicit procedures for territorial change, local reorganisation, and cross-border continuity.

### 6. Supranational (union / confederation / treaty bloc)

**Default geometry mode:** **member territories plus competence overlays, not one fused undifferentiated territory.**

Use:
- member-state territories as the underlying map,
- competence-specific overlays for customs, market, migration, environment, transport, or funds where powers are conferred,
- and public crosswalks showing which acts run directly at union level and which still flow through national administrations.

### 7. Global (planetary / treaty-complex level)

**Default geometry mode:** **planetary constraint surfaces plus treaty membership and implementation maps.**

Use:
- Earth-system and commons geometries where the problem is atmospheric, oceanic, biodiversity, orbital, epidemiological, or financial-network in nature,
- treaty-membership and jurisdiction maps for legal coverage,
- and domestic implementation maps where delivery still happens nationally or locally.

### Non-territorial & indigenous polities (cross-cutting)

**Default geometry mode:** **membership map plus compact-facing territorial interfaces.**

Use:
- membership and recognized community boundaries for internal authority,
- territorial interfaces for land, service, mobility, jurisdiction, and compact relations,
- and explicit publication of where membership governance ends and territorial public power begins.

## Cross-scope defaults worth carrying everywhere

1. **Consult before changing local boundaries.** Reorganisation without timely consultation is boundary engineering, not legitimate reform.
2. **Publish equivalency files when lines change.** Old and new units, identifiers, addresses, and service obligations must be cross-walked.
3. **Prefer overlays where the problem is networked.** Metro, basin, corridor, grid, and commons problems rarely justify cloning a whole new general-purpose sovereign.
4. **Keep addressability underneath every grand map.** Buildings, parcels, assets, and persons still need joinable local references.
5. **Do not confuse who counts with where the line sits.** Constituency and geometry are related, but not identical.
6. **Statistical and governing geographies should be crosswalked, not silently conflated.** Comparable data is a support layer, not a substitute for democratic choice.
7. **Planetary governance should publish coverage honestly.** Name where treaty membership, monitoring, and implementation exist — do not imply universal reach where none exists.
