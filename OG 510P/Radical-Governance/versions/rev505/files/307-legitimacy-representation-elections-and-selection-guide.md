# 307 — Legitimacy / Representation / Elections / Selection Guide

**Purpose:** turn the archive’s legitimacy-design family into a readable stack so legitimacy architecture, electoral-system choice, party-system design, election administration, legitimacy-engine comparison, selection integrity, delegation/mandate integrity, executive-form choice, head-of-state design, government-formation design, constitutional-conventions / cabinet-manual design, and the deliberation family stop competing for the same conceptual role.

**Why this memo exists:** `21`, `147`, `56`, `180`, `328`, `119`, `141`, `284`, and now `311` / `312` / `313` / `314` / `315` all surface for queries like “how should legitimate representation work”, “what electoral system fits this scope”, “how do election operations differ from election design”, “how should parties and nominations fit between elections and governments”, “what kind of executive should democratic representation produce”, “what should a non-executive president or constitutional monarch actually be allowed to do”, “how should a government actually be formed once seats exist”, “when should deliberation or sortition complement elections”, “which legitimacy channel should dominate at a given scale”, “how do you keep random selection auditable”, or “how do you stop representation from becoming an invisible proxy problem between elections”. They are not duplicates, but they are dense enough to deserve an explicit family router.

**Evidence anchors:** OECD’s 2025 *Government at a Glance* chapter on citizen participation reports 716 representative deliberative processes across 28 OECD countries between 1979 and 2023, involving more than 80,000 randomly selected citizens, with 148 new cases recorded between 2021 and 2023 and 70% of those recent cases at the local or regional level [BIB-OECD-GAAG-DELIB-2025]. International IDEA still frames electoral-system choice as one of the most important institutional decisions in any democracy, and its comparative Electoral System Design Database now covers 217 countries and territories [BIB-IDEA-ESD] [BIB-IDEA-ESD-DATABASE-2026]. OSCE/ODIHR’s 2023 election-administration handbook notes that election management bodies must manage election processes efficiently, impartially, and transparently, because their work directly affects the rights of voters and contestants and public confidence in elections [BIB-OSCE-ELECTION-ADMIN-2023]. The Venice Commission’s 2024 declarations on stability of electoral law and on digital technologies and AI in elections are useful reminders that election legitimacy depends both on stable core rules and on explicit safeguards for new digital manipulation surfaces [BIB-VENICE-ELECT-STABILITY-2024] [BIB-VENICE-ELECT-DIGITAL-AI-2024].

## Canonical reading order

### 1. Start with `21-legitimacy-architecture.md`
Use `21` when the question is:
- what makes a decision or institution rightful rather than merely imposed,
- how legitimacy should cash out as contestability, reasons, and remedy,
- which legitimacy generators should be combined for different decision types,
- or how to think about legitimacy before choosing a specific selection mechanism.

`21` is the **broad legitimacy-architecture front door**.

### 2. Use `147-representation-and-electoral-system-choice.md`
Use `147` when the real issue is:
- which electoral system fits the task and scope,
- how to balance proportionality, governability, geographic accountability, and manipulation risk,
- how to compare PR, MMP, STV, ranked-choice, or majoritarian systems,
- or how to reform electoral-system design without confusing design with administration.

`147` is the **representation / electoral-system-design specialization**.

### 3. Use `56-elections-and-electoral-administration.md`
Use `56` when the question is:
- how elections should actually be run,
- registration, ballot curing, polling-place changes, recounts, audits, or certification,
- what election management bodies must publish and receipt,
- or how to make election operations auditable and non-exclusionary.

`56` is the **election-operations / administration specialization**.

### 4. Use `180-legitimacy-engines-elections-sortition-deliberation-recall.md`
Use `180` when you need:
- the comparative logic of elections, sortition, deliberation, and recall,
- a legitimacy stack for a new institution,
- guidance on when elections should be supplemented rather than treated as self-sufficient,
- or a compact answer to “which legitimacy engines belong together here?”.

`180` is the **comparative legitimacy-engine stack**.

### 5. Use `328-scope-legitimacy-defaults-voice-elections-delegation-dual-legitimacy-and-verification.md` when the question is which legitimacy channel fits a given scale
Use `328` when:
- the question is not legitimacy design in general, but which authorization channel should dominate at a given scope,
- you need to distinguish micro-local voice, municipal elections, metropolitan delegation, regional elected authority, national electoral legitimacy, supranational dual legitimacy, or global verification-heavy consent structures,
- or the archive needs a compact answer to “how should this scale be democratically authorized without importing the wrong constitutional theater?”.

`328` is the **scope-sensitive legitimacy-channel memo**.

### 6. Use `284-deliberation-stack-binding-and-legitimacy-guide.md` when the question enters the deliberation family
Use `284` when:
- the issue is no longer broadly about representation and legitimacy, but specifically about deliberative institutions,
- you need to distinguish theory vs civic-lottery infrastructure vs binding response vs process rails,
- or several deliberation memos look plausible at once.

`284` is the **deliberation-family bridge**, not the whole elections/representation family.

### 7. Use `119-selection-and-sortition-integrity.md`
Use `119` when the real problem is:
- whether a random selection process is actually auditable,
- draw receipts, randomness beacons, frame integrity, and replacement rules,
- lotteries for assemblies, audits, scarce allocations, or constrained appointment pools,
- or how to keep “sortition” from turning into unverifiable theater.

`119` is the **selection-integrity / draw-audit specialization**.

### 8. Use `141-delegation-and-representation-integrity.md`
Use `141` when the issue is:
- proxy authority between elections,
- mandates, delegates, revocation, and standing,
- representative authority that must be legible in day-to-day administration,
- or how to stop “acting on your behalf” from becoming an invisible capture surface.

`141` is the **delegation / mandate / proxy-authority specialization**.

### 9. Route to `295-public-information-media-and-access-routing-guide.md` when the real problem is the information environment
Use `295` when:
- the main issue is media pluralism, public-information integrity, or access to official information,
- the question is more about the informational conditions for democratic choice than the choice mechanism itself,
- or legitimacy failure is being driven by public-knowledge collapse rather than election or selection design.

`295` is the **public-information neighbor**.

### 10. Route to `309-representative-chambers-committees-opposition-rights-and-confidence-architecture.md` when the question is chamber design after seats are allocated
Use `309` when:
- the electoral result is not the issue, but what kind of legislature should exist after it,
- the main question is unicameral vs bicameral design, upper-chamber purpose, committee systems, or opposition rights,
- the problem is confidence / investiture / dissolution design rather than election administration,
- or the archive needs a compact answer to “how should representative chambers actually be built so they can govern and scrutinize?”

`309` is the **chamber / committee / confidence neighbor**.

### 10. Route to `308-peaceful-transfer-of-power-caretaker-government-and-continuity-of-office-rails.md` when the question is the handover seam
Use `308` when:
- the election has happened or authority is otherwise changing hands,
- the issue is caretaker government, acting authority, government formation, or transfer-of-power restraint,
- the main risk is midnight appointments, contracts, pardons, records tampering, or service collapse during transition,
- or the legitimacy problem is not who won, but how authority is handed over.

`308` is the **transfer / caretaker / continuity neighbor**.

### 11. Route to `311-executive-system-choice-parliamentary-presidential-semipresidential-and-collegial-rails.md` when the question is what kind of executive the polity should have
Use `311` when:
- the real issue is parliamentary vs presidential vs semi-presidential vs collegial design,
- the question is whether head of state and head of government should be separated,
- the problem is not seat allocation but the structure of the executive mandate it produces,
- or the archive needs a compact answer to “what kind of executive should an ideal democracy or treaty union actually use?”

`311` is the **executive-form / head-of-state–head-of-government neighbor**.

### 11a. Route to `313-head-of-state-design-non-executive-presidents-constitutional-monarchs-reserve-powers-and-succession-rails.md` when the question is the head-of-state layer itself
Use `313` when:
- the real issue is whether a polity should have a distinct head of state at all,
- the question is whether a non-executive president, constitutional monarch, or rotating chair best fits the intended role,
- the problem is not executive form in general, but countersignature, reserve powers, assent / veto fit, or succession / regency design,
- or the archive needs a compact answer to “what should the head of state actually be allowed to do?”

`313` is the **head-of-state / reserve-powers / succession neighbor**.

### 12. Route to `312-party-systems-candidate-selection-caucus-governance-and-anti-defection-rails.md` when the question is how parties translate representation into governing
Use `312` when:
- the real issue is party recognition, candidate nomination, caucus / whip design, or anti-defection rules,
- the question is not mainly how votes become seats, but how parties shape the field around those seats,
- the problem is not executive form itself, but whether the party system beneath it will sustain pluralism, scrutiny, and governability,
- or the archive needs a compact answer to “how should parties actually fit between elections and governments?”

`312` is the **party-system / nomination / caucus-governance neighbor**.

## Confusion boundaries

### `147` vs `56`
- `147` is about **electoral-system design**.
- `56` is about **election operations and administration**.

A system can be well-designed but badly administered, or well-administered but badly designed. Keep those roles separate.

### `180` vs `284`
- `180` compares **legitimacy engines across elections, sortition, deliberation, and recall**.
- `284` routes the **internal deliberation family** once the question is specifically about assemblies, civic lotteries, binding response, or process rails.

### `119` vs `147`
- `147` decides **how representation should be allocated**.
- `119` decides **whether a claimed random selection process is actually trustworthy and replayable**.

### `141` vs `147` / `56`
- `147` and `56` mostly govern **how public authority is selected and run**.
- `141` governs **how authority is exercised by proxies, representatives, delegates, guardians, or agents after selection**.

### This family vs `106-legitimacy-protocols.md` / `107-governance-test-suite.md`
- Stay in this family when the issue is **how legitimacy should be generated and routed**.
- Move to `106` / `107` when the issue is **how to test legitimacy claims, failure conditions, and operational proof**.

### This family vs `295-public-information-media-and-access-routing-guide.md`
- Stay here when the issue is **selection, authorization, or mandate legitimacy**.
- Move to `295` when the issue is **the information conditions that make democratic choice meaningful or distorted**.

### This family vs `309-representative-chambers-committees-opposition-rights-and-confidence-architecture.md`
- Stay here when the issue is **how seats, legitimacy engines, and mandate authority are generated**.
- Move to `309` when the issue is **how the representative chamber built from those seats should be structured, bounded, and able to scrutinize government**.

### This family vs `311-executive-system-choice-parliamentary-presidential-semipresidential-and-collegial-rails.md`
- Stay here when the issue is **how democratic authorization and representation are generated**.
- Move to `311` when the issue is **what executive form those authorizations are supposed to sustain or constrain**.

### This family vs `313-head-of-state-design-non-executive-presidents-constitutional-monarchs-reserve-powers-and-succession-rails.md`
- This family governs **how democratic authorization is generated and translated**.
- Move to `313` when the issue is **how a distinct head-of-state office should be bounded once that authorization already exists**.

### This family vs `312-party-systems-candidate-selection-caucus-governance-and-anti-defection-rails.md`
- Stay here when the issue is **how authority is selected, represented, or supplemented in broad legitimacy terms**.
- Move to `312` when the issue is **how parties, nominations, caucuses, and party discipline convert that authorization into actual representative politics and government formation**.

### This family vs `308-peaceful-transfer-of-power-caretaker-government-and-continuity-of-office-rails.md`
- Stay here when the issue is **how authority is generated, represented, selected, or supplemented**.
- Move to `308` when the issue is **how authority is handed over, limited, or kept continuous after a result, resignation, vacancy, or deadlock**.

### This family vs `315-constitutional-conventions-cabinet-manuals-and-codified-restraint-rails.md`
- Stay here when the issue is **how democratic authority is generated and translated into representation**.
- Move to `315` when the issue is **how caretaker, formation, reserve-power, or coalition norms should be published in manuals or public guidance once that authority structure is already known**.
