# Public Service & State Capacity (Merit, Skills, Continuity)

**Purpose:** treat administrative capacity as a governance obligation (staffing, process, culture) so rights aren’t theoretical.

Many “ideal constitutions” fail because the state cannot **execute** decisions without patronage, chaos, or vendor capture. This memo defines a minimal *public service capability* baseline.

**Anchor set:** see [BIB-OECD-PSLC].

---

Institutional culture matters: even perfect interfaces can be sabotaged by indifference, humiliation, or retaliation at the counter. Public service capability therefore includes **dignity discipline**—clear expectations for how people are treated, how errors are acknowledged, and how frontline discretion is supervised and reviewed (see `98-persons-path-and-accessibility-invariants.md` and `08-remedy-and-grievance.md`).


This archive can specify **interfaces and floors** (what must be emitted, how it can be contested, what happens when deadlines pass). It cannot fully design institutional culture; it can only make culture *legible enough to correct* (through complaint lanes, retaliation monitors, training expectations, and supervision receipts).

## Named tensions (design must surface these)
- **Merit vs responsiveness:** professional administration vs democratic control.
- **Controls vs throughput:** anti-corruption and audit checks vs service delivery capacity.
- **Standardization vs judgment:** rule-following vs humane discretion at the counter (`98-persons-path-and-accessibility-invariants.md`).
- **Continuity vs purge:** institutional memory vs political turnover (`31-...`).

## A. Minimum viable public service (MVPS)
1) **Merit and non-partisan administration**
- MUST: merit-based recruitment and promotion; clear job classifications; transparent pay bands.
- MUST: limits on political appointments and “acting” roles in core delivery/oversight functions.

2) **Leadership and capability (fit-for-purpose)**
- SHOULD: workforce planning; competency framework; training pipeline; mobility across departments.
- SHOULD: “skills core” for procurement, digital, data, evaluation, and frontline management.


2a) **Interiors matter (process, supervision, and care)**
- MUST: clear supervision/accountability chains, manageable caseloads, and basic workplace safety—otherwise “paper compliance” and coercion drift become default.
- SHOULD: train frontline staff on receipt/reason‑giving, navigation duty, and anti‑retaliation norms (`31`, `47`, `83`, `98`).


2b) **Person-facing capacity (time, literacy, safety)**
The archive’s interfaces fail in practice if only the literate, safe, and well‑resourced can use them.

- MUST: **navigator / interpreter** capacity as core delivery infrastructure (help people read receipts, file appeals, and route “wrong door” cases) (`98-persons-path-and-accessibility-invariants.md`, `08-...`, `47-...`).
- SHOULD: offline and low‑literacy publishing paths (bulletin boards, radio, in‑person explanations) for rules, service standards, and deadlines (`31-...`, `82-...`).
- SHOULD: reduce time costs (pre‑filled forms, once‑only proof where safe, extended hours, mobile intake) so contestation is not reserved for those with leisure (`47-...`).
- MUST: treat fear/retaliation as a capacity constraint; secure channels and follow‑through are part of service delivery (`83-...`, `32-...`).

**Mercy in practice:** frontline systems SHOULD have lawful “hard case” space (hardship relief, humanitarian exceptions) with supervision and receipts—so compassion does not require informal favoritism (`66-...`, `85-...`).


3) **Ethics and protection**
- MUST: conflict-of-interest rules; asset disclosure for high-risk roles; procurement integrity controls (see `22-public-integrity-and-procurement.md`).
- MUST: whistleblower protection and retaliation monitoring (UNCAC supports this baseline); implement a protected disclosure lane (see `83-whistleblowing-and-protected-disclosures.md`).
- Optional comparative reference: EU whistleblower directive text (Directive (EU) 2019/1937) [BIB-EU-WHISTLE-2019]

4) **Operational continuity**
- MUST: records management; continuity plans for critical services; clear delegation and decision logs.
- SHOULD: post-incident learning loop (after-action review) integrated into training.

5) **Contracting without “hollow state”**
- MUST: capacity to specify, audit, and exit vendor contracts (no dependency on a single supplier for core sovereignty functions).
- SHOULD: open standards + portability clauses; public system register for high-stakes systems (`IOP-5`).

6) **Service legibility (reduce procedural cruelty)**
- MUST: publish and maintain a Service Catalog & Access Journeys Register keyed by `SRV-*` for core public services; keep versions/as-of aligned with `RULE-*` (see `47-service-catalog-and-access-journeys-register.md`, `39-rulebook-and-instruments-registry.md`).
- MUST: service-facing denials and restrictions issue `DRR` receipts citing `SRV-*` + `RULE-*` (as-of) + `AL-*` (see `47-service-catalog-and-access-journeys-register.md`, `08-...`, `31-...`).
- SHOULD: measure and reduce administrative burden (fold into CAD-2 burden indicators) as a first-class capacity metric; treat burden as a design choice, not an accident. See [BIB-RSF-ADMINBURDEN-2018], [BIB-OECD-GPP-SERVICE-2022], [BIB-UK-SERVICESTANDARD].

## B. Failure modes (and the smallest countermeasure)
- **Patronage / politicization:** hard boundary for political appointments + transparent hiring + independent appeals.
- **Churn and brain drain:** pay transparency, career pathways, and training; protect technical roles from political cycles.
- **Vendor capture:** procurement competence + auditability + exit plans (`OPEN-2`, `IOP-3/5`).
- **Administrative burden:** simplify forms and eligibility rules; measure “time tax” on residents.
