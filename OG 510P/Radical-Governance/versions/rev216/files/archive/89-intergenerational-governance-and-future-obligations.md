# Intergenerational Governance & Future Obligations (Time, Irreversibility, Stewardship)

**Purpose:** make duties to future persons enforceable through durable records, standing, and fiscal/plan joins.

Many governance failures are **time failures**: benefits are immediate, costs arrive later, and the harmed parties cannot vote yet.

This memo defines a small set of **intergenerational primitives** that can be composed at any scope without inventing a new branch of government.

Many intergenerational duties are a **representation‑without‑self‑advocacy** problem (structurally like children and incapacity): future‑affected parties cannot contest, so the system must provide representation that is *independent of the actors causing harm*.

Anchors: UN Declaration on Future Generations [BIB-UN-DFG], UN Pact for the Future [BIB-UN-PACT-FUTURE-2024], and statutory models such as Wales’ Well‑being of Future Generations Act [BIB-WALES-FUTUREGEN-ACT-2015].

## Named tensions (design must surface these)
- Present needs vs future obligations (intergenerational injustice as a governance failure).
- Durable memory for accountability vs privacy and changing norms over time.
- Binding commitments vs democratic revision (lock-in vs betrayal).
- Aggregated long-term benefits vs concentrated near-term harms.

---
## 1) When intergenerational governance is required

Add an intergenerational lane when decisions involve:
- **irreversibility** (extinction, catastrophic ecosystem loss, long‑lived pollutants, nuclear waste)
- **deep lock‑in** (infrastructure/path dependence with multi‑decade lifetimes)
- **systemic risk** (financial fragility, biosecurity, AI/critical tech safety)
- **rights over time** (children’s rights; long‑term displacement; cultural loss)
- **intergenerational distribution** (debt, pensions, asset booms, housing access)

---

## 2) Minimal institutional patterns (choose ≥2)

These are deliberately small; do not overbuild.

### A. Future‑impact statement (FIS) + publication duty
For high‑impact decisions, publish an **FIS** with:
- horizon(s): 10/30/100 years (choose what fits the hazard)
- who bears costs later, and why that is acceptable
- uncertainty bounds + scenario sensitivity
- “irreversibility test” result (see below)

### B. Independent reviewer / guardian function
Create an independent office (or extend an ombuds/auditor) with authority to:
- review FIS quality and assumptions
- require revisions (procedural veto)
- escalate to a court/tribunal on process failures

### C. Long-term targets + accountability metrics
Adopt explicit long‑term goals (e.g., net‑zero pathways, biodiversity targets) with:
- measurable indicators
- annual reporting
- a remedy lane for persistent non‑compliance (see `08-...`, `36-...`)

### D. Sunset + renewal discipline for long-lived powers
For discretionary programs with long tails, require:
- time‑bound authorization
- evaluation gates (`GATE-*` in `03-...`)
- renewal only on published evidence

### E. Stewardship funds / trusts for commons
Where assets are extracted or depleting (land, minerals, carbon budgets), ring‑fence:
- revenues into a fund with rules for intergenerational equity
- publication of withdrawals and benefits distribution

### F. Durable standing + record across time
Intergenerational governance fails if future-impact claims cannot persist across administrations.
- Future-impact statements and review outcomes MUST be stored as point‑in‑time retrievable artifacts (versioned `REL-*` where publishable; durable record rules per `31-...` and integrity/hashes per `53-...`).
- The authorizing decision SHOULD issue (or reference) a `DRR-*` so later challengers can trace **why** the choice was made, under which rules, and with what assumptions (`70-...`).

## 3) The irreversibility test (a small decision gate)

For any decision that crosses an irreversibility threshold:
1) **Can the harm be reversed within 1 generation without heroic assumptions?**
2) **If not, is there a rights‑consistent justification with bounded downside?**
3) **Are mitigation/compensation mechanisms credible and funded?**
4) **Is there an exit path if assumptions fail?** (sunset, adaptive management, stop rules)

If the answer to (1) is “no” and (2–4) are weak, escalation to a higher scope is presumptively required (`54-subsidiarity-and-scope-assignment-test.md`).

---

## 4) Interface obligations (make the future legible)

A future lane must be **joinable** in the archive’s registers:

- Create a `DRR` entry with `DRR-TYPE: FUTURE` for major future-impact decisions (see `25-...`, `36-...`).
- Include `RC-*` reason codes for deferrals and discounting choices (`52-...`).
- Publish the FIS and link it from the relevant `RULE-*` or program entry (`28-...`, `39-...`).
- If deliberation is used for long-horizon tradeoffs, register it in `ENG-*` (`88-...`, `41-...`).

---

## 5) Cross-scope notes (how it shows up)

- **Micro-local / municipal:** land use, water, transport, school siting, housing; easiest win is FIS + deliberation for irreversible siting decisions.
- **Regional / national:** debt, pensions, industrial policy, climate transition; requires independent reviewer + targets + renewal discipline.
- **Supranational / global:** planetary boundaries and existential risk; the UN’s 2024 Summit of the Future outputs provide a current normative anchor for long-horizon duties [BIB-UN-PACT-FUTURE-2024], [BIB-UN-DFG], [BIB-UN-GDC].
