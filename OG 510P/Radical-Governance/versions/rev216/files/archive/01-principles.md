# Principles

**Purpose:** state the non‑negotiable governance floors (what power owes people) that every domain memo must satisfy.

These principles are intentionally *cross-scope*: they apply to neighborhood councils and global treaties alike.



## 0) Purpose (structures over virtue)

This archive is designed for a world where people in power are ordinary: sometimes good, sometimes bad, usually busy, always capable of rationalization.  
It is **not** a substitute for political struggle or ethical leadership. It is the infrastructure that makes abuse **harder to hide**, reasons **easier to contest**, and correction **more feasible**.


**Plural pathways:** this archive is written as an engineering-spec for contestable governance (receipts, rule-citations, remedy lanes, oversight). Some traditions achieve functional equivalents through different social forms (e.g., consensus-based governance where “appeal” is a category error). Treat those as compatible when they **actually deliver the same person-facing protections**: reasons people can understand, safe challenge paths, and correction when authority is wrong.

Governance failures are lived as **confusion, waiting, fear, humiliation, and material loss**. The specifications in this archive should be judged by whether they measurably reduce that suffering for governed people (`98-persons-path-and-accessibility-invariants.md`, `31-...`, `82-...`).

**Adoption is political (design requirement):** the same actors who benefit from discretion often control implementation. Any proposal in this archive SHOULD name who loses discretionary power, who gains contestation capacity, and what incentives/coalitions make the constraints durable (see `99-protective-legibility-and-adoption-dynamics.md`).

## 1) Subsidiarity with teeth
- **Default:** decisions are made at the *lowest* level that can competently handle the problem.
- **Escalate** only for: cross-border spillovers, scale economies, rights protection, or coordination failures.
- Higher levels MUST publish a **scale/effects** justification and keep interventions proportionate (mature formulation: TEU Article 5 conferral/subsidiarity/proportionality).  
  Reference: see [BIB-TEU-A5].

## 2) Legitimacy is multi-channel
Legitimacy comes from **(a) voice**, **(b) rights**, and **(c) performance**:
- **Voice:** elections and structured participation (see `DEC-*` and `OPEN-*`).
- **Rights:** non-negotiable constraints on power; due process; equal political standing (see `LAW-*`).
- **Performance:** institutions deliver safety, basic services, and fair dispute resolution (see `CAP-*`, `SAFE-*`).
- **Exit:** where feasible, people need credible off‑ramps (mobility, alternative providers, jurisdictional competition, safe switches) so “voice” is not the only channel.
- **The governed is not fixed:** policies and boundary decisions (migration status, incarceration, disability determinations, residency rules) can silently change who is protected or heard. Collective subjects (communities, peoples, organizations) can also be governed as groups. These boundary moves MUST be receipted and contestable.
  Public service capability anchor: see [BIB-OECD-PSLC].

Rights baselines:
- [BIB-UDHR] (participation and equal dignity)
- [BIB-ICCPR] Article 25 (political participation rights)

**Procedural + relational legitimacy:** contestable artifacts (rules, receipts, appeals) are the *procedural* half. Legitimacy also depends on the *relational* half—dignity, being heard, and belonging. This archive mostly specifies the procedural machinery, but systems SHOULD explicitly protect dignity in service standards and frontline practice (`82-...`, `09-...`).

**Pluralism / functional equivalents:** this archive describes one high-confidence path to contestable governance (receipts + registers + remedy). Many traditions (including consensus-based or customary systems) can supply functional equivalents. The requirement is not the form; it is that people can reliably learn the rule, understand the reason, and reach a safe, enforceable path to contest and repair harm.


- **Representation duty:** systems MUST name how they protect those who cannot effectively contest (children, people under custody/control, people with severe disability, future persons). This requires explicit standing/guardianship rules, resourcing, and conflict-of-interest controls (see `89-intergenerational-governance-and-future-obligations.md`, `66-justice-and-administrative-justice-governance.md`, and `98-persons-path-and-accessibility-invariants.md`).

- **The governed category is not fixed:** this archive is written primarily for human persons and communities, but its constraint logic applies to any entity subject to public power. If new governed subjects emerge (including advanced AI), they require legible rules, reasons, and recourse—not opaque control.

- **Resistance boundary (when institutions fail):** this archive assumes institutions are reformable. When remedy lanes are closed, oversight is captured, coercion is unconstrained, and institutional channels are exhausted, the governed retain moral standing to resist through non‑institutional means. This archive does not specify when or how; it specifies the structures that make resistance *unnecessary* by making reform possible.

## 3) Rule of law, not rule by paperwork
- **Predictability:** rules are public, stable, and knowable.
- **Constraint:** no actor is above the law; power is checkable.
- **Remedy:** accessible, impartial dispute resolution with enforceable outcomes.
- Operational definition anchor: see [BIB-WJP-ROL].
- Checklist method anchor: see [BIB-VENICE-ROL-2025].
- Remedy baseline anchor: see [BIB-UN-REMEDY-60147].



### 3a) Legibility is a constraint (no invisible power)
- If an action affects rights, obligations, or access to essential services, it MUST generate a **portable receipt** (`DRR-*`) with cited rule basis (`RULE-*` as‑of), reasons (`RC-*`), and a discoverable remedy lane (`AL-*`).
- **No receipt → no legitimate effect:** missing required artifacts are treated as a governance incident (auditable, appealable, and escalation‑triggering), not a paperwork glitch.
- Remedy systems MUST treat **delay as denial**: missed deadlines become explicit outcomes (`AO-NORESP`) and trigger auto‑escalation/interim protection rules (see [TM-23]).
### 3b) Legibility is a tool, not a value (weaponization risk)
Legibility is a constraint on authority (so power can be contested), but it is not automatically protective.

**Protective legibility conditions (name them):**
- **independent enforcement** capacity that is not dependent on the goodwill of those being made legible (`32-...`),
- **organized countervailing power** (unions, civil society, independent media) with standing and resourcing to act on evidence (`69-...`, `41-...`),
- **accessible remedy** that is low‑cost/low‑risk and can compel relief (`08-...`, `36-...`, `98-persons-path-and-accessibility-invariants.md`),
- **credible exit options** where feasible so “voice” is not the only channel (mobility, alternative providers, jurisdictional competition).

**Joinability is not justice:** joinable IDs and clean metadata are *infrastructure*, not outcomes. You can have perfect joins and zero relief if no one has standing, safety, or enforcement capacity to use them; conversely, partial joins can still support justice when a determined advocate connects dots manually. Treat joins as audit enablers, not a terminal value.

Where these are absent, treat increased legibility as a **risk** that must be paired with minimization, access controls, and regime‑change safety planning (`77-...`, `99-protective-legibility-and-adoption-dynamics.md`).

Legibility makes power contestable, but it can also be weaponized when regimes change or when safeguards fail.
- Designs that centralize sensitive person‑data MUST assume hostile re‑use is possible and minimize what is centralized (see `77-sensitive-information-and-secrecy-governance.md` and `99-protective-legibility-and-adoption-dynamics.md`).
- Where full transparency would create danger, systems SHOULD publish **redacted existence proofs** (possession‑based verification) plus logged access + independent inspection rather than silent opacity.

### 3c) Accessibility is part of legitimacy (the person’s path)
A right that cannot be exercised in practice is not a right.
- Rights‑affecting systems MUST satisfy the **accessibility invariant** and **receipt comprehension test** (see `98-persons-path-and-accessibility-invariants.md` and the Decision Receipt fields in `31-records-foi-and-government-memory.md`).
- **Person’s Path design test:** for every register/receipt/lane a system defines, implementers SHOULD be able to name the concrete person it serves (and how they find/use it). If no plausible person can use it under the persona constraints, refactor before adding complexity.
- Remedy systems MUST treat **fear/retaliation** and **delay** as legitimacy failures, not user errors (see `08-remedy-and-grievance.md`, `83-whistleblowing-and-protected-disclosures.md`).

## 4) Open government by default
- Budgets, procurement, and outcomes MUST be transparent (with privacy safeguards).
- **Access to official documents** is a baseline transparency right (design FOI as a byproduct of recordkeeping): see [BIB-COE-TROMSO].
- Public input MUST be structurally supported (not purely symbolic).
- Anchor: see [BIB-OECD-OG].
- Procurement transparency anchor: see [BIB-OCDS].

## 5) Polycentricity beats monocultures
- Multiple centers of decision-making reduce single-point failure and enable learning.
- Overlap in **checks** (audits, courts) is healthy; overlap in **operational control** is usually costly.
- **Cultural pluralism (functional test):** the archive’s primitives (written receipts, registries, formal lanes) are one *family* of solutions. The test is functional: does the governed person have a credible, accessible way to understand, challenge, and correct authority? Oral, customary, restorative, or consensus mechanisms can satisfy the spirit even if they do not emit `DRR-*`—and implementation MUST not be used to delegitimize traditions that genuinely protect the vulnerable. (See `99-protective-legibility-and-adoption-dynamics.md`.)

## 6) Commons logic for shared resources
For shared resources and shared infrastructures (land-use, water, data, networks), institutions SHOULD aim for:
- When harms are slow and boundary-crossing, treat **ecological limits** as explicit budget objects (ceilings/floors + registries) — see `11-commons-and-ecological-governance.md`.
- clear boundaries and membership,
- locally fitted rules,
- participatory rule-making,
- monitoring,
- graduated sanctions,
- accessible conflict resolution,
- recognition of self-organization,
- nested governance for large systems.
Reference: Ostrom design principles (overview and extensions): see [BIB-STERN-2011-IJC-305].

## 7) Finance must match authority
- Every level MUST have:
  - predictable revenue,
  - a duty to publish a comprehensible budget,
  - independent audit,
  - hard limits on off-book liabilities.
- Costs SHOULD be internalized where harms/benefits occur, with transfers for equity and shared burdens.
- Fiscal transparency anchor: see [BIB-IMF-FTC-2019].

- **Governance infrastructure costs are real:** budgeting must explicitly fund the oversight, records, translation, and remedy machinery that makes legitimacy workable (see `07-fiscal-and-budgetary-governance.md`, `09-public-service-and-state-capacity.md`).


## 8) Safety, coercion, and the “minimum violence constraint”
- Coercive powers MUST be:
  - narrowly defined,
  - publicly overseen,
  - reviewable by independent bodies,
  - subject to due process and proportionality.
- Emergency powers MUST be sunset-bounded and reviewable (`SAFE-3`).
- **Assurance case for high-stakes power:** high-discretion/high-harm regimes SHOULD maintain an `AC-*` case (claim → argument → evidence + monitoring + stop-conditions) (`73-assurance-case-and-governance-safety-case.md`).

## 9) Continuous improvement as a constitutional feature
- Institutions MUST have:
  - measurement of outcomes (`03-metrics-and-evidence.md`),
  - safe-to-fail experimentation pathways,
  - sunset clauses for high-risk policies,
  - structured review cycles.
- Include explicit long-horizon review (e.g., Future Council triggers) for high-impact decisions (`DEC-6`).
  Anchor: see [BIB-UN-DFG].

## 10) Digital infrastructure is governance
- Where digital systems mediate rights or services, they MUST be auditable, contestable, and privacy-preserving.
- Anchor framing: see [BIB-UN-DPG].
- Information integrity MUST be treated as public infrastructure: independent statistics/evaluation, provenance of official claims, and transparent corrections (`OPEN-4/5`).
  Anchor: UN Fundamental Principles of Official Statistics — see [BIB-UNFPOS].

## Named tensions (design must surface these)
- Legibility vs safety (registries can be weaponized).
- Transparency vs retaliation (people won’t speak if they get punished).
- Joinability/auditability vs privacy and non-targeting.
- Comprehensiveness vs cognitive manageability (complexity budget).
- Flexibility/mercy vs equal treatment and capture risks.
- Technocratic specification vs democratic choice (the spec must remain contestable).
