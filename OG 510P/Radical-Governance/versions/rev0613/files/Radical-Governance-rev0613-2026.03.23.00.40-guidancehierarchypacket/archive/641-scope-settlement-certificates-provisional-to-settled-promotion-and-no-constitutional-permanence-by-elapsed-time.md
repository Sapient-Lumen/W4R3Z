# 641 — Scope settlement certificates, provisional-to-settled promotion, and no constitutional permanence by elapsed time

## One-line thesis

An ideal multilevel polity should require one public **scope-settlement certificate** before any materially stack-altering reform moves from **provisional live** to **settled live** status: the certificate should say whether the authorised settlement truly survived contact with budgets, staff, systems, supervision, and remedies, retire or carry forward every temporary exception openly, and refuse constitutional permanence by mere elapsed time.

## Why this matters

The archive’s recent stack-governance cluster is now much stronger. It says **how scope should fit** (`628`-`632`), **where the live stack should be written down and remembered** (`633`-`634`), **how stack-changing proposals should be described, classified, overridden, and interpreted** (`635`-`638`), **how they should be checked before launch** (`639`), and **how they should be observed after launch** (`640`).

What it still lacked was the final promotion rule. `640` says that a materially stack-altering reform should leave its observation window only through one explicit settlement statement. But a short closure statement is not yet a full constitutional memory object. Without a more disciplined promotion surface, provisional operation still tends to become settled fact by drift. Everyone remembers that a reform “went live”, that the first weeks were rough, and that things eventually stabilised. But no one can later say with confidence **what exactly became settled**, **which temporary deviations were retired**, **which ones survived under fresh legal cover**, **which parts of the original design were abandoned**, or **what future readers should treat as the live constitutional baseline**.

That omission matters because many multilevel distortions happen precisely at the handoff from “still being watched” to “now considered normal.” The finance ministry keeps one temporary approval hook because nobody wrote down when it expired. A regional platform retains admin rights that were acceptable during migration but not in steady state. A national supervisor continues to clear decisions case by case because the emergency lane faded into memory rather than being closed or renewed. Local associations stop challenging a workaround because the law was never updated, yet everyone has adjusted around it. The result is a new constitutional settlement that has been **lived into existence**, but never clearly promoted, bounded, or owned.

The archive therefore needs one compact doctrine of **scope-settlement certificates**. Observability tells the polity what happened after go-live. A settlement certificate tells the polity whether the provisional live arrangement has genuinely earned ordinary status, whether it remains provisional under a named exception lane, or whether the stack must be reopened. Without that step, the archive still permits one serious failure mode: **constitutional permanence by elapsed time**.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Require one explicit promotion act from provisional live to settled live

A materially stack-altering reform should not become part of the ordinary constitutional baseline merely because its observation window ended, the news cycle moved on, or users adapted. Promotion from **provisional live** to **settled live** should require one named public act: the **scope-settlement certificate**.

That act is not ceremonial. It is the moment when the polity says, in effect: this is the version of the reform that now counts as the live stack, and here is the evidence for treating it as such.

### 2. Dock the certificate to the whole stack-change chain

The settlement certificate should not stand alone. It should link back to the full change chain:

- the **scope-diff packet** (`635`);
- the **scope opinion** (`636`);
- the **scope override** if one existed (`637`);
- the **canonical scope register** entry (`633`);
- the **scope jurisprudence digest** if courts or tribunals mattered (`634`);
- the **pre-go-live conformance attestation** (`639`);
- and the **post-launch variance ledger** (`640`).

A reader who cannot follow that chain should not be asked to accept that the reform has honestly settled.

### 3. State which kind of settlement has actually been reached

Not every reform exits observation in the same way. The certificate should therefore classify the result openly. At minimum it should distinguish:

- **exact conformance settlement** — the live arrangement now matches the authorised one closely enough to be treated as settled;
- **amended conformance settlement** — the live arrangement differs in ways that were openly regularised through the proper lane before settlement;
- **constrained continuation** — the reform remains live only within a narrower, named footprint while further repair or re-clearance continues;
- **rollback or handback settlement** — the attempted reform has been withdrawn in whole or part and the prior or repaired allocation now governs.

This prevents the misleading sentence “the reform settled” from hiding materially different constitutional outcomes.

### 4. Carry every temporary exception forward explicitly or retire it visibly

The certificate should list every temporary exception that shaped provisional live operation and force a visible choice on each one:

- retired;
- renewed under a lawful temporary lane with a fresh clock;
- absorbed into a re-cleared permanent settlement;
- or left unresolved and therefore blocking promotion.

Temporary approvals, emergency shortcuts, migration permissions, legacy admin rights, host-government substitutions, manual budget controls, vendor workarounds, and special review fast lanes should not simply disappear into narrative memory. If they mattered during provisional live operation, the certificate should say what became of them.

### 5. Freeze one baseline description of the settled stack for future comparison

A settlement certificate should do more than close a file. It should create one crisp baseline snapshot against which later drift can be measured.

That snapshot should say, at lane level where material:

- who now holds legal title;
- who exercises operational control;
- what territorial footprint is actually in force;
- what finance path and supervision path now apply;
- what remedy routes are live;
- and which exceptions remain active.

Without one baseline snapshot, future reviewers must reconstruct the post-launch settlement from scattered memories and operational residue, which is exactly how folklore returns.

### 6. Refuse settlement when the live arrangement differs materially but has not been re-cleared

A settlement certificate should not be used to launder a changed reality into legitimacy. If the live arrangement differs materially from what the packet and opinion honestly covered, the proper response is not to bless the mismatch. The proper response is to reopen the constitutional lane.

The certificate should therefore be unavailable where:

- live control has shifted materially without fresh authorisation;
- supervisory or fiscal powers remain broader than approved;
- territorial reach changed without the required consultation or consent;
- review and remedy routes remain blocked or degraded;
- or digital and administrative defaults are still doing constitutional work beyond the approved design.

No polity should be able to use settlement language to certify a reform that still needs re-clearance.

### 7. Preserve challengeability at the moment of promotion

The move from provisional live to settled live is often the moment when practical challenge becomes hardest, because institutions present the arrangement as already normal. The certificate should therefore keep legal and political challenge visible rather than implying that settlement ends reviewability.

It should say:

- who may challenge the settlement classification;
- through which administrative, judicial, parliamentary, audit, or intergovernmental lanes;
- on what clock;
- and whether any issues remain under dispute.

This matters especially where local or regional self-government claims may still be live. Settlement is a classification of the current arrangement, not a magical extinguisher of rights.

### 8. Give the settlement certificate a short public and a machine-readable form

The archive should prefer one short human-readable settlement statement plus one structured machine-readable record. The public version should be intelligible to non-specialists. The machine-readable version should update the canonical scope register, exception fields, review dates, and lineage markers in ways later audits can query.

Without this split, the polity drifts toward either opaque data fields with no public meaning or eloquent prose that cannot be tracked across the stack.

### 9. Set the next review date and the next tripwires at settlement time

A settled arrangement is not an eternal arrangement. The certificate should therefore name:

- the next routine review date, if any;
- which continuing exceptions or dependencies require earlier review;
- which trigger conditions should reopen the stack (`632`);
- and what evidence owners must preserve so later review is possible.

This keeps settlement from becoming constitutional hibernation. The point is not to keep everything provisional forever, but to avoid mistaking one completed observation window for a permanent exemption from future scrutiny.

### 10. Preserve lineage about what was attempted, not only what survived

The settlement certificate should retain the constitutional memory of abandoned or superseded elements: temporary upper-tier approvals that ended, supervisory powers that were narrowed, territorial footprints that were reduced, workflow controls that were rolled back, or remedies that were restored after being blocked.

That memory matters for future reform. Without it, each later debate starts from a false story in which the final arrangement appears inevitable rather than historically chosen through contest, correction, and repair.

## Guardrails

- Do not let elapsed time, operational familiarity, or political fatigue count as promotion to settled live status.
- Do not certify settlement while material temporary exceptions remain undocumented.
- Do not use a settlement certificate to regularise a live arrangement that still requires packet, opinion, override, or handback work.
- Do not collapse human-readable settlement into opaque registry fields with no public explanation.
- Do not treat settlement as the end of challenge rights, only as the named description of the current live arrangement.

## Failure modes

- **constitutional permanence by elapsed time**: a temporary or provisional arrangement is treated as settled because enough time passed without a formal promotion act.
- **closure without baseline**: the observation window ends, but nobody can later say what precise live settlement became the baseline for future reviews.
- **exception sediment**: temporary controls, shortcuts, and emergency permissions survive into ordinary government because no certificate forced a visible disposition on them.
- **settlement laundering**: a materially changed live arrangement is blessed as settled rather than sent back through the proper re-clearance lane.
- **amnesia by success narrative**: the public story says the reform “worked,” but the archive loses memory of what had to be repaired, narrowed, rolled back, or reauthorised to make it workable.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Does promotion from provisional live to settled live require one explicit settlement certificate rather than silence or habit?
2. Is the certificate linked to the packet, opinion, override, register, conformance attestation, and variance ledger?
3. Does it classify what kind of settlement was reached instead of implying one generic success state?
4. Does it dispose of every temporary exception openly rather than letting them linger as residue?
5. Does it create one baseline snapshot of the settled stack for future comparison?
6. Is settlement blocked when the live arrangement differs materially from the authorised one without fresh clearance?
7. Are challenge routes and dispute posture still visible at the moment of promotion?
8. Does the public get a readable settlement statement while the register gets machine-readable updates?
9. Does the certificate set the next review date and tripwires instead of narrating eternal closure?
10. Can future readers see not just what survived, but what was tried, corrected, narrowed, or abandoned?

## Closing image

A constitutional stack does not become honest merely because launch passed and people learned the workaround. It becomes honest when the polity can say, in public and with evidence, **this is the arrangement that survived contact with reality, this is what was repaired or rejected, these exceptions are gone or freshly bounded, and this is the baseline from which future drift will be judged**. Anything weaker is just **constitutional permanence by elapsed time**.
