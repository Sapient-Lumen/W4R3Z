# 481 — Ephemeral working aids, decisional capture thresholds, and no shadow dossiers

## One-line thesis

Consequential public-AI operations should clearly separate ephemeral drafts, scratchpads, convenience copies, and personal working aids from authority-bearing records, but once those aids are circulated, relied on, or add unique decisional substance, they must either be captured into the governed record or barred from carrying decision weight.

## Why this matters

The archive already covers authorship classes, record schedules, official communications, source-backed summaries, and native evidence lanes. What it still lacked was one direct rule for the messy middle layer where real work often happens first: the scratch note, working draft, operator side panel, exported recap, AI-assisted rough summary, personal checklist, or temporary convenience copy.

That layer becomes dangerous when it stays half-visible. A caseworker uses an AI-generated scratch summary to decide what to ask next, but the scratch summary is never retained. A reviewer circulates an annotated draft with unique warnings, then later treats it as “just working material.” An operator keeps a private workaround note that silently governs which cases get escalated. A shadow spreadsheet or notebook becomes the place where the real decision basis lives, while the official record only preserves the polished outcome.

The problem is not that every rough note must be immortal. The problem is that institutions often let materially relied-on working aids float between two incompatible stories:

- too informal to govern,
- but important enough to shape action.

A public-law archive needs a sharper threshold. If something is only a convenience aid, it should stay non-authoritative and disposable under the right rules. If it carries unique decisional substance, it should be promoted, linked, and retained as part of the governed record. The archive should reject the middle state where hidden working matter does real governance work off the books.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Class working material explicitly

Working material should be classified into compact, visible classes such as:

- ephemeral personal aid,
- team working draft,
- circulated draft for comment,
- AI-assisted scratch summary,
- convenience copy of recordkeeping material,
- case-working file,
- and promoted recordkeeping artifact.

The archive should not force one retention rule on all rough material. It should force institutions to say what class they think they are handling.

### 2. Use a decisional-capture threshold

A working aid crosses into capture territory when it does one or more of the following:

- is circulated for approval, comment, recommendation, or follow-up,
- contains unique annotations or reasoning that materially shaped action,
- documents a substantive case judgment, risk decision, exception, or workaround,
- becomes the practical source of truth for staff,
- or is necessary to reconstruct how the institution formulated or executed a consequential act.

Below that threshold, disposal may be appropriate. Above it, “just a draft” is not an honest governance answer.

### 3. Keep ephemeral aids explicitly non-authoritative

Where temporary aids are allowed to remain ephemeral, the archive should make clear that they:

- do not set policy,
- do not substitute for the case record,
- do not count as the official explanation,
- do not silently override controlled registers or packets,
- and cannot be the only place where a material basis exists.

Ephemeral status should be an explicit boundary, not a vague hope.

### 4. Promote, link, or digest relied-on rough material

When a working aid crosses the capture threshold, the institution does not always need to preserve every keystroke. But it should preserve enough to keep governance reconstructable, such as:

- the working artifact itself,
- a promoted clean copy with substantive annotations retained,
- a structured digest of the unique reasoning,
- or a receipt tying the temporary aid to the later official artifact it informed.

The archive should prefer minimal sufficient capture over total shadow loss.

### 5. Forbid off-record operating instructions for consequential work

Teams should not run a consequential public-AI service on the basis of hidden cheat sheets, quiet exception notebooks, unofficial escalation lists, or unregistered AI-generated crib notes that materially determine outcomes.

If staff genuinely need such aids to operate safely, that is evidence that the governed runbook, packet, or trace surface is incomplete and needs promotion into the official control stack.

### 6. Separate convenience copies from divergent working copies

A copy kept only for convenience may remain non-recordkeeping where a governed recordkeeping copy exists and the convenience copy adds no unique business value. But once the copy diverges through annotations, selective edits, scoring, risk flags, routing hints, or case-specific judgments, it should no longer inherit the harmlessness of a duplicate.

The archive should care about functional divergence, not cosmetic file ancestry.

### 7. Apply retention and deletion rules by class and promotion state

The archive should define different expectations for:

- ephemeral aids eligible for routine deletion,
- working files that may need temporary retention,
- promoted decisional artifacts that require recordkeeping disposition,
- and litigation-hold or appeal-bound materials that must not be deleted.

This lets institutions avoid both extremes:

- keeping everything forever,
- or destroying the only real decisional substrate.

### 8. Treat hidden working layers as governance signal

If staff repeatedly depend on scratch summaries, copied extracts, or shadow trackers to make the service operable, that is evidence of a design deficit. The answer is not merely better secrecy discipline. The answer is often to improve:

- official trace surfaces,
- runbooks,
- case-state models,
- review packets,
- or controlled knowledge artifacts.

## Guardrails

- Do not treat all drafts and notes as disposable by default.
- Do not preserve ephemeral convenience material as if it were automatically authoritative.
- Do not let unique annotations or AI-assisted scratch reasoning disappear when they materially shaped action.
- Do not run consequential workflows on hidden sidecars that the archive refuses to acknowledge.
- Do not confuse byte identity with governance identity; a copied artifact that acquires unique operational meaning is no longer just a copy.

## Failure modes

- **shadow dossier**: the real basis for action lives in an unofficial notebook, sheet, or scratch export.
- **draft laundering**: a circulated or relied-on draft is later dismissed as non-record working material.
- **ephemeral authority**: staff treat temporary AI summaries or convenience notes as if they were the true case record.
- **duplicate fiction**: a divergent copy inherits the harmless status of a mere convenience duplicate.
- **capture panic**: fear of over-retention leads teams to destroy the only material that explains how a consequential action formed.

## Practical tests

An ephemeral-aid governance discipline passes when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Are working materials explicitly classed instead of all being treated as either permanent record or harmless scratch?
2. Does the institution have a visible threshold for when rough material must be captured, promoted, or linked?
3. Can ephemeral aids stay genuinely non-authoritative rather than silently governing action?
4. Are hidden operating notes and shadow trackers prohibited from becoming the true control surface for consequential work?
5. Do retention and deletion rules vary honestly by class, promotion state, and legal hold posture?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a consequential service can say **the official record is complete** while unique decisional substance still lives in hidden scratchpads, drafts, or convenience copies, then it is still letting **working matter impersonate nonexistence**.
