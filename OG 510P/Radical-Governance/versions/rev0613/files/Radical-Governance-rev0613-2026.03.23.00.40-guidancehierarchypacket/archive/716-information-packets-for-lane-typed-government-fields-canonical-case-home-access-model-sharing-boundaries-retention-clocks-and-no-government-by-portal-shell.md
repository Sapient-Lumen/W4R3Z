# 716 — Information packets for lane-typed government fields: canonical case home, access model, sharing boundaries, retention clocks, and no government by portal shell

## One-line thesis

Once the archive has lane packets, interface packets, a control plane, operating modes, repair and sunset rules, evidence, challenge, participation, oversight, amendment discipline, delegation rules, a fiscal packet, and a capability packet for a field, it should also publish one narrow **information packet** saying where the field’s canonical case and record homes live, which surface is the system of record and which are only presentation or intake layers, what role-and-access model governs staff and delegates, what sharing boundaries and information agreements apply, what retention/export/handback rules preserve public memory, and what recovery path keeps the field governable when platforms, vendors, or host systems fail — so the field does not become government by portal shell, spreadsheet relay, or vendor-admin shadow.

## Why this matters

The recent packet spine can now carry much of a whole field constitution without forcing every later note to rewrite a miniature theory of the state.

- `699` gives the scope spine.
- `700` gives the minimum design packet.
- `701` gives rivalry tests.
- `702` gives lane-splitting discipline.
- `703` gives interface packets.
- `704` gives the control plane.
- `705` gives mode packets.
- `706` gives repair packets.
- `707` gives sunset packets.
- `708` gives the evidence packet.
- `709` gives the challenge packet.
- `710` gives the participation packet.
- `711` gives the oversight packet.
- `712` gives the amendment packet.
- `713` gives the delegation packet.
- `714` gives the fiscal packet.
- `715` gives the capability packet.

That sequence now explains who owns the field, how seams work, how it shifts mode, how the public can challenge it, how oversight reaches it, how the money and workforce constitutions stay visible, and how delegated slices remain subordinate.

But one common real-world question still lacks a compact constitutional object:

**where does the field’s live informational reality actually reside once law, money, staff, and service promises become operational?**

The archive already has broader doctrine here.

- `406` says public identity should separate person, role, authority, and delegation.
- `470` says intake seams need typed receipt, registration, and manual fallback routes.
- `544` says delegated public functions cannot become constitutional escape hatches through contractors or platforms.
- `571` says public records, archives, and official publication are constitutional memory layers rather than clerical afterthoughts.
- `576` says authoritative registries and identifiers need visible stewardship rather than contradictory copies.
- `621` says real shared government needs a digital operating home rather than a portal shell.
- `658` and `659` say evidentiary continuity and burden allocation cannot dissolve during migration or missing-file events.
- `692` and `693` say digital, cyber, and privacy constitutions need named system owners, access tiers, continuity rules, and lawful data handling.
- `713` says delegation is unsafe when the public lane loses records, data, or step-in capability.
- `708` says a field should already know what evidence matters, but that only works if there is a trustworthy place where the evidence is actually created, held, and replayed.

This note does not replace those. It compresses them into one reusable packet for the newer field-constitution stack.

Without an information packet, fields drift in predictable ways.

First, they drift by **portal shell**: the public sees a clean front end, but the real case state lives in inboxes, spreadsheets, vendor consoles, or multiple contradictory back ends.

Second, they drift by **vendor-admin shadow**: the field appears public in law, budget, and staffing, but the practical sovereign over roles, settings, logs, exports, and incident recovery is whichever supplier or host tenant controls the admin keys.

Third, they drift by **copy-sprawl governance**: registries, dashboards, extracts, and local workarounds multiply until nobody can say which record is authoritative, which copy is stale, or which change is actually live.

Fourth, they drift by **memory failure under stress**: an outage, migration, provider collapse, reorganisation, or emergency workaround breaks the link between case state, records, evidence, notice, and review rights.

An information packet exists so a field can have an information constitution as explicit as its authority, fiscal, workforce, and challenge constitutions.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Separate the information packet from the evidence packet

`708` already requires signals, thresholds, trigger maps, blind-spot registers, and assurance rhythm.

The information packet answers a different question: **where is the field’s live informational truth made, stored, accessed, shared, and recovered?**

An evidence packet may say that repeat delays above a threshold open repair review. An information packet then has to say which workflow state, case object, queue ledger, and audit trail make that threshold testable, and which system remains authoritative when extracts, BI dashboards, or downstream reports disagree.

If a note still says only that data should be “good” or “shared”, it has not yet described a governing field. It has described a hope.

### 2. Name one canonical case home and one canonical record home

The packet should begin by naming two homes.

- the **canonical case home** for live workflow state, routing, status, queue position, obligations, and decision progression;
- the **canonical record home** for the durable public record, retention class, official notices, decision trace, and later review.

Sometimes those homes are in one system. Often they are not. A case system may run live operations while records are exported or bound into a records repository, official publication system, archive, or document-management layer.

The packet should say so explicitly. Otherwise fields quietly let portals, inboxes, or working spreadsheets impersonate the case home, while downstream PDF bundles or vendor exports impersonate the record home.

### 3. Distinguish system of record from presentation, intake, and convenience layers

A serious information packet should say which surfaces are authoritative and which are not.

Typical non-authoritative layers include:

- intake forms and upload portals;
- mobile apps and status pages;
- call-centre or counter screens;
- dashboards, data extracts, and reports;
- shared spreadsheets used for temporary coordination;
- local caches, mirrors, or offline working copies.

The packet should also say how updates propagate, what happens when a non-authoritative layer disagrees with the system of record, and which visible fallback route applies when the presentation layer fails.

This docks directly to `470`: a broken intake surface should not erase a right, an application, or a challenge route merely because the shell failed before registration.

### 4. Publish one role-and-access model for the field rather than letting account structure become hidden constitution

The packet should say who can see, change, approve, export, publish, delete, and administer each information layer.

That means naming at minimum:

- role classes for frontline staff, supervisors, specialists, auditors, inspectors, elected or oversight actors, contractors, delegates, and emergency staff;
- joiner, mover, leaver, and delegation rules;
- privileged-admin tiers and dual-control rules where stakes justify them;
- emergency-access procedures and expiry;
- and the log and review duties for high-consequence access or override.

Using a host identity provider or national trust service can be acceptable. What is not acceptable is allowing the constitutional question of **who may do what in this field** to remain hidden inside another organisation’s directory groups, inherited admin roles, or vendor support permissions.

### 5. Make sharing boundaries explicit instead of narrating interoperability as virtue by itself

Fields often need to exchange information across lanes, tiers, providers, regulators, and public interfaces.

The packet should therefore say:

- what information classes are shared;
- for what purpose and lawful basis;
- with which counterparties;
- under what agreement, protocol, or standing interface;
- with what minimisation, masking, or segregation rules;
- and with what receipt, reconciliation, and error-correction path when data arrive late, wrong, or incomplete.

This keeps the field from pretending that more integration is always better. Often the constitutional question is not whether systems can connect, but **how far they should connect before the field loses governability, privacy discipline, or reviewable provenance**.

### 6. Keep publication, notice, and disclosure routes visible

A field may store many things privately, but it should still name the surfaces by which it becomes publicly knowable.

A normal packet should say:

- which decisions or rules generate official notice;
- which publication lane is legally or operationally authoritative;
- how people can retrieve their own case materials, notices, and reasons;
- which records feed disclosure, transparency, or freedom-of-information routes;
- and what stability, citation, or versioning rules keep publication from becoming screenshot folklore.

This docks to `571`. A field that can transact digitally but cannot later say what notice was sent, what version was operative, what record proves it, and where the public can see the live official position still lacks a constitutional memory layer.

### 7. Make retention, export, and handback rules part of the packet, not procurement annex leftovers

The packet should say what the field must still possess and preserve if a system changes, a contract ends, a provider fails, or a lane is transferred.

That usually means naming:

- retention clocks by record class;
- export format and metadata minimums;
- replayable decision traces and audit logs;
- which identifiers survive migration;
- what handback bundle is due from operators or host bodies;
- and how records, data, and logs remain reviewable after exit.

This docks directly to `586`, `657`, `658`, `659`, and `713`. A field that cannot leave one platform, provider, or host tenant without losing evidence, queue state, publication history, or challenge rights is not merely badly procured. It is constitutionally fragile.

### 8. Bind continuity and recovery to the information packet rather than treating them as purely technical operations

The packet should connect to `705`.

For each degraded, emergency, or recovery mode, the field should say:

- which information surfaces must remain live first;
- what manual fallback or offline route is lawful;
- how new case intake, notice, and challenge registration continue during outage;
- what reconciliation clock restores offline actions into the canonical case home;
- and who may declare a temporary workaround closed.

Otherwise fields discover during stress that a “temporary spreadsheet” or inbox is the real constitution and nobody can later reconstruct queue order, reasons, or rights-bearing timestamps.

### 9. Keep privacy, security, and usability in the same constitutional sentence

The packet should not split these into different moral universes.

A good information packet should therefore name:

- lawful information classes and minimisation boundaries;
- identity and authentication strength by risk class;
- admin and support restrictions;
- logging and monitoring proportionate to the stakes;
- and user-side accessibility, assisted-digital parity, and channel continuity.

A field becomes dishonest when one office optimises access, another optimises secrecy, a vendor optimises convenience, and nobody is visibly accountable for the whole information constitution.

### 10. Let information stress trigger repair, amendment, delegation review, or capability review

A normal information packet should say what conditions force review, such as:

- repeated divergence between portal status and canonical case state;
- privileged access or vendor support dependence becoming normal rather than exceptional;
- unresolved duplicate records or contradictory identifiers across lanes;
- repeated inability to reconstruct decision traces or notice history;
- migration or outage events causing evidentiary break;
- or delegated operators holding the only usable operational records or access paths.

Some of those triggers may open a repair packet. Others may require delegation review, capability rebuild, amendment of the field constitution, or even sunset of one brittle information layer. The packet should say so explicitly.

### 11. Keep the packet small enough that later field notes can cite it rather than rebuilding digital-government theory from scratch

A normal information packet should fit in one compact block.

If a later note needs pages of prose to explain where live case truth sits, how records and notices remain durable, who controls access, how sharing is bounded, what exports survive exit, and how outage fallback preserves rights, then the field still lacks a reusable information constitution.

## Compact information packet

A normal information packet should usually fit in one table-like block:

| Field | Canonical homes and access model | Sharing, continuity, and handback rule | Review trigger and consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Housing** | metropolitan housing lane owns one canonical placement and repairs case home; durable record home binds notices, inspection outcomes, safeguarding escalations, and complaint traces; role model separates placement staff, inspectors, provider users, ombuds access, and emergency override | borough referral interfaces are purpose-bound; providers receive only the slice needed to perform placement or repairs; outage fallback keeps phone and desk intake with timestamped manual registration; operator exit requires occupancy ledger, notices, logs, and property-condition handback | repeated mismatch between resident notice, provider view, and canonical case state opens repair packet, delegation review, or replatform amendment |
| **Transport** | transport lane keeps one canonical disruption and obligations case home plus one durable record/publication home for timetables, notices, exemptions, and incident decisions; access model separates control-room, operator, safety, regulator, and public-information roles | operators receive bounded route, asset, and incident data; manual degraded-mode entry can continue during control-system outage with fixed reconciliation clock; replacement operator handback includes logs, maintenance traces, route files, and admin-rights reset | repeated portal-shell divergence, vendor-admin dependence, or inability to replay incident decisions opens repair, capability rebuild, or contract step-in |
| **Care** | public care lane keeps canonical intake, assessment, safeguarding, and discharge case home; record home preserves eligibility reasons, care plans, notices, family/advocate submissions, and challenge history; access model distinguishes clinicians, assessors, provider staff, auditors, and advocates acting under lawful delegation | sharing agreements govern hospital, social-care, and approved-provider exchange; offline continuity preserves urgent intake and safeguarding escalation; provider exit requires care plans, contact history, records metadata, and access revocation proof | repeated lost referrals, inaccessible records, or provider-held-only history opens repair, oversight escalation, delegation review, or re-insourcing decision |

The exact field content will vary, but the packet form should stay recognizable.

## Failure modes

- **government by portal shell**: the public-facing surface looks authoritative, but the real state lives elsewhere and can diverge without notice.
- **vendor-admin shadow**: suppliers or host bodies hold the practical sovereign power over access, settings, logs, exports, or recovery.
- **copy-sprawl governance**: extracts, spreadsheets, and local caches become de facto systems of record.
- **intake without registration**: rights-bearing submissions are accepted by a surface that cannot prove registration, timestamp, or queue entry.
- **notice folklore**: people learn what is operative from screenshots, emails, or call-centre scripts because the authoritative publication lane is unstable.
- **memory failure under stress**: outage, migration, reorganisation, or emergency workaround breaks evidentiary continuity and later review.
- **exit without replay**: the field can export data blobs but not replay case history, logs, identifiers, reasons, or notices in reviewable form.
- **delegated record capture**: an operator or host body becomes the only reliable holder of the field’s operational history.

## Anti-escape-hatch tests

1. Can the field name one canonical case home and one canonical record home for each consequential workflow?
2. Can it distinguish system of record from presentation, intake, dashboard, and convenience layers?
3. Is there one visible role-and-access model rather than inherited account folklore from host bodies or vendors?
4. Are sharing boundaries explicit enough to say what is shared, why, with whom, and under what correction path?
5. Can the field prove official notice, publication version, and decision trace without relying on screenshots or verbal reconstruction?
6. Are retention, export, identifiers, and handback rules concrete enough to survive provider exit or system migration?
7. Do degraded and emergency modes preserve lawful intake, registration, notice, and later reconciliation rather than improvising away rights?
8. Can privacy, access, and usability be seen as one governed design rather than three disconnected offices fighting after the fact?
9. Do repeated information failures route into repair, capability review, delegation review, amendment, or sunset rather than another patch and another spreadsheet?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a later note can tell you **which lane owns the field, how seams work, what money and staff sustain it, what evidence matters, how the public can challenge it, and how oversight reaches it** but cannot also tell you **where the canonical case and record homes live, which surfaces are truly authoritative, who controls access, how sharing is bounded, what survives exit or outage, and how publication and notice remain durable**, then it still has **a field constitution that becomes vague exactly where public authority turns into informational reality**.
