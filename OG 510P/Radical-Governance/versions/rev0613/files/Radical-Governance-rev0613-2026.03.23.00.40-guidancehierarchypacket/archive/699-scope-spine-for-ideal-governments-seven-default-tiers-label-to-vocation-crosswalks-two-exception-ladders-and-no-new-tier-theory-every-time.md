# 699 — Scope spine for ideal governments: seven default tiers, label-to-vocation crosswalks, two exception ladders, and no new tier theory every time

## One-line thesis

The archive should treat ideal government from micro-local to global as one reusable **scope spine** — micro-local witness-and-stewardship units, municipalities as the first complete governments of ordinary life, metropolitan bodies for shared urban systems, regions for territorial platforms and shared rule, nations as guarantor states, continental bodies as bounded compacts, and global institutions as treaty narrow waists — while mapping ambiguous labels like county, district, province, state, and union back to those archetypes and using collapsed-stack and shared-regime exception ladders where the default seven do not fit.

## Why this matters

The archive already has a serious run on ideal governments by scope (`541`, `565`, `602`, `628`, `629`, `630`, `631`, `660`, `661`, `662`, `663`, `664`). That is enough substance to anchor a full constitutional theory.

What it still needed was one tighter archival object:

**a compression spine that future notes can cite instead of re-expanding the whole ladder every time.**

That matters for two reasons.

First, "everything in between" is where scope arguments get noisy. Political systems use labels such as **county**, **district**, **borough**, **province**, **state**, **region**, **union**, or **community** in radically different ways. A county may be a first-tier municipality, a metropolitan systems body, a sparse-territory region, or merely a deconcentrated state office. A district may be a neighborhood subdivision, an electoral map, a county-equivalent local government, or a ministry field administration. A province may be a genuine self-governing regional tier or a decorative duplicate of the center.

Second, archive growth becomes wasteful when every later sector note repeats the same constitutional preface before getting to its actual subject. A compact scope spine lets later notes say, for example, **this function belongs to the municipal archetype with a metropolitan override where spillovers are real**, or **this is a regional-platform function unless the polity is a collapsed-stack microstate**.

The aim here is therefore not to replace the earlier notes. It is to make them easier to reuse, easier to compress, and harder to quietly contradict.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Treat the seven default tiers as archetypes, not sacred map labels

The archive should default to seven archetypes:

- **micro-local** for witness, stewardship, and escalation;
- **municipal** for ordinary place government;
- **metropolitan** for shared urban systems;
- **regional** for territorial platforms and shared rule;
- **national** for equal citizenship, solidarity, and constitutional arbitration;
- **continental or plurilateral** for bounded cross-border compacts;
- **global** for treaty-bound narrow waists around genuinely planetary coordination problems.

These are constitutional jobs, not magic names. A polity may call them ward, village, town, county, city, department, province, state, land, autonomous community, union, common market, treaty body, or something else entirely. The archive should map names back to jobs.

### 2. Use one stable design grammar at every tier

Whenever the archive names a tier, it should be able to answer the same recurring questions already stabilized in `660`:

1. what is the decision forum;
2. what executive or administrative spine carries the work;
3. how much civil-service density the level honestly needs;
4. what fiscal base funds the mandate;
5. what review, audit, appeal, or remedy routes correct abuse or drift;
6. and what participation channel lets the governed reach it directly.

A tier named without that minimum kit is still only a label.

### 3. Map common public labels back to archetypes instead of theorizing from the name alone

The archive should use a simple crosswalk habit.

- **neighborhood, village, ward, parish, block, settlement** usually map to the **micro-local** archetype;
- **town, city, commune, municipality, borough, county** usually map to the **municipal** archetype when they are the first complete general governments of ordinary life;
- **metro, combined authority, city-region, county, capital region** usually map to the **metropolitan** archetype when they bind shared urban systems;
- **province, state, land, region, governorate, county** usually map to the **regional** archetype when they govern basins, corridors, hospitals, universities, grids, or constitutionally meaningful shared rule;
- **state, republic, kingdom, federation** map to the **national** archetype when they guarantee equal citizenship, redistribution, rights floors, macro-stability, and constitutional settlement;
- **union, community, common market, confederation, treaty organization** map to the **continental or plurilateral** archetype when they solve bounded cross-border problems through compact institutions;
- **international organizations, treaty regimes, assemblies, secretariats, scientific panels, courts, registries** map to the **global** archetype when the problem class is genuinely planetary.

The same label can therefore live in different archetypes in different countries. The archive should say which one it means.

### 4. Keep the allocation rule simple: ordinary place local-first, systems tiers by proof, guarantor tiers by duty, cross-border tiers by necessity

The default burden-of-proof rule from `629` should remain the archive’s routing grammar.

- keep **ordinary place** functions close to municipal government unless evidence shows they plainly spill over;
- upgrade to **metropolitan** or **regional** tiers when shared systems or territorial platforms are real enough to justify them;
- reserve **national** government for guarantor duties that depend on equal citizenship, solidarity, rights floors, or constitutional arbitration;
- reserve **continental** and **global** tiers for problems that cannot be honestly solved inside one state.

The archive should not create a new scope merely because a policy problem is politically difficult.

### 5. Treat counties, districts, and provinces as ambiguous until their vocation is stated

Some labels need extra discipline because they are chronically misleading.

A **county** might be:

- a municipal general government,
- a metropolitan systems government,
- a sparse-area regional government,
- or a merely administrative arm of the center.

A **district** might be:

- a submunicipal participatory unit,
- an electoral geography,
- a municipal-equivalent local government,
- or a deconcentrated field office of the state.

A **province** or **state** might be:

- a real regional self-government,
- a federal constituent unit with shared-rule dignity,
- or a map-shaped managerial shell.

The archive should therefore force later notes to say **which archetype the named body actually instantiates** before drawing constitutional conclusions from it.

### 6. Use two exception ladders rather than inventing fresh ad hoc scope theory

Most deviations from the seven-tier default already fit two exception families.

The first is the **collapsed-stack ladder** from `661`: city-states, microstates, archipelagos, and other polities too small or too fused to host the whole ladder as separate apparatuses. These should fuse tiers openly, preserve typed lanes inside the fused institutions, protect local voice, and pool specialist capacity where necessary.

The second is the **shared-regime ladder** from `662`, `663`, and `664`: problem-specific cross-border or global institutions with typed compacts, funding formulas, compliance ladders, and narrow operational mandates. These should not be mistaken for full territorial governments merely because they are real.

If a case fits one of those exception ladders, the archive should cite it instead of inventing a fresh theory of levels.

### 7. Separate scope archetypes from constitutional upgrade tests

A body can begin in one archetype and then outgrow its honesty envelope.

A metropolitan board may need democratic upgrade once it makes real territorial bargains. A regional consortium may need legislature-grade scrutiny once it carries serious debt, planning, or distributive power. A continental compact may need stronger parliamentary review once its domestic effects deepen.

The archive should therefore pair this scope spine with the upgrade and honesty tests in `628`, the rescoping rules in `630`, the reversibility rules in `631`, and the later scope-register and stack-change notes (`633` onward). Scope classification and scope change are related, but they are not the same question.

### 8. Make later notes cite archetype plus delta, not archetype plus full rewrite

Future notes should usually state only:

- the relevant archetype;
- any exception ladder that modifies it;
- the sector-specific delta;
- and any upgrade or reversibility rule that matters.

That means later notes should say things like:

- **this belongs to the municipal archetype, with metropolitan coordination where the housing market is already regional**;
- **this is a national guarantor duty delivered through municipal and regional operating tiers**;
- **this is a collapsed-stack case, so the municipal and national kits are fused but their fiscal and review lanes must still stay typed**.

The archive should regard this as editorial discipline, not stylistic austerity.

## Scope spine table

| Archetype | Core vocation | Minimum honest kit | Common labels it may hide behind | What it should not be mistaken for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Micro-local** | witness, stewardship, escalation, tiny commons | direct forum, light steward, micro-budget, open records, upward complaint route, direct participation | neighborhood, ward, village, block, parish, settlement | a miniature sovereign state |
| **Municipal** | ordinary place government and the first complete civic front door | elected forum, executive and civil service, operating budget, local review and appeal routes, public participation | town, city, commune, municipality, borough, county | a volunteer club or a deconcentrated branch office |
| **Metropolitan** | shared urban systems, city-region tradeoffs, integrated planning and infrastructure | public chamber or equally legible democratic chain, specialist executive, shared fiscal base, review and audit lanes, region-wide participation | metro, city-region, combined authority, county, capital region | a mayors’ club or grant cartel |
| **Regional** | territorial platforms, landscape systems, large service networks, shared rule inside the state | legislature or comparably real forum, executive departments, substantial finance, audit and review, territorial participation | province, state, land, governorate, region, county | a decorative map or duplicate ministry shell |
| **National** | equal citizenship, solidarity, macro-capacity, constitutional arbitration | parliament, rights-bound executive, national civil service, redistribution and equalization tools, constitutional and judicial review | state, republic, kingdom, federation | an everything-government that hoards lower-tier work |
| **Continental / plurilateral** | bounded cross-border compact for recurrent shared problems | treaty forum, secretariat or agencies, rule-bound contributions, compliance and dispute lanes, public scrutiny | union, community, common market, confederation, treaty organization | an ambient upper state without explicit mandate |
| **Global** | treaty narrow waist for peace, commons, alerting, standards, and planetary coordination | assemblies, secretariats, scientific panels, registries, courts, funds, transparency, and review | international organizations, treaty regimes, conventions, specialized agencies | a world municipality or a fiction that nothing global can be real |

## Exception crosswalk

| Exception family | When to use it | Archive anchor | Minimum discipline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Collapsed stack** | the polity is too small, fused, remote, or capacity-constrained to host separate municipal, regional, and national apparatuses honestly | `661` | fuse tiers openly, keep internal lanes typed, preserve local voice, and pool specialist capacity visibly |
| **Shared regime** | the institution is cross-border or global but problem-specific rather than a full territorial government | `662`, `663`, `664` | type the mandate, funding, compliance, review, and exit rules rather than letting compact language impersonate full government |

## Guardrails

- Do not infer constitutional vocation from the public label alone.
- Do not create a new tier merely because one existing tier is currently failing.
- Do not let counties, districts, or provinces drift through the archive as if their meaning were universal.
- Do not treat fused microstates and city-states as evidence that all lower tiers are optional everywhere.
- Do not treat cross-border or global compacts as if they were either world government or nothing at all.
- Do not let later notes re-expand the whole scope ladder when an archetype-plus-delta statement would do.

## Failure modes

- **label fundamentalism** — county, district, province, or state is treated as a theory in itself.
- **archive re-expansion** — each new note rewrites the full scope ladder before saying anything sector-specific.
- **exception laundering** — a case that clearly fits collapsed-stack or shared-regime doctrine gets dressed up as a novel constitutional species.
- **tier invention by frustration** — a new level is proposed mainly because the existing stack is politically blocked or badly run.
- **scope drift by naming** — a body acquires a grander title and is silently treated as a different archetype without proving it changed function.
- **map nostalgia** — provinces, districts, or counties are preserved as constitutional truths even after their real vocation has disappeared.

## Practical tests

A later archive note is using the scope spine honestly when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Has it named the relevant archetype rather than relying only on the body’s local label?
2. Has it stated the vocation that places the function at that tier rather than merely assuming scale from habit?
3. Could an outsider tell the minimum complete kit the body should have at that scope?
4. If the labels are ambiguous, has the note mapped them explicitly back to micro-local, municipal, metropolitan, regional, national, continental, or global archetypes?
5. If the case is unusual, has the note used the collapsed-stack or shared-regime exception ladder instead of improvising a new scope cosmology?
6. Has the note described only the sector-specific delta instead of re-describing the entire multilevel constitution from zero?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a later note can say **what a body is called** but cannot say **which scope archetype it instantiates, what vocation places the function there, whether an exception ladder applies, and what the sector-specific delta actually is**, then it still has **scope language without a reusable archive spine**.
