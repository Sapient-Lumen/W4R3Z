# 720 — Decision packets for lane-typed government fields: determination class, reason-giving, effectivity, appeal linkage, and no government by outcome letter alone

## One-line thesis

Once the archive has service and case packets for a lane-typed government field, it should also publish one narrow **decision packet** saying what counts as a consequential determination, which decision classes exist, who may make and sign them, what minimum findings, reasons, legal basis, conditions, effect dates, implementation duties, and review links must travel with them, and how correction, withdrawal, supersession, and reconsideration preserve lineage — so the field does not decay into government by outcome letter alone, silent conditions, or appeal clocks detached from the act they challenge.

## Why this matters

The recent packet spine can now say a great deal about a live governing field without forcing later notes to rebuild the full stack.

- `699` gives the scope spine.
- `700` gives the minimum design packet.
- `701` gives rivalry tests.
- `702` gives lane-splitting discipline.
- `703` gives interface packets.
- `704` gives the control plane.
- `705` gives mode packets.
- `706` gives repair packets.
- `707` gives sunset packets.
- `708` gives the evidence packet.
- `709` gives the challenge packet.
- `710` gives the participation packet.
- `711` gives the oversight packet.
- `712` gives the amendment packet.
- `713` gives the delegation packet.
- `714` gives the fiscal packet.
- `715` gives the capability packet.
- `716` gives the information packet.
- `717` gives the asset packet.
- `718` gives the service packet.
- `719` gives the case packet.

That sequence now explains the field’s scale, lanes, seams, money, records, workforce, assets, public entry routes, and live case movement.

But one practical constitutional object is still missing:

**what is the authoritative act that actually grants, denies, varies, conditions, pauses, revokes, or closes a rights-bearing case?**

The archive already has strong precursor doctrine here.

- `425` says consequential decisions should produce a portable review packet rather than leaving contestability to scattered logs.
- `446` says consequential status and notice cues must stay durably visible rather than vanishing with a transient message.
- `478` says consequential action should leave one structured decision trace naming basis, route, bindings, and reason codes.
- `575` says official notice and proof-of-delivery rules matter because legal effect cannot rest on presumed receipt folklore.
- `656` says pending matters need cutover rules when the operating home changes.
- `709` says fields need challenge packets for complaint, petition, and second-look routes.
- `711` says independent oversight must exist outside the live control plane.
- `718` says the service packet must publish the public promise and route classes.
- `719` says the case packet must publish the live state model, clocks, lineage, and notice duty.

This note does not replace those. It compresses them into one reusable field-level packet for the **decision as governing object**.

Without a decision packet, fields drift in predictable ways.

First, they drift into **outcome-letter government**: a person receives a grant, refusal, warning, suspension, closure, or condition list, but the field cannot tell whether the letter is the decision, a summary of the decision, a courtesy notice about a decision already made elsewhere, or merely an invitation to infer what happened.

Second, they drift into **reason exile**: the practical reasons live in officer notes, committee memory, contractor tooling, email chains, or internal dashboards, while the official surface says only approved, refused, or not eligible.

Third, they drift into **effect-date ambiguity**: the field cannot distinguish the day the decision was made, the day it was notified, the day it became legally operative, the day implementation begins, and the day review clocks expire.

Fourth, they drift into **shadow conditions and shadow implementation**: material duties, holds, referrals, payment changes, inspection triggers, or compliance steps follow from the determination, but those consequences are not visibly docked to the decision object itself.

Fifth, they drift into **appeal-clock mismatch**: challenge rights refer to a notice date, a decision date, an effective date, or a separate reconsideration step, yet the field has not published one stable rule tying those clocks to the decision the person is contesting.

A case packet tells the field what live matter is moving. A decision packet tells the field what authoritative act changes that matter’s legal or practical position.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Separate the decision packet from the service, case, challenge, and oversight packets

`718` already says what the field promises to the public. `719` already says what unit of work becomes a live case and which states it can occupy. `709` already says how challenge and reconsideration routes open. `711` already says how independent scrutiny sits outside the field.

The decision packet answers a narrower question:

**when a field takes a consequential step on a live matter, what type of determination is it, who is authorized to make it, what minimum content must accompany it, when does it take effect, and what review and implementation routes are visibly attached?**

A field can have a strong service packet and still fail because its final act is vague. It can have a strong case packet and still fail because a matter reaches decided with no stable determination object. It can have a strong challenge packet and still fail because people do not know which exact act they are challenging.

If a later note still says only that “the authority informs the applicant of the outcome,” it has not yet described a constitutional field. It has described a polite ending to an opaque process.

### 2. Publish a short list of determination classes before describing any one field’s merits logic

A serious decision packet should start by naming the field’s **determination classes**.

Typical classes differ by field, but most live systems need some subset of:

- grant or approve;
- refuse or deny;
- grant subject to conditions;
- vary an existing grant or entitlement;
- suspend temporarily;
- revoke or terminate;
- administratively return or reject before merits;
- refer to another lane or authority;
- close without merits because the case was withdrawn, merged, superseded, or extinguished on a named basis.

The point is not the exact vocabulary. It is that materially different consequences should not hide behind one generic word such as outcome or closure.

A field that cannot tell the difference between refusal, administrative return, withdrawal acknowledgement, suspension, and revocation has not yet made its decision constitution visible.

### 3. Bind every consequential decision to one case lineage anchor and one authority anchor

The packet should say that each consequential determination must point to:

- the case identifier or case lineage head from `719`;
- the deciding lane or authorized office;
- the decision maker or signatory class, even where a named individual need not appear publicly;
- the governing law, rule, policy head, or delegated instrument;
- and the evidence or decision-trace anchor from `478` and `708`.

This matters because many fields preserve the outcome but not the anchor set that makes the act contestable. The result is that oversight can find a case and a notice, but not the precise determination object that linked law, facts, reasons, and effect.

### 4. Publish one minimum reason-giving kernel

A normal decision packet should define the smallest content block that must accompany any consequential determination.

At minimum that kernel usually needs:

- what the decision is;
- what facts or findings materially support it;
- what law, policy, or rule basis supports it;
- the reasons for moving from those findings to that result;
- any conditions, limitations, or obligations created;
- any material amount, duration, status, or route consequence created;
- and what happens next if no review request or compliance step occurs.

The packet should be written so the field cannot satisfy the reason-giving duty merely by saying not eligible, failed criteria, or inconsistent with policy. Those are headings, not reasons.

### 5. Keep decision date, notice date, operative date, and implementation date separate

A serious decision packet should force the field to distinguish several dates that many institutions casually merge.

At minimum it should say:

- when the determination was made;
- when and how it was notified;
- when it becomes legally or operationally effective;
- what review or appeal clock starts from which date;
- and what implementation clock or payment, enforcement, or service change begins from which date.

This matters because one of the most common forms of administrative confusion is letting people infer legal effect from whichever date happens to be printed most prominently on the letter.

A field that cannot say whether a right ended on decision, on notification, on a later effective date, or only after review time expired is already governing by date folklore.

### 6. Dock decision content directly to implementation duties and holds

A decision is not only a statement. It is a routing act for the rest of the field.

The packet should therefore say, for each material determination class:

- what implementation obligations it triggers;
- which changes must wait for the end of a review window and which do not;
- what temporary hold or stay rules exist;
- which downstream lanes must receive the determination;
- and what public-facing status surface must update when implementation begins, pauses, completes, or is stayed.

Otherwise the field splits into two dishonest halves: a formal decision object that seems contestable and a practical implementation machine that moves elsewhere on undocumented assumptions.

### 7. Keep challenge and second-look linkage explicit without collapsing it into the decision itself

A decision packet should dock directly to `709` and, where relevant, to tribunal or court routes.

That means saying, at minimum:

- whether there is an internal reconsideration or second-look stage;
- whether that stage is mandatory, optional, or unavailable;
- what clock and trigger open the next review route;
- whether review pauses implementation, partly pauses it, or leaves it live;
- and what record travels automatically into review.

This matters because many systems force people to guess whether the next step is complaint, reconsideration, appeal, judicial review, or reapplication. A decision packet should make the immediate redress map visible at the moment the consequential act is issued.

### 8. Treat correction, withdrawal, supersession, and replacement as typed decision events

The field should not silently edit a consequential determination after issue.

A normal packet should therefore distinguish:

- clerical correction;
- explanatory addendum that does not change legal effect;
- reconsidered decision replacing the first;
- variation decision changing only part of the original;
- withdrawal of a defective determination;
- supersession by a later event or law;
- and nullity or voiding where the first act should not have stood at all.

Each of those needs a lineage link back to the earlier determination, plus a visible statement of what remains live and what no longer binds.

### 9. Keep the packet small enough that later field notes can cite it instead of rebuilding administrative-decision doctrine from scratch

A normal decision packet should fit in one compact block.

If a later note needs pages of prose to explain what counts as a decision, what minimum reasons must travel with it, which date matters, how implementation follows, and what review route opens next, then the field still lacks a reusable decision constitution.

## Compact decision packet

A normal decision packet should usually fit in one table-like block:

| Field | Determination classes and deciding lane | Minimum reason-giving kernel | Effectivity, implementation, and review linkage | Drift trigger and consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Housing** | housing lane issues grant, refusal, conditional placement, priority variation, temporary suspension, termination, and administrative return determinations | decision states the facts found about eligibility or priority, the governing housing rule, the reasons for the result, any conditions or temporary accommodation duty, and what happens next | packet distinguishes decision date, notice date, move-in or payment effective date, and any hold pending review; reconsideration and tribunal routes are named with the exact clock anchor | repeated use of free-text letters without clear reasons, dates, or review route opens repair and oversight escalation |
| **Planning** | planning lane issues permission, permission with conditions, refusal, discontinuance, enforcement-linked, and administrative invalidity determinations | decision states the material planning findings, legal and plan basis, reasons, conditions, compliance obligations, and route for challenge | packet distinguishes notice date from the effective date of a discontinuance or similar measure and names when appeal clocks run from notice versus non-decision failure-to-decide routes | routine ambiguity about whether conditions, refusals, or no-decision cases are appealable opens packet redesign and official-form revision |
| **Income support** | benefit lane issues award, refusal, revision, sanction, suspension, overpayment, and reconsidered determinations | decision states the facts relied on, the benefit rule applied, the reasons, payment or sanction consequences, and what evidence or route may change the result | packet distinguishes decision date, mailing or digital notice date, payment-change date, mandatory-reconsideration clock, and later tribunal route | if people must call or complain just to discover the reason or next step, the field opens challenge-led repair and notice redesign |

The exact field content will vary, but the packet form should stay recognizable.

## Failure modes

- **government by outcome letter alone**: the public receives a result but cannot tell whether the letter is the decision, a summary, or an after-the-fact explanation.
- **reason exile**: the practical reasons exist only in internal notes, email, or staff memory.
- **effect-date folklore**: nobody can say which date governs legal effect, implementation, or review rights.
- **silent condition drift**: material obligations or limits attach to the decision but are not visibly docked to the determination object.
- **appeal-clock mismatch**: challenge deadlines run from one date while the public reasonably reads another.
- **shadow implementation**: the downstream enforcement, payment, or service change happens without a visible link back to the decision that triggered it.
- **stealth replacement**: a defective or changed determination is quietly overwritten instead of corrected, varied, withdrawn, or superseded with lineage.
- **review-path roulette**: similar decisions imply different complaint, reconsideration, or appeal paths depending on office habit rather than a published rule.
- **operator-side determinations**: contractors or local host bodies become the practical source of grant or refusal reality even though the public authority supposedly owns the act.

## Anti-escape-hatch tests

1. Can the field name a short stable list of consequential determination classes rather than one vague outcome bucket?
2. Does every consequential decision point to one case identifier, one authority anchor, and one evidence or trace anchor?
3. Does every consequential decision carry facts, reasons, legal basis, conditions, and next-step consequences in one minimum kernel?
4. Can the field distinguish decision date, notice date, operative date, implementation date, and review-clock anchor without improvisation?
5. Is the immediate review route visible on the same determination rather than discoverable only after complaint or legal advice?
6. Are correction, variation, supersession, and withdrawal typed events with lineage rather than silent edits?
7. Can oversight reconstruct not only what result was issued, but what exact authoritative act produced the result and what still binds now?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a field can show that a case reached **decided** but cannot also show **what type of determination was made, on what findings and legal basis, with what reasons, from which date, under which review path, and with what implementation consequences**, then it is still letting **administrative outcome impersonate constitutional decision**.
