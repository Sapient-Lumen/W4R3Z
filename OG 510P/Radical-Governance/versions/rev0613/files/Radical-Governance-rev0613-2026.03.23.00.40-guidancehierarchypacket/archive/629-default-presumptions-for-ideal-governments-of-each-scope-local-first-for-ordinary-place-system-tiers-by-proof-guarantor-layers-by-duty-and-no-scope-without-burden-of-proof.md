# 629 — Default presumptions for ideal governments of each scope: local first for ordinary place, system tiers by proof, guarantor layers by duty, and no scope without burden of proof

## One-line thesis

An ideal multilevel polity should not treat scope assignment as a blank slate every time; it should carry default constitutional presumptions: micro-local institutions presumptively hold stewardship, signal, and civic-entry functions, municipalities ordinary-place government, metropolitan and regional tiers only the functions that truly live in shared systems or territorial platforms, nations the guarantor duties of equal citizenship and solidarity, and continental or global bodies only explicitly named cross-border necessities under bounded compact or treaty forms.

## Why this matters

The archive now says **what the ideal government of each scope looks like** (`602`), **how to design the stack in order** (`603`), and **how to test whether a claimed tier is honest** (`628`). What it still lacked was the most compressed statement of **where the burden of proof starts** before powers travel up or down the ladder.

That missing default matters because constitutional arguments often cheat by pretending every function is undecided until someone with enough prestige, urgency language, or institutional convenience claims it. That produces three recurrent distortions. First, **upward drift by anxiety**, where anything politically salient is pushed to a higher level because bigger looks safer. Second, **downward dumping by romance**, where states offload expensive or conflict-ridden duties onto smaller governments while still controlling the terms. Third, **blank-slate constitutionalism**, where reformers behave as though scope choice begins from zero each time, instead of from accumulated lessons about ordinary place, spillovers, solidarity, and genuinely planetary risks.

An archive that keeps growing needs a tighter prior than that. The right question is not only which level can possibly do a task, but **which level should be presumed to hold it unless a visible contrary case is made**. Good multilevel design becomes smaller, sharper, and more repeatable once those presumptions are named.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Give each scope a default constitutional presumption, not a permanent blank cheque

Every level should have a starting presumption that reflects its honest vocation. That presumption is not absolute ownership and not a ban on exceptions. It is a **burden-of-proof rule**. Ordinary functions start where ordinary life is actually governed. Shared-system functions start where the system is real. Guarantee functions start where equal citizenship and solidarity can be secured. Cross-border functions start only where the border itself is part of the problem.

This lets the archive speak in defaults rather than reheating first principles every time a new domain appears.

### 2. Micro-local presumption: stewardship, detection, convening, and escalation

The smallest scale should be presumed competent for what depends on proximity more than coercive depth: noticing breakdown, stewarding tiny commons, convening residents, surfacing conflict, routing requests, checking whether official promises reached the street, and preserving early warning around neglect or exclusion.

It should not be presumed competent for broad policing, heavy borrowing, serious inspection regimes, complex procurement chains, or redistributive solidarity it cannot honestly finance. A micro-local unit may exceptionally host more, but the burden should be on anyone claiming it ought to be a little sovereign.

### 3. Municipal presumption: ordinary place government and the first full civic front door

The municipality should be the default home for the ordinary government of place: local streets and public space, sanitation, routine permitting, libraries, parks, everyday code enforcement, local land-use administration, and the first general civic interface through which residents can actually enter the state.

That presumption should hold unless a clear case shows that the relevant function is too networked, too large-scale, too risk-pooling dependent, or too rights-sensitive to remain there alone. Municipal default is not municipal monopoly. It is the starting point for ordinary place.

### 4. Metropolitan presumption: only where the functional city-region is real and binding

The metropolitan tier should not be presumed merely because a city is large, fashionable, or economically important. It should be presumed when the **functional urban region** is already the real geography of transport, housing pressure, trunk infrastructure, freight, air quality, flood risk, or other shared urban systems, and when municipal self-help has become structurally insufficient.

The burden of proof for metropolitanization is therefore not population size alone but evidence of recurrent spillover, distributive bargaining, shared asset management, or system decisions that one municipality cannot honestly make for all.

### 5. Regional or provincial presumption: territorial platforms and shared rule only when they are substantively real

Regional government should not be presumed just because a map already has provinces, counties, states, or departments. It should be presumed where large territorial platforms are real: hospital systems, watersheds, grids, logistics corridors, major higher-education systems, ecological landscapes, or constitutionally meaningful shared rule in plurinational or federal settings.

The archive should therefore begin from a demanding regional presumption: regions earn default status when they govern a territorially coherent platform or a genuine shared-rule settlement, not when they merely duplicate ministries or preserve inherited map nostalgia.

### 6. National presumption: guarantees, solidarity, macro-capacity, and constitutional arbitration

The national level should be the default home for the duties that require common status across the polity: equal citizenship, rights floors, major redistribution, macroeconomic stabilisation, national elections architecture, top-level audit and integrity frameworks, and constitutional settlement among lower tiers.

This is a different kind of presumption from the local one. It is not a presumption for ordinary place management. It is a presumption for **guarantor functions**. The nation should carry the burden of making common life and common protection real, but it should not translate that burden into routine possession of every function below it.

### 7. Continental or plurilateral presumption: named interfaces, bounded rules, and shared infrastructures

A continental or plurilateral body should not be the default destination for unresolved political disagreement. Its presumption begins only when the relevant problem is truly cross-border and recurrent: movement regimes, trade or market interfaces, basin or corridor governance, standards for interoperability, research platforms, or other functions whose efficacy depends on several states acting through a common rule frame.

Its starting form should be compact and competence-bounded. The burden remains on anyone claiming a continental body should become a general upper state rather than a named solver of named cross-border problems.

### 8. Global presumption: irreducibly planetary coordination only

The global layer should be presumed only for problems that plainly outrun every lower frame even when lower tiers co-operate seriously: war restraint, planetary commons, global alerting, selected technical or financial standards, shipping and aviation rules, and comparable treaty-bound risks.

This is the narrowest presumption in the stack. Global government is honest when it is thin, specific, and durable. The burden of proof should be very high for any claim that a global institution should act as a general domestic government for humanity.

### 9. Deviations from the default should travel with a visible proof packet

Whenever a function is placed above or below its default presumptive home, the reform should ship a short packet stating:

- the problem class,
- the real geography,
- the specific reason the default level is insufficient or excessive,
- the finance and capability consequences,
- the rights or solidarity implications,
- the review and redress route,
- and the conditions for later handback, further upgrade, or reversal.

Without that packet, exceptions become habit and habit becomes constitutional folklore.

### 10. The burden of proof should rise as governments move farther from lived experience unless the function is plainly a guarantor or cross-border duty

There is no neutral upward move. For ordinary place functions, the burden of proof gets heavier as authority travels farther from the people, sites, and failures it governs. For guarantor functions, the reverse is often true: the burden lies on anyone trying to push them downward in a way that fractures equal citizenship, solidarity, or legal coherence. The archive should therefore treat burden-of-proof direction as part of the design, not as rhetoric added later.

## Default presumption table

| Function class | Presumptive scope | What must be shown to move it elsewhere? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| stewardship, detection, convening, tiny commons | **micro-local** | that proximity is not doing the decisive work, or that coercive / fiscal demands exceed the cell’s honest capacity |
| ordinary place management and first civic access | **municipal** | that spillovers, network topology, solidarity requirements, or rights risks make solo municipal control dishonest |
| shared urban systems | **metropolitan** | that the functional city-region is real enough to justify a public kit, or else that co-operation can still honestly stay narrow |
| territorial platforms and shared rule | **regional / provincial / state** | that the platform is real and coherent, not just an inherited map or a ministry convenience |
| equal citizenship, solidarity, macro-stability, constitutional arbitration | **national** | that the function can be decentralised without fracturing common status, risk-pooling, or legal coherence |
| cross-border interfaces and shared transnational infrastructures | **continental / plurilateral** | that the problem is genuinely recurrent across borders and needs a bounded common rule frame |
| irreducibly planetary risks and commons | **global** | that lower frames cannot solve the problem even through serious compacting and co-ordination |

## Guardrails

- Do not treat every contested function as up-for-grabs from zero.
- Do not use subsidiarity language to dump expensive guarantees downward.
- Do not use solidarity language to hoard ordinary place functions upward.
- Do not constitutionalize a metropolitan or regional tier without proving the shared system or territorial platform is actually real.
- Do not let continental or global layers acquire ambient domestic reach just because lower tiers are messy.
- Do not confuse exceptions with a new default until the archive says so explicitly.

## Failure modes

- **scope by vibes**: a function is assigned to the level that feels most prestigious, urgent, or modern rather than the level with the right default and proof.
- **upward drift by anxiety**: political salience alone becomes enough to centralise or internationalise a function.
- **downward dumping by romance**: upper tiers praise local empowerment while exporting unfunded or rights-sensitive burdens downward.
- **blank-slate constitutionalism**: every scope dispute is treated as if no prior principles already governed the starting presumption.
- **map nostalgia regionalism**: inherited territorial units are treated as presumptively necessary even when they lack a real platform vocation.
- **upper-layer opportunism**: continental or global institutions expand because lower tiers are difficult, not because the problem is truly cross-border or planetary.

## Compression rule for the archive

If a note can name **which scope should hold a function** but cannot also name **the default presumption, the burden of proof for moving away from it, and the facts that would justify exception, upgrade, or handback**, then it is still missing **the constitutional starting prior for ideal governments by scope**.
