# Supranational Governance (Unions / Confederations / Cross-Border Regimes)

**Typical scale:** multi-state regions and blocs  
**Primary advantage:** manages cross-border markets and externalities without full statehood  
**Primary risk:** democratic deficit; uneven compliance; competence creep

## What this level must own (and nothing else)
- rights baselines and guardrails for cross-border coercion (extradition, policing cooperation)
- limited, enumerated competences where cross-border coordination is essential
- mutual recognition + minimum standards for rights, safety, and integrity
- dispute resolution for treaty/compact compliance
- shared infrastructure and market rules where scale economies dominate

## Minimum Viable Government (MVG)
- treaty of **conferral** with explicit subsidiarity/proportionality rules  
  (mature example: TEU Article 5) — https://eur-lex.europa.eu/LexUriServ/LexUriServ.do?uri=CELEX:12008M005:EN:HTML
- dual-legitimacy decision system (states + people) (`DEC-1` + a people’s channel)
- independent court / arbitral body for treaty enforcement (`LAW-2`)
- transparency baseline for lawmaking and procurement (`OPEN-1/2`)
- regulatory alignment baseline: common rulemaking transparency + procedural fairness to enable mutual recognition and prevent regulatory arbitrage (`CAP-7`; see `13-regulation-utilities-and-soes.md`)
- mutual recognition for identity/credentials/public documents with rights and privacy baselines (`IOP-6`, `IOP-2`; see `12-identity-and-recognition.md`)
- ecological standards + leakage controls (harmonized measurement; mutual recognition only with audits) (`OPEN-6`; see `11-commons-and-ecological-governance.md`)
- small shared budget with consolidated public accounts + audit + fiscal risk disclosure (`CAP-2/3`; `ACC-1`; see `07-fiscal-and-budgetary-governance.md`)
- independent costing/forecast capacity for shared fiscal commitments (`CAP-4`)
- grievance/complaint channel for cross-border harms (incl. mutual recognition failures) (`LAW-5`; see `08-remedy-and-grievance.md`)
- interoperable safeguards for automated decisions when mutual recognition applies (`IOP-5`; see `06-digital-and-algorithmic-governance.md`)
- compact framework for shared programs with metrics and exit clauses (`IOP-1`)
## Ideal institutional stack (bounded)
### A) Dual legitimacy, not faux-federalism
- **Council of states** (governments) for high-sovereignty questions.
- **Parliament/assembly of people** for market rules, budgets, and oversight.
- Clear “who decides what” ledger (competence ledger) (see `70-interoperability.md`).

### B) Compliance system with graduated responses
- reporting + peer review,
- infringement proceedings,
- conditional funding,
- final judicial/arbitral resolution.

### C) Small fiscal capacity with hard rules
- limited shared budget for: cross-border infrastructure, research, crisis response.
- strict audit, open contracting, and public accounts.

## Top failure modes + countermeasures
- **Competence creep:** hard conferral + proportionality publication + sunset clauses (`70-interoperability.md`).
- **Democratic deficit:** strengthen people’s channel (deliberation + oversight) (`DEC-2`, `OPEN-1`).
- **Uneven rule-of-law compliance:** rule-of-law checklists + conditionality (`LAW-4`, `CAP-3`).

## Success metrics (minimal set)
- cross-border dispute resolution time
- compliance rates with reporting/verification
- market fairness indicators and corruption risk measures
- legitimacy indicators (trust, transparency, participation)
