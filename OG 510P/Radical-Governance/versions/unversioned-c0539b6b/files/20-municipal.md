# Municipal Government (City / Town)

**Typical scale:** ~10,000–10,000,000 residents  
**Primary advantage:** service delivery + land-use authority + public infrastructure  
**Primary risk:** patronage, zoning capture, uneven service distribution

## What this level must own (and nothing else)
- local infrastructure: streets, water, sanitation, public spaces
- land-use, housing, permitting
- local public health operations
- primary safety services (with strict coercion controls)
- local economic regulation/enforcement where devolved

## Minimum Viable Government (MVG)
- elected council and transparent elections (`DEC-1`, `DEC-5`)
- professional administration with merit hiring + capability baseline (`CAP-1`, `CAP-6`; see `09-public-service-and-state-capacity.md`)
- readable budget + quarterly execution reporting + debt/risk disclosure (`CAP-2/3`; see `07-fiscal-and-budgetary-governance.md`)
- independent audit + accessible grievance/appeal stack (`ACC-1`, `LAW-5`; see `08-remedy-and-grievance.md`)
- open contracting and vendor transparency (`OPEN-2`)
- transparent utility/concession tariffs + basic rulemaking openness for local regulation (`CAP-7`; see `13-regulation-utilities-and-soes.md`)
- identity access points and documentation support for residents (enrollment assistance, fee waivers, correction pathways) (`IOP-6`; see `12-identity-and-recognition.md`)
- ecological governance baseline: permit/discharge registries + open environmental data + watershed/airshed coordination (`OPEN-6`; see `11-commons-and-ecological-governance.md`)
- accessible administrative tribunal for permits/services (`LAW-3`)
- public register + safeguards for automated decisions used in permitting/benefits (`IOP-5`; see `06-digital-and-algorithmic-governance.md`)
- coercion controls + oversight (`SAFE-1/2/4/5`; see `05-public-safety-and-coercion.md`)
## Ideal institutional stack (template)
### A) Council + professional executive (council-manager)
- Council sets policy and budgets; manager runs operations with performance review.
- Reduces personality-cult risk while preserving democratic control (`CAP-1`).

### B) Representation that discourages zero-sum politics
- Prefer multi-member districts with proportional allocation where feasible (`DEC-5`).
- Add a standing Citizens’ Assembly for major plans (zoning, policing, climate) with duty-to-respond (`DEC-2`).
- Require “future impact statements” for 10–30 year plans (housing, infrastructure, climate) and empower review triggers (`DEC-6`).

### C) Service charters + measurable guarantees
- Publish service baselines (permit timelines, maintenance cycles, response targets).
- Use ombuds + tribunal pathways for fast remedies (`ACC-3`, `LAW-3`).

### D) Transparent budgets + procurement
- Proactive publication of contracts, budgets, and outcomes (`OPEN-1`).
- Use OCDS-style contracting disclosure (`OPEN-2`): https://standard.open-contracting.org/

## Safety & coercion controls (municipal-specific)
- Separate response functions:
  - unarmed crisis teams for behavioral health and social crises,
  - civil enforcement where possible without weapons,
  - armed response narrowly scoped to imminent violence.
- Oversight board publishes force, stops, complaints, and discipline outcomes (`SAFE-1/2`).

## Top failure modes + countermeasures
- **Zoning capture:** conflict disclosures + transparent variances + audit trail (`ACC-4`, `OPEN-1`).
- **Procurement fraud:** open contracting + independent audit closure (`OPEN-2`, `ACC-1/2`).
- **Legitimacy collapse:** institutionalize deliberation + PB in bounded budgets (`DEC-2/3`).

## Interfaces downward and upward
- Downward: fund and legally recognize neighborhood councils (`10-micro-local.md`).
- Upward: join regional compacts for transit, watersheds, housing targets, and disaster response (`IOP-1`).

## Success metrics (minimal set)
- service reliability (water, sanitation, transit)
- housing affordability + permit cycle time
- procurement competitiveness (single-bid rate, vendor concentration)
- safety outcomes + coercion incidents per capita
