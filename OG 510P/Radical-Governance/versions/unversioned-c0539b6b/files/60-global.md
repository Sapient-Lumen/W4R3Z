# Global Governance (Planetary Scope)

**Primary advantage:** only level that can fully address planetary externalities  
**Primary risk:** coercion without legitimacy; weak enforcement; geopolitics

This memo treats “global government” as a **layered system**: treaties + institutions + measurement + finance + compliance. The goal is not a unitary world state, but *effective governance of global public goods and existential risks*.

## What the global level must own (and nothing else)
- prevention/management of catastrophic war and mass violence
- pandemic preparedness and cross-border outbreak response
- climate stabilization + biodiversity and ocean commons
- systemic financial stability (crisis coordination)
- baseline human rights norms and accountability for mass atrocities
- global technical standards where interoperability is essential

## Minimum Viable Governance (MVG)
- a charter layer of rights + procedural commitments (legitimacy constraints)
- topic compacts with measurable obligations + reporting (treaty bodies)
- independent measurement/verification capacity (`OPEN-4` patterns)
- baseline norms for regulatory transparency + procedural fairness in cross-border market coordination (standards, network industries, competition cooperation) (`CAP-7`; see `13-regulation-utilities-and-soes.md`)
- baseline standards for legal identity and cross-border document recognition (to reduce exclusion/statelessness and enable safe mobility) (`IOP-6`; see `12-identity-and-recognition.md`)
- ring-fenced finance for public goods with consolidated public accounts, audit, and fiscal risk disclosure (`CAP-2/3`, `ACC-1`; see `07-fiscal-and-budgetary-governance.md`)
- independent costing/forecast capacity for major multi-year commitments (`CAP-4`)
- dispute resolution and compliance pathways (graduated) + due process remedies for listings and sanctions (`LAW-5` / `LAW-2` equivalents; see `08-remedy-and-grievance.md`)
- a “people’s channel” for legitimacy (advisory but structured) (`DEC-2`)
- baseline norms for high-stakes automated decisions used in global regimes (sanctions lists, migration/refugee coordination, aid targeting) (`IOP-5`; see `06-digital-and-algorithmic-governance.md`)
## Ideal institutional architecture (modular)
### 1) Global Charter Layer (legitimacy + constraints)
- Compact rights and procedural commitments emphasizing participation, non-discrimination, due process, transparency, and remedy.
- Human rights anchor: UDHR — https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights
- Recent reform agenda anchor: UN Pact for the Future (with the Global Digital Compact and Declaration on Future Generations annexes) — https://www.un.org/en/summit-of-the-future/pact-for-the-future

### 2) Global Public Goods Compacts (topic-specific operating agreements)
Each compact includes:
- clear obligations + targets + reporting cadence
- inspection/verification (where relevant)
- graduated sanctions and incentives
- dispute resolution and appeal pathways
- funding mechanism tied to benefits/harms

Example anchors:
- **Pandemic governance:** International Health Regulations (IHR) + 2024 amendments (in force 19 Sep 2025; introduces “pandemic emergency”) — https://www.who.int/news/item/19-09-2025-amended-international-health-regulations-enter-into-force  
- **Climate governance:** Paris Agreement (global goals + transparency framework) — https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/english_paris_agreement.pdf
- **Biodiversity governance:** Kunming–Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (CBD COP15 Decision 15/4) — https://www.cbd.int/doc/decisions/cop-15/cop-15-dec-04-en.pdf
  Complement: WHO Pandemic Agreement (WHA78.1, 20 May 2025) — https://apps.who.int/gb/ebwha/pdf_files/WHA78/A78_R1-en.pdf
- **Climate:** Paris Agreement (transparency and NDC cycles) — https://unfccc.int/process-and-meetings/the-paris-agreement/the-paris-agreement
- **Digital cooperation / AI governance:** Global Digital Compact (2024) — https://www.un.org/digital-emerging-technologies/global-digital-compact

### 3) Global Measurement & Early Warning (trust infrastructure)
- Multi-stakeholder, auditable measurement bodies; publish comparable indicators.
- Use small anchor sets (see `03-metrics-and-evidence.md`) rather than indicator explosions.

### 4) Global Finance (capability infrastructure)
Ring-fenced funds for:
- pandemic preparedness,
- adaptation and loss/damage,
- peacebuilding and refugees,
- rapid disaster response.
Funding principles: ability-to-pay; polluter-pays where measurable; strong audit + anti-fraud.

### 5) Peace & Security (hardest constraint)
- Maintain collective security mechanisms with tight legal constraints and transparency.
- Today’s enforcement anchor is UN Charter Chapter VII authority (threats to peace; measures) — https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/chapter-7
- Incremental improvements (where politically attainable):
  - clearer thresholds for action,
  - stronger due process for sanctions listings,
  - transparent deliberation norms,
  - standing investigatory capacity,
  - veto restraint norms in mass-atrocity contexts.

### 6) Representation: add a People’s Channel without pretending it is a world parliament
Create a **Global Citizens’ Assembly**:
- sortition-based, regionally stratified, recurring (`DEC-2`)
- advisory opinions on compacts, crises, and legitimacy controversies
- duty-to-respond triggers by treaty bodies and state parties
- publishes minority reports and evidence packs

## 7) Future generations (long-horizon legitimacy)
- Create a small Future Generations function that can trigger review of compacts and emergency measures, publish future-impact statements, and demand duty-to-respond (`DEC-6`).
- Norm anchor: Declaration on Future Generations (2024) — https://www.un.org/en/summit-of-the-future/declaration-on-future-generations

## Top failure modes + countermeasures
- **Weak compliance:** verification + conditional finance + graduated sanctions (compact design).
- **Legitimacy gaps:** structured people’s channel + transparency + long-horizon review (`DEC-2`, `DEC-6`, `OPEN-1`).
- **Great-power veto paralysis:** narrower mandates + coalition compacts where universalism fails (polycentricity).

## Success metrics (minimal set)
- outbreak detection-to-action time; IHR core capacity improvements
- emissions trajectory relative to targets; verification coverage
- compliance rates with reporting/inspection regimes
- reduction in major interstate conflict and mass atrocities (imperfect but trackable)
- legitimacy indicators: transparency, trust, participation in global deliberative processes
