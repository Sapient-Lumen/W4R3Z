# Principles

These principles are intentionally *cross-scope*: they apply to neighborhood councils and global treaties alike.

## 1) Subsidiarity with teeth
- **Default:** decisions are made at the *lowest* level that can competently handle the problem.
- **Escalate** only for: cross-border spillovers, scale economies, rights protection, or coordination failures.
- Higher levels MUST publish a **scale/effects** justification and keep interventions proportionate (mature formulation: TEU Article 5 conferral/subsidiarity/proportionality).  
  Reference: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/LexUriServ/LexUriServ.do?uri=CELEX:12008M005:EN:HTML

## 2) Legitimacy is multi-channel
Legitimacy comes from **(a) voice**, **(b) rights**, and **(c) performance**:
- **Voice:** elections and structured participation (see `DEC-*` and `OPEN-*`).
- **Rights:** non-negotiable constraints on power; due process; equal political standing (see `LAW-*`).
- **Performance:** institutions deliver safety, basic services, and fair dispute resolution (see `CAP-*`, `SAFE-*`).
  Public service capability anchor: OECD/LEGAL/0445 — https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/public/doc/641/641.en.pdf

Rights baselines:
- UDHR (participation and equal dignity) — https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights  
- ICCPR Article 25 (political participation rights) — https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/international-covenant-civil-and-political-rights
- Legal identity for all (SDG 16.9 framing / UN Legal Identity Agenda): https://unstats.un.org/legal-identity-agenda/

## 3) Rule of law, not rule by paperwork
- **Predictability:** rules are public, stable, and knowable.
- **Constraint:** no actor is above the law; power is checkable.
- **Remedy:** accessible, impartial dispute resolution with enforceable outcomes.
- Operational definition anchor: World Justice Project rule of law factors — https://worldjusticeproject.org/about-us/overview/what-rule-law
- Checklist method anchor: Venice Commission Updated Rule of Law Checklist (2025) — https://www.venice.coe.int/webforms/documents/default.aspx?pdffile=CDL-AD%282025%29002-e
- Remedy baseline anchor: UN Basic Principles on Remedy and Reparation (A/RES/60/147) — https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/basic-principles-and-guidelines-right-remedy-and-reparation


## 4) Open government by default
- Budgets, procurement, and outcomes MUST be transparent (with privacy safeguards).
- Public input MUST be structurally supported (not purely symbolic).
- Anchor: OECD Recommendation on Open Government (2017) — https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0438
- Procurement transparency anchor: Open Contracting Data Standard — https://standard.open-contracting.org/

## 5) Polycentricity beats monocultures
- Multiple centers of decision-making reduce single-point failure and enable learning.
- Overlap in **checks** (audits, courts) is healthy; overlap in **operational control** is usually costly.

## 6) Commons logic for shared resources
For shared resources and shared infrastructures (land-use, water, data, networks), institutions SHOULD aim for:
When harms are slow and boundary-crossing, treat **ecological limits** as explicit budget objects (ceilings/floors + registries) — see `11-commons-and-ecological-governance.md`.
- clear boundaries and membership,
- locally fitted rules,
- participatory rule-making,
- monitoring,
- graduated sanctions,
- accessible conflict resolution,
- recognition of self-organization,
- nested governance for large systems.
Reference: Ostrom design principles (overview and extensions) — https://thecommonsjournal.org/articles/10.18352/ijc.305

## 7) Finance must match authority
- Every level MUST have:
  - predictable revenue,
  - a duty to publish a comprehensible budget,
  - independent audit,
  - hard limits on off-book liabilities.
- Costs SHOULD be internalized where harms/benefits occur, with transfers for equity and shared burdens.
- Fiscal transparency anchor: IMF Fiscal Transparency Code (2019) — https://www.imf.org/external/np/fad/trans/Code2019.pdf

## 8) Safety, coercion, and the “minimum violence constraint”
- Coercive powers MUST be:
  - narrowly defined,
  - publicly overseen,
  - reviewable by independent bodies,
  - subject to due process and proportionality.
- Emergency powers MUST be sunset-bounded and reviewable (`SAFE-3`).

## 9) Continuous improvement as a constitutional feature
- Institutions MUST have:
  - measurement of outcomes (`03-metrics-and-evidence.md`),
  - safe-to-fail experimentation pathways,
  - sunset clauses for high-risk policies,
  - structured review cycles.
- Include explicit long-horizon review (e.g., Future Council triggers) for high-impact decisions (`DEC-6`).
  Anchor: UN Declaration on Future Generations (2024) — https://www.un.org/en/summit-of-the-future/declaration-on-future-generations

## 10) Digital infrastructure is governance
- Where digital systems mediate rights or services, they MUST be auditable, contestable, and privacy-preserving.
- Anchor framing: UN digital public goods — https://www.un.org/digital-emerging-technologies/content/digital-public-goods
- Information integrity MUST be treated as public infrastructure: independent statistics/evaluation, provenance of official claims, and transparent corrections (`OPEN-4/5`).
  Anchor: UN Fundamental Principles of Official Statistics — https://unstats.un.org/unsd/dnss/gp/fundprinciples.aspx
