# 850 — Applied cross-boundary case packet for intermunicipal shared-service consortium: thin backbone, municipal ownership, service-level compact, fiscal queue, and no county government by admin hub

## One-line thesis

An intermunicipal shared-service consortium should usually stay a **thin service backbone owned by its member municipalities**: it may pool staff, platforms, procurement, records, and service standards where small jurisdictions lack capacity, but it should not silently become a county, regional government, or general-purpose authority merely because the administrative hub works.

## Why this matters

`849` tested the `848` dispatcher on a strong metropolitan transport-authority case and reached a thickened functional-authority verdict. The archive still needed the opposite stress test: a cross-boundary problem where the answer is not another authority, metropolitan shell, or constitutional upgrade.

Many small or rural municipalities face real capacity gaps. They may need shared payroll, permitting, inspection, emergency dispatch, procurement, digital-service, waste, water, social-service, archive, or professional-support capacity. The boundary mismatch is real: the minimum viable service unit may be larger than one municipality. But the public problem is often not a new territorial people, a single metropolitan system, or a regional constitution. It is an operating backbone that lets municipalities keep ordinary democratic ownership while sharing scarce capability.

This packet therefore tests whether `848` can downshift. The honest form is **intermunicipal compact plus service backbone**, with clear member ownership, budget shares, service levels, exit / accession rules, audit, complaint routing, and no independent general-purpose mandate.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Case object and verdict

| Field | Holding |
| --- | --- |
| case object | an intermunicipal shared-service consortium or joint administrative backbone for small / capacity-constrained municipalities |
| claimed scope | cross-boundary service capacity, not a new general-purpose regional government |
| archive rule applied | `848` dispatcher; `812` form ladder; live chain notes `814`, `815`, `816`, and `817`, with `813` only if the consortium starts making policy choices that should belong to elected bodies |
| verdict | **keep thin, contract hard, publish clearly** |
| short reason | the shared problem is capacity and continuity across municipal borders; the thinnest honest form is a legally grounded compact / joint service body with municipal ownership, service-level duties, fiscal transparency, audit, complaint routes, and exit rules |
| narrow thickening needed | service catalogue, cost-share formula, delegation map, records home, complaint route, audit lane, change-control rule, accession / exit rule, and annual member-public reporting |
| forbidden thickening | do not let the hub become a county government, regional policy maker, shadow tax authority, or permanent route around municipal councils |

This packet deliberately rejects the prestige move: because several municipalities share an office, platform, contractor, or professional staff pool, it does not follow that the archive should create a new democratic layer. Shared administration is not yet shared territorial sovereignty.

### 2. Trigger proof under `848`

| Trigger field | Shared-service packet answer |
| --- | --- |
| boundary mismatch | one municipality alone may be too small to staff, procure, maintain, or continuously operate a service at acceptable quality |
| harmed population | residents who receive lower-quality, delayed, or unavailable services because their municipality lacks scale, staff, technology, or procurement capacity |
| incumbent failure | purely local provision fails when each municipality duplicates scarce capacity, hires below minimum expertise, or cannot sustain continuity |
| form family | intermunicipal compact, shared-service center, joint board, host-municipality arrangement, or special-purpose backbone with no general mandate |
| lower-form sufficiency | a thinner connector is usually sufficient when the problem is capacity pooling, not one indivisible metro-scale system with network effects, large assets, or unified public strategy |
| upgrade test | escalate only if the consortium begins setting binding policy across the territory, carrying large debt / assets, coercing nonmembers, or making distributive choices that require direct democratic authorization |
| handback test | downshift or dissolve if member municipalities regain capacity, the service can be modularized, the compact becomes capture-prone, or the hub cannot show service-level value |

The key test is not whether cooperation is useful. It is whether cooperation has become government. Most shared-service consortia should answer: **not yet, and not unless the mandate changes**.

### 4. Evidence lanes

| Lane | What the case should show | Archive caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| legal basis | lawful municipal cooperation, compact, joint board, host-agency agreement, or special-purpose service body | legal permission to cooperate is not permission to govern everything jointly |
| service proof | baseline service gaps, staffing limits, procurement limits, continuity problems, or performance failures that justify pooling | vague efficiency claims are not enough; the packet needs service-level evidence |
| fiscal proof | cost-share formula, budget, reserves, liabilities, procurement savings, and member exposure | pooled money can hide cross-subsidy, exit penalties, or deferred liabilities |
| delegation proof | which decisions remain with member councils and which are delegated to the hub | no delegation map means ordinary accountability has been moved without being named |
| lived-path proof | residents can find the service, make complaints, obtain records, and know which council remains politically answerable | a shared back office can still create a front-door maze |

A future place-specific packet should add named member municipalities, service lines, statutory authority, budgets, performance measures, complaints, and exit / accession history. This packet fixes the form grammar first.

### 5. Form holding

The honest form is **shared-service backbone under municipal ownership**. It may pool professional staff, back-office systems, procurement, service delivery, records, training, emergency standby, inspection capacity, and common service standards. It may not become a general-purpose regional government, set broad territorial policy, tax independently, borrow beyond compact authorization, coerce nonmember municipalities, or use administrative efficiency to erase local political responsibility.

The backbone should have a compact that names: members, service lines, authority source, decision rules, cost shares, asset / liability allocation, service-level commitments, records custody, data responsibilities, complaint routing, audit, publication duties, procurement rules, amendment, accession, exit, dissolution, and handback.

### 7. Rival interpretations

| Rival rule | Why it is tempting | Why this packet rejects or limits it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| every shared service needs a regional authority | gives cooperation a visible home | overbuilds when the live problem is capacity, not shared territorial sovereignty |
| pure bilateral contracting is enough | keeps institutions light | fails when multiple members need stable records, common service levels, pooled staff, audit, and durable complaint routing |
| merge the municipalities | eliminates coordination costs | too blunt if identity, representation, geography, or service differences still justify municipal existence |
| host municipality runs everything | administratively cheap | risks giving one member unreviewed power over others unless delegation, cost, records, and dispute rules are hard |
| private vendor as backbone | may supply expertise quickly | cannot become the accountability home; public authority, audit, records, and complaints must remain with the member-public compact |

The preferred rule is **not fragmentation and not new government by office-sharing**. It is compacted municipal ownership with hard service machinery.

### 8. Review clock

Reopen this packet when any of the following occurs:

- the consortium adds a new service line with policy, coercive, fiscal, or distributive consequences;
- a member exits, joins, defaults, or disputes the cost-share formula;
- shared assets, debt, long-term leases, or major vendor contracts become material;
- residents cannot identify the correct complaint or records route;
- the hub begins issuing rules that member councils did not authorize;
- performance gains disappear or service inequality grows between members;
- the compact is used to avoid merger, boundary, or capacity questions that should be faced openly.

A thin backbone should remain thin only while it can prove that it is solving capacity without displacing democratic ownership.

## Failure modes

- **administrative regionalism by stealth**: treating a shared office or platform as if it can make broad public policy.
- **host capture**: letting one member municipality control staff, records, procurement, or agenda-setting for all members.
- **cost-share fog**: hiding cross-subsidy, reserves, exit penalties, unfunded liabilities, or grant dependence behind efficiency language.
- **complaint maze**: bouncing residents between municipality, consortium, host agency, and vendor.
- **vendor substitution**: outsourcing the backbone so deeply that the public compact can no longer inspect records, continuity, or remedies.
- **immortal pilot**: leaving a provisional cooperation arrangement in place for years without review, renewal, or dissolution options.
- **merger avoidance theater**: using shared services to postpone a necessary boundary, capacity, or representation decision.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Can a resident find the right service and complaint route without knowing which municipality, host office, vendor, or consortium performs the task?
2. Can each member council see its contribution, liability, service level, performance result, and exit exposure?
3. Does the compact say which decisions the hub may not make?
4. Are records, data roles, procurement files, and audit materials assigned to a named public home?
5. Can a small member challenge host-member behavior without leaving the service immediately?
6. Is there a review clock that can renew, amend, downshift, merge, or dissolve the arrangement?
7. Would the public notice the moment this stopped being a service backbone and started being a government?
