# 875 — Applied model-decision case packet for Robodebt: income averaging, welfare debts, appeal friction, and no social-security debt by automated proxy

## One-line thesis

Robodebt should be classified as an unlawful evidentiary-proxy and automated debt-recovery public-law incident: a data-matching and income-averaging workflow turned discrepancy signals into social-security debts, shifted practical burden to recipients, exposed people to collection pressure, and now requires evidence, notice, review, legal-advice, remediation, and automated-decision guardrails before any successor system is trusted.

## Why this matters

The archive needed a case where the problem was not a regional body, a treaty waist, a database-alert system, a cloud platform, a fragile-jurisdiction support mission, or a symbolic mandate. Robodebt is different: the public authority already existed, the legal welfare system already existed, and the machine did not need to become a separate institution. The governance failure was that an automated compliance workflow changed the evidentiary and human-service structure inside an existing entitlement-and-debt regime.

That makes it a necessary stress test for `874`. It shows that the most dangerous model-mediated decision may be administratively ordinary: a data match, a letter, a portal, a call centre, a debt ledger, a recovery script, a collection pause that does not pause, a draft advice not escalated, a tribunal signal not generalized, and a budget saving that makes the system look productive while the proof floor collapses.

The packet's holding is narrow. It does not say every data match is unlawful or every automated welfare process is suspect. It says that a public debt cannot be raised by statistical proxy unless the law authorizes that proof method and the person receives an appeal-ready decision packet. Robodebt failed that test.

### 2. Legal form boundary

Robodebt should not be classified as a supplier-dependency case even though it belongs in the public-automation family. Its central failure was not private platform control. It was public-administration proof failure.

| Wrong label | Why wrong |
| --- | --- |
| ordinary compliance | ignores that averaged income was used to raise and pursue debts at scale |
| mere IT bug | hides legal authority, burden, notice, collection, and budget-design failures |
| human-reviewed system | overstates review if staff cannot or do not reconstruct lawful evidence |
| fraud detector | imports stigma and enforcement posture before legal proof |
| AI scandal only | narrows the case to model novelty rather than public-law evidence, advice, review, and debt recovery |
| closed historical scandal | misses live settlement, implementation, transparency, and ADM-framework repair clocks |

The correct label keeps the mundane administrative pieces visible.

### 3. Evidence-grade failure

| Stage | Honest grade | Failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ATO / Centrelink discrepancy | signal | mismatch treated as a debt candidate at industrial scale |
| averaged annual income over fortnights | assumption / estimate | proxy treated as evidence of actual fortnightly income |
| recipient non-response or incomplete response | engagement signal | practical burden shifted to recipient to disprove the debt |
| debt calculation | proposed fact at best | amount became actionable debt before lawful evidence was assembled |
| human contact | service / review opportunity | call-centre and online pathways did not cure proof, burden, and vulnerability problems |
| recovery | legal effect | collection pressure hardened the proxy into practical finality |
| refund / settlement | remediation | money repair does not by itself repair legality, records, trauma, trust, and future guardrails |

The core lesson is **evidence-grade inflation**. The system converted a signal into a debt and then made the recipient fight backward from the state's assumption.

### 5. Model-decision test docket

This case activates the full model-decision matrix:

1. **Legal-authority gate** — income averaging must be tied to explicit legal authority before it can support debt.
2. **Evidentiary-proxy ban** — averaged tax data may be a signal or investigatory lead, not proof of fortnight-specific debt unless law and evidence support it.
3. **Burden-of-proof floor** — the state must not silently shift proof to recipients through portal design, non-response rules, or collection pressure.
4. **Data lineage and periodization** — annual or employer-level data must be mapped to the statutory reporting period before legal effect.
5. **Human determination test** — staff involvement must examine evidence and legality, not merely process workflow state.
6. **Notice and explanation packet** — recipients must see the calculation, assumptions, evidence grade, and contest route.
7. **Review and stay-of-collection** — disputed debts require review routes and recovery restraint.
8. **Vulnerability and assisted-channel gate** — digital-only or stigma-heavy pathways must not exclude people least able to respond.
9. **Algorithm / business-rule scrutiny** — rules, assumptions, and system design must be inspectable by expert, legal, ombuds, and parliamentary routes.
10. **Remediation and learning loop** — refunds, zeroing, compensation, apology, implementation updates, and future ADM reform must be linked to root-cause repair.

### 6. Opposition and rival readings

**Rival 1: data matching is necessary compliance.** The archive accepts that premise. A welfare system can use data matching to detect inconsistencies and protect public funds. The case fails only when the signal is treated as proof without lawful evidence and humane review.

**Rival 2: Robodebt was not really automated because people could engage.** The archive rejects the shortcut. The question is whether the human layer had the legal evidence, authority, time, and service design needed to prevent the proxy from becoming debt.

**Rival 3: refunds and settlements closed the matter.** The archive rejects closure by money alone. Remediation must also repair legal-advice pathways, automated-decision transparency, review publication, vulnerability handling, records, and future proof floors.

**Rival 4: the case proves governments should avoid automated decision-making.** The archive rejects that overcorrection. Automation can be lawful and useful when it is limited to evidence-supported, disclosed, reviewable, and audited functions.

### 7. Capture and theater channels

- **budget-savings capture**: expected savings made the system's scale and speed appear as administrative success.
- **proxy proof**: averaged income became practical debt evidence.
- **burden-shift by interface**: the recipient's task became disproving a state assumption.
- **human-loop theater**: interaction and review channels were treated as fairness despite weak proof.
- **notice theater**: letters and portals existed without an adequate proof route.
- **legal-advice fog**: legal risk was not transformed into decisive stop, repair, or escalation action quickly enough.
- **tribunal-signal suppression**: adverse review signals did not become system-wide correction at the needed speed.
- **stigma amplification**: compliance and debt language intensified harm for people receiving support.
- **remediation narrowing**: refund and settlement processes risk being treated as complete institutional repair.

### 9. Holding

Robodebt should be classified as an **unlawful evidentiary-proxy and automated debt-recovery public-law incident**. It is not merely an AI cautionary tale, not merely a debt-collection dispute, and not merely a closed historical failure. Its continuing value for the archive is the proof rule: no public debt, sanction, benefit loss, or collection path by statistical proxy unless lawful evidence, burden, notice, review, and remediation can be reconstructed.

## Failure modes

- **social-security debt by automated proxy**: averaged income over a year substitutes for evidence of actual fortnightly income.
- **appeal after harm**: review exists but recovery pressure, stress, or hardship makes the proxy practically final.
- **departmental boundary fog**: operating agency, policy department, ministers, tax-data holder, tribunal, ombuds, and collectors each see only part of the failure.
- **legal advice without operational stop**: uncertainty or adverse advice does not freeze expansion, collection, or debt raising.
- **class error without class remedy**: individual reviews cannot repair a common proof defect.
- **implementation-update theater**: accepting recommendations is treated as repair before the public can inspect durable changes.
- **ADM-transparency gap**: agencies disclose automation vaguely, inconsistently, or only after scandal.
- **settlement closure**: compensation is used to imply institutional cure before decision rules and proof duties are rebuilt.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Would the debt survive if the automated or averaged component were removed?
2. Can the government show period-specific evidence for the amount demanded?
3. Was the recipient told the assumption, source data, calculation, and lawful burden in plain language?
4. Did non-response or partial response become a practical admission?
5. Could staff pause, reverse, or escalate the matter based on legal insufficiency, not only customer contact?
6. Did tribunal, ombuds, legal, advocate, or frontline warnings trigger a system-wide incident response?
7. Did debt recovery pause while review was live?
8. Does remediation cover people who paid, people who did not pay, people harmed by process, and people still uncertain about eligibility?
9. Are current and future automated-decision systems disclosed by decision type, legal basis, and logic class?
10. Is the successor framework audited independently for fairness, bias, client usability, and legal authority?

Add this packet to the case-packet matrix as the archive's first applied model-mediated public-decision failure case. Use it whenever an automated compliance, eligibility, debt, sanction, fraud, immigration, tax, policing, or benefits system turns data mismatch or model output into legal effect.
