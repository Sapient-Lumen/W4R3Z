# 848 — Cross-boundary form dispatcher for Radical Governance: trigger first, ladder second, chain only as needed, one holding per case, and no institutional form by spillover drama

## One-line thesis

The `812`–`824` cross-boundary form chain should now be entered through one dispatcher: first prove that a cross-boundary trigger is real, then choose the thinnest honest form through `812`, then cite only the chain notes that are live in the case, and finally issue one public form holding with escalation, downshift, and no-hidden-sovereignty limits.

## Why this matters

The archive has a strong run of cross-boundary design notes, but the run is deliberately granular. It separates form choice, legitimacy, finance, administration, oversight, participation, information, membership, decision rules, disputes, assets, contracts, and enforcement. That separation prevents institutional cosplay, but it also creates a reader problem: a real case can become a tour through thirteen notes before anyone names the actual form.

This is the next compression hazard after the broad scope-answer cluster. The danger is not that `812`–`824` are wrong. The danger is that they are too easy to cite as an omnibus manual and too hard to enter quickly. A dense chain without a dispatcher can make two opposite mistakes: it can thicken every spillover into a new tier, or it can hide heavy cross-boundary power inside a connector because nobody walked the chain far enough.

The reconstruction therefore should not add a fourteenth chain note. It should install an entry point. Use this note when a reader asks: **what kind of cross-boundary institution is honest here, and which chain notes do I actually need?**

## Pattern pack

### 1. Start with trigger proof, not institutional appetite

Before choosing a form, state the trigger in six short fields:

| Field | Question |
| --- | --- |
| shared system | What real system crosses the boundary: transport, housing market, watershed, grid, basin, waste, public health, disaster logistics, corridor, data spine, or another named system? |
| harmed population | Who is harmed by the mismatch between the system and the existing map? |
| incumbent failure | Which existing government or compact cannot solve the problem alone, and why? |
| authority gap | What legal, fiscal, administrative, or enforcement power is missing? |
| lower-form test | Why would a thinner connector, contract, or pooled service be insufficient? |
| handback test | What would show that the heavier form can be downshifted, buried, or returned to principals? |

If those six fields cannot be answered, do not form a new institution. Open a case packet, source repair, or problem diagnosis instead.

### 2. Use four form families before inventing hybrids

| Form family | Use when | Core danger | First note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| thin connector | coordination, data-sharing, calendars, scenario planning, mutual notice, or soft alignment is enough | laundering hard decisions through a soft forum | `812`, then usually `818` or `820` |
| shared-service backbone | the problem is pooled capacity while member governments should keep title to the duty | principal evasion, contractorization, or service company drift | `812`, `814`, `815`, `816`, `823` |
| bounded functional authority | one named system needs continuous cross-boundary operations, assets, rules, or incident command | technocratic capture, creditor capture, or hidden territorial government | `812` through the live chain notes |
| general-purpose territorial tier | multiple systems now require recurring distributive, land-use, borrowing, coercive, rights, or political balancing choices | decorative duplication, patronage mapping, or nation-state mimicry | `841`, `790`, `812`, then full chain review |

The slogan is **as thin as possible, as thick as necessary**. Thinness is not virtue when it conceals power; thickness is not virtue when it only copies prestige.

### 3. Dispatch by the question actually live

Do not cite the whole chain by default. Use this routing table.

| Live question | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Is any cross-boundary form justified at all? | this note, then `812` |
| Which form family is honest? | `812` |
| Who owns or authorizes the body? | `813` |
| How does money move, and who bears fiscal risk? | `814` |
| Who employs staff, keeps records, procures, and operates assets? | `815` |
| Who audits, investigates, hears complaints, and supplies judicial route? | `816` |
| How do residents, users, firms, and affected territories enter the process? | `817` |
| Where do shared facts, observatories, dashboards, and official records live? | `818` |
| Who can join, leave, alter boundaries, amend the charter, or dissolve the body? | `819` |
| What voting rule, veto, reserve, or deadlock breaker applies? | `820` |
| How are intergovernmental disputes resolved without service hostage-taking? | `821` |
| Who owns assets, liabilities, reserves, stranded costs, and handback duties? | `822` |
| Which powers are implemented by contract, concession, service agreement, or change control? | `823` |
| What inspections, permits, sanctions, enforcement badges, or regulatory acts are allowed? | `824` |

The dispatcher succeeds when an applied case cites only live chain notes precisely, not all thirteen defensively. Thin service packets may cite three or four; middle risk platforms may cite more because information, boundaries, and review are part of the live form.

### 4. Require one form holding in every applied verdict

Every applied case should end with one short holding:

> The honest form is `[thin connector / shared-service backbone / bounded functional authority / general-purpose tier]` because `[trigger proof]`; it may exercise `[named powers]`; it must not exercise `[forbidden powers]`; its legitimacy, finance, administration, oversight, and handback conditions are `[citations to live chain notes]`; it must be reviewed after `[event or clock]`.

This holding keeps the archive from producing attractive descriptions without an institutional decision.

### 5. Name both false positives and false negatives

| Error | Example pattern | Repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| false positive | a funding stream, regional brand, crisis meeting, donor preference, or one failed local project is treated as proof that a new body is needed | return to trigger proof and lower-form test |
| false negative | real cross-boundary harm is minimized because every incumbent can point to a formal boundary | force harmed-population and incumbent-failure fields into the record |
| under-thickening | a connector silently makes hard choices about debt, enforcement, land, rates, or service rationing | escalate to backbone, authority, or tier review |
| over-thickening | a general-purpose layer is created for a single operational system | downshift to a functional authority or shared-service backbone |
| prestige copying | the form resembles a parliament, cabinet, regulator, or court because those names sound serious | ask what powers actually require those offices |
| contract escape | public authority is routed through contracts so duties become hard to see or contest | use `823` plus `816` before approving the form |

The archive should be hostile to both lazy regionalization and lazy localism.

### 6. Political-economy check by form

| Form | Who may benefit from blur? | Required guard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| thin connector | officials who want regional decisions without regional accountability | no binding hard powers without reauthorization |
| shared-service backbone | principals who want to outsource blame while keeping symbolic title | service-level, records-home, complaint, and exit rules |
| bounded functional authority | engineers, creditors, vendors, or dominant anchor governments | chartered powers, assigned revenue limits, independent audit, and public amendment gates |
| general-purpose tier | party machines, land coalitions, or national actors seeking map leverage | directly legible elections, equalization, integrity rules, and constitutional review |

A form is not honest until its likely capture channel is named.

### 7. Use escalation and downshift gates symmetrically

The chain should not only say when to thicken. It should also say when to thin.

Escalate when a body repeatedly makes decisions outside its form family: a connector allocates burdens, a backbone sets policy, a functional authority balances several unrelated systems, or an elected tier is necessary to settle distributive conflict honestly.

Downshift when the reason for the body has expired, the system becomes technically routine, member governments regain capacity, pooled assets can be safely handed back, or the form carries more constitutional weight than the live problem.

A cross-boundary body without a downshift path becomes a shadow tier by inertia.

### 8. Keep jurisdictional instruments subordinate to form choice

Interlocal agreements, joint powers authorities, municipal consortia, special districts, regional districts, metropolitan authorities, treaty compacts, European groupings, river-basin organizations, and other legal instruments are not the design answer by themselves. They are vessels.

First name the form family. Then choose the legal vessel available in the jurisdiction. If the vessel cannot carry the legitimacy, finance, oversight, or handback conditions required by the form holding, use a lighter form or seek a proper legal foundation.

### 9. Case-packet minimum for cross-boundary forms

A compact applied packet should include:

1. boundary mismatch;
2. harmed population;
3. incumbent failure;
4. lower-form test;
5. proposed form family;
6. live chain notes cited;
7. powers granted;
8. powers denied;
9. capture channel;
10. review, escalation, and downshift gate.

This is the smallest useful packet. Anything less risks becoming institutional marketing.

### 10. Status of the `812`–`824` chain after this pass

The chain remains active. It is not ready for merger, because applied use has not yet shown which chain fields recur and which are rarely used. But it now has a first stop.

Use `848` before `812` when a reader has not yet chosen a form. Use `812` when the form ladder itself is the question. Use `813`–`824` only for live chain questions. Do not add more cross-boundary chain notes until at least two applied case packets show a missing field that cannot be handled by this dispatcher.

## Failure modes

- **omnibus citation**: citing all of `812`–`824` to avoid deciding which issue is live.
- **spillover drama**: treating any boundary-crossing effect as proof that a heavier institution is needed.
- **connector laundering**: using a soft forum to make hard decisions without voters, principals, records, or remedy.
- **authority cosplay**: creating a functional authority with a logo, board, and dashboard but no honest revenue, operator, audit, or liability chain.
- **tier mimicry**: creating a general-purpose layer because it looks constitutionally grand rather than because several systems need public political balancing.
- **handback amnesia**: forgetting that every non-general-purpose form needs a downshift, burial, or return-to-principals test.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Can the trigger proof be stated in six fields?
2. Did the verdict choose one form family?
3. Did the case cite only live chain notes?
4. Are both escalation and downshift gates visible?
5. Is the likely capture channel named?
6. Can a resident tell where records, complaints, budgets, and remedies live?
7. Does the form holding deny powers as clearly as it grants them?
