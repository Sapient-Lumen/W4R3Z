# 889 — Applied platform-migration case packet for UK eVisa / View and Prove digital-status migration: share codes, legacy documents, and no right by portal uptime

## One-line thesis

The UK eVisa transition should be classified as a public-rights proof platform migration, not simply a digital convenience upgrade: the underlying immigration status may not change, but practical access to work, rent, benefits, travel, services, and third-party recognition can fail if account access, data correctness, legacy-document continuity, share-code systems, checker education, outage response, and assisted routes are not migration-gated.

## Why this matters

An eVisa is not the underlying immigration permission. But in everyday administration, the proof channel can become the right's practical waist. Employers, landlords, carriers, public authorities, banks, education providers, benefit offices, and border systems may rely on digital proof, share codes, account records, and system-to-system checks. If those channels fail, a person with lawful status can be treated as if they cannot prove it.

That makes the eVisa transition a hard case for the archive. The official story is not trivial: digital status can reduce document loss and fraud, improve sharing, and simplify checks. The rival story is also serious: a digital-only proof system can strand people through wrong data, inaccessible accounts, third-party non-recognition, outages, unlinked documents, legacy proof gaps, and weak offline alternatives.

The honest form is a public-rights proof platform migration with legacy-document and affected-user continuity duties.

### 5. Platform-migration docket

This case activates the platform-migration tests, with special force on proof-of-status continuity:

1. **Old/new authority map** — distinguish immigration permission, physical proof, UKVI account, eVisa record, share code, checker service, and system-to-system status sharing.
2. **Identity/status/data mapping** — prove correct linking of person, passport, BRP/BRC, legacy proof, photo, status, conditions, and expiry.
3. **Legacy and unregistered cohort tail** — identify who still relies on old documents, who lacks an account, and who cannot be reached.
4. **Third-party checker propagation** — show employers, landlords, carriers, banks, agencies, and public bodies know what proof to accept and how to escalate.
5. **Account and document update resilience** — test phone/email loss, passport changes, name changes, photo mismatch, account recovery, and linked-document failure.
6. **Outage and incident disclosure** — publish incident thresholds for portal unavailability, release errors, wrong-status display, and share-code failure.
7. **Assisted and non-digital route** — preserve access for digitally excluded, elderly, disabled, language-barrier, homeless, detained, or vulnerable users.
8. **Error correction and stay route** — prevent work, rent, benefit, travel, or service harm while the proof record is being corrected.
9. **Legacy-document fallback** — specify what older documents remain usable, for what purposes, and when the fallback will end.
10. **Cutover / expansion gate** — require evidence before moving further visa classes or checker obligations into eVisa-only status.
11. **Post-migration harm audit** — track denied boarding, work suspension, rent refusal, benefit delay, data error, and account-lockout cases.

### 6. Opposition and rival readings

**Rival 1: eVisas are more secure and cannot be lost like physical documents.** The archive accepts this as a real advantage. It rejects the conclusion that digital proof is therefore safe without stable account access, data correction, and outage fallbacks.

**Rival 2: status does not change when a person updates to an eVisa.** The archive accepts the legal distinction. It rejects proof complacency: a right that cannot be proven to a checker can still fail in practice.

**Rival 3: millions of accounts have already been created.** The archive accepts scale as relevant. It rejects account-count theater. Account creation does not prove data correctness, third-party acceptance, accessibility, outage resilience, or remaining-cohort coverage.

**Rival 4: legacy documents can still be used in some cases.** The archive accepts the mitigation, but requires clear purpose, duration, checker education, and remedy when a checker refuses lawful proof.

**Rival 5: errors can be reported through existing help routes.** The archive accepts that support routes matter. It asks whether they are fast enough to prevent work, rent, travel, benefit, or service harm while correction is pending.

### 7. Capture and theater channels

- **status/proof laundering**: officials say status is unchanged while proof failure causes practical loss.
- **account-count theater**: registration totals are treated as migration completion.
- **share-code chokepoint**: a temporary code becomes the practical gate to rights.
- **checker ignorance**: employers, landlords, carriers, and agencies misrecognize proof and shift risk to the status holder.
- **legacy erasure**: old lawful proof is formally preserved but operationally ignored.
- **outage invisibility**: portal downtime is treated as inconvenience rather than rights-access incident.
- **data-platform capture**: a status record is trusted because it appears in the digital account, even when the person contests it.
- **assisted-route theater**: webchat and help pages exist but cannot resolve urgent proof harms in time.

### 9. Holding

The UK eVisa transition is a public-rights proof platform migration. The rule is: **no right by portal uptime**. Digital proof may improve security and convenience, but the archive should not score migration as complete while lawful status can be practically defeated by account failure, wrong data, checker non-recognition, legacy-proof confusion, or unavailable support.

## Anti-theater tests

This packet fails if the archive treats "status is unchanged," "millions of accounts created," "digital is more secure," or "support is available" as sufficient. It passes only with proof that identity/status mapping, legacy fallback, checker propagation, incident disclosure, correction speed, assisted routes, and post-migration harm audits protect the practical ability to prove lawful status.
