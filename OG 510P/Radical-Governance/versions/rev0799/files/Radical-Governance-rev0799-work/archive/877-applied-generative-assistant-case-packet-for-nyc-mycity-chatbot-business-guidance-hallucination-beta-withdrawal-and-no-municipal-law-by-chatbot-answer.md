# 877 — Applied generative-assistant case packet for NYC MyCity Chatbot: business-guidance hallucination, beta withdrawal, and no municipal law by chatbot answer

## One-line thesis

The NYC MyCity Chatbot should be classified as an official public guidance assistant whose beta status did not neutralize reliance risk: it was launched to give trusted business guidance, later audited for inaccurate and inconsistent answers, and now requires a withdrawal / transition receipt, incident ledger, corpus and evaluation record, and no municipal law by chatbot answer.

## Why this matters

This packet gives `876` a negative-control case. The issue is not that New York City experimented with a generative assistant. The issue is that an official city interface can tell users how to comply with public rules while the actual legal authority, source hierarchy, and answer reliability sit elsewhere. Once a government assistant tells a business, landlord, employer, parent, or applicant what they can do, the practical governance question is not only “was there a disclaimer?” It is whether the city can reconstruct the source, correct the error, route the user to the official rule, and decide whether the tool should continue.

The MyCity record is useful because it includes launch rhetoric, later public criticism, audit findings, performance and feedback data, and an eventual public page saying the chatbot beta test has ended. That makes it stronger than a mere scandal story. It lets the archive test how a generative guidance assistant should be launched, monitored, corrected, and retired.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Trigger and dispatcher table

| Field | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| trigger | citywide AI chatbot embedded in a public portal for business and later broader city-service guidance |
| boundary mismatch | official city brand, small-business compliance questions, beta label, generative answers, source pages, 311 expansion, vendor / platform support, public feedback, audits, and retirement notice diverged |
| first-form question | whether the chatbot was search, general guidance, legal advice, public-service triage, or model-mediated decision |

| heavier-form test | model-decision or municipal lawmaking is too thick unless the answer itself legally binds the user or agency |
| form verdict | public generative guidance assistant with failed reliability / reliance controls and withdrawal-receipt requirement |

### 2. Legal form boundary

| Wrong label | Why wrong |
| --- | --- |
| harmless beta | public beta can still create reliance when launched on an official portal and framed as trusted guidance |
| legal advice machine | too strong as formal law; the assistant did not become a court, agency adjudicator, or code authority |
| search bar | too thin because the assistant composed answers, not just links |
| vendor failure | incomplete because public owner, public launch, public corpus, and public correction duties remain city responsibilities |
| closed issue | too easy because ending a beta is not a lessons-learned or affected-user receipt |

The archive's honest label is **public generative guidance assistant with failed reliability / reliance controls**.

### 5. Generative-assistant test docket

This case activates the full `metadata/generative_assistant_tests.json` matrix:

1. **Public-owner and boundary test** — name the owner, scope, high-stakes exclusions, and decision boundary.
2. **Canonical corpus and source trace** — show which pages are authoritative, current, excluded, or superseded.
3. **Reliance and warning test** — prove that warnings match practical reliance, not just legal defensiveness.
4. **Answer consistency and hallucination test** — test repeated, paraphrased, multilingual, and edge-case queries.
5. **Accessibility and language robustness** — test users with different syntax, literacy, disability, and language needs.
6. **Feedback-to-correction clock** — connect user feedback and audit findings to correction, pause, or withdrawal decisions.
7. **Incident disclosure threshold** — decide when wrong official guidance becomes a public incident.
8. **Supplier / model-change record** — preserve platform, model, prompt, guardrail, and admin records.
9. **Human / authoritative handoff** — route high-stakes questions to official pages or staff before harm.
10. **Withdrawal / transition receipt** — when a beta ends, show what replaced it, what was learned, and how users are redirected.

### 6. Opposition and rival readings

**Rival 1: the chatbot was only a beta and users were warned.** The archive accepts that beta status matters, but rejects it as sufficient. Public beta is still public. The warning must be matched to the actual reliance environment.

**Rival 2: all new generative systems have errors, and public testing is necessary.** The archive accepts the need for controlled public testing. The case fails only if launch, testing, incident response, and withdrawal records do not let the public see whether the risk was governed.

**Rival 3: media reports overstated the problem.** The archive uses the later audit record and official beta-ended page as harder evidence. External reports remain useful as incident signals.

**Rival 4: ending the beta solved the problem.** The archive rejects closure by disappearance. Ending a tool requires a transition receipt: what users should use now, what errors were found, what records were preserved, and what policy changed.

### 7. Capture and theater channels

- **trusted-info launch capture**: public language about trusted answers increased reliance pressure.
- **beta theater**: experimental status risked operating as permission to learn on users without full incident discipline.
- **disclaimer theater**: warnings could not repair wrong compliance guidance after the answer was issued.
- **source-waist opacity**: users could not always know which page, rule, or agency owned the answer.
- **feedback theater**: ratings and comments existed but needed correction clocks and public thresholds.
- **performance theater**: a slow or inconsistent chatbot still shaped service access if it was the promoted channel.
- **retirement theater**: a beta-ended page is not a lessons-learned record by itself.

### 8. Repair surfaces

The case should remain open until these records are preserved or marked unavailable:

- source corpus and excluded-page record;
- prompt / model / retrieval / guardrail version history;
- answer logs for high-risk topics;
- incorrect-answer incident ledger;
- user-feedback-to-correction tracker;
- accessibility and language robustness tests;
- red-team reports and remediation decisions;
- vendor deliverable and change-control record;
- public withdrawal / transition receipt;
- policy update showing how future public assistants will be launched, paused, or ended.

### 9. Holding

The MyCity Chatbot is not proof that cities should avoid generative AI. It is proof that official public assistants need a guidance-liability docket before a city treats conversational access as better government. A chatbot answer cannot become municipal law, and a beta label cannot substitute for public records, source grounding, correction, and handoff.

## Anti-theater tests

This packet fails if the city treats beta status, disclaimers, or quiet decommissioning as sufficient repair. The public record must show what domains failed, how false or inconsistent answers were corrected, whether affected users were notified, what vendor / model controls changed, and how any successor assistant will be gated before business owners are again invited to rely on generated guidance.
