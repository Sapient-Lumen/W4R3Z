# 941 — Applied special-education continuity case packet for IDEA Section 618, SPP/APR, Section 504, CRDC, restraint, dispute resolution, and no FAPE by IEP row

## One-line thesis

IDEA Section 618 data, annual IDEA reports, SPP/APR determinations, Section 504 FAPE guidance, CRDC discipline/restraint/seclusion data, NCES disability enrollment indicators, and GAO dispute-resolution findings are necessary evidence lanes, but none proves that an individual student’s evaluation, services, accommodations, placement, discipline protections, transition, or remedy actually worked.

## Why this matters

This packet operationalizes note 940. It is not a general education-policy essay. It is a test harness for IEP and Section 504 continuity: eligibility, evaluation, plan design, delivered services, LRE, language/accessibility, discipline, restraint/seclusion, dispute resolution, transition, and source-currentness.

The governing repair phrase is **no FAPE by IEP row**.

## Pattern pack

- **IDEA data are denominators, not delivered service.** Section 618 and annual reports show state/federal reporting surfaces, but student-level service delivery still requires local records.
- **SPP/APR determinations are oversight signals, not local FAPE proof.** A state determination can coexist with district, school, or student-level failures.
- **Section 504 guidance is a rights lane, not implementation proof.** A 504 plan or FAPE obligation must be joined to accommodations, aids, services, and discrimination-remedy records.
- **CRDC is a civil-rights denominator, not incident closure.** Discipline, restraint, seclusion, law-enforcement referral, and school-arrest counts require student-level authority, notice, disability nexus, and repair.
- **Dispute resolution is not equally usable by default.** Mediation, state complaints, and due process exist, but cost, expertise, time, and information imbalance can prevent meaningful use.
- **Inclusion guidance is not inclusion delivery.** LRE and inclusive-practices letters need schedule, supports, curriculum, peer access, and progress evidence.
- **No transition by exit row.** Part C-to-Part B, high-school transition, graduation, age-out, VR, health, and adult-service handoffs need successor receipt.

## Source-to-duty map

| Source surface | What it proves | What it cannot prove alone |
|---|---|---|
| IDEA Section 618 data | Required state reporting on infants/toddlers and children served under IDEA. | Timely evaluation, correct eligibility, delivered services, or remedy for a student. |
| IDEA annual reports | Federal national implementation and progress reporting. | Local FAPE, accommodation, discipline, or transition continuity. |
| SPP/APR and determination letters | State implementation/compliance/result indicators and federal determinations. | District or student-level service delivery. |
| Section 504 FAPE FAQ and overview | OCR civil-rights frame for equal access and regular/special education aids/services. | Whether a 504 plan was implemented or discrimination repaired. |
| CRDC 2021–22 reports/data | School civil-rights data on opportunity, discipline, restraint, seclusion, law enforcement, and school climate. | Lawfulness, disability nexus, trauma follow-up, or compensatory services in a specific case. |
| OSEP discipline letter | Federal discipline-protection guidance and restraint/seclusion warning surface. | Whether a specific removal, manifestation determination, restraint, or seclusion episode was lawful. |
| NCES students-with-disabilities indicator | National education-statistics context and denominator for students served under IDEA. | Student-level eligibility, service delivery, or placement quality. |
| GAO IDEA dispute-resolution report | Usability and access problems in formal dispute channels. | Whether a specific family had meaningful access to mediation, state complaint, due process, or OCR complaint. |

## Applied continuity ladder

1. **Identify the legal lane.** Is the matter IDEA Part C, IDEA Part B, Section 504-only, ADA/Title II, OCR civil-rights enforcement, state special-education law, or a local service-delivery issue?
2. **Separate aggregate from student proof.** Mark whether evidence is federal data, state determination, district record, student file, provider log, family communication, complaint, or hearing record.
3. **Prove evaluation and eligibility.** Join referral, consent, assessments, timelines, primary language, reevaluation, disability category, adverse impact, and need for specially designed instruction or aids/services.
4. **Prove plan design.** Join IEP/504 goals, accommodations, modifications, related services, behavior supports, assistive technology, transport, LRE, ESY, and progress reporting.
5. **Prove delivery.** Join provider assignments, service logs, missed minutes, substitute coverage, make-up services, accessible materials, interpreter/translation, and communication access.
6. **Prove discipline protections.** Join removals, days, manifestation determination, FBA/BIP, interim placement, restraint, seclusion, law-enforcement referral, arrest, notice, and return-to-learning.
7. **Prove transition.** Join Part C exit, Part B receipt, school-level change, district transfer, foster/custody/hospital return, high-school transition, diploma/certificate/age-out, VR/adult-service handoff, and records transfer.
8. **Prove remedy.** Join meeting request, prior written notice, mediation, state complaint, due process, OCR complaint, stay-put, corrective action, compensatory education/services, and implementation proof.
9. **Prove equity.** Join disability category, race, English learner status, sex, school/district, placement, discipline, restraint/seclusion, law-enforcement referral, and denominator.
10. **Preserve source-currentness.** Mark the data year, federal/state/local level, OCR/OSERS/NCES/GAO source role, and whether the claim is guidance, dataset, annual report, or case record.

## Applied examples

### IDEA Section 618 and annual reports

Use Section 618 and annual IDEA reports to establish reporting duties and national/state denominators. Do not use them to infer that a particular child was evaluated on time, received services, learned in the LRE, or obtained compensatory education after missed services.

### SPP/APR determinations

Use SPP/APR and determination letters to identify state-level implementation posture and indicators. Then open local files. A state determination is not a district service log, parent notice, manifestation decision, or remedy receipt.

### Section 504 FAPE

Use OCR’s Section 504 FAQ and overview to identify the civil-rights lane for qualified students with disabilities. Then demand accommodation and aid/service implementation evidence: accessible materials, health plans, behavior supports, communications, testing accommodations, transportation, and remedy.

### CRDC discipline, restraint, seclusion, and law-enforcement data

Use CRDC as a denominator and red-flag surface. It can show overrepresentation and national patterns. It cannot prove whether a specific restraint, seclusion, removal, referral, or arrest was lawful, disability-aware, trauma-informed, noticed, reviewed, and repaired.

### GAO dispute-resolution findings

Use GAO’s dispute-resolution findings as an access warning. Existence of mediation, state complaints, due process, and OCR complaints does not mean families can afford counsel, experts, time off, translation, records, or procedural knowledge.

## Repair surfaces

- student/legal-lane map for IDEA Part C, IDEA Part B, Section 504, ADA/Title II, OCR, state law, and local owner;
- evaluation, consent, eligibility, disability category, adverse-impact, reevaluation, and timeline docket;
- IEP/504 plan design, goals, accommodations, related services, LRE, behavior supports, AT, transport, ESY, and progress monitoring;
- service-delivery log with missed minutes, provider vacancies, substitutes, tele/service modality, makeup, compensatory education, and family notice;
- discipline/restraint/seclusion/law-enforcement file with manifestation, FBA/BIP, disability nexus, return plan, trauma/health follow-up, and remedy;
- Part C/Part B, school transfer, foster/custody/hospital return, high-school transition, graduation/age-out, VR/adult-service, Medicaid/health, and record-transfer receipt;
- dispute-resolution and civil-rights route covering meeting request, prior written notice, mediation, state complaint, due process, OCR complaint, stay-put, corrective action, and implementation proof;
- source-boundary map separating Section 618, SPP/APR, CRDC, NCES, GAO, guidance, and student-level records.

## Anti-theater tests

- Do not cite an IEP row, eligibility code, 504 plan, or meeting date as FAPE without delivered-service and accommodation evidence.
- Do not cite SPP/APR or federal datasets as proof of local/student-level compliance.
- Do not cite LRE percentage as inclusion without supports, curriculum, peer access, and progress.
- Do not cite discipline disposition as lawful without manifestation, behavior-plan, notice, and return-to-learning proof.
- Do not cite CRDC as incident closure; it is an aggregate civil-rights denominator.
- Do not cite dispute-route existence as usable remedy without access, records, cost, language, representation, and implementation proof.
- Do not cite transition meeting or exit code as successor receipt.

## Source posture

This case packet uses U.S. official and primary oversight surfaces current enough for a continuity packet as of rev0753. It must not be used as a substitute for live district, school, student-file, provider-log, complaint, OCR, state special-education, hearing, or family records.
