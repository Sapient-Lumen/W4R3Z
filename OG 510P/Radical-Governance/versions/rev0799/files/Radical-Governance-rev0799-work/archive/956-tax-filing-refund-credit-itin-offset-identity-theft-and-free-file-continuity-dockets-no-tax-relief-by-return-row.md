# 956 — Tax filing, refund, credit, ITIN, offset, identity-theft, and free-file continuity dockets: no tax relief by return row

## One-line thesis

Taxpayer relief is not a filed return, accepted e-file, transcript row, refund tracker status, credit schedule, ITIN notice, IP PIN, Free File/VITA option, direct-deposit attempt, offset notice, or identity-theft case code. A household can file correctly and still lose practical tax relief through delayed processing, credit disallowance, missing identity proof, expired ITIN, rejected direct deposit, refund offset, paper-return delay, inaccessible filing help, or unresolved tax-related identity theft. The archive should join return, person, preparer, credit, filing channel, identity, refund, payment, offset, notice, appeal, and hardship evidence before treating tax relief as continuous.

## Why this matters

The archive already had benefit, payment-redress, identity, representative-access, food, housing, immigration, veterans, and broadband packets. It still lacked a direct tax-continuity packet even though tax administration is one of the largest recurring public-service delivery systems and the refund can function as a household liquidity bridge. A return row can be true while the person still lacks a usable refund, qualifying credit, ITIN renewal, identity-theft resolution, protected direct deposit, offset review, VITA/TCE filing help, or Taxpayer Advocate escalation.

The governing repair phrase is **no tax relief by return row**.

## Pattern pack

- **No tax relief by return row.** A filed or accepted return is denominator evidence; it does not prove refund issuance, credit correctness, offset safety, or household receipt.
- **No refund by tracker status.** Where's My Refund and account-status views are useful signals, not proof of payment, bank acceptance, check delivery, or debt-offset remedy.
- **No credit by schedule.** EITC, CTC, ACTC, refundable-credit, and dependent rows must be joined to qualifying-person, SSN/ITIN, residency, income, due-diligence, disallowance, and appeal evidence.
- **No access by free-file label.** Free File, Free File Fillable Forms, VITA/TCE, site locators, and online tools require eligibility, language/disability access, device/broadband, appointment capacity, and state-return handoff.
- **No identity by PIN alone.** IP PINs, ID.me, Taxpayer Protection Program letters, and identity-verification routes must be tied to the correct taxpayer, year, return, dependent, and assistive channel.
- **No refund by direct deposit attempt.** Direct-deposit routing, rejected deposit, frozen refund, paper-check election, and bank/prepaid-card ownership must be joined before treating payment as delivered.
- **No hardship resolution by offset notice.** TOP/BFS/IRS offsets must identify the debt, agency, notice, injured-spouse/offset-bypass/hardship route, and restored payment consequence.
- **No ITIN continuity by W-7 submission.** ITIN application or renewal must be joined to original-document handling, processing time, return attachment, credit eligibility, and refund delay.
- **No remedy by phone queue.** IRS/TAS contacts, e-requests, letters, and case logs count only when they restore refund, credit, payment, deadline, or account state.

## Minimum joined record

A serious tax-relief packet should contain at least:

1. **Taxpayer/household spine.** Taxpayer, spouse, dependents, address, TINs, language/disability, representative/preparer, bank account, and hardship stakes.
2. **Return and filing spine.** Tax year, return type, e-file or paper, reject/accept state, amendment/prior-year status, extension, state filing, and preparer/free-file route.
3. **Credit and eligibility spine.** EITC, CTC/ACTC, refundable credits, dependents, valid SSN/ITIN, income, residency, due diligence, disallowance, recertification, and appeal.
4. **ITIN/identity spine.** W-7, renewal, CAA/VITA route, original documents, IP PIN, TPP letter, identity verification, duplicate filing, and IDTVA status.
5. **Refund/payment spine.** Refund amount, tracker/account status, direct deposit, rejected/frozen deposit, paper check, debit/prepaid account, bank rejection, and receipt.
6. **Offset/debt spine.** TOP/BFS/IRS debt, creditor agency, notice, injured-spouse or offset-bypass route, hardship, and restored payment outcome.
7. **Notice/remedy spine.** CP/math-error/audit/disallowance letters, e-request, phone/TAC/TAS, administrative appeal, Tax Court/deficiency deadline, and correction receipt.
8. **Outcome spine.** Whether household liquidity, food, rent, utilities, childcare, work, school, benefit eligibility, or debt consequences were actually stabilized.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Was the return accepted, processed, adjusted, held, offset, or merely submitted?
2. Which taxpayer, spouse, dependent, bank account, representative, and tax year does the row bind?
3. Are EITC/CTC/ACTC and other refundable credits proven by qualifying-person and TIN evidence, not just a schedule line?
4. Is an ITIN application or renewal blocking processing, credit eligibility, original documents, or refund timing?
5. Is the refund tracker joined to payment issue, bank acceptance, check delivery, and account ownership?
6. Did an offset occur, and can the person identify the debt, creditor agency, hardship route, injured-spouse route, and remedy deadline?
7. Did a fraud or identity-theft filter protect the fisc while leaving the taxpayer without refund or account correction?
8. Are free filing and assistance routes practically available in the person's language, disability context, income level, state, and device/broadband situation?
9. Is a notice deadline preserved by actual receipt, comprehension, representative authority, and route to appeal?
10. Did the correction restore household liquidity, not merely close an IRS workflow?

## Failure modes

- A return is accepted but a refund is frozen because identity verification, ITIN renewal, rejected direct deposit, or fraud review has not cleared.
- A family appears to claim EITC/CTC, but TIN, residency, age, income, disallowance, recertification, or due-diligence records defeat the credit.
- A refund status page says processing or sent, but TOP/BFS offset, bank rejection, check nonreceipt, or direct-deposit freeze prevents household receipt.
- An ITIN application attaches to the return, but original documents, tax-season processing clocks, or renewal status delay the refund and credits.
- A taxpayer receives a math-error, audit, disallowance, or identity letter but lacks language, disability, representative, phone, mail, or portal access to answer.
- Free filing exists in the abstract, but eligibility, state-return availability, filing complexity, appointment capacity, device access, or language support excludes the person.
- Tax-related identity theft is identified, but the taxpayer waits months for account correction and refund issuance.
- A hardship route exists but the case file cannot prove rent, food, utility, medical, or debt consequences and restored liquidity.

## Negative control

Do **not** activate this packet for ordinary tax-policy commentary that does not affect a person's filing, refund, credit, ITIN, identity, offset, notice, appeal, or payment outcome. Do activate it when any public body, court, benefits program, employer, lender, school, landlord, or service provider is tempted to say that a tax return, transcript row, refund tracker, credit schedule, or offset entry settles the matter.
