# 790 — Ideal governments of each scope: answer key, micro-local voice, municipal full kit, metropolitan democracy, regional platforms, national guarantees, continental compacts, and global narrow waists

## One-line thesis

The archive should now treat the question **what would the ideal government at each scope be?** as having one default answer-key: **micro-local institutions for voice, witness, and upkeep; municipalities as the first full-spectrum governments of ordinary life; metropolitan governments for shared urban systems; regional or provincial governments for major territorial platforms and shared rule; national government for equal citizenship, solidarity, macro-capacity, and constitutional guarantee; continental bodies for bounded cross-border compacts; and global bodies for narrow treaty-bound coordination of peace, commons, alerting, and interoperability**.

## Why this matters

The archive already contains the substance of this answer in several places — especially `541`, `565`, `579`, `628`, `629`, `630`, `631`, and `660`. That is useful, but it also creates a predictable archive problem: the same constitutional answer keeps being restated in adjacent forms.

What this revision should therefore add is not another long scenic route through the theory. It should add one **answer-key note** that future notes can point to when they need the stack in its compact ideal form. Later notes should now also treat `797` as the place to go once the question stops being **what is the right scope design?** and becomes **who may challenge the benchmark, trigger, intervention, or handback judgment attached to that design?**

That matters because the governing question is now settled enough to compress. The archive no longer needs to rediscover, each time, that neighborhoods are not miniature sovereigns, that municipalities need a complete civic kit, that the functional city needs democratic metropolitan government when shared systems are real, that regions should exist only when real platforms or shared-rule bargains exist, that nations should guarantee rather than swallow the stack, and that the global layer should be sparse, real, and treaty-bound.

## Pattern pack

## Answer-key rule

### 1. Start from one default stack rather than from institutional symmetry

The archive should presume that ideal government is **nested and differentiated** rather than cloned at every scale. A good stack does not make every level look like a tiny nation-state or a stretched municipality. It gives each scope the smallest honest institutional kit that can carry the work actually living there.

### 2. Micro-local government should be high-voice, low-sovereignty

At the smallest scale, the ideal institution is usually a neighborhood assembly, ward committee, village council, block body, or similarly reachable steward cell. Its jobs are to notice breakdown early, steward tiny commons, allocate small discretionary money where appropriate, and escalate unresolved failures upward.

It should keep minutes, surface local knowledge, and remain easy to reach. It should not try to become a tiny sovereign with heavy debt, broad policing ambition, or a full bureaucratic state.

### 3. Municipal government should be the first complete government of ordinary life

The municipality is the first level that should normally have the whole visible civic kit: elected council, public committees, executive leadership, professional administration, records, procurement, an intelligible budget, own-source revenue where possible, and lawful complaint and review routes.

This is the level for streets, sanitation, permitting, parks, libraries, direct public-realm maintenance, ordinary local service design, and the daily encounter between person and state. The municipal tier should therefore be politically democratic and administratively professional.

### 4. Metropolitan government should become normal once the shared city is real

When transport, housing pressure, freight, flood risk, air quality, major utilities, emergency spillovers, and labour markets are metropolitan in fact, the ideal government is not an incumbent summit, a host-city workaround, or a grant cartel. It is a democratically legible metropolitan authority with a public forum, specialist executive core, stable shared finance, records, formulas, and review routes proportionate to the systems it binds.

### 5. Regional or provincial government should exist for real territorial platforms or real shared rule

A region earns its place when it governs systems too large for the metro or municipality, too place-specific for the nation, and too operationally dense for a thin compact body: hospital systems, watersheds and basins, grids, corridors, higher education platforms, disaster logistics, ecological landscapes, or constitutionally meaningful self-government within a federation or plurinational state.

Where those platforms or bargains are absent, ornamental regionalism should not be preserved just because maps like symmetry.

### 6. National government should be guarantor, not permanent absorber

The ideal national government should secure equal citizenship, rights floors, redistribution, macroeconomic stabilization, social insurance, national infrastructure backbones, constitutional settlement, and the independent institutions that stop power from eating the stack.

The national tier is indispensable for guarantee and solidarity. It is not entitled, by that fact alone, to monopolize every ordinary place function.

### 7. Continental and plurilateral government should be competence-bounded

Between nation and globe, the archive should prefer bounded compact bodies for recurring cross-border problems: mobility regimes, basin and corridor systems, common markets, research and industrial compacts, rights machinery, security cooperation, and similar functional domains. These bodies need named mandates, visible member-state and parliamentary or assembly scrutiny, bounded finance, and reviewable amendment and exit rules.

### 8. Global government should stay thin, real, and treaty-bound

The ideal global layer is neither a world municipality nor a fiction of total sovereign isolation. It is a narrow-waist architecture for problems that plainly outrun any one state: peace and conflict restraint, planetary commons, public-health alerting, selected technical and financial standards, interoperable reporting, and related dispute or review mechanisms.

Its honest institutions are assemblies, secretariats, courts, scientific panels, registries, and specialized agencies with explicit mandates rather than one general world executive.

| **Micro-local** | neighborhood, ward, village, block, or other steward body | direct forum, tiny budget, visible records, upward escalation rights | witness, upkeep, tiny commons, local signal routing | miniature sovereign theater |
| **Municipal** | elected local government with council and professional administration | council, executive, civil service, treasury, records, local rules, complaint routes | ordinary place government and the first full civic front door | elective shell or opaque managerate |

| **Global** | narrow treaty-bound governance for irreducibly global problems | assemblies, secretariats, courts, scientific panels, registries, compliance and reporting routes | peace, commons, alerting, interoperability, selected standards | world-state cosplay or empty sovereigntist denial |

## Guardrails

- Do not mistake direct participation for a reason to keep people trapped in underscaled institutions.
- Do not call a municipality ideal if it has elections but not the books, staff, or legal routes needed to govern.
- Do not leave a real metropolitan economy constitutionally invisible.
- Do not preserve regional tiers that cannot name a real territorial platform or shared-rule vocation.
- Do not let the national tier use solidarity language to justify ordinary operational monopoly.
- Do not let continental or global institutions widen by sentiment, emergency drift, or elite convenience alone.

## Failure modes

- **scope cloning**: every level is asked to imitate the same constitutional shape regardless of problem fit.
- **micro-sovereign romance**: neighborhood or village institutions are overloaded with powers, liabilities, or coercive claims they cannot carry honestly.
- **municipal shellism**: local elections exist but the books, staff, records, or remedy routes needed for real daily government do not.
- **metro invisibility**: the functional city is governed through bargains among incumbents rather than a democratically legible metropolitan tier.
- **regional ornament**: provinces or states survive mainly as decorative duplication, patronage geography, or nostalgic symmetry.
- **national absorption**: the guarantor tier treats rights, solidarity, or efficiency as standing excuses to monopolize ordinary place functions.
- **upper-tier drift**: continental or global institutions widen their reach without owning a new mandate, fiscal basis, or legitimacy claim.

## Compression rule for the archive

If the archive cannot answer the question **what is the ideal government here?** in one sentence naming the level’s **vocation, minimum kit, and anti-cosplay limit**, then the design is still too vague to count as settled.
