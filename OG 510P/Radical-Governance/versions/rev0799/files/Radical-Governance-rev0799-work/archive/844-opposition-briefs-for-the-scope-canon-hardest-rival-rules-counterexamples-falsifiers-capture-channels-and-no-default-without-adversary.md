# 844 — Opposition briefs for the scope canon: hardest rival rules, counterexamples, falsifiers, capture channels, and no default without adversary

## One-line thesis

The archive should not let its ideal-scope answer become a self-confirming doctrine: every strong default in `841`, `790`, and `825`–`840` needs a compact opposition brief that names the serious rival rule, the embarrassing case, the falsifier, the harmed party, and the capture channel before the default is treated as first-line guidance.

## Why this matters

The previous reconstruction made opposition briefs mandatory in principle. This note makes that discipline concrete for the scope canon itself.

The archive’s favored stack is elegant: micro-local voice, municipal full-kit government, metropolitan shared-system democracy, regional platform government, national guarantee, continental compacts, and global narrow-waist coordination. Elegance is not proof. The same language can hide local exclusion, metropolitan technocracy, regional patronage, central ministries using rights floors to hoard ordinary administration, or global expert bodies drifting beyond consent.

Opposition briefing is therefore not a tone of moderation. It is an archive safety device. A default that cannot survive its strongest rival, worst plausible use, and named falsifier should remain a hypothesis or threshold frontier rather than a settled holding.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Treat the broad scope answer as rebuttable by named failures

`841` remains the pocket answer and `790` remains the long answer. But neither should be cited as if it had abolished political judgment. The scope canon now has three layers of authority:

| Authority class | What it may do | What it must not do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| settled holding | set the first design answer for ordinary cases | erase counterexamples or context |
| strong default | guide design unless rebutted by evidence | pretend the proof burden is zero |
| threshold frontier | mark a boundary requiring case evidence | pass itself off as settled doctrine |

The archive’s posture should be: **default first, objection visible, falsifier explicit**.

### 2. Opposition brief for micro-local voice

| Field | Brief |
| --- | --- |
| favored default | small steward cells for voice, witness, tiny commons, and escalation |
| strongest rival rule | keep the smallest scale informal; do not create official bodies below the municipality |
| embarrassing case | local forums can be captured by homeowners, kin networks, local notables, partisan machines, or exclusionary cultural majorities |
| falsifier | evidence that formal micro-local bodies reduce participation diversity, delay service repair, or systematically block equal municipal service |
| harmed party | renters, migrants, informal residents, youth, disabled residents, linguistic minorities, and people dependent on quiet access rather than public assembly |
| capture channel | “local knowledge” becomes a gatekeeping phrase that lets the loudest residents speak as the place |
| repair | keep coercion, debt, licensing, exclusion, and hard entitlement decisions above this layer; require open minutes, rotation, tiny budgets, anti-harassment rules, and upward appeal |

Micro-local government is justified by reachability, not sovereignty.

### 3. Opposition brief for municipal full-kit government

| Field | Brief |
| --- | --- |
| favored default | the municipality as the first complete government of ordinary life |
| strongest rival rule | use professional managers, shared services, or national field offices instead of elected municipal capacity where local capability is weak |
| embarrassing case | municipal autonomy can produce exclusionary zoning, corrupt procurement, poor service capability, fiscal favoritism, or local-rights inequality |
| falsifier | evidence that a municipality cannot maintain lawful records, budgets, staffing, procurement, complaint routes, or minimum service floors after support and pooling are tried |
| harmed party | poor neighborhoods, non-property owners, peripheral settlements, service users with low political power, and residents of fiscally weak jurisdictions |
| capture channel | “home rule” becomes a shield for land hoarding, discriminatory service choices, or patronage hiring |
| repair | pair municipal headroom with national rights floors, equalization, external audit, ombuds routes, anti-corruption checks, and shared-service backbones where scale is below minimum viability |

The municipal full kit is a democratic front door, not a license to localize injustice.

### 4. Opposition brief for metropolitan shared-system government

| Field | Brief |
| --- | --- |
| favored default | democratically legitimated metropolitan authority for transport, housing pressure, utilities, flood risk, freight, air quality, and shared urban systems |
| strongest rival rule | use intermunicipal compacts, special districts, or national infrastructure agencies; avoid creating another elected tier |
| embarrassing case | metropolitan bodies can become remote technocratic authorities, contractor pipelines, central-city dominance machines, or suburban veto clubs |
| falsifier | evidence that the metropolitan problem can be solved through a thin connector with clear accountability, without durable cross-boundary coercion or shared finance |
| harmed party | outer suburbs, informal settlements, low-income riders, small municipalities, and communities displaced by region-scale infrastructure bargains |
| capture channel | “functional urban area” becomes a pretext to thicken government before democratic legitimacy, fiscal visibility, and review lanes are ready |
| repair | require a named shared system, direct or double-key legitimacy, stable formula finance, public transport and housing evidence, anti-displacement duties, and sunset or handback rules for temporary forms |

A metropolitan tier must earn its thickness system by system.

### 5. Opposition brief for regional or provincial platform government

| Field | Brief |
| --- | --- |
| favored default | regions or provinces for major territorial platforms, constitutional self-government, and shared-rule bargains |
| strongest rival rule | avoid intermediate tiers unless constitutionally necessary; use national standards plus municipal implementation |
| embarrassing case | regional autonomy can entrench patronage machines, separatist elites, regional oligarchies, or administrative inequality between territories |
| falsifier | evidence that regional government adds vetoes, cost, and patronage without improving platform governance, rights implementation, or territorial solidarity |
| harmed party | residents of weak regions, internal minorities, municipalities inside dominant regions, and national minorities whose rights are traded by regional elites |
| capture channel | “self-government” becomes a brand for elite insulation rather than public capability or plural voice |
| repair | distinguish federal bargain autonomy from ordinary administrative regions; require fiscal congruence, municipal protection, internal minority safeguards, national rights floors, and independent audit |

Regional government should be thick only where the platform or bargain is real.

### 6. Opposition brief for national guarantee

| Field | Brief |
| --- | --- |
| favored default | national government as guarantor of equal citizenship, solidarity, rights floors, macro-capacity, and constitutional settlement |
| strongest rival rule | give the national tier broad administrative command so equality and service quality are not hostage to weak local capacity |
| embarrassing case | national guarantee language can become permanent absorption, bureaucratic uniformity, emergency centralization, or partisan control of local discretion |
| falsifier | evidence that national intervention improves equality only by destroying accountable local capacity, suppressing plural self-government, or creating unreviewable field administration |
| harmed party | local publics, indigenous or plurinational communities, territorial minorities, and residents whose needs are invisible in central templates |
| capture channel | ministries invoke equality, security, or efficiency to occupy ordinary local functions without proportionality, handback clocks, or local headroom |
| repair | distinguish floors from ceilings; require explicit preemption, proportional supervision, handback clocks, local legal protection, and fiscal rather than merely command-based solidarity |

The national tier is indispensable; that is exactly why its guarantee power needs boundaries.

### 7. Opposition brief for continental and plurilateral compacts

| Field | Brief |
| --- | --- |
| favored default | bounded compact bodies for recurring cross-border problems such as markets, corridors, basins, mobility, rights machinery, research, and security cooperation |
| strongest rival rule | keep interstate cooperation diplomatic and reversible; avoid supranational drift unless a full democratic constitution exists |
| embarrassing case | compact bodies can become democratic-distance machines, judicial or expert overreach channels, or market-rule engines that outrun social accountability |
| falsifier | evidence that the compact repeatedly expands competence without consent, lacks usable exit or amendment routes, or imposes burdens on local and regional governments without representation |
| harmed party | small states, border regions, local governments implementing unfunded rules, workers in exposed sectors, and groups whose rights depend on domestic politics but are reshaped externally |
| capture channel | “technical harmonization” becomes a quiet constitutional amendment by experts, courts, dominant states, or regulated industries |
| repair | require named competences, subsidiarity checks, parliamentary and territorial scrutiny, fiscal visibility, local/regional impact review, and explicit amendment and exit rules |

A compact is legitimate because it is bounded, not because it is above the nation.

### 8. Opposition brief for global narrow-waist governance

| Field | Brief |
| --- | --- |
| favored default | thin, real, treaty-bound global coordination for peace, planetary commons, public-health alerting, selected standards, registries, and dispute mechanisms |
| strongest rival rule | keep global bodies advisory and nonbinding; enforce only through states to preserve sovereign consent |
| embarrassing case | global institutions can be donor-captured, secretariat-driven, dominated by powerful states, too weak to solve urgent problems, or too insulated from affected communities |
| falsifier | evidence that a global waist cannot trigger compliance, cannot keep scientific integrity, or repeatedly imposes obligations without adequate representation, remedy, or resourcing |
| harmed party | small states, indigenous peoples, low-income countries, frontline communities, and people affected by global rules but unable to contest them |
| capture channel | “coordination” becomes a vocabulary for expert rule, donor conditionality, security exceptionalism, or symbolic treaties with no implementation spine |
| repair | keep mandates narrow and explicit; publish registries and scientific bases; require state consent plus affected-community channels; separate alerting from ordinary administration; fund implementation rather than merely issuing duties |

The global layer fails both when it pretends to be a world state and when it pretends coordination is enough without teeth.

### 9. Require electoral and minority stress before calling any tier democratic

A tier is not democratically adequate merely because it has elections. Opposition should ask whether electoral administration, districting, campaign finance, minority voice, language access, turnout inequality, and complaint routes make the mandate legible. This applies at municipal, metropolitan, regional, national, and compact-linked assembly levels.

The archive should therefore treat representation as a live evidence question, not an adjective.

### 10. Convert failed opposition into status demotion

When a default repeatedly fails its opposition tests, it should be demoted:

| Failure pattern | Status action |
| --- | --- |
| one case exception | preserve default; add case diagnosis |
| repeated sector failure | demote to strong default with sector carve-out |
| repeated scope failure | demote to threshold frontier |
| capture by design language | rewrite default and add capture warning |
| source failure | repair source waist before more doctrine |

A doctrine that cannot be demoted is dogma, not research.

## Failure modes

- **friendly opposition**: naming weak objections that make the archive look balanced while protecting the favored rule.
- **case laundering**: treating one successful example of a tier as proof that every similar tier deserves the same powers.
- **rights-floor overreach**: using equality language to justify unnecessary national absorption.
- **local-romance overreach**: treating proximity as if it guarantees inclusion.
- **technical-compact drift**: letting harmonization, interoperability, or expert standards become hidden constitutional law.
- **global-waist theater**: building registries and declarations that lack implementation resources, review, or compliance pressure.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Can the default name the person or group most likely to be harmed if applied mechanically?
2. Can it name an institutional actor likely to exploit the default’s language?
3. Does it have a falsifier that would actually change the archive’s recommendation?
4. Does it distinguish lack of capacity from lack of legitimacy?
5. Does it preserve a repair route short of either abolition or unlimited thickening?
6. Does it tell future case packets what evidence to collect?

## Reconstruction instruction

Future scope notes should cite this note whenever they strengthen a default. A new default that lacks an opposition brief should be held as a hypothesis until the opposition fields are filled.
