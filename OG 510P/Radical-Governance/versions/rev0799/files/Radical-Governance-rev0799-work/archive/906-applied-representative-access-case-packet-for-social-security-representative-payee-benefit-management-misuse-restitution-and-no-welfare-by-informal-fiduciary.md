# 906 — Applied representative-access case packet for Social Security representative payee: benefit management, misuse, restitution, and no welfare by informal fiduciary

## One-line thesis

The Social Security representative payee programme should be classified as a payment-fiduciary representative-access waist: SSA appoints a person or organization to receive and manage Social Security or SSI payments for a beneficiary who cannot manage them, but the appointment does not transfer ownership of the funds, erase beneficiary voice, authorize non-SSA financial control, or prove welfare delivery merely because money reached the payee.

## Why this matters

SSA's representative-payment programme is a mature example of the representative-access problem. It exists because some beneficiaries cannot manage or direct management of benefit payments. SSA appoints a suitable payee, often family or friends and sometimes qualified organizations. That makes the payee route protective. It is also governance-significant because the payee receives public money and decides how it is used for the beneficiary.

The programme also shows why representation must be scoped. SSA materials distinguish incapability for benefit-payment management from legal incompetence, and they do not treat a state-law guardian or power of attorney as automatically sufficient for handling Social Security or SSI payments. A payee is appointed to manage SSA payments only. Misuse is not a private family disagreement; SSA rules treat misuse as repayment and restitution territory, with different repayment obligations depending on the kind of payee and agency negligence.

The honest form is therefore not “family help” and not “full guardianship.” It is a programme-specific payment-fiduciary waist with appointment, accounting, review, misuse, restitution, and replacement controls.

### 2. Boundary map

| Component | Boundary rule |
| --- | --- |
| beneficiary entitlement | remains the beneficiary's entitlement, not the payee's asset |
| payment receipt | payee may receive payments only under SSA appointment |
| spending | must be for current and foreseeable needs and best interest of beneficiary |
| conserved funds | remain beneficiary funds and need traceable account treatment |
| power of attorney | does not by itself authorize receipt and negotiation of SSA or SSI payments |
| legal guardianship | relevant evidence but not automatic payee selection in every case |
| beneficiary voice | incapability for payment management should not erase complaint, change, or restoration routes |
| misuse | creates restitution, overpayment, repayment, corrective-action, and possible prosecution paths |

### 5. Representative-access docket

This case activates the representative-access tests:

1. **Appointment basis** — record the evidence that the beneficiary cannot manage or direct payment management.
2. **Least-restrictive scope** — state that payee authority is over SSA / SSI payments, not all life decisions.
3. **Beneficiary voice** — preserve direct complaint, change-payee, direct-payment, and misuse-report routes.
4. **Fiduciary accounting** — require records of received, spent, and conserved funds.
5. **Current-needs test** — ask whether food, shelter, medical, personal, and foreseeable needs were met.
6. **Conserved-funds trail** — show beneficiary ownership, account titling, transfer, and closeout.
7. **Review and site visit** — preserve P&A, SSA, accounting, and beneficiary-interview records.
8. **Misuse restitution** — separate payee liability, SSA repayment duty, negligence, restitution, and beneficiary repair.
9. **Replacement and restoration** — show how the payee changes or direct payment resumes.
10. **Non-SSA authority boundary** — prevent POA, guardian, and payee labels from laundering one another.

### 6. Opposition and rival readings

**Rival 1: Payees prevent financial exploitation and unmet needs.** The archive accepts this. The repair target is not the existence of payees; it is unscoped fiduciary power and weak beneficiary records.

**Rival 2: Family members are often the most humane payees.** Often true. But family status does not substitute for spending records, beneficiary voice, and misuse remedies.

**Rival 3: Detailed accounting burdens small payees.** Also true. The answer may be risk-sensitive review, not no review.

**Rival 4: State guardianship or power of attorney should be enough.** Not always. SSA has a programme-specific payment duty and may need its own appointment and best-interest judgment.

### 7. Capture and theater channels

- **informal-fiduciary theater**: payment control is treated as ordinary family help.
- **welfare-by-payment theater**: payment to the payee is counted as support to the beneficiary without unmet-needs proof.
- **capacity-overreach theater**: inability to manage payments is treated as inability to complain or participate.
- **accounting-lite theater**: annual forms or exemptions replace meaningful records in risky cases.
- **guardian-fusion theater**: state-law guardianship or POA is treated as automatically equivalent to SSA payeeship.
- **misuse-repair theater**: restitution owed by the payee is confused with actual repayment to the beneficiary.

### 8. Repair surfaces

This packet remains open until the record can show:

- appointment evidence;
- beneficiary wishes and direct-contact route where possible;
- payee suitability and conflict checks;
- payment receipt and spending ledger;
- conserved-funds account trail;
- accounting or review status;
- misuse allegation and restitution route;
- corrective-action record;
- payee-change and direct-payment restoration path;
- treatment of POA, guardian, and non-SSA authority boundaries.

## Anti-theater tests

This packet fails if the archive treats “family member,” “organization,” “payee report,” “best interests,” “guardian,” or “power of attorney” as enough. It passes only when payment control, beneficiary ownership, spending, accounting, review, misuse, restitution, and restoration are all named.

## Holding

Social Security representative payees are a payment-fiduciary representative-access waist. The rule is: **no welfare by informal fiduciary**. A payee can protect a beneficiary only if the public record preserves the beneficiary's ownership, needs, voice, review, and repair routes.
