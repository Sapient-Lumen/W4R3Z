# 896 — Applied entitlement-continuity case packet for UK Universal Credit managed migration: migration notices, transitional protection, legacy closures, and no income support by deadline letter

## One-line thesis

Universal Credit managed migration should be classified as a deadline-triggered entitlement-continuity and benefit-conversion case: the migration notice is a lawful operating waist, but income-support continuity is not proven unless the record separates claim made, transitional protection awarded, not-claimed closure, in-progress cases, late claims, assisted support, vulnerability safeguards, appointee routes, and payment-gap remedies.

## Why this matters

Managed migration is not simply a platform migration and not simply a policy choice to replace six legacy benefits with Universal Credit. It is a mass conversion of existing income-support relationships into a new claim architecture. The state does not automatically move every claimant. It sends a migration notice. The claimant must claim Universal Credit by a deadline to continue receiving means-tested support and to preserve transitional protection, subject to extensions and special routes.

That creates a clean governance stress test for `894`: a deadline letter can become the practical public-power waist between old entitlement and new entitlement. A person may have had a legacy benefit, may still need support, and may even be potentially eligible for Universal Credit, but their income can still stop if the migration process is not completed.

The archive should not treat managed migration as inherently illegitimate. A unified benefit can simplify administration and reduce duplication. But a simplification claim is not continuity proof. The archive must see who received notices, who claimed, who did not claim, whose old benefit closed, who received transitional protection, who had enhanced support, who was late, and what happened to vulnerable claimants and appointee-managed claims.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Dispatcher result

| Field | Holding |
| --- | --- |
| trigger | migration notice, deadline, new UC claim requirement, transitional protection, legacy-benefit closure, enhanced support, late claim, and DWP / local / appointee roles |

### 2. Legal and operational boundary

| Component | Boundary rule |
| --- | --- |
| migration notice | the notice is the conversion trigger; it does not prove the person no longer needs or qualifies for support |
| claim deadline | the deadline is a high-risk public-power gate and should be tested for reach, accessibility, extensions, and good cause |
| legacy closure | closure after nonclaim is an administrative consequence, not proof that the person would have failed UC eligibility |
| transitional protection | top-up protection is a continuity control, but it depends on claiming in the managed-migration route and by deadline |
| enhanced support | support for selected groups must be logged as an operating control before closure, not described only as policy intent |
| appointee lane | where a person has an appointee or representative, the notice, claim, and responsibility route must be separately visible |
| local authority lane | housing benefit, supported accommodation, council tax, and local advice services may remain separate from UC and need handoff clarity |
| statistics | claim / nonclaim / closure statistics are useful but must be paired with reason, vulnerability, late-claim, and remedy evidence |

### 5. Entitlement-continuity docket

This packet activates these tests:

1. **Old-benefit / new-benefit status split** — show legacy benefit, migration notice, UC claim, in-progress, closure, and award states.
2. **Deadline reach and comprehension** — show notice receipt, reminders, language, disability, appointee, scam-confusion, and support signposting.
3. **Transitional protection proof** — show entitlement, automatic award, exceptions, erosion, and loss conditions.
4. **Nonclaim classification** — separate voluntary nonclaim, late claim, unreachable, confused, disabled / vulnerable, appointee failure, and eligibility-likely nonclaim.
5. **Enhanced support evidence** — record outbound calls, system checks, home visits, partner contact, and whether support occurred before closure.
6. **Payment continuity** — measure old-benefit stop date, UC first payment, advances, deductions, rent effects, and arrears / hardship routes.
7. **Late-claim / reinstatement route** — track extensions, good cause, backdating, late claims, complaints, and appeals.
8. **Local-benefit handoff** — test housing benefit exceptions, council tax reduction, supported accommodation, and local authority advice lanes.
9. **Statistics honesty** — use denominators that distinguish households, individuals, notices, claims, closures, in-progress cases, and transitional-protection awards.
10. **Learning loop** — use nonclaimant research and complaints to change notices, support, appointee routing, and closure gates.

### 6. Opposition and rival readings

**Rival 1: Managed migration is simpler and most claimants successfully claim.** The archive accepts that a high claim rate matters, but it does not settle the continuity question for the nonclaim and late-claim tail.

**Rival 2: People who do not claim have chosen not to claim.** The archive requires proof. Some nonclaim may be voluntary; some may be confusion, incapacity, contact failure, digital exclusion, appointee breakdown, or inability to complete the process.

**Rival 3: Transitional protection prevents cliff-edge loss.** The archive treats transitional protection as a real continuity control, but only for those who claim through the right route and meet timing / eligibility conditions.

**Rival 4: Enhanced support solves vulnerable-customer risk.** The archive treats enhanced support as evidence only when the trigger, contact attempts, assistance, and pre-closure outcome are recorded.

**Rival 5: Legacy benefits were ending by policy, so closure is expected.** The archive accepts the policy transition but still tests whether a deadline-triggered income stop is a proportionate and proof-backed way to move existing claimants.

### 7. Capture and theater channels

- **deadline-letter laundering**: a mailed instruction is treated as proof of informed choice.
- **claim-rate triumphalism**: successful claims hide nonclaimant harm and late reappearance.
- **transitional-protection headline**: top-up availability is treated as continuity despite deadline and route conditions.
- **enhanced-support theater**: support is described but not tied to individual closure gates.
- **household / individual denominator fog**: statistics switch units and obscure who lost what.
- **legacy-closure amnesia**: old benefit ends while local support, housing, and debt effects remain untracked.
- **digital-claim dependency**: the online claim becomes the hidden entitlement gate.

### 8. Repair surfaces

The packet remains open until these records are visible or expressly unavailable:

- migration-notice issuance, reminder, extension, and closure records by cohort;
- old-benefit and UC status mapping with household / individual denominator clarity;
- transitional-protection awards and loss / erosion conditions;
- nonclaim reason and late-claim evidence;
- enhanced-support triggers and outcomes;
- appointee / representative routing records;
- payment gap, advance, deduction, rent-arrears, and hardship evidence;
- local authority handoff for housing and council tax support;
- complaint, mandatory reconsideration, appeal, backdating, and reinstatement data;
- notice and support changes after nonclaimant research.

### 9. Holding

Universal Credit managed migration is a deadline-triggered benefit-conversion and entitlement-continuity waist, not ordinary web migration and not proof that nonclaimants no longer need support. The rule is: **no income support by deadline letter**. A migration notice may lawfully trigger action, but clean closure requires notice reach, assistance, transitional-protection proof, nonclaim classification, payment-continuity evidence, and remedy routes.

## Anti-theater tests

This packet fails if high overall claim rates, benefit-simplification language, or transitional-protection availability are used to erase nonclaim closures, late claims, vulnerable-customer support failures, appointee routing problems, or payment gaps.
