# 791 — Benchmark cards for ideal governments of each scope: vocation fit, kit floors, fiscal congruence, review lanes, tripwires, and no admiration without scoring

## One-line thesis

The archive should pair its canonical scope matrix and answer-key notes with one compact **benchmark-card format** for each scope — vocation fit, minimum complete kit, fiscal and capability congruence, spillover or solidarity fit, review and remedy integrity, participation and scrutiny proportionality, and named upgrade or handback tripwires — so real constitutions, reform proposals, and case studies can be compared tersely against the ideal stack without reopening the whole theory.

## Why this matters

`660` gives the archive a canonical matrix for what each scope should minimally contain. `700` gives a packet for stating an ideal-government claim. `701` gives a matrix for adjudicating rival packets. `632` gives trigger logic for when scope drift should force review. `790` now gives a short answer key for the whole stack.

What the archive still lacked was one compact object for a different task:

**how to benchmark a real arrangement against the ideal stack once the design argument is already mostly settled.**

That gap matters because the archive is no longer only inventing ideal forms. It is also judging proposals, constitutions, field cases, and hybrid regimes. Without a benchmark-card format, those judgments tend to sprawl in one of three bad directions.

First, they become **admiration by prose**: a system is praised as local, metropolitan, federal, or global without saying which concrete threshold it meets.

Second, they become **diagnosis without comparability**: one case is judged mainly by participation rhetoric, another by fiscal autonomy, another by whether the map feels familiar, making cross-case comparison thin.

Third, they become **repetitive**: each new case note restates the whole theory of what a municipality, metro, region, nation, or treaty body should be before saying the one thing that is actually distinctive.

The archive therefore needs one tighter instrument: not a fresh constitutional essay, but a **benchmark card** that asks the same few questions of every scope and makes both adequacy and failure legible at glance length.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Use benchmark cards after the answer is mostly known

A benchmark card is not the archive's first design instrument.

Use:

- `700` when the archive is stating a proposed ideal-government packet,
- `701` when rival packets must be compared,
- `790` when a reader needs the short answer for the whole stack,
- `792` when the question is how that arrangement performs under shock,
- and this note when the archive is asking **how close a real arrangement is to the ideal form it claims to instantiate in steady-state constitutional terms**.

The card is therefore an empirical and comparative instrument rather than a first-principles one.

When the archive first needs to say **which governments count as the right peers, what territorial unit is actually being compared, and why obvious prestige cases are being excluded**, it should use `798` before stretching the card into a comparator argument. When the archive instead needs to say **whether a live metro, pooling, regional-platform, fiscal-floor, or legitimacy-thickness threshold has actually been crossed before a benchmark can even be applied**, it should use `810` before treating the card as if the trigger question were already settled. When the archive instead needs to say **which institutional form is actually honest once that need is real — thin connector, shared-service backbone, bounded authority, or general-purpose tier —**, it should use `812` rather than pretending the benchmark card can settle form choice by itself. When the archive instead needs to say **who should occupy that form and which decisions require ordinary board power versus ratification or a second key**, it should use `813` rather than pretending the benchmark card can settle legitimacy-chain design by itself. When the archive instead needs to say **what fiscal chain honestly belongs to that form — dues, service payments, assigned revenues, equalization, reserves, or capital lanes —**, it should use `814` rather than pretending the benchmark card can settle shadow-treasury design by itself. When the archive instead needs to say **where the real operator home, records home, staffing spine, and continuity chain should live once that body is real**, it should use `815` rather than pretending the benchmark card can settle administrative-chain design by itself. When the archive instead needs to say **who actually owns strategic assets, carries debt, books reserves, bears contingent obligations, and takes the handback if the body changes shape**, it should use `822` rather than pretending the benchmark card can settle asset-and-liability-chain design by itself. When the archive instead needs to say **whether a body may honestly buy, franchise, concession, outsource, or rescue through contracts in its own name, and when those commercial instruments have become constitutional escape hatches rather than delivery tools**, it should use `823` rather than pretending the benchmark card can settle contracting-boundary design by itself. When the archive instead needs to say **whether a body may merely align standards and route cases, may pool permit and inspection support while principals keep legal title, or may hold its own openly bounded licensing, inspection, and sanction powers**, it should use `824` rather than pretending the benchmark card can settle regulatory-and-enforcement-boundary design by itself. When the archive instead needs to say **where scrutiny, audit, complaint, legality-review, and final remedy should live once that body is real**, it should use `816` rather than pretending the benchmark card can settle oversight-chain design by itself. When the archive instead needs to say **what participation chain honestly belongs to that form — open dockets, hearings, user panels, deliberative mini-publics, and published response duties —**, it should use `817` rather than pretending the benchmark card can settle participation-chain design by itself. When the archive instead needs to say **where the shared facts of that body should actually live — common indicators, service telemetry, case and record homes, official-statistics links, and publication discipline —**, it should use `818` rather than pretending the benchmark card can settle information-chain design by itself. When the archive instead needs to say **who belongs to that body, what legal and functional territory count, how accession or amendment should work, when exit is honest, and how the map itself should be reviewed or unwound**, it should use `819` rather than pretending the benchmark card can settle boundary-and-membership-chain design by itself. When the archive instead needs to say **what ordinary vote, qualified-majority, unanimity-reserve, quorum, ratification, deadlock, or emergency grammar honestly belongs to that body once it exists**, it should use `820` rather than pretending the benchmark card can settle decision-rule-chain design by itself. When the archive instead needs to say **what kinds of disputes that body should distinguish, where they enter, how they climb from negotiation to mediation, expert determination, regulator, tribunal, or court, and what continuity protection survives while they are live**, it should use `821` rather than pretending the benchmark card can settle dispute-resolution-chain design by itself. When the archive instead needs to say **how the benchmark should actually be carried out in practice — self-assessment, desk review, site verification, hearings, contradiction logging, and management response —**, it should use `800` rather than pretending the card itself is a review method. When the archive instead needs to say **who may sit in the judging chain at all, what firebreaks apply to host-picked experts or secondees, and when recusal is required**, it should use `807` rather than pretending that a good card automatically yields an independent mission. When the archive instead needs to say **how similar cases stay judged in a similar way across teams — score anchors, hard-case thresholds, precedent notes, and documented departures —**, it should use `808` rather than pretending the card itself already calibrates reviewer judgment. When the archive instead needs to say **how the resulting judgment should be expressed — lane by lane, with confidence and severity rather than as one blended headline score —**, it should use `801` rather than pretending the card itself already contains a verdict grammar. When the archive instead needs to say **how those findings are converted into owned repair work — response posture, named owner, dependencies, budget, closure proof, and overdue escalation —**, it should use `802` rather than pretending the card itself is already an action ledger. When the archive instead needs to say **how claimed completion is later verified, what counts as honest close-out, and when a supposedly fixed item must reopen**, it should use `803` rather than pretending the benchmark card can certify repair on its own. When the archive instead needs to say **how often** that benchmark should be refreshed, what drills should run between refreshes, or what public closure follows each review, it should use `793` rather than stretching the card into a maintenance calendar. When the archive needs to turn a weak score into a phased repair route, it should use `794` rather than pretending the card itself is an implementation plan. When the archive needs to say **what part of the benchmark judgment must actually be published outwardly**, it should use `795` rather than leaving the card as an internal scoring device. When the archive instead needs to say **who may object to the score, what can be paused, and where final independent review lives**, it should use `797` rather than overloading the card into its own appeal system. When the archive instead needs to say **how those several objects should be bundled back into one compact applied judgment for a real polity, proposal, or case study**, it should use `811` rather than leaving the verdict scattered across cross-references.

### 2. Ask the same seven questions at every scope

Every benchmark card should ask the same seven questions.

1. **Vocation fit** — does the scope actually match the class of problem it claims to govern?
2. **Minimum complete kit** — are the forum, executive spine, fiscal lane, review lane, and participation channel visibly present?
3. **Fiscal and capability congruence** — can the level actually finance and staff the mandate it carries?
4. **Spillover or solidarity fit** — does it honestly handle the externalities or equal-citizenship burden attached to the field?
5. **Review and remedy integrity** — can abuse, drift, illegality, or maladministration be challenged somewhere real?
6. **Participation and scrutiny proportionality** — is the democratic weight proportionate to the power exercised?
7. **Tripwires** — are there named upgrade, pruning, handback, or rescoping triggers if the arrangement drifts?

Those seven questions keep case judgments comparable without making them mechanical.

### 3. Keep the scoring typed, not theatrical

The archive should not pretend to scientific precision where constitutional judgment is still partly interpretive.

A benchmark card should therefore use a small typed scale such as:

- **meets the benchmark**,
- **conditionally meets**,
- **underbuilt**,
- **mis-scoped**,
- or **ornamental / shadow form**.

This is enough to preserve comparability without creating fake decimal objectivity.

### 4. Distinguish underbuild from mis-scope

A weak arrangement may fail for two very different reasons.

Sometimes the scope is still the right home, but the kit is too thin: no staff, no records, no formula finance, no audit, no review route. That is **underbuild**.

Other times the scope itself is dishonest: a municipality is asked to carry metropolitan spillovers, a region exists without platform work, or a treaty body quietly performs domestic-government functions it was never designed to own. That is **mis-scope**.

A benchmark card should separate these diagnoses rather than treating all weakness as one blur.

### 5. Benchmark the anti-cosplay limit explicitly

Every scope in the archive has an anti-cosplay rule.

- Micro-local bodies must not pretend to be little sovereigns.
- Municipalities must not be elective shells.
- Metropolitan systems must not hide in mayoral clubs or operator patchworks.
- Regions must not survive as decorative map furniture.
- Nations must not absorb the whole stack by guarantor rhetoric.
- Continental bodies must not drift into ambient upper-state power.
- Global institutions must not perform world-state theater.

A benchmark card should always include one explicit field for whether the arrangement has crossed its anti-cosplay limit.

### 6. Benchmark cards should be short enough to compare side by side

The archive should be able to place several cards next to one another.

That means a card should usually fit on one small table or one compact list. If the archive needs several pages to judge whether a metropolitan authority, region, or treaty body meets the benchmark, it should first ask whether the relevant field has been wrongly bundled and needs `702`, or whether rival packets have not yet been narrowed through `701`.

### 7. Use scope-specific pass signs rather than one universal institutional fetish

The benchmark should be shared, but the pass signs should still reflect the vocation of the level being judged.

A good micro-local body and a good national state do not look alike. The card should therefore preserve scope-specific signs of honesty rather than forcing one institutionally symmetrical ideal.

### 8. Pair every card with at least one tripwire for future review

The benchmark is not a permanent accolade.

Every card should end with one or more tripwires saying what would force reclassification. Examples include:

- metropolitan borrowing and distributive power deepening without a stronger forum,
- a municipality becoming grant-hollow,
- a region losing its platform work,
- a nation swallowing ordinary place government by emergency habit,
- or a global body widening from reporting and alerting into domestic administration without a fresh mandate.

That keeps the card connected to the archive's living audit logic in `632`.

Where a pass sign, tripwire, or threshold remains provisionally contested rather than fully settled, later archive work should now also treat `809` as the place to say so explicitly instead of smuggling a frontier judgment into the card as if it were timeless doctrine.

## Benchmark-card grammar

| Field | What the card should say | Typical outputs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Archetype claimed** | which scope the arrangement says it is | municipal, metropolitan, regional, national, continental, global, etc. |
| **Vocation fit** | whether the real problem class matches the claimed scope | meets / conditional / mis-scoped |
| **Minimum complete kit** | whether forum, executive spine, fiscal lane, review lane, and participation channel are visibly present | meets / underbuilt / shadow |
| **Fiscal and capability congruence** | whether money, staff, and operating depth match the mandate | meets / conditional / hollow |
| **Spillover or solidarity fit** | whether cross-border externalities or equal-citizenship burdens are honestly carried | meets / conditional / mis-scoped |
| **Participation and scrutiny proportionality** | whether democratic and scrutiny weight matches power exercised | proportionate / thin / decorative |
| **Anti-cosplay limit** | what form of constitutional overclaim is visible, if any | none / shellism / summitry / ornament / absorption / creep / world-state cosplay |
| **Tripwires** | what future changes would force reclassification | upgrade / pruning / handback / rescoping triggers |
| **Overall benchmark result** | compact synthetic judgment | meets / conditional / underbuilt / mis-scoped / ornamental-shadow |

## Scope cards

| Scope | Pass signs | Failure smell |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Micro-local** | direct reachable forum; tiny but real steward budget; minutes and publicity; visible upward escalation rights; no serious debt, police, or opaque patronage chain | miniature sovereignty theater or clientelist neighborhood shell |
| **Municipal** | elected council; accountable executive; professional administration; intelligible budget; meaningful own-source room or formula finance; complaint and review routes; open books | elective shell, discretionary-grant dependence, or opaque managerate |
| **Metropolitan** | public-legible metro forum; specialist executive core; shared fiscal base; planning and infrastructure records; audit and challenge lanes; region-wide participation on tradeoffs | government by summit, host-city proxy rule, or project cartel |
| **Regional / provincial / state** | real territorial-platform or shared-rule vocation; legislature or equivalent forum; executive departments; substantial finance; review, audit, and territorial accountability | decorative duplication, map nostalgia, or patronage geography |
| **National** | parliament; rights-bound executive; independent courts and integrity institutions; progressive taxation, social insurance, and equalization; constitutional conflict routes | guarantor sprawl, operational monopoly, or permanent emergency absorption |
| **Continental / plurilateral** | named treaty competences; bounded forum and secretariat; typed common finance; dispute, amendment, and exit rules; visible domestic scrutiny chains | competence creep by co-operation rhetoric or secret technocracy |
| **Global** | explicit treaty-bound vocation; assemblies, secretariats, courts, registries, panels, and compliance lanes matched to peace, commons, alerting, or interoperability tasks; no general domestic executive | world-state cosplay or empty forum theater with no operative narrow waist |

## Example card grammar

A compact card should usually be readable in a few lines:

| Field | Example entry |
| --- | --- |
| **Archetype claimed** | metropolitan |
| **Vocation fit** | meets: the labour market, transit spine, and flood-risk basin are already shared across the whole urban area |
| **Minimum complete kit** | conditional: there is a public chamber and specialist executive core, but review routes for regional planning remain thin |
| **Fiscal and capability congruence** | conditional: shared tax and fare base exists, but borrowing still depends on ad hoc ministerial approval |
| **Spillover or solidarity fit** | meets: trunk systems and housing-balance pressures are actually metro-wide |
| **Participation and scrutiny proportionality** | thin: residents can vote for the chamber, but committee and audit depth lag the authority's distributive power |
| **Anti-cosplay limit** | risk of summitry returning through operator boards and emergency deals |
| **Tripwires** | upgrade scrutiny if borrowing and allocation powers deepen; rescope if major functions drift back to host-city control |
| **Overall benchmark result** | conditionally meets the metropolitan benchmark |

The point is not this exact wording. The point is that the archive should be able to benchmark several real cases side by side without rewriting the entire stack each time.

## Guardrails

- Do not use a benchmark card before the arrangement's claimed archetype is clear enough to name.
- Do not score a case against a hidden or prestige-picked peer set; declare the comparator cohort first.
- Do not confuse underbuild with mis-scope.
- Do not let fiscal glamour hide review or remedy weakness.
- Do not let election alone satisfy the benchmark if the operating spine is hollow.
- Do not let technical capacity alone satisfy the benchmark if the democratic forum is too thin for the power exercised.
- Do not score a treaty body as if it were supposed to be a national state.
- Do not turn the card into fake numerics when typed judgment is the more honest instrument.

## Failure modes

- **admiration without scoring** — a regime is praised by narrative and labels rather than benchmark fields.
- **one-field romanticism** — participation, autonomy, or capacity alone is allowed to dominate all other fields.
- **scope confusion by weakness** — any failing arrangement is called mis-scoped when it may simply be underbuilt.
- **kit fetishism** — the archive demands the same visible furniture at every scale instead of scope-specific pass signs.
- **anti-cosplay amnesia** — the arrangement's overclaim limit is not checked explicitly.
- **frozen accolade** — a case is treated as benchmark-satisfying forever, with no tripwires for later drift.

## Compression rule for the archive

If the archive wants to say that a real constitution, reform proposal, or institutional case is **close to** or **far from** the ideal government for its scope, it should usually be able to do so with one short card naming the claimed archetype, vocation fit, minimum complete kit, fiscal and capability congruence, spillover or solidarity fit, participation and scrutiny proportionality, anti-cosplay limit, and future tripwires. If it cannot, the judgment is still too impressionistic to count as a stable archive comparison.
