# 700 — Minimum design packet for ideal governments of each scope: archetype, vocation, boundary, kit, fiscal proof, exception ladder, upgrade triggers, and no essay-length restatement

## One-line thesis

Any later archive note that claims what the ideal government at a given scope should be should default to one small **design packet** — name the scope archetype, state the vocation, define the boundary logic, specify the minimum complete kit, show the fiscal and capability proof, declare any exception ladder, and list upgrade or reversibility triggers — so the archive can compare designs, merge revisions, and stay compact without re-arguing the whole multilevel constitution every time.

## Why this matters

The archive already has most of the underlying doctrine.

- `579` gives the allocation grammar for deciding which functions belong at which level.
- `603` gives the order of operations for multilevel design.
- `628` through `631` give honesty, burden-of-proof, rescoping, and reversibility rules.
- `660` gives a canonical government matrix by scope.
- `661` through `664` give the main exception and cross-border ladders.
- `699` gives a reusable scope spine and label-to-vocation crosswalk.

What is still easy to lose, however, is **format discipline**. Later notes can still bloat by re-explaining all of that doctrine before saying the one thing that is actually new. The archive therefore needs a smallest useful object for future ideal-government claims: not another constitutional essay, but one **typed packet** that says exactly what level is being proposed, why, with what minimum machinery, under what exceptions, and subject to what later review.

This matters not only for size control but for judgment quality. Design claims become harder to compare when one note gives a full ladder, another gives only a slogan, and a third gives a sector-specific patch without saying which underlying archetype it assumes. A packet makes the claim inspectable.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Default to packet form whenever a note says what level should govern

If a note says a function should live at the municipal, metropolitan, regional, national, continental, or global layer, it should normally do so by filling out one packet rather than by narrating the whole stack from scratch.

The packet is the archive’s preferred compression unit for **ideal-government-by-scope** work.

### 2. Make the first field the archetype, not the local label

The packet should begin by naming the relevant scope archetype from `699`:

- micro-local,
- municipal,
- metropolitan,
- regional,
- national,
- continental / plurilateral,
- or global.

Only after that should it mention the public label used in the case at hand — county, district, province, state, union, authority, council, or anything else. This keeps names subordinate to vocation.

### 3. State the governing vocation in one sentence

The packet should then state, in one sentence, why this level is the honest home for the function. That sentence should use the archive’s scope-allocation grammar from `579` and the burden-of-proof rule from `629`: ordinary place, system geography, solidarity, guarantor duty, cross-border necessity, or another clearly named routing logic.

If the note cannot name the vocation cleanly, it is probably not ready to assign the function.

### 4. Name the boundary logic explicitly

A serious packet should say what geography or membership logic makes the unit real.

Examples:

- one municipality,
- the continuous urban labour market,
- the watershed or corridor,
- a constituent state within a federation,
- the whole national demos,
- treaty members exposed to the same cross-border externality,
- or all parties to a planetary-commons regime.

That keeps scale claims from floating free of the actual territory, population, or problem field they are supposed to govern.

### 5. Specify the minimum complete kit rather than the maximal wish list

The packet should give only the minimum complete kit needed for honesty at that scope, drawing from `660` and `565` rather than improvising fresh institutional furniture.

At minimum the packet should say:

- decision forum,
- executive or administrative spine,
- fiscal base,
- review or redress lane,
- participation channel.

Anything beyond that should be added only if it is genuinely sector-specific.

### 6. Show fiscal and capability proof, not just constitutional desire

A scope claim should not be treated as serious until the packet can say, in a few lines, how the level will actually be financed and staffed.

This should usually include:

- whether the mandate has its own-source base, formula transfers, common contributions, or treaty finance,
- whether the level has direct staff, shared services, pooled capacity, or delegated administration,
- and what minimum professional density is necessary to keep the arrangement from becoming democratic shell government or deconcentrated administration in costume.

### 7. Force an exception field rather than silently smuggling in special cases

The packet should contain an explicit **exception ladder** field.

Possible entries include:

- **none**,
- **collapsed stack** under `661`,
- **shared regime** under `662`–`664`,
- or a clearly named asymmetric arrangement already justified elsewhere in the archive.

This keeps unusual cases from being narrated as though they were ordinary default doctrine.

### 8. Name upgrade, review, and reversibility triggers in advance

Because honest scope design can change, the packet should also declare what would force later reconsideration.

Examples include:

- powers becoming more binding or distributive than first claimed,
- fiscal weight crossing from grant-funded coordination into full government,
- dependency on borrowed staff becoming chronic,
- territorial spillovers outrunning the current unit,
- or a problem-class shrinking enough that handback becomes possible.

This field should cite the archive’s upgrade, rescoping, and reversibility doctrine from `628`, `630`, and `631` rather than inventing one-off transition stories.

### 9. Distinguish settled doctrine from local delta

The packet should end with one short **delta** field: what is special about this function or case beyond the archive’s default doctrine.

That delta might be:

- unusual indigenous or treaty-grounded authority,
- a capital-city asymmetry,
- a basin-specific ecological spillover,
- a global risk class requiring inspection rather than reporting alone,
- or some other genuinely new design pressure.

If the delta is empty, the note probably does not need a full new note.

## Minimum design packet template

| Field | What the note should say | Default archive anchor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Archetype** | which of the seven default scope archetypes this body or function belongs to | `699` |
| **Public label** | what the jurisdiction or body is locally called, without treating the label as theory | `699` |
| **Vocation** | the one-sentence reason this scope is the honest home for the function | `579`, `629` |
| **Boundary logic** | what geography, membership rule, or problem field defines the unit | `603` |
| **Minimum complete kit** | decision forum, executive spine, fiscal base, review lane, participation channel | `660`, `565` |
| **Fiscal and capability proof** | how money, staff, and professional density match the mandate | `581`, `582`, `603` |
| **Exception ladder** | none, collapsed stack, shared regime, or another already-justified asymmetry | `661`–`664` |
| **Upgrade / review triggers** | what conditions would force constitutional upgrade, rescoping, or handback review | `628`, `630`, `631`, `632` |
| **Sector-specific delta** | what is genuinely new here beyond default archive doctrine | varies |

## Packet example grammar

A later note should usually be compressible into something like this:

| Field | Example entry |
| --- | --- |
| **Archetype** | metropolitan |
| **Public label** | county |
| **Vocation** | shared urban transport, housing-balance, and flood-risk systems already operate across municipal borders, so the honest home is the metropolitan tier rather than each municipality alone |
| **Boundary logic** | continuous labour market plus shared trunk-infrastructure basin |
| **Minimum complete kit** | directly legible decision forum, specialist executive core, shared fiscal base, planning and audit review routes, region-wide participation channel |
| **Fiscal and capability proof** | formula contributions plus fare revenue; own transport and planning staff; pooled data and emergency operations capacity |
| **Exception ladder** | none |
| **Upgrade / review triggers** | upgrade if distributive and borrowing powers deepen without direct legitimacy; review for handback if the shared systems fragment or shrink materially |
| **Sector-specific delta** | basin-wide resilience obligations require stronger ecological and emergency interfaces than the baseline metro kit assumes |

The point is not this exact wording. The point is that the archive should be able to **read the institutional claim at packet length** before deciding whether more prose is warranted.

## Guardrails

- Do not let the public label substitute for the archetype field.
- Do not let “ideal” claims skip fiscal and capability proof.
- Do not hide collapsed-stack or shared-regime cases inside ordinary territorial language.
- Do not write a full new ladder when a packet plus one local delta would do.
- Do not leave upgrade and reversibility triggers implicit.
- Do not add a new note when the only content is restatement of `565`, `603`, `660`, or `699`.

## Failure modes

- **packetless theory** — a note makes a strong scope claim but never states the minimum inspectable fields.
- **label drift** — county, district, province, or union is treated as self-explanatory.
- **romantic assignment** — a level is chosen by ideological preference without boundary, fiscal, or capability proof.
- **exception smuggling** — an unusual case depends on collapsed-stack or shared-regime logic but never says so.
- **upgrade amnesia** — a body receives more power over time while the note still describes the smaller original kit.
- **archive essay inflation** — new notes expand horizontally by repeating settled doctrine instead of naming only the local delta.

## Practical tests

A future ideal-government note is using packet discipline honestly when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Does it name the scope archetype before the local label?
2. Does it say why this level is the honest home for the function in one sentence?
3. Could a reader reconstruct the minimum complete kit without inferring hidden institutional furniture?
4. Does it show how finance and capability match the mandate?
5. Does it declare whether an exception ladder applies?
6. Does it name what would trigger upgrade, rescoping, or handback review?
7. Is the genuinely new content small enough to identify as a delta rather than a whole fresh constitutional cosmology?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a later note can say **which level it favors** but cannot provide **one packet naming the archetype, vocation, boundary logic, minimum complete kit, fiscal and capability proof, exception ladder, upgrade or reversibility triggers, and the actual sector-specific delta**, then it still has **an ideal-government preference without a reusable design object for the archive**.
