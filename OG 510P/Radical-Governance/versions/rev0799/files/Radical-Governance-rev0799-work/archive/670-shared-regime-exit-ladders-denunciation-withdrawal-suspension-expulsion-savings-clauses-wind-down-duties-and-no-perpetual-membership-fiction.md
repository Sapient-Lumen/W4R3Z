# 670 — Shared-regime exit ladders: denunciation, withdrawal, suspension, expulsion, savings clauses, wind-down duties, and no perpetual-membership fiction

## One-line thesis

Shared regimes should publish a **typed exit ladder** that distinguishes withdrawal or denunciation, temporary suspension, lawful expulsion where the constituting text allows it, module exit, territorial rollback, and post-exit survival clauses — with trigger, notice lane, decision body, effective date, pending-case rule, continuing liabilities, record duties, and re-entry path — so participation can end without pretending either that membership is irrevocable or that exit erases prior obligations.

## Why this matters

Once a regime has an honest entry constitution (`669`), it also needs an honest way out.

The archive already addresses compliance (`666`), differentiated obligations (`667`), and change over time (`668`). But those notes still leave one practical constitutional problem under-specified:

**what happens when participation is interrupted, suspended, or terminated?**

Without a typed exit ladder, regimes drift toward two opposite fictions.

The first is **perpetual-membership fiction**: the public is told that entry is all but irreversible even when the legal text plainly permits withdrawal, denunciation, or partial exit.

The second is **instant-amnesia fiction**: once a participant announces departure, governments behave as though prior obligations, pending review, accrued financial duties, historical violations, or shared operational unwind all vanish immediately.

Both are constitutionally dishonest. Exit is often lawful. But exit is rarely consequence-free. Some regimes allow temporary suspension of rights. Some charters allow expulsion only through very specific procedures. Some treaty systems permit denunciation only after a minimum period and with notice. Some instruments make clear that withdrawal does not erase responsibility for acts that occurred before the effective date. Nearly all serious regimes need some wind-down discipline for money, data, records, cases, inspections, or shared assets.

This note therefore adds one compact doctrine for **exit discipline**.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Distinguish kinds of exit instead of calling every break a "withdrawal"

Shared regimes should keep separate at least these conditions:

1. withdrawal or denunciation by the participant,
2. temporary suspension of rights or privileges,
3. lawful expulsion where the constituting text permits it,
4. termination of only an optional protocol or deeper module,
5. territorial contraction or rollback,
6. and lapse of provisional application before full entry.

Each carries a different trigger, authority, and legal residue.

### 2. Start from the constituting text and applicable law, not from political wish

A participant leaves, or is suspended, only through the lane the constituting instrument or applicable treaty law actually allows.

The archive should therefore reject three shortcuts:

- declaring exit where no lawful notice or procedure has been completed,
- treating political non-cooperation as if it automatically dissolved legal status,
- and inventing expulsion powers that the regime text never granted.

Exit discipline begins with source discipline.

### 3. Use notice periods and effective dates as part of the constitution, not as clerical detail

Where a regime allows withdrawal or denunciation, the notice period matters because it preserves time for response, replacement planning, and legal certainty.

The archive should therefore require public display of:

- the notice date,
- the legal basis,
- the countdown to effectiveness,
- and any minimum membership period or other threshold that had to be satisfied first.

A system that shows only "withdrawing" or "withdrawn" hides the legally important middle state.

### 4. Keep suspension temporary, proportionate, and visibly distinct from expulsion

Suspension is not the same as exit. It should therefore say what rights are suspended, what obligations continue, what the trigger was, and what cure or review path exists.

The archive should resist suspension becoming a stealth form of expulsion where the participant remains nominally inside the regime but is left in indefinite rights-darkness with no route to restoration.

### 5. Use expulsion only where the constituting instrument expressly authorizes it

Some organizations or charters provide explicit expulsion powers. Others do not.

Where expulsion exists, the archive should require the regime to name:

- the legal standard,
- the proposing body,
- the deciding body,
- any recommendation prerequisite,
- the vote rule,
- and the effective date.

A regime without an express expulsion clause should not pretend that public outrage or secretariat displeasure created one.

### 6. Preserve accrued obligations and past accountability unless the text says otherwise

Exit should not normally erase:

- financial arrears already due,
- reporting duties attached to past periods,
- pending disputes or reviews,
- investigative or inspection residue,
- record-transfer duties,
- or responsibility for acts that occurred before the effective exit date.

The archive should therefore favor explicit **savings clauses** and pending-matter rules.

### 7. Give module exit and territorial rollback their own lanes

Leaving an optional protocol, annex regime, or deeper club is not necessarily the same as leaving the parent regime. Nor is territorial contraction necessarily the same as full regime exit.

The archive should insist that the public can tell whether the participant has:

- left the whole regime,
- left only an optional module,
- narrowed territorial application,
- or ceased provisional application before ever becoming a full party.

Otherwise the map of obligation becomes unreadable precisely when it is most contested.

### 8. Maintain one visible wind-down packet for operational disentanglement

Even where exit is legally clear, operational residue usually remains.

A serious regime should therefore publish or maintain, as appropriate:

- outstanding money obligations,
- pending cases or investigations,
- record and data handback duties,
- treatment of shared assets or jointly funded facilities,
- continuing confidentiality or document-protection duties,
- and the responsibility for notifying affected users, firms, or other governments.

Legal exit without operational winding-down is how regimes create hidden continuity crises.

### 9. Separate effect on rights from effect on obligations

Some exits mainly remove participation rights such as voting, representation, or access to benefits. Others terminate future obligations but leave prior liabilities standing. Others suspend rights while preserving most duties.

The archive should force regimes to declare those effects separately rather than letting one word — "suspension", "withdrawal", or "termination" — obscure the legal bundle.

### 10. Keep a canonical status register from notice through post-exit residue

One official register should let outsiders follow the whole exit sequence:

- notice given,
- review or cure phase,
- suspension if any,
- effective exit date,
- survival clauses and pending matters,
- wind-down completion,
- and any later re-entry.

This is the exit-side counterpart to the participation register required in `669`.

### 11. Give re-entry its own path instead of treating cure as automatic restoration

After withdrawal, expulsion, or lapse, re-entry should occur only through the regime's lawful admission path.

The archive should therefore reject assumptions that repayment alone, later good behaviour alone, or a political declaration alone automatically restores full participation. Re-entry may require a fresh application, new deposit, new approval, or satisfaction of explicit reinstatement conditions.

## Typed exit ladder

| rung | what it is for | minimum public fields | anti-theater warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| lapse of provisional application | end temporary application before full party status | legal basis, notice date, end date, residual duties | pretending no obligations ever attached |
| withdrawal / denunciation | participant exits under the treaty or charter lane | notice date, legal basis, effective date, minimum waiting rule if any | acting as though announcement alone completed exit |
| suspension of rights | temporary consequence or protection short of exit | triggering rule, suspended rights, continuing obligations, review path | using suspension as indefinite stealth expulsion |
| expulsion | forced removal where the text expressly permits it | legal standard, proposing body, deciding body, vote rule, effective date | inventing expulsion power from anger alone |
| module exit / territorial rollback | narrow exit to a protocol, annex system, or territory | affected module or territory, notice lane, effective date, relation to parent regime | describing partial exit as either no change or total departure |
| post-exit residue / savings phase | preserve accrued obligations and pending matters after exit takes effect | arrears, pending cases, records, inspections, confidentiality, wind-down owner | treating effective exit as legal amnesia |

## Failure modes

- **false irrevocability**: the regime pretends participation can never lawfully end even though the text provides an exit lane.
- **instant amnesia**: governments act as though departure cancels past liability, arrears, or pending review overnight.
- **stealth expulsion**: an entity is functionally pushed out through indefinite suspension without the formal protections of an expulsion clause.
- **partial-exit fog**: nobody can tell whether the participant left the whole regime, only a module, only provisional status, or only a territory.
- **wind-down neglect**: records, data, shared assets, and affected users are left without a continuity plan.
- **automatic-restoration myth**: later political warmth is treated as if it recreated membership without a lawful re-entry act.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Does the regime distinguish withdrawal, suspension, expulsion, module exit, territorial rollback, and lapse of provisional application?
2. Can the public see the exact legal basis and effective date of the exit step?
3. Are notice periods and any minimum membership thresholds visible?
4. Where suspension is used, are the suspended rights and continuing obligations clearly separated?
5. If expulsion is claimed, does the constituting text actually authorize it?
6. Are pending cases, arrears, prior violations, and record duties preserved through a savings rule or equivalent lane?
7. Can outsiders tell whether the exit affected the whole regime or only an optional module or territory?
8. Is there a defined re-entry path rather than a myth of automatic restoration?

## Closing synthesis

If a shared regime can say **who entered** but cannot say **how departure works, when it becomes effective, what rights stop, what obligations survive, what operational wind-down remains, and how re-entry could lawfully occur**, then it still has **membership language without an honest exit constitution**.
