# 893 — Applied ecological-personhood negative control for Ganga / Yamuna: stayed juristic personhood, guardian liability, and no river repair by impracticable personhood

## One-line thesis

The Ganga / Yamuna case is the archive's negative-control packet for ecological personhood: a court can recognize rivers as living legal persons and appoint state officials as guardians, yet the order may fail governance tests when jurisdiction, guardian liability, basin complexity, implementation capacity, and existing river-management duties are not made practicable.

## Why this matters

The archive needs a failure case. Without one, ecological personhood risks becoming a success-only category, where every rights-of-nature recognition is treated as deeper governance merely because the language is morally attractive.

The Ganga / Yamuna litigation shows the danger. The High Court order was powerful: it drew on river protection, public-interest litigation, existing river-management failures, sacred and cultural significance, and the idea that a river can be a juristic person. But the Supreme Court stay demonstrates the archive's central point: recognition without an implementation docket can become legally fragile. If guardians are state officials with unclear powers and potentially massive liabilities, if the rivers cross jurisdictions not fully resolved in the case, if existing management boards and pollution controls remain weak, and if ecological repair is not matched to money, records, indicators, and enforcement, then personhood becomes an unstable remedy.

This does not defeat Atrato, Mar Menor, Te Awa Tupua, or Taranaki Maunga. It makes them harder to evaluate honestly.

### 5. Ecological-personhood docket

This packet activates the negative-control side of the docket:

1. **Status boundary** — distinguish high-court recognition from stayed / unsettled legal effect.
2. **Guardian practicability** — test whether named officials have authority, time, budget, and basin reach.
3. **Liability boundary** — prevent duties and liabilities from becoming absurd or unbounded.
4. **Jurisdiction map** — map national, state, local, basin, and board powers.
5. **Management-board repair** — treat the statutory board failure as live, not solved by personhood language.
6. **Ecological baseline** — require pollution, sewage, industrial, mining, flow, and encroachment indicators.
7. **Remedy budget** — require cleanup and enforcement funding.
8. **Cultural-rights separation** — respect sacred and cultural importance without making religious framing the governance mechanism.
9. **Appeal and stay record** — track whether personhood is operative, stayed, narrowed, or redesigned.
10. **Alternative-form test** — ask whether a management board, enforcement injunction, or statutory river authority is the thinner honest form.

### 6. Opposition and rival readings

**Rival 1: the stay proves rights of nature are legally impossible.** The archive rejects the overbroad inference. The failure is about implementation design, not the concept as a whole.

**Rival 2: personhood was necessary because ordinary environmental law failed.** The archive accepts the frustration but requires proof that the new remedy is more implementable than the old one.

**Rival 3: appointing senior officials as guardians solves responsibility.** The archive rejects title-based accountability. Guardians need powers, money, records, and liability boundaries.

**Rival 4: the river's spiritual importance justifies extraordinary remedy.** The archive treats cultural significance as evidence of affected-community importance, not as a substitute for jurisdiction and repair.

### 7. Capture and theater channels

- **impracticable guardian theater**: officials are named but cannot govern the basin.
- **liability panic**: personhood creates unclear exposure that encourages appellate retreat.
- **jurisdiction omission**: interstate and basin-scale powers are not mapped.
- **management-board bypass**: an old statutory failure is left unrepaired behind new language.
- **sacred-river laundering**: reverence substitutes for ordinary pollution, sewage, and land-use records.
- **stay amnesia**: the stayed status is ignored in later advocacy or comparison.

### 8. Repair surfaces

A redesigned Ganga / Yamuna personhood model would need:

- operative legal status after appeal or statute;
- basin and tributary boundary;
- guardian powers, duties, conflicts, budget, staff, and liability shield;
- management-board constitution or replacement;
- national / state / local power map;
- pollution, sewage, mining, hydropower, flow, and encroachment indicators;
- cleanup and enforcement budget;
- affected-community and nonreligious user participation route;
- public records and compliance reports;
- stay / appeal / reauthorization history.

### 9. Holding

Ganga / Yamuna is a negative-control ecological-personhood packet. The rule is: **no river repair by impracticable personhood**. A strong court order can still fail if guardianship, jurisdiction, liability, indicators, money, and ordinary river-management duties are not made administratively workable.

## Anti-theater tests

This packet fails if the High Court recognition is cited as a successful rights-of-nature model without its stay, guardian-liability problem, jurisdictional complexity, and implementation gaps. It passes only as a negative control that makes future ecological-personhood design stricter.
