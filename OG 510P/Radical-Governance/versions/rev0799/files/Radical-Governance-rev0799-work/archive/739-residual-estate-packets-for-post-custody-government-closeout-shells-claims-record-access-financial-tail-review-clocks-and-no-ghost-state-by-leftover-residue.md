# 739 — Residual-estate packets for post-custody government closeout shells: claims, record access, financial tail, review clocks, and no ghost state by leftover residue

## One-line thesis

Once the archive can place named surfaces into temporary neutral custody (`736`), release them out again (`737`), and discharge the custodian itself (`738`), it should also keep one compact **residual-estate packet** for the narrower afterlife that sometimes honestly remains: a typed closeout shell for surviving claims, residual funds, record-access duties, litigation or audit tails, and other bounded obligations, with one public route, one residue ledger, one accountable owner, and one termination proof so a leftover estate does not quietly become a ghost state by residue.

## Why this matters

The recent ideal-government transfer cluster is now much stronger. The archive can say **where a function should live** (`726`-`728`), **who must ratify a move** (`729`), **what must survive it** (`730`), **how to witness cutover** (`731`), **how temporary hosting works** (`732`), **how to prove that bridge ended** (`733`), **how to test independent live operation** (`734`), **how rollback works when stabilization fails** (`735`), **what to do when neither ordinary holder is yet honest** (`736`), **how surfaces leave neutral custody** (`737`), and **what proof object stands the custodian itself down** (`738`).

What the cluster still lacked was the compact object for the honest but awkward case where **the custodian is gone, yet a narrow residual estate still remains**.

That case is common in real reorganisations and dissolutions. The live front door has moved, the new holder is operating, and the interim custodian can stand down. But some residue still survives: final claims remain open; one ring-fenced fund must pay closeout costs or legacy liabilities; one records-and-disclosure route must remain reachable; audit or litigation holds still bind; warranties, retentions, or pension-like tails have not yet expired; and a small closeout function still needs a lawful home. If the archive has no dedicated packet for that state, future notes are forced to smuggle the residue into vague phrases like “post-transfer matters”, “legacy issues”, or “closeout to follow”.

That vagueness is dangerous. A residue shell can quietly keep powers that belong to the new ordinary holder. A sponsor department can inherit the tail without any visible public route. A contractor or archive team can become the practical gatekeeper of the historical file. A claims pool can keep paying or denying matters through obscure internal rules. A temporary reserve can survive for years because nobody wrote the real termination test. The custodian is gone, yet a narrow shadow constitution still lingers in the leftover shell.

The archive therefore needs one more compact packet in the chain: a **residual-estate packet** for the period after custody discharge when a bounded residue remains and should be governed honestly as residue rather than narrated as either full extinction or ordinary government. This packet should not recreate the custodian under another name. It should do the opposite: keep the tail explicitly narrow, publicly legible, and review-bound until it can terminate, merge into an ordinary home, or reopen a wider lane. Without that object, the archive still permits **ghost state by leftover residue**.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Trigger a residual-estate packet only when full extinction would be dishonest but ordinary government would overstate the tail

A residual-estate packet should open only when the archive can already say that the **custodian itself has been discharged** or is being discharged, yet some bounded duties still remain that cannot honestly vanish overnight.

At minimum, the packet should distinguish:

- **financial-tail residue** — ring-fenced funds, reimbursements, recoveries, wind-down costs, retentions, or other closeout money still need a named owner;
- **claims-tail residue** — pending legacy claims, complaints, liabilities, or compensation routes still need an accountable handling lane;
- **records-tail residue** — access, disclosure, archival, preservation, or evidentiary duties still remain after ordinary operations moved;
- **assurance-tail residue** — audits, inquiries, reporting, or investigation follow-up still require one durable home;
- **contract-tail residue** — warranties, indemnities, surviving obligations, or residual supplier closeout still remain active.

If the residue class is not typed, the shell will sprawl.

### 2. Keep the residual estate surface-specific and smaller than the prior custody schedule

A residual estate should never be described as if the whole function remains in a twilight zone. The packet should list exactly which surfaces still exist in residual form and which ones do **not**.

At minimum, the packet should make clear whether residue still includes any of the following:

- a claims or liabilities ledger;
- a restricted payment or reserve account;
- a records-access and disclosure route;
- a litigation, inquiry, or audit file;
- a narrow supplier or warranty-management lane;
- or a final public notice and lookup lane.

Everything else should be explicitly marked as already transferred, retired, or extinct. No one should be allowed to use the phrase “legacy estate” to imply wider continuing authority than the schedule proves.

### 3. Name one accountable residue owner and its legal basis

Every residual-estate packet should say:

- who owns the residual estate;
- under what instrument or delegated authority it exists;
- whether the owner is a successor authority, sponsor department, trustee-like closeout unit, archive-holding department, or other bounded home;
- and what powers the owner does **not** possess.

The residue owner may be small, but it should still be lawfully visible. The archive should reject “someone will deal with it” as a constitutional answer.

### 4. Forbid the residual estate from reopening ordinary government by habit

A residual estate is not a second life for the old body. The packet should therefore block it from:

- adopting new policy unrelated to the residue;
- reopening ordinary intake for the live function;
- changing eligibility or rights floors except where a surviving legal duty requires a narrow correction;
- using residual funds or records custody to steer current operations;
- or extending itself simply because unresolved matters still exist.

If new operational work starts flowing into the residue shell, the archive should treat that as leakage back into ordinary government or as evidence that the wider settlement was not actually complete.

### 5. Keep one residue ledger that classifies every surviving matter

The center of the packet should be one **residue ledger**. That ledger should classify every surviving matter by type, owner, current state, next step, and closure condition.

At minimum, each ledger line should say:

- the residue class;
- whether it is monetary, records-based, procedural, contractual, or mixed;
- the accountable owner;
- the current amount, stock, or count where meaningful;
- the next review or action date;
- and the condition that will close, transfer, or escalate the matter.

Without that ledger, the residual estate becomes folklore again.

### 6. Ring-fence financial residue and tie it to explicit permissible uses

Where money survives discharge, the packet should require one ring-fenced account or accounting lane. It should state:

- what the funds may be used for;
- what they may not be used for;
- who approves payments or releases;
- what reporting and audit rules apply;
- and what happens to any remainder when the estate ends.

Residual money should not become a hidden discretionary budget simply because it was left over from transition.

### 7. Preserve records access and disclosure duties as residue, not as personal memory

Many residual estates exist because the live function moved but the historical file and its duties did not vanish. The packet should therefore say:

- where the authoritative custody-era and post-custody records now live;
- who answers disclosure, subpoena, discovery, audit, and archival requests;
- how the lookup path works for records created during custody or before settlement;
- and which access, redaction, or loan rules still apply.

A residual estate should never force later readers to depend on staff recollection, ad hoc mailbox forwarding, or vendor archives to reconstruct the public record.

### 8. Keep one public route that is narrower than the live service route

The public should not be told that a body is gone while still needing to guess where its legacy matters went. The packet should therefore keep one narrow public route for residual-estate matters only.

That route should state:

- what kinds of matter it still accepts;
- what kinds it no longer accepts because they belong to the ordinary live holder;
- what response clock applies;
- how unresolved residue can be escalated;
- and where the ordinary live function now sits.

This avoids both public-route amnesia and accidental re-creation of the old front door.

### 9. Put hard review clocks and a real termination test on the residual estate

Every residual estate should look temporary because it is temporary. The packet should therefore name:

- a periodic review date;
- the trigger for accelerated review if the residue grows, changes class, or starts attracting new ordinary work;
- the termination event or threshold;
- and the proof object that will finally retire the estate.

An honest residual estate may last a while, but it should never survive on the theory that “there are always a few legacy matters left”.

### 10. Convert, merge, or reopen rather than letting the residual estate quietly mutate

If the residual estate begins doing substantive new work, accumulates novel liabilities, changes its public route, or needs powers wider than closeout and memory, the packet should block silent drift. The next step should be one of three honest moves:

- **convert** the residue into a named ordinary home;
- **merge** it into an already existing authority with explicit ledger transfer;
- or **reopen** the wider settlement, release, or redesign lane if the residue proves the prior closure story was incomplete.

The archive should not let a closeout shell become a stealth institution by accretion.

## Guardrails

- Do not open a residual-estate packet for ordinary operating work that belongs to the live holder.
- Do not let the residue shell keep broad policy, licensing, supervisory, or front-door powers.
- Do not let residual money become an ambient discretionary fund.
- Do not leave records-access, disclosure, or audit routes implicit.
- Do not let residual ledgers disappear into case notes, finance systems, or vendor correspondence.
- Do not allow open-ended residue with no review clock or termination proof.
- Do not let the public confuse the residue route with the live service route.

## Failure modes

- **ghost state by closeout shell**: the old body is supposedly gone, but the residual estate keeps acting like a miniature government.
- **residue fog**: surviving matters exist, but nobody can say what classes they belong to, how many remain, or when they should end.
- **legacy-fund drift**: ring-fenced money becomes a quiet discretionary budget because its permissible uses were never narrowed.
- **records afterlife opacity**: the historical file exists, but access and disclosure routes depend on personal memory or improvised forwarding.
- **public-route relapse**: the residue contact point quietly becomes the effective front door for the live function.
- **tail inflation**: new ordinary matters are pushed into the residue shell because it is easier than rerouting them properly.
- **temporary forever**: the estate remains for years because no termination threshold or rereview lane was attached.

## Practical tests

A residual-estate regime is honest when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Is the residue class typed rather than narrated vaguely as “legacy issues”?
2. Is the surface schedule clearly narrower than the earlier custody schedule?
3. Is there one accountable residue owner with visible legal basis and visible non-powers?
4. Does one residue ledger classify every surviving matter and its closure condition?
5. Are residual funds ring-fenced with explicit permissible uses and audit rules?
6. Are records-access, disclosure, audit, and evidentiary duties given a durable home?
7. Is there one narrow public route for residual matters and a separate route for the live function?
8. Are review clocks and a final termination test attached to the estate?
9. If the residue grows or changes character, does the packet force conversion, merger, or wider reopening rather than silent drift?
10. Can the polity prove that the residual estate is a bounded tail rather than a hidden second life for the old body?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a polity can say **the custodian is gone but a little residue remains**, it should also be able to show one compact packet proving **what class of residue still exists, which surfaces it still touches, who owns it, what ledger tracks it, what money or records remain ring-fenced, what public and disclosure routes survive, what powers are forbidden, what clocks bind it, and what event will finally terminate it**. Otherwise it still permits **ghost state by leftover residue**.
