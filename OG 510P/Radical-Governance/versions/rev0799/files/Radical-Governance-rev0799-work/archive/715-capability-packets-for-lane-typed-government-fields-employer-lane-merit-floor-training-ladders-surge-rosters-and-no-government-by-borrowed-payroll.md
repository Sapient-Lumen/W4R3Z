# 715 — Capability packets for lane-typed government fields: employer lane, merit floor, training ladders, surge rosters, and no government by borrowed payroll

## One-line thesis

Once the archive has lane packets, interface packets, a control plane, operating modes, repair rules, sunset rules, evidence, challenge, participation, oversight, amendment discipline, delegation rules, and a fiscal packet for a field, it should also publish one narrow **capability packet** saying which lane is the employer of record and capability steward, what minimum in-house core the field must retain, what merit and qualification floor applies, what training and profession ladders sustain scarce skills, what secondment and surge rules are allowed, and what vacancy or churn triggers open repair or redesign — so the field does not rely on borrowed payroll, consultant memory, or permanent emergency staffing to remain governmental at all.

## Why this matters

The recent packet spine can now carry much of a whole field constitution without forcing every later note to rewrite a miniature theory of the state.

- `699` gives the scope spine.
- `700` gives the minimum design packet.
- `701` gives rivalry tests.
- `702` gives lane-splitting discipline.
- `703` gives interface packets.
- `704` gives the control plane.
- `705` gives mode packets.
- `706` gives repair packets.
- `707` gives sunset packets.
- `708` gives the evidence packet.
- `709` gives the challenge packet.
- `710` gives the participation packet.
- `711` gives the oversight packet.
- `712` gives the amendment packet.
- `713` gives the delegation packet.
- `714` gives the fiscal packet.

That sequence now explains who owns the field, how seams work, how the public can challenge it, how oversight reaches it, how change is ratified, how delegated delivery stays subordinate, and how the money constitution remains visible.

But one common real-world question still lacks a compact constitutional object:

**who actually carries the field’s living capability once the legal lane, the money lane, and the service promises are real?**

The archive already has broader doctrine here.

- `549` says civil service and the public workforce should run on merit, neutrality, capability, fair pay, and no patronage.
- `609` says real shared government needs its own administrative operating core rather than borrowed host machinery.
- `620` says shared territorial power needs a workforce home rather than borrowed payroll.
- `684` says shared regimes need a typed secretariat workforce constitution rather than patronage or permanent secondment fog.
- `713` says delegation is unsafe if the principal lane loses the retained capability needed to specify, inspect, and resume.
- `714` says a field’s money constitution should stay visible rather than hiding behind grants, one hidden payer, or reserve theater.

This note does not replace those. It compresses them into one reusable packet for the newer field-constitution stack.

Without a capability packet, fields drift in predictable ways.

First, they drift by **borrowed payroll**: the field looks real in law and budget, but the actual staff doing the work remain employed, supervised, promoted, or lent by some other ministry, municipality, donor vehicle, or operator.

Second, they drift by **consultant memory**: the field can write plans and buy systems, but the durable institutional knowledge of commissioning, inspection, records, data, procurement, workforce modeling, and emergency routing sits outside the public lane.

Third, they drift by **vacancy federalism**: constitutional promises are shared, but the actual capability to deliver them depends on which place can hire, retain, or poach scarce workers this year.

Fourth, they drift by **permanent surge staffing**: degraded or emergency modes remain nominally temporary, yet the field quietly lives on agency labour, emergency premiums, repeated secondments, or heroic overtime long after the shock that supposedly justified them has passed.

A capability packet exists so a field can have a people constitution as explicit as its authority, finance, and evidence constitutions.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Separate fiscal capacity from human capability

`714` already requires a visible operating base, equalization spine, reserve rule, and capital lane.

The capability packet does something narrower and later. It says what durable workforce and skill architecture the field uses once the design has been chosen and the money path exists.

A design packet can say that metropolitan housing needs inspection, commissioning, data, repairs, and emergency-placement capability. A fiscal packet can say which lane pays for those functions. A capability packet then has to say which lane is the employer of record, what minimum in-house team remains non-optional, how hard-to-fill roles are trained and retained, and what staffing stress opens repair or redesign.

If a note still says only that capability should be “adequate”, it has not yet described a governing field. It has described a hope.

### 2. Name one employer lane and one capability steward

The packet should begin by naming the **employer lane** for the field’s core public workforce.

That lane might be:

- the lead field authority itself;
- a clearly designated host body acting as employer under explicit delegated rules;
- a regional or national civil-service home that lawfully staffs several field authorities under one professional regime;
- or a mixed arrangement where frontline staff sit in one lane and commissioning, inspection, or strategic functions in another.

The packet should also name the **capability steward** — the lane responsible for workforce planning, role architecture, qualification discipline, and capability review.

If a field cannot say who employs the core staff and who owns the capability plan, then its people constitution is still occurring somewhere else.

### 3. Publish the minimum retained public core before discussing augmentation

A capability packet should say which capabilities the public lane must retain even when some execution is delegated, shared, or temporarily borrowed.

That minimum retained core will vary by field, but often includes:

- policy and eligibility interpretation;
- commissioning and commercial judgment;
- inspection, assurance, or safeguarding;
- records, data, and evidence stewardship;
- demand, backlog, and capacity modelling;
- emergency or degraded-mode command; and
- the ability to resume or replace failed outside delivery.

This rule docks directly to `713`. A field that delegates some work does not thereby outsource the capability to know what work it is buying or whether the public duty is still being met.

### 4. Declare the merit, qualification, and conduct floor

The packet should say what workforce floor the field requires for ordinary legitimacy.

That means naming, at minimum:

- which roles require open competition or equivalent merit discipline;
- what professional licenses, certifications, or role standards are mandatory;
- what conduct, conflicts, and disclosure duties bind staff;
- what temporary or exceptional appointment routes are lawful;
- and what clock or approval path prevents exceptions from becoming the real constitution.

A field may use probation, secondments, temporary appointments, or specialist hires. It should not become government by exception-filled staffing.

### 5. Distinguish four staffing lanes: core, specialist, surge, and temporary bridge

Fields often go wrong when all labour is treated as one workforce pool.

A normal capability packet should therefore distinguish at least four staffing lanes:

- **core workforce** for ordinary rights-bearing or continuity-bearing work;
- **specialist lane** for scarce expertise such as engineering, data, procurement, inspection, medicine, or legal craft;
- **surge lane** for lawful degraded or emergency reinforcement;
- **temporary bridge lane** for start-up gaps, transition, or bounded secondment.

This prevents governments from narrating chronic vacancies as mere flexibility, or from using emergency staffing as the disguised ordinary constitution.

### 6. Bind training, profession ladders, and succession into the packet rather than treating them as HR garnish

The packet should say how the field renews capability over time.

That usually means naming:

- profession homes or craft ladders for key role families;
- induction and mandatory training;
- continuing development and certification maintenance;
- succession rules for fragile or single-point roles;
- and whether cross-level mobility, secondment, or shared academies are part of the design.

A field that can only hire expertise from outside each time it needs it does not yet own the capability it claims to govern.

### 7. Keep secondments, agency labour, and consultant support visibly supplemental

Secondments, locums, agency staff, mutual aid, consultants, and volunteer reserve rosters can all be useful.

But the packet should say which of them are truly supplemental and what would count as unhealthy dependence, such as:

- core public functions staffed mainly by borrowed personnel;
- repeated extension of temporary appointments because ordinary recruitment cannot fill the posts;
- consultant teams holding the only usable process maps, data models, or operator knowledge;
- or repeated emergency premiums being used to maintain the field’s normal service floor.

A field may borrow capability. It should not become government by borrowed payroll.

### 8. Dock surge staffing to mode packets and handback discipline

The capability packet should connect directly to `705`.

For each degraded, emergency, or recovery mode, the field should say:

- what surge roster or mutual-aid mechanism may be used;
- who can trigger it and on what evidence;
- what training or credential checks still apply under stress;
- what fatigue, safety, or labour-relations rules still bind;
- and what handback rule returns the field from surge labour to ordinary staffing.

Otherwise emergency staffing becomes a ratchet: easy to enter, institutionally addictive, and politically easier to extend than to repair.

### 9. Make workforce evidence and fiscal truth face each other

The capability packet should connect directly to `708` and `714`.

A field should therefore track, at minimum:

- authorised versus filled posts;
- vacancy and time-to-hire rates;
- turnover and first-year attrition;
- reliance on temporary labour, secondments, or consultants for core functions;
- mandatory-training completion;
- credential gaps in safety- or rights-bearing roles;
- and the fiscal share consumed by premiums, overtime, or repeated emergency cover.

This keeps the field from sounding well designed in prose while actually living on hidden staffing stress.

### 10. Give the packet review triggers when the people constitution stops matching the field constitution

A normal capability packet should say what conditions force review, such as:

- repeated inability to fill core posts;
- unsafe training or credential gaps;
- chronic vacancy cover through temporary or surge labour;
- secondment dependence becoming normal rather than transitional;
- delegated slices revealing that the principal lane no longer understands the field well enough to supervise it;
- or equalization disputes that are really unresolved workforce-distribution problems.

Some of those triggers may open a repair packet. Others may require fiscal reset, delegation review, redesign, or amendment. The packet should say so explicitly.

### 11. Keep the packet small enough that later field notes can cite it rather than rebuilding public-workforce theory from scratch

A normal capability packet should fit in one compact block.

If a later note needs pages of prose to explain who employs the field’s people, what capability must remain public, what staffing lanes are lawful, how training works, and when stress staffing becomes structural failure, then the field still lacks a reusable people constitution.

## Compact capability packet

A normal capability packet should usually fit in one table-like block:

| Field | Employer lane and retained core | Training and surge rule | Review trigger and consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Housing** | metropolitan housing lane is employer of record for inspectors, repair commissioners, data and placement leads; emergency placement providers may be contracted but safeguarding, inspection, records, and prioritization stay public | mandatory induction on tenancy law, safety, safeguarding, and records; emergency shelter surge roster may open only during declared unsafe-stock or displacement events and must hand back within a fixed clock | repeated use of agency inspectors, consultant-led stock intelligence, or emergency-placement coordinators opens repair packet, fiscal reset, or redesign of provider mix |
| **Transport** | transport lane directly employs network planners, schedulers, safety oversight, contract managers, and incident command core; operators may employ drivers and maintainers under explicit reserved-power rules | profession ladders for planning, engineering, control-room, and safety roles; mutual-aid or emergency staffing may open only under declared disruption with fatigue and competence limits still binding | chronic overtime, thin route dependence on emergency staffing, or loss of contract-management capability opens repair, delegation review, or amendment of service floor |
| **Care** | public care lane remains employer or capability steward for eligibility, safeguarding, provider oversight, discharge coordination, and records even where some direct care is delivered by approved providers | training floor covers safeguarding, records, infection control, and rights routes; outbreak or provider-collapse surge staff may open only with visible supervision ratios and handback plan | repeated vacancy crises, uncredentialed cover, or provider dependence that strips the public lane of assessment or safeguarding capability opens repair, fiscal review, or re-insourcing decision |

The exact field content will vary, but the packet form should stay recognizable.

## Failure modes

- **government by borrowed payroll**: the field’s real operating workforce is permanently lent by another government, contractor, or donor vehicle.
- **consultant memory**: the public lane no longer holds the process knowledge, data craft, or commercial memory needed to govern the field itself.
- **vacancy federalism**: place-to-place hiring power quietly decides what rights or services exist in practice.
- **permanent surge mode**: emergency premiums, locums, agency labour, or mutual aid become the field’s ordinary staffing model.
- **exception-staffing drift**: short-term or non-merit routes quietly become the normal doorway into consequential public roles.
- **credential theater**: the field speaks the language of professionalism while tolerating persistent training or qualification gaps in safety-bearing work.
- **delegated capability collapse**: outside providers remain in place, but the principal lane loses the skill to specify, inspect, challenge, or resume.
- **repair by overtime habit**: structural failure is answered by another quarter of burnout, premiums, and moral heroics instead of a governed fix.

## Anti-escape-hatch tests

1. Can the field name one employer lane and one capability steward for its core public workforce?
2. Can it say which capabilities must remain public even when some execution is delegated or shared?
3. Does it distinguish core, specialist, surge, and temporary bridge staffing lanes rather than narrating all labour as one flexible pool?
4. Are merit, qualification, conduct, and temporary-exception rules explicit enough to stop staffing by folklore?
5. Are training, profession ladders, and succession part of the field constitution rather than afterthoughts?
6. Are secondments, agency labour, consultants, and volunteer reserve mechanisms visibly supplemental rather than silently constitutional?
7. Do surge staffing and mutual aid dock to the field’s mode packet with real handback discipline?
8. Can the field see its own staffing stress through vacancy, churn, training, and premium-cost indicators before collapse becomes normalized?
9. Do repeated capability failures route into repair, delegation review, fiscal reset, or amendment rather than another round of heroic cover?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a later note can tell you **which lane owns the field, how seams work, what evidence matters, how the public can challenge the field, how oversight reaches it, how delegated slices stay subordinate, and how the money constitution works** but cannot also tell you **who employs the core public workforce, what capability must remain inside the public lane, what training and profession ladders sustain the field, what surge staffing is lawful, and when borrowed payroll has become the real constitution**, then it still has **a field constitution that becomes vague exactly where public authority meets the people who must actually carry it**.
