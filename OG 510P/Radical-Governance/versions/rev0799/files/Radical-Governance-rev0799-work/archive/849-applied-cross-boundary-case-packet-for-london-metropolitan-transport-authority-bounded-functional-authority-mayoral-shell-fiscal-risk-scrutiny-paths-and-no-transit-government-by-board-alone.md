# 849 — Applied cross-boundary case packet for London metropolitan transport authority: bounded functional authority, mayoral shell, fiscal-risk scrutiny paths, and no transit government by board alone

## One-line thesis

A metropolitan transport system should usually be governed as a bounded functional authority inside a visible metropolitan democratic shell: London’s GLA/TfL arrangement is a strong working case for that rule, but it still exposes fiscal-risk, appointment, complaint, and strategy-scrutiny pressures that prevent a transport board from becoming a government by stealth.

## Why this matters

`848` told the archive to stop treating every boundary mismatch as proof of a new institution and to test the cross-boundary form chain against applied cases. This packet is the first test.

Metropolitan transport is one of the clearest cases where municipal boundaries do not match the public problem. Commuters, buses, rail lines, roads, fares, accessibility, air quality, capital assets, incident response, and long-range investment all cross local borders. A mere meeting of municipalities is often too thin. But a transport authority can also become a shadow government if it controls mobility, debt, development leverage, data, enforcement, and user access without a legible democratic, fiscal, and complaint path.

London is useful because the democratic shell and the functional transport authority are both visible: Londoners elect a Mayor and Assembly; TfL is a statutory transport body; the Mayor’s transport strategy and TfL’s business plan / budget create public planning and fiscal surfaces; Assembly scrutiny and London TravelWatch supply partial public-accountability and user-complaint routes. The case therefore tests whether the archive’s preferred form is **bounded functional authority inside metropolitan democracy**, not either municipal fragmentation or technocratic transit sovereignty.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Case object and verdict

| Field | Holding |
| --- | --- |
| case object | Greater London Authority metropolitan shell plus Transport for London as the principal functional transport body |
| claimed scope | metropolitan shared-system governance, not full general-purpose government by TfL alone |
| archive rule applied | `848` dispatcher; `812` form ladder; live chain notes `813`, `814`, `815`, `816`, and `817` |
| verdict | **keep and thicken narrowly** |
| short reason | the problem is a continuous cross-boundary transport system with public strategy, assets, fares, roads, accessibility, procurement, safety, and climate implications; the honest form is a bounded functional authority under a directly visible metropolitan executive and Assembly scrutiny |
| narrow thickening needed | clearer public fiscal-risk ledgers, appointment/delegation visibility, user complaint routing, and stronger Assembly-facing budget and strategy traceability |
| forbidden thickening | do not let TfL become an independent general-purpose metropolitan government or let transport-program decisions quietly settle housing, policing, land, or distribution questions outside their statutory and democratic routes |

The case is not a universal endorsement of the London model. It is a form-choice test: **bounded authority works only because the Mayor, Assembly, TfL Board, budget, strategy, audit, and watchdog lanes remain separable enough to inspect**.

### 2. Trigger proof under `848`

| Trigger field | London transport packet answer |
| --- | --- |
| boundary mismatch | movement, roads, transit networks, fares, accessibility, air quality, and capital projects cross borough boundaries and affect the whole functional metropolis |
| harmed population | riders, disabled users, workers, low-income households, freight and service users, borough residents affected by roads and emissions, and taxpayers exposed to capital and operating risk |
| incumbent failure | borough-by-borough control cannot honestly run a unified citywide transport network, fare system, road strategy, or long-range investment program |
| form family | bounded functional authority embedded in a metropolitan democratic shell |
| lower-form insufficiency | a connector or inter-borough compact cannot continuously operate assets, procure fleets and infrastructure, set network-level service standards, manage road-user policies, or carry systemwide fiscal risk |

This is a strong positive case for a functional authority. The lower-form test fails for the core network, but it may still pass for peripheral projects, borough delivery partnerships, or local street-design choices.

### 5. Form holding

The honest form is **bounded functional authority inside a directly visible metropolitan democratic shell** because London-scale transport is a continuous cross-boundary system that cannot be run honestly by borough coordination alone. The authority may plan, operate, procure, maintain, publish service data, implement the Mayor’s transport strategy, manage defined transport assets, and carry named transport finance within statutory and budget limits. It must not become a free-standing general-purpose government, use transport strategy to settle unrelated metropolitan distributive questions without proper democratic routes, or bury fiscal risk in technical board papers. Its legitimacy, finance, administration, oversight, and participation conditions are held by `813`, `814`, `815`, `816`, and `817`; it should be reviewed at each mayoral term, major business-plan revision, material capital-funding settlement, or serious service/fiscal shock.

### 7. Rival interpretations

| Rival rule | Why it is tempting | Why this packet rejects or limits it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| borough compact only | preserves local control and avoids metropolitan bureaucracy | cannot run the integrated network, common fare/user surfaces, major assets, safety, accessibility, and capital program honestly |
| independent transit corporation | protects operations from politics | hides fare, access, debt, climate, and land-use consequences from public metropolitan accountability |
| general-purpose metropolitan government everywhere | makes the democratic shell more complete | over-thickens if the live problem is one named system rather than a full territorial constitution |
| national department control | may stabilize capital finance | weakens metropolitan fit, user proximity, and strategy accountability unless limited to funding, standards, or emergency support |

The preferred rule is neither local fragmentation nor transit sovereignty. It is **metropolitan democratic authorization plus bounded operational autonomy**.

A case packet without a reopen trigger is only institutional admiration.

## Failure modes

- **board sovereignty**: treating a competent transport board as if operational expertise supplies democratic authority.
- **mayoral opacity**: routing every hard question through the Mayor while leaving delegation, appointment, and board discretion hard to inspect.
- **budget fog**: publishing large budgets and accounts without a resident-readable fiscal-risk ledger.
- **strategy sprawl**: using transport strategy to make adjacent housing, land, labor, climate, policing, or distribution decisions without the right public route.
- **complaint maze**: scattering user redress across operator, TfL, watchdog, ombuds, and legal paths without a clear lived route.
- **project exceptionalism**: letting one large capital project escape the ordinary chain because it is too technical, too urgent, or too politically sponsored.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Can a rider identify the complaint path without knowing the institutional map?
2. Can a taxpayer see where operating risk, capital risk, and fare risk sit?
3. Can the Assembly scrutinize a mayoral transport decision before irreversible commitment?
4. Can the authority explain which powers it does not have?
5. Can a borough see when it is a partner, objector, delivery body, or subordinate route?
6. Can a major project show its asset, liability, procurement, and handback chain without invoking the whole system’s prestige?
7. Can a mayoral appointee’s authority be traced to statute, delegation, strategy, and public record?
