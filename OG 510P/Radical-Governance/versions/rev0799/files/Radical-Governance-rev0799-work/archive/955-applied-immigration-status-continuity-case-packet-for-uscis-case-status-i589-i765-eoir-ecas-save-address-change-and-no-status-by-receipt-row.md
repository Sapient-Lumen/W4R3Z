# 955 — Applied immigration status continuity case packet for USCIS case status, I-589, I-765, EOIR, ECAS, SAVE, address change, and no status by receipt row

## One-line thesis

USCIS case-status tools, processing-time pages, Form I-589, Form I-765, asylum EAD-clock materials, USCIS and EOIR change-of-address rules, EOIR workload/case portals, SAVE and CaseCheck, and Respondent Access/ECAS sources are necessary immigration-status evidence lanes. They cannot be collapsed into proof that a person has current status, notice, work authorization, court safety, benefit/license access, or remedy.

## Why this matters

This applied packet turns the immigration-status docket into a source discipline. The sources are strong enough to show case tracking, processing-time context, asylum filings, work-authorization applications, asylum-clock effects, address duties, EOIR case/workload information, respondent portal limits, electronic filing, and SAVE verification. But each source has a narrow lane. A receipt number can exist, a case can be pending, a form can be uploaded, a court can list a hearing, a portal can show an eROP, or a SAVE response can verify something while the person still lacks notice, counsel, work authorization, a corrected downstream record, a safe hearing route, or a repaired benefit/license decision.

The governing repair phrase is **no status by receipt row**.

## Pattern pack

- **No status by receipt row.** USCIS receipts and online status are tracking evidence, not person-level immigration continuity.
- **No adjudication by processing time.** Processing-time pages and e-request thresholds are queue discipline, not proof that the case moved correctly.
- **No asylum protection by I-589.** Form I-589 must be joined to filing lane, acceptance, DHS service, evidence, interview/hearing, and appeal/remedy.
- **No work authorization by I-765.** A work-permit application or asylum clock must be joined to eligibility category, clock stops, approval, card delivery, renewal, replacement, and employer recognition.
- **No notice by address form.** USCIS and EOIR address changes are separate; proof requires agency update, service, and non-delivery handling.
- **No due process by Respondent Access.** EOIR portals provide bounded access and filing routes; they do not prove counsel, language, evidence acceptance, or safe hearing attendance.
- **No benefits access by SAVE.** SAVE helps agencies verify citizenship/immigration information; it is not itself a downstream benefit/license remedy.
- **No relief by contact center.** Service requests, inquiries, and portal feedback need traceable correction receipts before counting as repair.
- **No record continuity by one agency.** USCIS, EOIR, DHS enforcement, SAVE, state agencies, employers, schools, and benefits agencies may hold different operative fragments.

## Source-to-duty map

| Source surface | What it proves | What it cannot prove alone |
|---|---|---|
| USCIS Case Status Online | Receipt-number based status lookup and status-message surface. | Current lawful status, card delivery, RFE receipt, notice understanding, or downstream service access. |
| USCIS processing/e-request tools | Historical processing-time context and outside-normal inquiry path for certain cases. | Case correctness, officer action, expedited need, or successful remedy. |
| Form I-589 / USCIS asylum page | Asylum/withholding application surface and USCIS asylum information. | Protection, court acceptance, DHS service, hearing fairness, or work authorization. |
| Form I-765 / asylum EAD clock | Work-authorization application and asylum-clock effects. | EAD card in hand, renewal continuity, employer acceptance, or no gap in income. |
| USCIS AR-11 / address-change pages | USCIS address duty and E-COA/AR-11 route. | EOIR address update, notice receipt, or avoidance of missed hearing/order consequences. |
| EOIR Workload and Adjudication Statistics | Aggregate immigration-court workload/adjudication statistics subject to update. | Individual hearing safety, judge/court readiness, representation, or case remedy. |
| EOIR ACIS / Respondent Access / ECAS | Basic case lookup, eROP/filing routes, document acceptance/rejection, and limited portal functions. | Complete paper records, mobile-compatible filing, appeal availability, deadline relief, or counsel. |
| EOIR-33/IC | Immigration-court change-of-address route and warning consequences. | USCIS address update, actual notice, or safe hearing attendance. |
| SAVE / CaseCheck / response-time pages | Agency verification and applicant ability to track some SAVE checks. | Correct downstream eligibility, person-level record correction, or benefit/license restoration. |

## Applied continuity ladder

1. **Classify the consequence.** Status, hearing, removal risk, work authorization, benefit/license eligibility, school/employment, family unity, detention, or record correction.
2. **Bind the person and case.** A-number, receipt number, EOIR case, family/sponsor links, representative, address, language/disability needs, and urgent harm.
3. **Split source lanes.** USCIS benefit case, EOIR court case, DHS enforcement/custody, SAVE verification, employer/school/benefits agency, and portal surfaces must not be merged.
4. **Prove notice.** Address record, service lane, mail/portal/email/SMS, translated notice, returned mail, and deadline consequence.
5. **Prove filing acceptance.** Upload/submission, rejection/acceptance, filing date, DHS service, fee/waiver, biometrics, evidence, RFE/NOID, and receipt.
6. **Prove work authorization.** I-765 category, asylum clock, approval, card production/delivery, replacement, renewal, automatic-extension rule if any, and employer acceptance.
7. **Prove court continuity.** ACIS/Respondent Access/ECAS/eROP, hearing, filings, evidence acceptance, interpreter, counsel, appeal/motion, and outage/deadline treatment.
8. **Prove SAVE/downstream continuity.** Verification request, initial/additional response, agency decision, mismatch, CaseCheck status, record correction, and restored benefit/license/work/school access.
9. **Prove assisted access.** Representative authority, trusted helper, legal-aid handoff, paper fallback, language/disability accommodation, and portal/account recovery.
10. **Prove repair.** Reopened clock, accepted late filing, rescheduled hearing, stayed removal, corrected record, reissued card, restored benefit/license, and written after-action evidence.

## Case diagnosis

The core failure pattern is **receipt-row capture**: every system has a useful row, but each row only covers one institutional lens.

- A USCIS receipt or case status can prove that USCIS has a tracked matter; it does not prove status, work authorization, or EOIR court continuity.
- A processing-time table can help classify queue expectations; it does not prove that a person received notice, biometrics, RFE, interview, or decision.
- A Form I-589 can place a protection claim in motion; it does not prove DHS service, evidence sufficiency, court acceptance, or relief.
- A Form I-765 can request work authorization; it does not prove approval, card delivery, replacement, renewal, or employer recognition.
- An EOIR docket or portal can expose a hearing or filing lane; it does not prove that an unrepresented person could file, see the complete record, or avoid an in absentia consequence.
- A SAVE response can support an agency's eligibility check; it does not prove that the downstream decision, denial, or correction is right.

## Repair packet

A repaired immigration-status packet should include:

1. **Identity and authority spine:** A-number, receipt numbers, names/aliases, family links, representative, language/disability needs, and urgent consequences.
2. **USCIS case spine:** form, receipt, office, biometrics, RFE/NOID, processing-time posture, service request, decision, card/notice production, and address record.
3. **EOIR court spine:** NTA, court/BIA case, ACIS/Respondent Access/ECAS/eROP, hearing, filings, evidence, DHS service, interpreter, counsel, appeal/motion, outage, and address record.
4. **Protection and work spine:** I-589, asylum clock, I-765 category, EAD approval/card, renewal/replacement, employer or agency acceptance, and income/family effects.
5. **SAVE/downstream spine:** requesting agency, benefit/license/work/school decision, initial/additional verification, CaseCheck, mismatch/correction, and restored access.
6. **Remedy spine:** inquiry/service request, motion, appeal, reopened or stayed consequence, corrected record, reissued notice/card, and downstream repair receipt.

## Failure modes

- A USCIS receipt exists, but the person missed an RFE, biometrics notice, interview notice, or card-delivery problem.
- USCIS and EOIR address records diverge, causing returned mail, missed court, or in absentia risk.
- A Form I-589 is uploaded but not accepted, served, routed, or paired with evidence and interpretation.
- An I-765 is pending or the asylum clock is running, but the person has no usable EAD, renewal, replacement, or employer-recognized proof.
- ACIS or Respondent Access shows a case, but the person lacks the full record, counsel, language access, or a practical filing channel.
- SAVE verifies or fails to verify a status fragment, but the downstream agency never corrects the benefit/license/school/work decision.
- A portal outage, device limit, desktop-only form, file-size limit, or account lockout becomes a missed deadline.
- A service request or inquiry is logged without a corrected notice, reopened clock, reissued card, accepted filing, or restored downstream access.

## Negative controls

Do **not** activate this packet for ordinary immigration background material with no person-level status, notice, work, court, SAVE, benefit/license, or remedy consequence. Do activate it when any public body, employer, school, court, benefits agency, health provider, shelter, landlord, or contractor is tempted to say that a receipt, portal status, docket, clock, or verification row settles the matter.
