# 509 — Work and income security by scope: local access, regional labour markets, national social insurance, and transnational portability

## One-line thesis

An ideal multi-level polity should split work governance by function and scale: neighborhoods and municipalities remove practical barriers to participation and surface abuse; metropolitan and regional institutions govern the real labour market through mobility, matching, skills, and transition platforms; nations set labour law, collective-bargaining architecture, minimum standards, and social insurance; regional compacts widen portability and fair mobility where states consent; and global institutions anchor labour rights and migrant-worker protections without pretending to run every labour market from Geneva.

## Why this matters

A government can look fiscally competent and still fail the ordinary test of whether people can find decent work, change jobs without catastrophic loss, survive unemployment, bargain without retaliation, and age without destitution. Work is governed badly when every problem is handed either to the market or to one remote labour ministry.

The smallest scales see which bus route blocks access to jobs, which documents workers cannot produce, where wage theft is routine, which informal workers remain invisible, and how childcare or disability barriers destroy labour-market participation. But those same scales cannot stabilize the economy, finance unemployment insurance, or set a national floor for wages and worker protections. Labour markets sprawl across municipal borders, while social insurance and macroeconomic risk pooling usually require national solidarity.

An ideal government therefore needs a work-and-income constitution by scope. It should keep access and problem-sensing close to daily life, govern labour markets at the geography where matching and commuting actually happen, reserve labour rights and social insurance to levels that can enforce and pool them, and use regional and global layers for portability and rights rather than for fantasy world labour administration.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Start from decent work and income security, not from employment counts alone

The archive should treat work governance as a joint problem of access, bargaining power, security, and transitions. A polity that celebrates job creation while normalizing wage theft, dangerous work, unstable hours, or income collapse during shocks is counting activity rather than governing work well.

### 2. Give micro-local and municipal institutions barrier-removal and signal-surfacing roles

Local institutions can identify the practical frictions that keep people out of decent work: childcare gaps, unsafe commutes, inaccessible services, documentation barriers, language access failures, local discrimination, and the everyday invisibility of informal or casual workers. Municipal government can also shape local contracting, procurement standards, worker centres, and referral paths. These are valuable functions, but they are not substitutes for labour law, inspection architecture, or macro risk-pooling.

### 3. Treat the metropolitan or regional scale as the real labour-market geography

In most urbanized economies, labour markets are regional even when politics is municipal. Job matching, commuting, sector clusters, adult skills systems, transition support, and many public employment services should therefore be organized at the metro, regional, or state scale that matches real movement of workers and firms. The archive should prefer labour-market governance that follows the actual shed of work rather than inherited local borders.

### 4. Keep public employment services and skills transitions close to real employers and workers

Employment services, active labour-market policy, retraining, and sector-transition support work best when they can see both individual barriers and regional employer demand. The ideal design is therefore neither one purely local employment counter nor one purely national dashboard. It is a layered system in which local access points feed into regional labour-market intelligence and national funding guarantees.

### 5. Reserve national government for labour law, social insurance, and equal-status floors

National government is usually the right level for minimum labour standards, collective-bargaining architecture, labour inspection mandates, unemployment insurance, pensions, parental and sickness protection, large redistribution, and macroeconomic stabilization. These functions need equal citizenship, enforceable floor rules, and broad pooling. The nation should therefore set the legal and fiscal spine without pretending it alone can solve matching, transition, and informal-work visibility from afar.

### 6. Treat social dialogue as governance infrastructure rather than ceremonial consultation

Work is one domain where governments should not act alone. Employers' organizations and workers' organizations need structured roles in labour governance because they carry information, legitimacy, and bargaining capacity the state does not possess by itself. The archive should therefore prefer durable tripartite and social-dialogue machinery over executive improvisation or one-way consultation theatre.

### 7. Use regional and plurilateral governance for portability, mobility, and fair competition where consent exists

Where states choose deeper coordination, regional bodies can support mutual recognition, portability of social-security rights, protection of migrant workers, and rules against undercutting by regulatory arbitrage. These arrangements are useful when they enlarge lawful mobility and fairness together. They fail when they widen labour mobility while leaving rights, enforcement, and welfare portability politically orphaned.

### 8. Keep the global layer focused on labour rights floors and cross-border protection

Global governance should not attempt to set wages town by town. Its role is narrower and essential: articulate international labour standards, support decent-work monitoring, constrain exploitation in supply chains and migration corridors, and protect workers whose bargaining power evaporates across borders. The ideal global layer locks in floors and cooperation; it does not impersonate a world labour ministry.

## Guardrails

- Do not confuse high employment with decent work if insecurity, informality, or abuse remain normal.
- Do not let local institutions bear responsibility for labour-market outcomes without powers or revenue that match the task.
- Do not splinter labour-market and skills governance into jurisdictions smaller than the real region of work.
- Do not offload unemployment, age, illness, or disability risk onto household luck when national pooling is available.
- Do not widen cross-border labour mobility without portability, enforcement, and protection against exploitation.

## Failure modes

- **job-rich precarity**: the headline employment rate improves while work remains unsafe, underpaid, or too unstable to build a life on.
- **municipal labour fiction**: each locality is told to solve a labour market that is obviously regional.
- **training without transitions**: people are retrained into sectors or places that the governance system does not actually connect them to.
- **insurance gaps**: people can work, but cannot survive unemployment, sickness, caregiving, or old age without falling through the floor.
- **mobility without rights**: migrant and mobile workers circulate while benefits, voice, and enforcement do not travel with them.

## Practical tests

A scale-matched work-and-income system passes when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Do local institutions remove practical barriers to participation and surface labour abuse early?
2. Are employment services, matching, and transition systems organized at the scale of the real labour market?
3. Does national government guarantee labour floors, social insurance, and macro stabilization?
4. Is structured social dialogue part of normal governance rather than a crisis-only ritual?
5. Where mobility crosses borders, do portability and worker protection travel with it?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a polity can say **who counts employment** but not **which level removes barriers, which level governs the real labour market, which level pools risk, and which safeguards make mobility compatible with rights**, it still has **a jobs story without a work-and-income constitution**.
