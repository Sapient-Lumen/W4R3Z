# 862 — Applied FATF soft-law waist: recommendations, mutual evaluations, greylisting, and no global financial police by recommendation

## One-line thesis

The Financial Action Task Force is best classified as a global soft-law, peer-review, and listing waist for anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, and counter-proliferation-financing governance: its Recommendations, mutual evaluations, follow-up processes, and high-risk / increased-monitoring lists can harden through domestic law, supervisors, banks, correspondent relationships, procurement, insurance, and investor behavior, but they are not themselves a general world financial police power.

## Why this matters

The archive needed a recommendation-laundering packet. The FATF case is a clean stress test because the formal instrument is not a world statute, yet the practical effects can be extremely hard. Jurisdictions adapt national law and supervision to FATF standards. Evaluations and follow-up create public pressure. High-risk and increased-monitoring lists can influence banks, investors, regulators, donors, payment corridors, and compliance vendors.

That makes FATF neither a harmless advisory club nor a global financial government. It is a **soft-law waist with hardening channels**. The governance problem is to label those channels honestly: standard setting, peer evaluation, public listing, domestic implementation, private-sector de-risking, and international financial-system access must not be collapsed into one fictional command.

This packet therefore extends `856` by showing how a recommendation can become a chokepoint even when no single body directly arrests, fines, licenses, or taxes.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Case object and verdict

| Field | Holding |
| --- | --- |
| case object | FATF Recommendations, interpretive notes, assessment methodology, mutual evaluations, follow-up, International Co-operation Review Group processes, high-risk jurisdictions, and jurisdictions under increased monitoring |
| claimed scope | global AML/CFT/CPF standard-setting and assessment waist, not global financial government |
| archive rule applied | `848` dispatcher, `812` form ladder, `856` waist-capture lens, `857` claim ledger, and `861` negative-control discipline |
| verdict | **soft-law hardening waist; keep bounded and label downstream coercion** |
| short reason | FATF sets international standards and assessment processes that are implemented through domestic law and global financial practice; the practical force often arrives through domestic regulators, banks, market access, and correspondent relationships rather than through direct FATF command |
| forbidden thickening | do not treat recommendations, ratings, greylisting, or blacklisting as direct domestic legal authority without national legal basis, affected-party routes, and proportionality review |
| required caution | distinguish standard, evaluation, list, domestic legal act, supervisory demand, bank de-risking decision, donor condition, and compliance-vendor risk score |

The case is not an attack on AML/CFT/CPF standards. It is a warning against losing the legal and remedial chain when standards harden.

### 4. Soft-law hardening chain

| Stage | Governance label | Required guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| recommendation | international standard | current text, scope, interpretation, and status label |
| methodology | assessment lens | public criteria, evidence class, reviewer discipline |
| mutual evaluation | peer-review finding | country response, factual accuracy, publication route |
| follow-up | implementation pressure | time-bound action plan, progress record, proportionality |
| increased monitoring | public list / greylisting | affected-sector analysis, domestic legal route, exit criteria |
| high-risk call | public list / countermeasure signal | legal basis for downstream restrictions, rights / humanitarian safeguards |
| domestic implementation | national law / regulation / supervision | ordinary legality, consultation, judicial / administrative review |
| private hardening | bank, insurer, investor, donor, vendor, or platform action | no blanket exclusion without risk-sensitive reason and correction route |

This table is the reason the packet belongs in the waist-capture line: the practical sanction may be outside the body that wrote the standard.

The packet should not claim that FATF directly caused every market effect. It should preserve the hardening chain so causation can be debated honestly.

### 6. Form holding

The honest form is **global soft-law standards, peer-review, and listing waist**.

It may set standards, run evaluations, publish ratings and follow-up, identify high-risk and increased-monitoring jurisdictions, and encourage domestic implementation. It must not be described as direct global financial police, direct domestic legal authority, or a substitute for national legislative, regulatory, judicial, and rights routes. When its recommendations harden through banks, supervisors, donors, procurement, insurance, correspondent relationships, or compliance vendors, those downstream actors must be named and reviewed as separate authority surfaces.

### 7. Rival interpretations

| Rival rule | Why it is tempting | Why this packet rejects or limits it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| harmless advice | FATF recommendations are formally standards, not statutes | ignores evaluation, listing, correspondent-banking, supervisory, and market-hardening effects |
| world financial police | listing can have severe consequences | overstates FATF’s direct legal power and hides domestic legal responsibility |
| domestic sovereignty only | AML/CFT law is enacted nationally | underplays cross-border externalities, peer pressure, and global financial-system access |
| bank discretion only | banks choose risk appetite | ignores how standards, regulators, and lists structure private de-risking incentives |
| compliance-vendor rule | vendor risk scores are operationally convenient | turns a private risk model into the practical public waist without correction and accountability |
| security-first override | illicit-finance risks are severe | can erase financial inclusion, humanitarian access, remittances, privacy, and due process |

| Surface | Required repair |
| --- | --- |
| status label | distinguish recommendation, methodology, evaluation, list, domestic law, supervisory instruction, and private-risk decision |
| currentness | mark when Recommendations, procedures, methodology, or list entries changed |

| humanitarian guardrail | prevent AML/CFT hardening from blocking lawful humanitarian, nonprofit, or remittance channels without reasoned review |

## Failure modes

- **recommendation laundering**: treating a standard as if it were directly binding law.
- **greylist absolutism**: converting increased monitoring into blanket financial exclusion.
- **blacklist spillover**: applying calls for action without domestic legal basis, proportionality, or humanitarian guardrails.
- **risk-score sovereignty**: letting compliance vendors operationalize FATF status into unreviewable exclusion.
- **domestic blame shifting**: national authorities say “FATF made us do it” when they made a domestic implementation choice.
- **market-effect denial**: FATF-adjacent actors deny practical coercion because formal instruments are not statutes.
- **currentness drift**: using old Recommendations, methodology, procedures, or list status after updates.
- **financial-inclusion erasure**: ignoring remittance users, charities, migrants, small firms, and legitimate high-risk customers.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Can the file say which stage of the hardening chain is being invoked?
2. Is the current FATF text, methodology, procedure, evaluation, or list entry identified?
3. Does any domestic measure have its own legal basis and review route?
4. Are private de-risking decisions separated from public-law obligations?
5. Can affected users or firms challenge factual errors or blanket exclusion?
6. Does the packet name humanitarian, remittance, charity, privacy, and financial-inclusion effects?
7. Is exit from increased monitoring or high-risk status tied to public criteria and action plans?
8. Does the archive avoid both “mere advice” and “world police” exaggerations?
