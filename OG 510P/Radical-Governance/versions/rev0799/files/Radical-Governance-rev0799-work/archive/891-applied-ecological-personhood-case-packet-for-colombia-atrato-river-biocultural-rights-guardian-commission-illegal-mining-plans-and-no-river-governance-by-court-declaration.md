# 891 — Applied ecological-personhood case packet for Colombia Atrato River: biocultural rights, guardian commission, illegal-mining plans, and no river governance by court declaration

## One-line thesis

The Atrato River judgment is best classified as a biocultural-rights and ecological-personhood implementation waist: the river, basin, and tributaries were recognized as rights-bearing, but the governance proof lives in the guardian commission, community co-representation, anti-mining / decontamination plans, health and food-security duties, public records, and continuing capacity to turn a court declaration into ecological repair.

## Why this matters

Atrato is the necessary comparator to `865` because it does not rest on the same settlement architecture as Te Awa Tupua or Taranaki Maunga. It comes through constitutional litigation about ethnic communities, illegal mining, mercury contamination, health, water, food security, culture, territory, state omission, and biocultural rights.

The case shows why legal personhood can be both serious and insufficient. The court did not merely name the river. It required representation, guardians, plans, and state-community action. But those implementation duties are precisely where governance can fail. The archive should not stop at the celebrated line that the river is a subject of rights. It should ask whether the guardian spine has usable authority, whether communities retain voice, whether illegal mining can actually be suppressed, whether remediation is funded, whether health and toxicology studies occur, and whether public data shows restoration.

Atrato therefore becomes the archive's first Latin American ecological-personhood packet.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Dispatcher result

| Field | Holding |
| --- | --- |
| trigger | grave ecological and human-rights harm from mining, contamination, state omission, and affected ethnic communities' territorial / cultural claims |
| first-form question | whether the right form is ordinary environmental enforcement, ethnic-community rights remedy, basin authority, constitutional structural injunction, or legal-personhood order |
| lower-form test | ordinary permit enforcement and pollution-control law were too thin once illegal mining, state omission, toxic contamination, health, food security, culture, territory, and participation were joined |
| heavier-form test | creating a general river government would be too thick unless specific public duties, community representation, enforcement, budgets, and records are separately authorized |
| form verdict | biocultural-rights and ecological-personhood implementation waist with guardian commission and state-community remedial plans |

### 2. Legal and operational boundary

| Component | Boundary rule |
| --- | --- |
| river rights | recognition creates a rights-bearing subject for protection, conservation, maintenance, and restoration; it does not itself clean the river |
| government representative | the state representative can carry legal duties but cannot erase community co-representation or local knowledge |
| community guardians | community representation is not ordinary consultation; it is part of the legal representation spine |
| state agencies | ministries, governors, mayors, environmental authorities, defense, health, agriculture, finance, planning, and enforcement bodies retain direct implementation duties |
| illegal mining | suppression of illegal mining is an enforcement and security problem, not merely an ecological aspiration |
| contamination | mercury and other toxic substances require monitoring, public-health, decontamination, and food-security records |
| scientific information | lack of reliable mining and environmental data is itself a governance failure |
| public record | progress must be visible through plans, compliance records, budgets, indicators, and community-accessible reporting |

### 5. Ecological-personhood docket

This packet activates these tests:

1. **Subject and basin boundary** — identify the river, basin, tributaries, and territory covered by the order.
2. **Rights catalog** — separate protection, conservation, maintenance, restoration, health, water, food, culture, and territory claims.
3. **Guardian spine** — show the government representative, community representative, commission, advisors, meeting record, and decision rule.
4. **Community authority** — prove the community lane is not reduced to consultation theater.
5. **Remedy plan** — identify anti-mining, decontamination, environmental, health, food-security, and participation plans.
6. **Budget and capacity** — show funding, staff, technical assistance, enforcement access, and interagency coordination.
7. **Scientific monitoring** — publish baselines, toxicology, epidemiology, mining, deforestation, water-quality, and biodiversity indicators.
8. **Forum and standing** — preserve court, information, participation, and review channels.
9. **Liability boundary** — ensure the river's personhood does not substitute for state, polluter, or criminal responsibility.
10. **Failure route** — identify what happens when implementation stalls.

### 6. Opposition and rival readings

**Rival 1: This is symbolic judicial activism.** The archive rejects the blanket dismissal. The court created representation and remedial obligations, so there is a real governance form.

**Rival 2: This is a complete river government.** The archive also rejects that overstatement. Guardianship and legal personhood do not by themselves create a full basin state.

**Rival 3: Community guardians solve representation.** The archive treats community guardianship as a necessary but contestable lane. It still needs selection, funding, dissent, capacity, and public record.

**Rival 4: Illegal mining is outside the personhood issue.** The archive rejects that. If mining was central to the judgment, enforcement capacity is part of personhood implementation.

**Rival 5: Progress reports prove compliance.** The archive treats reports as evidence, not conclusion. Reports must show indicators, money, actions, and effects.

### 7. Capture and theater channels

- **river-personhood headline**: recognition is treated as restoration.
- **guardian ceremonialism**: the commission meets but lacks funds, access, or enforcement leverage.
- **community-lane laundering**: community representatives are cited while local disagreement or capacity limits disappear.
- **plan multiplication**: plans proliferate without budgets, deadlines, or ecological indicators.
- **anti-mining displacement**: illegal mining is named but not suppressed.
- **toxicology fog**: health and mercury evidence remains inaccessible or inconclusive without follow-up.
- **interagency diffusion**: every agency has a duty and no agency owns failure.

### 8. Repair surfaces

The packet remains open until these records are visible or expressly unavailable:

- guardian commission constitution, decision rules, and minutes;
- government and community representative mandates;
- anti-illegal-mining and anti-mercury enforcement records;
- decontamination, restoration, health, and food-security action plans;
- budget and staffing ledger;
- toxicology, epidemiology, water-quality, and biodiversity indicators;
- public progress reports with community-readable summaries;
- dispute / contempt / compliance hearing records;
- community dissent and participation record;
- plan-revision and failure-escalation route.

### 9. Holding

The Atrato case is a biocultural-rights and ecological-personhood implementation waist, not ordinary environmental enforcement and not a full river government. The rule is: **no river governance by court declaration**. The legal subject must be backed by guardian independence, community authority, ecological monitoring, budgets, enforcement, and public compliance records.

## Anti-theater tests

This packet fails if river personhood, constitutional language, or a guardian commission is treated as proof of ecological improvement. It passes only with public records showing that illegal mining, contamination, health, food security, community authority, and restoration duties have moved from judgment to implementation.
