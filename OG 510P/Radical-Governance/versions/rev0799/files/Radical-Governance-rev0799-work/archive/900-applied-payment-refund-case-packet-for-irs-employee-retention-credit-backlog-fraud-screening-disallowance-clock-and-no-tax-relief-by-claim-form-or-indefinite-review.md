# 900 — Applied payment-refund case packet for IRS Employee Retention Credit: backlog, fraud screening, disallowance clock, and no tax relief by claim form or indefinite review

## One-line thesis

The Employee Retention Credit refund tail is best classified as a fraud-screened refundable-tax-credit payment waist with eligibility proof, amended-return processing, promoter-risk controls, claim inventory, disallowance notices, refund-suit limitation clocks, and communication duties; it should not be scored as relief merely because a claim was filed, nor as program integrity merely because payment was delayed or disallowed.

## Why this matters

ERC is the non-UK comparator this payment-redress layer needed. It is not a scandal redress scheme like Horizon and not a health-harm compensation authority like IBCA. It is a pandemic-era refundable tax credit administered through the tax system, with complex eligibility criteria, aggressive promoters, improper-payment risk, paper amended returns, processing moratoria, disallowance, and statutory refund-right deadlines.

The case forces the archive to hold two truths at once. First, improper claims and scam promoters are real governance problems. Second, legitimate claimants can be harmed when an agency's inventory, communication gaps, or disallowance-review delay causes refund rights to expire before review is complete.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Case trigger

The trigger is a refundable employment-tax credit created for pandemic relief, later producing a large amended-return and refund inventory, promoter-driven risk, fraud screening, processing moratorium, disallowance notices, and deadline-protection issues for taxpayers who responded to disallowances.

### 2. Form verdict

**Fraud-screened refundable-tax-credit payment waist.**

The honest form is not automatic relief and not ordinary audit. It is a payment claim routed through tax administration where eligibility, documentation, risk screening, refund timing, disallowance, and judicial-review clocks must be kept separate.

### 3. Source waist facts

IRS guidance describes ERC as a refundable tax credit for eligible businesses and tax-exempt organizations, with eligibility depending on specific facts, time periods, qualified wages, government-order suspension, gross-receipts decline, or recovery-startup status. It also warns about promoter claims that every employer qualifies and lists recordkeeping and scam risks.

GAO reported that IRS had processed nearly five million ERC claims as of June 2025, implemented a processing moratorium in September 2023 to address improper claims, and, according to IRS officials, closed most claims by December 31, 2025. GAO also identified design and administration lessons around improper-payment risk, eligibility reporting, manual amended returns, and communication.

The National Taxpayer Advocate separately warned that the ERC claim window had closed while a large inventory and disallowance tail remained, and later described a 2026 streamlined process using Notice CP320B and Form 907 to protect some taxpayers whose two-year refund-suit period was close to expiring after they responded to a disallowance.

### 5. Anti-theater tests

- **Claim-form theater**: filing an amended return does not prove eligibility.
- **Fraud-panic theater**: improper claims do not justify indefinite limbo for documented eligible claims.
- **Moratorium theater**: a processing pause is not a case-specific determination.
- **Disallowance-clock theater**: administrative review should not consume the taxpayer's refund-suit window without an extension route.
- **Promoter-blame theater**: promoter misconduct is relevant, but the public system still needs notice, evidence, review, and rights preservation.
- **Inventory-closure theater**: closing most claims does not identify who remains unpaid, disallowed, under review, or rights-barred.
- **Communication theater**: generic warnings do not substitute for taxpayer-specific status and next steps.

### 6. Opposition brief

A fair opposition says ERC's complexity and promoter abuse made strong screening unavoidable, and that paying improper claims would itself violate public accountability. It may also point to GAO's improper-payment concerns and IRS scam warnings as justification for risk-based processing and disallowance.

The archive accepts that integrity premise. The holding is narrower: integrity requires a time-bounded, documented, reviewable payment waist, not an indefinite queue or an agency-created expiration of refund rights.

### 7. Success condition

ERC administration becomes a positive-control case only if the remaining payment tail can show status by claim cohort, risk category, allowed / disallowed / appealed / paid outcomes, refund-right extension protection where review delay threatens rights, taxpayer-specific communication, and lessons for future emergency refundable credits.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Do not treat an ERC claim form as relief; separate filed, risk-screened, under review, disallowed, appealed, extension-protected, refunded, and closed states.
2. Do not let promoter abuse justify indefinite limbo for claimants who can provide eligibility evidence.
3. Do not report inventory closure as rights protection unless disallowance clocks, refund-suit windows, Form 907 extensions, and taxpayer-specific notices are visible.
4. Do not treat fraud screening as neutral if the agency cannot publish reason categories, reversal tails, processing times, and communication standards.
5. Do not design future emergency credits without eligibility data, electronic-claim capacity, improper-payment controls, and taxpayer-rights clocks from the start.

## Holding

ERC is neither free money nor merely a fraud problem. It is a refundable-credit payment-rights system that must separate claim filing, eligibility proof, risk screening, disallowance, payment, and judicial-review preservation. The core rule is: **no tax relief by claim form, and no program integrity by indefinite review.**
