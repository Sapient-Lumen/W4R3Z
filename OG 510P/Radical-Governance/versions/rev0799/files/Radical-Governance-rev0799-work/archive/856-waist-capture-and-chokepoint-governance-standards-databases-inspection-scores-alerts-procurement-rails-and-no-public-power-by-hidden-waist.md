# 856 — Waist capture and chokepoint governance: standards, databases, inspection scores, alerts, procurement rails, and no public power by hidden waist

## One-line thesis

A narrow waist is legitimate only while it stays thin, auditable, correctable, and defeasible; once a standard, database, risk score, alert label, procurement vehicle, cloud platform, or model-provider interface becomes the practical gate through which public power must pass, the archive should treat it as a capture-prone chokepoint requiring its own ownership map, correction route, appeal route, exit plan, and public record.

## Why this matters

The archive has become good at asking whether a problem needs local, municipal, metropolitan, regional, national, continental, or global authority. It is less complete on a darker question: what happens when the official authority is not the real choke point?

Modern public power often moves through thin-looking interfaces. A standard defines which systems may talk. A database assigns risk. An inspection score chooses who is stopped. An alert label changes public behavior. A procurement framework decides which vendors can become infrastructure. A cloud environment controls logs, access, evidence, and exit. A model provider controls the practical explanation surface even when the public agency remains legally responsible.

The archive should not abandon narrow waists. They are still the right answer for many cross-boundary problems. But it should stop treating thinness as innocence. Thin layers can be more powerful than ministries because they sit where all traffic must pass.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Define the waist before admiring it

Every proposed narrow waist should be named as an operating object, not just as an institutional aspiration.

| Waist type | Hidden power question | Minimum record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| standard | who defines conformance? | versioned standard, exception log, conformance test, dissent path |
| database | who controls entry, correction, deletion, and downstream use? | data steward, correction route, audit trail, sharing map |
| risk score | who chooses variables, thresholds, and escalation? | scoring method, threshold file, false-positive review, override log |
| inspection queue | who decides who gets inspected first? | priority rules, campaign calendar, inspector discretion record |
| alert label | who decides emergency status and public message? | evidence file, uncertainty note, review clock, termination record |
| procurement rail | who can sell, integrate, maintain, and update? | vendor list, entry criteria, conflict disclosures, off-ramp |
| cloud platform | who controls identity, logs, regions, backups, keys, and exit? | shared-responsibility map, admin log, portability file, escrow or migration plan |
| model interface | who controls model behavior, refusals, updates, safety filters, and explanations? | model card or system card, change notice, output log class, fallback channel |

The waist is not the agency name. It is the practical control surface.

### 2. Treat chokepoints as quasi-authority

A chokepoint becomes quasi-authority when losing access to it prevents a person, municipality, firm, ship, hospital, school, or agency from using an otherwise available right, service, market, route, or remedy.

The archive should therefore ask:

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Who can be excluded? | exclusion may be the real sanction |
| Who can correct the record? | error without correction becomes punishment |
| Who sees the reasons? | opacity makes review nominal |
| Who controls timing? | delay can decide outcomes without a formal decision |
| Who controls version changes? | quiet updates can alter rights without enactment |
| Who can exit? | no exit turns convenience into dependence |
| Who audits? | self-audit is weak when the waist benefits from traffic |
| Who pays? | fees can become a private tax on public power |

The more consequential the chokepoint, the more it should inherit public-law disciplines even if it is technically a standard, platform, contract, registry, recommendation, or scoring method.

### 3. Separate authority, infrastructure, and effect

A public agency may own the legal authority while a different actor owns the infrastructure and a third actor controls the effect.

For each serious waist, preserve a three-column file:

| Layer | Required owner label | Typical failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| legal authority | who may decide or compel? | domestic law or statute is hidden behind global, regional, or vendor language |
| infrastructure | who operates the system of action? | platform owner becomes invisible co-governor |
| practical effect | who experiences denial, delay, inspection, detention, classification, fee, or warning? | affected party has no route to the real control layer |

This is the core anti-theater move. The archive should not allow a government to say “the system did it,” a vendor to say “the agency decided,” or a secretariat to say “we merely coordinated” when the practical effect is coercive.

### 4. Add a waist-capture test to every cross-boundary packet

The test is compact:

| Capture field | Question |
| --- | --- |
| entry capture | who decides who may join or interoperate? |
| classification capture | who assigns categories, scores, risk bands, or emergency labels? |
| data capture | who owns the correction, retention, and sharing route? |
| clock capture | who controls deadlines, renewal, expiry, and review cadence? |
| vendor capture | who profits from complexity, lock-in, migration cost, or exclusive integration? |
| expertise capture | who converts technical judgment into non-reviewable policy? |
| sanction capture | who creates downstream effects while denying that a sanction occurred? |
| exit capture | who can make exit legally possible but operationally impossible? |

No packet should call a waist “thin” until it has answered these questions.

### 5. Make recommendation laundering explicit

A recommendation, guideline, model clause, code of practice, emergency notice, score, or shared list can become coercive without formally changing legal status. The laundering route often runs through procurement, insurance, funding conditions, border practice, professional discipline, platform access, or audit expectations.

The archive should preserve both labels:

- **formal status**: binding law, regulation, contract, voluntary code, guidance, recommendation, technical standard, private policy;
- **operational status**: optional, expected, prerequisite, fundable condition, procurement gate, insurance gate, inspection trigger, sanction proxy, or market-access condition.

A nonbinding instrument with binding effects should be treated as a governance surface, not dismissed as soft law.

### 6. Build exit and correction before scaling

A waist that cannot be exited or corrected should not be scaled merely because it improves coordination.

Minimum exit-and-correction package:

| Package element | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| data portability | what moves, in what format, with what audit trail |
| decision portability | what pending cases, reasons, notices, and appeals survive migration |
| identity portability | how credentials, roles, and delegated authority transfer |
| log portability | which operational records remain reviewable after exit |
| fallback service | how users continue receiving service during failure or migration |
| correction file | how false data, wrong classifications, or obsolete scores are corrected downstream |
| decommission plan | who pays, who signs off, and what records remain public |

Exit is not a technology preference. It is a legitimacy condition.

### 7. Use source currentness labels for volatile waists

Some waists change quickly. AI obligations, cloud switching rules, pandemic instruments, emergency standards, and digital-service interoperability requirements should carry currentness labels.

| Label | Use when | Required review clock |
| --- | --- | --- |
| stable legal anchor | constitution, statute, treaty text, or settled institutional fact | ordinary revision cycle |
| implementation clock | legal rule exists but obligations phase in | date-specific review |
| volatile guidance | guidance, code, tool, or interpretation may update | short review clock |
| market-shaping practice | procurement, platform, or vendor practice changes incentives | event-based review |
| emergency layer | alert, declaration, incident, or temporary recommendation | immediate review on continuation, expiry, or termination |

A waist is capture-prone when the law is stable but the operating surface changes faster than public review.

## Failure modes

- **thinness theater**: calling a waist narrow while it controls entry, classification, timing, and sanction effects.
- **database sovereignty**: making the database the practical legislature, inspector, or court.
- **standard-setting constitutionalism**: moving policy into conformance criteria that only specialists can see or challenge.
- **recommendation laundering**: turning guidance or temporary recommendations into domestic coercion without legal enactment and remedy.
- **platform public law**: letting cloud, identity, or model-provider controls determine public outcomes while legal accountability remains elsewhere.
- **exit-right theater**: promising portability while logs, contracts, keys, data schemas, or trained workflows make exit impracticable.
- **score punishment**: using a risk score as a sanction while denying that a decision has occurred.
- **audit capture**: letting the same actor who benefits from the waist define its evidence of safety.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Can a non-specialist identify the waist, its owner, its legal authority, and its operating effect?
2. Can affected parties correct the data, classification, score, or alert that drives the outcome?
3. Can the public distinguish binding law, voluntary code, guidance, recommendation, and operational prerequisite?
4. Can the waist be exited without losing public records, appeals, service continuity, or lawful authority?
5. Can a reviewer reconstruct the version of the standard, model, rule, or database used at the time of action?
6. Can the agency name which vendor, secretariat, standard setter, cloud platform, or expert body has practical leverage?
7. Can the archive show who profits from delay, complexity, opacity, lock-in, or repeated certification?
8. Can the waist fail closed without silently suspending rights or essential services?
