# 748 — Historical-object-boundary-repair packets for retired government bodies, scopes, and shells: split, merge, creator links, and no one dead authority by cataloguing accident

## One-line thesis

Once the archive can absorb tombstones into grouped historical registers (`745`), move those grouped registers across homes (`746`), and restore one absorbed entry back into a standalone memorial surface (`747`), it should also keep one compact **historical-object-boundary-repair packet**: one bounded proof object for the harder case where the memorial object boundary itself turned out to be wrong — one entry actually conflated several distinct dead authorities, or several entries were really one dead authority all along — so the polity can split, merge, or relink historical objects without rewriting closure history, losing aliases, or mistaking successor routing for object identity.

## Why this matters

The archive now has a credible maintenance grammar for dead public objects.

It can retire a body or scope lane without constitutional ghosting (`642`, `740`). It can leave an honest tombstone (`741`), reroute it when successor facts drift (`742`), correct it when the public story is materially wrong (`743`), and keep it maintained over time (`744`). It can absorb standalone memorial pages into grouped historical registers (`745`), move those grouped registers across homes or catalogues (`746`), and later pull one absorbed entry back out into a standalone memorial surface when grouping proved too lossy (`747`).

What it still lacked was one compact packet for the next deeper failure: **what happens when the memorial object was drawn incorrectly in the first place**.

That gap matters because dead public bodies rarely sit still in simple, archivally tidy shapes. One abolished authority may have changed name several times without ever becoming a new object. A machinery change may have created a genuinely new body even though the public-facing label barely changed. A grouped memorial entry may have silently collapsed a predecessor, successor, and temporary shell into one “same thing” story because they served a similar field. Or two apparently separate dead pages may later turn out to describe one legal body under different cataloguing assumptions, different creator fields, or different route-home conventions. In the opposite direction, one dead page may turn out to have bundled together several distinct authorities that shared a nickname, building, records transfer path, or final service channel.

Without one explicit boundary-repair packet, maintainers are pushed toward two bad habits. They either leave the mistaken object boundary in place because it is already published, or they repair it through ad hoc editing that rewrites history without preserving the fact that the archive once drew the object differently. In both cases, the public loses the ability to tell whether a change reflects a name correction, a new creator relationship, a true split into multiple dead objects, or a true merge of duplicate memorials. Successor maps become confused with identity. Alias trails break. Old citations become misleading. And the archive starts teaching future maintainers that whatever the current card says must always have been the truth.

The archive therefore needs one compact packet for **historical-object boundary repair**. This packet should make it possible to say, in a disciplined way, whether the archive is correcting a name-only issue, repairing creator or parent-child linkage, splitting one memorial object into several, or merging duplicates back into one. It should also force the polity to preserve the old identifiers, explain the evidence and dated reasons, reroute aliases and citations, and keep visible whether the archive changed the historical story because the object boundary was wrong or because later stewardship merely became neater. Without that packet, the archive still permits **one dead authority by cataloguing accident**.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Require one explicit boundary-repair packet before changing the object count of a memorial surface

A dead body should not silently turn from one memorial object into several, or vice versa, through ordinary editing.

If the polity wants to repair the boundary of a retired authority, scope lane, or closeout shell, it should open one explicit packet saying:

- which memorial entry or entries are under repair;
- whether the proposed act is a split, merge, relinking, or name-only correction;
- what evidence shows the current boundary is wrong;
- which identifiers, aliases, grouped-register rows, and standalone pages are affected;
- and what the public lookup map will look like after repair.

Boundary repair should be a named act, not a side effect of tidying a card.

### 2. Distinguish boundary repair from name correction, successor drift, restoration, and simple catalogue migration

A boundary-repair packet should classify the move correctly.

At minimum it should distinguish:

- **name-only correction** — the dead object stays the same, but the preferred memorial label changes;
- **creator-link or parent-child repair** — the object stays the same, but its contextual link to a creator, parent register, or grouping level was wrong;
- **one-to-many split** — one memorial entry actually covered several distinct dead objects and must be separated;
- **many-to-one merge** — several memorial entries actually described one dead object and should be collapsed into one canonical memorial;
- **duplicate-with-trace cleanup** — one duplicate becomes non-canonical but remains discoverable as a historical alias surface;
- **uncertain-boundary hold** — the archive has reason to doubt the current object boundary but not enough proof yet to split or merge cleanly.

Without typed classes, every identity repair looks like an arbitrary editorial preference.

### 3. Keep object identity tests separate from successor routing tests

A successor is not always the same object in a new place, and a shared service route is not always evidence of one authority.

The packet should therefore ask separately:

- did the legal body itself continue or end;
- did only the name change;
- did functions move while the body died;
- did records move without identity moving;
- did the public front door stay the same while legal responsibility changed;
- and did a temporary shell or custodian sit between bodies without becoming the same object.

If those questions are collapsed into one, memorial identity drifts toward whichever route happens to be easiest to explain.

### 4. Preserve one visible lineage from the mistaken boundary to the repaired one

Boundary repair should not erase the fact that the archive once drew the object differently.

The packet should preserve:

- the prior memorial identifier or identifiers;
- the date range during which the mistaken boundary was published;
- the new canonical identifier or identifiers;
- the dated reason for the repair;
- and the exact relation between old and new objects, such as split-from, merged-into, duplicate-of, or relinked-under.

That keeps historical maintenance legible without freezing the archive in error.

### 5. Treat name changes and inherited records as evidence to be weighed, not automatic triggers for a new memorial object

A changed title does not always mean a new dead authority, and inherited records do not always mean the same authority continued.

The packet should therefore force maintainers to say whether the best reading is:

- one continuing object with changed labels;
- one new object that inherited records or functions from another;
- one grouped catalogue level that should expand creator context without changing the memorial object count;
- or several genuinely distinct objects that previously sat under one convenience heading.

This keeps the archive from creating or suppressing dead objects just because naming or transfer facts are messy.

### 6. Recompute successor, records-home, archive-home, and legacy-question routing for each repaired object

Once a boundary is repaired, the downstream routing facts may also change.

A one-to-many split may require distinct successor maps, different records homes, or separate archive homes. A many-to-one merge may show that several apparent legacy-question routes were really one shared residual lane. A creator-link repair may leave routing untouched while improving only context.

The packet should therefore show, object by object, which routing facts change and which stay the same. Otherwise the archive repairs identity but leaves the practical crosswalk wrong.

### 7. Keep old pages, grouped rows, and aliases discoverable as superseded lookup surfaces rather than deleting them outright

Boundary repair should improve lookup, not punish people who learned the old map.

The archive should therefore keep non-canonical residue where needed:

- old standalone tombstone URLs;
- grouped-register entry aliases;
- prior preferred names;
- citation-facing labels used in reports or litigation;
- and duplicate memorial pages that now resolve to the surviving canonical entry.

These residues should say clearly that the object boundary has been repaired and where the canonical memorial now lives.

### 8. Use bounded uncertainty markers when the archive has evidence of error but not enough proof for a clean split or merge

Sometimes the archive can tell that one memorial object is wrong before it can prove the right replacement.

In that case it should allow one bounded **uncertain-boundary hold** with:

- a visible under-review mark;
- the candidate interpretations still being tested;
- the evidence still missing;
- and a review clock with a named steward.

The archive should not force premature certainty merely because users prefer a clean card.

### 9. Forbid boundary repair from becoming shadow resurrection or convenience deletion

A repaired memorial object is still a historical object.

The packet should block designs that:

- make a merged or split page look like a reactivated live service;
- use a merge to erase a controversial predecessor from public view;
- use a split to invent separate bodies that never existed;
- or use a name-only correction to bury an old identifier people still need for record requests or legal references.

Boundary repair should be truth-seeking, not reputation management.

### 10. Keep later regrouping, deconsolidation, or further repair reversible through the same explicit lineage rules

Boundary repair should not be the archive’s final irreversible verdict.

The packet should name whether later moves remain possible, such as:

- re-absorption into a grouped register after a split;
- later deconsolidation if a merge proves over-broad;
- further creator-link repair if better provenance is found;
- or conversion of an uncertain-boundary hold into a split or merge.

This keeps the archive honest about the difference between best current judgment and eternal certainty.

## Failure modes the packet is meant to block

- **name-change inflation**: a body that only changed label is given several memorial objects, fracturing search and citations for no real identity reason.
- **successor-equals-same-object fiction**: the archive treats every function transfer or public front-door continuity as proof that the old and new bodies were the same object.
- **duplicate memorial permanence**: two dead pages clearly describe one object, but neither is marked canonical and both keep accreting updates.
- **split-by-reputation management**: an embarrassing predecessor is separated into a “different body” story without real identity grounds.
- **merge-by-convenience flattening**: distinct dead authorities are collapsed because they shared a building, acronym, host, or archive home.
- **boundary repair without routing repair**: the archive fixes the identity theory but leaves records, archive, and question lanes mapped as if the old mistake were still true.
- **historical rewrite by cleanup**: old identifiers and pages disappear so later users cannot see that the archive once described the object differently.

## Practical tests

A historical-object-boundary-repair packet is honest when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Is the boundary repair a named act rather than an ad hoc edit?
2. Does it classify whether the move is name-only correction, creator-link repair, one-to-many split, many-to-one merge, duplicate cleanup, or uncertain-boundary hold?
3. Does it keep identity tests separate from successor, record-home, archive-home, and front-door routing tests?
4. Does it preserve the lineage from the mistaken boundary to the repaired one with dated reasons and relation types?
5. Does it say explicitly why the archive is not merely dealing with a name change or a normal successor drift?
6. Are successor, records-home, archive-home, and legacy-question routes recomputed for each repaired object?
7. Do prior pages, grouped rows, aliases, and citation labels still resolve as superseded lookup surfaces where users will realistically need them?
8. If proof is incomplete, is there a bounded under-review state rather than a fake clean answer?
9. Is the resulting memorial surface still visibly historical rather than a shadow live authority?
10. Is there a credible lane for later regrouping, deconsolidation, or further repair if the current fix proves too broad or too narrow?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a polity can already absorb, move, and restore historical tombstones, it should also be able to show one compact packet for **when the memorial object boundary itself was wrong, what kind of split/merge/relink/name-only correction is actually happening, which evidence and dated reasons justify that repair, how old and new identifiers remain connected, how successor and records routing are recalculated object by object, how uncertainty is marked when proof is incomplete, and how old lookup paths remain visible without preserving a false boundary**. Otherwise it still permits **one dead authority by cataloguing accident**.
