# 951 — Applied broadband connectivity continuity case packet for FCC National Broadband Map, BDC, Lifeline, ACP, BEAD, NORS, broadband labels, and no access by coverage polygon

## One-line thesis

FCC National Broadband Map, BDC, availability challenge, broadband label, Lifeline, USAC National Verifier, ACP, BEAD, NORS/DIRS, and digital-discrimination sources should be read as separate evidence lanes. They cannot be collapsed into proof that a household had usable, affordable, reliable internet or phone access for a public-service task.

## Why this matters

This applied packet turns the broadband docket into an operational source surface. The sources are strong enough to show national availability mapping, provider-reported service, challenge channels, consumer pricing/performance disclosures, Lifeline eligibility and recertification, ACP termination, BEAD deployment milestones, outage reporting, and equal-access policy boundaries. But each source has a narrow lane. A map can say service is reported available; a label can disclose price/performance terms; a verifier can decide eligibility; a dashboard can show program progress; an outage system can collect carrier reports. None of those proves household-level connectivity.

The governing repair phrase is **no access by coverage polygon**.

## Pattern pack

- **No access by coverage polygon.** The National Broadband Map begins the inquiry; it does not prove usable service at the household.
- **No performance by availability claim.** A provider-reported service row does not prove speed, latency, congestion, installation, or data-cap usability.
- **No affordability by label.** Broadband labels support comparison but do not prove the bill is payable, the subsidy is active, or fees are survivable.
- **No Lifeline by National Verifier.** Eligibility determination must be joined to enrollment, recertification, service plan, device, and benefit loss state.
- **No ACP by past discount.** ACP ended for now, so past ACP enrollment is a source-currentness warning, not current affordability proof.
- **No BEAD by progress dashboard.** Dashboard progress and awards do not prove household activation or adoption.
- **No restoration by NORS/DIRS.** Outage reporting and government sharing must be joined to user-level restoration, credits, and fallback.
- **No access remedy by complaint.** Map challenges, FCC complaints, and equal-access records do not repair access unless they change service, price, installation, or fallback.

## Source-to-duty map

| Source surface | What it proves | What it cannot prove alone |
|---|---|---|
| FCC National Broadband Map | Public map of reported fixed and mobile broadband availability. | Actual subscription, affordability, performance, installation, or adoption. |
| FCC Broadband Data Collection | Provider-reported availability data architecture and reporting windows. | Household truth without challenge, local verification, or provider ticket records. |
| BDC availability challenge | Route to challenge provider availability claims. | Immediate service or corrected access. |
| Broadband consumer labels | Standardized disclosure of plan price/performance terms. | Household affordability, device readiness, subsidy continuity, or actual speed. |
| FCC/USAC Lifeline and National Verifier | Eligibility, application, and recertification lanes for monthly support. | Working phone/internet service, device, data allowance, or successful recertification. |
| FCC ACP page | Historical/program-currentness anchor that ACP ended for now. | Current affordability for former ACP households. |
| NTIA BEAD program/dashboard | National buildout program and milestone/progress evidence. | Construction, installation, activation, adoption, or user outcome at an address. |
| FCC NORS/DIRS/outage sharing | Carrier outage reporting and government information-sharing lane. | Household restoration, public-service fallback, or customer credit/repair. |
| Digital-discrimination/equal-access materials | Policy/remedy boundary for broadband access discrimination questions. | Current service delivery or individual remedy without case-level enforcement evidence. |

## Applied continuity ladder

1. **Classify the connectivity consequence.** Benefits, telehealth, school, court, tax, work, identity proofing, disaster notice, emergency call, appeal, or ordinary civic access.
2. **Bind household and location.** Household, address/unit, BSL, mobile geography, rural/tribal status, disability/language need, housing/custody/care context, and deadline.
3. **Separate source surfaces.** Mark each source as map, provider self-report, challenge, label, subsidy, dashboard, outage, complaint, or user outcome.
4. **Prove serviceability.** Provider installation answer, technology, line extension, modem/router, premises wiring, mobile signal, and activation date.
5. **Prove affordability.** Actual bill, fees, deposit, data cap, subsidy, Lifeline/ACP state, bill shock, disconnection, and low-cost fallback.
6. **Prove device and usability.** Device, router, power, backup, assistive technology, accessibility, language, privacy, and skills/support.
7. **Prove performance and reliability.** Speed, latency, congestion, throttling, data cap, outages, repair appointment, restoration, and credit/remedy.
8. **Prove public-service continuity.** Whether the person completed the portal, upload, notice, telehealth, school, court, benefit, tax, work, or emergency task.
9. **Prove fallback.** Phone, mail, in-person, assisted digital, library, school, clinic, shelter, mobile hotspot, or offline channel that preserved the duty.
10. **Close with remedy.** Corrected map row, installed service, bill reduction, subsidy restoration, device repair, outage credit, deadline preservation, and learning loop.

## Applied examples

### FCC National Broadband Map and BDC

Use the map and BDC records to locate provider-reported availability and technology claims. Then require installation records, household bills, local challenge outcomes, measured performance, and user outcome evidence before scoring access.

### Availability challenges

Use the challenge process to correct false serviceability claims. Do not treat the challenge form as access; the repair is a corrected row, installation, alternate service, or funding eligibility change.

### Lifeline, National Verifier, and recertification

Use Lifeline and National Verifier materials to identify eligibility and recertification lanes. Then check enrollment, service plan, device, benefit amount, recertification deadline, tribal enhanced support, disconnection risk, and appeal/assisted route.

### ACP and affordability cliffs

Use the ACP page as a currentness boundary: the program ended for now. Former ACP households require a fresh affordability file, not an old enrollment row.

### BEAD

Use BEAD program and dashboard materials for buildout-stage evidence. Do not score BEAD as connectivity until construction, installation, activation, subscription, affordability, and performance are verified.

### NORS/DIRS and outages

Use NORS/DIRS/outage-sharing sources to identify reporting lanes. Then check household outage, repair ticket, restoration time, battery/power fallback, public-service deadlines, and customer credits or alternative channels.

### Broadband labels and complaints

Use labels for plan disclosure and complaints/equal-access materials for remedy channels. Then join to actual bills, fees, data caps, accessibility, language, provider response, and corrected service.

## Repair surfaces

- household/address/BSL and public-service dependency file;
- provider availability, technology, installation, challenge, and local verification file;
- subscription, price, fee, deposit, subsidy, data-cap, billing, and shutoff file;
- Lifeline/National Verifier/recertification and ACP cliff file;
- device, modem/router, premises wiring, power, backup, accessibility, language, and digital-skills file;
- speed, latency, congestion, throttling, outage, repair, restoration, and credit file;
- public-service portal/telehealth/school/court/benefit/tax/work/emergency task file;
- community anchor, public Wi-Fi, device lending, privacy, hours, transport, and safety file;
- complaint, map challenge, state broadband office, digital-discrimination/equal-access, and provider-escalation file;
- source-currentness and supersession file.

## Anti-theater tests

- Do not cite a coverage polygon, BSL, or provider-reported availability row as actual connectivity without installation, subscription, affordability, device, and performance proof.
- Do not cite broadband labels as affordability without actual bill and household budget evidence.
- Do not cite Lifeline eligibility or National Verifier status as usable service without enrollment, recertification, device, and plan evidence.
- Do not cite ACP enrollment after the program ended as current affordability.
- Do not cite BEAD progress as access without local buildout and activation proof.
- Do not cite outage reporting as restoration without household-level repair and fallback evidence.
- Do not cite portal uptime as access without user connectivity, device, accessibility, and deadline-preservation evidence.
- Do not cite complaint or challenge availability as remedy without a corrected row, installed service, bill credit, restored subsidy, or preserved public-service outcome.

## Source posture

This packet uses official FCC, USAC/Lifeline, NTIA, and Federal Register sources current enough for rev0758, with volatile source-currentness noted where programs or rules are implementation-clock or supersession-prone. For live cases, require provider, household, state broadband office, local library/school/clinic, outage, billing, installation, and public-service task records.
