# 855 — Applied cross-boundary case packet for International Health Regulations global alert waist: national focal points, PHEIC and pandemic emergency recommendations, core-capacity review, and no world health government by outbreak notice

## One-line thesis

The International Health Regulations should be governed as a **treaty-bound global alert and coordination waist**: States Parties carry legally binding duties to build capacities, assess, notify, verify, and respond to cross-border public-health risks, while WHO maintains early warning, convenes expert advice, issues temporary recommendations, and coordinates support without becoming a world health government able to command domestic law, lockdowns, vaccination policy, or policing.

## Why this matters

`854` tested `824` through a coercive regional inspection regime. The archive still needs the different upper-tier problem: a global narrow waist where the trigger is real, the treaty is legally important, the information flow is urgent, and the institutional temptation is to imagine either a powerless bulletin board or a world government.

The IHR pattern is a strong test because it combines legal obligation, national focal points, surveillance and response capacities, WHO early warning, emergency committees, PHEIC declarations, pandemic-emergency alerting after the 2024 amendments, temporary recommendations, points-of-entry rules, and safeguards for travel, trade, data, consent, and non-discrimination. Yet its honest form remains narrow. It is global enough to require a treaty waist and alert machinery; it is not general domestic health sovereignty.

### 6. Form holding

The honest form is **treaty-bound global alert and coordination waist**. It may require States Parties to designate responsible authorities, build and maintain capacities, assess public-health events, notify WHO, verify information, apply point-of-entry protections, exchange information, and participate in emergency advice and temporary-recommendation cycles. WHO may maintain early warning, coordinate international response efforts, provide support when requested, convene expert advice, publish statements, and issue recommendations within the IHR frame.

It must not be described as world health government. WHO recommendations, emergency statements, and alert labels do not by themselves enact domestic lockdowns, vaccination mandates, travel bans, criminal penalties, censorship, property seizures, or health budgets. Those acts, if taken, must be legally owned by competent domestic authorities or by separate international instruments with their own authorization and review.

### 8. Rival interpretations

| Rival rule | Why it is tempting | Why this packet rejects or limits it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pure national sovereignty | protects domestic legitimacy | fails when delayed notification or verification harms other states and global response |
| WHO command model | appears efficient in pandemics | overstates legal authority and can destroy trust by hiding domestic accountability |
| advisory bulletin only | avoids sovereignty fears | too weak for legal notification, verification, capacities, and emergency coordination |
| expert committee rule | respects scientific expertise | expert advice is not democratic, fiscal, police, or legislative authority by itself |
| treaty maximalism | treats legal obligation as evidence of full governance | mistakes a narrow waist for general government |
| conspiracy / denial frame | names real fear of overreach | often erases the actual legal boundaries, non-binding recommendation status, and national implementation route |

The preferred rule is **binding alert waist plus domestic legal ownership of coercive measures**, not world government and not optional rumor-sharing.

### 9. Review clock

Reopen this packet when any of the following occurs:

- the IHR text, amendment package, rejection / reservation status, or entry-into-force calendar changes;
- a PHEIC or pandemic emergency is declared, continued, modified, or terminated;
- temporary recommendations expire, are renewed, or become confused with binding domestic measures;
- National IHR Authorities or Focal Points are created, renamed, weakened, or left without cross-sector power;
- a country delays notification, refuses verification, or imposes travel / trade measures disproportionate to risk;
- emergency product-access, financing, or assistance routes fail during a cross-border event;
- misinformation about WHO authority or domestic sovereignty materially affects compliance or public trust;
- a new pandemic agreement, protocol, annex, or product-access mechanism changes the boundary between treaty coordination and domestic law.

A global alert waist should have a sharper review clock than ordinary policy because timeliness is part of its legitimacy.

## Failure modes

- **alert sovereignty theater**: pretending that a PHEIC or pandemic-emergency label itself governs domestic residents.
- **national denial theater**: hiding behind sovereignty while failing to notify, verify, or build basic capacities.
- **recommendation laundering**: treating non-binding temporary recommendations as if they were domestic law without enactment and review.
- **capacity paperwork**: reporting surveillance, laboratory, point-of-entry, or response capacity that cannot survive a real event.
- **trade panic**: imposing unnecessary or disproportionate travel / trade restrictions while claiming the IHR as cover.
- **expert advice opacity**: making emergency advice unreviewable by hiding membership, evidence, uncertainty, or dissent.
- **equity without route**: promising product access and solidarity without financing, logistics, allocation, or national-law interfaces.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Can a reader identify which IHR text applies to the state and whether any amendment rejection or reservation is live?
2. Can a public-health event be traced from signal to assessment to notification or verification to public statement?
3. Can the National IHR Focal Point / Authority be named, contacted, and tied to domestic legal power?
4. Can the file separate WHO advice, temporary recommendations, domestic legal measures, and individual-rights remedies?
5. Can a PHEIC or pandemic-emergency decision show evidence, expert advice, uncertainty, and review timing?
6. Can travel / trade restrictions show public-health necessity and proportionality rather than panic or politics?
7. Can capacity reports be tied to tested surveillance, laboratories, workforce, points of entry, and funded improvement plans?
8. Can the archive state exactly what WHO cannot compel?
