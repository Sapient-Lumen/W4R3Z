# 950 — Broadband, telecommunications, connectivity, digital access, Lifeline, ACP, BEAD, outage, and portal-continuity dockets: no access by coverage polygon

## One-line thesis

Digital access continuity is not a broadband coverage polygon, Broadband Serviceable Location, provider availability claim, speed tier, mobile-coverage layer, broadband label, Lifeline eligibility row, expired ACP enrollment, BEAD milestone, outage report, public Wi-Fi promise, or portal uptime metric. A household can appear served while the actual address, price, subscription, device, wiring, router, power, mobile signal, data cap, accessibility, language route, payment, outage, installation, or complaint remedy is broken. The archive should join location, provider, subscription, affordability, device, performance, outage, public-service dependency, and remedy records before treating a coverage row as access.

## Why this matters

The archive already covers credential access, platform migration, health benefits, housing, utilities, cyber incidents, elections, food, water, emergency response, and transportation. It still lacked a direct broadband and telecommunications access packet. That was risky because many other public-service dockets quietly assume connectivity: applying for benefits, receiving notices, joining telehealth, using school services, completing court tasks, responding to disasters, proving status, calling help, and appealing denials all fail if the household has no usable connection.

The governing repair phrase is **no access by coverage polygon**.

## Pattern pack

- **No access by coverage polygon.** A map, BSL, or provider availability row is denominator evidence, not proof of household connectivity.
- **No affordability by plan label.** A broadband label or posted price does not prove the household can pay, avoid fees, retain service, or survive a subsidy cliff.
- **No Lifeline by eligibility row.** Eligibility, verifier approval, or recertification notice does not prove enrollment, usable service, device, minutes/data, or continuity.
- **No ACP by historical enrollment.** The Affordable Connectivity Program ended for now; old enrollment records are not current affordability proof.
- **No BEAD by milestone.** Planning, challenge, award, and dashboard milestones do not prove construction, installation, activation, or adoption.
- **No outage repair by NORS/DIRS row.** Outage reporting, government sharing, or restoration statements must be joined to user-level restoration and public-service fallback.
- **No portal access by uptime.** A benefit, court, school, health, or tax portal can be up while the person lacks connectivity, device, authentication, accessibility, or assisted fallback.
- **No equality by complaint lane.** Digital-discrimination, consumer, or map-challenge routes are not repaired access unless the location, price, service, or remedy changes.

## Continuity docket

| Ledger | What it must preserve | Bad shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Household and location | Person/household, address, unit, BSL, tribal/rural/mobile status, public-service dependency, and urgency. | Treating a green dot as access. |
| Provider availability | Provider claims, technology, speed tier, installation feasibility, line extension, mobile signal, and challenge state. | Treating reported availability as serviceability. |
| Subscription and affordability | Plan, price, fees, deposit, data cap, subsidy, bill shock, shutoff, late fee, and low-cost alternative. | Treating a label as affordable service. |
| Lifeline / subsidy | National Verifier, eligibility, documentation, enrollment, annual recertification, benefit amount, tribal enhanced support, and loss notice. | Treating eligibility as usable connection. |
| Device and premises | Device, router, modem, in-home wiring, power, battery, accessible equipment, shared/public access, and maintenance. | Treating network availability as household readiness. |
| Performance and reliability | Measured speed, latency, congestion, data caps, throttling, outage, repair appointment, and restoration. | Treating advertised speed as usable service. |
| Public-service dependency | Benefits, telehealth, school, court, work, emergency, identity, notices, appeals, and status proof that require connectivity. | Treating portal uptime as user access. |
| Community anchors | Libraries, schools, clinics, shelters, public Wi-Fi, E-rate/RHC/anchor routes, opening hours, security, and privacy. | Treating public Wi-Fi as home access. |
| Equity and complaint | Map challenge, consumer complaint, digital-discrimination/equal-access lane, state broadband office, and local escalation. | Treating complaint availability as repair. |
| Source-currentness | Whether the source is map, provider self-report, challenge process, rule, dashboard, subsidy route, outage report, or user outcome proof. | Treating all broadband sources as live proof. |

## Minimum joined record

A serious broadband-continuity packet should contain at least:

1. **Household/location file.** Address, unit, BSL, household class, disability/language, public-service dependency, and consequence of disconnection.
2. **Availability file.** Provider claim, technology, speed tier, installation path, mobile coverage, serviceability answer, challenge/appeal state, and local verification.
3. **Subscription/affordability file.** Plan, price, fees, deposit, data cap, subsidy, bill due date, shutoff risk, and lower-cost alternative.
4. **Lifeline/subsidy file.** Eligibility, verifier state, enrollment, annual recertification, tribal support, ACP cliff, notices, and benefit loss.
5. **Device/premises file.** Device, modem/router, wiring, power, backup, assistive technology, shared device, public-access fallback, and privacy risk.
6. **Performance/reliability file.** Speed/latency tests, congestion, throttling, outage, repair appointment, restoration, and provider accountability.
7. **Public-service dependency file.** Portal, notice, identity, telehealth, school, court, benefit, tax, work, emergency, or appeal tasks that require connectivity.
8. **Community anchor file.** Library/school/clinic/shelter/public Wi-Fi hours, capacity, device availability, privacy, transport, and accessibility.
9. **Complaint/remedy file.** FCC/state complaint, map challenge, provider escalation, billing dispute, outage credit, installation remedy, and corrected source row.
10. **Currentness boundary.** Mark whether each source is live map, self-report, dashboard, rule, subsidy page, challenge process, outage record, or user outcome proof.

## Upgrade triggers

Route to the full broadband continuity docket when any of these appear:

- a coverage map, BSL, speed tier, provider row, mobile layer, or BEAD dashboard is cited as proof that a household has usable service;
- a public service requires online access, identity proofing, digital notice, telehealth, school platform, court portal, benefits portal, or digital evidence upload;
- affordability, subsidy loss, Lifeline recertification, ACP expiration, data caps, deposits, fees, or shutoff risk can interrupt service;
- rural, tribal, disability, language, housing instability, custody/reentry, care setting, disaster, or domestic-violence context changes access risk;
- an outage, cyber incident, power failure, damaged premises, provider repair delay, or public Wi-Fi fallback can strand the user;
- a complaint, map challenge, or equal-access route is used as remedy proof.

## Downshift conditions

A lighter treatment is acceptable only when the source is used as national broadband background and no household, address, serviceability, subsidy, portal, outage, device, public-service deadline, or remedy consequence is live. Once a person or class may lose practical connectivity, open the full packet.

## Failure modes

- **No access by coverage polygon:** the location appears served but installation, price, device, or performance fails.
- **No affordability by label:** posted prices or broadband labels do not capture actual household affordability or billing continuity.
- **No subsidy by verifier:** Lifeline or historical ACP state does not prove current, usable service.
- **No buildout by dashboard:** BEAD milestones do not prove fiber, fixed wireless, satellite, or mobile service is installed and adopted.
- **No portal by uptime:** the government system is online but the person is offline.
- **No outage repair by report:** provider filings or restoration claims do not prove household reconnection.
- **No digital equity by plan:** digital inclusion plans do not prove devices, skills, accessibility, privacy, or safe public access.
- **No remedy by challenge form:** map/consumer complaints do not repair service unless the row, bill, installation, or access changes.

## Audit questions

- Which household, address, unit, BSL, mobile area, or public-service task is at risk?
- Is the cited source a map, provider self-report, challenge record, label, subsidy page, dashboard, outage report, complaint lane, or user outcome?
- Can the provider actually install or activate service at the address on the necessary timeline and price?
- Does the household have a device, router, wiring, power, data allowance, language/accessibility route, and safe/private place to use the service?
- Is Lifeline/ACP/BEAD evidence current, or does it show only eligibility, past subsidy, planning, or program milestone state?
- Did an outage, provider repair delay, power event, cyber incident, or local disaster interrupt the public-service task?
- What complaint, challenge, credit, installation, subsidy, device, assisted channel, or offline fallback repaired the harm?

## Source posture

Use FCC National Broadband Map and BDC sources as map/availability evidence, not as adoption or affordability proof. Use Lifeline, National Verifier, ACP, BEAD, broadband labels, NORS/DIRS, and digital-discrimination/equal-access sources as separate evidence lanes. For live matters, require local provider records, household bills, installation tickets, speed/performance evidence, outage/restoration records, device/public-access records, portal task logs, and remedy receipts.
