# 915 — Watchlist, border, law-enforcement automation, biometric screening, redress, and no enforcement by match

## One-line thesis

Watchlist, border, and law-enforcement automation must split nomination, dissemination, screening hit, biometric candidate, officer confirmation, action, redress, deletion, and downstream correction; no enforcement action should be treated as justified by a match alone.

## Why this matters

The archive already has credential-access, model-decision, database-alert, transition-receipt, and supplier-dependency dockets. They are not enough for watchlist and border / law-enforcement automation, because this domain joins secrecy, national-security exceptions, identity uncertainty, law-enforcement urgency, biometrics, vendor systems, state/local users, and border consequences in one high-pressure chain.

The risky failure is not only that a system may be wrong. The risky failure is that several partial states can be collapsed into one word: **match**. A watchlist nomination can become a screening record; a screening record can become a name hit; a name hit can become an officer encounter; a face match can become a lead; a lead can become detention, secondary inspection, denied boarding, extra questioning, visa or passport friction, or a local police response; then redress may only touch one surface while stale copies, downstream alerts, or nonfederal training gaps remain.

The practical rule is: **no enforcement by match**. A match may open a controlled inquiry lane. It is not an adjudicated fact, a complete basis, or a portable justification unless the record shows the source, threshold, context, human confirmation, action authority, notice limits, redress route, correction propagation, and audit trail.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Decompose the match chain

| State | Governance question |
| --- | --- |
| nomination | Who nominated the person or record, under what criterion, and with what identifying information? |
| modification / deletion | What later information changed, merged, corrected, or removed the record? |
| dissemination | Which subset, agency, partner, or nonfederal system received which version? |
| screening hit | Was this a name/date-of-birth hit, biometric candidate, document alert, risk score, or manual officer suspicion? |
| confirmation | Who distinguished possible match, negative match, positive match, no match, inconclusive, and false hit? |
| action | What legal power supported delay, secondary, refusal, arrest, detention, search, seizure, questioning, referral, or information collection? |
| affected-person route | What, if anything, could the person see, request, challenge, or correct without compromising protected intelligence? |
| redress | Which office received the complaint, which record holders were consulted, what was changed, and what remains undisclosed? |
| propagation | Did correction or deletion reach TSA, CBP, State, FBI / TSC, NCIC, state and local systems, partners, vendors, and derived case files? |
| audit / oversight | Can inspectors, civil-rights offices, courts, Congress, PCLOB / GAO, and public summaries test both security utility and rights harm? |

A governance record that only says “hit,” “match,” “watchlist,” “biometric verified,” or “cleared” is not a record of public action. It is a surface label over a chain of partly secret, partly operational states.

### 2. Treat redress as propagation repair, not a mailbox

DHS TRIP, FOIA, litigation, agency ombuds routes, civil-rights complaints, and direct operational correction are not interchangeable. Redress works only if it distinguishes:

- a person incorrectly on a list;
- a person correctly listed but subject to mistaken identity;
- a stale or overbroad record that should be modified or deleted;
- a name collision that requires a recurring traveler / encounter disambiguation route;
- a non-watchlist screening reason that still creates travel or border burden;
- a downstream system that keeps reflecting a corrected upstream state.

The archive should not count redress as complete until it can show a correction receipt or a reasoned non-correction receipt for each relevant record holder or screening surface, subject to legitimate secrecy constraints.

### 3. Separate biometrics-as-lead from identity-as-fact

Facial recognition, contactless fingerprints, mobile field-capture tools, document-image comparison, gallery search, and watchlist biometric comparison can support inquiry. They do not decide identity by themselves. The record must preserve:

- image / capture context and quality;
- gallery searched and source authority;
- threshold, candidate rank, and confidence treatment;
- demographic / operational testing limits;
- manual comparison and second-review rule;
- training status of the user;
- prohibited reliance states;
- action taken after the candidate result;
- retention and deletion state for probe images, templates, and associated biographic data.

The harder the operational context is — street capture, protest adjacency, border encounter, low-quality image, third-party gallery, or time pressure — the more explicit the “candidate only” label and action boundary should be.

### 4. Nonfederal users are part of the system

When state, local, tribal, territorial, airport, carrier, foreign partner, contractor, or private infrastructure users receive alerts, the governance object has widened. Training, message wording, call-back instructions, encounter reporting, negative-match closure, and misuse limits become part of the federal system's effect.

A federal agency should not score a watchlist or biometric system as governed if the downstream users who act on the alert do not understand what the alert means, when to call back, what not to disclose, how to avoid escalation, how to report encounter facts, and how correction reaches them later.

### 5. Security utility must be paired with harm accounting

Watchlist and biometric systems can have real security, immigration, counterterrorism, anti-fraud, and identity-verification value. The archive should not erase that. But the security denominator must be paired with:

- false hits and false non-hits;
- additional screening, denial, detention, or referral counts;
- redress submissions, outcomes, deletions, and unresolved cases;
- demographic, nationality, religion, language, disability, and citizenship-status burden evidence where lawful and appropriate;
- training completion and prohibited-use violations;
- downstream correction latency;
- vendor / model / gallery change logs;
- incident and complaint learning loops.

A system that reports only encounters, arrests, overstays, or facilitation wins is measuring one side of a two-sided governance problem.

## Findings table

| Risk | Bad shortcut | Repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| watchlist hit | treat alert as identity and threat fact | possible / negative / positive / inconclusive match state and call-back log |
| biometric result | treat candidate as verification | candidate-only label, threshold, manual review, quality, and action boundary |
| redress | route complaint to one mailbox | propagation repair across record holders and screening surfaces |
| secrecy | refuse all explanation | safe explanation classes, correction receipts, and protected evidence lanes |
| nonfederal use | send NCIC or partner alerts without training evidence | outreach, state training review, message wording, and encounter feedback loop |
| border expansion | count throughput and security only | add secondary, delay, refusal, opt-out, manual review, and correction metrics |
| vendor / AI inventory | list use case without operational evidence | deployment state, model / gallery source, impact assessment, monitoring, and incident record |
| mobile capture | collect face / fingerprints / documents in the field | statutory basis, consent / compulsion state, retention, deletion, and challenge route |

## Failure modes

- **Match monism**: a name hit, biometric candidate, or risk score becomes the whole action basis.
- **Redress mailbox theater**: the person can submit an inquiry, but no visible correction propagates to all systems causing the burden.
- **Secret record fatalism**: classification becomes a reason to provide no safe explanation, no correction receipt, and no oversight surface.
- **Officer reliance drift**: training says “lead only,” but operational messages, deadlines, or incentives make the lead function like a command.
- **Nonfederal blind spot**: state and local users receive alerts but do not understand reporting, protection, or response requirements.
- **Biometric verification laundering**: a candidate result from a face or fingerprint search is marketed as identity verification.
- **Inventory laundering**: an AI use case appears in a public inventory without deployment-specific authority, testing, monitoring, incident, and redress evidence.
- **Propagation lag**: a corrected or deleted record continues to live in derived files, local systems, partner caches, or vendor logs.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Pick any watchlist or border hit. Can the record show nomination source, subset, match state, officer confirmation, action authority, and post-action outcome?
2. Pick any biometric candidate. Does the record prove it was treated as a lead, not as identity fact, before any coercive action?
3. Pick any redress case. Can correction, deletion, or non-correction be traced to every system that caused the person's burden?
4. Pick any NCIC or state/local encounter. Can the officer training, message wording, call-back instruction, and encounter reporting obligation be verified?
5. Pick any public AI inventory entry. Does it link to authority, testing, monitoring, privacy / civil-rights assessment, incident handling, and redress?
6. Pick any border biometric expansion. Are delays, manual review, opt-out / alternative process, secondary inspection, false match, and data-retention outcomes reported?
7. Pick any non-disclosure rationale. Is there still a safe explanation, receipt, or independent review route that does not expose protected intelligence?
8. Pick any corrected record. Is there a propagation receipt across federal, nonfederal, partner, and vendor surfaces?
