# 957 — Applied tax/refund continuity case packet for IRS filing, refunds, EITC, CTC, ITIN, Free File, identity theft, offsets, and no tax relief by return row

## One-line thesis

IRS filing-season statistics, refund-status tools, EITC/CTC tables, Free File/VITA/TCE options, ITIN pages, IP PIN/identity-theft controls, direct-deposit guidance, TAS refund help, and Treasury Offset Program materials are separate evidence lanes. None proves that a household actually received tax relief. The applied packet should use them to build a taxpayer-level chain from return filing to refund/credit receipt, offset/remedy, and hardship repair.

## Why this matters

Tax administration is a public-service delivery system. In 2026 the IRS expected about 164 million individual income-tax returns for tax year 2025, and filing-season data already showed over 140 million returns received by April 17, 2026. That scale makes small failure rates operationally huge. The archive should not let a return row, refund tracker, credit table, or offset record substitute for the household's actual liquidity and rights state.

The governing repair phrase is **no tax relief by return row**.

## Pattern pack

- **No relief by accepted return.** Acceptance is not processing, correctness, payment, or appeal closure.
- **No refund by Where's My Refund.** Status tools are navigation surfaces; proof of receipt requires payment/offset/deposit/check evidence.
- **No credit by table.** EITC/CTC tables are eligibility reference surfaces; person-level credits require qualifying records and TIN/filing proof.
- **No tax access by Free File option.** Free File, VITA/TCE, and TACs must be checked against eligibility, complexity, appointment capacity, state handoff, language, disability, and digital access.
- **No identity repair by IP PIN.** IP PINs prevent some misuse but do not by themselves process a held return or resolve victim-assistance inventory.
- **No refund safety by direct deposit.** Direct deposits can reject or freeze; bank-account control and paper-check fallback remain part of the continuity chain.
- **No debt justice by offset.** An offset record must be joined to debt origin, notice, agency contact, hardship, injured spouse, and restoration.
- **No administrative closure by TAS case.** Advocate involvement matters only when the refund, credit, notice, deadline, or account state is actually corrected.

## Source-to-duty map

| Source surface | What it proves | What it cannot prove alone |
|---|---|---|
| IRS filing-season statistics | Aggregate return, e-file, refund, and processing volumes. | Person-level refund, credit, or error resolution. |
| IRS refund pages / Where's My Refund | Status-check timing and account/refund navigation. | Payment receipt, bank acceptance, no offset, or identity clearance. |
| IRS EITC and CTC/refundable-credit pages | Eligibility, income, credit, and filing reference rules. | Actual household qualification, valid TIN, due diligence, or audit outcome. |
| IRS Free File / VITA / TCE pages | Free filing and in-person assistance options and income/access populations. | Appointment availability, state return completion, language/disability support, or preparer quality. |
| ITIN application/renewal pages | W-7 route, renewal risk, and processing-time expectations. | Credit eligibility, original-document return, return processing, or refund issuance. |
| IRS identity-theft/IP PIN pages | Identity-verification and preventive PIN routes. | Timely IDTVA resolution, refund release, or account correction. |
| TAS refund/annual-report/direct-deposit pages | Taxpayer-rights, delay, hardship, and refund-delivery warnings. | Automatic correction, payment, or deadline protection. |
| Treasury Offset Program / BFS materials | Federal payment offset mechanism and debtor information route. | Correct debt, hardship relief, injured-spouse outcome, or restored refund. |

## Applied continuity ladder

1. **Classify consequence.** Refund delay, credit denial, ITIN block, identity-theft hold, direct-deposit failure, offset, notice deadline, hardship, or filing-access failure.
2. **Bind taxpayer and household.** Taxpayer, spouse, dependents, TINs, address, bank account, representative/preparer, language/disability, and hardship stakes.
3. **Prove filing state.** Return year/type, e-file/paper, accept/reject, amendment/prior-year status, extension, free-file/VITA/TCE route, and state-return handoff.
4. **Prove credit state.** EITC, CTC/ACTC, refundable credits, qualifying children, residency, income, TIN status, due diligence, disallowance, recertification, and appeal.
5. **Prove identity/ITIN state.** W-7/new/renewal, original documents, CAA/VITA, IP PIN, TPP/identity letter, Form 14039, IDTVA, and dependent identity.
6. **Prove refund/payment state.** Refund tracker, transcript, issue date, direct deposit, rejected/frozen deposit, paper check, nonreceipt, and bank/prepaid account control.
7. **Prove offset/debt state.** TOP/BFS/IRS offset, creditor agency, notice, debt type, injured spouse, offset bypass or hardship route, and restored payment outcome.
8. **Prove remedy.** Notice response, e-request, phone/TAC/TAS, audit/exam appeal, Tax Court/deficiency deadline, corrected account, reissued refund, and household stabilization.

## Case diagnosis

The core failure pattern is **return-row capture**. Each source row can be true, yet the practical relief can still fail:

- Filing-season statistics prove scale and aggregate throughput; they do not prove a household's refund.
- Where's My Refund can show status; it does not prove bank acceptance, no offset, or household receipt.
- EITC and CTC pages state the law and thresholds; they do not prove a qualifying child, valid TIN, income, residency, or audit outcome.
- ITIN pages describe application/renewal routes; they do not prove return processing or credit eligibility during tax season.
- IP PIN and identity-theft pages describe prevention and verification; they do not prove rapid IDTVA resolution or refund release.
- TOP/BFS materials describe offset mechanics; they do not prove debt correctness, hardship, or injured-spouse relief.
- TAS pages describe rights and escalation; they do not prove correction until the account and payment state change.

## Repair packet

A repaired tax-relief packet should include:

1. **Household file:** taxpayer, spouse, dependents, TINs, address, representative/preparer, bank, language/disability, and hardship stakes.
2. **Return file:** tax year, form, filing method, accept/reject, processing, amendment/prior-year state, extension, and state-return handoff.
3. **Credit file:** EITC, CTC/ACTC, other refundable credits, qualifying-person evidence, valid TINs, income, due diligence, disallowance, and appeal.
4. **Identity/ITIN file:** IP PIN, identity-verification letter, Form 14039/IDTVA, W-7, renewal, original documents, and dependent identity.
5. **Refund/payment file:** refund amount, tracker/transcript, direct deposit, rejection/freeze, paper check, nonreceipt, bank/prepaid account, and payment proof.
6. **Offset/debt file:** TOP/BFS/IRS offset, creditor agency, notice, debt type, injured spouse, hardship/offset bypass, and returned funds.
7. **Remedy/outcome file:** notice response, e-request, TAS, appeal, corrected account, reissued refund, restored credit, and proof that food/rent/utility/childcare/debt harm was repaired.

## Failure modes

- E-file accepted but identity verification letter or IP PIN issue prevents processing.
- Return filed with an expired ITIN, delaying processing and reducing/refusing credits until renewal.
- EITC/CTC line exists but child, TIN, residency, income, or due-diligence evidence is missing or later disallowed.
- Where's My Refund reports sent, but the direct deposit is rejected, frozen, or routed to an inaccessible account.
- Refund is offset for child support, federal debt, state debt, or unemployment debt without practical hardship or injured-spouse review.
- Taxpayer tries to use Free File/VITA/TCE but income, return complexity, appointment capacity, language/disability, or state-return handoff breaks access.
- Identity-theft victim receives confirmation but waits months for account correction and refund release.
- Notice deadlines run while the person lacks mail stability, language access, representative authority, or portal/phone access.

## Negative controls

Do **not** use this packet to decide ordinary tax-policy questions where no filing, refund, credit, identity, ITIN, offset, notice, or hardship consequence is at issue. Do use it whenever tax records are being cited as proof that a person or household received relief, preserved a deadline, had enough income, paid a debt, or regained liquidity.
