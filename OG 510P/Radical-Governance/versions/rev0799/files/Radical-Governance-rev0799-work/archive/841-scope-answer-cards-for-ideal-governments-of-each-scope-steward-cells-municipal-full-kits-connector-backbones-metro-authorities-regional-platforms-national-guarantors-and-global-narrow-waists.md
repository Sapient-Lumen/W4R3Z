# 841 — Scope answer cards for ideal governments of each scope: steward cells, municipal full kits, connector backbones, metro authorities, regional platforms, national guarantors, and global narrow waists

## One-line thesis

The archive should carry one citation-ready answer-card layer for the question **what would the ideal government at each scope be?**: **micro-local steward cells for voice and upkeep; municipalities as the first full governments of ordinary life; connector, county, or intermunicipal backbones only where shared service scale is real; metropolitan authorities for named shared urban systems; regional or provincial governments for major territorial platforms and shared rule; national governments as guarantor states for equal citizenship, solidarity, macro-capacity, and constitutional order; continental or plurilateral bodies as bounded treaty compacts; and global governance as a narrow-waist treaty system for peace, commons, alerting, interoperability, and selected standards rather than a world state**.

## Why this matters

The archive already has the substantive answer in `541`, `565`, `579`, `628`, `629`, `660`, and `790`, then deepens it through the default lenses in `825` through `840`. That is now enough theory to answer the headline question without re-opening the whole stack.

The remaining archive risk is **answer diffusion**. Later notes can cite the regime-form default, fiscal default, adjudication default, or preemption default, yet still fail to give a reader the compact answer: **what government should this scope usually have, what kit makes it real, and what limit stops it becoming cosplay?**

This note is therefore not a new theory of scope. It is a **small answer-card object**. It makes the archive easier to use in applied design conversations, audits, and benchmark packets: each scope gets a vocation, a usual form, a minimum kit, a hard limit, and a proof burden for upgrade or handback.

After `842` and `843`, this note should also be treated as the **pocket canon**, not as permission to keep generating nearby scope-summary notes. New work should cite this card first, then add only a lens, case packet, opposition brief, source repair, or retirement map that passes the no-thesis-echo gate.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Treat the answer card as the pocket version, not the whole constitution

Use this note when the question is broad and practical: **what should the government at this scope be?**

Then reach for the deeper notes only when a live design question needs them: `825` for regime form, `826` through `828` for mandate, `829` and `830` for selection and decision rules, `831` for finance, `832` for oversight, `833` for coercion, `834` for information, `835` for emergency powers, `836` for participation, `837` for adjudication, `838` for guarantees, `839` for amendment, and `840` for override.

The answer card should be short enough to cite whole, but precise enough to prevent scope cloning.

### 2. Give every scope card five fields

A usable scope card should name:

- **vocation** — what kind of problem belongs there;
- **usual form** — the normal institutional shape;
- **minimum real kit** — the smallest set of offices, records, money, review, and participation channels that makes the form honest;
- **hard limit** — what the scope must not pretend to be;
- **proof burden** — what evidence justifies thickening, thinning, handback, or override.

If one of those fields is missing, the card is probably a slogan rather than a design object.

### 3. Micro-local steward cell

**Vocation.** Direct voice, witness, local knowledge, small-common upkeep, early warning, and escalation.

**Usual form.** Neighborhood assembly, ward committee, village council, block body, settlement forum, parish-style meeting, or another reachable steward cell recognized by the municipal or territorial charter.

**Minimum real kit.** Open meeting practice, simple minutes, public notice, a tiny discretionary fund where appropriate, a steward or rotating chair, a conflict-of-interest rule, a petition or escalation lane, and a clear statement of what the body may decide locally.

**Hard limit.** It should not become a miniature sovereign with broad taxation, ordinary policing, heavy debt, full licensing, or a standing veto against municipal law.

**Proof burden.** Thicken only when remoteness, indigenous or customary governance, legal history, or service-access failure proves that a light steward cell is not enough. Otherwise keep it high-voice and low-sovereignty.

### 4. Municipal full-kit government

**Vocation.** Ordinary place government: streets, public realm, sanitation, parks, local permits, libraries, local services, neighborhood implementation, civic front doors, and the daily visible encounter between person and state.

**Usual form.** Elected council with a mayor, cabinet, manager, or committee executive; professional administration; public budget; local rules; clerk or records home; procurement; complaint handling; and ordinary legality review.

**Minimum real kit.** Council, executive leadership, civil-service spine, treasury, own-source revenue or predictable transfers, record office, procurement rules, service standards, participation dockets, ombuds or complaint lane, and access to courts or administrative review.

**Hard limit.** It should not be an electoral shell whose budget, staff, platforms, and discretion are effectively controlled elsewhere; nor should it claim immunity from rights floors, national solidarity duties, or metropolitan systems that genuinely outrun its borders.

**Proof burden.** Preserve municipal headroom by default. Move functions upward only with named spillover, capacity, rights-floor, solidarity, or system-integrity evidence; return them when the evidence expires or the municipality can honestly carry the function again.

### 5. Connector, county, district, or intermunicipal backbone

**Vocation.** Shared service scale between municipality and region: rural coverage, county roads, libraries, records support, emergency dispatch, public health support, joint procurement, specialized technical staff, or back-office infrastructure that individual municipalities cannot sustain alone.

**Usual form.** County government where historically and territorially real; intermunicipal authority; special district; shared-service company; service union; joint board; or principal-owned backbone with bounded tasks.

**Minimum real kit.** Founding instrument, member or territorial representation, service inventory, budget and dues formula, records home, audit, complaint route, staff home, asset register, and exit or handback rules.

**Hard limit.** It should not become a hidden general-purpose government by accumulation of contracts, grants, data platforms, debt, or operator discretion.

**Proof burden.** Keep it a backbone if the need is technical, shared, and bounded. Upgrade toward a true public authority only when it holds durable coercive, fiscal, land-use, infrastructure, or rights-relevant power that deserves direct legitimacy and independent oversight.

### 6. Metropolitan shared-system authority

**Vocation.** The functional city: trunk transport, fare integration, spatial planning, major housing allocation formulas, watershed or flood systems, freight interfaces, regional utilities, air-quality coordination, emergency spillovers, and labor-market infrastructure.

**Usual form.** Democratically legitimated metropolitan authority with a public chamber, executive core, named system mandates, stable finance, transparent formulas, records and observatory capacity, and review lanes.

**Minimum real kit.** Whole-territory mandate source, system-specific legal powers, assigned revenue or formula transfers, capital plan, system observatory, public docket, procurement and asset rules, audit, complaint and legality review, and clear primacy inside named systems.

**Hard limit.** It should not be a mayoral summit, grant cartel, host-city workaround, or project office that is blamed for shared systems but unable to decide inside them. It also should not swallow unrelated municipal functions just because the metro tier exists.

**Proof burden.** Create or thicken only when repeated cross-boundary spillovers, shared infrastructure, fiscal misalignment, or emergency workarounds show that the city is already metropolitan in fact. Keep the mandate typed to the systems that prove it.

### 7. Regional, provincial, state, or major territorial platform government

**Vocation.** Large territorial platforms, constitutionally meaningful self-government, shared rule in a federation or devolved state, major health or education systems, grids, basins, corridors, disaster logistics, ecological landscapes, and other functions too broad for municipalities or metros but too place-specific for the nation to operate alone.

**Usual form.** Regional or state legislature, assembly-confidence executive or constitutionally defined administration, substantial budget, territorial civil service, audit and ombuds institutions, courts or review routes, and shared-rule participation in the national settlement where the bargain requires it.

**Minimum real kit.** Competence map, elected territorial forum, executive departments, own or assigned revenues, equalization link, records and statistics capacity, public procurement, independent audit, human-rights and equality implementation duties, and recourse when the center oversteps.

**Hard limit.** It should not be decorative duplication, patronage geography, or a ministry field office wearing an autonomy label. It also should not fragment equal citizenship or national solidarity floors.

**Proof burden.** Protect it strongly when it is part of a real constitutional bargain or carries real platform systems. Thin, merge, or redesign when the claimed platform is absent, the territory is ornamental, or the tier cannot carry its fiscal and administrative obligations.

### 8. National guarantor state

**Vocation.** Equal citizenship, rights floors, constitutional settlement, macroeconomic stabilization, broad taxation, redistribution and social insurance, defense and foreign relations, national infrastructure backbones, national statistics, apex courts, and independent integrity institutions.

**Usual form.** Constitutional state with parliament or assembly, accountable executive, independent judiciary, treasury, civil service, national audit, electoral administration, statistics authority, rights institutions, regulators, and rule-bound relations with subnational governments.

**Minimum real kit.** Justiciable rights floor, fiscal and equalization system, macro-capacity, national public-service standards, legislative procedure, court and review routes, emergency handback discipline, public records and gazette, and open intergovernmental machinery.

**Hard limit.** It should not become everything-government. Guarantee, solidarity, and system integrity are real national duties; they are not standing excuses to hoard every ordinary place function or erase lower-tier headroom through grants, platforms, or guidance.

**Proof burden.** Nationalize or preempt most strongly for rights, solidarity, macro-stability, security, cross-border harms, and interoperability. Prefer floors plus local headroom where that can satisfy the protected interest.

### 9. Continental, regional-international, or plurilateral compact

**Vocation.** Recurring cross-border problems below the planetary level: mobility, corridors, basins, energy grids, trade areas, research and industrial compacts, rights machinery, security cooperation, and regional dispute settlement.

**Usual form.** Treaty compact, union, council, court, agency network, parliamentary or assembly scrutiny body, secretariat, registry, fund, or standards body with explicit competences.

**Minimum real kit.** Treaty mandate, member-state participation, public instrument register, budget or dues rule, institutional seats, dispute route, audit or evaluation lane, amendment and exit provisions, and clarity about domestic legal effect.

**Hard limit.** It should not become an ambient upper state by elite habit, secretariat drift, donor leverage, emergency workaround, or judicial expansion without a visible competence basis.

**Proof burden.** Thicken only when the cross-border problem is recurrent, the domestic and bilateral lanes are insufficient, and the compact can name its legitimacy, finance, enforcement, and review chain.

### 10. Global narrow-waist governance

**Vocation.** Irreducibly global problems: peace and conflict restraint, planetary commons, global public-health alerting, minimum interoperability, selected technical and financial standards, treaty registries, scientific assessment, and dispute or compliance mechanisms where no smaller scale can close the problem.

**Usual form.** Charter organization, specialized agency, treaty conference, court or tribunal, scientific panel, registry, reporting mechanism, sanctions committee, standards network, or pooled assistance facility with explicit mandate and member-state anchoring.

**Minimum real kit.** Treaty or charter authority, assembly or conference of parties, secretariat, public registry, reporting schedule, dispute or compliance route, budget and assessed-contribution logic where real, and limits on enforcement unless expressly authorized.

**Hard limit.** It should not pretend to be a world municipality, world parliament, or universal executive of ordinary affairs. It also should not collapse into empty sovereigntist denial where real global commons or peace duties exist.

**Proof burden.** Use the global layer only when the problem is genuinely irreducible, the mandate is explicit, the reporting and compliance lane is public, and the legal effect is named rather than inferred from altitude.

### 11. Use the card stack as a routing device

When an applied case asks **what government belongs here**, begin with the smallest card that can honestly carry the work. Move upward only if one of the following is proved:

- spillovers outrun the smaller territory;
- the infrastructure is one system in fact;
- equal citizenship or due process needs a wider floor;
- fiscal risk-pooling or redistribution cannot be carried below;
- coercion or emergency capacity needs stronger review;
- the cross-border or global commons problem cannot be closed domestically;
- or the lower tier is in genuine distress and a lawful temporary intervention lane is active.

The answer should also name when the upward move expires, what capacity repair is expected, and what handback evidence would restore the lower scope.

### 12. Keep “ideal” conditional, not utopian

An ideal government is not the most participatory, centralized, decentralized, technocratic, or constitutionalized form imaginable. It is the smallest scope-matched institution that can carry the problem with enough legitimacy, money, records, review, and repair capacity.

That means an ideal stack can contain light steward cells, full municipalities, thin connectors, thick metropolitan authorities, strong regions, a national guarantor, treaty compacts, and global narrow waists without forcing them into one institutional species.

## Failure modes

- **answer diffusion** — the archive can cite forty supporting notes but cannot state the compact form that should govern the scope.
- **scope cloning** — every level imitates a nation-state, municipality, or agency regardless of problem fit.
- **connector creep** — shared-service or county backbones accumulate power without accepting direct legitimacy, audit, recourse, or exit rules.
- **metro invisibility** — the functional city is governed by incumbent bargains because nobody will admit that a real metropolitan authority is needed.
- **regional ornament** — a province or region survives as map nostalgia without platform, autonomy, or shared-rule work.
- **national absorption** — the guarantor state converts rights, efficiency, or solidarity into ordinary operational monopoly.
- **upper-tier aura** — continental or global bodies widen by prestige rather than by treaty, mandate, finance, and review.

## Practical tests

A scope answer card is ready to use when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Does it name the scope’s vocation in one sentence?
2. Does it say whether the form is a full government, a steward cell, a connector, a bounded authority, a compact, or a narrow-waist treaty body?
3. Does it include money, records, review, and participation rather than only elections or legal powers?
4. Does it state the anti-cosplay limit for that scope?
5. Does it give the proof burden for moving power up, down, or sideways?
6. Does it preserve municipal and regional headroom where floors suffice?
7. Does it keep metropolitan primacy tied to named shared systems?
8. Does it keep global authority explicit, treaty-bound, and narrow?

## Compression rule for the archive

If the archive can explain **why subsidiarity matters, why metropolitan systems need real authority, why regions need platform proof, why nations must guarantee equal citizenship, and why global governance must stay treaty-bound**, but cannot also produce **one short scope card naming the usual form, minimum kit, hard limit, and proof burden**, then the archive has analysis without an answer object.
