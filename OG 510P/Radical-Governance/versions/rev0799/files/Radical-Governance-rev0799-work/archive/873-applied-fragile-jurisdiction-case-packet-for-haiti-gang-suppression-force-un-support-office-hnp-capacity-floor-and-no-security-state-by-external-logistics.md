# 873 — Applied fragile-jurisdiction case packet for Haiti Gang Suppression Force / UN Support Office / HNP: capacity floor and no security state by external logistics

## One-line thesis

The honest form for Haiti's post-MSS security-support architecture is a fragile-jurisdiction international security-support and logistics waist around Haitian public responsibility: the Gang Suppression Force and UN Support Office may supply temporary capacity, protection, logistics, and pressure against armed groups, but they must not be read as a Haitian security state, a UN peacekeeping mission, a donor government, or proof of restored authority until access, staffing, funding, records, civilian-protection, accountability, and handback evidence exist.

## Why this matters

This packet tests the archive where formal authority and operating capacity are furthest apart. Haiti has a legal state and an urgent public-security need, but the relevant governance surface includes armed-group territorial control, displacement, humanitarian access, police and justice capacity, donor money, international contingents, logistics, allegations around private-force support, and public accountability for civilian harm. A normal form verdict would be misleading unless it first proves the capacity floor.

The case also prevents two opposite mistakes. The first is treating international support as a foreign government, erasing Haitian public responsibility. The second is treating a Security Council mandate, support office, or contingent arrival as restored state capacity. The useful holding sits between them: a temporary security-support waist must be bounded, rights-constrained, record-preserving, funding-honest, and tied to handback.

## Case packet

### 1. Trigger

Haiti presents the archive's missing low-capacity stress test. The formal state remains the legal owner of security, public order, justice, elections, humanitarian access, and territorial administration, but armed groups, displacement, under-resourced police, weakened courts, donor dependence, private-force allegations, and interrupted services make ordinary administrative assumptions unsafe.

The immediate institutional trigger is the transition from the Kenya-led Multinational Security Support mission to the Security Council-authorized Gang Suppression Force, alongside the UN Support Office in Haiti. The authorization expands the support architecture while still emphasizing Haitian responsibility. That combination is exactly where the archive needs a capacity-floor docket: large mandate, thin host capacity, international logistics, voluntary support, urgent humanitarian need, and high risk of both underbuilt mission theater and shadow-sovereignty drift.

### 2. Form verdict

**Fragile-jurisdiction international security-support and logistics waist under Haitian public responsibility.**

This is not ordinary subsidiarity, because the local and national operating floors are partly broken. It is not a global or UN government, because the support arrangement is supposed to assist Haitian institutions rather than replace them. It is not merely symbolic, because armed personnel, logistics, detention / arrest authority, intelligence, infrastructure protection, and security operations can change life, movement, humanitarian access, and political possibility. It is also not a complete security authority unless staffing, funding, rules, records, accountability, and handback are proven.

### 3. Lower-form test

A lower form such as ordinary technical assistance, police training, sanctions alone, or humanitarian coordination alone is too thin where armed groups block access, attack infrastructure, displace people, and overwhelm police capacity. But a thicker form, such as trusteeship, protectorate, or externally run security government, is too thick and would hollow out Haitian public responsibility.

The minimum honest form is therefore a support waist with hard boundary labels:

| Boundary | Required label |
| --- | --- |
| domestic owner | Haitian authorities retain primary public responsibility |
| external force | temporary authorized support with defined powers and review |
| UN support office | logistics and operational support, not general government |
| donors | funding and equipment contributors, not public principals |
| humanitarian actors | independent protection and service lanes, not force auxiliaries |
| affected people | rights holders with complaint, protection, and remedy claims |
| armed groups | coercive risk field, not a reason to erase state-duty labels |

### 4. Capacity-floor table

| Field | Current stress | Required repair surface |
| --- | --- | --- |
| personnel | prior mission operated far below intended strength; new force authorization is larger than deployed reality | deployed-roster ledger by contingent, function, location, command, training, and rotation |
| funding | voluntary or donor-dependent support can lag behind mandate | pledge / receipt / disbursement / usable-asset ledger |

### 6. Capacity-test docket

The case activates the full capacity-floor matrix:

1. **Mandate-capacity match** — authorization, mandate language, and force size must be compared to deployed staff, funds, equipment, access, and command.
2. **Host-state owner** — every support function must name the Haitian public owner and what ordinary institution should inherit it.
3. **Pledge-to-deployment** — pledges must be traced to delivered people, equipment, money, fuel, facilities, and services.
4. **Access-class honesty** — territorial or service claims must be labelled by access class.
5. **Civilian-harm accountability** — force, detention, drone, contractor, and self-defense incidents require complaint and investigation routes.
6. **Humanitarian boundary** — humanitarian agencies must not be folded into force legitimacy or operational targeting.
7. **Justice-chain continuity** — arrest / detention powers must be tied to prosecution, courts, custody conditions, evidence, and release routes.
8. **Private-force and contractor clarity** — drones, advisors, logistics contractors, and private security support need public command, procurement, incident, and remedy records.
9. **Handoff ladder** — transition or exit must be judged by domestic capacity receipt, not mission title.

### 7. Opposition and rival readings

**Rival 1: international force as necessary emergency floor.** On this reading, local capacity is so degraded that outside security support is the minimum condition for humanitarian access, elections, courts, and public life. The archive should not reject that out of localist reflex.

**Rival 2: externalized security as repeated failure.** On this reading, decades of interventions have created dependency, resentment, rights risk, and no durable Haitian capacity. The archive should not dismiss that as ideology; it is an accountability claim.

**Rival 3: security-first sequencing.** On this reading, governance, elections, services, and courts cannot recover until armed groups are suppressed. The archive should ask for the handoff ladder that shows how security gains become ordinary public authority.

**Rival 4: justice-and-services-first sequencing.** On this reading, force without courts, prisons, schools, clinics, jobs, and trust will only displace violence. The archive should ask whether operations are connected to civil administration and rights repair.

The packet's holding is not that one rival is right. It is that all four must be preserved as evidence lanes before mandate renewal, expansion, withdrawal, or praise.

### 8. Capture and theater channels

- **mandate theater**: the Security Council authorization is read as if it deploys capacity.
- **donor theater**: pledges and conferences are read as budget execution.
- **deployment theater**: arrival of contingents is read as territorial control.
- **support-office creep**: logistics, housing, mobility, intelligence, and procurement become the practical state.
- **command fog**: Haitian police, armed forces, international force, donors, UN support, and contractors act without replayable decision records.
- **human-rights paper shield**: complaint mechanisms exist but do not produce investigation, remedy, or public findings.
- **private-force invisibility**: drone or contractor operations affect life and territory without ordinary public-law files.
- **election laundering**: security operations are justified by elections without an honest electoral access, intimidation, and legitimacy record.
- **humanitarian dependency**: aid access becomes proof that security operations are legitimate, even when humanitarian actors need independence.

### 9. Repair surfaces

The packet should remain open until these records exist or are clearly marked absent:

- deployed-force roster by contingent, function, location, command, rotation, and training;
- pledge / receipt / disbursement / equipment-delivery ledger;
- access-class map for roads, neighborhoods, ports, airports, courts, hospitals, schools, police stations, prisons, and aid corridors;
- incident, casualty, detention, arrest, seizure, and use-of-force logs;
- complaint, whistleblower, investigation, remedy, and public-finding routes;
- command / coordination records among Haitian police, armed forces, GSF, UNSOH, donors, humanitarian actors, and contractors;
- humanitarian deconfliction record;
- custody-to-court chain and justice-capacity dashboard;
- asset and logistics register;
- handback ladder naming the Haitian institution that inherits each function.

### 10. Holding

The GSF / UNSOH / HNP architecture should be classified as a **fragile-jurisdiction security-support waist**, not as a restored Haitian security state and not as a foreign security government. Its legitimacy depends on whether temporary external capacity is bounded, recorded, reviewable, rights-constrained, funding-honest, locally owned, and connected to a real handback ladder.

## Pattern pack

Use this packet whenever an international, donor, peace-support, stabilization, emergency, or technical-assistance structure is proposed for a jurisdiction where ordinary administrative floors are broken. The pattern is:

1. name the domestic owner;
2. identify the external capacity supplied;
3. label access class;
4. separate pledge from deployment;
5. preserve incident, asset, payment, custody, and complaint records;
6. protect humanitarian independence;
7. connect security action to justice and civil administration;
8. publish a handback ladder;
9. require a fresh trigger for renewal or expansion.

## Failure modes

- **security state by logistics**: whoever controls housing, fuel, mobility, maintenance, and communications becomes the real security state.
- **authorization without arrival**: the mandate promises more personnel, equipment, and capability than is deployed.
- **arrest without justice**: detention authority is not matched by prosecution, courts, custody conditions, evidence handling, or release routes.
- **humanitarian-hostage governance**: aid access becomes dependent on security-force legitimacy.
- **civilian-harm opacity**: operations produce deaths, injuries, displacement, or arbitrary detention without independent investigation.
- **mission relabeling**: a failed or underbuilt mission is renamed, expanded, or renewed without capacity proof.
- **handoff vacuum**: temporary support creates no domestic institution able to inherit the function.

## Claim-status note

This packet is a **case diagnosis** and **threshold frontier**. It should be reviewed whenever the GSF mandate is renewed, major contingents deploy or withdraw, a trust-fund or donor shortfall materially changes operations, a civilian-harm report is issued, a political transition changes Haitian consent, or a handback / election milestone is claimed.
