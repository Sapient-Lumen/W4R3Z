# 854 — Applied cross-boundary case packet for regional port-state-control inspection regime: risk-based inspections, detention and banning route, flag-state safety net, and no maritime government by shared badge

## One-line thesis

A regional port-state-control regime should be governed as a **bounded regulatory/enforcement coordination waist**: participating maritime authorities may harmonize inspection targeting, board foreign ships in national ports, record deficiencies, detain unsafe ships, exchange data, and refuse access under published rules, but the shared badge must not become a hidden maritime government over trade, labor, port policy, shipping markets, or flag-state sovereignty.

## Why this matters

`853` made `823` concrete by testing a contract-heavy crossing. The remaining pressure point in the `812`–`824` chain is `824`: a case where the cross-boundary object is not a metropolitan authority, not a shared-service hub, not a basin platform, not a utility asset ledger, and not a P3 project agreement. It is a joined-up inspection and enforcement regime.

Port state control is a strong `824` test because it is coercive but narrow. A port authority or maritime administration may board a foreign ship, check certificates, inspect condition, record deficiencies, detain the ship, or trigger refusal of access. The reason to coordinate regionally is also real: ships move across many ports, and repeated uncoordinated inspection can both miss substandard vessels and harass compliant ones. The honest form is therefore neither isolated national enforcement nor a free-standing maritime sovereign. It is a regional inspection waist with public law, data, targeting, detention, appeal, and proportionality surfaces.

### 6. Form holding

The honest form is **bounded regional regulatory/enforcement coordination waist**. Participating authorities may share data, agree inspection commitments, prioritize high-risk ships, publish procedures, coordinate concentrated campaigns, record deficiencies, detain unsafe ships, refuse access under rules, and compare results.

They may not turn the inspection waist into general maritime policy. The regime should not determine port development, shipping competition, labor bargaining, customs entry, immigration, trade sanctions, national prosecution policy, or flag-state administration unless the competent public authority independently holds and exercises that power through the correct route.

### 8. Rival interpretations

| Rival rule | Why it is tempting | Why this packet rejects or limits it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| flag-state-only rule | respects primary responsibility for ship standards | fails when substandard ships can enter foreign ports and move through regional trade routes |
| port-alone inspection | keeps legal authority local | loses risk history, creates duplication, and weakens regional deterrence |
| regional maritime government | names the cross-border reality | overbuilds beyond the inspection / safety / pollution-prevention waist |
| industry self-certification | lowers inspection burden | cannot substitute for public boarding, detention, and public record where safety risk is material |
| database-first enforcement | appears efficient and objective | hides the judgment, evidence, correction, and appeal rules that make coercion legitimate |

The preferred rule is **public inspection authority plus regional data and consistency waist**, not isolated ports, industry self-policing, or secretariat sovereignty.

### 9. Review clock

Reopen this packet when any of the following occurs:

- the memorandum, selection scheme, inspection procedure, ship-risk profile, or banning rule is materially amended;
- a detention, refusal of access, or campaign exposes inconsistent officer practice or poor evidence;
- inspection data are wrong, stale, non-correctable, or reputationally punitive without remedy;
- a new convention, environmental rule, security requirement, or labor standard is added to the inspection scope;
- a data platform, inspection support contract, or private provider starts shaping inspection priorities and triggers `823`;
- a port, coastal community, or industry actor shows that burden, delay, or fees are distorting inspection choices;
- a regional secretariat begins making policy choices that belong to participating authorities or legislatures.

The badge should become more visible as its effects become more coercive.

## Failure modes

- **shared-badge sovereignty**: pretending that because many authorities use the same inspection grammar, the regional regime may exercise general maritime power.
- **database detention**: letting risk-profile scores drive detention without officer evidence and correction routes.
- **campaign theater**: running concentrated campaigns that look like action but cannot show purpose, scope, results, or follow-through.
- **flag-state blame loop**: using flag-state responsibility to avoid port-state correction, or using port-state control to erase flag-state obligations.
- **appeal mirage**: offering appeals that cannot correct records, remedy disproportionate action, or improve future targeting.
- **fee-fed enforcement**: letting inspection costs, detention incentives, or publication effects distort safety judgment.
- **secretariat creep**: allowing coordination staff or databases to become the real rulemaker.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Can the file show exactly what legal authority allowed the officer to board, inspect, detain, release, or refuse access?
2. Can a ship see why it was selected and what evidence supports each deficiency?
3. Can the public distinguish a detention, a deficiency, a ban, a campaign result, and a mere risk-profile signal?
4. Can errors in inspection data be corrected without erasing necessary safety history?
5. Can the regime prove it targets substandard ships rather than merely repeating easy inspections?
6. Can participating authorities show who owns the appeal route and who owns the safety decision?
7. Can the archive name what powers the regional memorandum does **not** hold?

## Reconstruction instruction

`854` converts `824` from a template into an applied case field. Treat inspection authority, officer qualification, selection rules, deficiency evidence, detention / release, refusal of access, data correction, appeal, and regional audit as the minimum regulatory/enforcement waist. After `854`, the remaining pre-consolidation test is treaty / global narrow-waist governance, because coercion under a shared badge is not the same problem as global alerting under a treaty.
