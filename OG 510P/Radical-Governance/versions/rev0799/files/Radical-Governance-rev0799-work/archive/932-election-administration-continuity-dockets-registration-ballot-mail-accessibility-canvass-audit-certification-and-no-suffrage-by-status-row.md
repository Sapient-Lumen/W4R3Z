# 932 — Election administration continuity dockets: registration, ballot mail, accessibility, canvass, audit, certification, and no suffrage by status row

## One-line thesis

An election is not proven by a registration row, polling-place lookup, mailpiece scan, accessible-machine checkbox, unofficial result, audit slogan, or certified total. Election continuity needs a joined docket across eligibility, registration, ballot issuance, voting channel, accessibility, mail and custody, provisional and cure paths, tabulation, audit, canvass, certification, recount, contest, and voter-facing remedy.

## Governing repair phrase

**No election by certified total.**

## Why this matters now

The archive already has democratic representation, political competition, and multi-level government doctrine, but it has lacked a practical election-administration waist. That is dangerous because modern elections are operational systems: voter-registration feeds, motor-vehicle and agency registration routes, list maintenance, ballot styles, mail contracts, drop boxes, polling places, e-pollbooks, accessible voting devices, tabulators, canvass boards, audits, certification schedules, recount windows, and court-contest routes all have to line up.

The riskiest failures are not always wholesale fraud or dramatic cyber compromise. They are often continuity failures: a voter is marked inactive without useful notice, a mail ballot is issued but not received, a postmark does not carry the legal meaning a voter expected, a polling place is technically open but inaccessible, a provisional ballot has no visible cure route, an unofficial result is treated as final, or a certified total closes the public narrative before the audit, recount, contest, and remedy tails are intelligible.

## Split the docket before scoring the election

Use six ledgers, not one legitimacy story.

| Ledger | What it asks | Bad shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Eligibility and registration | Who is eligible, registered, active, inactive, moved, challenged, restored, or excluded? | Treating the registration database as the vote right. |
| Ballot access | Which ballot style, language, accessibility, military/overseas, absentee, early, Election Day, or provisional route was available? | Treating a ballot request, portal status, or polling-place lookup as ballot access. |
| Custody and channel | How did the ballot move through mail, drop box, polling place, chain of custody, scanner, tabulator, and storage? | Treating a mailpiece scan or precinct report as counted vote proof. |
| Exception and cure | Which signature, ID, address, eligibility, provisional, accessibility, or technical issue opened a cure path? | Treating cure as real without notice, time, proof, and assistance. |
| Count and audit | How were ballots tabulated, adjudicated, reconciled, audited, recounted, and canvassed? | Treating unofficial results, "100% precincts reporting," or a thin audit as final truth. |
| Certification and remedy | Who certified, what contests or recounts remain, and what voter-level or candidate-level remedies are still open? | Treating certification as proof that no one was wrongly excluded. |

## Minimum joined docket

A usable election-continuity packet includes:

1. **Eligibility and registration state.** Legal eligibility, registration route, effective date, address, precinct, inactive status, list-maintenance notice, challenge, restoration, and voter-facing verification.
2. **Ballot-style and channel map.** Correct ballot style, language and disability access, early-vote option, Election Day location, mail/absentee request, UOCAVA route, emergency ballot, and provisional fallback.
3. **Mail and custody clock.** Issue date, outbound delivery, voter possession, return method, postmark or acceptance evidence, drop-box or office receipt, signature/ID screening, chain of custody, and legal counting deadline.
4. **Polling-place and e-pollbook continuity.** Site accessibility, staffing, line management, check-in failure mode, paper backup, address correction, same-day or provisional route where available, equipment failure, and emergency extension.
5. **Accessible voting proof.** Accessible registration, website/app content, polling-place access, ballot-marking privacy and independence, alternative formats, assistance-of-choice boundaries, drop-box access, and mail-voting communication aids.
6. **Provisional, challenge, and cure ledger.** Reason code, evidence needed, notice channel, deadline, assistance route, outcome, and public denominator for rejected, cured, counted, and unresolved ballots.
7. **Tabulation and adjudication record.** Scanner, tabulator, central count, ballot duplication, write-in and overvote adjudication, vote-center reconciliation, batch totals, hand-count or audit evidence, and discrepancy disposition.
8. **Canvass, certification, recount, and contest ladder.** Unofficial result date, canvass board action, audit timing, certification authority, recount threshold or petition route, contest forum, public records, and final result boundary.
9. **Cyber, vendor, and physical-security dependency.** E-pollbooks, election-management systems, ballot-on-demand, voting systems, websites, reporting feeds, vendor access, malware risk, DDoS, ransomware, and physical intimidation or disruption.
10. **Voter-facing source-of-truth map.** Official election office pages, hotlines, translated materials, accessibility channels, result pages, correction notices, and warnings against treating third-party summaries as authority.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Registration status is not suffrage

A registration row can show only one administrative state. It does not prove notice, address correctness, list-maintenance lawfulness, language access, disability access, ballot style, polling-place assignment, or ability to cure a mistake before the voting opportunity ends.

### 2. Mail voting is a custody-and-deadline chain

A mailed ballot is not one event. It includes request, issuance, delivery, voter return, postal possession, postmark or acceptance evidence, election-office receipt, signature or ID review, cure, acceptance, counting, and voter notice. A postmark rule or mail-visibility tool should be treated as a channel-specific evidence surface, not as the vote itself.

### 3. Accessibility is not an equipment checkbox

An accessible voting machine at a polling place does not answer whether registration, website content, ballot requests, vote-by-mail materials, drop boxes, paths of travel, lines, communication, assistance, and privacy/independence worked.

### 4. Provisional ballots are not a safety valve unless cure works

A provisional ballot protects suffrage only when the reason code is correct, the voter is told what to do, the proof route is reachable, the deadline is realistic, and the counted/rejected outcome is visible.

### 5. Certification is not the end of evidence

Certification can be legally final for a result while still requiring public explanation of audits, recounts, contests, rejected ballots, cure rates, accessibility complaints, system outages, and records needed for future repair.

## Upgrade triggers

Route the matter to a full election-continuity docket when any of the following are true:

- voter registration, address, party, inactive, citizenship, felony, age, or list-maintenance state can block ballot access;
- a mail, absentee, UOCAVA, drop-box, early-vote, or emergency-ballot route carries a deadline or custody risk;
- polling-place access depends on e-pollbooks, ballot-on-demand, vote centers, accessible equipment, temporary remedies, or staff discretion;
- provisional, challenged, signature-cure, ID-cure, or eligibility-cure ballots could affect voters or public trust;
- unofficial result pages, precinct-reporting percentages, audit claims, or certification announcements are being cited as complete proof;
- cyber, vendor, DDoS, ransomware, website, reporting-feed, or physical-security events touch election operations.

## Downshift conditions

A lighter information-page or election-law summary is enough only when no voter-facing deadline, channel, accessibility, provisional, custody, counting, audit, certification, recount, or contest consequence is live. If a voter can lose the opportunity to cast or have a ballot counted, stay at the full continuity level.

## Failure modes

- **No suffrage by status row:** registration status is treated as the vote right.
- **No ballot by request receipt:** a ballot request confirmation is treated as receipt, marking, return, acceptance, and counting.
- **No mail vote by postmark mythology:** postmark, scan, or visibility evidence is used beyond what it proves.
- **No accessibility by machine:** one accessible device substitutes for whole-process access.
- **No cure by envelope:** provisional or challenged ballots are counted as safeguarded without notice, proof, and outcome evidence.
- **No result by unofficial feed:** "100% precincts reporting" is treated as certified, audited, or uncontestable.
- **No audit by slogan:** an audit label is used without sample, scope, discrepancy, timing, and public-record boundaries.
- **No election by certified total:** certification is used to erase remaining voter-level or system-level repair duties.

## Audit questions

- What is the first point at which a voter can wrongly lose access, and what proof would reveal it?
- Which deadline is hardest for voters to see: registration, ballot request, postmark, receipt, cure, provisional proof, recount, or contest?
- Which systems can fail on Election Day, and what paper, staffing, or extension route keeps voting open?
- Which accessibility obligations apply before Election Day, not only at the voting booth?
- Which unofficial result, audit, certification, or recount surface is being asked to prove more than it can prove?
- Which election records must remain available for public trust without exposing ballot secrecy or voter privacy?

## Source posture

This note uses official election-administration, postal, accessibility, military/overseas voting, audit, certification, and election-security sources: EAC EAVS and election-administration resources, CISA election-security resources, USPS Election Mail and postmark materials, FVAP/UOCAVA materials, ADA.gov voting accessibility guidance, and current local election-office pages. These sources are necessary, but the packet should not treat any one of them as the whole election record.
