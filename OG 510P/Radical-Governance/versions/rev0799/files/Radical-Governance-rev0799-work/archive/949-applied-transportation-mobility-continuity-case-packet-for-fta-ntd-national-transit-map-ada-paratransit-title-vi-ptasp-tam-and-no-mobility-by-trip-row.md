# 949 — Applied transportation mobility continuity case packet for FTA NTD, National Transit Map, ADA paratransit, Title VI, PTASP, TAM, and no mobility by trip row

## One-line thesis

FTA National Transit Database, BTS National Transit Map, FTA ADA/paratransit guidance, FTA civil-rights complaint and Title VI materials, Public Transportation Agency Safety Plans, Transit Asset Management, and GAO rural/tribal and asset-management oversight reports should be read as separate evidence lanes. They cannot be collapsed into proof that a rider reached a stop, boarded an accessible vehicle, completed a transfer, paid a fare, received paratransit, avoided a safety hazard, or repaired a missed trip.

## Why this matters

This applied packet turns the mobility docket into an operational source surface. The sources are strong enough to show national reporting, route/feed architecture, ADA and Title VI obligations, safety planning, asset-management frameworks, and rural/tribal access limits. But each source has a narrow lane. A route map can show scheduled service; a paratransit rule can set the service envelope; an NTD table can show service statistics; a Title VI program can show compliance posture; a TAM target can show asset management. None of those proves practical mobility for a rider.

The governing repair phrase is **no mobility by scheduled trip row**.

## Pattern pack

- **No mobility by scheduled trip row.** Schedule, NTD, or GTFS evidence starts the inquiry; it does not close it.
- **No fixed-route access by National Transit Map.** National Transit Map data supports analysis and is not realtime navigation or a rider access guarantee.
- **No paratransit by ADA boundary.** The 3/4-mile corridor, eligibility, or comparable-service rule does not prove booking, pickup, assistance, ride quality, or return trip.
- **No complaint remedy by form.** FTA/local ADA or civil-rights complaint routes do not prove that a missed trip was repaired.
- **No equity by Title VI program.** Title VI filings and service standards must be joined to specific service/fare changes and affected rider outcomes.
- **No safety by PTASP.** Agency safety plans and national safety guidance must be joined to hazards, incidents, mitigations, and rider/operator experience.
- **No reliability by TAM target.** Asset measures and state-of-good-repair targets must be joined to local disruption and accessible-route consequences.
- **No rural/tribal access by grant program.** Funding programs and technical assistance do not prove service availability, staffing, vehicles, or essential-trip completion.

## Source-to-duty map

| Source surface | What it proves | What it cannot prove alone |
|---|---|---|
| FTA NTD home, data, and 2024 service database | National reporting lane for operating, financial, asset, service, ridership, and safety statistics. | That a particular trip operated, boarded a rider, or completed a journey. |
| BTS National Transit Map | Nationwide catalog of fixed-guideway/fixed-route service from GTFS Schedule data, with quarterly updates and known intended-use limits. | Realtime operation, accessibility, fare, crowding, detour, disruption, or person-level navigation proof. |
| FTA ADA guidance and FAQ | Federal ADA/Section 504 obligations, complementary paratransit service area, comparability, and accessibility vocabulary. | Eligibility, booking, pickup, assistance, ride, return trip, or complaint repair for a person. |
| FTA complaint and Title VI materials | Civil-rights filing and program-compliance lanes for ADA, Title VI, LEP, fare/service equity, and local process. | That a rider received equal service or that a complaint remedied a missed trip. |
| PTASP materials | Safety-management planning obligations and safety-risk themes for federally supported transit systems. | That a specific stop, route, vehicle, operator, or incident was safe or repaired. |
| TAM / performance-management materials | Asset-management, state-of-good-repair, target, inventory, and performance-measure architecture. | That an elevator, bus, railcar, track segment, or paratransit vehicle was available for a rider. |
| GAO rural/tribal and TAM oversight | Independent limits on funding/access effectiveness and asset-performance data interpretation. | Local availability, staffing, essential-trip completion, or rider-level reliability. |

## Applied continuity ladder

1. **Classify the mobility consequence.** Medical, work, school, food, court, benefits, voting, family, evacuation, shelter, recreation, or ordinary civic participation.
2. **Bind rider and trip.** Rider class/person, disability/access need, language, age, fare credential, companion/PCA, origin, destination, deadline, and harm from failure.
3. **Separate source surfaces.** Mark each source as national report, map/feed, rule/guidance, civil-rights process, safety plan, asset target, oversight report, or local operational proof.
4. **Prove fixed-route availability.** Schedule, GTFS, realtime/AVL/APC where available, cancellations, detours, headways, transfer protection, crowding, and customer notice.
5. **Prove accessible path and vehicle.** Stop/station approach, curb ramps, elevators/escalators, lift/ramp, securement, operator assistance, wheelchair space, and safe boarding/alighting.
6. **Prove fare usability.** Fare medium, reduced fare, cash/mobile/account fallback, fare cap, transfer, paratransit fare, card replacement, refund, and locked-account repair.
7. **Prove ADA/paratransit delivery.** Eligibility, visitor status, reservation, negotiation, pickup window, origin-to-destination assistance, no-show policy, vehicle fit, dropoff, return trip, and complaint.
8. **Prove equity/safety/asset effects.** Title VI/LEP participation, major service/fare change analysis, PTASP hazard mitigation, TAM asset state, elevator/vehicle outages, and operator/rider safety.
9. **Prove rural/tribal and essential-service continuity.** Funding route, service area, provider capacity, staffing, vehicle condition, essential destinations, inter-provider handoffs, and fallback.
10. **Close with remedy.** Did the rider reach the destination, miss the trip, receive an alternate trip, get reimbursed, preserve a deadline, and trigger corrective action?

## Applied examples

### NTD and National Transit Map

Use NTD and BTS National Transit Map data to understand national service, map, route, and reporting surfaces. Then require agency schedules, realtime operations, rider-facing notices, stop/station access, fare, and incident records before treating a service row as mobility.

### ADA and paratransit

Use FTA ADA guidance and FAQ to define complementary paratransit and accessibility obligations. Then check local eligibility, reservations, same-day negotiation, pickup windows, vehicle fit, driver assistance, no-show decisions, return trip, and complaint resolution.

### Title VI and language access

Use FTA Title VI guidance and circular materials to frame service standards, fare/service equity, and LEP access. Then check actual service/fare changes, outreach, translations, demographic/ridership denominators, mitigations, and rider outcomes.

### PTASP and safety

Use PTASP materials to identify required safety-management lanes. Then check stops, vehicles, assaults, pedestrian conflicts, operator staffing/training, hazard reports, safety-risk mitigations, and incident learning.

### TAM and state of good repair

Use TAM and performance-management materials to define asset categories and state-of-good-repair targets. Then join elevator, vehicle, facility, guideway, bridge, station, and paratransit vehicle conditions to actual disruptions and rider consequences.

### Rural and tribal access

Use GAO rural/tribal transit findings to keep funding/technical-assistance rows separate from actual accessible service. Then check service availability, staffing, vehicle condition, essential-service trips, medical/grocery connections, and handoffs.

## Repair surfaces

- rider/person/trip-purpose and harm file;
- origin, destination, stop, sidewalk, elevator, station, and transfer path file;
- schedule, GTFS, realtime, cancellation, detour, headway, transfer, and customer-notice file;
- vehicle, operator, lift/ramp, securement, crowding, pass-up, and incident file;
- fare, reduced-fare, account, card/mobile/cash, transfer, refund, and replacement file;
- ADA eligibility, paratransit reservation, pickup/dropoff, assistance, no-show, visitor, return-trip, and complaint file;
- Title VI, LEP, service/fare equity, outreach, demographic/ridership denominator, mitigation, and public-participation file;
- PTASP safety, hazard, assault, pedestrian conflict, worker/rider safety, emergency response, and corrective-action file;
- TAM asset, elevator/escalator, vehicle, track/facility condition, shutdown, shuttle, and accessible-alternative file;
- rural/tribal funding, provider capacity, staffing, vehicle, essential-trip, and after-action file.

## Anti-theater tests

- Do not cite a trip, route, GTFS, or NTD row as mobility without actual operation, stop access, fare, boarding, transfer, and completion evidence.
- Do not cite National Transit Map data as navigation, realtime operation, or accessibility proof.
- Do not cite ADA paratransit eligibility or a 3/4-mile corridor as delivered service without reservation, pickup, assistance, ride, return, and complaint records.
- Do not cite Title VI program files as equal mobility without fare/service-change, LEP, mitigation, and affected-rider evidence.
- Do not cite PTASP as safety without local hazard, incident, mitigation, and learning evidence.
- Do not cite TAM target or asset inventory as reliability without disruption and rider consequence records.
- Do not cite grant/funding program availability as rural or tribal mobility without provider capacity and completed essential trips.
- Do not cite complaint forms as remedy without repair, reimbursement, deadline preservation, or corrective action.

## Source posture

This packet uses official FTA, BTS, Federal Register, and GAO sources current enough for rev0757. Use them as national/federal evidence lanes. For live cases, require local transit agency records, GTFS-realtime or AVL/APC where available, ADA/paratransit logs, fare-account records, stop/station/elevator data, Title VI analysis, PTASP hazard/incident files, TAM asset records, complaints, and rider outcome evidence.
