# 933 — Applied election-continuity case packet for EAC EAVS, CISA election security, USPS postmarks, FVAP UOCAVA, ADA accessibility, NYC calendar, and no election by certified total

## One-line thesis

The EAC 2024 EAVS release, CISA election-security resources, USPS 2026–2027 Election Mail and postmark materials, FVAP/UOCAVA clocks, ADA voting accessibility guidance, and current NYC election-office surfaces show one administrative lesson: an election is a continuity chain, not a certified total. Registration, ballot access, mail custody, accessibility, provisional cure, tabulation, audit, canvass, certification, recount, and contest states must remain distinct.

## Why this matters

This packet is not a partisan election-law brief. It is an operations packet for a recurring public-service failure mode: officials and observers often collapse the election into one status surface too early. The status row, ballot request, mail scan, polling-place lookup, unofficial result, audit label, or certification certificate may all be true as far as they go, while still failing to prove that every eligible voter had a reachable, accessible, timely, correct, and countable route.

## Case packet verdict

Route election operations through a vote-continuity waist before accepting status, result, audit, certification, or security claims.

The applied rule is **no election by certified total**.

## Pattern pack

- **EAVS is a national administrative baseline, not a voter-level cure docket.** It helps identify scale, channel mix, registration activity, mail voting, polling operations, and equipment trends, but a local packet still needs deadline, cure, accessibility, and exception records.
- **CISA election security is an infrastructure support surface, not a certification of democratic outcome.** Cybersecurity, physical security, DDoS, vendor, and resilience services matter, but they need to be joined to actual voting operations and fallback routes.
- **USPS Election Mail is a custody channel, not a ballot-right guarantee.** Official Election Mail tools, ballot mail visibility, postmark rules, manual-postmark advice, and mailing recommendations matter only when joined to state ballot deadlines and voter-facing notice.
- **FVAP/UOCAVA clocks prove the need for deadline-specific lanes.** Military and overseas voters can be disenfranchised by small timing, transmission, and return failures that ordinary domestic metrics hide.
- **ADA accessibility is a whole-process obligation.** Registration, websites, polling places, mail ballot applications, ballot marking, drop boxes, communication, assistance, and privacy/independence must all be checked.
- **NYC 2026 local pages show why current public surfaces matter.** Election calendars, early-voting dates, ranked-choice unofficial results, poll-site tools, ballot tracking, meetings, notices, and multilingual access are live operational surfaces; none should be mistaken for the whole election record.

## Why these sources belong together

| Surface | What it shows | Continuity question |
|---|---|---|
| EAC 2024 EAVS | National administrative scale, registration, turnout, channel mix, equipment, and election-office reporting. | Which local voters, ballots, rejected/cured records, accessibility complaints, and deadline tails are hidden by aggregate success? |
| CISA election security | Election infrastructure is a protected critical-infrastructure sector with cyber and physical risk services. | Which security event or vendor dependency could stop voting, tabulation, reporting, or public communication, and what degraded mode exists? |
| USPS Election Mail / postmarks | Ballot mail is a specialized postal channel with visibility tools, recommendations, and postmark-possession caveats. | Which state deadline depends on postmark, receipt, or other evidence, and how are voters told what proof is safe? |
| FVAP / UOCAVA | Military and overseas voters need specific ballot-transmission and return clocks. | Was the ballot sent early enough, returned through an allowed path, and counted or cured with visible status? |
| ADA.gov / EAC accessibility | Disability access applies across all parts of voting, not just the in-person booth. | Did the voter have an accessible path from registration through ballot casting, return, tracking, and cure? |
| NYC election-office surfaces | Live local election calendars, result pages, ballot tracking, multilingual pages, and public meetings. | Which official surface is authoritative for which step, and where can the voter repair a mismatch? |

## Continuity state ladder

1. **Eligible person.** Citizenship, age, residence, registration window, disqualification rules, restoration, and special voter category.
2. **Registration state.** New, active, inactive, updated, canceled, challenged, transferred, corrected, or restored.
3. **Ballot entitlement.** Correct precinct, district, party where relevant, ballot style, language, disability accommodation, and overseas/military status.
4. **Voting channel.** Election Day, early in-person, vote center, mail/absentee, drop box, emergency ballot, UOCAVA, provisional, or assisted route.
5. **Custody and deadline.** Issued, delivered, possessed, returned, postmarked or accepted, received, signature/ID screened, cured, accepted, rejected, or counted.
6. **Access condition.** Website, registration form, polling place, line, machine, ballot marking, drop box, communication, and assistance are accessible enough for equal opportunity.
7. **Operational dependency.** E-pollbook, voting system, ballot-on-demand, tabulator, election-management system, website, reporting feed, vendor, postal route, and physical-security surface.
8. **Tabulation and reconciliation.** Ballot accounting, scanner totals, adjudication, duplication, write-ins, provisional resolution, audit sample, discrepancy resolution, and chain of custody.
9. **Canvass and certification.** Unofficial result, canvass board review, post-election audit, certification, recount request or automatic trigger, contest route, and finality boundary.
10. **Repair and learning.** Voter notice, cure outcome, complaint, accessibility remediation, records release, system fix, training update, and next-cycle rule change.

## Case application

### EAC EAVS baseline

The EAVS surface should be used as the national denominator layer. It tells the packet where to look: registration, list maintenance, voting channels, polling operations, mail voting, equipment, provisional ballots, and turnout. It should not be used to conclude that every local exception, cure, accessibility problem, or rejected-ballot path is repaired.

A strong local packet would pair EAVS-style categories with jurisdiction-specific records: active/inactive registration, confirmation notices, registration-source errors, mail-ballot issue and receipt logs, signature cure, provisional reasons and outcomes, equipment failures, polling-place accessibility, wait times, audit results, recounts, and contests.

### CISA and election-security surfaces

Election security belongs in the continuity packet because an outage, DDoS event, vendor failure, ransomware incident, intimidation event, or reporting-feed disruption can affect whether people can vote and whether the public can understand what happened. But security claims should be downgraded if they cannot name the affected election function and fallback route.

A strong packet would include asset inventory, vendor access, contingency procedures, paper pollbook or emergency-ballot fallback, public-communication authority, reporting-feed caveats, and post-incident reconciliation.

### USPS ballot mail and postmark surfaces

USPS Election Mail materials are operationally important because election mail sits inside a legal deadline system that varies by state. Postmark and postal-possession rules should be treated as evidence-boundary material: they clarify what a postmark proves, what it does not prove, and when manual postmark or other proof may be needed.

A strong packet would include state deadline law, voter instructions, outbound issue logs, return envelope design, postal visibility, retail/manual postmark education, receipt logs, cure notice, and public rejected-ballot denominators.

### FVAP / UOCAVA voters

Military and overseas voting is a built-in stress test for the election-continuity doctrine. If transmission and return clocks are not separately visible, aggregate domestic voting metrics can hide a distinct failure mode for voters abroad or in uniform.

A strong packet would include ballot-transmission date, allowed return methods, waiver or emergency procedures, Federal Write-In Absentee Ballot route, receipt status, cure or counting outcome, and post-election reporting.

### ADA accessibility

Accessibility belongs at every step. A jurisdiction should not be allowed to claim accessible voting merely because it owns accessible equipment. Registration, websites, mail ballot applications, poll-site selection, parking, path of travel, check-in, ballot marking, vote privacy, drop boxes, communication, and assistance all need evidence.

A strong packet would include site surveys, temporary-remedy logs, accessible-format materials, poll-worker training, incident reports, disability-complaint intake, corrective-action deadlines, and public accessibility indicators.

### NYC local operational surfaces

NYC is included here not as a national model, but as a current surface reminder. On June 13, 2026, early voting for the June primary begins, and the official board surfaces expose voter info, ballot tracking, registration, accessibility, meeting notices, unofficial ranked-choice and Election Night results, and a petition calendar for the November 3, 2026 general election. A user-facing election packet must distinguish those live surfaces from certification and contest finality.

A strong packet would map which page answers which voter question, which results are unofficial, what cure or correction route exists, and which meetings, canvass, certification, and recount records later control the result.

## Required test packet

An election-continuity file should contain:

- eligibility and registration-state record;
- ballot style, channel, language, accessibility, and UOCAVA map;
- mail, drop-box, in-person, provisional, and emergency-route clocks;
- polling-place, e-pollbook, equipment, paper-backup, and staffing fallback proof;
- accessibility evidence across registration, websites, mail, drop boxes, polling place, and ballot marking;
- provisional, signature, ID, address, and challenge cure ledger;
- tabulation, adjudication, audit, recount, canvass, and certification record;
- public-result source-of-truth map with unofficial/final labels;
- cyber, vendor, website, reporting, physical-security, and continuity incident log;
- voter complaint, cure, correction, and next-cycle learning loop.

## Anti-theater tests

Apply these as hard falsifiers before accepting an election-status claim.

## Anti-theater rules

- No election by certified total.
- No suffrage by registration row.
- No ballot by request receipt.
- No mail vote by postmark mythology.
- No accessibility by machine.
- No cure by provisional envelope.
- No result by unofficial feed.
- No audit by slogan.
- No recount by press release.
- No voter confidence by dashboard.

## Falsifiers

The packet should be downgraded if it cannot show who could be excluded, which deadline mattered, which channel carried the ballot, how exceptions were cured, how accessibility was verified, how tabulation discrepancies were handled, which result surface was unofficial, what audit or recount occurred, and what voter-level repair remained after certification.

## Dispatch rule

Use this packet whenever registration, ballot access, mail voting, UOCAVA, polling-place operations, accessibility, e-pollbooks, voting systems, provisional ballots, signature/ID cure, tabulation, audit, canvass, certification, recount, contest, or election-security services are being used as evidence that voters were able to cast countable ballots and that the result is administratively trustworthy.
