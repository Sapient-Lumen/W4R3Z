# 719 — Case packets for lane-typed government fields: intake, triage, state model, lineage, notice duty, and no government by unofficial casework

## One-line thesis

Once the archive has a service packet for a lane-typed government field, it should also publish one narrow **case packet** saying what unit of work counts as a case, how a matter becomes one, what intake and triage rules apply before and after acknowledgement, which visible states and clocks bind, what lineage and notice duties survive pause, transfer, merge, withdrawal, supersession, or reopen, and where the authoritative status truth lives — so the field does not decay into government by unofficial casework, invisible queues, or closure by folklore.

## Why this matters

The recent packet spine can now say a great deal about a whole governing field without forcing later notes to re-derive the entire stack.

- `699` gives the scope spine.
- `700` gives the minimum design packet.
- `701` gives rivalry tests.
- `702` gives lane-splitting discipline.
- `703` gives interface packets.
- `704` gives the control plane.
- `705` gives mode packets.
- `706` gives repair packets.
- `707` gives sunset packets.
- `708` gives the evidence packet.
- `709` gives the challenge packet.
- `710` gives the participation packet.
- `711` gives the oversight packet.
- `712` gives the amendment packet.
- `713` gives the delegation packet.
- `714` gives the fiscal packet.
- `715` gives the capability packet.
- `716` gives the information packet.
- `717` gives the asset packet.
- `718` gives the service packet.

That sequence now explains who owns a field, how the seams work, how the field changes mode, how it repairs, sunsets, amends, funds, staffs, records, equips, and publicly promises a service.

But one practical constitutional object is still missing:

**what is the rights-bearing unit of work that actually moves through the field once a person, organisation, or frontline actor enters the service?**

The archive already has a strong precursor doctrine here.

- `446` says consequential status cues must remain durably visible rather than vanishing with the transient message.
- `470` says intake failure should distinguish route failure from artifact failure and preserve manual fallback lanes.
- `472` says withdrawal, cancellation, supersession, duplicate merge, expiry, and reopen need typed lineage rather than disappearance theater.
- `473` says timeout, disconnect, and abandonment states should not impersonate substantive decisions.
- `478` says consequential action should leave one structured decision trace naming basis, route, bindings, and reason codes.
- `482` says queue dwell, service clocks, and anti-starvation escalation must stay visible.
- `656` says pending matters need cutover rules when the operating home changes.
- `716` says the information packet must name the canonical case and record home.
- `718` says the service packet must publish the whole-service promise, front doors, route classes, clocks, and continuity rule.

This note does not replace those. It compresses them into one reusable field-level packet for the **case as governing object**.

Without a case packet, fields drift in predictable ways.

First, they drift into **unofficial casework**: a matter is “being handled” somewhere in email, chat, office memory, contractor tooling, councillor escalation, or a spreadsheet, but nobody can say when the matter formally entered the field, whether it has a live case head, or which clock and notice duty bind.

Second, they drift into **invisible queue government**: users can enter a service, receive a generic confirmation, and then disappear into a backlog without knowing whether the matter is incomplete, screened out, waiting triage, under substantive review, paused for missing information, transferred to another lane, or simply lost.

Third, they drift into **closure by folklore**: a matter is treated as ended because a worker believes it was abandoned, merged, or overtaken, yet the field cannot show a typed closure state, the clock effect, or the notice given.

Fourth, they drift into **operator-side case capture**: contractors, host bodies, or local offices become the de facto owners of case truth because the formal public lane never defined what the case is, when it exists, or what must survive transfer, outage, or exit.

A service packet tells the public what the field promises. A case packet tells the field what unit of work must remain legible while that promise is performed.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Separate the case packet from the service, information, and challenge packets

`718` already says what whole-service outcome is promised and which front doors or route classes the public may use. `716` already says where the canonical case and record homes live. `709` already says how people complain, petition, or seek a second look.

The case packet answers a narrower question:

**once a matter enters the field, what is the named unit of work, when does it formally exist, what states can it occupy, what clocks bind it, and what evidence and notice survive every meaningful transition?**

A field can have a strong service packet and still fail at casework because nobody can tell whether a submitted matter is merely received, actually acknowledged, or substantively live. It can have a strong information packet and still fail because many copies exist but no visible state model binds them. It can have a strong challenge packet and still fail because ordinary service only becomes legible once a person complains.

If a later note still says only that “applications are processed” or “requests are tracked in the system,” it has not yet described a governing field. It has described a hope plus software.

### 2. Name the case object and the case-opening act

A serious case packet should begin by naming the field’s **case object**.

Examples differ by field:

- an application,
- permit request,
- benefit claim,
- housing assistance matter,
- safeguarding concern,
- inspection case,
- complaint,
- enforcement file,
- procurement challenge,
- incident case,
- or other rights-bearing matter.

The point is not the label. It is whether the field can say what unit of work becomes governable once the public or another actor has crossed the service boundary.

The packet should then distinguish at least three moments:

- **submission or receipt**;
- **acknowledgement or acceptance into the field’s case spine**;
- **substantive activation**, if activation depends on completeness, screening, urgency, or jurisdiction.

Those moments often collapse in easy cases, but they should not be assumed identical. If the field cannot say when a matter was merely received, when it became a live case, and when the main service clock actually started, invisible queue government has already begun.

### 3. Publish one small intake-and-triage grammar before the full state model

A normal case packet should publish a compact grammar for the period between entry and full substantive handling.

At minimum it should distinguish:

- route validity or wrong-door failure;
- receipt of a matter by an official route;
- completeness or administrative screening;
- urgency or safeguarding triage where applicable;
- jurisdiction or lane allocation;
- request for more information;
- acceptance into ordinary processing;
- and named rejection or return states where the matter never becomes a live case.

This matters because many fields give people one bland “we received your request” signal while hiding several constitutionally different realities behind it. A matter that is merely delivered is not yet the same thing as one screened complete, accepted into the queue, or escalated as urgent.

### 4. Use a small durable state model and keep closure types separate

A good case packet should give one visible state model that later notes can reuse.

The exact labels will vary, but a serious field usually needs some form of:

- received;
- acknowledged;
- awaiting-completeness or information;
- queued for work;
- in triage or in substantive review;
- paused on a named basis;
- transferred or handed off;
- decided or completed;
- withdrawn;
- cancelled or screened out by the institution;
- merged or superseded;
- expired or abandoned after notice;
- reopened.

The key discipline is not vocabulary purity. It is that **non-active states remain typed**. A generic closed or inactive bucket is too weak for consequential government. It lets timeout, silence, withdrawal, refusal, merge, and supersession impersonate each other.

### 5. Bind clocks to state transitions, not slogans about timeliness

A normal case packet should say, for each material route class or case class:

- what event starts the clock;
- which intermediate clocks matter, such as acknowledgement, triage, request-for-information response, substantive decision, or implementation;
- what pauses a clock lawfully;
- what resumes it;
- what must be notified when a pause occurs;
- and what anti-starvation escalation applies when a matter dwells too long in one state.

A service packet can say the field owes timely service. The case packet has to say what that means for a particular live matter.

Without this, “within a reasonable time” becomes managerial mood rather than a governed operating rule.

### 6. Keep status truth, notice duty, and decision trace visibly docked together

The packet should dock directly to `716`, `718`, `709`, and `478`.

That means saying, at minimum:

- where the authoritative case state lives;
- which user-facing surfaces may display status;
- what notice must be given when the state changes materially;
- what reference number or case identifier binds those surfaces together;
- and what decision trace survives a consequential step.

This matters because many fields are full of partial status surfaces: portal, email, text message, caseworker note, helpdesk answer, contractor tool, paper letter, council office printout. The case packet does not require one interface. It requires one **authoritative status discipline** across all of them.

### 7. Preserve lineage across merge, transfer, supersession, reopen, and cutover

The packet should not treat lineage as exceptional cleanup.

For any material predecessor-successor event, it should preserve:

- predecessor head;
- successor head;
- reason for the change;
- actor or lane causing it;
- clock effect;
- and notice duty to the affected person or organisation when applicable.

This is where `472` and `656` matter most. If the field changes route, lane, operator, office, or platform without preserving case lineage, then transfer turns into evidence laundering and closure by migration.

### 8. Separate ordinary service rework from challenge and appeal

A case packet should say when a matter remains in ordinary service even if it is being corrected, supplemented, or re-routed, and when it has crossed into challenge, reconsideration, appeal, complaint, ombuds, or judicial review.

Otherwise the field ends up using complaint or appeal routes to do work that should still have been ordinary case handling, or disguising challenge as “further processing” to avoid rights-triggering language.

The packet should therefore distinguish:

- ordinary case progression;
- service-side clarification or cure;
- second-look or reconsideration within the ordinary service lane;
- and formal challenge or appeal.

### 9. Make operator, host, and local-office contributions subordinate to the case constitution

A field may use many worker groups, sites, and systems. None of that should be allowed to decide whether a case exists.

The packet should therefore say:

- which acts may be performed by local offices, shared-service centres, host bodies, or contractors;
- which acts reserve case-opening, case-closing, or decisive status changes to the public lane;
- what happens if an operator-side tool is down or exits;
- and how manual fallback keeps the live case and clock intact.

Otherwise the practical owner of a case becomes whichever office has the inbox or whichever vendor controls the status panel.

### 10. Keep the packet small enough that later field notes can cite it rather than rebuilding case doctrine from scratch

A normal case packet should fit in one compact block. If a later note needs pages of fresh prose to explain when a matter exists, which state it occupies, when a queue clock started, where the official status lives, what happened after a merge or pause, and whether the matter quietly turned into a complaint or appeal, then the field still lacks a reusable case constitution.

## Compact case packet

A normal case packet should usually fit in one table-like block:

| Field | Case object and case-opening act | Intake and state model | Clock, notice, and lineage rule | Review trigger and consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Housing** | case object is a housing-assistance matter; case opens on official receipt and becomes acknowledged once minimum identity, contact, and route-validity checks pass; urgent homelessness or safeguarding route can create immediate active status before full completeness | route classes distinguish routine application, urgent route, repairs or habitability, and update route; visible states distinguish received, acknowledged, awaiting information, urgent triage, in assessment, awaiting placement or repair action, decided, withdrawn, merged, and reopened | acknowledgement, urgent-triage, and decision clocks are visible; every material status change issues one durable case update with reference number and next step; transfer between borough, contractor, or provider preserves predecessor-successor links and clock effect | repeated handling by councillor email, charity escalation, or contractor spreadsheet opens repair, front-door redesign, or delegation review |
| **Permits** | case object is a permit or licence matter; case opens on formal submission and becomes active when completeness screening passes or a legally valid low-risk notification route is accepted | states distinguish received, incomplete, complete, queued, under technical review, paused for further information, approved, approved with conditions, refused, withdrawn, expired, superseded, and appealed | the packet names when the statutory or policy service standard commences, what pauses it, when the party must be notified in writing, and what record survives amendment, transfer, or supersession | repeated uncertainty about whether an application is merely received or actually complete opens procedural repair, status-surface redesign, or oversight escalation |
| **Care** | case object is a care or support matter; self-referral, family or advocate contact, hospital referral, or safeguarding alert may all open one case spine with typed urgency and route labels | states distinguish received, safeguarding triage, accepted for assessment, awaiting evidence, package design, provider matching, active package, paused, review due, and reopened | triage, assessment, and package-activation clocks are visible; hospital or provider handoffs cannot reset the case identity; notice duty survives digital outage through manual fallback and later reconciliation | repeated discharge or package change by phone folklore rather than case-state transition opens repair, capability review, or redesign of the service-home and information-home relation |

The exact field content will vary, but the packet form should remain recognizable.

## Failure modes

- **unofficial casework**: a matter is live only in inboxes, chats, office memory, or contractor tools rather than in one visible case constitution.
- **invisible queue government**: users cannot tell whether a matter is merely received, accepted, complete, triaged, paused, or lost.
- **closure by folklore**: a matter is treated as ended because someone assumes withdrawal, abandonment, merge, or supersession without typed proof.
- **status split-brain**: different portals, workers, or operators report different live states for the same matter.
- **operator-side capture**: whoever controls the workflow tool becomes the de facto owner of case truth.
- **clock theater**: service standards are published, but nobody can say when the clock started, paused, resumed, or ended.
- **challenge-as-casework**: complaints, appeals, or ombuds routes become the only way to obtain ordinary status clarity.
- **lineage loss**: transfer, merge, cutover, or reopen destroys the predecessor-successor chain.

## Anti-escape-hatch tests

1. Can the field name the case object and the exact act that creates receipt, acknowledgement, and substantive activation?
2. Is there one small intake-and-triage grammar that distinguishes wrong-door failure, incompleteness, urgency, jurisdiction, and acceptance into the live case spine?
3. Does the field use a small visible state model with typed closure states instead of one vague closed or inactive bucket?
4. Are clocks tied to specific transitions, pauses, resumptions, and anti-starvation escalation rather than to generic claims of timeliness?
5. Can every material status change be located in one authoritative case-status surface tied to a reference number and durable notice?
6. Do merge, transfer, supersession, withdrawal, expiry, and reopen preserve lineage, clock effect, and reason-giving rather than silently replacing the head?
7. Is ordinary case progression kept visibly separate from complaint, challenge, appeal, or ombuds routing?
8. Can the field survive office moves, platform changes, vendor exit, or outage without losing the live case or rewriting its clocks?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a government field can tell you **how to submit a request** and **which service standard it advertises**, but cannot tell you **when the matter formally became a case, what state it is in now, which clock is actually running, what notice survived the last transition, and what predecessor it replaced or succeeded**, then it still has **service surfaces without a case constitution**.
