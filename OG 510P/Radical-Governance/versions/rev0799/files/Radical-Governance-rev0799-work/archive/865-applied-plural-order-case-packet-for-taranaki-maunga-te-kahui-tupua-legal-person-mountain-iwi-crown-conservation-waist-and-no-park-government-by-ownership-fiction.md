# 865 — Applied plural-order case packet for Taranaki Maunga / Te Kāhui Tupua: legal-person mountain, iwi-Crown conservation waist, and no park government by ownership fiction

## One-line thesis

The Taranaki Maunga / Te Kāhui Tupua settlement is best treated as a plural-order legal-person natural-entity and conservation-governance waist: it recognises an ancestral mountain as a legal person and creates iwi-Crown voice, plan, approval, and monitoring routes, but it should not be described either as ordinary national-park consultation or as a fully separate territorial government.

## Why this matters

This is the archive's first plural-order applied packet. It stress-tests the cross-boundary chain against a case where the relevant boundary is not simply municipal, regional, national, or ecological. The live field is also historical, ancestral, spiritual, settlement-based, statutory, public-access, and operational.

The case is useful because it separates legal status from operational control. The mountain and surrounding park framework can be vested in a legal personality while the Department of Conservation retains operational management responsibilities. Iwi institutions can appoint members, approve plans, participate in concessions, and speak through statutory bodies without the arrangement becoming a general local government or an ordinary advisory committee.

That makes it a hard case for the archive's anti-theater discipline: if the cube calls the arrangement merely symbolic, it erases Indigenous authority; if it calls it a new sovereign park government, it hides retained state duties and ordinary public-law boundaries.

### 5. Personhood without ownership theater

The key analytic move is to separate these propositions:

| Proposition | Status in this packet |
| --- | --- |
| the Crown no longer owns the Crown-owned parts of the national park in the ordinary way | live but not sufficient |
| the legal person owns itself or receives vested interests | live but must be tied to assets and duties |
| the park remains subject to conservation law and public access principles | live and rights-preserving |
| the Department of Conservation retains operational management responsibilities | live and anti-theater-critical |
| iwi institutions receive appointment, approval, monitoring, cultural, and concessions roles | live and authority-bearing |
| the arrangement creates a general-purpose government | rejected |

A legal-person mountain can be a real public-law transformation without becoming a complete government.

### 7. Form holding

The honest form is **plural-order legal-person natural-entity waist under retained conservation public law**.

That form says the maunga / park framework is not just a Crown-owned park plus stakeholder consultation. It also says the arrangement is not a new general territorial government. The public-law record must preserve the legal person, the iwi voice and approval functions, the retained Department of Conservation operating role, the plan waist, the public-access / private-rights boundary, the funding lane, and the dispute route.

## Failure modes

- **ownership-fiction government**: saying the park owns itself while hiding who operates, budgets, plans, permits, and records.
- **consultation shrinkage**: treating iwi appointment, approval, and relationship functions as ordinary stakeholder consultation.
- **departmental continuity theater**: saying nothing changed because the Department of Conservation still manages operations.
- **public-access panic**: treating recognition as a loss of public access without reading the rights-preservation boundary.
- **personhood romanticism**: assuming the legal person solves capacity, pests, climate, tourism, funding, or enforcement problems by status alone.
- **settlement closure overreach**: using historical settlement finality to silence future operational, environmental, or rights disputes that are not actually settled.
- **mandate invisibility**: failing to show who appoints, approves, speaks, and can be challenged within the iwi-side institutions.
- **asset fog**: land, minerals, buildings, concessions, and funding move or remain split without a public ledger.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Can a reader identify the legal person, its face and voice, the iwi entity, the state operational manager, and the plan waist?
2. Does the packet distinguish ownership / legal personality from day-to-day operations?
3. Are public access, private rights, aboriginal title, customary rights, concessions, and conservation duties mapped together?
4. Are iwi approval and appointment powers treated as authority rather than generic consultation?
5. Is the Department of Conservation's retained role visible enough for accountability?
6. Is the funding and support package visible enough to test unfunded delegation?
7. Are plan approval, land change, concessions, and cultural-material decisions separated by decision type?
8. Is there a route for public, iwi / hapū, concession, private-right, and environmental disputes?
9. Does the arrangement remain a targeted natural-entity / conservation waist rather than being inflated into a general government?
