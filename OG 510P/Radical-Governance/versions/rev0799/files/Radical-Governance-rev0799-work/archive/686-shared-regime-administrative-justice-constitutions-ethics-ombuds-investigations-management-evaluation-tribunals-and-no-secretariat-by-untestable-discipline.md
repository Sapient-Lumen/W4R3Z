# 686 — Shared-regime administrative-justice constitutions: ethics, ombuds, investigations, management evaluation, tribunals, and no secretariat by untestable discipline

## One-line thesis

Shared regimes should publish a **typed administrative-justice constitution** that separates confidential advice, ombuds or informal resolution, protected disclosure, investigations, interim protection, management review, disciplinary decision, independent tribunal review, and public jurisprudence memory — so the secretariat is not governed by rumor, retaliation fear, or untestable disciplinary power.

## Why this matters

The archive now has notes on integrity (`678`) and workforce discipline (`684`). Those notes explain **who may be hired, what conduct duties exist, and how conflicts and secondments should be governed**. They do not yet explain **how the institution handles disputes, allegations, retaliation claims, disciplinary actions, and review of administrative decisions once something has gone wrong**.

That gap matters because international and shared regimes often sit partly outside national labor courts, civil-service courts, or ordinary administrative law routes. The institution therefore cannot borrow legitimacy from a surrounding national justice system and hope the gap goes unnoticed. It needs its own visible internal justice map.

Without a typed administrative-justice constitution, six familiar pathologies appear.

The first is **channel confusion**: staff do not know whether to go to an ombuds, ethics office, manager, investigator, grievance panel, or tribunal, and each office behaves as if intake itself were power.

The second is **retaliation fog**: protected disclosure exists in theory, but reporters cannot see how protection is requested, who decides it, or what interim measures are available.

The third is **investigation creep**: fact-finding, advice, discipline recommendation, and final sanction blur into one office or one managerial clique.

The fourth is **review exhaustion theater**: internal review stages multiply without adding independence, speed, or reasoned correction.

The fifth is **discipline without reasons**: adverse action lands through opaque letters, incomplete evidence access, or vague standards that cannot be tested cleanly.

The sixth is **doctrinal amnesia**: tribunal judgments exist, but the institution lacks a durable memory of holdings, common error patterns, timing rules, and remedial consequences.

This note therefore adds one compact doctrine for **shared-regime administrative-justice discipline**.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Publish one visible justice map

A serious regime should show in one place:

- what counts as an administrative decision,
- which channels are informal,
- who gives confidential ethics advice,
- where misconduct is reported,
- who investigates,
- what review must be exhausted before tribunal filing,
- what deadlines apply,
- and what tribunal or external body has final jurisdiction.

If staff and contractors need insider coaching just to find the first lawful door, the regime is already administering justice by maze.

### 2. Separate confidential advice from formal reporting and adjudication

Ethics advice, ombuds services, and other informal resolution channels should be distinct from investigative and adjudicative lanes.

The archive should prefer a rule that confidential advice offices may help people understand options and risks, but do not silently become investigator, prosecutor, and judge. This is how regimes avoid treating help-seeking as self-exposure.

### 3. Type protected disclosure and retaliation protection explicitly

A serious regime should say:

- what kinds of reporting are protected,
- who may seek protection,
- how a retaliation claim is filed,
- what confidentiality rule applies,
- who makes the initial protection determination,
- and what interim measures may be ordered.

Protection against retaliation is not a moral statement. It is a constitutional lane.

### 4. Keep intake, assessment, investigation, and disciplinary decision distinct

Misconduct handling should travel through stages.

The regime should separate:

- intake and triage,
- preliminary assessment,
- administrative fact-finding,
- referral or recommendation,
- and the final disciplinary or administrative decision.

This does not require many institutions. It does require visible role separation so the fact-finder is not also the unchecked decider.

### 5. Provide interim-protection tools

Some cases cannot wait for the final merits outcome.

The regime should therefore type whether it can order temporary reassignment, contact limits, evidence preservation, administrative suspension where lawfully justified, stay of execution, or anti-retaliation measures pending review. Interim protection should be exceptional, reasoned, and time-bounded — but real.

### 6. Use management review or evaluation as a correction lane, not an anti-access moat

For ordinary administrative decisions, a first internal review stage can be useful if it is quick, reasoned, and capable of real correction.

The archive should prefer a rule that management evaluation or equivalent review has:

- a short filing clock,
- a short answer clock,
- authority to correct or settle,
- and clear exhaustion consequences.

It should not become a performative waiting room that merely delays access to independent review.

### 7. Guarantee independent tribunal review

A serious regime should have either its own independent administrative tribunal with appellate review, or lawful access to an external international administrative tribunal whose jurisdiction the regime has recognized.

The tribunal lane should be visibly separate from the managerial chain and able to rescind decisions, order performance, award compensation where appropriate, and publish reasoned judgments.

### 8. Give parties notice, access to material basis, and reasoned outcomes

Justice architecture is not real if the subject of a decision cannot see the case against them in a usable way.

The regime should therefore prefer typed rules for:

- notice of the impugned act or allegation,
- time to respond,
- access to the material basis subject to lawful confidentiality limits,
- the burden or standard relevant to the decision,
- and reasoned written outcomes.

### 9. Distinguish remedies by decision type

Not every wrong should produce the same remedy.

The archive should prefer separate remedy logic for:

- correction or rescission of an unlawful administrative decision,
- performance of an obligation,
- compensation where restoration is impossible or inadvisable,
- disciplinary measures,
- retaliation protection,
- and systemic recommendations flowing from investigations or jurisprudence.

This keeps justice from collapsing into either symbolic declarations or damages-only fatalism.

### 10. Publish judgments, digests, and timing rules as institutional memory

A shared regime should not make staff infer justice from rumor about what happened in past cases.

The institution should therefore publish, at minimum, the tribunal statute or recognized jurisdiction, rules of procedure, public judgments or accessible archives, key filing clocks, and plain-language process maps. Where possible it should maintain digests or recurrent-issue guides that improve consistency without pretending to replace the judgments themselves.

## Working matrix

| Justice object | Honest rule | Why | What to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| advice vs complaint | keep ethics advice and ombuds functions distinct from formal reporting and adjudication | preserves safe intake and role clarity | channel confusion |
| reporting and retaliation | define protected disclosure, confidentiality, protection determinations, and interim measures | makes speaking up real | retaliation fog |
| investigation and discipline | separate fact-finding from final disciplinary choice | limits concentrated power | investigator-as-judge |
| internal review | use quick management evaluation or equivalent for correction, not obstruction | preserves speed without closing access | exhaustion theater |
| tribunal review | guarantee an independent tribunal lane with final, reasoned decisions | supplies lawful externality and legitimacy | secretariat by untestable discipline |
| jurisprudence memory | publish judgments, rules, and process maps | prevents doctrine by folklore | amnesia and inconsistency |

## Guardrails

- Do not force people to choose among overlapping offices whose powers are undefined.
- Do not let confidential ethics or ombuds channels mutate into covert investigative or disciplinary lanes.
- Do not advertise anti-retaliation protection without naming filing, confidentiality, and interim-relief routes.
- Do not collapse intake, fact-finding, recommendation, and sanction into one office without visible checks.
- Do not turn management review into a purely dilatory exhaustion ritual.
- Do not impose adverse decisions without notice, usable access to the material basis, and written reasons.
- Do not keep tribunal judgments technically public but practically inaccessible.

## Failure modes

- **administration by maze**: no one can tell which office does what or where to start.
- **retaliation fog**: protection exists on paper but not as an operable route.
- **investigation creep**: fact-finding and final punishment collapse into one unchecked lane.
- **review exhaustion theater**: internal steps multiply while independence does not.
- **discipline without reasons**: affected staff cannot test the basis of adverse action.
- **jurisprudential amnesia**: the institution relearns the same justice failures case by case.

## Practical tests

A regime passes this note when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Does it publish one visible map of ethics, ombuds, reporting, investigation, review, and tribunal lanes?
2. Are confidential advice channels separated from formal complaint and adjudication channels?
3. Is protected disclosure defined with clear retaliation-protection intake and confidentiality rules?
4. Are intake, assessment, investigation, recommendation, and final disciplinary decision visibly separated?
5. Are interim-protection tools available and bounded for urgent cases?
6. Does internal management review have short clocks and real corrective authority?
7. Does the regime guarantee access to an independent administrative tribunal or recognized external tribunal?
8. Are notice, response opportunity, access to material basis, and written reasons part of the ordinary justice path?
9. Are remedies typed by decision class rather than reduced to one blunt answer?
10. Can staff and outsiders easily find the tribunal statute or jurisdiction, rules, judgments, and process guidance?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a shared regime can **discipline staff, reject grievances, deny protection, or impose administrative decisions** but cannot say **which office gives advice, who investigates, how retaliation protection works, what review must be exhausted, what tribunal hears complaints, and how judgments remain publicly knowable**, it still has **administrative power without an honest justice constitution**.
