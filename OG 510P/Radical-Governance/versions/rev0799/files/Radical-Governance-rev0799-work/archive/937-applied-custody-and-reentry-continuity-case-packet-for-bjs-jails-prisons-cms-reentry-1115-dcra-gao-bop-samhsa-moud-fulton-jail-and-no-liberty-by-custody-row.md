# 937 — Applied custody and reentry continuity case packet for BJS jails/prisons, CMS Reentry 1115, DCRA, GAO BOP, SAMHSA MOUD, Fulton Jail, and no liberty by custody row

## One-line thesis

The official U.S. custody and reentry surfaces should be read as a continuity packet, not as separable proof that a person was lawfully held, safely confined, medically treated, released on time, or successfully reentered. BJS counts, CMS reentry demonstrations, DCRA records, GAO BOP findings, SAMHSA MOUD guidance, BOP First Step Act data, and DOJ jail/prison findings each prove one slice; none proves liberty, safety, health, or reentry alone.

## Why this matters

Custody is one of the archive's highest-consequence public-service states because the state controls the person's body, schedule, communication, medical access, records, property, family contact, and exit. The sources opened for this packet show scale, volatility, and operational fragmentation: local jails hold hundreds of thousands of people at any time; prisons hold more than a million; community supervision is larger than incarceration; death-in-custody reporting has its own implementation and denominator problems; reentry health coverage and ID documents are still implementation work; and DOJ findings show that formal custody can coexist with unconstitutional conditions.

The applied rule is **no liberty by custody row**.

## Case packet verdict

Route detention, corrections, release, and reentry through a continuity waist whenever a custody row, jail/prison count, death record, reentry referral, Medicaid demonstration, First Step Act credit, RRC placement, ID document record, MOUD policy, or DOJ findings report is used to prove liberty, safety, health care, accountability, or community return.

## Pattern pack

- **BJS jail and prison counts are denominator anchors, not person-level custody proof.** They show scale and trend, but not lawful hold, medical care, or release execution for any individual.
- **BJS community-supervision counts prevent a false ending.** Release from a facility may become probation, parole, home confinement, RRC, electronic monitoring, fee, treatment, or violation risk.
- **DCRA and MCI are mortality-accountability entry points.** They require reporting discipline and denominator clarity, but incident accountability still needs investigation, family notice, and corrective-action records.
- **CMS Reentry 1115 is a health-transition opportunity, not automatic continuity.** Pre-release Medicaid services and provider connections must be joined to actual eligibility, enrollment, medication, appointments, and community providers.
- **GAO BOP reentry findings are implementation tests.** Health coverage, ID documents, and residential reentry capacity are operational gates, not afterthoughts.
- **SAMHSA MOUD guidance turns withdrawal and opioid treatment into custody-safety tests.** MOUD access, continuation, induction, diversion controls, and release handoff must be documented.
- **DOJ Fulton County Jail and Georgia prison findings are stress tests.** Formal custody and facility operation can coexist with violence, unsafe conditions, inadequate medical/mental health care, and unlawful segregation.

## Applied continuity ladder

1. **Custody authority.** Identify arrest, charge, sentence, detainer, warrant, probation/parole violation, transfer order, and release authority.
2. **Booking and intake risk.** Verify suicide, withdrawal, medications, disability, pregnancy, age, language, victimization, and emergency medical triage.
3. **Conditions and protection.** Join housing, violence, staffing, contraband, sexual abuse, use of force, segregation, sanitation, food, ADA, and grievances.
4. **Health and MOUD.** Join records, medications, chronic conditions, mental health, MOUD access, hospital transfer, and release prescriptions.
5. **Death and serious incident.** Join DCRA/MCI reporting to incident narrative, investigation, family notice, litigation or monitor status, and corrective action.
6. **Communication and counsel.** Verify attorney access, court transport/video, mail, phone/video, family notice, interpreter, disability accommodation, and grievance protection.
7. **Release calculation.** Verify sentence credits, FSA credits, parole/release eligibility, detainers, transfer, transportation, gate time, property, and discharge conditions.
8. **Reentry bridge.** Verify Medicaid/coverage, ID documents, prescriptions, appointments, address, phone, housing, treatment, employment/education, food/cash benefits, and family reunification.
9. **Supervision tail.** Verify probation/parole/home confinement/RRC/electronic monitoring conditions, fees, treatment rules, violation process, and modification route.
10. **Public oversight.** Mark which sources are aggregate, live policy, findings, audits, or case files; never collapse them into one proof of success.

## Applied examples

### BJS jail, prison, and correctional-population data

Use BJS as the denominator spine. A jail table, prison table, or correctional-population table can tell the archive the scale of custody and supervision. It cannot decide whether the person in front of the state was lawfully held, safe, medically treated, released at the right time, or connected to reentry supports.

### DCRA and mortality surfaces

Death-in-custody data require a stronger accountability chain than annual counts. A serious packet needs the reported death record, the incident circumstances, independent investigation, family notice, facility corrective actions, local oversight, and litigation/monitor status where present.

### Medicaid reentry demonstrations

CMS Reentry 1115 authority belongs in the packet because health coverage can begin before release for eligible people under approved demonstrations. The continuity question is not whether a state has authority, but whether the person received eligibility, medication, provider connection, record transfer, and a post-release appointment in time.

### GAO BOP health care, IDs, and RRCs

GAO's BOP work turns reentry from aspiration into operational gates. Health coverage, IDs, and RRC/home-confinement capacity must be treated as release prerequisites where they are necessary for medicine, housing, work, benefits, supervision, and family stability.

### SAMHSA MOUD guidance and BOP First Step Act data

OUD treatment is a custody-safety and reentry-continuity issue. The packet must distinguish treatment availability, patient eligibility, continuation or induction, diversion-control policies, release medication, and community provider connection. First Step Act data help with federal denominator and program questions, but not with individual release execution.

### DOJ Fulton County Jail and Georgia prison findings

These findings are included as stress tests: official custody can coexist with violence, unsanitary conditions, inadequate medical and mental health care, discriminatory or unconstitutional segregation, special-education failures, and deaths. Therefore custody rows and facility operation cannot be accepted as safety.

## Repair surfaces

- booking, charge, sentence, detainer, warrant, transfer, parole/probation, credit, and release-authority docket;
- intake suicide/withdrawal/medication/disability/pregnancy/mental-health and urgent-care triage;
- housing, staffing, violence, contraband, sexual abuse, use-of-force, segregation, sanitation, food, ADA, and grievance ledgers;
- medical record, medications, MOUD, hospital transfer, chronic care, disability, pregnancy, and release-prescription handoff;
- DCRA/MCI death record, incident investigation, family notice, corrective action, litigation, and monitor tail;
- attorney/court/family communication, interpreter/accommodation, grievance, and retaliation-safe route;
- release calculation, FSA/good-time/parole credits, detainer resolution, transport, property, gate time, prescriptions, ID documents, health coverage, appointments, address, phone, housing, benefits, work/school, and supervision conditions.

## Anti-theater tests

- Do not cite a custody row as lawful, safe, or current unless authority, hold, transfer, and release state are visible.
- Do not cite jail or prison counts as safety evidence unless conditions, health, and incident records are joined.
- Do not cite death-in-custody aggregate data as accountability without investigation and corrective action.
- Do not cite Medicaid reentry authority as care continuity without enrollment, medication, provider, and appointment evidence.
- Do not cite ID assistance as reentry unless the documents are in hand and usable for housing, work, benefits, and care.
- Do not cite release date, FSA credit, RRC referral, or home confinement as executed release without the gate-time and reentry packet.

## Source posture

This case packet is U.S.-anchored. Its sources are current enough to establish the operational shape of the continuity problem, but every live custody or reentry judgment still needs local jail/prison/court/probation/parole medical, release, grievance, inspection, monitor, and benefits records.
