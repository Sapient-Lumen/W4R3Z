# 924 — Applied health-coverage and prescription transition case packet for Medicaid / CHIP, Marketplace, and Medicare Part D renewals, SEPs, formulary exceptions, and no medication continuity by plan card

## One-line thesis

The U.S. Medicaid / CHIP unwinding, Marketplace transfer, and Medicare Part D transition-fill / exception / payment-plan stack shows why health-benefit governance must track treatment-level continuity: a person can move across coverage statuses and still lose medication, cost protection, prescriber support, or appeal time.

## Why this matters

Medicaid / CHIP, Marketplace, Medicare, Part D, LIS / Extra Help, managed-care plans, pharmacy benefit managers, state renewal systems, special-enrollment rules, and pharmacy point-of-sale systems are all legitimate pieces of public health administration. The applied failure is not that any one of them is fake. The failure is that a person may need all of them to line up before the next dose, infusion, appointment, home service, or drug refill.

This case packet treats health coverage as a transition chain rather than a single entitlement. The relevant event may be Medicaid renewal, procedural termination, Marketplace special enrollment, plan selection, premium payment, Medicare eligibility, Part D plan switch, formulary update, prior authorization reset, transition fill, exception request, expedited appeal, LIS loss, or payment-plan election. Each is a different state; none alone proves clinical continuity.

The anti-theater rule is **no medication continuity by plan card**. A card, enrollment row, SEP, model notice, or transition-fill letter does not prove the person can keep treatment unless the specific medication / service and its cure path are preserved.

### 3. Case-state decomposition

Before citing this packet, split the case into these states:

1. **coverage state**: Medicaid / CHIP active, pending, terminated, reinstated, Marketplace selected, Marketplace effectuated, Medicare active, Part D active, dual, LIS / Extra Help, or uninsured;
2. **transition state**: renewal, procedural termination, eligibility denial, SEP, document verification, premium payment, plan year change, or formulary update;
3. **drug / service state**: ongoing therapy, new therapy, covered, non-formulary, UM-blocked, excluded, transition-filled, substituted, abandoned, or interrupted;
4. **cost state**: capped, payment-plan eligible, elected, LIS-protected, billed, unpaid, unaffordable, reversed, or reimbursed;
5. **clinical clock**: days of supply remaining, risk of relapse / hospitalization / withdrawal / functional decline, expedited-review need, or bridge window;
6. **remedy state**: notice, assister, navigator, prescriber statement, coverage determination, exception, appeal, fair hearing, reimbursement, or retroactive correction;
7. **metric state**: enrollment, plan effectuation, claim approval, refill gap, therapy discontinuation, new treatment not started, crisis event, or public learning loop.

### 5. Rival readings

**Rival 1: Coverage transition is solved if the person has an enrollment row.** It is not. Enrollment can coexist with non-formulary status, prior authorization reset, high cost, or pharmacy claim rejection.

**Rival 2: A transition fill solves continuity.** It buys time, but it can also hide a looming exception, appeal, or prescriber-evidence deadline.

**Rival 3: Cost caps and payment plans solve affordability by policy design.** They help only when people receive notice, elect or retain the route where required, can pay the monthly bill, and see corrected point-of-sale costs.

**Rival 4: Marketplace SEP availability proves successful transfer.** It proves a doorway, not document verification, plan selection, premium payment, effectuation, formulary fit, or first use.

**Rival 5: Public enrollment metrics are enough.** They are not enough for treatment continuity unless joined to drug, service, cost, appeal, and clinical-risk denominators.

## Applied state ladder

### 1. Medicaid / CHIP renewal and termination state

The packet begins with `895`: identify whether the person was renewed ex parte, renewed by form, terminated for confirmed ineligibility, terminated procedurally, pending, transferred, or reinstated. Then add the treatment overlay:

- active prescriptions and services at the renewal date;
- conditions requiring uninterrupted therapy;
- managed-care plan, PBM, provider, pharmacy, and prior authorization state;
- notice, representative, navigator, and disability / language route;
- late renewal, fair hearing, retroactive coverage, and reimbursement tail.

A procedural termination without treatment overlay is not a safe closure.

### 2. Marketplace special-enrollment and plan-effectuation state

Where Medicaid or CHIP is lost, will be lost, or is denied, the Marketplace path must show more than eligibility for a special-enrollment period:

- account transfer or application state;
- Medicaid denial / loss evidence and document upload state;
- plan picked within the relevant window;
- premium paid and coverage effectuated;
- first day coverage can actually be used;
- formulary and network match for existing drugs and providers;
- assister, navigator, broker, or representative action record;
- appeal route if the SEP or document verification fails.

A special-enrollment opportunity is a doorway, not coverage.

### 3. Medicare and Part D enrollment state

For older adults, disabled people, dual-eligible people, and people moving between Medicaid and Medicare-related help, the packet must keep Medicare, Medicaid, MSP, Extra Help / LIS, MA-PD, PDP, employer group, and state assistance states separate. Relevant receipts include:

- Medicare effective dates and plan selection;
- Part D plan / formulary / pharmacy / PBM state;
- LIS / Extra Help or Medicaid dual-eligible status;
- notices when LIS is lost or cost-sharing changes;
- Part D model-material and plan-notice state;
- transition-fill, emergency-fill, exception, and appeal evidence.

Dual status is a protection only if point-of-sale and plan records carry it correctly.

### 4. Formulary and utilization-management state

A new plan or new year can change non-formulary status, tier, prior authorization, step therapy, quantity limit, specialty pharmacy, or refill rules. The case packet requires a drug-by-drug line:

| Drug / service state | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- |
| covered with no change | formulary and claim-confirmation evidence |
| covered with UM | prior authorization / step / quantity rule and cure path |
| non-formulary | transition-fill and exception route |
| excluded / non-Part-D / not covered by plan | alternate payer / medical-benefit / appeal / assistance route |
| high-risk clinical gap | bridge supply or expedited review before exhaustion |

Do not let a plan-level row hide a drug-level denial.

### 5. Transition fill, exception, and appeal state

Part D transition policy and model materials are bridge tools. The packet should ask:

1. Was the person within the relevant transition window?
2. Was the drug ongoing therapy or new therapy?
3. Was the temporary supply enough for the clinical clock?
4. Did the notice explain temporary status and next steps?
5. Was an exception or coverage determination requested?
6. Did the prescriber provide medical-necessity evidence?
7. Was expedited treatment available where delay threatened health?
8. Did the appeal preserve access, reimbursement, or retroactive correction?

A transition letter is not the end of the case. It is the beginning of the cure clock.

### 6. Affordability and payment state

The 2025/2026 Part D redesign, out-of-pocket cap, Medicare Prescription Payment Plan, Extra Help, LIS notices, and premium/cost-sharing rules can materially reduce burdens. They can also fail operationally if people do not know, do not opt in where required, receive bills they cannot pay, lose LIS, encounter premium arrears, or abandon a drug before relief applies.

The packet requires:

- likely-to-benefit or outreach record;
- payment-plan election / automatic renewal / termination state where relevant;
- monthly bill and nonpayment protections;
- LIS / Extra Help status and notices;
- pharmacy point-of-sale cost before and after correction;
- reimbursement or reversal route for erroneous cost-sharing.

Cost protection must be measured at the pharmacy counter and billing tail, not only in statute.

### 7. Treatment and medication metrics

The applied denominator should count:

- Medicaid / CHIP renewal cohorts and procedural terminations;
- Marketplace transfers, plan selections, premium payments, and effectuated coverage;
- high-risk medication users in transition cohorts;
- transition fills, emergency fills, exception requests, denial reasons, appeals, reversals, and expedited decisions;
- refill gaps, therapy abandonment, treatment episodes ending, new treatment episodes not starting, and hospitalization / crisis proxies where feasible;
- subgroup, language, disability, geography, plan, and pharmacy variation.

The key falsifier is treatment-level data showing that coverage transitions did not interrupt care. Without it, enrollment metrics are incomplete.

## Failure modes

- **No treatment continuity by enrollment row**: eligibility, plan, or card status is treated as proof that the next dose, refill, infusion, visit, or home service is available.
- **No medication continuity by plan card**: plan membership hides non-formulary status, prior authorization reset, quantity limit, specialty-pharmacy routing, or unaffordable point-of-sale cost.
- **No SEP by doorway**: Marketplace special-enrollment eligibility is counted as transfer success before document verification, plan selection, premium payment, coverage effectuation, and first use.
- **No bridge by temporary fill alone**: a transition fill is treated as cure even though exception, appeal, prescriber evidence, or alternate therapy must occur before the supply runs out.
- **No affordability by cap announcement**: cost protection is counted at the policy layer while pharmacy or billing records still produce abandonment, debt, or nonpayment risk.
- **No appeal by form availability**: coverage determination, exception, fair hearing, or appeal routes exist on paper but miss the clinical clock.
- **No access metric without treatment denominator**: public reporting counts enrollment, renewal, or plan rows without refill gaps, therapy discontinuation, treatment starts, or crisis proxies.

## Upgrade triggers

- procedural Medicaid or CHIP termination touches active treatment;
- Marketplace transfer has plan-selection but no premium / first-use / formulary receipt;
- Part D plan change or formulary update affects ongoing therapy;
- prior authorization, step therapy, quantity limit, or specialty-pharmacy routing resets at transition;
- transition fill is used but exception / appeal / prescriber route is not completed before the drug runs out;
- cost-sharing relief exists but pharmacy point-of-sale cost remains unaffordable;
- LIS / Extra Help, dual-eligible, or payment-plan status changes without clear notice and cure;
- public metrics count enrollment but not medication or treatment gaps.

## Downshift or handback triggers

- coverage, plan, formulary, cost, provider, pharmacy, and treatment states are reconciled before transition;
- high-risk treatment has bridge supply or expedited review before interruption;
- plan selection, premium, document verification, and first-use receipts are complete;
- exception / appeal / retroactive correction routes are usable and tracked;
- treatment-gap metrics show no material interruption, or interruptions are publicly repaired.

## Anti-theater core

- no treatment continuity by enrollment row;
- no medication continuity by plan card;
- no bridge by temporary fill alone;
- no affordability by cap announcement;
- no SEP by doorway without effectuation;
- no appeal by form availability;
- no access metric without treatment denominator.
