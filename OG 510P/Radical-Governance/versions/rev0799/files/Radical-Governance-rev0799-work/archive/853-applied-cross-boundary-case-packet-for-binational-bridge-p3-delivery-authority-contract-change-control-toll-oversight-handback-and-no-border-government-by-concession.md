# 853 — Applied cross-boundary case packet for binational bridge P3 delivery authority: contract change control, toll oversight, handback, and no border government by concession

## One-line thesis

A binational bridge delivered through a long-term P3 should be governed as a **contract-backed public crossing authority under treaty/statutory floor**: the concession may design, build, finance, operate, and maintain the crossing, but the public authority must keep contract publication, change control, toll and service oversight, performance deductions, dispute routing, asset handback, audit, and international compliance visible so that the project does not become border government by concession.

## Why this matters

`852` made `822` concrete by testing an asset-heavy regional utility. The next missing field is `823`: the case where a public body still exists, but the live governance danger sits in the delivery contract, project agreement, concession term, payment formula, performance regime, redactions, step-in rights, change orders, and handback conditions.

This packet uses the Windsor-Detroit / Gordie Howe International Bridge pattern as the contract-heavy test object: a binational crossing authority, public-private partnership, private-sector consortium, ports of entry, tolls, public owner / sponsor, international statutory floor, oversight reports, and long-term operations / maintenance obligations. It is a strong stress test because the contract is not just a procurement artifact. It structures public access, tolling, safety, maintenance, interface control, payments, risk allocation, and asset handback across a border.

The verdict is **contract-backed public crossing authority under treaty/statutory floor**. The body is thicker than ordinary procurement support because it manages a strategic cross-border public asset and long-term concession. It is narrower than a general border government because trade, customs, immigration, policing, land, taxation, and diplomacy remain in other public hands.

### 6. Form holding

The honest form is **contract-backed public crossing authority under treaty/statutory floor**. It may deliver a binational crossing through a long-term P3, administer the project agreement, monitor performance, publish procurement and corporate materials, set or collect tolls where authorized, coordinate interfaces, manage public payments, receive reports, enforce contract rights, and prepare handback.

It must not let the concession function as border constitution. Customs, immigration, policing, trade policy, diplomacy, criminal enforcement, ordinary land-use authority, general taxation, and sovereign border functions remain outside the concession. Where project operations touch those fields, the crossing authority must route the issue to the relevant public authority rather than allowing contract text to decide by default.

### 8. Rival interpretations

| Rival rule | Why it is tempting | Why this packet rejects or limits it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P3 as mere procurement | keeps constitutional analysis out of delivery | fails when the contract governs decades of access, maintenance, payments, toll interfaces, and asset condition |
| public ownership is enough | names a Crown / public authority | misses that operative risk may be in redacted schedules, change orders, and performance payment rules |
| private concessionaire governs crossing | aligns delivery incentives with lifecycle performance | turns a contract into public law without democratic, statutory, audit, and complaint routes |
| full binational border authority | recognizes the cross-border stakes | overbuilds when the body delivers a crossing but does not own customs, immigration, policing, trade, or diplomacy |
| national department control only | supplies sovereign accountability | can bury daily project performance, local mitigation, toll route, and handback inside central bureaucracy |

The preferred rule is **public crossing authority plus contract waist plus statutory floor plus explicit non-border-government boundary**.

### 9. Review clock

Reopen this packet when any of the following occurs:

- project agreement, crossing agreement, toll regime, payment formula, or concession term changes materially;
- major delay, defect, claim, dispute, performance deduction, step-in, termination, safety, security, or toll complaint occurs;
- redactions prevent public reconstruction of payment, performance, change control, handback, or user-right fields;
- public audit, special examination, fairness report, or legislative scrutiny finds oversight weakness;
- construction ends and operations begin, because the live risk shifts from delivery to service, tolling, maintenance, safety, and handback;
- interface failure appears among concessionaire, crossing authority, border agencies, highway owners, emergency responders, or local communities;
- the project begins deciding border, land, policing, trade, immigration, or general fiscal questions outside its bounded crossing mission.

A long-term concession without a review clock becomes a constitution with invoices.

## Failure modes

- **concession sovereignty**: letting the private partner’s project agreement become the real public law of the crossing.
- **risk-transfer theater**: announcing that risk moved to the private sector without showing payment, deduction, claim, termination, and residual public exposure rules.
- **redaction fog**: hiding governance-critical contract fields behind undifferentiated confidentiality.
- **change-order constitutionalism**: materially altering public obligations through contract changes without public reapproval or explanation.
- **handback amnesia**: discovering asset condition, data, warranties, manuals, and lifecycle gaps only near the end of the term.
- **toll-route maze**: leaving users unable to distinguish toll policy, account administration, complaint, enforcement, and statutory appeal routes.
- **interface blame**: letting border agencies, highway owners, concessionaire, public authority, and governments point at each other after a failure.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Can a user find toll rates, account rules, complaint route, service route, and legal escalation without knowing the P3 structure?
2. Can the public identify the project agreement, redaction categories, contract hierarchy, payment logic, performance standards, change-control route, and handback requirements?
3. Can a delay, defect, safety issue, or service failure be tied to a named public authority, concessionaire duty, performance consequence, and continuity route?
4. Can the authority show which decisions remain with customs, immigration, policing, border security, environmental regulators, highway agencies, or local governments?
5. Can an auditor reconstruct whether risk transfer was real after claims, changes, inflation, delay, demand shifts, or termination exposure?
6. Can local communities find mitigation, construction-impact, employment / benefits, records, and claim routes without relying on project publicity?
7. Can handback condition be measured years before the end of the concession?
