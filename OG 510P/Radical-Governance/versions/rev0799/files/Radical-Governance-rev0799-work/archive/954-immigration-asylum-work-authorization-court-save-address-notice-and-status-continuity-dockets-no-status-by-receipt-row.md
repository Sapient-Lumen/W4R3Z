# 954 — Immigration, asylum, work authorization, court, SAVE, address, notice, and status continuity dockets: no status by receipt row

## One-line thesis

Immigration-status continuity is not a receipt notice, online case status, processing-time table, asylum-clock count, EAD card, SAVE verification, change-of-address submission, EOIR portal record, or court docket row. A person, family, sponsor, employer, school, landlord, benefits agency, court, or health provider can see a formally valid immigration row while notice, hearing, work authorization, status verification, representation, language access, detention/release, benefits access, or remedy fails. The archive should join person, case, notice, address, filing, work, court, status-verification, representative, custody, and downstream service records before treating immigration status as continuous.

## Why this matters

The archive had digital-status migration and watchlist/border automation packets, but it did not yet have a direct immigration-status continuity packet. That was risky because immigration status is a cross-service hinge: it affects work authorization, court deadlines, removal risk, eligibility checks, driver/license and benefits interactions, health and housing stability, family unity, and the ability to understand and correct government records. A USCIS receipt or EOIR docket can be true while a person misses a notice, cannot work, cannot prove status to a state agency, loses counsel, falls out of portal access, or is ordered removed after an address failure.

The governing repair phrase is **no status by receipt row**.

## Pattern pack

- **No status by receipt row.** A receipt number or pending case is denominator evidence, not proof of current lawful status, work authorization, hearing safety, or downstream service access.
- **No adjudication by processing-time table.** Processing-time pages and historical tables are queue context; they do not prove officer action, correct routing, notice, or remedy for a specific case.
- **No court safety by docket lookup.** Automated case information and Respondent Access show bounded case information; they do not prove notice receipt, counsel, language access, evidence acceptance, or safe hearing attendance.
- **No asylum protection by I-589 upload.** Filing an asylum form must be joined to jurisdiction, NTA/court routing, acceptance/rejection, service on DHS, evidence, clock, interview/hearing, and appeal/remedy.
- **No work authorization by clock.** An asylum clock or I-765 filing is not an EAD in hand, employer-recognized authorization, replacement, renewal, or card-delivery proof.
- **No notice by address form.** USCIS and EOIR address duties are separate; address submission must be joined to receipt, agency update, service lane, returned mail, and hearing/order consequences.
- **No benefits access by SAVE response.** SAVE is a verification exchange for agencies; it cannot alone prove a person's immigration record, eligibility, notice, or correction route.
- **No access by portal account.** myUSCIS, Case Status Online, EOIR Respondent Access, ECAS, and SAVE CaseCheck are useful surfaces, but portal visibility is not case continuity for people without stable devices, broadband, language access, counsel, or documents.
- **No remedy by contact center.** Help lines, inquiries, service requests, and portal feedback must produce traceable correction, reopened time, or stayed consequences before they count as repair.

## Minimum joined record

A serious immigration-status packet should contain at least:

1. **Person and authority file.** Person/family unit, A-number, receipt numbers, representatives, language/disability needs, address, sponsor/caregiver links, and immediate consequences.
2. **Case lane map.** USCIS benefit request, asylum office, EOIR immigration court/BIA case, NTA, court venue, filing date, acceptance/rejection, biometrics, RFE/NOID, decision, and appeal/motion tail.
3. **Notice and address proof.** USCIS address record, EOIR address record, proof of service, returned mail, portal notice, email/SMS, translated notice, and hearing/order consequences.
4. **Protection and work file.** I-589, asylum/withholding/CAT lane, asylum clock, I-765 category, EAD issuance/card delivery, renewal/replacement, employer/agency acceptance, and gap risk.
5. **Status-verification file.** SAVE request, requesting agency, initial/additional verification, CaseCheck result, mismatch/correction evidence, and downstream service decision.
6. **Court and record file.** ACIS/Respondent Access/ECAS/eROP, filings, evidence acceptance, DHS service, hearing, interpreter, counsel, appeal, motion, and outage/deadline handling.
7. **Digital and assisted-access file.** Portal login, device/broadband, mobile/desktop limitations, language, paper route, legal-aid/helper authority, and account recovery.
8. **Remedy and safety file.** Service requests, inquiries, motions, appeals, stay/reopen evidence, FOIA/record correction, card replacement, detention/release bridge, benefits/license repair, and after-action learning.

## Anti-theater tests

Ask these before relying on any immigration/status surface:

1. Which person and family unit does the status row actually bind?
2. Is the record a USCIS benefits record, an EOIR court record, a DHS enforcement record, a SAVE verification, or a downstream agency decision?
3. Was the filing accepted, rejected, transferred, administratively closed, reopened, appealed, or merely uploaded?
4. Did the person receive and understand the notice in time to act?
5. Are USCIS and EOIR addresses aligned where both matter?
6. Is work authorization proven by a current card/extension/receipt rule and employer-recognized evidence, or only by a clock or pending I-765?
7. Did a SAVE result cause a license, benefit, job, school, or health-care decision that needs its own correction packet?
8. Is portal access practical for the person, not just technically available?
9. Are detention, release, check-in, court transport, and family/medical consequences joined?
10. Does the remedy path restore status, work, hearing, benefits/license access, and records, or only log an inquiry?

## Useful adjacent packets

This docket should be used with:

- `889` for digital-status migration and no right by portal uptime.
- `903` and `905` for credential and representative-access boundaries.
- `913`–`916` for fraud flags, watchlists, border automation, and redress.
- `936`–`937` for custody/reentry where detention or release interrupts status continuity.
- `950`–`951` for broadband/portal dependency.

## Failure modes

- Filing accepted by one system but not visible to the person, counsel, downstream agency, or court.
- USCIS and EOIR address changes diverge, producing missed notices or in absentia risk.
- A case-status page shows pending while an RFE, hearing, fingerprint, or deadline has passed.
- A SAVE mismatch causes a benefit/license denial while the underlying immigration record remains pending or corrected elsewhere.
- A work-permit clock, card production notice, or automatic-extension assumption fails at the employer or downstream agency.
- EOIR portal access exists only for some users, devices, browsers, or case types, leaving unrepresented people with thinner information.
- An outage, upload rejection, file-size limit, or mobile-incompatible form converts access into missed filing.
- Representation, language, disability, or literacy gaps convert a formal right to file into nonuse.
- Detention or transfer breaks notice, counsel, medication, evidence gathering, or court attendance.
- A remedy route logs an inquiry without restoring the status, work, hearing, license, benefit, or record consequence.
