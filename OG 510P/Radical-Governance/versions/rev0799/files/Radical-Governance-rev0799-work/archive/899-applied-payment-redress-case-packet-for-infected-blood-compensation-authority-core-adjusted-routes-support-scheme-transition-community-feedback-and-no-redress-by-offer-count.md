# 899 — Applied payment-redress case packet for the Infected Blood Compensation Authority: core and adjusted routes, support-scheme transition, community feedback, and no redress by offer count

## One-line thesis

The Infected Blood Compensation Authority is best classified as a statutory compensation and support-transition authority for infected, affected, deceased-person, and support-scheme-linked claimants; it should not be scored as redress by offer count, tariff existence, or headline paid total until route choice, support-scheme continuity, estate and affected-person access, community-feedback response, reassessment, and payment-state tails are visible.

## Why this matters

This case tests payment repair in a setting where no amount of money can undo the harm, where many people have died or are seriously ill, and where continuing support payments, compensation routes, affected-person eligibility, estate claims, supplementary routes, community trust, and future worsening of health all matter.

It is also a governance stress test because the compensation scheme is being built while payments are being made. The source waist shows a move from initial powers to core payments, fuller regulations, later amendments, community-feedback channels, support-scheme transition, route choices, and fortnightly statistics. That is precisely the kind of system where an archive can be fooled by progress numbers unless it tracks who has been asked to claim, who started, who received an offer, who accepted, who was paid, which route they chose, and what support continues.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Case trigger

The trigger is a decades-long health, institutional, evidentiary, family, and compensation failure involving people infected and affected by contaminated blood, blood products, or tissue, with existing Infected Blood Support Schemes and a newer statutory compensation authority.

### 2. Form verdict

**Statutory compensation and support-transition authority.**

IBCA is not merely a claims-processing desk. It is a public repair body with a payment calculation function, claimant invitation sequence, route-choice architecture, support-scheme transition, estate and affected-person lanes, and community-feedback responsibilities.

### 3. Source waist facts

The May 21, 2026 progress update reported 4,402 people asked to start a claim, 4,064 who had started, 3,368 offers made with a total offer value of about £2.70 billion, 3,272 people paid, and about £2.13 billion paid. It also separated living infected people registered with existing support schemes, living infected people never compensated, people acting for deceased infected persons, and affected people. It separated adjusted-route and core-route payments.

The government scheme update describes the staged legal build: initial regulations, fuller March 2025 regulations expanding eligibility and supplementary routes, and later changes proposed to implement inquiry recommendations and improve administration. IBCA materials also state that existing support-scheme payments are included in compensation calculations from April 2025 and that responsibility for support-scheme payments is expected to move to IBCA in 2027.

### 5. Anti-theater tests

- **Offer-count theater**: offers made are not the same as accepted, paid, or adequate.
- **Tariff theater**: a tariff can simplify payment but cannot erase exceptional loss, care, worsening condition, or supplementary-route needs.
- **Support-transition theater**: compensation calculations must not silently destabilize existing support payments.
- **Estate-friction theater**: deceased-person and bereaved claims must not fail because probate, paperwork, or historic record barriers are too high.
- **Affected-person delay theater**: opening the scheme to affected people must be measured by invited, started, offered, paid, and pending tails.
- **Community-engagement theater**: meetings and feedback channels must show issue disposition, not only listening.
- **Route-choice theater**: core and adjusted routes must be intelligible, reversible where promised, and not coerced by delay.
- **Progress-statistics theater**: fortnightly statistics should include denominators and categories enough to show who is still outside payment.

### 6. Opposition brief

A fair opposition says IBCA is paying large sums, publishing frequent progress data, separating claimant classes, and prioritizing people with urgent health circumstances. It may also argue that scheme amendments and community-feedback routes show learning rather than failure.

The archive accepts those are real controls. The remaining governance question is whether the payment architecture can preserve dignity, continuity, route choice, and dispute routes while scaling to all infected and affected people without hiding exclusions behind offer totals.

### 7. Success condition

The scheme becomes a strong positive-control case only when the public record can show stable route-choice evidence, support-scheme transition receipts, estate and affected-person throughput, reassessment and worsening-condition routes, community-feedback disposition, payment receipt, and open-case tails by claimant class.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Do not score IBCA progress by offers alone; separate people invited, claims started, complete assessments, offers made, offers accepted, payments issued, payments received, route choices, and remaining claimant classes.
2. Do not treat core-route speed as adequate if adjusted-route claimants, affected people, bereaved partners, estates, supplementary categories, or support-scheme recipients face unclear or slower access.
3. Do not let compensation-route choice silently destabilize existing support payments or bereaved-partner continuity.
4. Do not treat community feedback as engagement unless the public record shows disposition, design response, amendment, or principled refusal.
5. Do not let tariff clarity substitute for individualized evidence where lifelong care, financial loss, family harm, or health deterioration requires reassessment.

## Holding

IBCA is a serious statutory redress authority, but the archive should not call the infected blood scandal repaired by regulation, offer count, or amount paid. The correct question is whether each claimant class can move from recognition to payment and ongoing support without losing dignity, evidence, continuity, or review.
