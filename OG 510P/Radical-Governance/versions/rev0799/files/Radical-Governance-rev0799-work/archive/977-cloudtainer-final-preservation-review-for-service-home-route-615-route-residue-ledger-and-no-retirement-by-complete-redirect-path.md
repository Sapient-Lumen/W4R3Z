## One-line thesis

A route with a successor note, generated tests, source parity, a protected-element redirect ledger, and reader-visible redirects is still not deletion-ready until a final preservation review proves that no behavior, source-boundary warning, cross-sector translation, or reader function remains only in the old route.

## Why this matters

The service-home route is now the archive's most advanced retirement candidate. Note `615` has a successor route in note `974`, a service-home test matrix, a protected-element redirect ledger, reader-visible redirect surfaces, and generated retirement-audit support. That is real progress, but it is also the dangerous moment when an archive can confuse a complete path with completed preservation.

The wrong move would be to delete or demote note `615` because the redirect path looks complete. The right move is a final preservation review: compare the old route to the successor route, list what was absorbed, list what remains as residue, and require a status guard before any future deletion authorization.

This revision records that review. The conclusion is conservative: note `615` remains active lineage. The successor route is now the preferred route for new service-home work, but the original note still carries preserved lineage and cross-sector caution that should not be silently discarded.

## Pattern pack

### 1. A complete redirect path is not a complete retirement

A retirement candidate can now have all of the attractive signals:

- successor route;
- generated test matrix;
- source-key parity;
- protected-element redirect ledger;
- reader-visible redirect from the old note;
- generated retirement-audit row; and
- current revision audit note.

Those signals reduce navigation risk. They do not prove that the older note has no remaining function.

### 2. Separate absorbed elements from route residue

For note `615`, the final preservation review finds six major absorbed elements: service map, authority/operator/regulator split, local front door, complaint handoff, continuity/failure takeover, and operator-chain constitution. Those are now mapped into note `974`, `SERVICE_HOME_TESTS`, source keys, and the redirect ledger.

But the review also finds route residue:

- note `615` still holds the original long-form formulation of the service-home constitutional upgrade;
- note `615` still carries a compact matrix contrasting thin service arrangements with honest service homes;
- the successor route is intentionally modular and test-oriented, while the old route remains useful as lineage explanation;
- source parity is claim-bound and does not prove real continuity or failure-takeover in each sector;
- cross-sector maturity remains stronger for transport/water examples than for social-service, emergency, administrative, and digital-service examples; and
- no deletion authorization has been recorded.

Residue is not a failure. Hidden residue is the failure.

### 3. Require a status guard after preservation review

After this review, `MP-003-615-to-849` should not read as a deletion track. It should read as:

`final_preservation_review_keep_active_not_deletion_ready`.

That status means note `974` is the preferred successor route, but note `615` remains active lineage until a later packet proves that the remaining route residue has been either preserved elsewhere or intentionally retired with reasons.

### 4. Preserve reader function, not only protected elements

The redirect ledger maps protected elements. The final review asks a different question: what does a reader lose if note `615` disappears?

The reader would lose the first long-form explanation of why shared territorial service power needs a service home as much as a democratic, fiscal, administrative, public-law, accountability, participatory, and knowledge home. That narrative function is not fully replaced by the successor route's test scaffold.

A future revision may still absorb that function, but it has not done so yet.

### 5. Treat source parity as limited by proof class

The service-home route uses useful sources, but each source proves only a bounded claim. Governance pages show authority structure. Complaint pages show escalation routes. Business plans show planning posture. Customer-community pages show service-area relationships. Metropolitan-governance literature shows coordination typology. None of them proves that a person actually received service during failure, operator exit, or intermunicipal conflict.

Therefore the final review blocks retirement by source parity alone.

### 6. Convert deletion pressure into next-action requirements

The next action is not deletion. The next action is either:

- build applied service-home packets outside London transport and MWRA-style utility governance;
- add source/test parity for social-service, emergency, administrative, and digital shared-service families;
- preserve the old route's matrix in a durable generated or successor section; or
- create a later deletion-authorization packet that explains why the residue is no longer needed.

Until then, note `615` stays active.
