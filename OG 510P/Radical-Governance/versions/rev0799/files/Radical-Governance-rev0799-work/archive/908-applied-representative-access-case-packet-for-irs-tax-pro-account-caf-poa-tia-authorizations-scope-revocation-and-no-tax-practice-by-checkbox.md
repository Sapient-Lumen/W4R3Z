# 908 — Applied representative-access case packet for IRS Tax Pro Account / CAF / POA / TIA authorizations: scope, revocation, and no tax practice by checkbox

## One-line thesis

IRS Tax Pro Account, Centralized Authorization File records, Power of Attorney, and Tax Information Authorization should be classified as a professional and third-party representative-access waist: digital authorization can speed tax representation and disclosure, but a checkbox, online-account approval, CAF entry, or tax-professional credential cannot replace matter scope, period limits, professional eligibility, taxpayer consent, revocation, notice routing, and non-digital alternatives.

## Why this matters

The IRS Tax Pro Account is a useful representative-access experiment. IRS says tax professionals can request Power of Attorney and Tax Information Authorization, view taxpayer information, withdraw authorizations, link business CAF numbers, and manage authorized users. It also says Tax Pro Account can provide real-time processing for individual POA / TIA requests, withdrawals, payments and payment plans, and CAF-number requests for individuals.

IRS guidance also preserves important boundaries. Tax Pro Account submissions are for individual taxpayers with online accounts and limited tax matters and periods; online form upload and fax / mail routes remain available for broader taxpayers, matters, periods, and signature formats. IRS authorization guidance distinguishes Power of Attorney, Tax Information Authorization, third-party designee, and oral disclosure. It also says POA and TIA authorizations are recorded in the Centralized Authorization File unless a specific-use exception applies, and that authorizations may be revoked or withdrawn.

This is exactly the archive's problem: a representative route can be faster and more secure while also becoming exclusionary if it depends on the taxpayer's online account, if matters or periods are misunderstood, if notification and revocation are weak, or if professional authority is treated as a general key to the taxpayer's public record.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Trigger and dispatcher table

| Field | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| trigger | taxpayer wants a professional or third party to represent them, receive information, or act on specified tax matters |
| boundary mismatch | taxpayer identity, tax professional identity, professional eligibility, CAF record, POA scope, TIA disclosure, third-party designee, oral disclosure, and online account access diverge |

### 2. Boundary map

| Component | Boundary rule |
| --- | --- |
| taxpayer account | route for approval, not proof that representation is valid for all matters |
| POA | representation before IRS for specified matters by eligible practitioner or authorized person |
| TIA | disclosure / receipt of confidential information, not full practice authority |
| CAF | verification record for authorized matters and periods, not blanket access |
| third-party designee | return-processing communication route with limited period and issues |
| oral disclosure | conversation-limited disclosure unless another authorization is created |
| Tax Pro Account | real-time and one-stop route for eligible digital authorizations, not the only valid route |
| revocation / withdrawal | must be recorded, visible, and timely enough to prevent stale access |

### 5. Representative-access docket

This case activates the representative-access tests:

1. **Authority type** — distinguish POA, TIA, third-party designee, oral disclosure, and specific-use authorization.
2. **Eligibility and identity** — prove the taxpayer and representative identities and, for POA, professional eligibility where required.
3. **Matter and period scope** — record form, tax type, years, periods, penalties, spouse issues, and specific-use exceptions.
4. **Approval route** — separate Tax Pro Account approval, online upload, fax, mail, and oral authorization.
5. **Notification and receipt** — show who receives confirmation, notice, and status.
6. **CAF recording** — verify that the authorization is recorded where expected and not recorded where specific-use rules apply.
7. **Revocation and withdrawal** — publish how authority ends and how stale authorizations are removed.
8. **Online-account dependence** — preserve routes for taxpayers without online accounts or whose account access fails.
9. **Firm user control** — when businesses link CAF numbers and add users, record internal role and access control.
10. **Deadline and dispute protection** — prevent authorization processing delays from consuming taxpayer response or appeal windows.

### 6. Opposition and rival readings

**Rival 1: Digital authorizations are faster and reduce paper backlogs.** The archive accepts this. It asks for scope, alternatives, and revocation proof.

**Rival 2: Professional access needs strong identity and account controls.** Correct. Strong controls should not collapse TIA into POA or online consent into all-matter authority.

**Rival 3: Taxpayers can still fax or mail forms.** This is a mitigation only if paper routes remain timely, visible, and effective for deadlines.

**Rival 4: CAF entries are internal administrative records.** They are also public-law reliance records because IRS assistors use them to decide who may receive confidential information or act.

### 7. Capture and theater channels

- **checkbox-practice theater**: a taxpayer online approval is treated as enough without scope comprehension.
- **CAF-entry theater**: internal record existence is treated as current authority despite revocation or matter limits.
- **real-time-processing theater**: speed is counted as governance quality without access and alternative-route proof.
- **professional-account capture**: firm user management becomes practical access law without public-owner boundaries.
- **online-account exclusion**: taxpayers who cannot access online accounts are pushed into slower representation routes with deadline risk.

### 8. Repair surfaces

The packet remains open until the record shows:

- authorization type;
- taxpayer and representative identity proof;
- professional eligibility where relevant;
- tax matter and period scope;
- signature and consent route;
- CAF or non-CAF recording rule;
- notification and notice routing;
- revocation / withdrawal confirmation;
- online and non-online alternatives;
- deadline protection for processing delay.

## Anti-theater tests

This packet fails if the archive treats “Tax Pro Account,” “CAF,” “POA,” “TIA,” “checkbox,” “real-time processing,” or “professional” as enough. It passes only when authority type, scope, period, eligibility, notice, revocation, and alternatives are visible.

## Holding

IRS Tax Pro Account / CAF / POA / TIA is a professional and third-party tax representative-access waist. The rule is: **no tax practice by checkbox**. Digital authorization can speed representation, but it cannot erase scope, consent, revocation, or non-digital public-service duties.
