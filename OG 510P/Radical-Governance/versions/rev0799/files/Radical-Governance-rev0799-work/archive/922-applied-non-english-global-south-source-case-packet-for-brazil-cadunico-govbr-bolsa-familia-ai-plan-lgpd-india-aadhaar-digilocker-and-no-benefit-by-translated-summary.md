# 922 — Applied non-English / Global South source case packet for Brazil CadÚnico, gov.br, Bolsa Família, AI plan, LGPD, India Aadhaar, DigiLocker, and no benefit by translated summary

## One-line thesis

Brazil’s CadÚnico / gov.br / Bolsa Família / AI-and-data-governance stack, with India’s Aadhaar and DigiLocker as a comparator, is a source-language and public-service delivery packet: digital identity, data integration, AI strategy, benefit qualification, and official multilingual surfaces only count when authoritative-language sources connect to cure, appeal, assistance, and payment evidence.

## Why this matters

This packet repairs the archive’s English-source bias without turning “Global South” into a slogan. The concrete object is a benefits-and-identity seam. In Brazil, social-program eligibility and payment depend on CadÚnico records, municipal and federal workflows, CPF / gov.br identity, Bolsa Família rules, qualification and review processes, data-protection law, digital-government strategy, and emerging AI policy. In India, Aadhaar authentication, offline verification, DigiLocker document sharing, and DPDP rules show a different but adjacent official-source problem: large-scale identity and document systems publish many official surfaces, some multilingual or machine-translated, that are useful only when the affected-person consequence and remedy route remain visible.

The packet does not claim Brazil and India are the same system. It uses them as a controlled comparator: both show how scale, identity, data exchange, and official digital-service pages can produce an illusion of clear governance while the actual burden sits in source language, publication status, local implementation, credential route, and human-access repair.

The anti-theater rule is **no benefit by translated summary**. A translated description of a registry, account level, AI plan, or document-wallet service cannot prove that a family, claimant, applicant, or user can update data, cure a mismatch, preserve a payment, correct an identity record, or appeal a consequential action.

## Case frame

This applied packet starts with Brazil because it has a tight, live public-service chain:

- Digital Government Law and 2024-2027 digital-government strategies create a national transformation frame.
- LGPD and ANPD sources establish personal-data and automated-decision-review relevance.
- CadÚnico identifies and characterizes low-income families and is used by multiple programs.
- Bolsa Família law and decree establish the benefit regime that can be affected by registry and qualification processes.
- gov.br account levels create differential access and security states for digital services.
- CadÚnico 2025 / 2026 informativos and FAQs show operational review, qualification, CPF migration, update, block, cancellation, and local-office routes.
- The Brazilian AI Plan and pending AI bill sources show policy direction and regulatory currentness, but not yet operational benefit repair.

India enters as a comparator, not a substitute:

- UIDAI regulations and Aadhaar authentication / offline verification materials show identity proofing and authentication infrastructure.
- UIDAI’s multilingual site surface is itself a translation-risk object where official page text can be machine translated.
- DigiLocker reports large-scale digital-document access, sharing, and verification, but the governance question is consent, scope, logs, requester behavior, and alternative access.
- DPDP Act sources frame personal-data processing obligations, but do not by themselves prove service-level remedy.

### 3. Case-state decomposition

Before citing this packet, split the case into these states:

1. **language state**: Portuguese original, English digest, machine translation, Hindi / regional-language site, or archive translation;
2. **publication state**: law, decree, regulation, bill, consultation, service page, FAQ, informe, portal, or local instruction;
3. **identity state**: CPF, gov.br level, Aadhaar number, biometric / offline verification, bank validation, phone, document wallet, or local interview;
4. **benefit state**: registry update, program eligibility, block, suspension, cancellation, payment, appeal, restoration, or local-office pending state;
5. **data state**: data source, integration, match, inconsistency, stale record, correction, audit log, and data-protection route;
6. **translation state**: controlling official text, courtesy official translation, machine translation, third-party summary, or unverified digest;
7. **remedy state**: notification, explanation, local cure, assisted route, appeal, stay, retroactive payment, identity correction, or data-access request;
8. **currentness state**: last checked, amendment, supersession, legislative status, review cadence, and source-health fallback.

### 5. Rival readings

**Rival 1: Official domains are enough.** They are not. Official service pages can be volatile, narrower than law, and insufficient for legal or remedy claims.

**Rival 2: English summaries democratize access.** They help, but they can also hide authoritative-language terms, procedural deadlines, local offices, and payment repercussions.

**Rival 3: Digital identity increases inclusion by default.** It can, but inclusion must be proven through fallback, correction, local assistance, appeal, and payment continuity.

**Rival 4: AI strategy proves responsible AI posture.** Strategy is useful policy context; it does not prove that a specific benefit or identity workflow has monitoring, redress, or explanation.

**Rival 5: Comparator cases can be summarized at country level.** Country-level summaries erase the source-state differences that determine whether a claim is legally and operationally safe.

## Failure modes

- **No benefit by translated summary**: an English or machine-translated description replaces the official-language operational rule that controls update, block, cancellation, or appeal.
- **No access by gov.br level**: account level or login state is treated as entitlement access without assisted routes and service-owner fallback.
- **No eligibility by Cadastro row**: registry status is treated as final eligibility without benefit-specific rule, notice, cure, and payment record.
- **No remedy by CadÚnico FAQ**: a service FAQ explains update routes but not appeal, stay, restoration, or retroactive payment.
- **No AI governance by PBIA**: AI strategy is treated as operational AI assurance for benefits or identity workflows.
- **No law by PL status**: pending AI-bill status is cited as enacted obligation.
- **No consent by document-wallet scale**: DigiLocker usage and document counts are treated as consent quality without scope, requester, log, revocation, and alternative-access evidence.
- **No multilingual access by Bhashini notice**: machine-translated official pages are treated as legally precise multilingual notice.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Does the packet identify the authoritative-language source and any translation caveat for every legal or service claim?
2. Does it distinguish laws, decrees, service pages, FAQs, informativos, consultations, bills, and strategy PDFs?
3. Does the source actually support the claim class being made?
4. If the case involves CadÚnico or Bolsa Família, can the family see the reason, update the data, preserve deadlines, reach local assistance, and recover payment where appropriate?
5. If the case involves gov.br account levels, can the service owner prove non-digital or assisted access for people who cannot raise account level?
6. If the case involves Aadhaar or DigiLocker, can the user see requester, scope, consent, logs, correction, revocation, and alternative route?
7. Does a data-protection source connect to a live access, correction, review, or automated-decision route rather than merely reciting principles?
8. Are local offices or frontline implementers named as evidence-bearing actors?
9. Is the source-health review cadence proportional to source volatility?
10. Can an affected person reconstruct the action without relying on an English summary?
