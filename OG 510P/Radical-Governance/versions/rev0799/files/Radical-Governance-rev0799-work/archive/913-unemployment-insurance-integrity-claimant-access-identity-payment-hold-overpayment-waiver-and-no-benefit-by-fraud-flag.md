# 913 — Unemployment insurance integrity, claimant access, identity, payment hold, overpayment, waiver, and no benefit by fraud flag

## One-line thesis

Unemployment-insurance integrity is not one control problem: eligibility, separation, identity, certification, payment release, fraud suspicion, overpayment, waiver, appeal, recovery, and claimant access are separate states, and no benefit should disappear by fraud flag alone.

## Why this matters

Pandemic UI exposed the archive's next highest-risk substantive gap. The existing payment-redress, credential-access, entitlement-continuity, and representative-access dockets cover adjacent terrain, but they do not yet name the particular waist where a federal-state unemployment system must pay quickly, verify identity, adjudicate separation and monetary eligibility, block fraud, preserve due process, recover overpayments, protect identity-theft victims, and avoid converting valid claimants into invisible false positives.

This docket is intentionally not a pro-payment or anti-fraud shortcut. It accepts that UI fraud was large, that emergency programs created attack surfaces, and that public dollars need controls. It also accepts that identity proofing, payment holds, overpayment notices, fraud labels, and recovery programs can become wrongful denial machinery when they do not publish enough state evidence to distinguish criminal claims, suspicious claims, unresolved identity proof, agency error, employer delay, claimant mistake, nonfraudulent overpayment, waived overpayment, pending appeal, and ordinary benefit entitlement.

The practical rule is: **no benefit by fraud flag**. A fraud indicator can start a control lane, but it should not be the whole record. If the system cannot say what state the claim is in, what proof is missing, what route cures it, what payment can proceed, what notice was given, what appeal exists, and what overpayment or waiver category applies, it is not governing UI integrity; it is exporting risk to claimants, staff, employers, auditors, and courts.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Split the UI state model

| State | Governance question |
| --- | --- |
| monetary eligibility | Was base-period / earnings / attachment evidence established? |
| separation and continuing eligibility | Is the separation issue, able-and-available issue, weekly certification, work-search rule, or employer contest actually at issue? |
| identity status | Is identity verified, pending, inconclusive, failed, fraud-victim, or routed to a non-digital path? |
| payment state | Is payment payable, held, partly paid, offset, intercepted, reversed, exhausted, or delayed for an identified reason? |
| fraud suspicion | What triggered the flag, what confidence level exists, and which fact remains unresolved? |
| overpayment classification | Is it fraudulent, nonfraudulent, agency-caused, claimant-caused, employer-caused, identity-theft-related, or still pending? |
| waiver / recovery | Is waiver legally available, requested, pending, granted, denied, or barred? |
| appeal / redetermination | What notice, deadline, record, hearing, decision, and retroactive payment result exist? |
| claimant access | Can the claimant see status, upload proof, use assisted and non-digital routes, appoint help, and obtain language / disability access? |
| state / federal oversight | Can DOL, OIG, GAO, state auditors, legislatures, and the public evaluate both payment integrity and valid-claimant harm? |

A UI record that collapses those states into “pending,” “fraud,” “denied,” or “overpaid” cannot be used for fair adjudication or honest integrity reporting.

### 2. Fraud control and access are the same system

Fraud control is not external to service delivery. The same systems that stop bots, stolen identities, mule accounts, suspicious IP patterns, duplicate SSNs, prison/death-record matches, foreign traffic, and employer-fabrication schemes also determine whether legitimate laid-off workers can prove identity, receive notices, cure documents, keep housing, and avoid ruinous overpayment recovery.

Therefore the control denominator must include false positives and claimant burden. “Dollars stopped” is not a sufficient metric unless paired with valid claimants delayed, abandonment, appeal reversals, identity-theft victim repair, time-to-payment after cure, and group-level access evidence.

### 3. Identity proofing must preserve non-digital and assisted routes

Shared digital identity services can reduce fraud and reuse stronger proofing, but they cannot become the public-benefit entitlement. The unemployment agency remains the public owner of the benefit action. If Login.gov, USPS in-person proofing, a state portal, a private identity vendor, device checks, document scans, selfie comparison, or MFA fails, the record must show what non-digital, assisted, disability-accessible, language-accessible, and representative-friendly route remains.

A hybrid in-person proofing path that still requires front-end online steps should not be counted as the only non-digital path. The access test is not whether a service exists in a diagram; it is whether a claimant who cannot pass the standard digital flow can still apply, cure, certify, appeal, and get paid when eligible.

### 4. Overpayment recovery is not integrity proof by itself

Overpayment totals and recovery rates can expose failures, but they can also hide the relevant state. A useful overpayment ledger separates fraudulent from nonfraudulent overpayments, waivable from nonwaivable debts, identity-theft victim cases from claimant debts, agency-caused errors from claimant-caused errors, pending appeals from final debts, waived amounts from uncollected amounts, and recovery amounts from actual repair of program controls.

A high recovery target can harm innocent claimants if it collects from people who should receive waiver, appeal relief, identity-theft correction, or corrected eligibility. A low recovery rate can indicate weak recovery systems, but it can also reflect legal waiver, old emergency program constraints, identity-theft complexity, or missing baseline. The archive should force those meanings apart.

### 5. State capacity and vendor dependence are not background facts

UI is a federal-state system. State workforce agencies operate different laws, systems, staffing levels, call centers, portals, vendors, identity contracts, payment processors, employer interfaces, and appeal processes. The docket therefore treats state system performance as a governance fact. A federal modernization grant, open-source prototype, or pilot is not repair unless it yields state-level performance evidence: task completion, status visibility, processing errors, call-center access, payment timeliness, appeal latency, identity false-positive repair, and improved outcomes for high-burden groups.

### 6. Audit and public metrics should count both harms

The minimum UI integrity dashboard should include:

- payment timeliness, by claim state and hold reason;
- identity verification attempts, failures, pending cases, non-digital route usage, and resolution time;
- fraud flags, referrals, confirmed fraud, cleared flags, and valid-claimant false-positive samples;
- overpayment totals by fraud / nonfraud / waiver / appeal / identity theft / recovery state;
- notices sent, returned, understood, appealed, reversed, and paid retroactively;
- call-center, portal, document-upload, language, disability, and representative-access performance;
- employer-separation response timing and adjudication effects;
- state IT performance against standards and modernization milestones.

A system that reports only improper payments or only claimant satisfaction is measuring one side of a two-sided failure.

## Findings table

| Risk | Bad shortcut | Repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| fraud flag | hold or deny without state detail | reason, confidence, proof, cure, and payment-state ledger |
| identity proof | digital path becomes benefit gate | assisted, non-digital, disability, language, and representative routes |
| overpayment | treat all debts as collectable fraud | fraud / nonfraud / waiver / identity-theft / appeal state split |
| recovery metrics | count money recovered as integrity | include waiver, legal limits, outstanding debt, and control repair |
| modernization | cite grants and pilots | require state IT performance and claimant outcome evidence |
| access metrics | report portal availability | measure task completion, abandonment, call access, and subgroup burden |
| appeals | treat hearings as downstream | treat appeals and reversals as core delivery evidence |
| federal-state split | hide behind state variation | publish comparable minimum metrics while preserving state law differences |

## Failure modes

- **Fraud-flag theater**: a suspicious signal becomes an all-purpose payment block without a cure route or appealable reason.
- **Integrity-only theater**: stopped dollars are reported but valid-claimant delays, false positives, and abandonment are not.
- **Access-only theater**: customer-experience improvements are cited without fraud, overpayment, and recovery-state controls.
- **Digital-identity gatekeeping**: the identity provider becomes the practical owner of benefit access.
- **Overpayment fog**: fraudulent, nonfraudulent, waivable, agency-caused, appeal-pending, and identity-theft debts are merged.
- **Recovery-by-collection**: collection totals are treated as program repair even when underlying controls and waiver justice remain unclear.
- **State-variation opacity**: fifty-plus different state systems are invoked to avoid comparable performance floors.
- **Pilot optimism**: a prototype, grant, playbook, or AI-adjudicator experiment is treated as repair before measured state performance changes.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Can a claimant tell why a payment is held, what evidence is missing, how to cure it, and what deadline or appeal applies?
2. Can an auditor distinguish identity failure, fraud suspicion, eligibility issue, employer issue, certification issue, payment processing issue, and overpayment recovery issue?
3. Does the fraud dashboard count valid claimants delayed or blocked, not only dollars stopped?
4. Does identity proofing preserve a non-digital, assisted, disability-accessible, language-accessible route that is not materially slower or punitive?
5. Are fraudulent and nonfraudulent overpayments, waivers, appeals, identity-theft cases, and recoveries separated?
6. Are state IT modernization claims tied to measured system performance rather than grants, pilots, or procurement events?
7. Are appeal reversals and retroactive payments treated as evidence of delivery failure to learn from, not as isolated exceptions?
8. Can Congress, DOL, OIG, GAO, state auditors, claimants, employers, and legal aid see enough comparable data to diagnose both fraud risk and claimant-access harm?
