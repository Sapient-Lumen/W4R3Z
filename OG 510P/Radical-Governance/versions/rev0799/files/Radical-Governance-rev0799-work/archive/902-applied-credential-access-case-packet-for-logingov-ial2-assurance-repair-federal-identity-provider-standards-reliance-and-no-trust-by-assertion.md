# 902 — Applied credential-access case packet for Login.gov IAL2 assurance repair: federal identity provider, standards reliance, and no trust by assertion

## One-line thesis

Login.gov should be classified as a federal shared identity-provider and assurance-repair waist: useful because it can centralize sign-in, proofing, in-person routes, fraud controls, and federation, but high-risk because relying agencies can over-credit assurance claims unless standard version, service offering, certification scope, billing, fallback, and user exclusion records stay public.

## Why this matters

Login.gov is not just a login page. It is a federal shared identity service used by agencies that need authentication and, for some transactions, identity proofing. It therefore sits between members of the public and many distinct public services. A single identity-provider representation can propagate trust into passports, benefits, tax, grants, immigration, contracting, veterans services, state and local partner services, and future wallets or passkey routes.

The case is a strong stress test because the public record contains both failure and repair. The GSA Inspector General found that GSA misled customer agencies about Login.gov's compliance with NIST SP 800-63-3 IAL2 requirements and billed agencies for alleged IAL2 services that did not meet those standards. Later Login.gov materials state that an independently certified IAL2-compliant identity verification option became available, and public programme updates describe in-person proofing, translations, passport evidence, anti-fraud investments, and partner upgrades.

The archive should neither freeze Login.gov at the failure report nor accept a repair claim as complete by announcement. The honest form is an assurance-repair credential-access waist.

Reserve `822` for monetary redress or billing repair, and `824` for enforcement or sanction contexts.

### 4. Credential-access docket

This case activates the credential-access tests:

1. **Relying-service consequence** — identify which agencies and transactions rely on Login.gov, and what happens if proofing fails.
2. **Assurance fit** — distinguish basic sign-in, identity proofing, IAL2, authentication strength, and federation assurance.
3. **Certification scope** — show exactly which offering, standard version, assessor, and transaction classes the certification covers.
4. **Partner migration receipt** — publish which partner agencies upgraded, which have not, and what legacy assurance statements remain.
5. **Billing and contract honesty** — repair any mismatch between charged service, promised assurance, and delivered assurance.
6. **Proofing route diversity** — measure remote, in-person, document, passport, language, accessibility, and assisted routes separately.
7. **Hard-to-proof equity** — disclose failure, abandonment, false rejection, and support rates by relevant accessibility and evidence classes where lawful.
8. **Fraud and account-lock boundary** — distinguish fraud prevention from service denial; preserve follow-on instructions and agency routes.
9. **Redress and recovery route** — make account recovery and proofing redress fast enough for time-sensitive public services.
10. **Standard-change reapproval** — reapprove when NIST guidance, service offerings, identity evidence, biometric routes, or relying transactions change materially.

### 5. Opposition and rival readings

**Rival 1: centralized identity prevents fraud and reduces agency duplication.** The archive accepts the benefit. It rejects the inference that centralization proves assurance fit for each relying transaction.

**Rival 2: the IAL2 issue has been repaired by a new certified option.** The archive treats the repair claim as relevant but not self-closing. Certification scope, partner migration, user exclusion, and old reliance statements still need receipts.

**Rival 3: higher proofing standards are necessary for high-risk services.** The archive agrees in principle. It asks whether low-risk services are over-proofed and high-risk services have fallback and redress.

**Rival 4: Login.gov is a provider, not the benefit agency.** Correct. That is why relying agencies cannot outsource their service-specific legal duties to an identity-provider status screen.

### 6. Capture and theater channels

- **trust-by-assertion theater**: agencies rely on an assurance claim without seeing certification scope or current standard version.
- **repair-by-roadmap theater**: future improvements are treated as proof that current exclusions are resolved.
- **in-person-proofing theater**: location count is cited without completion, accessibility, language, appointment, travel, and abandonment data.
- **billing theater**: an interagency charge is treated as proof of service delivered.
- **fraud theater**: fraud controls excuse indefinite lockouts or inaccessible proofing.
- **partner-abdication theater**: agencies blame Login.gov while their own service deadlines and fallback duties remain live.

### 7. Repair surfaces

The packet remains open until these surfaces are visible or deliberately unavailable:

- current assurance-level matrix by Login.gov offering;
- independent certification scope and expiry;
- partner-agency migration ledger;
- former IAL2 billing / agreement repair record;
- proofing-success, abandonment, redress, lockout, and recovery metrics;
- in-person proofing completion and accessibility data;
- language and disability-support evidence;
- fraud-lock and account-compromise response records;
- relying-service deadline and fallback instructions;
- standards-change review clock after NIST SP 800-63-4.

## Anti-theater tests

This packet fails if the archive treats "Login.gov is secure," "IAL2 is available," "certified," "in-person proofing exists," or "partner agencies choose their own assurance level" as enough. It passes only when standard scope, partner reliance, proofing equity, billing repair, fallback, recovery, and relying-service consequences are separately documented.

## Holding

Login.gov is an assurance-repair credential-access waist. The rule is: **no trust by assertion**. A shared identity provider can improve public access only if assurance claims remain dated, scoped, auditable, service-specific, and contestable.
