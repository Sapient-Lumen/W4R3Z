# 978 — Applied service-home case packet for LAHSA homelessness services: JPA waist, provider chain, transition receipts, and no service home by grant waist

## One-line thesis

A homelessness service home is not proved by a joint-powers authority, a Continuum-of-Care designation, a budget line, a provider network, or a public dashboard; it exists only when the service promise, operator chain, near-to-person front door, complaint handoff, payment continuity, data boundary, and transition/failure-takeover duty are visible across the city, county, provider, state, and federal lanes.

## Why this matters

Rev0779 left one serious residue behind note `615`: service-home doctrine had strong transport, water/utility, and intermunicipal anchors, but weaker applied evidence for social-service systems. That mattered because social-service waists are exactly where public institutions often mistake a coordination body for a service home. A person seeking shelter, outreach, case management, benefits help, or a housing pathway does not experience a joint-powers agreement. They experience handoffs, referrals, eligibility, provider capacity, casework, payment delays, data errors, and unresolved complaints.

The LAHSA packet is a useful stress test because it is neither a simple agency failure nor a simple principal failure. LAHSA is a joint-powers authority created by the City and County of Los Angeles; it coordinates and manages funds for shelter, housing, and services, but many decisive levers remain outside it: housing supply, land use, behavioral health, county departments, city contracts, federal grant rules, nonprofit provider capacity, and court/audit pressure. The service-home question is therefore not whether LAHSA is good or bad. It is whether the public service constitution is legible enough that residents, providers, auditors, city officials, county officials, and people experiencing homelessness can see who owes what when the chain breaks.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Start with the person-facing service promise, not the grant waist

The packet must name the person-facing promise before naming the regional body. In this case, the relevant promises include outreach contact, shelter referral, interim-housing access, case management, permanent-housing pathway support, complaint/correction routes, and continuity during transition. LAHSA can coordinate and fund parts of that chain, but the chain is not LAHSA alone.

Reject the phrase "homelessness service home" until the packet states:

- who can enter the service;
- where they enter;
- who determines next step;
- what provider or public body acts;
- what record follows the person;
- what happens when a provider, contract, data route, or payment lane fails; and
- where unresolved trouble can move.

A budget waist can fund services. It cannot by itself be the public service home.

### 2. Split the roles before assigning blame or credit

The minimum LAHSA service-home split is:

| Role | LAHSA packet reading | Do not hide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Authority / principal | City and County created the JPA and retain major policy, budget, land, health, and transition powers | city council, mayor, county supervisors, county departments, state/federal conditions |
| Coordinator / administrator | LAHSA coordinates and manages assigned funds and programs and is the HUD CoC lead | coordination is not ownership of all homelessness outcomes |
| Commissioner / grantor | contracts, advances, grant conditions, and provider monitoring are core waist functions | appropriations, measure funds, city/county direct contracting, federal terms |
| Operator / provider | nonprofit and contracted providers deliver much of the person-facing work | providers are not invisible arms of LAHSA, and provider failure is not automatically user failure |
| Data / reporting steward | HMIS, counts, dashboards, bed/service data, and performance records are waist functions | data-entry quality, offline beds, stale dashboards, and methodology limits |
| Regulator / auditor / monitor | city/county auditors, controllers, boards, federal grant monitors, and courts can inspect or compel | audit existence is not repair completion |
| Complaint / remedy owner | local service route, provider grievance, LAHSA route, city/county claims, audits, courts, and supervisors may all matter | no single contact page proves a remedy path |

The packet should make this table public before discussing reform.

### 3. Build a territorial service map instead of a homelessness institution map

A useful service map separates:

- services still owned by municipalities or city departments;
- county-owned health, behavioral health, housing, and social-service lanes;
- LAHSA-administered grants, contracts, CoC, count, data, coordination, and program lanes;
- nonprofit provider delivery lanes;
- state and federal subsidy, grant, disability, veterans, Medicaid/Medi-Cal, and housing lanes; and
- court, auditor, ombuds, claim, and supervisory remedy lanes.

A map of boards, commissions, departments, and programs is not enough. The map must show what a person or provider does when the previous lane fails.

### 4. Treat provider payment as service continuity, not back-office finance

In a social-service service home, payment failure is not merely an accounting matter. If providers cannot be paid, monitored, corrected, or reconciled on time, the person-facing chain becomes fragile even when the public dashboard remains visible.

The service-home packet should therefore track:

- invoice state;
- advance state;
- contract authority;
- deliverable evidence;
- monitoring status;
- corrective-action status;
- provider cash-flow risk;
- person-facing continuity impact; and
- who steps in if the provider cannot continue.

A public authority that coordinates services but cannot preserve provider continuity has not proven a service home.

### 5. Separate bed data, count data, and pathway data

The LAHSA case should never treat a count, shelter-bed row, dashboard, or pathway audit as interchangeable. Each answers a different question.

| Evidence surface | Can support | Cannot support alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| about / JPA / budget page | role, authority surface, budget route | delivered service, continuity, complaint success |
| shelter-bed / interim-housing audit | data-quality, availability, utilization, offline-bed risks | actual safe placement for each person |
| pathway audit | flow, exits, bottlenecks, permanent-housing path limits | full attribution to LAHSA or to any one principal |
| finance/contracts audit | payment, grant, contract, monitoring control risk | proof that providers stopped serving or that repair occurred |
| county transition page | planned institutional handback or redesign | continuity receipt, record transfer, or user protection |
| LAHSA response | agency repair claims and response posture | independent closure of audit findings |

The service home requires the join, not the row.

### 6. Require a transition receipt before calling restructuring repair

Moving functions from LAHSA to a county department, direct city contracting, or another arrangement may be justified. It is not repair unless the public can see transition receipts.

A transition receipt should list:

- functions moving;
- functions staying;
- contracts moving;
- records moving;
- data systems and dashboards moving;
- grants and federal roles moving or staying;
- provider payment and monitoring continuity;
- open audit recommendations;
- staff and delegated authority;
- complaint and claim routes; and
- user-facing service continuity protections.

Without that receipt, restructuring may only move opacity from one institution to another.

### 7. Keep the no-blame / no-credit boundary explicit

LAHSA should not be blamed for powers it does not hold, and city/county principals should not hide behind LAHSA for powers they retained. The packet must separate:

- LAHSA-owned failures, such as records, contracts, advances, monitoring, coordination, or data duties within its remit;
- principal-owned failures, such as land use, housing production, health services, enforcement choices, or appropriations;
- provider-owned failures, such as delivery, documentation, staffing, or compliance;
- state/federal constraints; and
- macro conditions outside any one entity.

A service home requires public attribution discipline.

### 8. Score the current service-home posture honestly

LAHSA is a service-home stress test, not a clean service-home success.

| Service-home test | LAHSA reading | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| service map | partially visible through LAHSA, city, county, provider, federal, audit, and transition surfaces | incomplete |
| authority/operator split | visible enough to reject one-body blame, but not always person-facing | partial |
| lawful delivery instrument | JPA / CoC / budget / contract routes exist | partial |
| near-to-person front door | mediated through providers, outreach, local/county/city systems, not one clean service home | weak / fragmented |
| complaint handoff | must be reconstructed across provider, LAHSA, city, county, audit, and legal lanes | weak |
| continuity / failure takeover | not proven by current source set; provider-payment and transition continuity are decisive | unresolved |
| source boundary | strong enough for route discipline, not for outcome proof | bounded |
| transition receipt | required before county/city redesign can be scored as repair | open |

The holding is: **service-home candidate with unresolved continuity and complaint-handoff proof**, not service-home achieved.

### 9. Use the packet to shrink note-615 residue, not to erase it

This packet reduces the rev0779 blocker that social-service service-home application was thin. It does not resolve emergency, administrative, or digital-service translation. It also does not reproduce all of note `615`'s long-form theory or the thin-versus-honest matrix.

Therefore this packet changes the status of the residue, not the status of note `615`.

### 10. Add source-boundary warnings to every applied claim

This packet relies on official and oversight source routes. It should not quote or litigate line-level findings unless a future revision performs page-level capture. Its source posture is enough to classify roles, evidence lanes, and service-home gaps; it is not enough to prove individual outcomes, full repair, or definitive current institutional status after every city/county action.

## Failure modes

- **service home by JPA**: the joint-powers structure is treated as proof of service continuity.
- **grant waist by public service**: fund coordination is mistaken for a complete person-facing service constitution.
- **provider fog**: contracted providers carry the public promise while invoices, monitoring, and continuity duties are hard to reconstruct.
- **dashboard halo**: bed counts, PIT counts, or pathway dashboards stand in for actual access, placement, or remedy.
- **audit-as-abolition**: audit findings become an automatic case for abolition instead of a typed repair docket.
- **department halo**: moving functions to a county department is treated as repair without transition receipts.
- **principal laundering**: city and county actors blame LAHSA for retained city/county levers.
- **LAHSA shield**: city and county actors hide their own decisions behind the JPA's technical role.
- **payment invisibility**: provider payment delays are treated as administrative noise rather than service-continuity risk.
- **source overreach**: official pages prove role and route, not lived homelessness repair.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Can a person experiencing homelessness identify the nearest entry route and what happens if that route fails?
2. Can a provider identify which public entity owes payment, monitoring, correction, escalation, and transition continuity?
3. Can a city or county official show which functions remain theirs and which are delegated?
4. Can the public distinguish LAHSA-administered funds from city/county/state/federal policy choices?
5. Can a dashboard row be joined to provider capacity, person-facing access, and complaint routes?
6. Can audit findings be traced to accepted, completed, transferred, disputed, blocked, or open states?
7. Can a county transition page be joined to contracts, records, staff, data, grants, and user routes?
8. Can an unsheltered person, a provider, and an auditor each find the same service promise owner?
9. Can the packet state who steps in if a provider exits, a payment lane fails, or a data route breaks?
10. Can the archive explain what LAHSA owns without turning homelessness itself into a LAHSA-owned outcome?
