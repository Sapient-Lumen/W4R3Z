# 798 — Reference classes and comparator cohorts for ideal governments by scope: morphology-matched peers, functional territories, capacity bands, and no benchmark by postcard

## One-line thesis

The archive should compare real governments-by-scope against the ideal stack through small, declared **comparator cohorts** built from the right **scope type, morphology, functional territory, legal vocation, and capacity band**, so benchmark work stays like-with-like instead of drifting into prestige case selection, capital-city halo, and false analogies between unlike institutions.

## Why this matters

The archive can now say **what the ideal stack is** (`790`), **how to score a real arrangement against it** (`791`), **how to test it under shock** (`792`), **how to keep that diagnosis current** (`793`), **how to move toward a better stack** (`794`), **what evidence should stay public** (`795`), **how failure can force intervention** (`796`), and **who may challenge those judgments** (`797`).

What it still lacked was one compact rule for a quieter but decisive research problem:

**what counts as the right comparison set before any of those judgments are made?**

Without that rule, the archive can still drift into flattering but weak comparison habits. A municipality gets praised or condemned against a national capital it does not resemble. A core city is compared with a real metropolitan authority even though one governs only municipal territory and the other governs a functional urban system. A deconcentrated regional field office is placed beside a devolved constitutional region as if both were the same species of government. A city-state, microstate, autonomous province, and ordinary municipality are thrown into one deck because all are “small”. A polity then appears to benchmark well or badly only because the peer set was chosen after the conclusion was already desired.

That is not a small methodological error. For this archive, it is a constitutional error. The wrong comparator class quietly rewrites what looks normal, feasible, underbuilt, overbuilt, or mis-scoped.

The archive therefore needs one compact doctrine of **reference classes and comparator cohorts**. Before a benchmark card is scored, a stress card interpreted, or an upgrade path proposed, the archive should be able to say what the relevant peers actually are, what territory counts as the unit of comparison, what official typology or morphology cue anchors the cohort, and why other tempting cases were excluded.

Otherwise the archive still permits one serious failure mode: **benchmark by postcard**.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Declare the comparator cohort before the score

A real case should not be scored first and matched to peers later. The archive should declare the comparator cohort at the start of the exercise.

That declaration should say, in compact form:

- the **scope being compared**,
- the **territorial unit actually being compared**,
- the **morphology or polity type**,
- the **legal vocation** of the unit,
- the **capacity or scale band**,
- and the small set of peers used for the first-pass comparison.

If the cohort is assembled only after the archive already knows which story it wants to tell, the benchmark is already contaminated.

### 2. Match the real scope before matching the visible label

The archive should compare governments according to what they **really are in the stack**, not according to whatever label is politically fashionable.

A “city” may actually be:

- a municipality,
- a municipality inside a larger metro,
- a fused city-county,
- a city-state,
- a deconcentrated national field platform,
- or the public face of a metropolitan authority that legally sits elsewhere.

Likewise, a “region” may be:

- a federal constituent unit,
- a devolved autonomous territory,
- a standard administrative region,
- a planning district,
- or a thin statistical shell.

The first comparator question is therefore not **what is it called?** but **what constitutional work does it actually do?**

### 3. Use morphology as the second filter, not as an afterthought

`750` already gives the archive a morphology rule for choosing honest stacks in dense metro-dominant, polycentric, federal, sparse, archipelagic, and cross-border settings. This note adds the comparison rule that follows from that insight.

A municipality in a sparse northern territory is not well benchmarked against a municipality of similar population in a dense polycentric corridor if the operating problem, distance burden, catchment, and back-office reality are radically different. A metropolitan government in a single dominant capital-region polity is not an ordinary peer of a metro in a large federal country unless that asymmetry is named.

Comparator cohorts should therefore be **morphology-matched** before they are treated as benchmark peers.

### 4. Use functional territories where the work is functional

For urban systems, the archive should normally compare the territory that actually carries the work: the functional urban area, commuting basin, service shed, catchment, corridor, watershed, logistics zone, or other real operational geography.

A municipality that governs only the legal core city should not be benchmarked as though it governs the entire labour market, housing market, flood plain, or transit basin unless it actually does. Likewise, a metropolitan authority should not be made to look overbuilt simply because it is being compared with a core municipality whose spillovers are silently handled by outside governments.

Where the function is metro, basin, corridor, or other cross-boundary system, the comparator territory should be functional too.

### 5. Separate legal vocation from capacity band

Two governments may occupy the same rough territorial tier while doing very different constitutional work.

The archive should therefore separate:

- **legal vocation** — municipality, metro, devolved region, federal state, planning shell, treaty compact, and so on;
- from **capacity band** — population served, budget depth, staff depth, own-source revenue room, borrowing space, and operational complexity.

This protects the archive from two opposite mistakes.

The first is **constitutional flattening**: treating all regions or all cities as one category even when one is a shared-rule partner and another is a light administrative shell.

The second is **capacity romanticism**: treating a low-capacity government as fully benchmark-satisfying because its legal label sounds strong on paper.

### 6. Prefer small named cohorts over giant prestige baskets

A useful comparator cohort is usually small. The archive should prefer a **base cohort** of perhaps five to twelve named peers rather than a giant bag of every institution that could conceivably fit.

That cohort should be tight enough that a reader can tell why each peer is in the set. It may be supplemented by:

- one or two **aspirational comparators** kept visibly separate from the base cohort,
- one or two **warning or anti-pattern comparators** used to show recurring failure forms,
- and one **near-neighbour institutional cousin** where border, legal, or historical similarity matters.

But these should not blur into the base peer class.

### 7. Publish why cases were excluded, not only why cases were included

The archive should briefly explain exclusion as well as inclusion.

Examples:

- capital cities excluded because they enjoy unusual national support or fused functions;
- city-states excluded because they collapse tiers that ordinary municipalities do not control;
- deconcentrated regional offices excluded because they are not self-governing regions;
- metropolitan transport authorities excluded because they are single-function agencies rather than whole-of-metro governments;
- and special autonomous territories excluded because their constitutional bargain is sui generis.

Exclusion logic is what stops the comparator cohort from becoming a hidden brief for the conclusion.

### 8. Refresh the cohort only on typed triggers

Comparator cohorts should be stable enough to support real learning. They should not change every time a more flattering case appears.

The archive should therefore refresh a cohort only when a typed trigger occurs, such as:

- boundary change,
- legal-status change,
- major function transfer,
- sustained population-band change,
- fiscal-autonomy shift,
- or a morphology reclassification that makes the old peer set plainly misleading.

Absent a trigger, the cohort should remain stable long enough for the benchmark series to mean something.

### 9. Compare continental and global bodies by function and legal depth, not by grandeur

Upper-tier and cross-border bodies are especially easy to compare badly. A continental compact, customs union secretariat, river-basin body, global alerting agency, treaty conference, or compliance committee should not be compared to a national state merely because both are “important”.

For these scopes, the archive should compare:

- **function class**,
- **legal depth**,
- **finance depth**,
- **dispute and compliance machinery**,
- and **democratic scrutiny chain**,

rather than surface prestige or headline salience.

A thin but effective treaty-bound body may benchmark very well against the right peers while looking weak only because someone compared it to a sovereign executive it was never meant to resemble.

### 10. Write the cohort into the benchmark card and the public proof pack

`791` gives the archive the scoring card, and `795` gives it the outward evidence surface. This note adds one discipline to both: the comparator cohort should be written down.

At minimum the benchmark card and proof pack should say:

- the cohort name,
- the criteria that generated it,
- the territorial unit being compared,
- and any important exclusion caveats.

Otherwise even a well-scored card leaves outsiders unable to tell whether the comparison class was honest.

## Comparator-cohort grammar

| Field | What the archive should declare | Typical outputs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Scope claimed** | what kind of government is being assessed | municipal / metropolitan / regional / national / compact / global |
| **Territorial unit used** | which actual geography is being compared | legal municipality / functional urban area / basin / province / nation / treaty area |
| **Morphology class** | which polity form shapes the comparison | dense-metro / polycentric / federal / sparse / archipelagic / cross-border overlay |
| **Legal vocation class** | what constitutional work the unit actually does | municipality / metro government / devolved region / planning shell / federal state / treaty body |
| **Capacity band** | broad scale and operating depth | low / medium / high capacity; small / mid / large population served |
| **Base cohort** | main peers used for the first-pass benchmark | five to twelve named peers |
| **Edge comparators** | aspirational or warning peers kept separate from the base cohort | one or two outliers, labelled as such |
| **Refresh triggers** | what would justify changing the cohort later | boundary / function / status / population / fiscal shift |

## Scope-sensitive defaults

| Scope being studied | First comparator rule | Common exclusion problem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Micro-local** | compare with similar steward cells inside similar municipalities or rural service settings | treating neighborhood voice bodies as miniature municipalities |
| **Municipal** | compare with municipalities in similar morphology and capacity bands, not with metros or city-states | capital-city halo or city-state confusion |
| **Metropolitan** | compare using the functional urban territory and fragmentation pattern | benchmarking a metro authority against a core municipality |
| **Regional / provincial / state** | compare by legal vocation first: federal partner, devolved region, administrative region, or platform region | treating a deconcentrated region as a self-governing one |
| **National** | compare nations with similar morphology, territorial spread, and subnational architecture | pretending a microstate and continental federation are ordinary peers |
| **Continental / plurilateral** | compare by competence bundle, finance depth, and scrutiny chain | comparing a treaty compact to a sovereign state |
| **Global** | compare by mandate type and compliance architecture | judging thin global bodies as failed world governments |

## Use rule for the archive

When later notes need to compare a real constitution, reform proposal, or field case with the archive’s ideal governments-by-scope stack, the archive should usually do these things in order:

1. **use `750` to identify the relevant polity morphology first**,
2. **use this note to declare the correct comparator cohort and territorial unit of comparison**,
3. **use `810` when the peer class is honest but the live question is still whether a frontier threshold has been crossed and therefore needs a typed threshold dossier before scoring or redesign begins**,
4. **use `791` to score the arrangement against the ideal stack once the peer class is honest and the relevant threshold question is settled enough to benchmark**,
5. **use `792` when the comparison also needs shock-weather evidence rather than steady-state scoring alone**,
6. **use `793` when the question is whether the benchmark should have been refreshed, drilled, or reopened already**,
7. **use `794` when the gap to peers implies a phased upgrade path rather than a static diagnosis**,
8. **use `795` when the public should be able to inspect the cohort, score, and exclusion logic outwardly**,
9. **use `797` when the comparator choice or benchmark judgment itself becomes disputed and needs a typed objection or review lane**.

The archive should not praise itself for benchmarking if it still cannot say **why these were the right peers**.

## Guardrails

- Do not compare legal core cities to real metro governments without naming the territorial mismatch.
- Do not compare deconcentrated regional administrations to devolved or federal regions as if both were self-government.
- Do not let capital cities, city-states, or microstates quietly dominate ordinary municipal comparisons.
- Do not choose the cohort after the score is already politically convenient.
- Do not mix morphology classes casually when distance, fragmentation, or settlement pattern changes the constitutional problem.
- Do not hide exclusion logic; publish why tempting cases were left out.
- Do not refresh the peer set opportunistically without a typed trigger.

## Failure modes

- **benchmark by postcard** — admired cases are selected for narrative glamour rather than because they are actual peers.
- **capital-city halo** — a capital with fused powers, unusual finance, or national support becomes the fake benchmark for ordinary municipalities.
- **municipal-metro conflation** — a core municipality and a metropolitan authority are treated as the same thing because both use city branding.
- **devolution mirage** — administrative regions are compared to constitutional regions as if legal vocation did not matter.
- **morphology blindness** — sparse, archipelagic, or federal realities are ignored and the archive imports a peer set from a different territorial world.
- **capacity flattening** — institutions with radically different fiscal and staff depth are treated as though the same benchmark result should be expected immediately.
- **cohort drift by preference** — the peer set changes whenever a better or worse story is needed.

## Practical tests

A comparator cohort is honest when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Is the unit being compared the real governing territory rather than the most photogenic legal label?
2. Has the archive distinguished municipality, metro, region, nation, compact, and global body before choosing peers?
3. Has morphology been declared rather than treated as background trivia?
4. Are legal vocation and capacity band both visible in the cohort design?
5. Is there a small named base cohort rather than an open-ended prestige basket?
6. Are aspirational or warning comparators kept visibly separate from the base peers?
7. Does the archive explain at least some important exclusions?
8. Are cohort changes tied to typed triggers rather than convenience?
9. Can an outsider read the benchmark card or proof pack and tell why these were the right peers?

## Compression rule for the archive

If the archive can say **what ideal government by scope should look like**, **how to score a real arrangement**, and even **how to stress, maintain, repair, or challenge that arrangement**, but cannot also say **which governments count as the right peers, what territory is actually being compared, and why tempting prestige cases were excluded**, then it still has **benchmarking without an honest reference class**.
