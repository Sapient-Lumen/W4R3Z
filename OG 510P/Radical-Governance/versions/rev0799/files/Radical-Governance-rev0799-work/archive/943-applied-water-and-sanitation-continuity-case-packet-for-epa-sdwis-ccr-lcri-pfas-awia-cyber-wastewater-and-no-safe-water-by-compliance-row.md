# 943 — Applied water and sanitation continuity case packet for EPA SDWIS, CCR, LCRI, PFAS, AWIA, cyber, wastewater, and no safe water by compliance row

## One-line thesis

EPA drinking-water data, Consumer Confidence Reports, lead-service-line rules, PFAS standards and proposed compliance changes, AWIA risk/resilience certifications, water-sector cybersecurity advisories, and wastewater infrastructure surveys are necessary evidence lanes, but none proves that people currently have safe drinking water, sanitation, notice, affordability, and repair. The applied rule is **no safe water by compliance row**.

## Why this matters

Water is a public-health and public-service continuity problem. SDWIS can show public-water-system information, violations, and enforcement; CCRs can tell customers about annual quality; lead and PFAS rules can set obligations; AWIA can require planning; cyber guidance can warn of increasing attacks; and infrastructure surveys can justify capital spending. But a family, school, clinic, nursing home, jail, shelter, tribal system, or small town still needs tap-level safety, reachable notices, alternate supply, sanitation, funding, and accountable repair.

## Pattern pack

- **No safe water by compliance row.** SDWIS, ECHO, CCR, AWIA, LCRI, PFAS, wastewater, and cyber rows remain separate lanes until joined to current user-level water and sanitation proof.
- **No tap safety by system data.** A public-water-system row does not prove premise plumbing, lead exposure, pressure, language notice, or affected-area boundaries.
- **No contamination repair by rule publication.** Lead and PFAS rules establish obligations and clocks; they do not prove replacement, treatment, filters, or interim health support.
- **No emergency by plan certification.** AWIA certification is a planning requirement; actual incidents require alternate water, mutual aid, staffing, chemicals, power, and communications.
- **No cyber resilience by alert.** A warning or checklist must be joined to utility-specific remote-access, OT segmentation, manual operation, and recovery proof.
- **No sanitation by drinking-water dashboard.** Wastewater, septic, stormwater, flood, overflow, and cleanup records must be joined to drinking-water and public-health records.

## Source-to-duty map

| Source surface | What it proves | What it cannot prove alone |
|---|---|---|
| EPA SDWIS / ECHO | Public-water-system identity, violations, enforcement, and data-download context. | Current tap safety, premise plumbing, user notice, alternate water, or repair receipt. |
| Consumer Confidence Reports | Annual public drinking-water quality notice and contaminant summary. | Current incident status, language/access reach, or household/facility receipt. |
| Lead and Copper Rule Improvements | National lead-pipe inventory/replacement/testing/communication obligations. | Completed replacement, filters, flushing, school/child-care tap safety, or interim exposure control. |
| PFAS drinking-water rule surface | PFAS standard, implementation posture, proposed changes, and compliance clocks. | Treatment installed, waste handled, bottled-water burden resolved, or exposure reduced. |
| DWINSA and CWNS | Drinking-water and wastewater/stormwater infrastructure need estimates. | Which household or facility currently receives safe water, sanitation, affordability, or repair. |
| CDC advisory guidance | Boil, do-not-drink, and do-not-use advisory categories and user behavior guidance. | Whether local authorities reached every affected user and supplied alternate water. |
| AWIA RRA/ERP | Risk/resilience and emergency-response planning obligations for covered systems. | Actual readiness, alternate supply, backup power, chemical supply, mutual aid, or cyber recovery. |
| EPA/CISA water cyber materials | Sector cyber-risk posture and response resources. | Utility-specific OT security, manual operation, vendor access control, or incident recovery. |

## Applied continuity ladder

1. **Identify the water lane.** Is the matter drinking water, wastewater, stormwater, septic, private well, premise plumbing, shutoff/affordability, cyber/OT, or emergency supply?
2. **Bind people and place.** Join household/facility, service address, PWSID, private well/septic edge, school/clinic/care/custody/shelter status, and vulnerable users.
3. **Separate source surfaces.** Mark whether evidence is SDWIS/ECHO, CCR, lab result, public notice, regulation, funding estimate, AWIA certification, cyber advisory, or local incident record.
4. **Prove current operating state.** Join source, treatment, pressure, storage, distribution, chemicals, staff, backup power, and wastewater/sewer/septic status.
5. **Prove contaminant lane.** Join lead, PFAS, microbial, chemical, radioactive, corrosion, and premise-plumbing evidence with testing dates and action levels/MCLs.
6. **Prove notice.** Join affected-area map, notice type, language, accessible format, school/clinic/care/custody dissemination, and user receipt before treating notice as effective.
7. **Prove alternate service.** Join bottled water, tankers, filters, flushing, formula/dialysis/medical use, hygiene/sanitation, transport, and reimbursement.
8. **Prove resilience and cyber recovery.** Join RRA/ERP, mutual aid, backup power, chemical supply, OT/cyber controls, vendor access, manual mode, and exercise/incident evidence.
9. **Prove repair and affordability.** Join capital/funding project, replacement/treatment timeline, rate and arrears impacts, shutoff protections, complaint routes, and repair receipts.
10. **Close the loop.** Publish after-action findings, source-currentness boundaries, equity denominators, and residual risk clocks.

## Applied examples

### SDWIS and ECHO

Use SDWIS and ECHO to identify systems, violations, enforcement, and data-currentness. Then open local operating records, lab reports, advisories, pressure logs, maps, and customer/facility records. A federal row is a denominator, not tap safety.

### Consumer Confidence Reports

Use CCRs as annual transparency surfaces. Then check whether the incident happened after the report, whether a vulnerable population or language-access warning was usable, and whether affected households or facilities actually received the notice.

### Lead service lines

Use LCRI, inventories, and funding sources to establish duties and clocks. Then demand replacement status, filter distribution, flushing instructions, child-care/school taps, disturbance risk, and post-replacement sampling. Inventory is not exposure control.

### PFAS

Use the PFAS NPDWR and 2026 proposed implementation changes as source-currentness flags. Then check monitoring, treatment procurement, disposal, vulnerable-population notice, interim water, and compliance-date effects. A future compliance clock cannot be a present-health guarantee.

### Drinking-water and wastewater infrastructure needs

Use DWINSA and CWNS for funding scale and prioritization. Do not use a national capital need estimate to infer that an affected small system, tribal community, rural household, or city facility has received repair.

### Boil / do-not-drink / do-not-use advisories

Use CDC advisory categories to separate microbial boil risk from chemical/toxin and contact hazards. Then test notification, bottled water, formula, dialysis, medication, school meals, elder-care, shelter, and sanitation logistics.

### AWIA and cyber

Use AWIA and EPA/CISA cyber materials to demand risk/resilience, ERP, remote-access, authentication, OT segmentation, backup power, mutual aid, and manual-mode evidence. Certification and checklists are not proof of response capacity.

## Repair surfaces

- PWSID / premise / private well / septic map;
- source, treatment, distribution, pressure, storage, staff, chemical, backup-power, and operating state file;
- SDWIS/ECHO/CCR/lab/public-notice source-currentness map;

- PFAS monitoring, treatment, disposal, vulnerable-population notice, compliance-clock, and interim-water file;
- boil/do-not-drink/do-not-use notice, language/access, affected area, user receipt, and alternate-water logistics;
- wastewater, septic, stormwater, overflow, flood/backflow, cleanup, and public-health file;
- AWIA RRA/ERP, emergency water, mutual aid, backup power, chemicals, cyber/OT, vendor access, manual-mode, and exercise file;
- affordability, shutoff, arrears, rate, reimbursement, complaint, and after-action repair docket.

## Anti-theater tests

- Do not cite SDWIS/ECHO as tap safety without local current operating, sampling, advisory, and affected-user records.
- Do not cite a CCR as current incident notice without language/access and receipt evidence.
- Do not cite a lead-service-line inventory as lead exposure reduction without replacement, filter, flushing, and sampling proof.
- Do not cite a PFAS rule or compliance extension as treatment without monitoring, treatment, interim water, and notice proof.
- Do not cite a boil-water advisory as protection without alternate water and high-risk user logistics.
- Do not cite AWIA certification as resilience without exercise, backup, mutual aid, manual-mode, and incident proof.
- Do not cite cyber guidance as utility security without asset, access, segmentation, recovery, and vendor proof.
- Do not cite drinking-water data as sanitation continuity without wastewater/septic/stormwater evidence.

## Source posture

This packet uses U.S. official public-health, drinking-water, wastewater, resilience, and cyber source surfaces current enough for a rev0754 continuity packet. It must not substitute for live utility operating logs, lab reports, state primacy records, local advisories, private well/septic records, school/clinic/care/custody facility records, rate/shutoff files, or user-specific repair receipts.
