# 860 — Cross-boundary authority manual: dispatcher fields, defeat tests, claim ledgers, and no chain by recitation

## One-line thesis

The `812`–`824` cross-boundary chain should now be read through one manual: start with the `848` dispatcher, choose the minimum honest form under `812`, fill only the live legitimacy, fiscal, administrative, oversight, participation, information, membership, amendment, dispute, asset, contract, and enforcement fields that the case actually triggers, run the `856` waist-capture test and `861` defeat tests, and publish claim-level proof under `857` instead of citing the whole chain as decorative authority.

## Why this matters

The archive has enough cross-boundary doctrine. The next failure risk is not absence of rules; it is **chain recitation**. A case packet can look rigorous while doing nothing more than name `812` through `824` in order. That is not a form holding. It is an index walk.

The earlier notes solved separate problems: `848` stopped spillover drama from becoming immediate institutional thickening; `812` supplied the form ladder; `813` through `817` supplied the recurring operating fields; `818` and `819` supplied information and membership fields; `820` through `824` supplied heavy-power annexes; `856` named waist capture; and `857` introduced claim ledgers. The missing object was a single manual that tells the archive how to use those notes together without flattening them.

This manual is deliberately procedural. It does not delete the chain notes. It tells the reader when each one is live, when it is only reserved, when it is dangerous to skip, and when invoking it would be theater.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Use one routing page before any authority design

A cross-boundary authority file should open with this routing page.

| Step | Question | Archive route | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| trigger | what boundary mismatch or shared system activates review? | `848` | trigger proof or downshift |
| minimum form | what is the thinnest form that can honestly carry the work? | `812` | thin connector, shared-service backbone, bounded authority, or elected tier |
| live fields | which chain fields are actually triggered? | `813`–`824` | live / reserved / not triggered map |
| capture | what hidden waist may become the real authority? | `856` | waist-capture register |
| defeat | why not a lower, thinner, temporary, or no-authority option? | `861` | defeat and handback record |
| proof | which claims are source-backed and which remain hypotheses? | `857` | claim-ledger entries |
| publication | what must affected parties and reviewers see? | `843`, `857` | public case packet |

The case should not move to design until the trigger and lower-form questions have been answered.

### 2. Keep the four form families stable

The archive should continue to use four baseline families.

| Family | Normal use | Minimum proof | First danger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| thin connector | coordination, diagnosis, alignment, shared learning | lower authorities can still decide and operate | hidden binding allocation |
| shared-service backbone | pooled staff, systems, procurement, case processing, specialist capacity | principals retain political title and review | host office becomes sovereign by workflow |
| bounded functional authority | one continuous cross-boundary system or asset class | continuous operation, finance, records, and oversight need one chartered body | special-purpose drift into general government |
| general-purpose elected tier | recurring multi-sector distributive, coercive, borrowing, or land-use choices | thin forms would hide political trade-offs | decorative democracy or overbuilt tier |

Hybrid forms are allowed only after the file explains why none of the four plain forms is honest enough.

### 3. Declare live, reserved, and dead fields

A manual-compliant packet should classify each chain field.

| Chain field | Live when | Reserved when | Dead when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `813` legitimacy | the body speaks, allocates, binds, appoints, or prioritizes | advisory body with possible future hardening | ordinary inter-staff contact only |
| `814` fiscal | dues, levies, borrowing, grants, pooled reserves, rate effects, or fiscal exposure exist | administrative cost is small but may grow | no material money channel exists |
| `815` administration | staff, secretariat, operator, records home, delegated workflow, or host role exists | temporary support office exists | no shared operation exists |
| `816` oversight | audit, scrutiny, ombuds, review, supervision, or public reporting is needed | later review may be needed | no public power is exercised |
| `817` participation | users, residents, regulated parties, members, or affected territories need voice or redress | technical pilots affect few parties | no external affected party exists |
| `818` information | shared facts, databases, dashboards, indicators, standards, or publication calendars matter | informal data exchange may become formal | no shared evidence layer exists |
| `819` boundary / membership | territory, accession, exit, eligible parties, service area, or treaty membership matters | membership may change later | one body only acts within its own ordinary jurisdiction |
| `820` decision / amendment | rule changes, reapproval, qualified votes, vetoes, or renewal clocks matter | future amendment is foreseeable | no shared rule can be amended |
| `821` disputes | parties can deadlock, contest interpretation, or hold service hostage | disputes are plausible but minor | no shared obligation exists |
| `822` assets / liabilities | infrastructure, debt, reserves, risk, ownership, or handback exists | project assets may later appear | no material asset or liability exists |
| `823` contracts | concession, outsourcing, procurement framework, data-processing deal, or private operator matters | support contracts may grow | no contract shapes public power |
| `824` regulation / enforcement | inspection, licensing, detention, recommendation, sanction, permit, or compliance path exists | rule advice may harden | no regulatory effect exists |

A case that marks everything live is suspicious unless it is a genuinely thick body. A case that marks nothing live is probably not a cross-boundary authority case.

### 4. Require a form holding, not an institutional description

A compliant packet should state one public holding in this shape:

```text
The honest form is [form family] because [trigger proof].
It may carry [named powers / functions].
It must not carry [forbidden drift].
Its live fields are [notes].
Its reserved fields are [notes].
It must be reopened when [events].
```

Institutional description is not enough. The packet must say what authority the form may carry, what authority it may not carry, and what would make the holding obsolete.

### 5. Add a public-power inventory before design praise

Each packet should inventory who controls the practical levers.

| Lever | Required question |
| --- | --- |
| entry | who can join, qualify, receive service, or be inspected? |
| classification | who labels risk, eligibility, urgency, alert status, or compliance? |
| data correction | who can correct the shared fact layer? |
| clock | who controls deadlines, emergency status, assessment timing, and review windows? |
| money | who sets dues, rates, grants, fiscal conditions, reserves, and debt exposure? |
| operation | who runs staff, systems, vendors, platforms, and incidents? |
| sanction | who can detain, deny, suspend, recommend, blacklist, terminate, or escalate? |
| appeal | who hears the challenge, and against what record? |
| exit | who can leave, dissolve, migrate, or hand back? |

This inventory is the bridge between the old form chain and the new waist-capture doctrine.

### 6. Treat fiscal, asset, contract, and enforcement fields as heavy-power annexes

`814`, `822`, `823`, and `824` need special suspicion because they can change lives while looking technical.

| Heavy field | Common theater | Manual rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| fiscal | dues, grants, rates, or fiscal conditions treated as mere implementation | expose incidence, reserves, debt, equalization, and who can change the money route |
| assets | infrastructure or data treated as background | map ownership, liabilities, maintenance, depreciation, disaster loss, and handback |
| contracts | private partner treated as vendor only | map change control, termination, records, audit, exit, and public-law duties |
| enforcement | inspection or recommendation treated as non-coercive | map legal owner, discretion, sanction, appeal, correction, and downstream hardening |

A thin body with one heavy annex may need only that annex, not a new general-purpose tier. A body with several heavy annexes may no longer be thin.

### 7. Link every strong holding to claim-level proof

Every manual-compliant case should preserve at least three claim-ledger entries:

| Claim type | Example |
| --- | --- |
| form holding | “This case is a bounded functional authority, not a connector.” |
| live-field holding | “Fiscal risk is live because rate, debt, reserve, or grant exposure exists.” |
| capture or defeat holding | “The database / standard / procurement rail does not become the real authority because correction, appeal, exit, and audit routes exist.” |

Do not make the claim ledger a dumping ground. It should carry only the holdings that a future reader would otherwise misquote.

### 8. Use case packets as consolidation evidence

The manual should evolve by applied recurrence, not by elegance. If a field recurs across many different case forms, it belongs in the manual core. If a field appears only in heavy asset, contract, regulatory, treaty, digital, or database cases, it belongs in an annex. If a field is never live after many cases, it should move toward retirement or narrow trigger status.

The case matrix and consolidation audit should therefore be read as evidence for future merging, not as a mandate to delete the chain notes.

## Failure modes

- **chain recitation**: naming every note from `812` to `824` without deciding which fields are live.
- **manual inflation**: turning this manual into a new universal checklist that must be completed even for thin cases.
- **form laundering**: using “connector,” “backbone,” or “authority” as labels rather than as proof-bearing form holdings.
- **annex burial**: hiding fiscal, asset, contract, or enforcement power inside generic administration.
- **waist optimism**: assuming that thin waists are safe because they are thin.
- **claim fog**: citing a whole note when the live authority is one sentence that needs its own evidence and falsifier.
- **case monoculture**: consolidating the manual only from successful cases and never from failures, refusals, sunsets, or handbacks.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Can the packet state the minimum sufficient form in one sentence?
2. Does it explain why the next-thinner form fails or remains sufficient?
3. Are all `813`–`824` fields marked live, reserved, or dead?
4. Are fiscal, asset, contract, and enforcement powers separated rather than hidden in one administrative paragraph?
5. Is there a waist-capture inventory for standards, databases, scores, alerts, portals, procurement rails, vendors, or secretariats?
6. Does the case preserve a handback, sunset, downshift, or review clock?
7. Are the strongest holdings visible in `metadata/claims.json` or a successor claim ledger?
8. Could an affected party find the record, challenge route, correction route, and responsible public owner?
9. Would the case still make sense if one admired institution were replaced by a hostile, incompetent, or captured one?

## Reconstruction instruction

Use this note as the consolidation manual for `812`–`824`, not as a deletion order. Future revisions may merge parts of `813`–`819` into the manual core only after negative controls, plural-order cases, supplier-dependent cases, soft-law hardening cases, and database-alert cases confirm that the merged text preserves the old tests. Use `861` for defeat and handback rules, `856` for waist capture, and `857` for claim-level proof.
