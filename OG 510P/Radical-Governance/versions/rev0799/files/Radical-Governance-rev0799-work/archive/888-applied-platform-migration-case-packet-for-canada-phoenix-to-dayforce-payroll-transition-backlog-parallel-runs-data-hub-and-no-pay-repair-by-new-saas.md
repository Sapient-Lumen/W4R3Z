# 888 — Applied platform-migration case packet for Canada Phoenix-to-Dayforce payroll transition: backlog, parallel runs, data hub, and no pay repair by new SaaS

## One-line thesis

Canada's Phoenix-to-Dayforce pay modernization should be classified as a high-risk payroll and HR platform migration under continuing affected-employee and public-owner duties; Dayforce feasibility and supplier selection are not pay repair until backlog residue, data migration, rule simplification, parallel runs, departmental readiness, compensation tails, and two-system operating risk are proven at cutover.

## Why this matters

Payroll is not ordinary back-office software. It is the state's recurring legal and economic obligation to its own workers. If the system is wrong, people can be underpaid, overpaid, taxed incorrectly, denied reliable retirement or benefits information, chased for debts, forced to file claims, or left in an anxiety-producing record fog.

Phoenix already proved that a replacement payroll platform can create systemic public harm when complexity, staffing, testing, business rules, data, and governance are underestimated. The current Dayforce transition is therefore not a clean modernization story. It is a live migration from a wounded system into a new enterprise HR/pay architecture while old errors, unsimplified rules, department readiness, and supplier/cloud dependencies remain active.

The honest form is not "new SaaS selected." It is a high-risk public payroll platform migration with an affected-worker remedy tail.

### 2. Legal and operational boundary

| Component | Boundary rule |
| --- | --- |
| public owner | Treasury Board / PSPC / central HR-pay governance cannot outsource payroll truth to the product vendor |
| Dayforce product | may be a feasible platform, but feasibility is not readiness and readiness is not cutover authority |
| Phoenix legacy state | old errors must be repaired, quarantined, or carried with a visible dispute status; they cannot silently become new truth |
| business rules | unsimplified pay rules and collective-agreement complexity are governance facts, not mere configuration details |
| cloud extensions | custom extensions should be treated as residual complexity and maintenance risk, not as free modernization |
| departments | department readiness is not uniform; wave gating must expose local data, HR, interface, and staff readiness |
| employees | affected workers need pay continuity, compensation, correction, appeal, repayment, tax, pension, and retirement routes |
| two-system period | parallel Phoenix/Dayforce operation is a risk-control phase only if reconciliation records and divergence rules are visible |

### 5. Platform-migration docket

This case activates all platform-migration tests:

1. **Old/new authority map** — identify which payroll and HR functions remain Phoenix, which are Dayforce, and which are external departmental or Pay Centre functions.
2. **Data / identity / entitlement mapping** — prove employee, job, pay, pension, benefits, acting, leave, tax, collective-agreement, and case records are correctly mapped.
3. **Backlog and error quarantine** — identify old Phoenix transactions that are resolved, disputed, carried, corrected, or excluded before wave cutover.
4. **Rule and process simplification** — show which business rules were simplified and which customizations / cloud extensions preserve old complexity.
5. **Interface and dependency map** — map departmental HR systems, data hub, pension, tax, banking, reporting, and vendor interfaces.
6. **Parallel-run reconciliation** — compare Phoenix and Dayforce outputs with divergence taxonomy and employee-visible correction route.
7. **Wave readiness gate** — require department-by-department readiness, staffing, training, data quality, and backlog thresholds.
8. **Cutover and rollback gate** — name the decision-maker and the failure threshold that blocks or reverses migration.
9. **Affected-worker continuity** — protect pay, tax, pension, retirement, repayment, and compensation routes during migration.
10. **Contract / supplier / exit ledger** — preserve licence, cloud, extension, data, support, warranty, and portability duties.
11. **Post-migration value proof** — measure accuracy, timeliness, cost, manual workload, service standards, employee experience, and claim reduction after live use.

### 6. Opposition and rival readings

**Rival 1: Dayforce feasibility proves the new solution is ready.** The archive rejects the inference. Feasibility is a real milestone, but official feasibility material itself distinguishes feasibility from readiness and final deployment.

**Rival 2: the government learned from Phoenix, so the replacement should proceed quickly.** The archive accepts the need to escape Phoenix. It rejects speed as a cure for inherited complexity. A shortened schedule must create thicker risk-monitoring, not thinner proof.

**Rival 3: the backlog is an old-system problem, not a migration problem.** The archive rejects this. An old-system backlog becomes a migration problem the moment records, cases, debts, or uncertain pay states are carried into the successor.

**Rival 4: customization is pragmatic because pay rules are complex.** The archive accepts pragmatic customization as sometimes necessary. It treats customization as residual complexity that must have lifecycle cost, support, change-control, and exit proof.

**Rival 5: payroll modernization is internal government administration.** The archive rejects the low-stakes label. Public servants are rights-bearing payees; a payroll platform can directly affect livelihood, tax, debt, retirement, and trust.

### 7. Capture and theater channels

- **replacement by award notice**: the new product is treated as repair before migration proof exists.
- **feasibility laundering**: a feasibility report is used as live-readiness evidence.
- **parallel-run theater**: old/new systems run together without public divergence taxonomy or correction route.
- **backlog laundering**: unresolved old pay cases are copied into a new platform as if recorded state were settled truth.
- **rule-complexity laundering**: unsimplified public pay rules are hidden inside cloud extensions.
- **two-system cost fog**: operating old and new systems together is treated as transitional background rather than a priced risk.
- **employee-remedy evaporation**: damages, tax, pension, retirement, and overpayment issues are treated as legacy claims rather than migration gates.
- **vendor-roadmap capture**: public pay duties depend on future product enhancements without public fallback.

### 9. Holding

Phoenix-to-Dayforce is a high-risk public payroll and HR platform migration, not merely a new SaaS procurement. The rule is: **no pay repair by new SaaS**. Dayforce may be the right successor, but the migration is not proven until old errors, data, rules, interfaces, departments, suppliers, users, and remedies survive a record-rich cutover.

## Anti-theater tests

This packet fails if officials or reviewers treat feasibility, supplier selection, cloud modernization, parallel-run plans, or backlog-reduction headlines as sufficient. It passes only with migration records showing payroll accuracy, backlog quarantine, employee continuity, department readiness, parallel-run reconciliation, contract/exit proof, and live value after cutover.
