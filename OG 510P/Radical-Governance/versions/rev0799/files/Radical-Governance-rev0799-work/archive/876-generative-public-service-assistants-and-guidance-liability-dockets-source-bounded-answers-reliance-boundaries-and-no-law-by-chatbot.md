# 876 — Generative public-service assistants and guidance-liability dockets: source-bounded answers, reliance boundaries, and no law by chatbot

## One-line thesis

A generative public-service assistant is a public guidance interface, not merely search and not a decision system by default; it needs a guidance-liability docket that proves source grounding, decision-boundary labels, freshness, evaluation, user reliance controls, incident correction, accessibility, safety, supplier / model-change records, and withdrawal or handoff routes before official-looking answers are trusted.

## Why this matters

The archive already has a model-decision docket in `874`, but a chatbot can alter public behavior without issuing a formal determination. A small-business owner, tenant, migrant, benefit claimant, parent, student, employer, or applicant may treat an official answer as the practical rule even when the agency says the answer is only guidance. That is a different risk from Robodebt. The harm is not an automated debt. The harm is **guidance-grade inflation**: a probabilistic summary, retrieval artifact, model completion, or generated paraphrase becomes the user's working law.

This note adds the missing middle lane between ordinary search and automated decision-making. A public generative assistant may be useful when it narrows confusion, links users to authoritative pages, refuses out-of-scope questions, shows uncertainty, preserves privacy, and collects correction signals. It becomes governance theater when it hides behind disclaimers while the public authority's brand, layout, urgency, personalization, and channel strategy invite reliance.

The rule is not anti-chatbot. The rule is anti-unowned advice. If an assistant speaks with public authority, the archive must know who owns its corpus, who tests answers, which pages are canonical, what the assistant may not say, how fast errors are corrected, what happens when guidance changes, and how a user reaches the official route.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Form boundary

| Label | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| ordinary search | too thin if the interface composes personalized answers, chooses sources, paraphrases rules, or tells a person what to do next |
| model-mediated decision | too thick unless the assistant changes eligibility, debt, benefit, sanction, licence, enforcement, or legal status |
| supplier dependency | sometimes live, but not the whole form; supplier risk does not replace public guidance ownership |
| public guidance assistant | honest default when the tool summarizes official sources, links onward, and claims no decision authority |
| advice-state interface | escalation label when users are predictably invited to rely on answers in high-stakes domains despite disclaimers |

The assistant's legal form is determined by effect, not by interface rhetoric. A chatbot that says it is experimental may still be an official guidance channel if it is embedded in a government portal, branded as trusted, and offered as the first path for service access.

### 2. Guidance-liability docket

A public generative assistant must carry at least these fields:

| Field | Required record |
| --- | --- |
| public owner | agency, team, accountable officer, service owner, and complaint route |
| answer boundary | what the assistant may summarize, what it must refuse, and what it must route to human / official channels |
| corpus | canonical pages, excluded pages, stale-page handling, personal-data filtering, update cadence, and source hierarchy |
| answer evidence | source links, retrieval trace, model / prompt version, refusal reason, and confidence / completeness class |
| user reliance | onboarding, warning, source-check prompt, high-stakes escalation, and no-advice boundary |
| evaluation | human expert review, automated metrics, red-team / jailbreak tests, ambiguity tests, multilingual and accessibility tests |
| incidents | erroneous-answer log, correction clock, public incident threshold, affected-user notification rule, and rollback path |
| supplier | model, hosting, data access, support access, model-change clock, procurement route, and exit proof |
| records | prompt, answer, source, feedback, guardrail, and admin logs with retention and review rules |
| withdrawal | beta end, replacement route, public notice, and lessons-learned receipt |

A disclaimer is only one line in this docket. It cannot substitute for the rest.

### 3. Reliance-grade ladder

| Grade | Public meaning | Controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| navigation aid | helps find pages | source links, refusal, no personal tailoring |
| general guidance summary | summarizes official pages | citation, freshness, answer completeness, error-report route |
| task triage | helps choose a service path | eligibility caveat, user-situation prompts, assisted channel, official-form handoff |
| compliance instruction | tells a regulated person what to do | legal owner, source hierarchy, authoritative fallback, correction and reliance policy |
| decision support | supports staff or user action affecting rights / obligations | invoke `874` model-decision tests and ordinary administrative-law review |
| decision | creates legal effect | chatbot form is insufficient; require statutory decision system, notice, reasons, appeal, and audit |

The higher the reliance grade, the less a generic warning can do.

### 4. Disclaimers as weak control

A warning that an assistant can be wrong is useful but weak. It fails when:

- the answer gives imperative instructions;
- the interface hides source hierarchy;
- users are sent to the chatbot because human channels are slow;
- the answer concerns deadlines, penalties, eligibility, immigration, health, tax, welfare, employment, housing, licences, fines, or enforcement;
- the user is unlikely to know which source to verify;
- the assistant is multilingual or accessible only in ways the authoritative page is not;
- the official brand makes the output look final.

The proper test is not whether a warning exists. The test is whether a reasonable user can reconstruct and verify the answer before harm.

### 5. Capture and theater channels

- **brand reliance capture**: the public seal turns generated text into practical authority.
- **source-link theater**: links exist but do not show which proposition came from which source.
- **freshness theater**: old or superseded pages remain in the corpus.
- **refusal theater**: the assistant refuses easy edge questions but gives overconfident answers in harder high-stakes cases.
- **evaluation theater**: accuracy percentages hide topic class, harm class, completeness, and subgroup performance.
- **feedback theater**: thumbs-up / thumbs-down records exist but are not tied to correction clocks.
- **human-route theater**: a page says contact the agency, but the human route is slow, unavailable, or not designed to correct model outputs.
- **supplier theater**: the agency says it owns the service while model, retrieval, hosting, and admin logs are opaque.
- **beta theater**: experimental labels are kept while the tool is publicly relied on.

### 7. Defeat rule

A public generative assistant should be downshifted, suspended, narrowed, or withdrawn if the agency cannot produce:

1. a public owner;
2. a canonical corpus and freshness record;
3. answer/source traceability;
4. harm-class evaluation;
5. incident and correction clocks;
6. accessibility and language robustness testing;
7. reliance boundary by domain;
8. supplier / model-change records;
9. human handoff for high-stakes tasks;
10. beta-end or public-continuity receipts.

### 8. Research watchlist

The doctrine should be revisited as public-sector assistants move from navigation to transaction: form completion, benefit pre-checks, appointment scheduling, case-status explanations, compliance reminders, immigration / tax / welfare summaries, and staff-facing drafting. The moment the assistant selects a form, pre-fills a field, changes triage, nudges eligibility expectations, or shapes staff advice, `874` may need to activate.

## Anti-theater tests

The assistant fails this note if any of the following become true: the public brand invites reliance while the agency says the answer is not owned; source links do not show which proposition they support; beta language continues after public dependence is predictable; feedback is collected without correction clocks; accuracy is reported without high-stakes topic classes; supplier or model changes are hidden from the public owner; or a user cannot reach an authoritative human, form, review, or complaint route before harm.
