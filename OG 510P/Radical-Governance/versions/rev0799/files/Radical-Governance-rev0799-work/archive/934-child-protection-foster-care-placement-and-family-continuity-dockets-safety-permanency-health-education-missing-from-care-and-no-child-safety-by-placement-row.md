# 934 — Child protection, foster care, placement, and family-continuity dockets: safety, permanency, health, education, missing from care, and no child safety by placement row

## One-line thesis

Child welfare is not proven by a hotline screen, investigation disposition, placement row, foster-care census, court order, prevention-plan approval, psychotropic-medication claim, or permanency target. A usable packet must join safety, family preservation, legal authority, placement suitability, kin/sibling/tribal continuity, health, education, missing-from-care response, youth voice, and permanency repair before claiming protection.

## Why this matters

The archive already treated public benefits, housing, cyber outages, elections, staff copilots, representative authority, and court-facing dockets as continuity chains. Child protection needed the same treatment because the harms are acute, the records are fragmented, and the official success surfaces are especially tempting. A placement row can be true while the child is in the wrong setting, separated from siblings, missing from care, medically unmonitored, out of school, unheard by counsel, or drifting toward another failed placement.

The governing repair phrase is **no child safety by placement row**.

## Pattern pack

- **No hotline safety by screen result.** Screening starts the state duty; it does not prove risk assessment, safety planning, investigation quality, family notice, or service delivery.
- **No family preservation by prevention plan.** Title IV-E prevention authority, service catalog, and five-year plan status must be joined to actual service availability, timeliness, family consent, candidacy, and court/removal clock.
- **No legal custody by docket caption.** The packet must distinguish emergency removal, protective order, voluntary placement, court custody, parental rights, counsel, notice, tribe, kin, and appeal/reunification route.
- **No placement safety by bed.** A bed, group-home slot, out-of-state setting, relative home, or congregate-care exemption is not suitability unless it is joined to sibling, kin, school, health, behavioral, disability, trafficking, and permanency needs.
- **No health continuity by Medicaid row.** Medicaid or insurance status is not proof of trauma screening, medication monitoring, consent, prior authorizations, records transfer, or continuity of therapy.
- **No recovery by child found.** Missing-from-care response must include report, search, law-enforcement and NCMEC/NCIC route where required, family/caregiver/court notice, return interview, trafficking screen, and prevention plan.
- **No permanency by target date.** Reunification, guardianship, adoption, APPLA, transition, or aging-out plans are only real if services, visitation, education, housing, documents, and youth voice are visible.

## Continuity docket

Use ten ledgers before scoring child safety.

| Ledger | What it must preserve | Bad shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Intake and investigation | Reporter, allegation, screen result, investigation clock, safety assessment, notice, immediate protection, and evidence. | Treating a hotline disposition as child safety. |
| Family-preservation route | Prevention services, in-home supports, kinship help, disability/language access, economic stabilizers, and reasonable-efforts record. | Treating plan approval as delivered support. |
| Legal authority and representation | Removal authority, court order, parent/child counsel, GAL/CASA, tribal notice, appeal, and family participation. | Treating custody status as due process. |
| Placement suitability | Kin, sibling, ICWA, least-restrictive setting, special needs, school, placement stability, out-of-state and congregate risk. | Treating an open bed as appropriate placement. |
| Health and medication | Medicaid/insurance, trauma screening, consent, psychotropic treatment planning, medication monitoring, dental, disability, and records transfer. | Treating medication fill or eligibility row as care. |
| Education and daily life | School enrollment, transportation, IEP/504, credits, activities, internet/phone, family time, and normalcy. | Treating school district assignment as school continuity. |
| Missing-from-care response | Report, search, NCMEC/NCIC/law-enforcement path, caregiver/court/attorney notice, return interview, trafficking screen, and prevention plan. | Treating return to placement as resolution. |
| Workforce and provider capacity | Caseload, visit cadence, supervisor review, foster-home availability, congregate capacity, specialized care, and provider incident history. | Treating contracted capacity as actual capacity. |
| Permanency and transition | Reunification, guardianship, adoption, APPLA, aging-out, documents, housing, benefits, education, and youth voice. | Treating permanency goal as permanency. |
| Public oversight | AFCARS/NCANDS/CFSR/NYTD/state dashboards, denominators, definitions, timeliness, and exception tails. | Treating aggregate compliance as individual safety. |

## Minimum joined record

A serious child-welfare packet should contain at least:

1. **Child and family identity map.** Child, siblings, parents, legal guardians, tribe, kin, school, health provider, language, disability, placement, and authorized representative.
2. **Safety and investigation clock.** Referral source, allegation, screen decision, response priority, home visit, safety assessment, substantiation or disposition, appeal route, and immediate safety plan.
3. **Prevention and reasonable-efforts evidence.** Concrete services, timing, family barriers, poverty-related needs, domestic violence/safety constraints, substance use, mental health, disability, kinship supports, and service gaps.
4. **Removal and court authority.** Emergency basis, court order, counsel, notice, evidence, placement approval, tribe/ICWA where relevant, review hearings, and family-time orders.
5. **Placement match.** Kin search, sibling placement, least-restrictive setting, special needs, out-of-state/congregate approval, incident history, school continuity, transportation, and stability plan.
6. **Health and education passport.** Insurance, records, medications, trauma assessment, psychotropic consent and monitoring, therapy, dental, immunization, IEP/504, credits, and school transportation.
7. **Missing-from-care protocol.** Trigger, report, law-enforcement and NCMEC/NCIC action, caregiver/court/attorney notice, search plan, return interview, trafficking screen, and updated prevention plan.
8. **Child and family voice.** Age-appropriate notice, youth participation, parent participation, counsel/GAL/CASA, grievance, interpreter/accommodation, and retaliation-safe route.
9. **Permanency and transition tail.** Family-time quality, reunification services, guardianship/adoption route, independent living, IDs, benefits, housing, education/work, and post-exit support.
10. **Oversight denominator.** Which aggregate system reports the event, what it excludes, how often it refreshes, and how a case-level exception is corrected.

## Upgrade triggers

Route the matter to the full child-welfare continuity docket when any of these appear:

- a child is removed, placed, moved, held in congregate care, placed out of state, or separated from siblings;
- a child is missing from care, returns from absence, or may have experienced trafficking, exploitation, injury, untreated illness, school loss, or criminal justice contact;
- psychotropic medication, restraint, seclusion, hospitalization, disability support, or specialized behavioral placement is present;
- a prevention plan, family-preservation claim, kinship route, or reasonable-efforts finding is used to avoid or justify removal;
- federal or state aggregate reporting is cited as proof that safety, permanency, or placement quality exists;
- the child is nearing permanency, aging-out, trial reunification, guardianship, adoption, or case closure.

## Downshift conditions

A lighter informational summary is acceptable only when no child-specific safety, placement, court, health, school, missing-from-care, medication, family-time, permanency, or transition consequence is live. If any child can lose family, school, liberty, health continuity, or safety because a status row is wrong or incomplete, stay at the full continuity level.

## Failure modes

- **No child safety by placement row:** placement existence is treated as protection.
- **No family preservation by program eligibility:** service authority is treated as delivered help.
- **No kinship by search note:** kin identification is treated as safe kin placement.
- **No sibling continuity by case plan:** sibling relationship is acknowledged but not operationally preserved.
- **No tribal compliance by checkbox:** ICWA notice and placement preference are asserted without record-level proof.
- **No health care by Medicaid:** coverage is treated as trauma care, medication oversight, and treatment continuity.
- **No missing-child recovery by return date:** return is treated as closure without experience assessment, trafficking screen, or prevention plan.
- **No youth voice by hearing date:** court appearance is treated as meaningful participation.
- **No permanency by goal:** plan label is treated as durable family, housing, education, and benefits continuity.

## Audit questions

- Which event first made the child visible to the state, and which duty did that event trigger?
- Which person or office owns the next deadline: investigator, caseworker, supervisor, court, provider, school, health plan, tribe, law enforcement, or counsel?
- What record proves the child is physically safe today, not merely assigned to a placement?
- What services were promised to avoid removal, support reunification, or stabilize kinship, and did the family receive them in time?
- What happened to siblings, school, health records, medications, disability accommodations, family time, and culture after placement?
- If the child went missing, who reported, searched, notified, screened, interviewed, and changed the plan afterward?
- Which aggregate data surface is being asked to prove more than its denominator can prove?

## Source posture

This note uses current federal child welfare reporting and oversight anchors, including ACF Child Maltreatment, AFCARS, Title IV-E Prevention, GAO congregate-care and residential-facility oversight reports, HHS OIG missing-from-care and psychotropic-medication oversight, Child Welfare Information Gateway missing-from-care summaries, eCFR ICWA placement rules, and a Connecticut DCF missing-from-care performance audit. These sources are necessary but not sufficient: local case files, court dockets, provider records, school records, health records, and child/family testimony remain required for live use.
