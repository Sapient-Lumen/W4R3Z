# 979 — Applied emergency service-home case packet for NG911, PSAPs, ESInet, GIS, CAD, outage handoff, and no rescue by platform waist

## One-line thesis

An emergency communications service home is not proved by an NG911 platform, statewide bridge contract, PSAP map, FCC reliability rule, GIS standard, CAD interface, outage notice, or alerting channel; it exists only when the caller-facing promise, carrier/originating lane, 911 authority, ESInet/core-services operator, PSAP/CAD/field-response chain, outage fallback, complaint/QA route, and recovery duty are visible together.

## Why this matters

Rev0780 reduced the service-home residue around note `615` by adding a social-service packet for LAHSA. The remaining residue was still serious: emergency communications had continuity dockets at notes `946` and `947`, but no service-home application. That left a risk that the archive could discuss NG911, PSAPs, 988, IPAWS, and outage reporting as emergency-response continuity surfaces while still failing the service-home question: who actually owns the public promise when a caller, dispatcher, carrier, PSAP, GIS layer, CAD bridge, vendor, or state network fails?

This note applies the service-home successor route to NG911 and emergency communications. It does not replace notes `946` or `947`. Those notes ask whether emergency-response evidence proves rescue. This note asks whether the emergency communications stack has an honest service home before anyone treats a platform migration, statewide provider, or PSAP map as public service government.

The governing repair phrase is **no rescue by platform waist**.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Start with the caller-facing promise, not the platform

The service promise is simple but hard to prove: a person in emergency danger can request help; the request can be received, located, routed, answered, dispatched, transferred, backed up during failure, and reviewed afterward. NG911 technology can improve parts of that promise. It does not itself own the promise.

The packet must state:

- who can request help;
- what channel is in scope: voice, text, RTT, VoIP, wireless, telematics, 988, or alerting;
- who receives the request first;
- how location is validated and routed;
- what PSAP or crisis/alerting lane acts;
- how CAD, radio, EMS/fire/police, or mobile crisis receives the event;
- who owns fallback if the network, GIS, CAD, vendor, or PSAP fails; and
- where complaints, QA, death/near-miss review, and outage learning go.

If the packet cannot name the fallback owner, it cannot call the arrangement an emergency service home.

### 2. Split carrier, 911 authority, core-services operator, PSAP, CAD, and responder roles

NG911 makes the operator chain more powerful and more fragile because routing, location, policy, and media handling move through shared digital services. A service-home packet must therefore split the roles before it credits or blames anyone.

| Role | Evidence needed | Thin or dishonest shortcut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Caller-facing promise owner | statute, state 911 authority, local emergency authority, or public service obligation | public safety by brand |
| Originating service provider | carrier/originating network, LIS/LNG or functional equivalent, outage notification route | rescue by phone subscription |
| 911 authority / ESInet owner | designated delivery point, ESInet, governance, funding, migration, procurement, and fallback duties | service home by statewide platform |
| NGCS / GIS / routing operator | LVF/GIS/ECRF/ESRP/PRF or equivalent, routing rules, vendor accountability, reliability evidence | continuity by core-services acronym |
| PSAP / call center | answer, transfer, overflow, abandoned call, text/RTT, callback, QA, backup PSAP, staffing | rescue by PSAP row |
| CAD / field response | event type, dispatch priority, unit availability, radio/MDT, arrival/contact | rescue by dispatch row |
| Regulator / auditor / monitor | FCC outage/reliability, state oversight, advisory board, audit, public reporting | reliability by certification |
| Complaint / learning owner | incident complaint, QA, medical review, near-miss, after-action, outage repair | remedy by ticket closure |

### 3. Treat the NG911 demarcation point as a constitutional seam

The demarcation between originating service providers and 911 authorities is not only a technical handoff. It is a service-home seam. A call can fail before the handoff, at the handoff, inside the ESInet/core-services route, at the PSAP/CAD layer, or after dispatch. The packet must name which public or regulated actor owns each seam.

A service home fails when every actor can say the call belonged to someone else at the moment of failure.

### 4. Treat GIS and location as service constitution, not map decoration

In NG911, location validation, GIS layers, emergency service boundaries, and routing policy can determine where the call goes and which responders see the scene. That makes GIS a public-service constitution for emergencies.

The packet should test:

- civic address validation;
- geospatial boundary maintenance;
- emergency service zone ownership;
- PSAP boundary updates;
- location confidence and uncertainty;
- GIS/CAD synchronization;
- local correction route; and
- degraded-mode routing when GIS is stale or unavailable.

A map can route a life. It must have a maintenance and correction owner.

### 5. Make outage notice and fallback part of the service home

Outage reporting is not a compliance side lane. In emergency communications it is part of service continuity. The packet should join provider outage detection, PSAP notice, public notice where appropriate, backup routing, manual logs, alternate numbers, radio/CAD fallback, restoration, and post-incident review.

The service home is incomplete if it can define outage notification but cannot show who keeps the caller-facing promise alive during the outage.

### 6. Do not let procurement or bridge contracts substitute for service repair

A bridge contract, statewide provider, vendor replacement, project restart, tariff, or implementation plan can be necessary. It is not repair unless it comes with public transition receipts: what moves, what remains, which PSAPs are protected, which provider support lanes change, how old and new systems run in parallel, and how failed project knowledge is preserved.

The California NG911 material is a useful warning: official bridge-contract and statewide-deployment language can coexist with public reporting about cost, confidence, and project-management breakdowns. A service-home packet should use both kinds of evidence without treating either as full proof.

### 7. Keep alerting and crisis lanes adjacent but not collapsed

IPAWS, WEA, EAS, 988, EMS, and NG911 may interact during emergencies, but they are not the same service home. The packet should treat each as a lane with its own entry, duty, operator chain, data, escalation, and remedy path. A warning sent is not a rescue; a 988 answer is not mobile crisis; an EMS dataset is not a patient handoff.

### 8. Require after-action and learning loops before calling the home complete

Emergency communications are high-consequence enough that complaints and outage reports are not enough. A credible service home needs incident review, PSAP QA, clinical/dispatch review where relevant, public transparency for systemic failures, vendor correction, GIS correction, training updates, and a way to reopen the service map after a near miss.

## Applied emergency service-home ladder

1. **Classify the request.** Voice 911, text-to-911, RTT, VoIP, wireless, telematics, 988, IPAWS/WEA/EAS, or local alerting.
2. **Bind the person and event.** Caller, victim, patient, location uncertainty, language, disability, facility/custody/housing status, callback, and immediate risk.
3. **Map the operator chain.** OSP, NG911 delivery point, ESInet, NGCS routing/location functions, PSAP, CAD, responder, crisis center, or alert originator.
4. **Prove routing and answer.** Delivery point, queue, abandoned/overflow, transfer, backup PSAP, and callback evidence.
5. **Prove location usability.** GIS, civic address, geospatial boundary, z-axis if relevant, CAD display, and responder-visible location.
6. **Prove action.** Dispatch, unit assignment, alert send, crisis safety plan, mobile response, EMS/fire/police arrival, or justified cancellation.
7. **Prove degraded-mode continuity.** Outage, cyber, power, vendor, CAD/radio, PSAP staffing, manual fallback, public alternate route, and restoration.
8. **Prove learning.** Complaint, QA, after-action review, vendor/GIS correction, public disclosure, and source-health refresh.

## Applied examples

### National NG911 and profile surfaces

Use National 911 Program NG911 and profile materials to understand nationwide transition posture and self-reported state progress. Do not treat those surfaces as proof that a local call reached the correct PSAP, was located, or survived an outage.

### FCC NG911 reliability and outage surfaces

Use FCC reliability and outage materials to define regulated seams: provider obligations, 911 authority access to reliability/interoperability information, NG911 delivery points, routing/location components, and outage reporting. Then require incident-level evidence before calling a failure harmless or repaired.

### California statewide NG911 transition

Use California’s NG911 page as an official state project and bridge-contract surface. Use current public-records reporting as a warning signal about cost, design, confidence, and project governance. Neither source alone proves whether a particular Californian can reach help, or whether the statewide transition has an honest service home.

### Alerting and crisis lanes

Use IPAWS and 988 surfaces only as adjacent emergency lanes. A true emergency service home should show how a warning or crisis contact is routed, escalated, translated, made accessible, backed up, and reviewed, not merely that a national channel exists.

## Repair surfaces

- caller / person / patient / alert-recipient record;
- OSP, LIS/LNG, delivery-point, ESInet, NGCS, PSAP, CAD, and responder chain;
- GIS, location-validation, emergency-service-zone, boundary, and correction file;
- outage detection, PSAP notice, public notice, reroute, backup-center, manual-log, and restoration file;
- bridge-contract, procurement, vendor, tariff, transition, and old/new parallel-run receipt;
- staffing, training, QA, abandoned-call, transfer, and complaint file;
- alerting, crisis, EMS/fire/police, hospital, mobile-response, and after-action file;
- public reporting and source-health refresh file.

## Anti-theater tests

- Do not cite NG911 platform migration as rescue without call, route, location, answer, dispatch, and outcome evidence.
- Do not cite a statewide provider or bridge contract as service repair without PSAP transition, support, fallback, and public receipt evidence.
- Do not cite FCC reliability rules as continuity without incident-level outage detection, notice, reroute, restoration, and learning evidence.
- Do not cite GIS capability as scene proof without a responder-visible location and maintenance/correction route.
- Do not cite a PSAP map as a service home without complaint, overflow, backup, staffing, CAD, and field-response handoff evidence.
- Do not cite a public-records investigation as the whole truth; use it to trigger official records, audit, and project-governance review.
- Do not cite a dispatch row as rescue; use note `947` to join dispatch to care, arrival, or protective action.

## Source posture

This packet uses official National 911 Program, FCC, FEMA/IPAWS, and California NG911 materials as current source routes, plus current investigative reporting as a risk signal. The sources prove system architecture, transition posture, reliability-rule surfaces, official bridge-contract/statewide-deployment claims, and public-records reporting. They do not prove lived rescue, outage harmlessness, PSAP readiness, statewide project repair, or person-level emergency continuity.
