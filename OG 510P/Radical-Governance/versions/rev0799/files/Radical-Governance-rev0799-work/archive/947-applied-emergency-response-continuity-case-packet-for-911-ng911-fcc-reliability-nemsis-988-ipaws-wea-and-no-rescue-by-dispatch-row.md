# 947 — Applied emergency response continuity case packet for 911, NG911, FCC reliability, NEMSIS, 988, IPAWS, WEA, and no rescue by dispatch row

## One-line thesis

National 911 Program NG911/profile materials, FCC reliability/location/outage rules, NEMSIS/EMS data, SAMHSA and 988 Lifeline performance surfaces, FEMA IPAWS, FCC WEA/EAS materials, and CDC emergency-communication resources should be read as separate evidence lanes. They cannot be collapsed into proof that a specific person reached help, was located, received the right responder, got clinical or crisis care, understood a warning, or had a failed emergency repaired.

## Why this matters

This applied packet turns the emergency-response docket into an operational case surface. The sources are strong enough to show standards, system migration, reporting structure, reliability rules, EMS data scale, 988 performance, and public alerting architecture. But each surface has a narrow lane. A 911 profile can describe system posture; an FCC rule can set obligations; NEMSIS can describe EMS activations; 988 metrics can show contact performance; IPAWS/WEA can show alert infrastructure. None of those proves rescue for a caller.

The governing repair phrase is **no rescue by dispatch row**.

## Pattern pack

- **No rescue by dispatch row.** Dispatch evidence starts the inquiry; it does not close it.
- **No NG911 by self-report.** Profile/database and roadmap progress must be joined to live routing, transfer, outage, GIS, and degraded-mode evidence.
- **No PSAP reliability by certification.** FCC reliability rules and certifications must be joined to outage detection, PSAP notice, reroute, and restoration.
- **No scene by location estimate.** Location-based routing and z-axis policy must be joined to the location actually visible and usable by responders.
- **No EMS by national dataset.** NEMSIS and EMS.gov data are denominator/performance lanes, not proof of treatment, transport, or handoff for a patient.
- **No crisis care by 988 KPI.** Contacts, answer rates, average answer speed, and state reports do not prove safety planning, mobile crisis, warm transfer, or follow-up.
- **No public protection by alert authority.** IPAWS/WEA/EAS authority and sends must be joined to receipt, accessibility, translation, timing, and protective action.
- **No after-action by metric.** Dashboards and monthly reports must be joined to complaints, deaths, near-misses, QA, corrective action, and learning loops.

## Source-to-duty map

| Source surface | What it proves | What it cannot prove alone |
|---|---|---|
| National 911 NG911 and profile materials | Self-reported state progress, implementation context, and 911 planning vocabulary. | Live routing, transfer, outage fallback, GIS accuracy, or incident rescue. |
| FCC NG911 reliability and outage materials | Rules, obligations, reliability benchmarks, outage-reporting and PSAP-notice posture. | That a particular outage was detected, routed around, disclosed, or harmless. |
| FCC location accuracy and location-based routing | Regulatory treatment of wireless location, vertical location, and routing improvement. | That responders found the caller or that indoor/high-rise location was actionable. |
| EMS.gov and NEMSIS | EMS data standard, national EMS database, public-release dataset, and performance-analysis surface. | That a patient received timely care, transport, or hospital transfer-of-care. |
| SAMHSA/988 and Lifeline reports | National and state performance metrics for crisis contacts and routing. | That crisis stabilization, mobile response, 911 transfer, or follow-up occurred. |
| FEMA IPAWS and FCC WEA/EAS | Alert authority, delivery channels, multilingual/WEA policy, and warning ecosystem. | That a person received, understood, or acted on a warning. |
| CDC emergency communication/access resources | Risk-communication, disability, and access/functional-needs planning context. | That an emergency message was accessible to a specific person. |

## Applied continuity ladder

1. **Classify the emergency route.** Voice 911, text/RTT, VoIP, landline, wireless, 988, mobile crisis, EMS, fire, police, WEA/EAS, IPAWS, or local alerting.
2. **Bind the person and event.** Caller, victim, patient, crisis contact, alert recipient, language, disability, age, facility/custody/housing status, and safety constraints.
3. **Separate source surfaces.** Mark every source as standard, self-report, rule, outage notice, dataset, dashboard, monthly report, alert authority, or live incident record.
4. **Prove routing and answer.** Carrier/originating path, PSAP, queue time, abandoned/overflow, transfer, ANI/ALI, text/RTT, 988 selection, and backup route.
5. **Prove location and context.** Civic address, coordinate, z-axis, dispatchable location, GIS layer, landmark, hazards, callback, and responder-visible map.
6. **Prove dispatch and response.** CAD event type, priority, unit recommendation, assignment, radio/MDT delivery, unit availability, travel, staging, and arrival/contact.
7. **Prove care or protective action.** EMS treatment/transport/handoff, fire/police action, mobile crisis/safety planning, alert receipt, evacuation/shelter, or justified cancellation.
8. **Prove degraded-mode continuity.** Outage, cyber, power, carrier/vendor, CAD/radio, PSAP, or alerting failure; reroute; backup center; manual logs; and restoration.
9. **Prove remedy and learning.** Complaint, QA, medical review, death/near-miss review, PSAP notice, public disclosure, corrective action, and source-currentness refresh.
10. **Close with outcome.** Was the person reached, warned, stabilized, treated, transported, sheltered, or repaired, and what remains unknown?

## Applied examples

### 911 and NG911 profile surfaces

Use 911.gov NG911 and profile materials to understand national progress and self-reported system posture. Then require PSAP-level records, GIS, transfer, outage, routing, CAD, and unit records before treating a 911 row as rescue proof.

### FCC reliability, outages, and location

Use FCC reliability, outage, location accuracy, and location-based-routing materials to define duties. Then check provider logs, PSAP notices, reroute evidence, location delivered to CAD/MDT, responder feedback, and restoration proof.

### EMS and NEMSIS

Use EMS.gov and NEMSIS to establish standardized EMS data and national incident-dataset context. Then check local ePCR, dispatch, unit, treatment, refusal, transport, diversion, transfer-of-care, and clinical QA records.

### 988 crisis lane

Use SAMHSA performance metrics and Lifeline monthly reports for national/state contact performance. Then check local crisis-center routing, language/Deaf/HoH service, backup flowout, safety plan, mobile crisis, 911 transfer, and follow-up.

### IPAWS/WEA/EAS warning lane

Use FEMA IPAWS and FCC WEA/EAS materials for alert authority and channel structure. Then check alert text, geography, language, accessibility, send/receive evidence, cancellation, shelter/evacuation proof, and after-action learning.

## Repair surfaces

- caller/person/patient/crisis-contact/alert-recipient map;
- channel, carrier, PSAP, 988, alert-authority, and routing evidence;
- location, GIS, z-axis, callback, and responder-visible scene file;
- CAD, priority, unit assignment, radio/MDT, mutual aid, and arrival/contact record;
- EMS clinical, transport, hospital diversion, transfer-of-care, and refusal/handoff record;
- 988 local routing, safety plan, mobile crisis, 911 transfer, language/access, and follow-up file;
- IPAWS/WEA/EAS message, geography, multilingual/accessibility, send/receive, and protective-action file;
- outage, cyber, power, vendor, telecom, radio, CAD, backup-center, manual-log, and restoration file;
- QA, complaint, death/near-miss, after-action, corrective-action, and public disclosure record.

## Anti-theater tests

- Do not cite a 911 call row as rescue without routing, location, dispatch, arrival/contact, and outcome evidence.
- Do not cite NG911 profile or roadmap progress as live interoperability without PSAP/GIS/outage/fallback proof.
- Do not cite FCC reliability rules or certifications as continuity without monitoring, outage notice, reroute, and restoration evidence.
- Do not cite wireless location policy as scene location without responder-visible actionable location proof.
- Do not cite NEMSIS as care delivery without local ePCR, treatment, transport, and handoff evidence.
- Do not cite 988 answer rates as crisis stabilization without local response, transfer, safety plan, and follow-up evidence.
- Do not cite IPAWS/WEA/EAS sends as public protection without receipt, language, accessibility, and protective action evidence.
- Do not cite dashboards as repair without incident-level QA and corrective action.

## Source posture

This packet uses official 911.gov, FCC, EMS/NEMSIS, SAMHSA/988, FEMA/IPAWS, and CDC sources current enough for rev0756. Use them as national and federal evidence lanes. For live cases, require PSAP, carrier, CAD, radio, EMS, crisis-center, alert-originator, hospital, emergency-management, complaint, QA, and after-action records.
