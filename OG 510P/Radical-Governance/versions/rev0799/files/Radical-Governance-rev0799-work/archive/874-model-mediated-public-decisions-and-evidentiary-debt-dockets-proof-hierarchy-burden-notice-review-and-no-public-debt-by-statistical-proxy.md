# 874 — Model-mediated public decisions and evidentiary debt dockets: proof hierarchy, burden, notice, review, and no public debt by statistical proxy

## One-line thesis

A model, data match, risk score, rule engine, calculator, or automated workflow may identify a question for public administration, but it must not become debt, benefit loss, sanction, custody, exclusion, enforcement, or other legal effect until the public authority can show a statute-specific evidence docket, lawful burden allocation, intelligible notice, meaningful review, and a public remedy route.

## Why this matters

The archive already has many public-automation controls: system cards, appeal packets, redress routes, registers, impact dossiers, model / supplier ledgers, material-change clocks, and incident logs. It also now has supplier-dependency tests for public platforms. But those controls are still incomplete when the automated system itself becomes the evidentiary bridge between a person and a legal consequence.

The hard problem is not simply whether a public decision used AI or whether a human signed the last screen. The hard problem is whether the legal proposition was proved. A data match can show inconsistency. A risk score can show a need to look. A model can summarize, classify, or recommend. A calculator can apply a formula. None of those steps automatically proves the legal fact that a person owes money, is ineligible, breached a condition, presents a risk, lacks a right, or should be sanctioned.

This note creates an evidentiary-decision docket for model-mediated public power. The docket forces the archive to separate detection, triage, calculation, proposed decision, legal determination, notice, collection / enforcement, review, and remediation. It also prevents a familiar failure: the government treats a proxy as evidence, treats non-response as admission, treats an online path as fairness, treats a call-centre script as review, and treats later refunds as full repair.

The core rule is: **automation may accelerate inquiry, but it cannot lower the proof floor.**

## Pattern pack

### 1. Split the decision stack

A model-mediated public decision should be decomposed before anyone praises efficiency or condemns automation.

| Layer | Permissible role | Non-permissible laundering |
| --- | --- | --- |
| data acquisition | collect or receive data under a lawful authority | treating a data source as automatically accurate for another statutory purpose |
| data matching | identify discrepancy, inconsistency, eligibility question, or risk signal | treating mismatch as legal proof of debt, breach, fraud, or ineligibility |
| model / rule output | triage, estimate, classify, summarize, recommend, or calculate under stated assumptions | converting assumed facts into final facts without evidence |
| human action | investigate, verify, decide, explain, and correct | clicking through a prefilled conclusion while burden and evidence remain wrong |
| notice | tell the person what is proposed, why, what evidence exists, and how to contest it | forcing the person to disprove an opaque assumption |
| legal effect | debt, cancellation, reduction, sanction, recovery, referral, detention, exclusion, or other consequence | imposing effect before proof, review, and hardship / vulnerability routes |
| collection / enforcement | recover only lawful, reviewable, correctly calculated amounts | using collection pressure to make the model's assumption practically final |

The archive should refuse a one-word label like automated, AI-assisted, digital, or human-in-the-loop until the layers are mapped.

### 2. Use an evidence-grade ladder

Every output should receive an evidence grade before it touches rights, money, liberty, status, or services.

| Grade | Meaning | Allowed consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| signal | possible discrepancy, anomaly, or risk | inquiry only |
| lead | plausible case for human investigation | request information with assistance and no adverse inference unless law permits |
| proposed fact | evidence supports a factual proposition but may be incomplete | proposed decision notice and review opportunity |
| determined fact | lawful decision-maker has evidence sufficient under the statute | legal effect if notice and review rules are satisfied |
| appeal-ready record | decision packet reconstructs law, evidence, calculation, discretion, notice, and review | defensible collection, enforcement, publication, or downstream use |

The failure pattern is **grade inflation**: a signal is treated as a lead, a lead as a fact, a proposed fact as a determination, and the determination as collection-ready.

### 3. Burden of proof is a governance surface

A digital system can silently move the burden of proof. That shift is often more important than the algorithm.

The docket must answer:

- Which statute or rule creates the duty, entitlement, debt, sanction, or enforcement power?
- Which public body bears the initial evidentiary burden?
- What facts must be proved, by whom, to what administrative standard?
- What inference can lawfully be drawn from non-response, missing records, old payroll data, address failure, or digital non-use?
- What assistance is owed to a person who lacks records, digital access, literacy, stability, health, language, disability support, housing, or representation?
- What happens to collection or enforcement while the matter is disputed?

A burden shift should be explicit. If it is hidden inside interface design, letters, default settings, workflow queues, or collection scripts, the system has failed the docket.

### 4. Human review must be decision review, not interface review

Human involvement helps only when the human can change the outcome and has the authority, training, time, evidence, and independence to do so.

| Weak human loop | Strong human loop |
| --- | --- |
| confirms that the system ran | examines legal authority, evidence, assumptions, and hardship |
| follows a script | can depart from model output and record reasons |
| sees only the model conclusion | sees source data, confidence, missing evidence, and counter-evidence |
| acts after collection begins | acts before adverse effect or with stay-of-collection power |
| has production targets | has quality, fairness, legality, and correction duties |
| cannot reopen systemic errors | can trigger incident command, sample review, and remediation |

The phrase human-in-the-loop should be banned from the archive unless the loop is described.

### 5. Notice must disclose the proof route

A notice is not fair because it exists. It is fair only if it lets an affected person understand and contest the case against them.

Minimum notice fields:

| Field | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| legal basis | statute, rule, delegation, and decision type |
| proposed effect | amount, benefit change, sanction, referral, or other consequence |
| evidence | source data, time period, data match, calculation, assumptions, missing records, and confidence |
| proof status | signal, proposed fact, determined fact, or appeal-ready record |
| person route | how to view, correct, supplement, or challenge the evidence |
| assistance route | phone, in-person, advocate, accessible format, interpreter, disability, hardship, and vulnerability support |
| stay route | whether adverse effect or collection pauses during review |
| review path | internal review, merits review, ombuds, court, tribunal, or other route |
| systemic trigger | how the person, advocate, worker, or reviewer can flag a class error |

A notice that says a person may respond, while hiding the calculation, assumptions, or lawful burden, is not a proof route.

### 6. Evidence, money, and harm need separate ledgers

For consequential automated systems, the archive should maintain three linked ledgers.

| Ledger | What it records |
| --- | --- |
| evidence ledger | data source, calculation, model / rule version, assumption, human signoff, counter-evidence, and review outcome |
| money / effect ledger | debt raised, benefit reduced, sanction imposed, collection started, fee added, referral made, refund paid, debt zeroed, or settlement made |
| harm / repair ledger | distress, access barrier, overpayment, underpayment, advocacy cost, legal cost, apology, remediation, compensation, and systemic repair |

A refund or settlement may repair money without repairing records, trust, legal authority, mental harm, or future process.

### 7. Public algorithms require independent scrutiny

The archive should distinguish four disclosure levels.

| Level | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| public existence | the agency says automated decision-making is used |
| public decision list | the agency names which decisions or workflows use automation |
| public logic | business rules, data sources, assumptions, thresholds, model class, and human role are intelligible |
| independent scrutiny | experts, advocates, auditors, courts, ombuds, or inspectors can test legality, fairness, bias, accuracy, usability, and impact |

The appropriate level depends on risk and secrecy constraints, but a legal or similarly significant effect cannot rely only on public-existence disclosure.

### 8. Model-mediated decision verdicts

| Verdict | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| inquiry-support only | automation may identify cases for human investigation but cannot decide effect |
| bounded calculator | deterministic formula is lawful, transparent, and reviewable |
| decision-support with human determination | model output is advisory and the decision-maker owns evidence and reasons |
| automated determination with statutory frame | law expressly authorizes automation and preserves notice, review, correction, and audit |
| unlawful evidentiary proxy | output or mismatch is used as proof without lawful evidence or burden allocation |
| public-law incident | systemic automation failure requires suspension, remediation, refunds, compensation, source repair, and future guardrails |

## Failure modes

- **debt by proxy**: an average, score, mismatch, or assumption becomes legal debt.
- **non-response as confession**: silence, digital exclusion, lost mail, old records, or trauma is treated as proof.
- **human-loop theater**: staff click, script, or collect without authority to examine evidence and change the outcome.
- **notice without proof route**: affected people receive a letter or portal task but not the calculation and legal test.
- **collection before contestability**: recovery, fees, garnishment, referral, or pressure begins before review can work.
- **legal-advice fog**: advice is draft, partial, hidden, ignored, not escalated, or described too vaguely to reveal the risk.
- **tribunal-signal suppression**: adverse tribunal, court, ombuds, or frontline signals are not routed into systemic review.
- **budget-savings capture**: expected savings make legality, evidence, and service design subordinate to fiscal targets.
- **remediation narrowing**: the state refunds money but leaves burden, notice, model, record, trauma, and appeal failures under-repaired.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Can the agency identify the exact legal fact the automation is supposed to prove or support?
2. Can it distinguish signal, lead, proposed fact, determined fact, and appeal-ready record?
3. Can a person reconstruct the source data, calculation, rule, assumption, human signoff, and review path?
4. Does collection or adverse effect pause while a plausible challenge is live?
5. Do staff have authority and time to override, reopen, compensate, and escalate systemic errors?
6. Are business rules, algorithms, and decision lists disclosed enough for independent scrutiny?
7. Can tribunal, ombuds, court, complaint, and frontline signals trigger system-wide correction?
8. Are savings claims separated from legality and evidence claims?
9. Is there a class-remediation path when the same proxy contaminates many decisions?
10. Can the government prove that a human reviewed the legal evidence, not merely the interface state?
