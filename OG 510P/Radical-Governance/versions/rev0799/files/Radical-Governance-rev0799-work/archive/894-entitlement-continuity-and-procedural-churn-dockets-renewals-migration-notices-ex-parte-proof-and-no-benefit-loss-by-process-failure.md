# 894 — Entitlement continuity and procedural churn dockets: renewals, migration notices, ex parte proof, and no benefit loss by process failure

## One-line thesis

When a person already holds health coverage, income support, housing support, disability-linked support, tax-credit support, immigration proof, or another public entitlement, the archive should not accept loss, closure, conversion, or migration as valid merely because a form, portal, notice, deadline, batch job, or system record says so; continuity requires a proof docket that separates legal eligibility, administrative status, notice reach, assistance, automatic renewal / transfer attempts, appeal and reinstatement routes, payment or service continuity, and actual remedy.

## Why this matters

The recent platform-migration notes (`887`-`889`) forced the archive to distinguish a legal right from the portal, account, payroll record, or share code that proves it. The model-decision notes (`874`-`875`) forced the archive to distinguish a signal from a determined legal fact. The transition-receipt notes (`884`-`886`) forced the archive to distinguish a system ending from a system being repaired. Entitlement-continuity cases combine all three problems.

A benefits or health-coverage system can cause harm without inventing a new substantive eligibility rule. It can lose people through mail return, language mismatch, disability barriers, appointee failure, call-center delay, form design, stale data, account lockout, batch scheduling, renewal cohort timing, a missed deadline, or a migration notice that technically complies but practically fails to reach the person who must act. The legal status may be uncertain; the administrative status may be closed; the person may experience the closure as loss of medicine, rent, food, transport, debt protection, or income.

That is why entitlement continuity needs its own docket. A public authority may have good reasons to resume ordinary renewals, consolidate benefits, reduce duplicate payments, modernize delivery, or require periodic eligibility checks. But the archive should ask whether the check is proof of ineligibility or only proof that an administrative process failed.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Honest forms

| Form | Honest label | Primary proof question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| routine renewal | periodic check of continuing eligibility | did the state first try to renew using reliable available information? |
| ex parte renewal | automatic renewal based on reliable data without contacting the beneficiary | which data sources were checked, and what disqualifying fact was actually found? |
| procedural closure | loss because the process was not completed | is there evidence of ineligibility, or only nonresponse / paperwork failure? |
| managed migration | existing claimant must claim a replacement benefit or service | was continuity preserved, including transitional protection and assisted claim routes? |
| digital-account proof | status exists but is proved through an account, portal, token, or checker | what fallback exists when proof or account fails? |
| payment conversion | old payment stream becomes new payment stream | was there a no-gap payment record and correction route? |
| batch redetermination | many cases are reopened, sequenced, or closed by cohort | what cohort, data, pending, and error metrics prevent invisible churn? |
| assisted-route exception | support is promised for vulnerable or hard-to-reach groups | is the assistance actually triggered, logged, and effective before closure? |

The archive should classify the form before scoring the outcome.

### 2. Minimum entitlement-continuity docket

| Field | Required proof |
| --- | --- |
| legal-status baseline | statute, regulation, eligibility rule, old award, coverage category, status period, protected cohort, and what must be proven before loss |
| administrative-status map | open, pending, renewed, closed, terminated, transferred, claim made, not claimed, appealed, reinstated, or in-progress statuses with dates |
| data and evidence lane | data sources used for automatic renewal, income / household / disability / residence / immigration / tax / work records, and reliability limits |
| notice and contact reach | address, digital account, language, disability accommodation, appointee / representative, returned mail, reminder, and phone / outreach record |
| assistance route | helpline, caseworker, home visit, navigator, assister, appointee, trusted organization, community partner, and supported-digital route |
| continuity lane | payment, coverage, medication, housing, food, premium, rent, work, or service continuity across the renewal / migration boundary |
| procedural-loss quarantine | record separating confirmed ineligibility from nonresponse, missing form, bad address, portal failure, or unverified data |
| review and reinstatement | appeal, fair hearing, mandatory reconsideration, good-cause extension, reconsideration, retroactive coverage, back pay, and stay-of-effect route |
| metric truth | cohort denominator, pending cases, late claims, re-enrollments, procedural closures, state / local variation, and data limitations |
| learning and repair | defect logs, corrective action, pause / mitigation authority, recontact campaigns, public reporting, and policy change after churn |

If the docket cannot tell whether people were ineligible or merely lost in process, the archive should not call the closure clean.

### 3. No benefit loss by process failure

The central anti-theater rule is: **a missed process step is not the same as substantive ineligibility.**

That does not mean every person remains eligible forever. It means that the public authority must keep separate records for:

1. eligibility confirmed;
2. eligibility likely but not administratively completed;
3. eligibility uncertain because data are missing;
4. ineligibility proven by reliable evidence;
5. process failure with no ineligibility finding;
6. voluntary nonclaim or opt-out;
7. unreachable despite documented, accessible, repeated contact;
8. late claim / re-enrollment / reinstatement after closure.

Without this separation, a system can manufacture savings, caseload reduction, or apparent modernization by turning administrative friction into legal loss.

### 4. Automatic renewal as a due-process floor

Where a public authority already has enough reliable information to keep a person enrolled, forcing that person to prove the same fact again is not neutral administration. It is a burden shift. The docket should therefore ask whether the authority made a serious ex parte, auto-renewal, data-match, or carry-forward attempt before demanding a form, portal action, account creation, or new claim.

Automatic renewal is not always possible. Data can be stale, inconsistent, legally insufficient, or incomplete. But the archive should require the public owner to identify which element failed: income, household, residence, disability, identity, age, citizenship or immigration status, resources, category, representative authority, or another legal element. A generic “renewal incomplete” label is too thin.

### 5. Deadline letters are chokepoints

A deadline notice can become a practical entitlement waist. It may be formally clear and still fail because the recipient has moved, lacks capacity, lacks language access, is ill, is digitally excluded, has an appointee, is homeless, mistrusts the letter, thinks the letter is a scam, receives confusing advice, or cannot obtain documents.

Minimum deadline-letter proof:

- the notice states the legal consequence in plain language;
- the notice reaches the right person or representative;
- the person has accessible ways to act;
- reminders occur before closure;
- vulnerability / capacity flags trigger outreach;
- extensions and good cause are available;
- benefit closure is not automatic where assistance duties are unmet;
- late claims and reinstatements are tracked.

A deadline letter is proof of a public instruction. It is not proof that the person no longer needs or qualifies for support.

### 6. Metric truth and churn honesty

Entitlement systems often report success through renewal, claim, closure, caseload, or backlog metrics. Those are not enough. The archive should ask for cohort truth:

- total due / total noticed;
- renewed or claimed;
- renewed automatically;
- renewed by form;
- terminated for confirmed ineligibility;
- terminated for procedural reasons;
- pending at month end;
- late claim or reinstatement;
- re-enrollment after closure;
- payment or coverage gap duration;
- assistance-triggered cases;
- appeals and reversals;
- subgroup and geography variation.

The most important hidden number is the remedy tail: people who reappear after the system counted them as a clean closure.

### 7. Vulnerability is an operating state, not a compassion label

A continuity docket should not merely list vulnerable groups. It should encode what the authority does differently before harm occurs. Relevant groups may include disabled people, people with serious illness, people with mental-health conditions, older people, children, people with limited English proficiency, people without stable housing, people without digital access, people with appointees or representatives, people in supported accommodation, people under coercive or abusive family control, and people whose benefit is a gateway to health care, rent, transport, food, or debt protection.

A vulnerability flag that does not change contact, deadline, assistance, or closure rules is a label, not a control.

### 9. Claim-status rule

Claims about caseload reduction, program integrity, modernization, simplified benefits, successful migration, reduced bureaucracy, or return to normal operations must be marked as **case diagnosis** or **strong default**, not settled doctrine, unless the archive can see procedural-loss quarantine, subgroup impact, continuity gaps, appeals, reversals, and late re-enrollment.

### 10. Holding

Entitlement continuity is a first-class governance surface. The rule is: **no benefit loss by process failure**. Renewals, managed migrations, proof portals, deadline letters, and batch redeterminations must preserve the distinction between legal ineligibility and administrative noncompletion, and they must carry notice, assistance, continuity, review, and remedy proof.

## Anti-theater tests

This docket fails if a public authority reports a clean renewal, closure, migration, or modernization outcome while procedural loss, late claims, re-enrollment, appeal reversals, disability / language access, assisted-route performance, and payment / coverage gaps remain invisible.
