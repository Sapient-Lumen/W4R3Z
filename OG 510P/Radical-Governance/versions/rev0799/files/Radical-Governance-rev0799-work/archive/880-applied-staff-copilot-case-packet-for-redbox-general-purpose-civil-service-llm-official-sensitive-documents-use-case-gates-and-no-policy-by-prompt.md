# 880 — Applied staff-copilot case packet for Redbox: general-purpose civil-service LLM, official-sensitive documents, use-case gates, and no policy by prompt

## One-line thesis

Redbox is best classified as a general-purpose internal civil-service LLM copilot with diffuse use-case risk: it may be a safer sanctioned alternative to ad hoc public-model use, but official-sensitive security approval and productivity value do not prove decision-grade reliability, record completeness, or policy legitimacy.

## Why this matters

Redbox is a useful stress case because it is not a single correspondence bot, intake classifier, or public chatbot. It is a sanctioned general-purpose environment where civil servants can use large language models for summarising, redrafting, translating, theme grouping, and other knowledge-work tasks. That generality is the point. It also creates a governance problem: the tool's formal use case may be “general productivity,” while the real use cases are numerous, local, and potentially rights-adjacent.

A broad internal LLM can reduce unsafe workarounds. It can keep official-sensitive material out of uncontrolled public services. It can improve drafting and reduce time spent on repetitive tasks. But the archive must not confuse **secure availability** with **substantive approval**. A system may be safe enough to host sensitive material and still not be reliable enough to summarize a disputed record, draft a ministerial answer, interpret policy, infer facts, or generate operational instructions.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Form verdict

| Field | Holding |
| --- | --- |
| form | general-purpose staff-facing AI copilot / sanctioned internal LLM environment |
| lower form rejected | ordinary productivity software is too thin because LLM outputs synthesize, draft, and reframe substantive material |
| thicker form rejected | automated-decision form is too thick unless a department integrates output into a decision, case route, inspection, enforcement, or legal-effect workflow |
| waist | approved interface for civil servants to use external model capability on internal material |
| live risk | use-case drift: local teams silently convert a general copilot into decision support |

The honest form is not “AI government.” It is a **many-use staff-copilot waist** that needs use-case gates.

### 2. Authority and ownership map

- central product / platform owner: the Redbox service and i.AI / departmental environment;
- departmental users: civil servants choosing tasks and supplying prompts / documents;
- data boundary: information up to official-sensitive classification, subject to tool rules;
- model boundary: users can select from available LLMs, making model-change and model-choice records important;
- public-action boundary: Redbox output is not itself a decision, reply, policy, or instruction until a civil servant or department turns it into one.

### 3. Use-case register

The archive should require separate review for at least these use cases:

| Use case | Minimum docket |
| --- | --- |
| summarising a disputed case record | source-document list, omitted-issue check, reviewer signoff |
| drafting public correspondence | sent-output log, source pack, QA, correction route |
| preparing policy advice | source hierarchy, uncertainty label, human analysis record |
| translating or simplifying public-facing material | bilingual / accessibility review, official-source retention |
| theme grouping consultation evidence | sampling, representativeness, excluded-text record |
| report or speech drafting | provenance of claims, factual verification, approval chain |
| operational instruction | prohibit or activate model-decision / administrative-law tests |

The tool can remain general. The governance cannot.

### 4. Evidence packet

Required packet for this case:

- approved users and departments;
- security classification and data-handling rule;
- model choices available to users;
- prompt / file retention and deletion rule;
- prohibited uses;
- training material and user reminders;
- use-case expansion approvals;
- sample quality audits;
- incident reports and misuse reports;
- model / provider change logs;
- records policy for when output is used in public action.

### 5. Capture channels

- **security-confidence laundering**: official-sensitive approval treated as reliability proof.
- **general-purpose fog**: because the tool has many tasks, no task receives a docket.
- **prompt policy**: a generated formulation silently becomes policy advice.
- **summary capture**: staff act on a summary without reading the source record.
- **model-choice opacity**: different models produce different drafts with no record of which one shaped the output.
- **productivity pressure**: time-saving claims create incentives to overuse the tool.
- **non-search confusion**: users treat the model as a knowledge source despite the boundary that it does not search the internet.

### 6. Live chain use

The case activates `813`, `815`, `816`, `817`, `818`, `819`, `820`, and `823`. It reserves `812`, `821`, `822`, and `824` unless a specific department links output to formal decisions, budgets, statutory records, procurement, or enforcement.

### 7. Repair surfaces

- public owner and department owner;
- general tool transparency record;
- departmental use-case register;
- prohibited-use policy;
- official-sensitive handling record;
- prompt / output retention rule;
- model-choice and model-change log;
- quality sampling and incident review;
- training and warning materials;
- public-record capture rule when output enters official action.

### 8. Upgrade and downshift triggers

Upgrade to `874` if Redbox output becomes a recommendation, case classification, evidence summary, or decision support in a rights-affecting workflow. Upgrade to `876` if Redbox output is served directly to the public. Downshift to ordinary office-software governance only for low-risk, personal drafting that never enters a case record, public communication, policy advice, or operational instruction.

### 9. Holding

Redbox is a legitimate case for a sanctioned internal AI waist, but it must be governed at two levels: the platform level and the use-case level. Platform approval answers whether civil servants may use the tool. It does not answer what any given prompt, summary, draft, or output is allowed to do.

## Anti-theater tests

The packet fails if “safe and secure,” “general purpose,” “human owned,” or “beta” becomes a substitute for use-case review. The archive should demand a visible line between tool access, task approval, output record capture, and public action.
