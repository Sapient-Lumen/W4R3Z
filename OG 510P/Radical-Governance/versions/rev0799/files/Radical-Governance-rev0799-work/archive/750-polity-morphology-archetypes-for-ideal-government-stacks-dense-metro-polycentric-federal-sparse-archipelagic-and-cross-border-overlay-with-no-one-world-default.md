# 750 — Polity morphology archetypes for ideal government stacks: dense metro, polycentric federal, sparse, archipelagic, and cross-border overlay, with no one-world default

## One-line thesis

The archive should stop implying that one ideal government stack fits every country-form: before designing tiers, it should classify the polity’s **morphology** — dense metro-dominant, polycentric unitary, federal or plurinational, sparse and peripheral, archipelagic, or border-interlocked — and then choose the thinnest honest stack for that morphology rather than copying one universal ladder.

## Why this matters

The archive already has a strong answer to **what each scope is for** (`579`, `660`, `699`, `749`). What it still lacked was one compact answer to a different design problem:

**which whole-country stack should be treated as normal for different kinds of polities?**

That matters because the phrase *ideal governments of each scope* can mislead if it is heard as *every polity should instantiate every scope in the same way*. Real countries differ too much for that.

A dense metro-dominant country, a polycentric medium-sized unitary state, a large federation, a sparse continental territory, an archipelago, and a border-interlocked basin region do not need the same vertical furniture. They need the same design discipline, but not the same territorial silhouette.

Without a morphology rule, archives drift toward two bad habits.

The first is **stack universalism**: one ladder is treated as normal everywhere, so countries with different settlement patterns get pushed toward ornamental tiers or hidden capacity pooling.

The second is **exception folklore**: every deviation gets narrated as a local oddity rather than as a recurring archetype the archive can reason about in advance.

A better answer is to keep one common scope grammar while allowing several recurring stack archetypes.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Classify polity morphology before choosing territorial furniture

Before asking how many tiers a polity should sustain, ask what kind of territorial and settlement pattern is actually present.

At minimum, the archive should distinguish six recurring archetypes:

1. **dense metro-dominant**,
2. **polycentric unitary**,
3. **federal or plurinational**,
4. **sparse or peripheral continental**,
5. **archipelagic or highly dispersed small state**,
6. **cross-border overlay territory**.

These are not identities for their own sake. They are routing clues for what stack can stay honest.

### 2. Dense metro-dominant polities should normally fuse city and nation more than they multiply regions

Where one metropolitan area overwhelmingly dominates demography, economy, and state capacity, the archive should usually presume:

- strong municipalities or boroughs for lived-place government,
- one directly legitimate metropolitan tier if the city-region is functionally real,
- a national guarantor layer,
- and only a limited regional layer unless a second territorial platform is genuinely present.

The danger here is decorative regionalism. The honest answer is often a **municipal–metro–nation** stack, not region-making by symmetry worship.

### 3. Polycentric unitary polities should usually rely on strong municipalities plus real regions

Where several urban and territorial platforms coexist without a federal bargain, the archive should usually prefer:

- real municipalities,
- metropolitan tiers only in city-regions that need them,
- regional governments where hospitals, universities, corridors, basin planning, or territorial development are genuinely regional,
- and a national guarantor state.

This is the archetype in which a regional tier is often useful because the country is too plural and spatially distributed for pure municipalism, yet not constituted as a federation.

### 4. Federal or plurinational polities should treat regions or states as constitutional partners, not service districts

Where constituent territories are part of the constitutional bargain, the honest stack normally includes:

- local government beneath,
- metropolitan layers where urban systems require them,
- states, provinces, Länder, nations, or autonomous territories as real shared-rule partners,
- and a federal union level for equal citizenship, macro-capacity, and common constitutional settlement.

In this archetype, the regional tier is not optional furniture. It is part of the polity’s democratic settlement.

### 5. Sparse and peripheral continental polities should tolerate county-style pooling and asymmetric regions

Where distance, thin settlement, mountains, desert, tundra, or other harsh geography raise minimum service cost, the archive should presume a more uneven but honest stack:

- micro-local voice where settlements are tiny,
- municipalities where viable,
- county or district pooling where pure municipalism would be fictitious,
- regions where catchments or platforms are very large,
- and a national solidarity layer that compensates for territorial disadvantage.

Here the danger is false localism. The archive should accept pooling and asymmetry as normal rather than as constitutional embarrassment.

### 6. Archipelagic and highly dispersed small states should combine local island voice with a compact national core

Where the state is small yet geographically scattered, the ideal stack is often:

- settlement, island, or village councils,
- municipal or district place government where viable,
- a compact national core for treasury, law, citizenship, and external representation,
- and explicit pooled or treaty-based specialist capacity where domestic duplication would be wasteful.

The archive should not force every island to host miniature sovereignty, and it should not let the capital monopolize every ordinary local question.

### 7. Cross-border overlay territories should add treaty or functional layers without pretending to be new domestic states

Some territories are defined by a basin, labour market, mountain chain, estuary, freight corridor, or border conurbation that crosses sovereign lines.

The honest answer there is usually **overlay governance**:

- keep domestic democratic tiers intact,
- add bounded cross-border institutions for the shared problem,
- and constitutionalize the overlay only to the degree its powers, money, and review burden justify.

The archive should reject both fantasies here: no border problem proves the need for a fake superstate, and no border problem disappears just because domestic ministries insist it does.

### 8. Treat morphology as a presumptive packet, not as destiny

A polity may combine several archetypes at once.

Examples:

- a federal state with one dominant metro,
- a sparse federation with an archipelagic province,
- a unitary country whose border basin needs treaty overlay,
- or a small island state whose capital metro needs stronger internal borough government.

The archive should therefore let morphology packets stack and combine. The point is not to pigeonhole. The point is to keep design honest.

## Archetype matrix

| Polity morphology | Usual honest stack | What is usually optional | Main danger if designed badly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Dense metro-dominant** | municipality / borough + metro + nation | full regional tier unless a second platform is real | decorative regions or city-state overcentralization |
| **Polycentric unitary** | municipality + selected metros + region + nation | federal shared-rule chamber | fragmented city competition or overcentralized ministry rule |
| **Federal / plurinational** | municipality + selected metros + state / province / nation + federal union | none of the core shared-rule tiers | treating constituent units as deconcentrated service districts |
| **Sparse / peripheral continental** | micro-local voice + municipality or county pool + region + nation | universal metro tier | false localism or service collapse by thin density |
| **Archipelagic / dispersed small state** | island / settlement voice + local government + compact national core + pooled specialist capacity | decorative provinces | capital monopoly or miniature-sovereignty cosplay |
| **Cross-border overlay territory** | domestic stack + bounded treaty / functional overlay | new general territorial sovereign tier | superstate theater or unmanaged border externalities |

## Guardrails

- Do not infer the right stack from map symmetry alone.
- Do not treat every deviation from the full ladder as constitutional failure.
- Do not confuse morphology with immutable identity; redesign remains possible when settlement and capacity change.
- Do not let cross-border overlays quietly become domestic shadow governments.
- Do not let small-state pooling stay invisible in law, budget, and review.

## Failure modes

- **one-world defaultism**: one territorial ladder is treated as normal for every polity regardless of settlement pattern.
- **map mimicry**: decorative regions, counties, or federal chambers are copied from other countries without local platform work.
- **exception folklore**: repeated structural patterns are treated as unique local quirks, so the archive never develops reusable doctrine.
- **hidden pooling**: a stack looks autonomous on paper while real specialist capacity lives elsewhere without visible terms.
- **overlay inflation**: corridor, basin, or border institutions silently drift into broad territorial government without constitutional upgrade.

## Compression rule for the archive

If a note can say **which scope should govern a function** but not also say **which polity morphology makes that stack choice honest**, it is still missing part of the design logic for ideal governments.
