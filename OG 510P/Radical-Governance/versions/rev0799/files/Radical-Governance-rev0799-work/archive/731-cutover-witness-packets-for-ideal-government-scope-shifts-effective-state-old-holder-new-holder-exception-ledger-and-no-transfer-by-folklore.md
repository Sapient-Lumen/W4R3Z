# 731 — Cutover witness packets for ideal-government scope shifts: effective state, old holder, new holder, exception ledger, and no transfer by folklore

## One-line thesis

Once the archive has a continuity packet for scope shifts (`730`), it should also keep one compact **cutover witness packet** for the handoff moment itself: every materially consequential move across scopes should leave behind one contemporaneous public-and-reviewable witness saying what changed, when it changed, which holder lost authority, which holder gained authority, what remained transitional, which queues, records, contracts, and budgets were still in flight, which exceptions stayed live, and what fallback path still existed — so later review does not have to reconstruct the transfer from rumor, screenshots, or contradictory portal residue.

## Why this matters

The archive can now say where a function should live (`725`, `726`), when the default should be overridden (`727`), how a bounded sideways authority should be built (`728`), who must ratify a move (`729`), and what continuity objects have to survive once the move is chosen (`730`).

That is enough to design a serious scope shift. It is still not enough to **prove** what happened at the moment the shift went live.

Real reorganisations often fail at that evidentiary seam. The law says the ministry changed on Tuesday, but the old website still takes submissions on Wednesday. The records system says the case moved at 09:00, but the appeal mailbox was still monitored by the predecessor until noon. Budget authority switched on paper, yet the invoice approval chain was still hosted by the former department. The new regional authority claims it owned the queue from day one, while the old municipalities insist they were only providing temporary hosting. Months later, auditors, courts, residents, suppliers, unions, or successor officials try to work out what actually happened and discover that nobody left one authoritative witness of the handoff state.

That gap is constitutional, not cosmetic.

Without a cutover witness, a polity cannot cleanly answer basic questions after a scope shift:

- when the former holder truly stopped acting in its own name,
- when the successor became live for each consequential lane,
- which front door, system, or notice-head was authoritative at the moment of dispute,
- which exceptions were still transitional rather than settled,
- and whether a breakdown should trigger rollback, repair, or nullity.

The archive already has adjacent notes on contemporaneous witnesses (`450`), production-entry receipts (`471`), pending-matter transition rules (`656`), evidentiary continuity (`658`), evidentiary break presumptions (`659`), settlement certificates (`641`), and the new continuity packet (`730`). What it still lacked was one smaller object tailored to **scope-shift cutover itself**.

That missing object is the **cutover witness packet**.

Its job is narrower than the continuity packet and earlier than the settlement certificate. It does not describe the whole transition plan, and it does not certify that the new arrangement has proven itself over time. It simply captures, at the moment of transfer, the minimum truthful state that later review will need.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Treat cutover as a witness-worthy constitutional event

A material move across scopes should be treated as a consequential state change in its own right, not as a background implementation detail.

The cutover witness packet should therefore be generated whenever a function, lane, or legally consequential sub-function becomes live in a new home, even if the wider reform still has transitional bridges around it.

No polity should rely on later recollection to establish the moment when authority actually crossed the boundary.

### 2. Capture the exact effective state, not only the legal intention

The packet should distinguish between:

- the legal instrument that authorised the move,
- the scheduled cutover time,
- the actual cutover time,
- and the effective state that existed immediately after the switch.

That means naming what was fully transferred, what was partially transferred, what remained hosted, what stayed with the predecessor, and what was frozen or paused pending later completion.

A cutover witness that records only aspiration is not a witness. It is press release residue.

### 3. Name both the outgoing and incoming holders lane by lane

For each materially relevant lane, the packet should say who held authority immediately before and immediately after cutover.

At minimum, the archive should expect this for:

- legal title or formal competence,
- operational control,
- budget execution,
- case or queue ownership,
- record custody,
- contract management,
- public notice or front-door control,
- and review or appeal routing.

This blocks a common later fiction: everyone claims the transfer happened, but no one can say which holder actually owned which lane at the moment of dispute.

### 4. Freeze one exception ledger at the handoff moment

A scope shift often goes live with temporary accommodations still active: hosted systems, dual signatures, temporary shared services, borrowed staff, exception approvals, manual workarounds, legacy notice heads, or split review lanes.

The cutover witness should therefore include a compact **exception ledger** naming:

- each live temporary exception,
- its lawful basis,
- its expiry or review clock,
- its owner,
- and the failure condition that should trigger escalation, rollback, or fresh clearance.

Otherwise temporary residue later impersonates the intended constitution.

### 5. Preserve one authoritative public-facing state

The cutover witness should record which public-facing surfaces were authoritative immediately after go-live.

This should include, where relevant:

- the canonical front door,
- the active notice head,
- the authoritative web or service endpoint,
- the public contact route,
- the filing destination,
- and any temporary forwarding or cure rule for misdirected submissions.

The public should not have to prove which of several half-updated surfaces counted at the moment they acted.

### 6. Witness the queue, record, and budget baselines that crossed the boundary

A scope shift dispute often turns less on abstract competence than on the state of live work and live obligations at handoff.

The packet should therefore record a compact baseline for the transferred population of work and resources, such as:

- the count or identifier range for live cases or files,
- the budget or appropriation line now financing the function,
- the contract or grant population still in force,
- the authoritative system or ledger head,
- and any declared exclusions still awaiting later migration.

The witness need not duplicate full operational data. It should capture just enough to prevent later folklore about what was supposedly included.

### 7. Bind the packet to rollback, repair, and nullity lanes

A cutover witness is not only for historical memory. It is also the object that later review should consult when asking whether the transfer succeeded lawfully.

The packet should therefore point clearly to:

- the fallback or rollback path if critical continuity failed,
- the repair lane if the defect is curable,
- the review lane for disputes about ownership or routing,
- and the nullity or standstill rule if the transfer never validly became effective.

That linkage stops the witness from becoming a passive archive ornament.

### 8. Separate the witness from later settlement claims

The cutover witness should be contemporaneous and minimal. It should not be rewritten after the fact to narrate success, excuse failure, or smuggle provisional exceptions into ordinary status.

If the transfer later settles cleanly, the settlement certificate (`641`) should say so. If the transfer later fails or narrows, that later object should say so. The witness itself should remain a fixed account of the handoff moment.

### 9. Give the witness both a public summary and a review-grade form

The public version should be short enough that residents, staff, counterparties, and overseers can understand what changed.

The review-grade form should be structured enough to support audit, litigation, records retrieval, and operational repair. It should include, at minimum:

- cutover identifier,
- legal basis,
- timestamp,
- affected function or lane list,
- old holder/new holder map,
- exception ledger,
- authoritative public surfaces,
- and named rollback or escalation contacts.

This preserves both civic legibility and institutional usefulness.

### 10. Require signed ownership of the witness

The packet should be attributable to named officials or offices from the relevant holders, not emitted by anonymous infrastructure.

Where the transfer is significant, the archive should prefer a signed or otherwise attested witness naming:

- the sending authority,
- the receiving authority,
- and any guarantor, registry, or oversight lane that validated the handoff state.

No consequential constitutional transfer should depend on an unattributed dashboard snapshot.

## Guardrails

- Do not treat the statute, treaty, order, or memorandum alone as proof of the handoff state.
- Do not let websites, portals, and internal systems drift without one preserved authoritative witness of which surface counted.
- Do not rewrite the handoff witness later to match the story officials wish had been true.
- Do not capture so little that the packet cannot resolve queue, record, budget, or notice disputes.
- Do not capture so much that the witness becomes an unusable dump instead of a portable review object.
- Do not let temporary exceptions remain live without appearing in the handoff witness.
- Do not let the cutover witness substitute for later settlement, review, or repair acts.

## Failure modes

- **transfer by folklore**: everyone talks as if the move happened, but no witness shows what state actually changed and when.
- **split-brain handoff**: old and new holders both act as if they own the same lane because the cutover state was never frozen.
- **ghost front door**: residents file through a stale surface and later cannot prove it still looked authoritative.
- **exception laundering**: temporary hosting, dual control, or manual workarounds survive because nobody logged them at handoff.
- **queue mythology**: later reviewers cannot establish which cases, files, contracts, or liabilities actually crossed the boundary.
- **retroactive narration**: months later the witness is silently rebuilt to fit the winner’s story of the reform.
- **unsigned transfer reality**: no named office can be held answerable for the truth of the handoff state.

## Practical tests

A scope-shift cutover witness regime is honest when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Does every materially consequential scope shift produce one contemporaneous cutover witness packet?
2. Does the packet distinguish legal authorisation, scheduled cutover, actual cutover, and effective post-cutover state?
3. Does it map old holder and new holder lane by lane rather than speaking only of “the function” in aggregate?
4. Does it record the authoritative public front door and notice state at the handoff moment?
5. Does it preserve a compact baseline for cases, records, budgets, contracts, and declared exclusions?
6. Does it freeze every live temporary exception together with owner, clock, and escalation path?
7. Is the packet linked to rollback, repair, review, and nullity lanes rather than left as inert prose?
8. Is there both a public-readable form and a structured review-grade form?
9. Is the witness attributable to named authorities rather than anonymous operational residue?
10. Can later reviewers tell what changed at handoff without reconstructing the event from screenshots, hearsay, or contradictory system traces?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a polity can explain **why a function moved**, **who ratified the move**, and even **how continuity was supposed to survive**, but still cannot show one fixed witness of **what actually changed at the handoff moment**, then the scope shift remains vulnerable to dispute, blame fog, and retroactive constitutional folklore. It still lacks **a cutover witness packet**.
