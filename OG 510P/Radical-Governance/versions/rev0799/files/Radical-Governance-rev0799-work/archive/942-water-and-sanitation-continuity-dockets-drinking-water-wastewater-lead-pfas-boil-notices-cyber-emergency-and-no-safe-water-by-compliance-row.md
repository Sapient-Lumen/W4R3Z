# 942 — Water and sanitation continuity dockets: drinking water, wastewater, lead, PFAS, boil notices, cyber, emergency, and no safe water by compliance row

## One-line thesis

Water and sanitation continuity is not a compliance row, Consumer Confidence Report, service-line inventory, boil-water notice, hydrant pressure reading, wastewater permit, cybersecurity checklist, or capital-plan estimate. A household, school, clinic, elder-care facility, shelter, prison, farm, tribal system, or small town can appear inside a regulated water system while drinking-water safety, pressure, source protection, premise plumbing, lead/PFAS treatment, shutoff protection, wastewater integrity, emergency supply, cyber resilience, and remedy are broken. The archive should join source, system, premise, household, vulnerable-user, wastewater, incident, funding, and repair records before calling water safe.

## Why this matters

The archive had water-by-scope and utility/medical-baseline notes, but it did not yet have a direct operational water/sanitation continuity packet. That was risky because water is a public-health service, a household affordability service, a school/clinic/custody/long-term-care dependency, a climate resilience system, and a cyber/industrial-control target. A legal or regulatory row can be true while people cannot safely drink, cook, bathe, wash hands, run medical equipment, keep schools open, evacuate, flush toilets, or receive timely contaminant notice.

The governing repair phrase is **no safe water by compliance row**.

## Pattern pack

- **No safe water by compliance row.** A system, permit, monitoring schedule, or absence of a current violation is not proof that every user has safe, pressurized, accessible water.
- **No drinking water by CCR.** A Consumer Confidence Report is a notice surface; it does not prove current tap safety, premise plumbing, lead exposure, translated notice, or bottled-water logistics.
- **No lead safety by inventory.** A service-line inventory and replacement plan are not proof that lead exposure is gone at the tap during replacement, disturbance, or premise-plumbing risk.
- **No PFAS protection by future compliance date.** A final or proposed rule must be joined to testing, treatment, public notice, funding, and interim risk communication.
- **No boil safety by advisory posted.** Boil, do-not-drink, and do-not-use advisories must be joined to language access, refrigeration, formula, schools, hospitals, dialysis, shelters, elder care, and people without fuel or bottled water.
- **No resilience by AWIA certification.** A risk/resilience assessment or emergency-response-plan certification does not prove inventory, backup power, chemicals, staffing, mutual aid, alternate source, or cyber recovery.
- **No sanitation by wastewater permit.** Clean-water permits and treatment rows do not prove sewer continuity, septic safety, overflow control, stormwater separation, or emergency pumping.
- **No equity by capital need.** Infrastructure-need surveys support funding and prioritization; they are not a household-level repair, rate affordability, or rural/tribal/small-system continuity record.

## Minimum joined record

A serious water/sanitation packet should contain at least:

1. **User and facility map.** Household, school, clinic, care setting, prison, shelter, business, or farm; service address; PWSID or private well/septic edge; and agency/utility owner.
2. **System operating state.** Source, treatment, pressure, storage, distribution, operator staffing, chemicals, backup power, and any current incident command.
3. **Compliance/source posture.** SDWIS/ECHO rows, sampling dates, violations, enforcement, CCR, public-notice class, and data freshness.
4. **Tap-risk file.** Lead service line, galvanized/plumbing risk, PFAS/chemical results, microbial risk, school/child-care taps, filters, flushing, and replacement/treatment schedules.
5. **Advisory and outreach file.** Boil/do-not-drink/do-not-use notice, affected area, languages, accessible format, door/text/radio/school/clinic channels, and user receipt.
6. **Emergency water logistics.** Bottled water, tankers, cooling/health facilities, shelters, formula, dialysis, elder care, medical equipment, and transportation.
7. **Wastewater/sanitation file.** Sewer or septic continuity, combined sewer overflow, treatment bypass, flood/backflow, sanitation at congregate settings, and cleanup.
8. **Cyber/OT resilience file.** Remote access, authentication, segmentation, incident response, manual operations, vendor access, KEV exposure, and recovery proof.
9. **Affordability/remedy file.** Shutoff/arrears, payment plan, low-income protections, complaint route, replacement funding, bottled-water reimbursement, and after-action repair.
10. **Equity and source-currentness boundary.** Small, rural, tribal, low-income, environmental-justice, language, disability, school, health, and custody effects; mark whether each source is a regulation, dataset, notice, plan, funding estimate, or incident record.

## Upgrade triggers

Route to the full water/sanitation continuity docket when any of these appear:

- a utility, regulator, or vendor cites a compliance row, SDWIS row, CCR, inventory, AWIA certification, permit, or dashboard as proof that water is safe;
- a main break, pressure loss, contamination, boil/do-not-drink/do-not-use advisory, PFAS/lead detection, wastewater overflow, flood, drought, wildfire, or cyber incident affects service;
- schools, child care, hospitals, dialysis, shelters, nursing homes, prisons, mobile homes, private wells, tribal systems, or small systems are in the affected area;
- a rule clock, capital grant, lead/PFAS treatment project, wastewater upgrade, or resilience plan is cited as if implementation were complete;
- shutoff, arrears, rate shock, bottled-water burden, inaccessible notice, or complaint friction can prevent actual safe water use.

## Downshift conditions

A lighter summary is acceptable only when the question is purely aggregate infrastructure context and no household, facility, public-health, notice, rate, replacement, incident, emergency, or remedy consequence is live. Once a person or critical facility may lack safe drinking water, hygiene, sanitation, notice, or repair, the full packet should be opened.

## Failure modes

- **No safe water by compliance row:** a system has no visible current violation but taps, pressure, premise plumbing, notices, or vulnerable-user logistics fail.
- **No notice by CCR:** a yearly report exists but current incident notice, language access, or affected-premise reach fails.
- **No lead safety by inventory:** a service-line inventory exists but replacement, filters, flushing, school taps, or post-replacement sampling are missing.
- **No PFAS protection by standard:** a rule, waiver, or proposed delay is treated as treatment and exposure reduction.
- **No boil safety by website:** an advisory is posted without user receipt, bottled water, formula, medical, or congregate-setting support.
- **No sanitation by treatment status:** wastewater/septic/stormwater failures are ignored because the drinking-water row looks normal.
- **No cyber resilience by checklist:** OT, remote access, manual mode, vendor access, and recovery remain untested.
- **No affordability by assistance page:** water remains unaffordable or shut off while payment-plan and complaint routes exist on paper.

## Audit questions

- Which people, facilities, and critical dependencies are affected, and which PWSID, well, sewer, septic, stormwater, or premise-plumbing records prove it?
- What is the current source, treatment, pressure, storage, distribution, and wastewater operating state?
- What are the latest monitoring results, violations, advisories, public notices, and enforcement records, and when were they updated?
- Are lead, PFAS, microbial, chemical, and radioactive risks treated as separate lanes with testing, treatment, notice, and interim support?
- Did users receive usable notice in time, in the right language/formats, and through channels that reach schools, clinics, shelters, care facilities, and custody settings?
- What alternate water, sanitation, repair, reimbursement, complaint, and after-action routes exist, and who owns them?
- Are small, rural, tribal, low-income, disabled, medically vulnerable, child, elder, and congregate-setting users visible in the denominator?
