# 974 — Service-home successor route for shared territorial services: service map, operator chain, continuity, and no service government by case example

## One-line thesis

A shared territorial service home is not proved by a strong transport case, a utility authority, a consortium, or a board map; it exists only when a generic route can show the service map, lawful delivery instrument, operator chain, near-to-person front door, complaint handoff, continuity/failure-takeover duty, and source boundary across more than one service family.

## Why this matters

The archive has spent several revisions protecting note `615` from premature retirement. The reason is not sentimental. Note `615` carries generic service-home grammar that a London transport case, a regional water/wastewater authority case, or an intermunicipal shared-service consortium cannot absorb by itself.

That became the risky unfinished work after rev0775. The capture queue was closed and service-home tests existed, but the surviving route was still missing. Without a successor route, the archive would either keep repeating that note `615` is not deletion-ready or eventually delete it because the generated tests looked mature. Both would be route bureaucracy rather than forward motion.

This note is the successor route. It does not retire note `615`. It gives future revisions a stronger target: a reusable service-home packet that can absorb protected elements only when source, test, and redirect parity are explicit.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Start with the service promise, not the institution

A territorial service home starts with the public promise: what service is owed, to whom, where, at what floor, with what continuity obligation, and through which access route. Institutional form is secondary. A mayoral board, joint powers authority, utility authority, operator company, regional office, or intermunicipal hub can be relevant only after the service promise is visible.

The packet should therefore state:

- the service family;
- the user group and territorial population;
- the service floor or standard;
- the entity that owns the promise;
- the local front door;
- the escalation route; and
- the fallback owner if ordinary delivery fails.

If the packet cannot name the promise owner, it cannot call the arrangement a service home.

### 2. Separate service-map proof from board-map proof

A board map shows who sits where. A service map shows who receives what and through which operating chain. The archive should not let the first substitute for the second.

A service-home route should distinguish at least five lanes:

- services remaining wholly local;
- services delivered locally under shared standards;
- services commissioned or coordinated by the shared tier;
- services directly operated by the shared tier or its arm's-length bodies; and
- services that remain state or national but enter the territory through local or regional doors.

A board, compact, statute, or strategy is useful only if it can be joined to this map.

### 3. Name the authority/operator/regulator/commissioner split

A service home has to say who is wearing which hat. The packet should not let an operator, contractor, public authority, regulator, and complaint handler collapse into one comforting noun.

The minimum split is:

| Role | Evidence needed | Failure if absent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Authority | statutory, charter, compact, or delegated service obligation | service by aspiration |
| Commissioner | procurement, grant, franchise, rate, or service-level instrument | service by informal bargain |
| Operator | public operator, municipal operator, contractor, concessionaire, or consortium | service by black box |
| Regulator/inspector/auditor | inspection, audit, Ombudsman, safety, rate, or legal review route | service by self-certification |
| Complaint and appeal owner | local route plus unresolved escalation path | service by ticket closure |

The split can be complex. It cannot be invisible.

### 4. Require a lawful delivery instrument before treating a service as shared government

Shared service government needs a delivery instrument, not just a plan. The instrument can be a statute, franchise, concession, service-level compact, rate-setting framework, procurement framework, delegated authority, public corporation charter, intermunicipal agreement, or direct-operating power.

The test is not whether lawyers can identify a source of power somewhere. The test is whether the public can see how the shared tier can actually commission, operate, enforce, change, substitute, or terminate the service arrangement.

### 5. Keep local access visible when the back end is shared

Shared territorial services often centralize planning, standards, data, procurement, or assets while leaving access near residents. That can be good. It becomes dishonest when the shared back end erases the local front door.

A successor service-home route should therefore require near-to-person entry paths: local counters, station or depot staff, assisted-digital access, city/town contacts, common helplines, neighborhood offices, or named local referral routes.

Back-end integration cannot become distance by design.

### 6. Treat complaint handoff as part of the service constitution

A service home is not proved by normal operating diagrams. It is proved when unresolved trouble can move. A credible packet should show where a person goes when the first operator says no, when the local municipality disclaims responsibility, when the contractor blames the authority, or when the regional body says it only sets policy.

The complaint path must preserve:

- first-contact route;
- operator response route;
- independent escalation or watchdog path where available;
- administrative, audit, legislative, or judicial route for systemic failures; and
- feedback into contract, standard, budget, or service redesign.

If a complaint cannot cross the operator/authority boundary, the service home is not yet real.

### 7. Make continuity and failure takeover first-class tests

The strongest service-home question is: if the current operator fails, who still owes the service? A packet should not wait for bankruptcy, cyber failure, strike, climate shock, fiscal distress, or supplier exit before asking this.

The continuity floor should include:

- emergency operating doctrine;
- substitution rights;
- step-in or takeover authority;
- transition records and data handoff;
- reserve capacity or mutual-aid route;
- contract-exit and reprocurement plans; and
- a public statement of who remains responsible during failure.

This is where service government differs from ordinary outsourcing.

### 8. Use cross-sector parity before absorbing generic doctrine

A London transport packet can show a mature functional authority. A regional water/wastewater packet can show an asset-liability and ratepayer route. An intermunicipal shared-service packet can show a thin backbone that should not be mistaken for county government. None of those alone absorbs the generic service-home doctrine.

A successor route must work across at least these service families:

- transport and mobility;
- water, wastewater, waste, or utility service;
- emergency communications or emergency operations;
- shared administrative/back-office services;
- social or benefit access front doors; and
- digital case or identity services that bind the public to a shared system.

Generic doctrine can retire only when it survives this cross-sector translation.

### 9. Preserve source-boundary discipline

Service-home evidence is often tempting but thin. Governance pages, annual reports, business plans, community lists, complaint pages, statutory text, and audit records each prove something different.

The packet should never claim:

- service continuity from a governance page;
- user access from a customer-community list;
- complaint remedy from a contact page;
- fiscal sustainability from a business plan;
- legal authority from a case example alone;
- delivered outcomes from an operator chart; or
- public accountability from a board roster.

Each source has to be joined to the claim it is strong enough to support.

### 10. Create a redirect ledger before retirement

A note can be retired only after the archive records where each protected element went. For note `615`, the redirect ledger must map at least:

- service map;
- authority/operator/regulator/commissioner split;
- lawful delivery instrument;
- local front door;
- complaint handoff;
- continuity/failure takeover;
- operator-chain constitution;
- cross-sector applicability;
- source-boundary proof; and
- anti-memo-network rule.

Until that ledger exists, `615` should remain active even if this successor route is stronger.

## Successor packet scaffold

| Field | Required answer | Reject if |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service family | Which public service or service bundle is at stake? | the packet only names an institution |
| Territorial population | Who is served and where? | the service area is inferred from board jurisdiction alone |
| Promise owner | Who owes the service floor? | strategy language has no accountable owner |
| Delivery instrument | What lawful instrument authorizes delivery? | memorandum, habit, or operator practice is the only proof |
| Operator chain | Who actually delivers each segment? | contractors or municipal operators are hidden |
| Local front door | Where does a resident enter? | the only route is a remote authority or generic portal |
| Escalation route | Where does unresolved trouble go? | complaint handling ends inside the first operator |
| Continuity owner | Who acts during failure, exit, or emergency? | continuity is treated as procurement aftercare |
| Source boundary | Which source proves which claim? | a page, report, law, or dashboard is treated as outcome proof |
| Redirect ledger | Which older protected element moved here? | retirement is based on similarity or convenience |

## Applied use

Run this route when the archive is tempted to say that a territory has service government because it has a mayoral transport body, a regional utility authority, a shared-services consortium, a metropolitan board, or a service platform.

The answer can be yes, no, or not yet. The answer cannot be based on one impressive case example.
