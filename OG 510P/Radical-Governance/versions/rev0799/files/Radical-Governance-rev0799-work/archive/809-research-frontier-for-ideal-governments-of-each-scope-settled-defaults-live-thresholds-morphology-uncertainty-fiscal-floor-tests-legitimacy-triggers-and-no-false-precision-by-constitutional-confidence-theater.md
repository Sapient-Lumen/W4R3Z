# 809 — Research frontier for ideal governments of each scope: settled defaults, live thresholds, morphology uncertainty, fiscal-floor tests, legitimacy triggers, and no false precision by constitutional confidence theater

## One-line thesis

The archive should now separate **settled default doctrine** from **live research questions**: it can state with high confidence the vocation, minimum kit, and anti-cosplay limit of each scope, but it should keep threshold questions — when a functional city truly needs metro democracy, when sparse territories need county-style pooling, when a region is a real platform rather than decorative duplication, how much fiscal congruence is enough, when intervention should trigger, and how thick upper-tier institutions may become — inside one compact research frontier rather than pretending the whole stack is equally settled.

## Why this matters

The archive already has a strong answer-key run for ideal governments by scope.

`579` gives the scope-allocation grammar. `603` gives the design order. `604` and `605` diagnose and repair misfit. `660`, `749`, `750`, and `790` say what the ideal stack looks like across different morphologies and scales. `791` through `808` now make that stack benchmarkable, stress-testable, maintainable, contestable, and reviewable with evidence, mission discipline, independence, and calibration.

What the archive still lacked was one compact note for a different problem:

**which parts of this answer should now be treated as default doctrine, and which parts should remain open research rather than hardened confidence theater?**

That distinction matters because governance design fails in two opposite ways.

The first failure is **timid vagueness**. Reformers refuse to say anything firm, so every scope question gets reopened from zero.

The second failure is **false precision**. Reformers talk as if one exact metro trigger, one exact regional formula, one exact fiscal floor, or one exact intervention threshold were already settled for all polities and all morphologies.

A serious research archive should sit between those errors. It should say what is already clear enough to guide design now, while keeping visible the questions that still need comparator work, threshold-testing, field evidence, and revision over time.

Official guidance points toward that split. The European Charter of Local Self-Government fixes a durable localist default while still allowing allocation upward when the nature and scale of the task require it. OECD guidance on decentralisation and multi-level public investment repeatedly frames outcomes as depending on design quality, coordination, capacity, and implementation rather than on labels alone. UN-Habitat and World Bank metropolitan-governance work likewise show that the right metro thickness depends on real urban-system conditions and on what powers are actually exercised. The archive should compress that lesson into one rule: **be decisive about vocations and anti-cosplay limits; be empirical and revisable about thresholds and thickness.**

## Pattern pack

### 1. Separate default doctrine from research frontier

A mature archive should not treat every sentence at the same confidence level.

For ideal governments by scope, it should distinguish at least three classes:

- **default doctrine** — strong enough to use as the presumptive design answer now;
- **context-contingent judgment** — usually answerable, but only after morphology, comparator class, and field conditions are declared;
- **research frontier** — not yet settled enough for one universal threshold or one universal benchmark cut-point.

This note exists so later archive work can name that difference explicitly.

### 2. Treat scope vocation and anti-cosplay limits as high-confidence defaults

Some answers are now stable enough to use without apology.

The archive should treat these as default doctrine:

- micro-local bodies are for **voice, witness, stewardship, and escalation**, not miniature sovereignty;
- municipalities are the **first complete governments of ordinary life**;
- metropolitan government becomes the honest home for **shared urban systems** once those systems are real and binding;
- regions belong where there are real **territorial platforms** or real **shared-rule bargains**;
- nations are the **guarantor tier** for equal citizenship, solidarity, macro-capacity, and constitutional settlement rather than the rightful operator of every local service;
- continental bodies should remain **competence-bounded compacts**;
- global institutions should remain **narrow-waist treaty architectures** for irreducibly cross-border problems.

Those are not the frontier. They are the settled answer-key of the archive.

### 3. Keep threshold questions open and evidence-led

What remains less settled is not the vocation of each scope, but the threshold at which a design must thicken, thin, split, or move.

Examples include:

- how strong metropolitan spillovers must be before indirect co-ordination stops counting and direct legitimacy becomes the default;
- when county or sparse-area pooling is the honest answer rather than stronger municipalities or direct regional provision;
- how much platform work a region must carry before it counts as a real territorial government rather than decorative duplication;
- what mix of fiscal autonomy, formula transfer, and equalization is enough for a municipality or region to count as non-hollow;
- and when a temporary bridge body has lasted long enough that it must graduate, narrow, or die.

These should stay inside the research frontier unless the archive has named the comparator class and evidence base tightly enough to sharpen them. When later work does try to sharpen one of them, it should now use `810` rather than improvising a threshold from one vivid indicator or one admired case.

### 4. Distinguish structural questions from threshold questions

The archive should not reopen structural defaults merely because thresholds remain contested.

For example:

- it is a structural answer that a real metro needs an honest government home;
- it is a threshold question exactly when a given urban region counts as “real enough” or “binding enough” to require metro democracy now rather than later.

The same rule applies across the stack. Structural answers can stay firm while trigger points remain empirical.

### 5. Keep morphology-sensitive questions inside the frontier

`750` already establishes that there is no one-world default territorial silhouette.

The frontier therefore includes questions such as:

- how dense or dominant a capital region must be before a municipal–metro–nation stack is presumptively cleaner than a full regional ladder;
- when a sparse or archipelagic polity should prefer pooled county-style service layers rather than strong standalone municipalities;
- when federal or plurinational settlements require thicker regional shared-rule machinery than service-platform logic alone would imply;
- and when cross-border overlays should remain narrow functional compacts rather than widen into quasi-territorial governance.

These are not empty caveats. They are recurring research problems that need comparator work rather than slogan.

### 6. Keep legitimacy-thickness questions open

The archive can already say that power should not hide in bodies too thinly legitimised for what they do.

But questions such as these remain frontier work:

- when does binding planning power require direct election rather than indirect intergovernmental composition;
- when does common borrowing require stronger parliamentary scrutiny;
- when does pooled service delivery become consequential enough to require a real public chamber;
- and when does upper-tier standard-setting become intrusive enough to require thicker domestic or transnational review?

The archive should prefer a trigger grammar for those questions, not one globally uniform answer.

### 7. Keep fiscal-floor questions empirical and revisable

The archive is right to reject fiscal theater.

But the frontier remains open on matters such as:

- what minimum own-source room a municipality needs before it counts as more than a grant shell;
- what equalization design is enough to protect local self-government without collapsing solidarity upward into full operational centralism;
- what debt and reserve discipline is proportionate at metro or regional scale;
- and what common-fund size or contribution rule is enough for continental and global bodies to be real without becoming ambient upper states.

These are design questions that require repeated comparative testing, not just doctrinal confidence.

### 8. Keep intervention and handback triggers inside the frontier

`796`, `797`, and `799` already give the archive a strong intervention and temporary-arrangement grammar.

What remains frontier work is where the trigger lines should sit in different comparator classes:

- how much repeated delivery failure justifies supervision rather than support;
- when fiscal insolvency should force rescoping rather than rescue;
- when national override of a lower tier is still proportionate rather than absorptive;
- and when a repaired institution is honest enough for handback.

Those are precisely the questions most likely to be distorted by prestige, crisis rhetoric, or reviewer mood if they are not treated as empirical thresholds.

### 9. Use confidence labels in benchmark and mission work

Later archive work should not only score cases; it should also label the confidence class of the relevant doctrine.

A benchmark or mission should be able to say, in compact form:

- **default doctrine applied**,
- **context-contingent judgment applied after declared morphology / comparator fit**,
- or **frontier judgment used; threshold remains provisional and should be tracked for later recalibration**.

That lets the archive be decisive without overclaiming universal exactness.

### 10. Build frontier questions around recurring classes, not one-off curiosities

A good research frontier should collect questions that recur across cases.

Examples:

- metro-trigger thresholds,
- county-pooling thresholds,
- regional-platform thresholds,
- fiscal-floor thresholds,
- legitimacy-thickness thresholds,
- intervention / handback triggers,
- and upper-tier creep limits.

The archive should not bloat itself with one-off puzzles that cannot travel beyond a single country case.

## Use rule for the archive

When later notes need to say not just **what the ideal government by scope is**, but **how confident the archive should be about that answer in this case**, the archive should usually do these things in order:

1. **use `790` for the settled answer-key on scope vocation and anti-cosplay limits**,
2. **use `750` when polity morphology changes what whole-stack silhouette is presumptively honest**,
3. **use `798` to define the right comparator class before any threshold is treated as meaningful**,
4. **use `791` when the benchmark can already be scored on stable doctrine**,
5. **use this note when the open issue is whether the relevant cut-point is settled doctrine or still a live threshold question**,
6. **use `810` when the open problem is not whether a threshold remains frontier work, but how that threshold claim should actually be assembled into a typed dossier**,
7. **use `800` and `805` when a field mission or evidence sample is needed to sharpen that threshold rather than guess it from theory alone**,
8. **use `808` when repeated missions need calibration around the same frontier question so provisional thresholds do not drift by reviewer style**,
9. **use `793` when a frontier question should stay on a review calendar for later refinement rather than being frozen now as timeless doctrine**.

The archive should be comfortable saying both **this default is settled** and **this trigger remains under study**.

## Guardrails

- Do not reopen settled scope vocations every time a threshold question becomes difficult.
- Do not turn one vivid case into a universal trigger rule before comparator fit is declared.
- Do not hide frontier uncertainty behind falsely exact benchmark language.
- Do not treat “context matters” as permission to abandon all default doctrine.
- Do not let crisis politics convert provisional intervention thresholds into permanent centralization doctrine.
- Do not let one-country institutional folklore masquerade as a universal morphology rule.

## Failure modes

- **constitutional confidence theater** — the archive speaks as if every threshold were already universally known.
- **timid anti-doctrine** — every settled default is reopened until the archive says nothing operationally useful.
- **frontier laundering** — a provisional judgment is quietly reused as if it were default doctrine.
- **case glamour thresholding** — famous metros, federations, or compact bodies become the hidden benchmark for everyone else.
- **morphology amnesia** — trigger rules are exported across dense, sparse, archipelagic, federal, and border-interlocked polities without adjustment.
- **reviewer settlement by repetition** — a threshold looks settled only because several missions copied one another without naming the underlying uncertainty.

## Compression rule for the archive

If the archive can say **what the ideal government here is** but cannot also say **whether that answer is a settled default, a context-contingent judgment, or a live research threshold**, then it is still mixing doctrine and uncertainty in a way that will either paralyze design or harden false precision.
