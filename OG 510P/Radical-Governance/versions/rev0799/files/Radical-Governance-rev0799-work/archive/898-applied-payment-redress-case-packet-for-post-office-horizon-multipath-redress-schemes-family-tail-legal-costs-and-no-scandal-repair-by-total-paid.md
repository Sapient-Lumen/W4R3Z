# 898 — Applied payment-redress case packet for Post Office Horizon: multipath redress schemes, family tail, legal costs, and no scandal repair by total paid

## One-line thesis

The Post Office Horizon redress architecture is best classified as a multipath public redress and payment-state waist for institutional harm, wrongful convictions, shortfalls, litigation-group claimants, appeals, late applications, legal costs, family harm, and scheme closure; it should not be scored as repaired merely because total redress paid is high or one scheme reports near-completion.

## Why this matters

Horizon is a hard case for the archive because the payment record is both substantial and incomplete. Public reporting shows large cumulative payments and detailed stage data, while also showing multiple live schemes, transferred claims, pending applications, appeals, family-member expansion, legal-cost disclosure, corrections to prior figures, and new publication duties for adjacent Capture Scheme data.

The governance risk is therefore not denial of progress. The risk is **total-paid theater**. A large public number can be true and still hide the claimant who has not been invited, the claim awaiting assessment, the person considering an offer, the family member outside the first compensation design, the estate with missing records, the legal cost that consumes capacity, or the claimant whose conviction route, shortfall route, appeal route, or GLO route has different proof and timing.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Case trigger

The trigger is not merely an IT failure. It is an institutional-harm and payment-repair field created by flawed accounting data, prosecutions and shortfall claims, civil litigation, wrongful-conviction redress, scheme reopenings, appeals, family harm, and continuing public trust damage.

### 2. Form verdict

**Multipath public redress and payment-state waist.**

The honest form is neither ordinary compensation litigation nor simple public grants. It is a set of administrative redress routes with different claimant populations, proof standards, payment stages, fixed-sum routes, interim payments, legal-cost rules, and closure / transition tails.

### 3. Live payment states

The packet must keep separate:

| State | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| eligible original HSS claim | different evidentiary and timing posture from late claims |
| eligible late HSS claim | large deadline-driven backlog can survive scheme closure to new applications |
| HSSA registration | appeal / reassessment route, not merely duplicate HSS activity |
| GLO claim | litigation-group population with scheme-specific targets and closure plan |
| overturned / quashed conviction claim | criminal-harm redress and legal-status correction are not ordinary shortfall claims |
| HCRS transferred claim | successor handling after OC closure must preserve continuity and proof |
| family-member harm | derivative harm field that may not be covered by the postmaster-only payment total |
| Capture Scheme data | adjacent accounting-system harm publication path requiring separate denominators |
| legal-cost spend | scheme operation cost is not claimant redress and needs separate publication |

A claimant should never disappear because the wrong route is counted as closed.

### 4. Source waist facts

The April 2026 public redress data reported approximately £1.56 billion paid to more than 12,300 claimants across live schemes and the closed OC scheme. It separated HSS, GLO, HCRS, HSSA, and OC amounts; it also published full-and-final claim stages, accepted offers awaiting payment, claims awaiting offers, scheme delivery targets, and web-accessible tables.

The same record shows why the archive must not collapse totals into repair. HSS still had claims being assessed, offers being considered, accepted offers awaiting payment, and a deadline-driven late-application backlog. HSSA had only a small share of full claims paid. GLO and HCRS were much further advanced but still carried remaining assessment, offer, registration, or transferred-claim tails.

### 5. Redress docket

| Docket field | Horizon application |
| --- | --- |
| claimant owner | postmaster, former postmaster, GLO member, convicted claimant, family member, estate, business entity, or Capture claimant |
| public owner | DBT, Post Office Ltd, scheme administrator, HCRS / HSS / HSSA / GLO route owner, and legal-cost publisher |
| harm basis | shortfall, prosecution, conviction, civil litigation, family harm, late evidence, appeal, or Capture-era accounting allegation |
| evidence burden | institution-controlled accounting records, lost or disputed records, claim narrative, legal proof, and appeal evidence |
| status ladder | claim received, eligibility passed, full claim, offer, accepted offer, paid, interim, final, challenged, registered but not complete |
| payment state | interim, fixed-sum, top-up, full-and-final, accepted-awaiting-payment, and legal-cost/public-admin spend |
| closure tail | scheme closing date, transition into HCRS, GLO closure plan, and late support for claimants needing more time |
| family tail | family-member scheme expected to open separately and should not be hidden inside postmaster payment totals |
| publication truth | monthly scheme-level totals, corrected figures, legal-cost data, web-accessible tables, and Capture Scheme notices |

### 6. Anti-theater tests

- **Total-paid theater**: large cumulative redress does not prove all cohorts are repaired.
- **Scheme-closure theater**: closing a route to new applications does not settle pending claims, appeals, accepted-offer tails, or late support.
- **Route-confusion theater**: HSS, HSSA, GLO, HCRS, OC, family redress, and Capture must not blur into one denominator.
- **Offer theater**: offers made and offers accepted are not the same as payment received or final repair.
- **Fixed-sum pressure theater**: speed and simplicity are useful only if claimants understand alternatives.
- **Family-harm erasure**: postmaster redress totals do not automatically repair family harm.
- **Legal-cost fog**: delivery costs should not be confused with claimant redress.
- **Correction blindness**: corrected public statistics are governance evidence and should trigger versioned reporting.

### 7. Opposition brief

A fair opposition says the schemes are delivering real money at scale, that fixed-sum and interim payments can speed justice, that scheme complexity reflects heterogeneous harms, and that publication of stage data and legal-cost data is evidence of serious repair rather than theater.

The archive accepts that opposition as partly true. The verdict remains: the payment waist is real, but it must be audited through payment-state separation and cohort-specific closure proof.

### 8. Defeat / success condition

The case can be scored as repaired only when each live cohort has a closure receipt: denominator, claim state, payment state, accepted / disputed / rejected status, legal-cost tail, family tail, correction log, and route-specific explanation for remaining cases.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Do not score total public redress as completion unless each Horizon route publishes its own claimant denominator, accepted-offer tail, payment tail, legal-cost tail, transfer tail, and late-application rule.
2. Do not call a route closed while accepted-but-unpaid claims, offers-under-consideration, family claims, estates, appeals, or Capture-related publication duties remain outside the receipt.
3. Do not merge wrongful-conviction redress, HSS / HSSA claims, GLO litigation claimants, OC fixed sums, family redress, and legal costs into one moral headline.
4. Do not treat a fixed-sum or expedited route as voluntary repair unless claimants had clear alternatives, support, and nonpunitive access to fuller assessment.
5. Do not let administrative convenience shift missing-record risk back onto the harmed claimant when the institution controlled the original transaction, prosecution, audit, or accounting record.

## Holding

Horizon redress should be treated as a live multipath redress payment waist. The correct archive question is not “how much has been paid?” but “which harmed population is in which payment state, under which route, with what evidence burden, dispute route, cost tail, and final repair receipt?”
