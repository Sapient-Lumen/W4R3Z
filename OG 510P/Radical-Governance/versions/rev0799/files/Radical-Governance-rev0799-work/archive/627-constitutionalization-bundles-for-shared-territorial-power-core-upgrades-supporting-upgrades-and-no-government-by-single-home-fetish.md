# 627 — Constitutionalization bundles for shared territorial power: core upgrades, supporting upgrades, and no government by single-home fetish

## One-line thesis

Once co-operation has crossed the threshold into real shared territorial government, an ideal polity should not pretend that one institutional fix — election alone, revenue alone, law alone, map alone, platform alone, or participation alone — makes the arrangement honest; it should constitutionalize in **bundles**, starting with a minimum core of public-law, democratic, fiscal, administrative, and accountability upgrades, then docking territorial, temporal, intergovernmental, and operating bundles as the functions actually governed require.

## Why this matters

The archive now has a long and useful line of notes on shared territorial power: the threshold note on when co-operation becomes government (`606`), followed by democratic, fiscal, administrative, accountability, public-law, intergovernmental, participatory, knowledge, service, planning, capital, regulatory, contract, workforce, digital, emergency, redress, territorial, temporal, and public-communication homes (`607`–`626`). That line is strong, but it now creates a new usability problem: **how should a real reformer know which upgrades are inseparable, which are supporting, and which can wait?**

That problem matters because live reform often takes the form of **single-home fetishism**. One camp says direct election is the cure, even if the body still lacks legal powers, money, staff, records, and review. Another says own-source revenue is enough, even if the representatives remain indirect and the authority still lives on a blurry contract shell. Another says put everything in statute, even if the territory, budget, redress routes, and operating core are still borrowed. Another says a digital platform or participatory process proves legitimacy, even though the real budget and planning bargains still happen elsewhere.

The result is not full constitutionalization but **partial upgrade theater**. A polity installs one impressive institutional surface and treats the rest as implementation detail. The archive therefore now needs a compact doctrine of bundles: which upgrades are minimally co-dependent, which follow from the actual work being governed, and how to refuse reforms that constitutionalize one plane while leaving the rest in workaround form.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Treat the threshold test and the bundle test as separate questions

The threshold note (`606`) asks whether a joint body is still honest co-operation or has become real shared territorial government. The bundle doctrine asks a different question: **once the threshold is crossed, what has to upgrade together for the polity to stop governing by workaround?**

The archive should therefore reject arguments that smuggle the second question into the first. A body may clearly have become real government even before every part of its constitutional kit is repaired. The threshold is the diagnosis. The bundle is the remedy plan.

### 2. Start from one irreducible core bundle

Some upgrades are too interdependent to be treated as optional add-ons. The archive should therefore treat the following as the **irreducible core bundle** of honest shared territorial government:

- **public-law home** (`611`): the body has legal personality, named competences, and a lawful rulemaking basis;
- **democratic home** (`607`): the representative chain is intelligible and proportionate to the powers exercised;
- **fiscal home** (`608`): money is commensurate, public, and not left to annual begging or invisible side deals;
- **administrative home** (`609`): there is a real executive and records-bearing operating core;
- **accountability home** (`610`): scrutiny, disclosure, audit, complaint, and review routes exist in their own right.

If one of these is missing, the others are forced to carry load they cannot honestly bear.

### 3. Refuse the fantasy that elections alone constitutionalize power

Direct election, double legitimacy, or even a very well-composed indirectly constituted chamber can improve legitimacy. But representation alone cannot make a territorial body honest if it still lacks legal powers, its own budget spine, a minimum administrative core, or review and scrutiny routes.

The archive should therefore reject **democracy without instrumentality**: the creation of electoral legitimacy for a body that still governs by host-government dependence, contractual improvisation, or ministry discretion.

### 4. Refuse the mirror fantasy that money alone constitutionalizes power

A stable revenue base and capital discipline are necessary once a shared body is real government. But finance alone does not answer who decides, under what law, with what records, under what scrutiny, and by what review route.

The archive should therefore reject **fiscal constitutionalization by itself**. A levy, grant block, fare pool, or tax share can finance territorial power, but it cannot by itself legitimate or bound that power.

### 5. Treat territorial and temporal design as binding stabilizers, not decorative extras

Once a shared tier is real, it also needs a real territorial map (`624`) and a real time constitution (`625`). These do not necessarily precede the core bundle, but they should be treated as near-core stabilizers because they answer two questions every honest government must be able to answer: **where does this authority govern, and when is this arrangement renewed, revised, or wound down?**

Without those answers, even a body with law, money, staff, and elections can still drift through opt-in geography and silent rollover.

### 6. Treat intergovernmental design as the docking bundle that keeps the new tier from becoming a floating island

A metropolitan or regional tier does not govern in empty space. It sits between municipalities and the state and often inside wider federal, national, or treaty structures. The archive should therefore treat the **intergovernmental home** (`612`) as the docking bundle that prevents the new tier from living on summit politics, host-city mediation, or ministry favour.

In practice, this means the polity should specify consultation rights, bargaining forums, compact lanes, escalation routes, and how the shared tier appears in state decision-making before declaring the constitutional job complete.

### 7. Add operating bundles according to the real work, not as ritual completeness

Beyond the core and docking bundles, the rest of the series should be treated as **operating bundles** that switch on when a tier actually governs that field in its own right. Typical examples include:

- **service + contract + regulatory** bundles (`615`, `618`, `619`) when the tier commissions, licenses, franchises, or directly operates territorial services;
- **planning + capital + knowledge** bundles (`614`, `616`, `617`) when the tier shapes long-horizon territorial development and investment;
- **workforce + digital + public communication** bundles (`620`, `621`, `626`) when it acts as a standing administration rather than a meeting table;
- **participatory + redress** bundles (`613`, `623`) when residents and regulated actors need direct civic-entry and challenge routes at the territorial scale;
- **emergency** bundle (`622`) when the tier carries continuity, warning, mutual-aid, or recovery authority.

The archive should not require every possible operating bundle at once. It should require the ones without which the actual work is being done dishonestly.

### 8. Prefer bundles over one-shot institutional branding

A polity often tries to solve legitimacy or effectiveness by renaming an entity: “metropolitan city”, “regional authority”, “combined authority”, “special district”, “strategic council”, “integrated transport authority”. The archive should treat labels as weak evidence unless a matching bundle exists behind them.

A good name without the bundle is branding. A hard-law shell without the bundle is legal theater. A directly elected shell without the bundle is democratic theater. A well-funded shell without the bundle is fiscal theater.

### 9. Sequence bundle upgrades by dependency, not by prestige

When resources or political feasibility force staging, the archive should still prefer a dependency-aware order:

1. **threshold honesty**: admit that real shared government exists;
2. **core bundle**: law, democracy, money, administration, accountability;
3. **stabilizers and docking**: territory, time, intergovernmental wiring;
4. **operating bundles**: add the bundles the real work requires;
5. **review and completion**: publish what is still partial and what triggers the next upgrade.

This order is not a universal constitutional script, but it protects against prestige-first sequencing where the most visible reform happens first and everything load-bearing is postponed indefinitely.

### 10. Publish bundle status explicitly

The archive should prefer one visible packet for any real metropolitan or regional reform that says:

- which bundle elements are already present,
- which are being created in this reform,
- which remain temporarily borrowed,
- the sunset or review rule for each borrowed element,
- and the threshold that would force the next bundle upgrade.

Without this, partial institutionalization can masquerade as completion and the public cannot tell whether the polity has created an honest government or only a better-decorated workaround.

## Bundle matrix

| Bundle type | What it answers | Notes in archive | Why it cannot be skipped when applicable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Core bundle** | who governs, by what law, with what money, staff, records, and scrutiny | `607`, `608`, `609`, `610`, `611` | without it, the body remains a workaround even if other upgrades exist |
| **Stabilizer bundle** | where the tier governs and on what review / renewal clock | `624`, `625` | without it, power drifts through unstable geography or immortal transition |
| **Docking bundle** | how the tier sits within the wider state and among member governments | `612` | without it, the tier governs by summit, favour, or host mediation |
| **Operating bundles** | how real work gets planned, delivered, regulated, financed, challenged, communicated, and continued | `613`–`623`, `626` | without the relevant operating bundle, functional power still lives in side systems |

## Guardrails

- Do not treat one constitutional upgrade plane as proof that the whole tier has become honest.
- Do not demand every possible operating bundle before admitting the need for the core bundle.
- Do not let direct election become a substitute for powers, money, administration, or review.
- Do not let legal personality become a substitute for democratic legitimacy.
- Do not let a budget settlement become a substitute for scrutiny, disclosure, and redress.
- Do not let territorial and temporal questions remain implicit once a body governs people and places in its own right.
- Do not let borrowed elements continue indefinitely without a named review or handoff rule.

## Failure modes

- **single-home fetish**: reformers constitutionalize one plane and declare victory.
- **partial upgrade theater**: the most visible institutional surface is fixed while load-bearing dependencies remain borrowed.
- **sequencing by prestige**: election, branding, or launch politics outrun legal, fiscal, administrative, and review reality.
- **bundle denial**: actors insist that law, money, administration, and scrutiny are independent when in practice each is compensating for the others’ absence.
- **floating tier**: a shared body is upgraded internally but still lacks docking with municipalities and the state.
- **operating residue**: the constitutional shell looks serious, but the real service, contract, data, capital, or emergency power still sits in legacy bodies.

## Practical tests

A polity is using the bundle doctrine honestly when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Has it separated the threshold diagnosis from the remedy bundle?
2. Can it name which elements belong to the irreducible core bundle?
3. If it claims the body is democratic, fiscal, or legal, can it show the rest of the core rather than one highlighted plane?
4. Are territory and review clocks explicit rather than assumed?
5. Is there a visible docking design between the shared tier, municipalities, and the state?
6. Are operating bundles added according to real work rather than institutional fashion?
7. Where something remains borrowed, is the borrowing temporary, declared, and review-bound?
8. Can the public see which bundle elements are present now and which still require upgrade?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a polity says it has made shared territorial power honest because it fixed **one** thing — elected the board, passed the statute, assigned a tax, drew a map, launched a portal, or added participation — while the body still lacks the rest of the co-dependent constitutional kit, then it still has **government by single-home fetish rather than honest constitutionalization by bundles**.
