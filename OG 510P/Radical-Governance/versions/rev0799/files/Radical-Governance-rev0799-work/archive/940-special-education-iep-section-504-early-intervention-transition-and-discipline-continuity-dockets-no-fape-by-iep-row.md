# 940 — Special education, IEP, Section 504, early-intervention, transition, and discipline continuity dockets: no FAPE by IEP row

## One-line thesis

Special education is not an IEP row, a 504 plan label, a Section 618 count, or a state determination letter. A student can be identified, enrolled, assigned services, placed, disciplined, restrained, secluded, transported, transitioned, or graduated while evaluation, service delivery, accommodations, related services, least-restrictive environment, family notice, dispute resolution, and compensatory repair are still broken. A public-service packet must join authority, evaluation, plan, delivered services, accessibility, discipline, transition, data source, and remedy before calling FAPE real.

## Why this matters

The archive had education, disability, child-welfare, health-benefit, custody, and legal-aid notes, but it did not yet have a direct special-education continuity packet. That was risky because IDEA and Section 504 are not only school-policy topics; they are daily public-service systems. A missed evaluation, unavailable aide, inaccessible transport, unprovided therapy, behavior-plan failure, unrecorded restraint, school exclusion, language barrier, or transition miss can change a child’s education, health, family life, and legal posture.

The governing repair phrase is **no FAPE by IEP row**.

## Pattern pack

- **No FAPE by IEP row.** An IEP, 504 plan, eligibility code, service grid, placement row, or annual review date cannot prove that the student actually received a free appropriate public education.
- **No service by minutes grid.** Speech, OT, PT, nursing, counseling, behavioral support, transportation, assistive technology, interpreter services, and paraprofessional support must be joined to delivered-service logs and missed-service repair.
- **No inclusion by placement percentage.** Least-restrictive environment requires access to peers, curriculum, supports, accommodations, and extracurriculars, not only a percentage inside a regular class.
- **No safety by discipline code.** Suspension, manifestation determination, interim alternative placement, restraint, seclusion, law-enforcement referral, and school arrest require authority, disability nexus, notice, review, and repair.
- **No rights by notice packet.** Parent/student rights, procedural safeguards, language access, accessible formats, and consent matter only when families can use them before deadlines expire.
- **No transition by meeting held.** Part C-to-Part B, preschool, secondary transition, graduation, age-out, vocational rehabilitation, Medicaid, and adult-service handoffs require successor receipt and service continuity.
- **No compliance by aggregate data.** Section 618, SPP/APR, CRDC, NCES, and state letters are denominator and oversight surfaces; they cannot replace student-level case records.

## Continuity docket

| Ledger | What it must preserve | Bad shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Student, authority, and consent | Student identity, disability, IDEA/504/Part C authority, parent/surrogate/guardian, language, consent, responsible LEA/SEA, and rights notice. | Treating enrollment or eligibility as service. |
| Evaluation and eligibility | Referral, consent, evaluation, reevaluation, category, adverse educational impact, need for specially designed instruction or aids/services, and timelines. | Treating a label as the evaluation record. |
| IEP / 504 design | Present levels, goals, accommodations, modifications, related services, placement, LRE, assistive technology, behavior supports, ESY, and progress monitoring. | Treating the plan date as implementation. |
| Delivered services | Actual minutes, provider availability, missed sessions, substitute coverage, tele/service modality, transport, nursing, therapy, interpreter, and make-up services. | Treating a service grid as delivery. |
| Accessibility and communication | Accessible formats, language access, assistive tech, digital accessibility, school communications, family participation, and student voice. | Treating a generic notice as usable participation. |
| Discipline and safety | Removals, manifestation determination, behavior plan, FBA/BIP, restraint, seclusion, law-enforcement referral, school arrest, and return plan. | Treating discipline disposition as lawful exclusion. |
| Attendance and learning access | Attendance, shortened day, homebound, hospital/home instruction, virtual access, transportation, extracurriculars, and compensatory education. | Treating attendance enrollment as access. |
| Transition and exit | Part C/Part B transition, kindergarten, grade changes, high-school transition plan, diploma/certificate, age-out, VR/adult services, and records transfer. | Treating a transition meeting as successor receipt. |
| Dispute and remedy | IEP meeting request, prior written notice, mediation, state complaint, due process, OCR complaint, stay-put, corrective action, and compensatory services. | Treating complaint closure as repair. |
| Oversight/source posture | Section 618 data, SPP/APR determinations, CRDC, NCES, GAO, local records, source dates, data limits, and currentness. | Treating aggregate compliance as student-level FAPE. |

## Minimum joined record

A serious special-education packet should contain at least:

1. **Student and authority map.** Student, school, district, IDEA/504/Part C status, parent/surrogate/guardian, language/access needs, consent, and responsible agency.
2. **Evaluation and eligibility trail.** Referral date, consent, assessments, reevaluation clock, eligibility category, adverse-impact finding, and service need.
3. **Plan design.** IEP or 504 plan, present levels, measurable goals, accommodations, modifications, related services, placement, LRE, behavior supports, ESY, progress measures, and meeting participants.
4. **Delivery proof.** Service logs, provider assignments, transportation, assistive technology, interpreter, nursing, therapy, paraprofessional support, missed minutes, substitute coverage, and make-up services.
5. **Access and participation proof.** Accessible materials, digital tools, language access, family meeting participation, student voice, extracurricular access, and peer/curriculum access.
6. **Discipline and safety file.** Removals, manifestation determination, FBA/BIP, restraint, seclusion, law-enforcement referral, arrest, disability nexus, notice, return plan, and trauma/health follow-up.
7. **Transition trail.** Part C exit and Part B receipt, preschool/kindergarten transition, graduation or age-out plan, VR/adult-service referral, Medicaid/health handoff, and records transfer.
8. **Dispute/remedy tail.** Prior written notice, meeting request, mediation, state complaint, due process, OCR complaint, stay-put, corrective action, compensatory education/services, and implementation proof.
9. **Equity and denominator check.** Race, disability category, English learner status, sex, placement, discipline, restraint/seclusion, law-enforcement referral, and school/district denominator.
10. **Source-currentness boundary.** Mark whether evidence is student-level record, district report, state SPP/APR, federal dataset, OCR civil-rights data, annual report, GAO finding, or guidance.

## Upgrade triggers

Route the matter to the full special-education continuity docket when any of these appear:

- an IEP/504 row, service grid, enrollment code, placement percentage, eligibility category, or state determination letter is cited as proof that the student actually received services;
- evaluations are late, incomplete, disputed, language-inaccessible, or disconnected from current needs;
- related services, transportation, assistive technology, nursing, counseling, interpreter services, behavior supports, or paraprofessional support are unavailable or undocumented;
- a student has repeated removals, shortened day, homebound placement, restraint, seclusion, law-enforcement referral, school arrest, or disability-related discipline;
- the student is transitioning between Part C/Part B, preschool, school levels, districts, foster care, custody, hospitalization, graduation, age-out, VR, Medicaid, or adult services;
- parents, students, advocates, or representatives face notice, language, deadline, meeting-access, stay-put, complaint, or due-process barriers.

## Downshift conditions

A lighter summary is acceptable only when the question is purely aggregate and no student-specific service, accommodation, discipline, transition, health, family, deadline, or remedy consequence is live. Once an individual consequence appears, district, school, family, provider, complaint, OCR, and state records must be joined.

## Failure modes

- **No FAPE by IEP row:** a plan exists but services, accommodations, or placement are not delivered.
- **No inclusion by percentage:** LRE is reduced to a placement number without curriculum, peer, and support evidence.
- **No therapy by service grid:** related-service minutes are scheduled but missed or undocumented.
- **No safety by discipline code:** removals, restraint, seclusion, or police referrals are treated as neutral events.
- **No participation by procedural safeguards:** families receive paper rights without language, accessible format, meeting access, or usable deadlines.
- **No transition by exit status:** a student is exited, graduated, or aged out without successor-service receipt.
- **No compliance by state determination:** an SPP/APR determination hides local/student-level failure.

## Audit questions

- Who owns the child’s current education, accommodation, transport, health, behavior, and transition duties?
- What was evaluated, what was planned, what was delivered, what was missed, and what was repaired?
- Did parents and students actually understand and use notice, consent, meeting, complaint, stay-put, and appeal routes?
- Are discipline, restraint, seclusion, and school-police consequences joined to disability nexus, behavior supports, trauma, and return-to-learning?
- Does the packet separate aggregate federal/state data from student-level proof?

## Source posture

Use IDEA Section 618, annual reports, SPP/APR determinations, Section 504 guidance, CRDC, NCES, and GAO dispute-resolution findings as source-specific evidence lanes. Do not collapse them into a single proof that a student received FAPE, accommodations, services, access, safety, or remedy.
