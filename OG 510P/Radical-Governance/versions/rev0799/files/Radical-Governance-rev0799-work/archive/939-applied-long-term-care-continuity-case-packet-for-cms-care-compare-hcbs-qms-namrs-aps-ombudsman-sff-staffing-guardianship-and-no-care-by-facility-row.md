# 939 — Applied long-term care continuity case packet for CMS Care Compare, HCBS QMS, NAMRS/APS, ombudsman, SFF, staffing, guardianship, and no care by facility row

## One-line thesis

Long-term care governance fails when the proof surface is a facility row, star rating, staffing standard, HCBS enrollment, APS report, ombudsman count, guardian order, or discharge notice. The applied repair is a joined continuity packet that proves resident-level care, community-living support, rights, abuse response, transfer/discharge safety, and remedy.

## Why this matters

This packet operationalizes note 938. It uses official CMS, ACL, Medicaid, HHS OIG, GAO, DOJ, and ombudsman/APS surfaces to show why a long-term care claim must preserve person-level and setting-level evidence. The packet is deliberately not a general elder-care essay: it is a test harness for nursing homes, HCBS, APS, ombudsman access, guardianship, discharge, staffing, SFF status, and emergency relocation.

The governing repair phrase is **no care by facility row**.

## Pattern pack

- **CMS nursing-home data are denominators, not person-level safety.** A provider row, bed count, rating, or inspection-data pause needs resident, staffing, survey, complaint, and incident joins before it can support a care claim.
- **Special Focus Facility status is an escalation flag, not durable repair.** Poor-performing facilities can cycle through oversight without lasting operational improvement unless ownership, staffing, corrective action, and resident outcomes are joined.
- **Staffing policy is not staffing reality.** A final rule, repeal, exemption, or state staffing rule must be joined to daily shift coverage, acuity, missed care, and emergency coverage.
- **HCBS QMS is a quality spine, not community-living proof.** Standardized measures need person-centered plans, delivered hours, backup staff, housing, transport, and institutional-risk data.
- **APS/NAMRS is a reporting spine, not safety completion.** Adult maltreatment data reveal investigations and program characteristics, but live safety requires case-level risk, services, legal action, and recurrence prevention.
- **Ombudsman programs are rights routes, not automatic remedy.** Complaint investigation, verification, resolution, resident access, retaliation safety, and systemic advocacy must be visible.
- **Guardianship is a control surface, not protection by default.** The archive needs scope, accounting, visitation, medical/property decisions, complaints, and restoration route.

## Source-to-duty map

| Source surface | What it proves | What it cannot prove alone |
|---|---|---|
| CMS nursing-home provider data / Care Compare | Provider, bed, rating, staffing, quality, and inspection-data surface. | Resident-level medication, staffing, rights, complaint, discharge, or emergency safety. |
| CMS staffing final rule and HHS repeal | Federal policy conflict over staffing baselines, transparency, and rural/Tribal access. | Actual staffing on a shift, acuity fit, or missed care. |
| CMS facility-initiated discharge memo | Federal recognition that improper discharges remain frequent complaints and need mitigation. | Whether a resident’s transfer/discharge was lawful or safe. |
| HHS OIG SFF report | SFF program weakness and lack of lasting improvement for many graduates. | Whether a specific facility is safe today without current survey/resident records. |
| HHS OIG emergency preparedness report | Disaster/emergency preparedness challenges, especially staffing and transportation. | Actual evacuation capacity, receiving beds, medication, or return plan. |
| Medicaid HCBS QMS / 2024 guidance | Standardized Medicaid HCBS quality measurement and reporting direction. | Delivered hours, provider adequacy, backup care, housing, or person-level community choice. |
| ACL NAMRS and APS rule | National APS data/reporting system and first federal APS regulations. | Case-level safety, protective services, recurrence prevention, or financial protection. |
| ACL LTC Ombudsman Program | Statutory rights route for residents’ health, safety, welfare, and rights. | Timely complaint access, resolution, and retaliation-safe remedy in a particular case. |
| GAO guardianship report and DOJ guardian abuse page | Guardianship data limits and fiduciary-abuse risk. | Whether a guardian’s authority is least-restrictive, accounted for, and reviewable. |

## Applied continuity ladder

1. **Identify the setting and authority.** Facility, assisted-living/residential setting, HCBS, hospital discharge, guardian/fiduciary, payer, and responsible agency.
2. **Separate aggregate from case proof.** Mark whether a source is a dataset, rule, survey, complaint, APS investigation, court order, facility record, or person-level care record.
3. **Prove care delivery.** Join assessment, care plan, delivered services, medications, staffing, missed care, restraints, infection control, and resident/family observations.
4. **Prove rights and autonomy.** Join informed consent, communication, visitation, privacy, resident council, accommodations, grievance, ombudsman access, and retaliation protection.
5. **Prove abuse/exploitation response.** Join APS intake, facility incident, survey, law enforcement, guardian/fiduciary accounting, protective service, and recurrence check.
6. **Prove transfer/discharge continuity.** Join notice, lawful basis, appeal, destination, medication, records, transport, receiving-care confirmation, and follow-up.
7. **Prove HCBS community continuity.** Join authorized hours, delivered hours, provider capacity, backup care, housing, transportation, technology, caregiver support, and institutional-risk mitigation.
8. **Prove emergency continuity.** Join facility/community hazard, staffing, transportation, receiving beds, records, medication, power/temperature, infection control, family notice, and return/closure tail.
9. **Prove oversight tail.** Join survey/enforcement, ombudsman complaint, APS case, court review, DOJ/GAO/OIG findings, corrective action, and public communication.
10. **Preserve source-currentness.** Mark policy volatility, dataset update pauses, phase-in/repeal status, and local record requirements.

## Applied examples

### CMS Care Compare and nursing-home provider data

Use CMS provider data as an entry point for facility identity, bed/rating/staffing surfaces, and public comparison. Do not treat the provider row or rating as resident-level truth. It must be joined to current survey results, staffing shifts, complaint logs, incident reports, medical records, transfer/discharge files, ombudsman contacts, and resident/family testimony.

### SFF and poor-performing facilities

SFF status is a red flag, not a cure. HHS OIG’s SFF findings show why the packet must ask whether the facility changed operations, ownership/accountability, staffing, care plans, incident response, and resident outcomes rather than merely graduating from a program.

### Staffing rule / repeal conflict

The archive should not litigate the staffing policy debate as doctrine. It should treat both the 2024 final rule and 2025 repeal as source-currentness and implementation-risk surfaces. The live continuity question is actual coverage for the acuity of residents and participants.

### HCBS QMS and community living

HCBS QMS is a valuable standardized measurement spine. It does not prove that a person received hours, found a worker, had backup coverage, kept housing, could travel, could avoid hospitalization or institutionalization, or controlled daily life.

### APS/NAMRS and ombudsman programs

NAMRS and APS rules give the archive a national APS data and rule spine. Ombudsman materials give it a resident-rights complaint route. Neither proves case closure without safety plan, services, legal action, financial protection, complaint resolution, and recurrence checks.

### Guardianship and fiduciary control

GAO and DOJ elder-justice materials make guardianship a high-risk control surface. Every guardianship packet should ask for scope, less-restrictive alternatives, accounting, fees, visitation, medical consent, property decisions, complaints, and restoration review.

## Repair surfaces

- resident/participant, setting, payer, facility/HCBS, guardian/representative, agency, and rights-notice map;
- assessment, acuity, care plan, medication, dementia/behavioral health, therapy, diet, infection-control, and missed-care record;
- actual staffing, weekend/night coverage, home-care worker availability, training, agency staffing, acuity fit, and backup-care docket;
- APS intake/investigation, facility incident, law enforcement, protective services, financial protection, recurrence, and closure proof;
- ombudsman complaint access, verification, resolution, retaliation-safe communication, and systemic escalation;
- transfer/discharge notice, lawful basis, appeal, destination, medications, records, transport, receiving-care proof, and follow-up;
- guardian/fiduciary scope, accounting, fees, visitation, medical/property decisions, complaints, and restoration route;
- emergency preparedness, evacuation transport, receiving beds, medication/records, generator/power, infection control, family notice, return, and closure tail.

## Anti-theater tests

- Do not cite a nursing-home row, rating, or SFF status as care without resident-level care and rights evidence.
- Do not cite staffing rule, repeal, or hours as safety without shift, acuity, and missed-care proof.
- Do not cite HCBS enrollment as community living without delivered hours, provider capacity, backup care, housing, transport, and choice evidence.
- Do not cite APS intake or NAMRS data as protection without case-level services and recurrence prevention.
- Do not cite an ombudsman program as remedy without complaint access, verification, resolution, and retaliation safeguards.
- Do not cite guardianship as protection without least-restrictive scope, accounting, review, and restoration route.
- Do not cite transfer/discharge or emergency plan as continuity without destination, records, medication, transport, and follow-up.

## Source posture

This case packet uses U.S. official and primary public-interest surfaces current enough for a continuity packet as of rev0752. It must not be used as a substitute for live state licensing, Medicaid waiver, APS, ombudsman, guardianship-court, facility, hospital-discharge, or person-level records.
