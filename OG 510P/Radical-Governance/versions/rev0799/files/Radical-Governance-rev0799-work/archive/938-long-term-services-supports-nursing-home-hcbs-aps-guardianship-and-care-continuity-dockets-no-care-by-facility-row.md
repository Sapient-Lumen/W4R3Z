# 938 — Long-term services and supports, nursing-home, HCBS, APS, guardianship, and care-continuity dockets: no care by facility row

## One-line thesis

Long-term care is not a place code. A person can be in a nursing facility, assisted-living setting, home- and community-based services slot, adult protective services investigation, ombudsman complaint, guardianship, hospital discharge queue, emergency relocation, or family-care arrangement while the official surface merely says active resident, enrolled participant, discharged, protected, or placed. A public-service packet must join setting, authority, staffing, care plan, medication, rights, abuse/exploitation response, transfer/discharge, community-living supports, guardian/fiduciary scope, emergency continuity, and remedy before calling care real.

## Why this matters

The archive had disability, health-benefit, child-welfare, custody, housing, and utility-continuity packets, but lacked a direct long-term services and supports waist. That was risky because older adults and people with disabilities often cross Medicaid, Medicare, state licensing, APS, guardianship courts, facility ownership, ombudsman programs, hospitals, home-care agencies, family caregivers, emergency management, and housing. A five-star row, facility census, HCBS enrollment, APS intake, guardian order, or discharge form can be true while safety, autonomy, medication, staffing, access, and remedy have failed.

The governing repair phrase is **no care by facility row**.

## Pattern pack

- **No care by facility row.** A bed, star rating, certification row, occupancy count, or chain name cannot prove that the resident receives safe, rights-respecting care.
- **No community living by HCBS slot.** HCBS enrollment does not prove provider availability, person-centered planning, paid hours, backup staff, transportation, housing, or freedom from institutional pressure.
- **No protection by APS intake.** Adult protective services reporting and investigation surfaces do not prove safety planning, services, legal action, financial protection, or repeat-harm prevention.
- **No resident rights by ombudsman existence.** Ombudsman authority matters only if residents have timely access, complaint investigation, retaliation-safe communication, and remedy follow-through.
- **No autonomy by guardianship order.** A court order or fiduciary title does not prove least-restrictive scope, accounting, visitation, medical-consent legitimacy, or rights restoration review.
- **No discharge by transfer notice.** Facility-initiated discharge, hospital-to-nursing-facility transfer, closure relocation, and emergency evacuation require destination, medication, records, appeal, transport, and receiving-care proof.
- **No safety by staffing policy.** Staffing rules, repeals, exemptions, or reported hours must be joined to actual shifts, acuity, call lights, medication passes, infection control, and emergency coverage.

## Continuity docket

| Ledger | What it must preserve | Bad shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Person, setting, and authority | Resident/participant identity, legal status, admission basis, payer, facility or HCBS setting, responsible agency, guardian/representative, and rights notice. | Treating a facility row or HCBS enrollment as care. |
| Care plan and clinical continuity | Assessment, acuity, ADLs/IADLs, medications, nursing, therapy, behavioral health, dementia, pain, diet, infection control, and care-plan changes. | Treating a care-plan date as care delivery. |
| Staffing and workforce | RN/aide coverage, agency staff, weekend/night staffing, home-care worker availability, backup plans, training, language, and acuity adjustment. | Treating staffing policy or reported hours as safe staffing. |
| Rights, consent, and communication | Resident council, visitation, family contact, interpreters, disability accommodations, privacy, restraints, chemical restraint, informed consent, and grievance. | Treating posted rights as usable rights. |
| APS and elder-justice response | Abuse, neglect, exploitation, self-neglect, mandated reporting, investigation, risk assessment, protective services, law enforcement, and recurrence. | Treating intake or substantiation as safety. |
| Ombudsman and complaint remedy | Resident access, complaint coding, verification, resolution, retaliation protections, systemic advocacy, and closure proof. | Treating ombudsman program existence as remedy. |
| Transfer, discharge, and relocation | Notice, permissible basis, appeal, destination, bed, receiving provider, medication, records, transport, family notice, and follow-up. | Treating discharge notice as safe transfer. |

## Minimum joined record

A serious LTSS packet should contain at least:

1. **Person and setting map.** Identity, payer, facility/HCBS setting, admission basis, service authorization, representative/guardian, responsible agency, and rights notice.
2. **Assessment and plan.** Acuity, ADLs/IADLs, cognition, behavioral health, medications, therapy, nursing, diet, fall/pressure-injury risk, infection risk, and care-plan revisions.
3. **Staffing reality.** Reported and actual shifts, weekend/night coverage, turnover, agency staff, training, acuity fit, home-care worker availability, backup plan, and missed visits.
4. **Rights and autonomy.** Consent, visitation, family/representative access, resident council, privacy, language/disability accommodations, restraints/chemical restraints, grievance, and retaliation route.
5. **Abuse and exploitation response.** APS report, facility incident report, law-enforcement referral, risk assessment, protective services, financial controls, recurrence check, and closure reason.
6. **Ombudsman complaint tail.** Complaint access, complaint code, verification, facility response, remedy, unresolved issue, systemic escalation, and retaliation follow-up.
7. **Transfer/discharge proof.** Notice, lawful basis, appeal, destination, receiving-care acceptance, records, medications, transportation, family notice, and follow-up after arrival.
8. **HCBS continuity.** Authorized hours, delivered hours, provider network, backup worker, transportation, housing, technology, caregiver support, emergency backup, and institutional-diversion proof.
9. **Guardianship/fiduciary accountability.** Order scope, least-restrictive alternatives, accounting, fees, visitation, medical/property decisions, complaint route, and restoration review.
10. **Source-currentness boundary.** Which source is a rating, dataset, federal rule, state/local case record, complaint, audit, or rights route; never collapse them into one proof of care.

## Upgrade triggers

Route the matter to the full LTSS continuity docket when any of these appear:

- nursing-home rating, SFF status, chain ownership, staffing rule, staffing repeal, or facility census is cited as proof of care quality;
- a resident faces transfer, discharge, eviction-like relocation, facility closure, hospital discharge to post-acute care, emergency evacuation, or return from evacuation;
- APS, ombudsman, law enforcement, licensing, survey, guardian, or fiduciary surfaces are cited as proof that abuse, neglect, exploitation, or self-neglect has been resolved;
- HCBS, Money Follows the Person, waiver services, home care, or community-living supports are cited as proof that institutionalization risk has been removed;
- medication, dementia care, restraints, staffing, infection control, disability accommodation, power/heat/cooling, transportation, or family contact are material to safety.

## Downshift conditions

A lighter summary is acceptable only when the question is purely aggregate and no resident-specific safety, autonomy, medication, discharge, rights, APS, guardianship, HCBS, emergency, or remedy consequence is live. Once an individual consequence appears, facility, HCBS, APS, ombudsman, court, survey, and local service records must be joined.

## Failure modes

- **No care by facility row:** facility presence, certification, occupancy, rating, or chain ownership substitutes for person-level care.
- **No safety by star rating:** a quality surface substitutes for staffing, medication, incident, complaint, survey, and resident-rights facts.
- **No community by waiver slot:** HCBS enrollment substitutes for provider capacity, delivered hours, backup care, housing, transport, and choice.
- **No protection by APS intake:** a report or investigation substitutes for safety planning and recurrence prevention.
- **No autonomy by guardian:** a guardian order substitutes for least-restrictive authority, accounting, review, and rights restoration.
- **No discharge by notice:** a transfer/discharge notice substitutes for receiving-care and medication continuity.
- **No emergency safety by plan binder:** emergency compliance substitutes for transport, beds, records, staff, power, and return/closure proof.

## Audit questions

- What setting, payer, legal authority, representative/guardian, and responsible agency control the person’s current care?
- What care was authorized, what was delivered, and what was missed?
- Who is responsible for medication reconciliation, controlled substances, behavioral-health treatment, dementia care, restraints, infection control, and hospital transfer?
- How can the resident or participant reach family, counsel, ombudsman, APS, court, and grievance channels without retaliation?
- If a facility initiates transfer or discharge, what is the lawful basis, appeal route, destination, records package, medication bridge, and arrival proof?
- If care is HCBS, what provider capacity, backup plan, housing, transport, and emergency supports prevent institutionalization?
- If a guardian or fiduciary is involved, what is the scope, accounting, visitation, complaint route, and restoration review?

## Source posture

This docket is U.S.-anchored because the current source stack is strongest for CMS, ACL, HHS OIG, GAO, DOJ, Medicaid, APS, ombudsman, and nursing-home data. It should later receive non-U.S. comparators for social care, capacity law, guardianship, and facility closure, but those should not delay the immediate continuity packet.
