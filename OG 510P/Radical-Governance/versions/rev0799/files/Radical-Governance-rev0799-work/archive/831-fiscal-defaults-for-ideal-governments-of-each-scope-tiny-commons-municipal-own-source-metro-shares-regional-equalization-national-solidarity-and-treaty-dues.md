# 831 — Fiscal defaults for ideal governments of each scope: tiny commons, municipal own-source, metro shares, regional equalization, national solidarity, and treaty dues

## One-line thesis

The archive should answer the question **how should each scope ordinarily be financed?** with scale-matched fiscal defaults rather than one treasury fetish: micro-local bodies should live on tiny discretionary funds, member contributions, and visible small grants rather than debt or payroll empires; municipalities should be the first level with meaningful own-source revenue, rule-bound transfers, ordinary operating reserves, and capital access for durable local assets; metropolitan governments should use assigned revenue shares, formula transfers, and user-charge lanes where systems justify them rather than annual pleas from member municipalities; regional and provincial governments should combine own revenues, shared taxes, equalization, and platform-scale capital budgets; national government should carry the broad tax base, macro-stabilization, and solidarity machinery; and continental or global bodies should usually rely on treaty dues, assessed contributions, and tightly bounded common finance rather than counterfeit world taxation or donor-whim constitutions.

## Why this matters

The archive can already answer **what each scope is for** (`790`), **which regime form usually fits it** (`825`), **from where authority should draw its mandate** (`826`), **how long it should last** (`827`), **how it should ordinarily end** (`828`), **how offices should be chosen** (`829`), and **what decision rule usually governs once the body is real** (`830`). It also already has deeper work on fiscal chains for cross-boundary forms in `814`, public budgeting and grants in older finance notes, and benchmark cards that keep asking whether fiscal congruence is real rather than theatrical.

What it still lacked was one short answer-key note for the narrower but recurring question:

**once the archive knows what authority should exist at a scope, how should that scope ordinarily get the money that lets it act honestly?**

That missing layer matters because many constitutional designs fail not at the level of chamber diagrams but at the level of money grammar.

A neighborhood body is praised as participatory, then quietly expected to run professional services with no money or staff. A municipality is called autonomous but depends so completely on earmarked transfers that it cannot really govern the place it supposedly owns. A metropolitan authority is declared essential, yet financed through annual bargaining among mayors so its public decisions are always hostage to inter-municipal cash politics. A region receives a legislature and ministries but no stable revenue or equalization logic, so it becomes a decorative map with a begging bowl. National government, meanwhile, either hoards every buoyant tax base and starves the stack below, or abdicates solidarity while pretending local self-government can survive on narrow local tax bases alone. Upper-tier treaty bodies swing between fake moral universalism and actual dependence on whichever states or donors feel like paying this year.

The archive should therefore add one compact doctrine of **fiscal fit by scope**. Fiscal design should track the real vocation of the tier, the breadth of the risk pool, the visibility of the public affected, the need for equalization, the difference between operating and capital money, and the honesty of delegated finance where the body is not in fact a general sovereign government.

## Scope table

| Scope | Default fiscal base | Why this fit is usually best | What it must not become |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Micro-local** | tiny discretionary fund, member dues where appropriate, participatory micro-grants, in-kind stewardship support | keeps local voice real without pretending the block or village is a miniature treasury state | debt-bearing pseudo-municipality or unfunded mandate theater |
| **Municipal** | meaningful own-source taxes and charges, rule-bound general transfers, ordinary operating reserves, lawful capital borrowing | gives the first full government of daily life money it can actually plan with | shell autonomy financed mainly by earmarked pleas |
| **Metropolitan** | assigned revenue shares, formula transfers, network user charges where honest, and capital lanes for trunk systems | matches real urban-system power with a real urban-system treasury | summit government financed by annual municipal hostage bargaining |
| **Regional / provincial / state** | mixed own revenues, shared taxes, equalization, multi-year platform budgets, bounded borrowing | fits large service systems and territorial shared rule without forcing every resource fight to the centre | decorative region with ministries but no real fiscal title |
| **National** | broad tax base, macro-stabilization capacity, national equalization, social-insurance and guarantee pools, sovereign debt management | carries solidarity, redistribution, rights floors, and national shock absorption | everything-treasury that starves the rest of the stack |
| **Continental / plurilateral** | treaty dues, formula contributions, bounded common programmes, and explicit capital or guarantee lanes only where the treaty says so | keeps cross-border co-operation real without smuggling in an ambient upper state | quiet tax union by drift or donor-captured compact |
| **Global** | assessed contributions, chartered budget classes, bounded voluntary funds as supplements, and tightly named special assessments where authorized | matches delegated global authority to delegated finance | fake world tax, empty mandate without money, or government by donor whim |

## Pattern pack

### 1. Separate six fiscal questions before choosing instruments

The archive should not let every finance question collapse into **“what tax should this level have?”** Ideal governments should usually separate at least six fiscal questions:

- what funds **ordinary operations**;
- what funds **capital and long-lived assets**;
- what corrects **territorial asymmetry or unequal fiscal capacity**;
- what absorbs **contingency and shock**;
- what money may be **earmarked** and what must remain discretionary;
- and which money moves require a **second key** rather than executive habit.

Those lanes solve different constitutional problems. A polity that muddles them will eventually use capital debt to hide operating shortfalls, use earmarked grants to erase self-government, or use contingency money as a permanent substitute for a real budget constitution.

### 2. Keep micro-local finance tiny, legible, and non-sovereign

At neighborhood, block, village, ward, or similarly intimate scale, the archive should usually prefer small money rather than proud money.

That usually means some mix of:

- tiny discretionary budgets;
- member dues or contributions where the body is genuinely associative;
- formula micro-grants from the municipality;
- participatory budgeting slices for highly local upkeep;
- and in-kind support such as meeting space, clerking, or maintenance help.

The archive should distrust any micro-local design that needs meaningful debt, complex payroll systems, a thick procurement state, or broad taxation powers merely to do work that is mostly about witness, stewardship, prioritization, and escalation.

### 3. Make municipalities the first real treasury home of ordinary life

Municipalities are usually the first scale where a broad everyday service portfolio becomes honest: streets, sanitation, local planning administration, permitting, parks, libraries, local facilities, and many direct civic interactions. They therefore need a fiscal base that is not merely symbolic.

The archive should usually prefer a municipal money constitution built from:

- **meaningful own-source revenue** rather than pocket-money taxes;
- **rule-bound general transfers** rather than pure discretion from above;
- **charges and fees** for services where pricing is honest and non-exclusionary;
- **operating reserves** proportionate to volatility;
- and **capital borrowing access** for long-lived local assets under lawful discipline.

The point is not that every municipality should fund itself alone. It is that municipalities should be the first level where autonomy can survive ordinary budget season.

### 4. Treat metropolitan finance as a public treasury question once metropolitan power is real

A metropolitan body that plans transport, allocates housing obligations, manages major flood or utility systems, or runs trunk infrastructure cannot honestly live on improvised municipal dues forever.

The archive should therefore usually prefer some combination of:

- assigned revenue shares;
- formula transfers tied to metropolitan functions;
- user charges where the system genuinely supports them;
- explicit subsidy for public-service obligations that charges cannot cover;
- and capital lanes sized to long-horizon network assets.

The key rule is simple: **if the power is metro-wide, the money constitution should also be metro-wide**. Annual begging from member municipalities is not fiscal federalism; it is confederal drift.

### 5. Regional and provincial governments need real fiscal title plus equalization, not only administrative mass

Regions, provinces, states, and other substantial subnational governments usually sit where major service systems and territorial bargains meet: hospitals, higher education platforms, watershed and corridor infrastructure, regional transport, land and ecological systems, or federal shared rule.

The archive should therefore usually prefer a mixed regional finance model that includes:

- own-source revenues or meaningful rate-setting power;
- shared taxes or revenue-sharing formulas;
- equalization to correct large interregional disparities where they matter;
- multi-year budget horizons for platform-scale systems;
- and bounded borrowing authority for durable assets.

A region with ministries but no stable revenue or equalization logic is usually not a real government. It is an administrative costume over a central treasury monopoly.

### 6. National government should carry the broadest risk pool and solidarity machinery without swallowing local autonomy

The archive should treat national finance as the place for the broadest and most buoyant tax bases, macro-stabilization, social insurance, national equalization, and the guarantee institutions that keep equal citizenship real across place.

That means national government should usually carry:

- the main redistributive and stabilization instruments;
- equalization across weaker local and regional governments;
- national guarantee funds and insurance pools where local risk-bearing would be cruel or unstable;
- and sovereign debt management for truly national obligations.

But the archive should resist the idea that because the nation can raise the broadest revenue, it therefore ought to control every ordinary place-facing budget. National fiscal strength should underwrite the stack, not erase it.

### 7. Continental and plurilateral finance should be bounded, formula legible, and treaty explicit

Between nation and globe, the archive should usually prefer bounded compact finance rather than ambient upper-state finance.

That usually means:

- member dues or formula contributions;
- tightly named programme funds;
- explicit cost-sharing for shared agencies, corridors, or common infrastructure;
- and any borrowing, guarantee, or common-resource lane spelled out in treaty text rather than improvised through emergency creativity.

The archive should distrust compact bodies that accumulate redistributive or debt-like power without ever naming the second fiscal key that authorizes it.

### 8. Global finance should remain delegated and assessed unless there is an openly authorized constitutional breakthrough

At global scale, the archive should usually begin from delegated finance rather than sovereign finance.

That means the honest default is usually:

- assessed contributions apportioned among member states for chartered budget classes;
- bounded special assessments for named mandates where the founding texts permit them;
- voluntary contributions as supplements rather than the whole operating constitution for core functions;
- and strong publication discipline so outsiders can tell which global tasks are actually financed and which are merely announced.

The archive should reject two opposite failures at once: the fantasy that the present world already has a legitimate general tax sovereign, and the opposite fantasy that global institutions can do real work with no stable money constitution at all.

### 9. Earmarking should narrow as the government becomes more general-purpose

The archive should usually tolerate more earmarking at narrow and delegated levels than at broad territorial levels.

- Micro-local grants can be highly specific.
- Bounded authorities can have highly typed revenue and spending lanes.
- Metropolitan finance may still contain substantial functional earmarks where the body remains system-focused.
- Regional and especially municipal and national governments need a larger zone of discretionary budgeting if they are to be politically honest general-purpose governments.

An upper tier that provides money for everything only through tightly earmarked channels is not really granting self-government. It is purchasing compliance.

### 10. Borrowing should usually follow asset life and revenue honesty, not political impatience

Across the stack, the archive should usually prefer borrowing rules that track long-lived assets and visible repayment capacity rather than election cycles or wishful accounting.

That means:

- no meaningful micro-local debt as a default;
- municipal and metropolitan borrowing mainly for durable assets with public benefit beyond one budget cycle;
- regional borrowing tied to platform-scale infrastructure and long-lived systems;
- national debt used for macro-stabilization, national investment, and shock absorption inside an intelligible constitutional and budget framework;
- and upper-tier treaty borrowing only where the authorizing text, risk-sharing map, and backstop obligations are overt rather than folkloric.

### 11. Publish the equalization rule or the real constitution will become side payment and resentment

Equalization is not an embarrassment to be hidden in ministerial folklore. It is part of the money constitution whenever weaker places cannot otherwise deliver the floor the polity claims to guarantee.

The archive should therefore prefer explicit, reviewable equalization rules over quiet patronage, ad hoc rescue, or donor-theater. That is true inside nations, often true inside regions, and sometimes true inside metropolitan bodies where burdens and tax base are sharply misaligned.

### 12. Require one short fiscal holding in every applied scope verdict

When later archive work uses `811` or `791` to judge a real polity, it should normally be able to add one short holding such as:

- **the honest municipal design exists on paper but lacks own-source revenue and general transfers proportionate to its workload**;
- **the metro now has enough real system power that annual municipal contributions are no longer an honest default**;
- **the region’s platform responsibilities now require published equalization and capital rules rather than central case-by-case bargaining**;
- or **the treaty body’s core mandate is not honestly financed because voluntary funds have replaced assessed or formula-based contributions for ordinary operations**.

That keeps applied verdicts from stopping at the words **“autonomous”**, **“metropolitan”**, or **“treaty body”** without naming the real money grammar underneath. Where the unresolved issue is not only the revenue chain but asset ownership and liability residue, use `822` alongside this note. Where the unresolved issue is not only the money but who can scrutinize it, use `816` and the audit line notes alongside this note.

## Diagnostic questions

1. Is this scope financed in a way that matches its actual vocation, or is the money grammar still attached to an older and smaller role?
2. Can the body plan ordinary operations without annual pleading to another tier?
3. Are capital, equalization, contingency, and ordinary operating money clearly separated, or are they being laundered through one another?
4. Does earmarking fit the narrowness of the body, or is it suffocating a genuinely general-purpose government?
5. Where territorial inequality is real, is there an explicit equalization rule or only hidden patronage?
6. Could an outsider tell who ultimately bears the debt, guarantee, or rescue burden if revenues fail?
7. At upper tiers, is the core mandate funded by assessed or formula logic, or by the whim of whichever members or donors pay first?

## Guardrails

- Do not give micro-local bodies fiscal grandeur they cannot administer honestly.
- Do not call a municipality autonomous if almost all meaningful money arrives through earmarked discretion.
- Do not make a metropolitan authority govern shared systems on annual municipal hostage finance.
- Do not create regional legislatures and departments without giving them a visible fiscal title.
- Do not let national equalization collapse into ministerial favoritism.
- Do not let treaty bodies accumulate broad spending or debt powers without openly naming the authorizing second key.
- Do not treat voluntary contributions as a stable substitute for core assessed finance where the mandate is meant to be standing.
- Do not hide operating weakness through capital gimmicks, one-off grants, or off-book vehicles.

## Failure modes

- **unfunded-localism**: the smallest units are romanticized and then abandoned with responsibilities they cannot finance.
- **shell autonomy**: a municipality holds elections and committees but lives on centrally earmarked oxygen.
- **metro hostage finance**: a real metropolitan government survives through annual bargaining among incumbent municipalities.
- **decorative regionalism**: a region receives ministries, symbols, and elections but not the money constitution of a real platform government.
- **solidarity inversion**: national government keeps the broad tax base but refuses honest equalization, leaving weaker places to fail publicly.
- **treaty-budget drift**: continental or plurilateral bodies accumulate real distributive power without visible treaty finance authority.
- **donor-whim globalism**: global institutions announce standing duties but rely on voluntary money for core functions.
- **capital laundering**: debt, leases, or special vehicles are used to hide operating deficits or political conflict.

## Compression rule for the archive

If the archive can name **what authority should exist at a scope, who should choose it, how long it should last, and how it should vote**, but cannot also name **what ordinary fiscal base belongs there, where equalization sits, where capital sits, and which money moves require a second key**, then the design still has **institutions without an honest money constitution**.
