# 811 — Scope verdict packets for ideal governments of each scope: settled holdings, frontier thresholds, benchmark findings, repair routes, public proof, and no applied judgment by scattered cross-reference

## One-line thesis

When the archive uses its ideal-governments-by-scope stack on a **real polity, proposal, or case study**, it should emit one compact **scope verdict packet** that bundles the settled scope holding, any live threshold holdings, the benchmark finding, material stress or maintenance modifiers, the active repair or intervention path, and the public-proof class — so later applied judgments stay portable, comparable, and compact instead of dissolving into essay recap or a trail of scattered note citations.

## Why this matters

`790` now gives the archive a short answer key for what ideal governments at each scope should generally be. `791` gives benchmark cards for judging whether a real arrangement matches that stack in ordinary time. `792` and `793` say how that arrangement should be stress-tested and maintained. `794` says how a polity should move toward the answer in sequenced steps. `795` says what outward proof should remain public. `796` and `797` say how failure, objection, and review should be handled. `798` says what peer class counts before scoring begins. `809` and `810` distinguish settled scope doctrine from live threshold questions and then say how those threshold claims should be assembled. `800` through `808` now give the archive a full review method, verdict grammar, repair docket, close-out discipline, evidence spine, mission-independence rule, and calibration pack. `812` through `823` now also let the packet carry compact holdings on form choice, legitimacy, fiscal chain, administrative chain, oversight, participation, information, membership, decision rules, disputes, assets, and the commercial perimeter when those questions are still live in the case.

What the archive still lacked was one compact object for the **applied output** of that whole run.

That gap matters because the archive is now strong enough to do more than theorize. It can judge a city-region proposal, a county-scale pooling debate, a regional-platform claim, a federal settlement, or a compact-body redesign with much sharper discipline than before. But without one verdict object, those judgments tend to fail in three recurring ways.

The first is **scatter-reference governance**: the reader is told to combine `790`, `791`, `798`, `810`, `801`, `794`, and `795` mentally, with no small packet showing the actual current holding.

The second is **essay relapse**: every applied case restates the whole constitutional theory before finally saying whether the arrangement is honest, underbuilt, mis-scoped, frontier, or mid-repair.

The third is **verdict drift**: one note records the benchmark result, another the threshold uncertainty, another the corrective path, and another the public summary, but nobody can later say which of those objects is the live applied constitutional judgment.

Official practice supports a tighter method. The OECD Recommendation on Public Policy Evaluation emphasizes systematic use of evaluation with quality, clarity, and institutional fit rather than loose report production. OECD peer-review practice likewise uses explicit analytical frameworks, findings, and recommendations rather than hoping readers will reconstruct the judgment from raw materials. WHO’s Joint External Evaluation model similarly combines assessed capacities, identified gaps, and priority actions in one reusable mission output rather than splitting the practical judgment across unrelated documents. OECD open-government guidance also supports publishing outward-facing, usable summaries that make institutional claims inspectable rather than leaving them trapped in insider files. The archive should compress those lessons into one rule for this domain: **when a real place or proposal is judged against the ideal-governments stack, the result should travel as one compact verdict packet.**

## Pattern pack

### 1. Use a scope verdict packet only for applied judgments

A scope verdict packet is not the archive’s first-principles design object.

Use:

- `790` for the short answer-key,
- `791` for the benchmark card,
- `798` for comparator discipline,
- `810` for frontier threshold dossiers,
- `800` through `808` for the method, evidence spine, and repair chain,
- and this note when the archive needs to say **what its current applied judgment actually is for one real polity, proposal, or case**.

The packet is therefore the archive’s preferred **judgment bundle**, not another theory note.

### 2. Start with one settled holding from the answer key

Every packet should begin by stating the archive’s settled holding in one sentence.

Examples:

- **This urban region should be judged against the metropolitan-government benchmark, not against stand-alone municipalities.**
- **This sparse territory should be judged against the county-pooling / intermunicipal-backbone expectation rather than against metro-style territorial thickening.**
- **This region should still be treated as a platform question, not presumed a full constitutional substate by label alone.**

This field keeps the verdict anchored in the settled doctrine rather than in the noise of the case.

### 3. Separate frontier threshold holdings from settled doctrine

If the case turned on a live threshold question, the packet should name that separately.

Examples:

- metro threshold **clearly crossed**,
- regional-platform threshold **borderline**,
- fiscal-floor threshold **probably not yet met**,
- legitimacy-thickness threshold **crossed for borrowing and land-use powers but model choice still open**.

Each threshold holding should cite the relevant dossier status and confidence class rather than pretending the threshold question never existed. Where the threshold is accepted but the design answer is still contested, the packet should also carry a short institutional-form holding under `812` — for example thin connector, shared-service backbone, bounded authority, or general-purpose tier — instead of leaving model choice implied. Where form choice is already settled but composition or ratification design is still live, the packet should also carry a short legitimacy-chain holding under `813` so later case work does not hide who sits in the room or which decisions require a second key. Where the remaining open question is how the form is actually financed, the packet should also carry a short fiscal-chain holding under `814` so later case work does not hide shadow treasury design behind abstract form language. Where the unresolved issue is who actually employs, operates, keeps the record home, or carries continuity, the packet should also carry a short administrative-chain holding under `815` so later case work does not hide borrowed bureaucracy behind institutional labels. Where the unresolved issue is who actually owns strategic assets, carries debt, books reserves, bears contingent obligations, or takes the handback if the arrangement changes, the packet should also carry a short asset-and-liability-chain holding under `822` so later case work does not hide off-book sovereignty behind neat institutional labels. Where the unresolved issue is whether procurement, concessions, franchises, PPPs, or managed-service agreements are still honest delivery tools for the form or have become constitutional escape hatches, the packet should also carry a short contracting-boundary-chain holding under `823` so later case work does not hide commercial thickening behind neat institutional labels. Where the unresolved issue is who scrutinizes, audits, hears complaints, supervises legality, or provides final judicially legible remedy, the packet should also carry a short oversight-chain holding under `816` so later case work does not hide self-marking power behind neat organizational charts. Where the unresolved issue is what public-input chain belongs to the body or decision — open dockets, hearings, user panels, deliberative mini-publics, or response duties —, the packet should also carry a short participation-chain holding under `817` so later case work does not hide consultation theater behind otherwise tidy institutional design. Where the unresolved issue is where the shared facts of the body actually live — common indicators, service telemetry, case or record homes, official-statistics links, method notes, or publication discipline —, the packet should also carry a short information-chain holding under `818` so later case work does not hide dashboard sovereignty behind otherwise tidy institutional design. Where the unresolved issue is who belongs to the body, what legal and functional territory count, how accession or amendment should work, when exit is honest, or how the body would dissolve or hand back its work, the packet should also carry a short boundary-and-membership-chain holding under `819` so later case work does not hide constitutional map change behind otherwise tidy institutional design. Where the unresolved issue is what ordinary vote, qualified-majority, unanimity-reserve, quorum, ratification, deadlock, or emergency grammar honestly belongs to the body, the packet should also carry a short decision-rule-chain holding under `820` so later case work does not hide procedural constitutions behind otherwise tidy institutional design. Where the unresolved issue is what kinds of disputes the body should distinguish, where they enter, how they escalate, or what continuity order survives while they are live, the packet should also carry a short dispute-resolution-chain holding under `821` so later case work does not hide conflict routing behind otherwise tidy institutional design.

### 4. Fix the territorial unit and comparator cohort in the packet itself

A verdict is not usable if the reader still has to guess what territory was judged or which peers counted.

The packet should therefore name:

- the legal unit,
- the functional unit where different,
- the morphology class,
- and the comparator cohort or exclusion logic.

This is a compact restatement of `798`, not a full replay of it. The point is that the live verdict should carry its comparison frame with it.

### 5. Express the benchmark judgment lane by lane, then give one overall class

The packet should not collapse all judgment into one blended score.

It should state the key lane findings — usually vocation fit, minimum complete kit, fiscal and capability congruence, spillover or solidarity fit, review integrity, participation proportionality, and tripwires — and then classify the case overall using the archive’s typed verdict grammar.

Typical overall classes may include:

- **meets the benchmark**,
- **conditionally meets**,
- **underbuilt**,
- **mis-scoped**,
- **ornamental / shadow form**,
- or **frontier / not yet settled** where the threshold question dominates.

### 6. Carry material stress and maintenance modifiers without replaying the whole drill file

A case may score acceptably in calm conditions but fail badly under stress or neglect.

The packet should therefore include a short modifier field when `792` or `793` is materially relevant, such as:

- **ordinary-time benchmark met, but stress tests show command blur under flood events**,
- **conditionally meets, but maintenance backlog and overdue review clocks lower confidence**,
- **frontier metro claim strengthened by repeated emergency workarounds that have not been constitutionalized**.

This prevents calm-weather benchmark results from floating free of operational reality.

### 7. Include the active repair, transition, or intervention route

A verdict packet should say not only what the arrangement is, but where it sits in the repair chain.

That can include:

- no active repair,
- sequenced upgrade underway,
- benchmark defects with management response open,
- close-out pending verification,
- intervention ladder active,
- temporary special measures in force,
- or handback review underway.

This keeps the verdict from freezing the case at diagnosis while the real constitutional story has already moved into repair, transition, or contestation.

### 8. State the publication class explicitly

Because `795` distinguishes what should be published outwardly from the deeper evidence room, the packet should carry one publication-class field.

Examples:

- **public summary publishable in full**,
- **public summary plus restricted annex**,
- **internal working verdict pending independent review**,
- **public benchmark and action ledger live; evidence room partly protected**.

That stops the archive from confusing the live internal verdict with the public-safe proof surface.

### 9. Require one review clock and one status date

A scope verdict packet should always say:

- when the verdict was reached,
- what status it currently holds,
- and when it must be refreshed or what trigger reopens it early.

Without those fields, packets become stale constitutional stickers instead of living judgments.

### 10. Distinguish provisional, operational, and settled packet states

The archive should use a small state discipline for verdict packets.

- **Provisional** — evidence still incomplete, contradiction residue is high, or an independent mission has not yet closed.
- **Operational** — the packet is the live working judgment for action and publication, but review or repair remains open.
- **Settled** — the packet has survived review, contradiction handling, and any declared observation window strongly enough to count as the current archive position.

This keeps applied judgments honest about maturity.

### 11. Keep the packet small enough to cite whole

The packet should usually fit in one compact table or short list.

If it grows into several pages, the archive should break the extra material back out into the threshold dossier, finding register, corrective-action ledger, or proof pack that already exists elsewhere. The packet is the citation-ready judgment object, not the whole case file.

## Guardrails

- Do not let the packet replace the deeper threshold dossier, evidence register, or action ledger it depends on.
- Do not describe a live threshold question as settled merely because a benchmark result has been written.
- Do not issue a packet without naming the territory and comparator cohort.
- Do not publish one headline grade without lane findings, confidence, or contradiction residue where material.
- Do not let repair status disappear once the benchmark has been scored.
- Do not confuse a public-safe packet with the whole evidence room.
- Do not leave packet state and review clock implicit.

## Failure modes

- **scattered cross-reference verdict** — the judgment exists only as a chain of citations to many other notes.
- **essay relapse** — each applied case retells the full constitutional theory before stating the actual holding.
- **false settledness** — live threshold disputes are quietly flattened into benchmark language.
- **snapshot without trajectory** — the packet records a score but not whether repair, intervention, or handback is underway.
- **public / internal blur** — no one can tell whether the packet is a public-safe summary, an internal working judgment, or a final archive position.
- **stale packetism** — an old judgment continues to circulate after its review clock has expired.

## Practical tests

A scope verdict packet is honest when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Does it state the settled answer-key holding for the case in one sentence?
2. Does it separate any live threshold holding from the settled doctrine?
3. Where model choice is live, does it name the institutional-form holding rather than leaving it implicit?
4. Where composition or ratification design is live, does it name the legitimacy-chain holding?
5. Where shadow-treasury design is live, does it name the fiscal-chain holding?
6. Where borrowed bureaucracy, shell administration, or records-home ambiguity is live, does it name the administrative-chain holding?
7. Where ownership, debt, reserves, contingent obligations, or handback residue are live, does it name the asset-and-liability-chain holding?
8. Where commercial perimeter or contract escape is live, does it name the contracting-boundary-chain holding?
9. Where permit, inspection, or sanction design is live, does it name the regulatory-and-enforcement-boundary-chain holding?
10. Does it name the territory and comparator cohort inside the packet itself?
11. Does it state lane findings and an overall typed benchmark class?
12. Does it carry any material stress, maintenance, repair, or intervention modifier rather than pretending the case is static?
13. Does it say what can be published outwardly and what remains in deeper evidence rooms?
14. Does it carry a status date, packet state, and review clock?
15. Is it short enough that later archive notes can cite the packet instead of rebuilding the whole judgment from scratch?

## Compression rule for the archive

If the archive can tell you **which answer-key note, comparator note, threshold dossier, benchmark card, finding register, repair ledger, and proof-pack rule are relevant**, but cannot also show **one compact packet saying what its actual current judgment is for this place or proposal**, then it still has **a strong scope-assessment stack without a reusable applied verdict object**.
