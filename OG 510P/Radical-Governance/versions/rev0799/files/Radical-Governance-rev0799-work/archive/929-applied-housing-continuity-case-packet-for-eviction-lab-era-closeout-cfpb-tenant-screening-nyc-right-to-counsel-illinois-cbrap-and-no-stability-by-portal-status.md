# 929 — Applied housing-continuity case packet for Eviction Lab, ERA closeout, CFPB tenant screening, NYC Right to Counsel, Illinois CBRAP, and no stability by portal status

## One-line thesis

The current U.S. housing-continuity case shows why eviction filings, rental-assistance closeout, tenant-screening enforcement, right-to-counsel implementation, court-based rental assistance, and homelessness counts must be joined: no household is stable merely because a portal, docket, program page, report, or screening record says so.

## Why this matters

This packet uses the housing sources that are hardest to finish later because they are time-sensitive and fragmented. Eviction Lab gives a monitored-sites filing denominator and a real-time tracking surface. Treasury shows that the federal ERA2 performance period has ended, making local and court-based alternatives more important and more variable. NLIHC shows the application-status problem for ERA programs. CFPB and FTC materials make tenant-screening accuracy a housing-access issue rather than a consumer-reporting side quest. NYC Right to Counsel and Comptroller materials show that a formal representation mandate can still be constrained by provider capacity and implementation. Illinois CBRAP shows a court-linked assistance route that only matters when it changes the court and ledger state. HUD AHAR gives the homelessness consequence surface when housing continuity fails.

The packet is deliberately not a national law summary. It is a continuity form: a way to read any eviction / assistance / screening / shelter case without letting one surface masquerade as the whole outcome.

## Pattern pack

1. **Eviction Lab gives the exposure denominator.** Filing counts and filing rates are an essential early-warning surface, but they do not by themselves prove judgment, possession, informal eviction, move-out, shelter entry, or re-housing outcome.
2. **ERA closeout makes local bridges more important.** When federal ERA2 award authority is no longer available for renter assistance, the live question becomes which state, local, court-based, philanthropic, or housing-authority route can actually stop a case or stabilize a household.
3. **NLIHC application status is a routing clue, not a guarantee.** A searchable rental-assistance database helps identify potential routes, but closed portals, limited funding, local eligibility, and landlord / court effects remain separate proof states.
4. **Tenant-screening enforcement belongs inside the housing case.** Inaccurate or incomplete eviction records can prolong the housing search, produce extra application fees, and deny housing even after the original case is corrected.
5. **Right to Counsel is an implementation test.** The relevant evidence is not only the law; it is outreach, intake, full representation, adjournments, provider capacity, settlement quality, case outcome, and stable-housing result.
6. **Court-based rental assistance is a joined-waist example.** Illinois CBRAP requires eviction-court posture and can cover arrears, court costs, and future rent; its value depends on payment recipient identity, ledger posting, and the court case clock.
7. **Homelessness counts are consequence evidence, not repair.** AHAR and local shelter data make displacement visible, but a housing-continuity docket must still show the path from court / assistance failure into shelter, unsheltered status, rehousing, or recurrence.
8. **The repair object is a household and unit timeline.** The timeline runs from arrears or notice through assistance search, court filing, screening risk, representation, payment posting or failure, possession / dismissal / move-out, shelter / relocation, and re-housing tail.

## Applied state ladder

- **Household and unit state:** household members, lease / occupancy, subsidy, rent amount, landlord / property manager, plaintiff / payment recipient, language, disability, authorized helper, safety and retaliation issues.
- **Arrears and assistance state:** arrears ledger, emergency rental assistance route, application, document status, eligibility, award, denial, landlord acceptance, payment issue, payment posting, residual balance, future-rent bridge.
- **Court and possession state:** notice, service, filing, case number, hearing, default, adjournment, settlement, judgment, warrant, execution / lockout, dismissal, appeal, sealing / expungement, informal move-out.
- **Representation state:** outreach, intake, advice, brief service, full legal representation, adjournment request, language access, disability accommodation, provider capacity, counsel continuity.
- **Tenant-screening state:** report source, eviction / criminal / credit entries, disposition, sealed / dismissed case treatment, adverse action, consumer disclosure, dispute, correction receipt, reapplication effect.
- **Shelter and relocation state:** emergency placement, motel / shelter / rapid rehousing, transport, storage, school and job continuity, disability accommodation, safety, pets, permanent housing search.
- **Outcome and learning state:** stable housing, safe relocation, shelter entry, unsheltered status, repeat filing, debt / judgment / collections, screening correction, subgroup denominator, local rule change.

## Failure modes

- **Portal approval without possession protection:** assistance can be approved while a hearing, default, judgment, or warrant proceeds.
- **Payment without ledger or court effect:** money can be obligated or sent but not posted to the right plaintiff / landlord / account, leaving arrears, court costs, or future rent unresolved.
- **Representation promise without counsel at the event:** an entitlement to counsel can fail when providers lack capacity, tenants are not reached, or the court clock outruns intake.
- **Filing count without displacement outcome:** a dataset can show the front door of court while missing informal evictions, executed warrants, shelter entry, and later denials.
- **Tenant-screening correction without housing repair:** a corrected report may not recover lost application fees, time, temporary-housing costs, or a specific denied unit.
- **Shelter metric without re-housing tail:** shelter entry, bed availability, or PIT counts do not show whether the person returns to stable housing.

## Minimum test packet

A complete applied record must show: tenant / household / unit identity; arrears ledger; notice and service clock; assistance application and portal state; eligibility, award, payment, landlord acceptance, posting, and residual balance; court case and possession state; representation state; tenant-screening adverse-action and correction route; shelter / relocation bridge; appeal / sealing / expungement / debt tail; and public metrics that separate filing exposure, legal representation, assistance posting, possession outcome, screening harm, and homelessness consequence.

## Anti-theater core

No stability by portal status. No eviction outcome by filing count. No right to counsel by statute. No housing access by tenant-screening report. No payment by award notice. No possession protection by call-center assurance. No shelter continuity by referral. No equity by citywide rate. No re-housing by case closure.

## Source posture

This case should cite Eviction Lab for monitored filing patterns and ETS currentness, Treasury and NLIHC for rental-assistance program status, CFPB / FTC for tenant-screening accuracy and consumer-reporting harms, NYC sources for right-to-counsel implementation, Illinois CBRAP for court-linked assistance, GAO for eviction-data limits, and HUD AHAR for homelessness consequence counts. Treat each as a partial source class that needs joining before the archive says housing stability was achieved.


## Rev0786 affected-person pilot status

Rev0786 does not treat this official-source packet as complete household proof. It adds note `984`, `HC-11`, and `HC-12` because a portal state, eviction filing, counsel mandate, assistance award, payment record, screening page, or shelter count is not yet evidence that a household remained safely and durably housed.

The next household sample must preserve at least one path from arrears or notice through application, court posture, counsel at the operative event, ledger posting, possession or lockout status, informal move-out if any, shelter or re-housing tail, tenant-screening correction or adverse action, reapplication effect, and follow-up stability. It must also account for people who never reached the portal, counsel intake, hearing, payment route, or screening-dispute process. Until that exists, this packet remains an official-source and operational-test packet, not field validation.
