# 795 — Public proof packs for ideal governments of each scope: benchmark publication, stress summaries, maintenance ledgers, transition boards, and no constitutional claim without outward evidence

## One-line thesis

The archive should pair its answer-key, benchmark, stress-test, maintenance, and upgrade-path notes with one compact **public proof-pack rule** for each scope: **publish the minimum outward evidence that lets residents, auditors, legislatures, journalists, courts, and peer polities verify what the arrangement is, how it scored, what failed under stress, what is due next, and where any transition actually stands**.

## Why this matters

`790` now gives the archive a compact answer key for what ideal governments at each scope should generally be. `791` gives a short benchmark card for judging whether a real constitution or proposal matches that stack in ordinary time. `792` asks whether the arrangement remains honest under shock. `793` says how that judgment should recur on a public calendar rather than by sporadic panic. `794` says how a real polity should move toward the target stack in phased and reversible steps. `796` says what outward intervention board should appear once repeated failure has crossed from diagnosis into compelled repair, special measures, handback, or formal rescoping. `799` now adds the narrower rule that temporary bridges, pilots, derogations, and special regimes should also appear on one outward ledger with expiry, renewal, graduation, or burial state visible.

What the archive still lacked as one compact rule was the outward-facing side of that stack. A polity can have elegant benchmark cards, disciplined drill calendars, and carefully staged upgrade pathways on paper, yet still leave the public with no stable way to see the live score, the last stress result, the open repair items, the next review date, or the current transition phase. In that condition, governance knowledge remains trapped in committee decks, consultant workspaces, ministry folklore, and one-off press lines.

That gap matters because good constitutions do not only allocate power. They make institutional claims testable. `497` already says where public knowledge should live by scope. `765` already says people need a real right to request official information. This note adds the missing bridge: the archive should expect **proactive publication of a minimum proof pack** so multi-level governance can be judged without forcing every resident to reconstruct the constitution through document scavenger hunts. Later notes should now also treat `800` as the place to go once the missing question is not only **what outward proof should be published** but **what compact trace of review method, evidence lanes, and contradiction handling should sit behind that proof pack**. They should now also treat `801` as the place to go once the missing question is **how the published finding itself should be typed — lane by lane, with confidence, severity, and unresolved contradiction residue rather than one blended headline verdict**. They should now also treat `802` as the place to go once the missing outward object is **the public-safe management-response ledger showing response posture, named owner, deadlines, and overdue status rather than only the finding itself**. They should now also treat `803` as the place to go once the missing outward object is **the later close-out state — verified closed, closed with monitoring tail, reopened, or escalated — rather than only the original action plan**. They should now also treat `811` as the place to go once the missing outward-facing object is **the compact applied verdict packet that bundles settled doctrine, threshold holdings, benchmark findings, repair state, and publication class for one real place or proposal rather than only one component of that chain**.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Treat public proof as part of the constitutional surface, not as optional communications polish

If a government claims that a scope arrangement is legitimate, benchmarked, maintained, resilient, or in orderly transition, the archive should expect one outward-facing proof surface showing the minimum facts behind that claim. A private memo or internal dashboard may support administration, but it is not enough to carry a public constitutional assertion on its own.

### 2. Publish one stable benchmark snapshot for the current ordinary-time judgment

When `791` is used, the polity should publish one compact benchmark snapshot that shows:

- which scope is being judged,
- which archetype or target stack applies,
- the latest score or classification,
- the main strengths,
- the open defects,
- the accountable owners,
- and the next refresh date.

The archive should reject benchmarking that exists only as expert admiration, private scoring notes, or a slogan about reform progress.

### 3. Publish one stress summary whenever the arrangement is drilled or seriously tested by events

When `792` is used, the polity should publish one compact stress summary saying:

- which shock family was tested or experienced,
- which essential services held,
- where command or escalation blurred,
- what legality, publication, and review duties remained live,
- what changed immediately after the event,
- and whether any rescoping or handback review was triggered.

The archive should reject resilience claims that depend on hero stories without a durable public incident summary. Later notes should now also treat `797` as the place to go when the missing outward fact is not the benchmark or intervention record itself, but the live objection, stay, remand, or handback-challenge posture attached to that record.

### 4. Keep one maintenance ledger live between formal reviews

When `793` is used, the polity should keep one always-on maintenance ledger that shows:

- the current benchmark status,
- the last completed drill,
- open follow-up items,
- named owners,
- deadlines,
- overdue items,
- trigger overrides already activated,
- and the next scheduled review points.

Without such a ledger, maintenance becomes a sequence of ceremonial launches and forgotten PDFs.

### 5. Keep one transition board live whenever the arrangement is being upgraded, merged, handed back, or rescaled

When `794` is used, the polity should publish one transition board showing:

- the current phase,
- what still sits with the old holder,
- what has moved,
- what proof gates are complete,
- what blockers remain,
- what fallback applies if the phase fails,
- and where residents should go right now for service, review, and accountability.

The archive should reject transitions that are visible only to insiders while the public is asked to trust that cutover is “on track.”

### 6. Match proof-pack thickness to scope and risk

The archive should not demand the same outward pack from every level.

A micro-local steward body may only need minutes, a tiny budget statement, a visible escalation log, and next meeting notice. A municipality may need a fuller benchmark sheet, service and finance baselines, complaint clocks, and drill follow-up. A metropolitan or regional authority may need shared-system maps, formulas, capital commitments, and intergovernmental dependency notes. National, continental, and global layers may need stronger publication on equalization, treaty reporting, compliance, implementation status, and methodological comparability.

The discipline is therefore **minimum honest outward proof**, not one giant disclosure template for the whole stack.

### 7. Publish methods, classifications, and denominators, not only headline scores

A proof pack should show enough method for an outsider to understand what is being claimed.

That usually means publishing the scoring rubric or classification rule, the date of judgment, the unit of analysis, the relevant thresholds, the baseline or comparator where one exists, and any material caveat. Otherwise a “green” score, a resilience label, or a transition milestone becomes branding rather than evidence.

### 8. Separate the proof pack from the raw record, but keep the crosswalk visible

The outward pack should be compact. It is not the full archive.

But it should link or point clearly to the underlying record homes: minutes, budgets, legal acts, evaluation reports, drill records, audit findings, treaty reports, or implementation logs. That lets the archive keep the public surface small while preserving a path to deeper inspection.

### 9. Do not confuse proactive proof with the right to request records

A published proof pack does not replace `765`. People should not have to file a request for the basic constitutional facts about which level holds which powers, what the latest benchmark found, whether a drill failed, or when the next review is due.

But neither should a proof pack be treated as full compliance with access-to-information law. It is the front door for legibility, not the ceiling of disclosure.

### 10. Make privacy, security, and diplomatic limits explicit instead of using them as a general blackout excuse

Some details should remain protected: personal data, live vulnerabilities, operational secrets, and negotiation confidences in bounded form.

The archive should still expect the polity to publish the existence of the check, the category of withheld material, the lawful reason for withholding, the review route where one exists, and the public-safe remainder. The proof pack should get thinner where necessary, not disappear.

### 11. Keep the proof pack canonical, versioned, and easy to cite

A scope arrangement should not have five mutually inconsistent public descriptions spread across portals, slide decks, mayoral speeches, and archived press releases.

The archive should prefer one canonical outward page or register entry per live arrangement, with a visible last-updated date, retained prior versions where material changes matter, and stable references for citation. This keeps benchmark refresh, drill follow-up, and transition status from dissolving into screenshot governance.

### 12. Tie nonpublication to escalation, not to polite disappointment

If a polity repeatedly fails to publish the minimum proof pack for an arrangement it says is benchmarked, maintained, resilient, or mid-transition, the archive should treat that as a substantive warning.

The consequence may be:

- a benchmark downgrade,
- a maintenance failure flag,
- a transition stop-go hold,
- a trigger for audit or legislative scrutiny,
- or in serious cases a reopened scope review under `632`.

Otherwise publication duty becomes etiquette rather than constitutional discipline.

## Proof-pack grammar

| Field | What the public pack should say | Typical proof objects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Live arrangement** | what scope-form is currently in force | archetype label, legal basis, current holder map |
| **Current judgment** | how the arrangement most recently scored or classified | benchmark snapshot, benchmark date, rating or typed finding |
| **Stress position** | what the last serious drill or shock showed | stress summary, incident note, after-action link |
| **Maintenance status** | what is open between formal reviews | maintenance ledger, owners, deadlines, overdue items |
| **Transition status** | where reform stands if the arrangement is changing | phase board, gate checklist, cutover or rollback note |
| **Evidence crosswalk** | where the deeper record lives | minutes, budgets, audit reports, legal acts, evaluation files |
| **Method note** | how the judgment was made | rubric, threshold, unit of analysis, caveats |
| **Next clock** | when the next public check is due | next benchmark date, drill date, review date |
| **Challenge route** | how outsiders can contest or inspect the claim | complaint lane, access request route, review body |

## Typical proof packs by scope

| Scope | Minimum outward proof pack |
| --- | --- |
| **Micro-local** | meeting notice, minutes, tiny budget or stewardship spend, unresolved escalation items, next meeting or review date |
| **Municipal** | benchmark snapshot, service and budget basics, complaint and review routes, latest drill summary where relevant, maintenance ledger, named accountable offices |
| **Metropolitan** | shared-system map, member allocation or finance formula, benchmark snapshot, incident or drill summaries for region-wide systems, transition board where powers are still staged |
| **Regional / provincial** | platform responsibilities, major service and capital baselines, interterritorial allocation notes, benchmark and stress summaries, open repair ledger |
| **National** | rights-floor and equalization position, benchmark and resilience summaries for major territorial arrangements, emergency-publication continuity, cross-level maintenance and reform board |
| **Continental / plurilateral** | mandate statement, member implementation scoreboard, common-fund or programme status, compliance and review outputs, amendment or review-conference clock |
| **Global** | treaty reporting status, alerting and assessment outputs, methodological notes, member-reporting comparators, compliance or review forum outputs, next reporting cycle |

## Use rule for the archive

When later notes ask not only **what ideal government by scope should look like**, **how it benchmarks**, **how it behaves under stress**, **how it is maintained**, or **how it should be upgraded**, but also **what must be publicly visible so outsiders can verify those claims**, the archive should usually do ten things in order:

1. **use `790` for the target stack**,
2. **use `791` for the current ordinary-time judgment**,
3. **use `792` for shock-weather performance**,
4. **use `793` for the standing review rhythm and open follow-up**,
5. **use `794` for the phased transition state when the arrangement is changing**,
6. **use `796` when the arrangement is under support, supervision, special measures, or handback and needs a public intervention board rather than only a transition board**,
7. **use `799` when the missing proof surface is the live temporary-arrangements ledger showing start date, next expiry, renewal posture, graduation test, or burial state for a bridge, pilot, derogation, or special regime**,
8. **use `497` for where the wider public knowledge infrastructure should live by scope**,
9. **use `765` when the issue is the request right or review lane for hidden records rather than proactive publication**,
10. **use `801` when the missing task is to express the judgment as a typed finding register rather than as a single headline result**,
11. **use this note to state the minimum public proof pack and outward evidence duty**.

The archive should not force readers to choose between atmospheric constitutional prose and a full forensic reconstruction of the state’s hidden working papers.

## Guardrails

- Do not publish only the headline score while hiding the owner, defects, and next review date.
- Do not treat a press release, speech, or FAQ as the canonical proof surface.
- Do not use privacy or security as a blanket excuse for eliminating all outward evidence.
- Do not let transition boards hide the current public front door, accountable holder, or challenge route.
- Do not make residents file information requests for the minimum facts needed to understand the live scope arrangement.
- Do not let a compact proof pack become an excuse to destroy or withhold the deeper record.

## Failure modes

- **constitutional black box** — the polity claims benchmarking, resilience, or reform progress but publishes no usable outward evidence.
- **headline-without-method** — the score or label is public, but the unit, threshold, and caveat structure remain hidden.
- **dashboard constitutionalism** — attractive visuals substitute for legal basis, ownership, evidence crosswalk, or challenge routes.
- **transition by rumor** — powers and responsibilities are moving, but only insiders can tell what phase is actually live.
- **access-by-scavenger-hunt** — the necessary proof exists only across scattered minutes, budget files, and buried reports.
- **security-blackout laundering** — genuine protection needs are cited to justify suppressing even safe, aggregate, or process-level publication.

## Practical tests

A public proof-pack discipline passes when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Can an outsider identify the live scope arrangement and legal basis from one canonical public surface?
2. Can they see the latest benchmark judgment, the main open defects, and the next refresh date without filing a records request?
3. Can they see what the last serious drill or shock revealed and what follow-up remains open?
4. If the arrangement is mid-transition, can they tell the current phase, current front door, and next gate?
5. Can they follow the proof pack back to the deeper record homes, methods, and challenge routes?

## Compression rule for the archive

If the archive wants to say that a government-by-scope arrangement is not only well designed, benchmarked, stress-tested, maintained, or being upgraded, but also **publicly legible**, it should usually be able to do so with one short proof pack naming the live arrangement, the current judgment, the latest stress position, the maintenance status, the transition state if any, the evidence crosswalk, the method note, the next clock, and the challenge route. If it cannot, the publication claim is still too atmospheric to count as a stable archive judgment.
