# 936 — Detention, corrections, health, release, and reentry-continuity dockets: booking, custody, medication, deaths, IDs, housing, and no liberty by custody row

## One-line thesis

Custody is not a single state. A person can be booked, held pretrial, sentenced, transferred, medically fragile, awaiting counsel, earning credits, missing medication, under a detainer, release-eligible, homeless at discharge, or re-confined by supervision while the official row merely says inmate, detainee, prisoner, resident, released, or supervised. A public-service packet must join legal custody, health care, safety, death/injury reporting, communication, release calculation, identity documents, Medicaid, housing, and supervision before calling liberty, safety, or reentry real.

## Why this matters

The archive already has a scope note for detention, corrections, and reentry, but it lacked an applied continuity waist. That gap was risky because custody status can simultaneously sever health care, counsel, family contact, benefits, identity documents, housing, employment, school, child/family ties, voting, and release timing. Aggregate jail or prison counts are essential denominators, but they do not prove that an individual was legally held, safely housed, medically treated, counted in death or injury records, released on time, or reconnected to community services.

The governing repair phrase is **no liberty by custody row**.

## Pattern pack

- **No legal custody by booking row.** Booking, transfer, hold, detainer, warrant, sentence, pretrial order, parole/probation violation, and release authority must be separated before custody is treated as lawful or current.
- **No jail safety by headcount.** Jail population counts do not prove intake screening, suicide prevention, withdrawal care, violence control, disability accommodation, sanitation, food, or medical access.
- **No prison safety by classification.** Security level, risk score, or housing assignment cannot substitute for protection from violence, excessive force, sexual abuse, segregation harm, and untreated illness.
- **No health continuity by sick-call log.** Medication, MOUD, mental health, disability, pregnancy, chronic disease, hospital transfer, prior authorization, and release prescription must be joined to actual care.
- **No death accountability by annual statistic.** Death-in-custody records need person, facility, event, cause, investigation, family notice, public reporting, and corrective-action tails.
- **No release by release date.** Sentence credits, First Step Act credits, parole, detainers, transportation, property, prescriptions, IDs, benefits, housing, and supervision conditions can make a date non-executing.
- **No reentry by referral.** Medicaid, ID documents, appointments, treatment, RRC/home-confinement capacity, housing, employment, family reunification, and supervision rules must be operational, not merely referred.

## Continuity docket

Use ten ledgers before accepting a custody or reentry claim.

| Ledger | What it must preserve | Bad shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Legal custody and hold state | Arrest, booking, charge, sentence, pretrial order, detainer, warrant, transfer authority, credits, parole/probation status, and release authority. | Treating a custody row as lawful authority. |
| Intake and acute-risk triage | Suicide, withdrawal, medication, disability, pregnancy, age, language, victimization, mental health, and emergency medical risk. | Treating booking completion as intake safety. |
| Conditions and protection | Housing unit, staffing, violence, contraband, use of force, sexual abuse, segregation, sanitation, food, ADA, grievances, and monitoring. | Treating jail count or classification as safe custody. |
| Health and medication continuity | Medical records, chronic disease, MOUD, mental health, medication administration, hospital transfer, pregnancy care, disability supports, and release medication. | Treating sick call as health care. |
| Death, injury, and serious incident | DCRA/MCI record, incident narrative, cause, investigation, family notice, public denominator, and corrective action. | Treating annual mortality data as incident accountability. |
| Communication, counsel, and family | Attorney access, phone/video/mail, family notice, interpreter, disability access, grievances, retaliation-safe reporting, and child/family contact. | Treating account access as meaningful contact. |
| Release calculation and execution | Credits, good time, FSA, parole board, detainers, transport, property, prescriptions, documents, and discharge time. | Treating projected release date as release. |
| Benefits, IDs, and health coverage | Medicaid/CHIP/Marketplace/VA, ID, Social Security card, birth certificate, address, phone, appointments, records, and consent. | Treating application or referral as continuity. |
| Housing, work, treatment, and supervision | RRC/home confinement, shelter/housing, employment, education, treatment, supervision conditions, fees, curfews, electronic monitoring, and violation route. | Treating reentry referral as stable community placement. |
| Oversight and public data | BJS jail/prison/community-supervision counts, MCI/DCRA data, DOJ findings, court monitors, audits, grievances, inspection records, and local dashboards. | Treating aggregate count as case truth. |

## Minimum joined record

A serious custody packet should contain at least:

1. **Person and authority map.** Identity, aliases, booking number, court case, sentence, holds, detainers, supervision authority, transfer chain, facility, unit, and responsible legal owner.
2. **Custody clock.** Arrest, booking, initial appearance, bail/pretrial status, sentence, credits, transfer, parole/release eligibility, expected discharge, and any detainer or unresolved warrant.
3. **Intake risk record.** Suicide, withdrawal, medication, pregnancy, disability, chronic disease, age, language, mental health, victimization, protective custody, and urgent referral.
4. **Conditions and safety log.** Housing assignment, staffing, violence, use of force, sexual abuse allegation, segregation, sanitation, food, ADA accommodation, grievance, and incident follow-up.
5. **Health passport.** Medical record, medications, MOUD, mental health plan, specialty care, hospital transfer, disability supports, pregnancy care, release prescription, and appointment handoff.
6. **Death/injury accountability tail.** DCRA/MCI status, cause and manner, independent investigation, family notice, public denominator, corrective action, and litigation/monitor status if any.
7. **Communication and counsel route.** Attorney access, court video/transport, mail/phone/video, interpreter, disability accommodation, family contact, grievance and retaliation safeguards.
8. **Release execution record.** Credit calculation, release order, transport, property, gate time, medications, IDs, benefits, address, phone, appointment, and supervision reporting instructions.
9. **Reentry supports.** Medicaid or other health coverage, ID documents, RRC/home confinement/housing, employment or education, treatment, family reunification, food/cash benefits, and supervision conditions.
10. **Source-currentness boundary.** Which source is an aggregate denominator, which is a live service rule, which is a finding/investigation, and which requires local case records.

## Upgrade triggers

Route the matter to the full custody/reentry continuity docket when any of these appear:

- a person is held pretrial, detained on a warrant or detainer, transferred between facilities, released late, or discharged without property, medication, IDs, or transport;
- suicide, withdrawal, overdose, pregnancy, disability, mental-health crisis, hospital transfer, solitary confinement, violence, sexual abuse, excessive force, or death is present;
- jail/prison counts, risk classifications, FSA credits, RRC capacity, Medicaid reentry authority, or death-in-custody reporting are cited as proof of safe custody or successful reentry;
- communication, counsel, family contact, interpreter, ADA access, grievance, or retaliation-safe reporting is operationally blocked;
- supervision, electronic monitoring, fees, treatment, housing, or employment conditions can turn release into immediate failure.

## Downshift conditions

A lighter summary is acceptable only when the question is purely aggregate and no person-specific custody, medical, death/injury, communication, release, benefits, housing, supervision, or remedy consequence is live. Once an individual consequence appears, aggregate BJS, CMS, DOJ, GAO, SAMHSA, BOP, or local facility sources must be joined to local case records.

## Failure modes

- **No liberty by custody row:** a row says in custody or released while the legal, medical, or reentry state is wrong.
- **No pretrial justice by jail census:** population totals obscure length of stay, bail, counsel, disability, and case delays.
- **No safety by classification:** risk score or housing level substitutes for protection from violence and medical harm.
- **No health by sick call:** requests and referrals substitute for medication, treatment, and release handoff.
- **No death accountability by annual count:** mortality data are used without incident-level investigation and corrective action.
- **No release by calculated date:** credits, detainers, transport, property, IDs, or discharge prescriptions fail after the date is computed.
- **No reentry by referral:** benefits, IDs, treatment, and housing are promised but unavailable or unconfirmed.
- **No supervision success by compliance flag:** electronic monitoring, fees, curfews, treatment rules, and reporting burdens create hidden failure points.

## Audit questions

- What legal authority currently holds the person, and who can end or correct that authority?
- What intake risks were identified, and what evidence proves they were acted on before harm occurred?
- Which medical, medication, MOUD, disability, pregnancy, or mental-health needs cross the facility gate?
- What would prove that a death, injury, use-of-force event, or sexual-abuse allegation was investigated and corrected rather than merely counted?
- What date, credit, detainer, court, parole, or transport condition controls release?
- What documents, coverage, medication, appointment, address, phone, housing, and supervision records exist at the moment of discharge?
- Which source is an aggregate denominator, which source is live policy, and which source is a case record that must be obtained before judgment?

## Source posture

The first version uses U.S. official and oversight sources: BJS jail/prison/community-supervision and mortality data; BJA Death in Custody Reporting Act surfaces; CMS Medicaid reentry demonstrations; GAO work on BOP health-care reentry, identification documents, and residential reentry centers; SAMHSA MOUD correctional guidance; BOP First Step Act materials; and DOJ findings on jail and prison conditions. It is strong enough for a U.S. custody-continuity waist, but not a substitute for court files, jail/prison medical records, release calculations, local inspection reports, monitor reports, grievance files, and person-level reentry evidence.
