# 981 — Applied local digital service-home case packet for NYC MyCity, Common Services, childcare, business, benefits, and no service home by portal

## One-line thesis

NYC MyCity is not a local digital service home merely because it offers a portal, account, eligibility screen, childcare form, business dashboard, benefits redirect, chatbot, or Common Services layer; it becomes a credible city-service home only when the public can see the service owner, agency obligation, workflow state, support and complaint handoff, data-sharing boundary, vendor chain, fallback, transition receipt, and outcome join for each service that the portal claims to streamline.

## Why this matters

Rev0779 kept note `615` active because the service-home successor route still had residue outside transport, water, and intermunicipal examples. Rev0780 added LAHSA as a social-service stress case, rev0781 added NG911 as an emergency-communications stress case, and rev0782 added GOV.UK One Login as a digital-administrative identity/shared-account stress case. The remaining gap is local digital administration: a city portal can look like a service home because it is the visible front door, even when the actual service, legal duty, staff workflow, vendor chain, data exchange, notice, appeal, and remedy still live elsewhere.

NYC MyCity is the right stress case because its promise is unusually service-home-shaped. It is framed as a one-stop route for City services and benefits, with childcare, business, jobs, and benefits surfaces. The New York City Comptroller audit, however, found that the project still depended heavily on redirects, rebranded websites, limited functionality, incomplete project/contract controls, and an AI chatbot that could not consistently provide accurate information. That does not make MyCity useless; it makes it a precise test for the archive's rule: **no service home by portal**.

This note does not replace note `877` on the MyCity chatbot as a generative public-service assistant case. It asks a wider service-home question: when a local government centralizes a digital front door, can people still see who owns the service promise and what happens when the portal, the agency, the vendor, the chatbot, or the legacy workflow fails?

## Pattern pack

### 1. Start with the service consequence, not the portal

A portal can show a path, store information, or route a user. It is not the service consequence. A MyCity user might screen for childcare, upload documents, track a status, use a business wizard, ask a chatbot, or click through to ACCESS NYC without receiving childcare, a permit, an inspection, a benefit, a job, a corrected notice, or a remedy.

A local digital service-home packet should begin with the public consequence:

- childcare voucher, contracted-care placement, recertification, or notice;
- business license, permit, inspection, violation payment, financing route, or regulatory answer;
- benefits eligibility, application, case status, agency notice, or referral;
- jobs/training referral or actual enrollment;
- support or complaint resolution; and
- outcome handoff back to the resident or business.

If the only visible fact is that the person entered the portal, the service home is not proven.

### 2. Split OTI, service agencies, vendors, and legacy systems

A local digital service home must keep roles separate.

| Role | Evidence needed | Thin shortcut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Citywide platform owner | OTI authority, product owner, roadmap, support route, security/privacy role | service home by OTI ownership |
| Service agency owner | ACS, DOE, SBS, DCWP, DOHMH, HRA, DHS, or other agency obligation | service by portal transaction |
| Vendor / contractor | scope, deliverables, acceptance, testing, invoices, maintenance, handover | delivery by purchase order |
| Data-sharing owner | agreement, minimum data, agency purpose, retention, correction | data reuse by digital wallet |
| User support owner | technical support, agency support, 311/complaint routing, escalation | remedy by contact form |
| Legacy system owner | agency website, ACCESS NYC, licensing, inspection, case system | one-stop by redirect |
| Chatbot / guidance owner | source hierarchy, abstention, correction, withdrawal, liability | law by answer |

### 3. Treat childcare as the strongest lane, not proof of the whole service home

The childcare lane matters because it digitized a real paper application and later added recertification. It is the strongest MyCity lane. But even that lane needs additional evidence before it becomes a service-home proof:

- ACS/DOE obligation split;
- voucher versus contracted-care routing;
- document completeness and resubmission route;
- mailed or emailed eligibility determination path;
- status meanings;
- deadline and recertification clocks;
- support and appeal handoff;
- language and assisted access;
- failure recovery when the portal is down; and
- outcome proof that the family actually receives care or a usable determination.

A high submission count is not a care-continuity receipt.

### 4. Treat business and benefits surfaces as redirects until transaction proof exists

The business and benefits portals are service-home candidates only when they carry transaction consequence. A business wizard, dashboard, chatbot, or status search may help a user, but the service remains elsewhere unless the packet can show the license/permit/inspection/violation/payment owner, transaction state, notice route, complaint route, and remedy.

The benefits lane is especially vulnerable to portal theater. A redirect to ACCESS NYC can be useful. It does not prove eligibility, application filing, interview completion, case activation, benefit issuance, or agency performance tracking.

### 5. Make chatbot guidance subordinate to source-bound service routing

A chatbot can be a navigation aid. It cannot be the service home. For MyCity, the chatbot history is a warning: legal or regulatory answers without a bounded source hierarchy, abstention rule, correction path, and liability handoff can mislead businesses and residents while appearing like official service.

The service-home packet should record:

- whether the answer is guidance, routing, or transaction support;
- approved source hierarchy;
- abstention/escalation conditions;
- correction and withdrawal receipts;
- named agency responsible for the underlying rule;
- complaint and harm reporting route; and
- whether the chatbot is excluded from consequential reliance.

### 6. Convert project-management failure into service-home evidence requests

The Comptroller audit's project-management and contract-oversight findings should not be treated merely as scandal. They are missing service-home proof. A local digital service-home packet should ask for:

- current-state map and future-state architecture;
- workstream roadmap;
- deliverable acceptance records;
- cost benchmarks and maintenance costs;
- vendor handoff and escrow/exit route;
- live performance and accessibility scores;
- user-support and agency-workload measures;
- workflow completion by agency; and
- benefit/service outcome metrics.

Without those records, the portal may be visible while the service home remains ungoverned.

### 7. Separate citywide digital strategy from local legal duty

Citywide technology consolidation can improve security, usability, and reuse. It can also blur accountability. OTI may operate a platform, but ACS, DOE, SBS, HRA, DHS, DCWP, DOHMH, and other agencies still own service consequences unless the law or transfer instrument says otherwise.

A local digital service home should therefore require a promise-owner row for every service lane.

### 8. Keep common services and stored documents from becoming silent evidence substitution

A Common Services layer, profile, stored document, or digital wallet can reduce repeated paperwork. It can also make old, wrong, stale, or misunderstood records travel across agencies.

The packet should require:

- source agency for each datum;
- user consent or legal basis;
- correction and revocation route;
- per-service reuse purpose;
- document freshness and expiry;
- agency acceptance rule;
- error propagation controls; and
- audit trail for downstream use.

### 9. Demand transition receipts before claiming streamlining

If a portal replaces or overlays agency routes, streamlining must be proved by transition receipts: old route, new route, remaining route, user cohort, support load, error rate, backlog, mail/email notice, appeal route, and fallback.

No one-stop claim should survive if the resident or business still has to discover the real service owner after the portal has failed.

### 10. Use MyCity to test service-home deletion pressure, not to delete note 615

This packet adds a local digital administrative example. It reduces a named blocker in the final preservation review for note `615`, but it also shows why note `615` should remain active: the practical problem is not solved by adding more cases. It is solved only when source/test/protected-element parity proves that the old route's service-home matrix has been genuinely absorbed.

## Applied local digital service-home ladder

1. **Name the service lane.** Childcare, business, benefits, jobs, or cross-program stored data.
2. **Name the promise owner.** Agency, legal program, service owner, support route, and notice/appeal authority.
3. **Name the platform owner.** OTI, product team, vendors, security/privacy role, support channel, maintenance and incident route.
4. **Name the legacy route.** Prior paper, agency website, ACCESS NYC, licensing, inspection, case system, or 311 route.
5. **Name the transaction state.** Submitted, incomplete, uploaded, under review, mailed notice, approved, denied, recertified, escalated, withdrawn, or redirected.
6. **Name the handoff.** Portal-to-agency, agency-to-user, agency-to-vendor, chatbot-to-source, support-to-case, or complaint-to-review.
7. **Name the fallback.** Paper, in-person, phone, assisted digital, language support, disability accommodation, non-portal route, or protective filing.
8. **Name the outcome evidence.** Determination, benefit issuance, care placement, permit, inspection, payment, correction, or appeal receipt.

## Applied examples

### Childcare portal

Use the childcare lane as the strongest case, not as a universal proof. It digitized application and recertification workflows, but a service-home packet still needs agency determination, notice, appeal, language/accessibility, and care-continuity evidence.

### Business portal and chatbot

Use business services to test transaction and guidance separation. A dashboard, wizard, chatbot, license/permit pointer, or violation-payment route is not the same as a lawful, correct, complete, and contestable business service.

### Benefits portal

Use benefits routing to test redirect honesty. A benefits portal that mainly redirects to ACCESS NYC is a useful navigation layer, not a universal application, eligibility, issuance, or agency-performance system.

### Common Services and data sharing

Use Common Services as the data-layer stress test. A data-sharing agreement, profile, or future wallet must be joined to purpose limitation, source agency, correction, agency acceptance, expiry, and reuse logs.

## Anti-theater tests

- Do not cite MyCity as a service home merely because it is the front door.
- Do not cite a single account as a single city service.
- Do not cite childcare submission count as childcare continuity.
- Do not cite a benefits redirect as benefits access.
- Do not cite a business wizard as permit, inspection, or compliance proof.
- Do not cite chatbot output as municipal law.
- Do not cite Common Services or a digital wallet as data correctness.
- Do not cite OTI ownership as agency service accountability.
- Do not cite agile project posture as delivery proof without milestone, cost, acceptance, and outcome evidence.
- Do not cite a maintenance allocation as repair.

## Source posture

This packet uses official MyCity, NYC Business, OTI, Local Law 35 / algorithmic-tool, and NYC Comptroller source routes, plus external reporting on chatbot failures. The sources prove portal posture, stated functionality, project/audit findings, chatbot-risk evidence, and source-boundary limits. They do not prove a completed one-stop shop, benefit issuance, childcare placement, permit issuance, successful inspection, accurate legal guidance, agency performance tracking, or lived service continuity.
