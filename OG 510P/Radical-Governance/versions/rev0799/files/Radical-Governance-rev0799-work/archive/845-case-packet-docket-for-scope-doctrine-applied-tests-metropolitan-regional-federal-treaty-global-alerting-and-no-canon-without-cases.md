# 845 — Case-packet docket for scope doctrine: applied tests, metropolitan, regional, federal, treaty, global alerting, and no canon without cases

## One-line thesis

The archive should now test its ideal-scope canon through compact case packets rather than more universal restatement: each packet should judge a real polity, compact, reform, or failure against the relevant scope default, record contrary evidence, assign a verdict, and set a review clock.

## Why this matters

`843` says the archive must prefer case packets once doctrine is settled enough. This note turns that command into a docket.

The core question — what ideal government belongs at each scope — has a usable answer. The remaining danger is that the answer becomes too beautiful to falsify. Real governments are messy: metropolitan government may be democratic but thin; municipal government may be elected but fiscally hollow; a treaty system may have real alerts but weak implementation; a regional tier may be constitutionally real but captured by elites; a compact may solve spillovers while moving decisions away from ordinary publics.

Case packets force the archive to touch ground. They make doctrine answer to evidence, affected people, fiscal capacity, legal competence, and visible accountability.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Use the minimum viable case packet

A case packet should be short enough to add without turning the archive into a country-study library, but complete enough to test a default.

| Field | Minimum content |
| --- | --- |
| case object | polity, compact, reform, agency, tier, or failure being judged |
| claimed scope | micro-local, municipal, connector, metropolitan, regional/provincial/state, national, continental/plurilateral, or global |
| archive rule applied | first-line note and claim-status label |
| supporting evidence | why the case seems to fit the rule |
| contrary evidence | why the fit may fail or overreach |
| harmed party | who is exposed if the archive’s default is wrong |
| capture channel | who can profit from the design’s failure |
| verdict | keep, thicken, thin, merge, split, hand back, sunset, supervise, or audit further |
| review clock | date, trigger, or evidence event that reopens the judgment |

The packet is not a neutral description. It is an applied governance verdict with humility attached.

### 2. First docket: metropolitan authority cases

| Candidate | Why it belongs in the docket | Live test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Greater London Authority | directly visible metropolitan executive and assembly model with transport, policing, fire, planning, housing, environment, skills, resilience, culture, health, and economic-development relevance | does the authority have enough democratic, fiscal, and scrutiny thickness for its shared-system mandate? |
| Metro in Oregon | rare directly elected regional government for the Portland metropolitan area | does direct election solve the legitimacy problem, or do functional limits and service boundaries still require connector discipline? |
| OECD metropolitan-governance typology cases | comparative basis for distinguishing coordination, intermunicipal, special-purpose, and metropolitan-authority forms | when does a shared urban system justify a thick tier rather than a compact? |

Expected verdict types: **keep and thicken**, **thin connector sufficient**, **audit fiscal chain**, or **hand back function**.

### 3. Second docket: municipal full-kit and reform cases

Use city-charter reforms, municipal mergers, distressed local governments, and assisted-digital service failures to test whether the archive’s municipal full-kit rule is real. The packet should ask whether council, executive, civil service, treasury, records, procurement, local rules, complaint routes, and own-source or transfer finance form a working kit rather than a ceremonial shell.

Potential stress questions:

- Does the municipality control enough revenue or staffing to be accountable?
- Are procurement, records, and complaint routes legible to ordinary residents?
- Does municipal autonomy protect service design or local exclusion?
- Are shared-service arrangements support, shadow government, or covert recentralization?

Expected verdict types: **keep**, **support**, **pool**, **supervise proportionally**, or **merge only after threshold proof**.

### 4. Third docket: regional, provincial, and federal bargain cases

Regional and federal cases should test whether a territorial tier has a real platform, a real constitutional bargain, or only map symmetry.

| Candidate class | Live test |
| --- | --- |
| federal states and provinces | does autonomy combine self-government with shared government, or does the center or region dominate the bargain? |
| devolved regions | is the tier protected by law, finance, and standing, or only delegated by ordinary grace? |
| administrative regions | do they operate platform systems better than national field offices or municipal compacts? |
| special-status territories | is asymmetry a justified bargain, a rights repair, or permanent exception theater? |

A regional packet should always include internal-minority protection, municipal headroom, fiscal congruence, and national rights-floor evidence.

### 5. Fourth docket: treaty compact and continental body cases

Treaty and compact cases should test whether bounded cross-border government is solving real spillovers without smuggling in unlimited competence.

Case packets should focus on:

- named competence and legal basis;
- member-state and parliamentary scrutiny;
- local and regional impact channels;
- fiscal visibility;
- dispute and amendment routes;
- exit or handback rules;
- implementation burden on lower tiers.

Possible verdicts include **bounded compact works**, **competence creep**, **implementation unfunded**, **democratic-distance repair needed**, and **treaty waist too weak for the problem**.

### 6. Fifth docket: global narrow-waist cases

Global cases should not ask whether global government exists in the full domestic sense. They should ask whether a narrow global waist is real enough for the exact problem.

| Candidate | Why it belongs in the docket | Live test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UN Charter peace and regional-arrangements machinery | canonical global and regional security relationship | does the global layer coordinate, authorize, and restrain without pretending to be an ordinary executive? |
| WHO International Health Regulations | treaty-bound alerting and preparedness system, now including the 2024 amendments entering into force in 2025 for many States Parties | does alerting and coordination have enough implementation support and contestability without becoming ordinary health administration? |
| Global Digital Compact | current narrow-waist example for digital cooperation and AI/data governance norms | is it a useful coordination compact or only soft-law aspiration without accountability? |
| Sendai disaster-risk framework | shared risk-governance model across national, local, and international responsibility | does the framework generate usable local and national readiness evidence rather than only reporting compliance? |

Expected verdict types: **thin but real**, **too symbolic**, **too broad**, **implementation-funded**, or **narrow-waist repair needed**.

### 7. Evidence discipline for packets

Every packet should separate four evidence lanes:

| Lane | Examples | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| legal basis | constitution, statute, charter, treaty, regulation | stops invented authority |
| operating proof | budgets, staff, service records, audits, incident reports | tests capability rather than form |
| lived-path proof | user journeys, complaint routes, access barriers, public hearing records | tests whether the tier is reachable |
| opposition proof | court challenges, ombuds reports, civil-society criticism, minority objections, dissenting audits | stops case laundering |

A packet with only legal basis should be labeled incomplete.

### 8. Do not let case packets become encyclopedic

A case packet should normally be two to six pages of markdown. It should cite official and high-quality sources, preserve only compact excerpts if any, and avoid becoming a historical monograph. Its job is to test doctrine, not to archive the world.

### 9. Case-packet verdict grammar

Use one of these verbs first, then explain:

| Verdict | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **keep** | current scope form is fit enough |
| **thicken** | mandate exists but legitimacy, finance, records, oversight, or review is too thin |
| **thin** | institution is heavier than the real scope problem requires |
| **merge** | separate bodies create more confusion than accountability |
| **split** | one body is carrying incompatible scopes |
| **hand back** | temporary or delegated power should return to the principal tier |
| **sunset** | form was justified by transition or emergency but should expire |
| **supervise** | proportional support or special measures are justified |
| **audit further** | evidence is insufficient for a final verdict |

The verdict should be visible near the top of the packet.

### 10. Initial priority order

The next applied notes should not choose famous cases merely because they are famous. They should choose cases that stress the archive’s default claims.

Priority order:

1. a metropolitan authority with real electoral and fiscal structure;
2. a metropolitan or intermunicipal system that deliberately stayed thin;
3. a fiscally weak municipality under support or supervision;
4. a region with real constitutional autonomy and internal-minority tension;
5. a treaty compact with local implementation burdens;
6. a global alerting or reporting system with contested authority;
7. a failed or symbolic body that looks like a government but lacks the minimum kit.

## Failure modes

- **showcase bias**: choosing successful examples and pretending doctrine has been tested.
- **country-study bloat**: letting case notes become descriptive files rather than verdict packets.
- **legal-form fetish**: treating a charter, treaty, or constitution as enough evidence of working government.
- **complaint omission**: ignoring users, minorities, auditors, courts, or civil society because official sources are neater.
- **timeless verdicts**: issuing a case judgment without a review clock.
- **global-soft-law confusion**: treating declarations, compacts, agencies, and courts as if they were the same kind of authority.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Does the packet state a verdict before drowning the reader in facts?
2. Does it include contrary evidence from a credible lane?
3. Does it say who is harmed if the archive default is wrong?
4. Does it name the capture channel?
5. Does it distinguish legal competence from operating capacity?
6. Does it set a review clock?
7. Does it reduce future doctrinal uncertainty rather than merely adding a case summary?
