# 871 — Applied symbolic-authority case packet for LAHSA: joint-powers homelessness waist, audit-triggered transition, city-county split, and no homelessness government by grant administrator

## One-line thesis

The Los Angeles Homeless Services Authority is best classified as a mandate-capacity-mismatched homelessness coordination, data, and grant / contracting waist under transition: it is a real joint-powers authority with major funds, contracts, dashboards, and Continuum-of-Care duties, but it should not be treated as a general homelessness government because land, housing supply, health systems, local law, county programs, city programs, provider capacity, and appropriations sit across other principals.

## Why this matters

LAHSA is a high-value failure-mode case because it is neither fake nor sovereign. It was created as a city-county joint powers authority; LAHSA describes itself as a joint powers authority of the City and County of Los Angeles, the lead agency for the HUD-funded Los Angeles Continuum of Care, and a coordinator / manager of federal, state, county, and city funds for shelter, housing, and services. That is real authority. It also provides tools and applications such as LA-HOP, HMIS, provider systems, budget reports, dashboards, and contracts.

But the same features make it a symbolic-overload risk. The public crisis is wider than LAHSA's direct authority. Homelessness outcomes depend on permanent housing supply, land-use approvals, rental markets, health and behavioral-health systems, county welfare and health duties, city encampment and shelter policies, service-provider capacity, state and federal grants, courts, police practices, nonprofit operators, and elected budget choices. A body can be central to the record and still not own the whole problem.

Recent audits and reorganization proposals make the distinction urgent. Los Angeles City Controller audit pages described unreliable shelter-bed data, lack of a centralized database for interim housing availability, underused beds, weak transition-to-permanent-housing outcomes, and recommended stronger data and corrective-action systems. Associated reporting described city and county moves to bypass, defund, or reassign substantial LAHSA functions after audits criticizing record-keeping, contract monitoring, late payments, and unsupported advances. LAHSA, meanwhile, publicly described corrective work: occupancy tracking, dashboards, provider performance monitoring, faster contract execution, active contract management, and permanent housing placement gains.

This packet therefore should not say simply “LAHSA failed” or “LAHSA was scapegoated.” The honest holding is narrower: LAHSA's case tests how a cross-boundary coordination / contracting waist can become both a real administrative failure site and an unfair symbolic owner of an outcome it cannot alone command.

### 4. Symbolic-authority tests applied

| Test | LAHSA reading |
| --- | --- |
| mandate owner | LAHSA owns coordination, funding administration, provider-contracting, data, and CoC lanes; it does not own all housing, health, land-use, public-space, or poverty levers |
| resource control | large budget and contract role are real, but appropriations and policy levers are distributed across city, county, state, federal, and voter-approved funding channels |
| contract traceability | audit criticism makes payments, advances, deliverables, provider monitoring, and corrective action live repair surfaces |
| data reality | shelter-bed availability, bed utilization, point-in-time counts, HMIS, dashboards, and occupancy tracking are live proof surfaces rather than communications extras |
| service pathway | interim-to-permanent housing outcomes must be separated from permanent housing shortage and from LAHSA-owned workflow defects |
| principal accountability | city and county principals should not hide behind LAHSA where city / county policy, land, health, or budget choices are theirs |
| provider dependence | nonprofit and contractor performance must be visible without pretending providers are LAHSA staff |
| transition receipt | county department integration and city direct-contracting moves need records, contracts, dashboards, staff, grants, provider, and user-continuity receipts |
| no-blame / no-credit boundary | LAHSA should neither absorb all blame for homelessness nor receive all credit for systemwide reductions unless attribution is source-backed |

### 6. Source-currentness warning

This packet is volatile. A 1993 / 2001 JPA description, a 2023 shelter-bed audit, a 2024 pathway or finance audit, a 2025 board motion, a 2026 county budget, and a future transition receipt will not say the same thing. Any claim about LAHSA's current duties should cite the latest LAHSA budget and commission packet, latest city / county action, latest county department implementation page, and latest federal Continuum-of-Care or court material.

### 7. Holding

The LAHSA case should be cited for **symbolic authority and mandate-capacity mismatch**, not for a simple lesson that joint powers authorities are bad or that homelessness coordination should always be centralized inside a county department.

Reusable holdings:

- a cross-boundary coordination body can be real without owning the whole social problem;
- a body can be unfairly scapegoated for external levers and still fail at internal record, data, contract, and payment duties;
- a visible dashboard or annual count does not prove the body owns all downstream housing outcomes;
- direct contracting, county integration, or department creation is not a cure unless transition receipts preserve records, users, providers, funds, and audit trails;
- the no-blame / no-credit boundary should be a public artifact, not an excuse.

## Failure modes

- **homelessness government by grant administrator**: LAHSA is treated as if it can solve homelessness because it administers funds and contracts.
- **JPA scapegoat**: city and county principals blame LAHSA for powers or choices they retained.
- **audit halo**: real audit findings become proof of total institutional worthlessness rather than proof of specific record, contract, payment, and data defects.
- **dashboard halo**: public dashboards and counts absorb political authority without showing source reliability, correction routes, and limits.
- **provider fog**: contracted providers become the actual delivery layer while performance, invoices, advances, and corrective action stay hard to reconstruct.
- **transition amnesia**: records, open recommendations, provider obligations, and user pathways disappear during county integration or city bypass.
- **county-department halo**: replacing a JPA with a department is treated as accountability even if city, federal, provider, and cross-boundary functions still need a waist.
- **attribution laundering**: decreases or increases in homelessness are credited or blamed to LAHSA without isolating housing supply, enforcement changes, economic conditions, public-health services, and data-method changes.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Can a person experiencing homelessness tell which public actor owns outreach, shelter referral, case management, complaint, and permanent-housing pathway steps?
2. Can a provider tell which contract, invoice, monitoring, and corrective-action route governs them during transition?
3. Can the public reconstruct which beds exist, which are offline, who can access them, and how current the data are?
4. Can a budget reader distinguish LAHSA-administered funds from city, county, state, federal, and voter-measure policy choices?
5. Do city and county officials retain public accountability for land-use, housing production, health, law, and budget decisions they keep?
6. Are audit findings tracked to closure or transfer rather than cited forever as general condemnation?
7. Does the county department transition publish a receipt for staff, records, contracts, funds, dashboards, grants, users, and residual LAHSA duties?
8. Are improvements and failures attributed only to lanes that evidence can support?
9. Does the archive name what would justify abolishing, retaining, shrinking, rechartering, or splitting LAHSA?
10. Does the case packet separate structural insufficiency, internal failure, and external mismatch?
