# 920 — Applied subnational AI case packet for NYC algorithmic tools, California ADS inventory, Colorado AI Act, courts, schools, local records, and no public service by local pilot

## One-line thesis

NYC’s algorithmic-tool reporting, California’s high-risk ADS inventory, Colorado’s AI Act, state-court AI guidance, school AI guidance, and local-government public-records practice form a subnational implementation packet: inventories, laws, pilots, policies, and records only count when they trace to a concrete public-service workflow and correction route.

## Why this matters

Subnational AI is where public doctrine becomes ordinary service experience. Residents do not encounter “AI governance” as an abstract national register; they encounter it as a city tool, a school rule, a court filing aid, a police lead, a permit queue, a benefits workflow, a procurement clause, or a public-records answer. This packet keeps those local seams testable.

## Case frame

This packet is not a generic “state AI regulation” survey. It is an applied test case for the layer where national doctrine becomes local delivery. The same governance claims repeat across the examples:

- a city publishes an algorithmic-tools report and open-data surface;
- a state collects a high-risk ADS inventory and reports no high-risk systems;
- a state AI statute creates deployer, developer, impact-assessment, notice, appeal, public-statement, and attorney-general enforcement obligations;
- courts build policy guidance for confidentiality, privacy, accuracy, hallucinations, bias, disclosure, and adjudicative boundaries;
- schools issue nonbinding guidance and convene a working group for local educational agencies;
- local-government advisors treat AI prompts, outputs, and meeting summaries as likely public records when tied to public business;
- municipal law creates an office, comment / complaint mechanisms, corrective action powers, public engagement, and periodic standards.

The packet’s job is to stop any one of these surfaces from pretending to be the whole case. A city report is not an impact assessment. A state inventory is not proof of absence. A state law is not implementation. A court model policy is not local adoption. School guidance is not a district records and student-data plan. A public-records rule is not a workflow risk assessment. A local pilot is not a service right.

### 3. Case state decomposition

For every local or state AI example, establish these states before scoring governance:

1. **authority state**: statute, ordinance, executive order, court rule, board policy, school policy, procurement rule, or agency guidance;
2. **workflow state**: candidate, sandbox, pilot, staff-only, public-facing, consequential, paused, retired, or vendor-feature latent;
3. **row state**: listed, absent, exempt, excluded branch, nonresponse, duplicate, aggregate, or stale;
4. **records state**: prompt, output, log, transcript, model evaluation, configuration, incident, notice, and correction retention;
5. **risk state**: high-risk, consequential, public-impacting, staff productivity, educational, adjudicative, public-safety, or low-risk rationale;
6. **affected-person state**: no direct effect, service guidance, triage, eligibility, enforcement, grading, court access, policing lead, permit decision, or appealable action;
7. **repair state**: notice, correction, human review, appeal, complaint, public comment, audit, suspension, shutdown, decommissioning, or compensation.

A missing state is not a minor documentation error; it is a failure to know what the local government did.

### 6. Rival readings

**Rival 1: Central AI registers already cover this.** They do not. State, city, school, court, and local records systems have different authority, custody, and affected-person routes.

**Rival 2: Local pilots should be allowed to move quickly.** They should, but bounded speed requires stronger records and exit rules, not weaker ones.

**Rival 3: A state report finding no high-risk ADS proves low risk.** It proves only the result of that scope, response process, definitions, and year. Nonresponse, excluded branches, non-high-risk ADS, vendor features, procurement records, and local use still need reconciliation.

**Rival 4: Schools and courts can use general staff-copilot guidance.** Not enough. Student data, grading, discipline, accommodations, parent rights, court confidentiality, adjudicative boundaries, self-help, and record integrity require specific tracks.

**Rival 5: Public-records questions can wait until someone asks.** Waiting destroys evidence. Retention, export, custody, and confidentiality rules must exist before operational use.

## Failure modes

- **No public service by local pilot**: pilot status becomes an excuse to avoid notice, records, appeal, metrics, and shutdown receipts.
- **No use by nonresponse**: inventory response rate, excluded branches, or definitions make absence look more complete than it is.
- **No fairness by city report**: public listing becomes a substitute for validation, monitoring, complaint handling, and correction.
- **No student protection by guidance PDF**: statewide educational guidance does not become local policy, vendor terms, or enforceable student-data practice.
- **No access to justice by productivity tool**: court staff AI improves drafting speed but harms accuracy, disclosure, confidentiality, or public trust.
- **No accountability by procurement silence**: vendor features and platform updates change workflows without risk review or public inventory updates.
- **No appeal by public-records loss**: the evidence needed for a complaint, appeal, or audit was never retained.
- **No implementation by statute**: legal duties exist, but forms, owners, rules, monitoring, and enforcement are not yet operating.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Can the tool be traced from law or policy to a named local workflow and accountable owner?
2. Is the tool listed, absent, exempt, out of scope, excluded by branch, or missing because of nonresponse?
3. Does the record show whether the tool is candidate, sandbox, pilot, staff-only, public-facing, consequential, paused, retired, or latent vendor feature?
4. Can procurement, vendor disclosures, platform settings, inventory rows, public-records schedules, and training records be reconciled?
5. Are prompts, outputs, transcripts, logs, configurations, model evaluations, risk assessments, complaints, and corrections retained or exportable?
6. If a state law applies, are impact assessments, annual reviews, notices, correction rights, appeals, public statements, and AG / enforcement evidence operating?
7. If a school uses AI, are student data, grading, discipline, accommodations, academic integrity, parental notice, teacher judgment, and vendor controls resolved locally?
8. If a court uses AI, are confidentiality, privacy, bias, hallucination review, disclosure, judicial / adjudicative boundaries, self-help, and record integrity resolved?
9. If the output affects policing, permitting, inspections, housing, benefits, or health, is there a human authority and cure route before consequence?
10. Do public metrics pair benefits with harms, including false positives, false negatives, complaints, appeals, corrections, shutdowns, and unserved populations?
