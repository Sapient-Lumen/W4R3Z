# 852 — Applied cross-boundary case packet for regional water/wastewater utility authority: asset-liability ledger, community assessments, ratepayer route, and no utility government by bond covenant

## One-line thesis

An asset-heavy regional water/wastewater system should be governed as a **bounded utility authority with an asset-liability waist**: pipes, tunnels, reservoirs, treatment works, permits, debt, reserves, and community assessments justify durable regional machinery, but they do not justify general territorial government by bond covenant, engineering plan, or wholesale-rate formula.

## Why this matters

`849` tested a thick metropolitan transport authority, `850` tested a thin shared-service backbone, and `851` tested a middle regional risk platform. The remaining gap was `822`: the case where the institution is heavy not because it has a broad democratic vocation, but because the shared assets and liabilities are too durable, capital-intensive, regulated, and interdependent to leave as orphaned balance-sheet residue.

This packet uses the Massachusetts Water Resources Authority pattern as the asset-heavy test object. The point is not to make every water region imitate one authority. The point is to force the archive to say what must be visible when a regional body owns or operates strategic utility assets, incurs debt, assesses communities, receives regulatory oversight, and sits between wholesale infrastructure and retail local users.

The verdict is **bounded regional utility authority with an asset-liability waist**. It is thicker than a shared-service compact because the body carries infrastructure, debt, regulatory compliance, maintenance, and assessment consequences across many municipalities. It is narrower than a metropolitan government because the body should not use the water/sewer balance sheet to settle general housing, land, policing, taxation, or social-policy questions.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Case object and verdict

| Field | Holding |
| --- | --- |
| case object | a regional wholesale water and wastewater utility authority serving multiple municipalities, with central assets, capital plans, operating budgets, debt, reserves, regulatory duties, community assessments, advisory routes, public records, and investor-facing disclosures |
| claimed scope | shared utility infrastructure and compliance system, not general metropolitan sovereignty |
| archive rule applied | `848` dispatcher; `812` form ladder; live chain notes `813`, `814`, `815`, `816`, `817`, `818`, `819`, and especially `822`; reserve `823` for named construction, design-build, service, concession, or finance contracts; reserve `824` for permitting, inspection, enforcement, and sanction packets |
| verdict | **keep as bounded regional utility authority with asset-liability waist** |
| short reason | the shared problem is not mere administrative capacity; it is a durable network of capital assets, regulatory obligations, debt service, maintenance, and community assessments whose failure or opacity harms users across local boundaries |
| narrow thickening needed | asset register, debt and reserve ledger, rate / assessment chain, community interface, regulatory compliance surface, maintenance backlog, capital-plan trace, public-records route, advisory board / user route, and asset handback / decommissioning map |
| forbidden thickening | do not let bond covenants, engineering expertise, rate models, or wholesale service territory become a hidden general-purpose regional government |

The key distinction is **infrastructure sovereignty versus infrastructure stewardship**. A regional utility may need real authority over assets, debt, compliance, and operations. That authority should be ring-fenced to the utility mission and surrounded by public fiscal, asset, records, and user-route surfaces.

### 2. Trigger proof under `848`

| Trigger field | Utility-authority packet answer |
| --- | --- |
| boundary mismatch | water sources, treatment plants, tunnels, interceptors, reservoirs, outfalls, environmental permits, and debt-backed capital programmes serve populations whose risk and benefit do not align with single municipal borders |
| harmed population | ratepayers, member municipalities, neighborhoods near works, downstream / receiving waters, bondholders only as fiscal signalers, workers, local public works departments, and future users inheriting deferred maintenance |
| incumbent failure | municipality-alone operation fails when the system requires shared treatment works, common source protection, region-wide sewer conveyance, capital sequencing, compliance orders, or debt-scale not available to one locality |
| form family | special-purpose regional public utility authority, wholesale service authority, water/sewer district, or jointly owned utility corporation with public-law constraints |
| lower-form insufficiency | a thin service hub cannot honestly own major assets, borrow at scale, allocate capital risk, carry reserves, meet environmental obligations, or impose wholesale assessments without stronger public machinery |
| upper-form excess | a general metropolitan government is too thick when the live powers are utility-specific assets, finance, operations, and compliance, rather than general territorial legislation |
| upgrade test | thicken if the body gains tax-like fiscal powers, land-use leverage, coercive enforcement, major non-utility redevelopment authority, or opaque debt/contract risks beyond the utility mission |
| handback test | downshift only if the assets, debt, compliance obligations, and maintenance duties become separable without system, fiscal, environmental, or user-right loss |

The trigger is not simply that pipes cross borders. The trigger is that the **asset-and-liability chain** would otherwise be invisible: title, debt, reserves, maintenance, compliance, depreciation, replacement, and user cost would scatter across actors while no one could reconstruct who owns the problem.

### 4. Asset-liability surfaces required by `822`

| Surface | Minimum reconstruction question | Repair if missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| legal title and control | who owns or controls each major plant, tunnel, interceptor, reservoir, outfall, laboratory, pump station, land interest, easement, and digital control system? | asset register with owner, operator, control right, restriction, and public-records location |
| debt and reserves | which bonds, loans, reserves, covenants, liquidity facilities, and debt-service schedules bind the authority and member communities? | debt / reserve ledger cross-walked to capital plan and assessment schedule |
| operating budget | which costs are operating, capital, debt service, regulatory compliance, emergency, labor, energy, chemical, insurance, and contracted service? | budget taxonomy linked to board adoption and public reports |
| community assessment chain | how does wholesale cost become municipal assessment and retail bill? | member-community assessment schedule plus local-retail handoff explanation |
| maintenance and renewal | which assets are aging, deferred, under consent order, under replacement, or under climate / capacity stress? | maintenance backlog and renewal clock, not just capital glamour projects |
| regulatory compliance | which environmental, drinking-water, wastewater, court, permit, or reporting obligations drive spending and operations? | compliance ledger naming regulator, duty, deadline, owner, and consequence |
| accession / exit / expansion | how do new communities enter, partial communities change service, and existing communities exit or modify obligations? | service-boundary and liability-transfer rule |
| decommission / handback | what happens when a facility, tunnel, debt instrument, or contract reaches end of life? | handback / retirement map with residual-risk owner |

An asset-heavy authority is legitimate only when a reader can reconstruct the whole chain from physical asset to debt instrument to assessment to local bill to public oversight.

### 6. Form holding

The honest form is **bounded regional utility authority with asset-liability waist**. It may own or control major utility assets, adopt operating and capital budgets, issue or carry debt under law, assess member communities, contract for works and services, operate treatment and conveyance systems, publish compliance and performance materials, and maintain public records.

It must not claim general government merely because its assets are essential. Essential infrastructure can justify special-purpose authority; it cannot silently create a regional parliament, land-use sovereign, redevelopment agency, tax government, or social-policy allocator. When the utility’s choices affect growth, land, environmental justice, affordability, regional development, or coercive enforcement, the packet must route those decisions to the correct democratic, fiscal, regulatory, judicial, or project-contract surface.

### 8. Rival interpretations

| Rival rule | Why it is tempting | Why this packet rejects or limits it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| municipality-only utility rule | protects local billing accountability | fails when source, treatment, sewer, outfall, debt, and compliance duties are regional |
| expert utility board decides all | matches technical complexity | launders fiscal and distributive choices through engineering expertise |
| bondholder-first governance | protects credit and long-term capital access | turns financial covenants into quasi-constitutional rules without user or municipal voice |
| full metropolitan government | admits the infrastructure is essential | overbuilds if the body only needs utility powers and asset/liability accountability |
| privatized utility concession | can deliver capital and expertise | may hide tariff, maintenance, handback, and residual-liability choices unless `823` is separately satisfied |

The preferred rule is **public utility authority, asset/liability ring fence, local-retail handoff, and public oversight**, not municipal denial, bond sovereignty, or infrastructure empire.

### 9. Review clock

Reopen this packet when any of the following occurs:

- a major capital improvement plan, borrowing, rate, assessment, or reserve policy changes;
- a consent order, permit, water-quality, sewer-overflow, drought, contamination, cyber, or treatment incident exposes authority blur;
- a community enters, exits, shifts service class, or contests an assessment formula;
- a large asset reaches end of life, decommissioning, climate-retrofit, replacement, or emergency-repair status;
- a major design-build, operations, concession, technology, or finance contract triggers `823`;
- a permit, inspection, sanction, discharge, or enforcement action triggers `824`;
- the authority uses utility powers to influence land, housing, redevelopment, or general regional policy outside its mission.

A regional utility without a review clock becomes a balance sheet with a seal.

## Failure modes

- **bond-covenant sovereignty**: letting debt rules become hidden constitutional rules for the region.
- **asset orphaning**: naming a service without naming who owns, maintains, insures, retires, and replaces the underlying asset.
- **wholesale-user erasure**: pretending households and businesses disappear because the authority assesses municipalities rather than retail customers.
- **maintenance deferral theater**: publishing capital plans while hiding renewal backlog, asset condition, and end-of-life risk.
- **regulatory blame maze**: letting the authority, municipalities, state regulators, federal regulators, courts, and contractors point at one another after a failure.
- **engineering mandate creep**: using technical necessity to settle land, affordability, development, or distribution questions without the right public process.
- **investor-disclosure substitution**: treating bond-market transparency as if it were enough for resident, worker, neighbor, and municipal accountability.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Can a resident trace the path from local water/sewer bill to municipal assessment to authority budget and capital plan?
2. Can a member community see the formula, schedule, and dispute route for its assessment?
3. Can the authority list major assets, debt, reserves, maintenance backlog, compliance duties, and handback / retirement responsibilities in one place?
4. Can a public user identify the board route, advisory route, records route, emergency route, quality route, and local-billing route?
5. Can a failed asset be tied to an owner, operator, maintainer, regulator, budget line, and repair clock?
6. Can the archive distinguish utility-specific powers from land-use, tax, policing, redevelopment, or general regional powers the authority does not hold?
7. Can the next major contract show procurement, performance, change-control, payment, termination, and handback terms rather than hiding behind the asset register?
