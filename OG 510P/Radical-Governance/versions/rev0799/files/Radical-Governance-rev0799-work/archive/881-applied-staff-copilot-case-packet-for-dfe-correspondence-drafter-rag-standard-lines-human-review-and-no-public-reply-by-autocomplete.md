# 881 — Applied staff-copilot case packet for DfE Correspondence Drafter: RAG, standard lines, human review, and no public reply by autocomplete

## One-line thesis

DfE Correspondence Drafter is best classified as a staff-facing public-correspondence drafting waist: it may improve speed and consistency, but a human-review label is adequate only if the sent response, source pack, draft edits, quality assurance, issue reporting, and correction route remain visible.

## Why this matters

Public correspondence is not merely “text.” It is one of the ways a department states policy, answers a person, explains a decision path, and sets expectations. A correspondence drafter can be below automated-decision grade while still becoming a practical public-law surface. The person receives a departmental reply, not an “AI output.” If the draft misstates policy or omits a hard fact, the public error is owned by the department.

This case is a middle case between Redbox and Robodebt. The tool is narrower than a general-purpose copilot and less coercive than an automated debt system. It generates a recommended correspondence response based on departmental materials and leaves staff to review, edit, copy, paste, quality-assure, and send. That is a plausible governance design, but it needs evidence.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Form verdict

| Field | Holding |
| --- | --- |
| form | staff-facing correspondence-drafting waist |
| lower form rejected | ordinary template bank is too thin because the tool computes a recommended response |
| thicker form rejected | model-decision system is too thick while staff review and send the reply and the output does not itself decide rights |
| waist | retrieval / drafting layer between public request, briefing materials, and departmental reply |
| live risk | draft capture and standard-line overreach |

### 2. Record map

The minimum correspondence docket should include:

- request text or correspondence issue;
- source pack / briefing documents used;
- retrieval or generation trace where available;
- first draft;
- human edits;
- final sent response;
- QA check;
- issue / error report;
- public reply-back route;
- policy-team correction route.

### 3. Human review quality

A human-in-the-loop statement is not enough. Review should ask:

- Did the draft answer the question asked?
- Did it cite or rely on the correct standard line?
- Did it omit exceptions, deadlines, appeal routes, or case-specific facts?
- Did it introduce policy claims absent from the source pack?
- Did the staff member edit because the draft was wrong, incomplete, or misleading?
- Are recurring errors fed back to the tool owner and policy teams?

### 4. Public-law boundary

The reply may not become:

- a benefit, school, safeguarding, immigration, employment, enforcement, or funding decision;
- a substitute for statutory reasons;
- a substitute for appeal or complaint rights;
- a policy change by generated wording;
- a final legal interpretation where ordinary legal review is required.

If any of those conditions arise, route to `874` and ordinary administrative-law review.

### 5. Chain use

The case activates `813`, `815`, `816`, `817`, `818`, `819`, `820`, `821`, and `823`. `822` and `824` remain reserved unless the correspondence layer becomes contractual, fiscal, regulatory, or coercive.

### 6. Repair surfaces

- algorithmic transparency record;
- source corpus / standard-line register;
- staff QA checklist;
- edit and final-output log;
- policy-team issue reporting;
- public reply / correction channel;
- sampling audit by topic and harm class;
- model / corpus change record;
- excluded-topic and escalation rule.

### 7. Upgrade and downshift triggers

Upgrade if the drafter begins to answer individualized rights questions, generate statutory reasons, recommend outcomes, or route cases. Downshift if the tool is limited to non-substantive style editing or template selection with no generated policy content. Pause if quality assurance cannot reconstruct which source supported the final answer.

### 8. Holding

The DfE Correspondence Drafter is not public law by autocomplete if the department keeps the human editor, source pack, and final sent response accountable. It becomes theater if “the human can edit” is the only evidence that a generated departmental reply is safe.

## Anti-theater tests

This packet fails if staff review is treated as a magic word. The archive should require sent-output lineage, source-pack evidence, QA sampling, topic-specific error logs, and a public correction route before correspondence automation is credited as merely assistive.
