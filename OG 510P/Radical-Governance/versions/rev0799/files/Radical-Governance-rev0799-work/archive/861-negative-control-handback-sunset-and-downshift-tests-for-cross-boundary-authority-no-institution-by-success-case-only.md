# 861 — Negative-control, handback, sunset, and downshift tests for cross-boundary authority: no institution by success case only

## One-line thesis

A cross-boundary doctrine is not trustworthy until it can say no: every proposed connector, shared-service backbone, bounded authority, elected tier, treaty waist, digital assessment waist, database waist, or soft-law standard must pass negative-control tests for lower-form sufficiency, local handback, sunset, capture, failure, and no-authority alternatives before the archive treats institutional creation or thickening as justified.

## Why this matters

The current case packets are mostly successful form classifications. They show that the dispatcher can distinguish transit authorities, shared-service hubs, flood platforms, water utilities, bridge P3 bodies, port-state-control regimes, global health-alert waists, and interoperability assessment waists. That is valuable, but it is not enough.

A theory that only studies cases where some cross-boundary form exists will tend to find cross-boundary forms necessary. The archive therefore needs a discipline of **negative controls**: packets where the correct answer is no new body, no thickening, municipal handback, sunset, split authority, contract only, statutory floor only, recommendation with warning label, or ordinary domestic administration.

The negative-control rule is not anti-institutional. It protects legitimate institutions from overclaiming. A cross-boundary authority becomes more credible when the archive can show that thinner, lower, temporary, and non-institutional options were tested and failed.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Require a defeat table before creation or thickening

Every institutional creation or thickening proposal should preserve a defeat table.

| Alternative | Defeat question | Possible result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| no action | is the harm tolerable, already addressed, or speculative? | no case, monitor only, or evidence docket |
| local-only action | can each jurisdiction solve its own part without material spillover harm? | local handback or common floor only |
| bilateral agreement | can a narrow pairwise compact solve the problem? | no regional body |
| thin connector | are coordination, data sharing, and voluntary alignment enough? | connector only |
| shared-service backbone | is the real problem administrative capacity rather than political authority? | principal-owned backbone |
| statutory floor | would an upper-tier rule plus local administration be enough? | no new operator |
| funding condition | would transparent grant / equalization rule solve the gap without a body? | fiscal repair without authority |
| contract / procurement | can a public contract solve delivery without transferring title? | contract with public-law guardrails |
| temporary emergency bridge | is time-limited escalation enough? | emergency-only sunset path |

If the proposal cannot defeat at least the next-thinner plausible form, it has not earned thickening.

### 2. Classify negative cases, not only positive cases

Use these negative-control verdicts.

| Verdict | Meaning | Archive consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| no-trigger | boundary mismatch or shared-system proof is too weak | do not route to the chain |
| lower-form sufficient | a thinner or lower form can honestly carry the work | block thickening; record why |
| statutory-floor sufficient | common minimum rule is needed but no shared operator is needed | use floor / preemption notes, not authority notes |
| fiscal-repair sufficient | money channel is the real problem | use fiscal chain; do not invent body |
| contract-only with guardrails | delivery can be contracted without authority transfer | activate `823` and supplier / records controls |
| emergency-only bridge | temporary escalation is justified but permanent form is not | require sunset, handback, and after-action review |
| split-function | one part needs cross-boundary governance, another should remain local | split the packet and prevent scope creep |
| sunset / retire | existing body no longer carries enough public value or has drifted | dissolution, handback, or merger docket |
| captured waist | the form exists but the waist is controlled by a standard, database, vendor, or secretariat | repair or downshift before expanding |

A negative verdict is a substantive archive product, not a failed packet.

### 3. Run the handback test before the upgrade test

Institutional design tends to ask “what more power is needed?” first. Reverse the order.

| Test | Question |
| --- | --- |
| decompose | can the function be decomposed into local pieces without serious network, rights, fiscal, or safety loss? |
| local capacity | can lower authorities perform if given finance, staff, data, or technical support? |
| floor-plus-headroom | can an upper floor protect rights while leaving local discretion? |
| service-user path | would users have clearer redress locally than through a remote body? |
| public-record path | would records become more reconstructable if the function returned downward? |
| capture path | would handback weaken a captured database, vendor, board, or secretariat? |
| continuity | can handback occur without service collapse or rights loss? |

Only after the handback test fails should the archive ask whether the body should be thickened.

### 4. Separate sunset from failure

Sunset is not always punishment. A body can sunset because it worked, because the project ended, because technology changed, because a lower body gained capacity, or because the original emergency passed.

| Sunset reason | Good close-out record |
| --- | --- |
| mission complete | final deliverables, asset transfer, records archive, residual obligations |
| emergency over | after-action review, temporary powers closed, handback receipt |
| capacity restored | staff / finance / systems transferred to ordinary authorities |
| legal basis expired | reauthorization decision or lapse notice |
| captured or unsafe | repair findings, interim continuity, independent review, replacement route |
| superseded | successor body, merger map, liability and records continuity |

A sunset without records is disappearance, not accountability.

### 5. Use failure packets to improve the manual

A failure packet should preserve:

- the original trigger claim;
- the form selected;
- the promised repair surface;
- the actual failure;
- the affected parties;
- the capture channel;
- the missing evidence;
- the handback or replacement route;
- and the manual field that should be strengthened.

Failure is especially useful when it reveals that a field marked “reserved” should have been live.

### 6. Watch for proposal-stage theater

The archive should review not only existing bodies but also proposals. Proposal-stage red flags include:

| Red flag | Likely repair |
| --- | --- |
| “regional” invoked without harmed-population proof | no-trigger or evidence docket |
| cost savings promised without service-user and worker evidence | shared-service caution packet |
| one project used to justify permanent authority | project body with sunset, not general tier |
| technical standard treated as democratic settlement | waist-capture review |
| voluntary recommendation tied to funding, procurement, insurance, or banking access | soft-law hardening label |
| fiscal imbalance blamed on institutions rather than money | fiscal-repair sufficient verdict |
| emergency coordination used as permanent operating model | emergency-only bridge verdict |

A proposal can fail before public money, legal authority, or political trust is spent.

### 7. Preserve affected-party opposition even when the proposal is rejected

Negative packets should still name affected parties. Rejection can harm people too: a region may remain fragmented, a service may remain underfunded, or a vulnerable group may lose a route to cross-boundary remedy. The opposition brief should therefore include the strongest argument for the rejected form.

## Failure modes

- **success-case bias**: deriving a general rule only from institutions that already exist.
- **anti-institutional reflex**: treating every negative control as proof that cross-boundary authority is bad.
- **cheap handback**: returning power downward without finance, staff, records, or continuity.
- **sunset erasure**: closing a body without preserving decisions, assets, liabilities, and lessons.
- **proposal fog**: letting a vague regional proposal avoid the same proof burden as an enacted authority.
- **fiscal misdiagnosis**: creating a new body when the problem is underfunding, equalization, or cost-shifting.
- **emergency permanence**: letting temporary coordination become the ordinary constitution by inertia.
- **capture by refusal**: blocking a body in a way that leaves private standards, vendors, or informal clubs as the real authority.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Does the packet name at least one plausible thinner, lower, temporary, or non-institutional alternative?
2. Does it explain why that alternative fails or succeeds?
3. Is the handback test run before the upgrade test?
4. Does the packet include affected parties who benefit from rejection and affected parties who may be harmed by rejection?
5. Does a sunset path preserve records, liabilities, contracts, assets, staff, and service continuity?
6. Does an emergency-only bridge include an end date, after-action review, and handback receipt?
7. Does the negative verdict identify what evidence would reopen the case?
8. Does the archive treat the negative packet as evidence for consolidation, not as an absence of evidence?
