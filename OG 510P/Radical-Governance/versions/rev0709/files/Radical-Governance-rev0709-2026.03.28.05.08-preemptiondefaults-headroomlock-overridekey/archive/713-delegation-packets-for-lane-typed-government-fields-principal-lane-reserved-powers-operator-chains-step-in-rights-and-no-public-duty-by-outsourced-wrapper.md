# 713 — Delegation packets for lane-typed government fields: principal lane, reserved powers, operator chains, step-in rights, and no public duty by outsourced wrapper

## One-line thesis

Once the archive has lane packets, interface packets, a control plane, operating modes, repair rules, sunset rules, evidence, challenge, participation, oversight, and amendment discipline for a field, it should also publish one narrow **delegation packet** saying which slice of the field is still constitutionally owned by the public lane, which execution work may be delegated to public bodies, contractors, concessionaires, nonprofits, co-operatives, or platforms, what powers remain non-delegable, what contract-management and records capacity the principal must retain, what step-in and handback rights keep continuity real, and what review route prevents public duty from disappearing into an outsourced wrapper.

## Why this matters

The recent packet spine can now carry much of a whole field constitution without forcing every later note to rewrite a miniature state theory.

- `699` gives the scope spine.
- `700` gives the minimum design packet.
- `701` gives rivalry tests.
- `702` gives lane-splitting discipline.
- `703` gives interface packets.
- `704` gives the control plane.
- `705` gives mode packets.
- `706` gives repair packets.
- `707` gives sunset packets.
- `708` gives the evidence packet.
- `709` gives the challenge packet.
- `710` gives the participation packet.
- `711` gives the oversight packet.
- `712` gives the amendment packet.

That sequence now explains how a field is structured, evidenced, challenged, scrutinized, repaired, retired, and changed.

But one common real-world move still lacks a compact constitutional object:

**how does a field hand bounded execution to an external operator without dissolving the public lane that still owes the duty?**

The archive already has broader doctrine here.

- `544` says delegated public functions cannot become constitutional escape hatches.
- `615` says service homes need visible operator chains, commissioning routes, and continuity.
- `619` says contract homes need procurement, change control, exit capacity, and no concession patchwork in place of government.
- `586` says function transfer needs explicit packs for staff, assets, records, contracts, liabilities, and cutover.
- `698` says implementing partners and pass-through actors need due diligence, cash-transfer assurance, and closeout rules.

This note does not replace those. It compresses them into one reusable packet for the newer field-constitution stack.

Without a delegation packet, fields drift in predictable ways.

First, they drift by **wrapper laundering**: the field says the contractor, concessionaire, nonprofit, platform, or arms-length body is “only delivering”, while in practice that operator now controls access, exceptions, timing, records, or priorities that the public lane never properly reserved.

Second, they drift by **client-hollowing**: the state retains the duty in law but no longer has enough in-house capability to specify the service, test claims, inspect performance, manage change, or resume control.

Third, they drift by **operator-chain invisibility**: the public can see the front brand but not the real chain of prime operator, sub-contractors, host platforms, data processors, call-centre vendors, maintenance firms, or partner agencies actually shaping service.

Fourth, they drift by **continuity theater**: handback, step-in, asset condition, escrow, records access, staff transition, or emergency operating rights exist only in sales language, not in a governed packet the field can actually execute.

A delegation packet exists so a field can use outside delivery capacity without losing constitutional honesty about who still owes the public duty.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Separate constitutional ownership from delegated execution

The packet should begin by naming the **principal lane**.

That lane remains publicly answerable for the function even when execution is delegated. It keeps the field’s constitutional duty to set the public floor, preserve lawful access, maintain records and review routes, monitor the live operator, and resume or redirect delivery if the delegation fails.

The delegated operator may run a service, maintain assets, handle routine interactions, or provide specialist delivery capacity. But the delegation packet should make clear that delegation does not change who owns the public duty.

If the field cannot say who the principal lane is after delegation, it has already delegated too much.

### 2. Name the delegable slice and the reserved powers before choosing an operator

The packet should not begin with a procurement vehicle or provider type. It should first classify the work.

A normal packet should say which part of the field is being delegated, for example:

- bounded operations under a public ruleset;
- asset maintenance under visible performance standards;
- specialist delivery capacity under public eligibility and safety rules;
- partner distribution or grant administration under a public programme design;
- platform or processing capacity under a public rights and records architecture.

It should then name the **reserved powers** that stay with the public lane, such as:

- rule setting and rights floors;
- final authority over exclusions, sanctions, and compulsory acts;
- budget authority and material change approval;
- standing complaint, appeal, ombuds, or court routes;
- evidence definitions, reporting standards, and audit access;
- emergency step-in, suspension, and termination powers.

Without this separation, the field outsources first and discovers later that it also outsourced its constitution.

### 3. Publish the operator chain instead of pretending there is only one provider

A field should not describe delegation as a bilateral relationship if actual delivery is a chain.

The packet should therefore name:

- the principal lane;
- the lead operator;
- any mandatory host agency or shared-service dependency;
- declared sub-contracting layers or partner tiers;
- data, platform, payment, and call-handling dependencies;
- and the public-facing route map showing where the governed person enters and where the public lane can still be reached directly.

This is what stops contractor sprawl from becoming authority fog. The public should not need an investigative journalist to discover which organization really made the effective choice.

### 4. Keep a retained client and contract-management core inside the public lane

Delegation does not remove the need for operating knowledge on the public side. It often increases it.

The packet should therefore declare the minimum retained capability the principal lane keeps in-house, such as:

- service specification and standards ownership;
- financial and supplier-risk review;
- demand and capacity modelling;
- performance verification and site inspection;
- records, data-access, and audit management;
- change control and commercial negotiation;
- emergency continuity planning and step-in readiness.

A field that has outsourced execution but not retained this client core has not created lean government. It has created dependency with ceremonial supervision.

### 5. Make money, assets, records, data, liabilities, and step-in rights explicit

Delegation fails most often in the supposedly boring edges.

The packet should therefore say:

- what funding model applies and who bears which risks;
- which assets remain public, which are operator-maintained, and what condition proof is required;
- which records are public records, how they are accessed, and what retention rules survive provider change;
- which data are controlled by the public lane, what processors or platforms may touch them, and what export or portability rights apply;
- what liabilities remain with the public side, which are transferred or insured, and where ultimate public exposure returns;
- and exactly what **step-in**, suspension, emergency operating, and handback rights can be executed when performance, solvency, legality, or continuity breaks.

A delegation packet should assume failure is possible and design the return path in advance.

### 6. Bind delegation into the other packets instead of treating it as a separate commercial universe

Delegation should not sit outside the rest of the field constitution.

- The **evidence packet** should declare which operator-supplied signals can trigger mode review, repair, or escalation and which blind spots remain dangerous.
- The **challenge packet** should keep a visible route to the public lane even when the first-line service interaction is external.
- The **oversight packet** should preserve audit, inspection, ombuds, or legislative access to the delegated slice.
- The **mode packet** should say what happens to operator powers and obligations in degraded, emergency, and recovery states.
- The **repair packet** should convert repeated supplier failure or client-hollowing into structural repair rather than one more performance warning.
- The **amendment packet** should open if delegation changes the actual field constitution rather than merely changing an operator.
- The **sunset packet** should be available when a temporary operator model or transitional body no longer deserves to survive.

The delegation packet exists so commercial form cannot become a secret constitution beneath the field constitution.

### 7. Keep delegation reviewable, reversible, and time-bounded enough to remain governable

The packet should say when the delegation begins, what review clock applies, what recompetition or redesign gate opens, and what conditions force reversal, rebundling, or re-insourcing.

That does not mean every delegation must be short-term. It means no delegation should become immortal because the field has forgotten how to retake control.

Typical triggers for review or reversal include:

- repeated performance failure;
- operator financial distress;
- evidence that the delegated slice has accumulated de facto policy or gatekeeping power;
- repeated challenge that cannot be corrected inside the current contract;
- mode shifts that reveal the public lane no longer has enough retained capability;
- or amendment-class changes that mean the live constitution is now being shaped by contractual workaround.

### 8. Keep the packet small enough that later field notes can cite it rather than rebuild it

A normal delegation packet should fit in one compact block.

If a later note needs pages of prose to explain who still owns the duty after outside delivery is introduced, the field has not yet separated public ownership, delegated execution, operator chain, retained capability, and continuity rights clearly enough.

## Compact delegation packet

A normal delegation packet should usually fit in one table-like block:

| Field | Principal lane and delegated slice | Reserved powers | Operator-chain controls | Continuity and review | Public route and consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Housing** | municipal housing lane delegates temporary-accommodation operations and repairs logistics to contracted providers under metro overflow rules | eligibility floor, unlawful-placement review, safeguarding escalation, complaint route, emergency placement override, and standards for habitability stay public | named prime provider list, capped subcontracting tiers, public records and inspection rights, financial-distress watch, landlord and call-centre dependencies declared | emergency step-in for unsafe placements; handback file and occupancy ledger; review on repeated unlawful-placement complaints or provider distress | residents can still reach one public housing-rights front door; recurring contractor failure can trigger repair packet, provider replacement, or re-insourcing review |
| **Transport** | metro network lane delegates corridor operations or maintenance bundles to operators while keeping fare policy and network obligations public | timetable floors, accessibility rules, sanctions, incident command, passenger complaint route, and durable route-map changes stay public | one visible operator chain, depot and platform dependencies, maintenance subcontract disclosure, open-book performance and asset-condition inspection | degraded-mode clause, emergency operating rights, takeover or replacement path, and asset-condition handback proof before renewal | riders keep one public challenge route; repeated operator-caused disruption can trigger seam redesign, mode review, or contract termination |
| **Care** | public care lane delegates bounded home-care or specialist capacity under public eligibility, safeguarding, and discharge rules | care entitlement, emergency priority rules, safeguarding referral, complaint and ombuds route, and data-retention rules stay public | approved provider roster, staff qualification duties, agency-chain disclosure, records portability, and site-audit rights | provider-failure continuity plan, temporary public or mutual-aid backfill, handover of care plans, and structural review if vacancy or churn persists | clients and families can still file through one public route; repeated provider instability can open repair, amendment, or sunset review of the delegated model |

The exact field content will vary, but the packet form should stay recognizable.

## Failure modes

- **public duty by outsourced wrapper**: the field says delivery is delegated, but the operator now controls access, exceptions, timing, or evidence in practice.
- **client hollowing**: the principal lane keeps the badge of accountability while losing the capability to specify, inspect, challenge, or resume.
- **operator-chain fog**: nobody can see the actual chain of primes, subs, platforms, processors, and partner agencies making the service real.
- **continuity theater**: step-in, handback, asset return, and records portability exist on paper but cannot be executed at speed.
- **commercial amendment by stealth**: a change order or platform setting quietly redesigns the live field constitution without opening amendment review.
- **review-route evaporation**: residents are pushed toward the supplier’s customer-service logic instead of the public challenge and oversight routes that should still govern.
- **delegated emergency capture**: degraded or emergency operation gives the operator wider practical discretion without visible public reauthorization.
- **exit incapacity**: everyone knows the model is failing, but the field no longer has the data, staff, rights, or money to change course.

## Anti-escape-hatch tests

1. Can the field name one principal public lane that still owns the duty after delegation?
2. Does the packet distinguish the delegable slice from the reserved powers that remain public?
3. Is the full operator chain visible enough that the public can tell who actually does what?
4. Has the principal lane retained enough client, commercial, records, and continuity capability to supervise and resume?
5. Are step-in, suspension, records access, asset-condition, and handback rights concrete enough to execute under stress?
6. Do challenge, oversight, evidence, mode, repair, and amendment routes still reach the delegated slice rather than stopping at the contract boundary?
7. Can repeated supplier failure or constitutional drift force redesign, replacement, re-insourcing, or sunset rather than another extension by habit?
8. Can later archive notes cite this packet in a few lines instead of rebuilding the whole outsourcing-versus-state debate every time?

A delegation packet exists so a field can borrow delivery capacity without borrowing away the public duties that made the field governmental in the first place.

## Compression rule for the archive

If a later note can tell you **which lane owns the field, how the seams work, how evidence triggers action, how the public can challenge it, how oversight reaches it, and how the constitution can be amended** but cannot also tell you **which slice is actually delegated, which powers stay public, what operator chain is live, what retained capability remains inside the principal lane, and how step-in plus handback really work**, then it still has **a field constitution that becomes vague exactly where public duty meets outside delivery capacity**.
