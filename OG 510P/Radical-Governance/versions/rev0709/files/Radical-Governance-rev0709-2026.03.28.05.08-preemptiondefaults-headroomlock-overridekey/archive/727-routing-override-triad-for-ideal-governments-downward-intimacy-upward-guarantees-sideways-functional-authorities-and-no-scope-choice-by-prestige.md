# 727 — Routing override triad for ideal governments: downward intimacy, upward guarantees, sideways functional authorities, and no scope choice by prestige

## One-line thesis

Once the archive has a default routing table for common public functions, departures from that default should occur only through one explicit override triad: **downward** when intimacy, place variance, or rapid correction dominates; **upward** when rights floors, solidarity, macro-risk, security, or interoperability dominates; and **sideways** into a bounded functional authority when the real system footprint mismatches ordinary civic territory.

## Why this matters

`726` gives the archive a reusable **default answer** to where common government work usually belongs. That already reduces one recurring failure: reopening the whole scale argument from block to planet every time a field is mentioned.

What it still leaves open is the next question. Once the default exists, **when may a serious polity depart from it?**

Without a compact override doctrine, the archive risks three opposite errors.

The first is **prestige drift**: work is pushed upward simply because higher tiers appear more important.

The second is **romantic localism**: intimate rhetoric is used to keep work below the level needed for equal protection, pooled risk, or network integrity.

The third is **quango escape**: a real cross-boundary system is routed into an in-between body without deciding whether it should remain a narrow functional authority or be constitutionalized as shared territorial government.

A tighter answer is to allow departure from the default routing table only through three declared moves: **down**, **up**, or **sideways**. The point is not to make the archive rigid. It is to make departures legible enough that later notes can explain why a routing default was rebutted, by what kind of reason, and with what review path.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Treat the default route as a rebuttable presumption, not as destiny

The routing table in `726` should be the archive’s starting position for common functions. A note may rebut that starting position, but only by saying what kind of override is being claimed.

That means every departure should be classed as one of three moves:

- **downward override**,
- **upward override**,
- or **sideways override into a bounded functional authority or compact**.

If the author cannot say which of those is happening, the archive is probably looking at scope drift rather than reasoned design.

### 2. Use a downward override only when intimacy, heterogeneity, or correction speed truly dominates

A function should move **below** its default home only when the key constitutional advantage comes from staying closer to lived context.

Typical downward reasons include:

- strong neighbourhood or site-specific variation,
- high dependence on trust, witness, and legible personal access,
- rapid local correction being more valuable than broad uniformity,
- manageable spillovers,
- and a financing model that does not secretly depend on higher-tier redistribution to remain honest.

This is the route for matters where the cost of distance is higher than the value of standardization.

### 3. Use an upward override only when floors, pooling, or cross-boundary integrity clearly dominate

A function should move **above** its default home only when a higher level is needed to protect something a smaller unit cannot reliably secure on its own.

Typical upward reasons include:

- a rights floor that should not vary by postcode,
- risk-pooling or equalization that needs a larger base,
- macroeconomic or strategic stability,
- strong spillovers onto neighbouring jurisdictions,
- one shared interface or standards spine,
- or peace, security, and commons protection that plainly outrun lower tiers.

The archive should therefore prefer upward override for guarantees, equalization, and interoperable baselines, not for status display or managerial impatience.

### 4. Use a sideways override when the real footprint is a system, not a civic territory

A function should move **sideways** when neither the smaller territorial unit nor the larger one fits the actual system footprint.

Typical sideways cases include:

- watersheds and flood systems,
- commuter sheds and fare networks,
- freight corridors,
- grids and market operators,
- ports and logistics platforms,
- hospital referral or trauma networks,
- ecosystem basins,
- and treaty-bound technical regimes whose operational footprint is cross-border but bounded.

The sideways move is not a loophole for constitutional vagueness. It is a recognition that some public work lives on a footprint that is real yet not general-purpose territory.

### 5. Keep sideways institutions narrow until they begin making broad territorial bargains

A sideways override should normally produce a **typed, bounded authority** rather than a disguised new government.

That authority may need a board, technical staff, a levy or budget lane, review routes, and public participation windows proportionate to its work. But it should remain narrow while it is mainly handling one system.

If it starts repeatedly deciding broad distributive questions for a territory — who grows, who pays, who bears risk, how housing and infrastructure burdens are shared, which communities absorb loss first — the archive should reopen the design under the constitutional-upgrade logic already developed elsewhere. Sideways routing should not be allowed to become permanent territorial rule by functional wrapper.

### 6. Split mixed fields before deciding whether the override is down, up, or sideways

Large sector labels often contain several override logics at once.

A field such as health, policing, education, housing, migration, or water may include:

- one lane that wants local witness and intake,
- one lane that wants metropolitan or basin-scale systems,
- and one lane that wants national or treaty-level guarantees.

The archive should therefore run the `702` decomposition discipline before treating the whole field as a single override case. One field can legitimately contain all three override directions across different lanes.

### 7. Require one short routing-rebuttal statement whenever a default is displaced

When a later note departs from the default routing table, it should leave behind one short routing-rebuttal statement naming:

1. the default route from `726`,
2. the override type claimed,
3. the load-bearing reason for rebuttal,
4. the lane or lanes affected,
5. what remains at the default home,
6. and what review or recheck should revisit the departure.

This keeps departures compact and mergeable. The archive should not require essay-length restatement every time a routing default is displaced.

### 8. Treat emergency upward moves as temporary overrides, not as silent constitutional settlement

Crises may justify moving authority upward for a period. Epidemic response, war logistics, cyber defence, evacuation, or grid integrity may require command concentration that would be excessive in ordinary times.

But the archive should classify that as a **temporary upward override** with named triggers, sunset or review clocks, and a visible route back to the ordinary stack. Emergency necessity should not be allowed to launder a permanent rerouting that no normal deliberation ever approved.

### 9. Prefer reversible overrides where the underlying reason may weaken

Some departures should be sticky because the underlying condition is durable. A national rights floor or a maritime treaty baseline is not likely to become hyper-local later.

Other departures should remain reviewable because the reason may weaken:

- local capacity may improve,
- a metro tier may finally be created,
- a data or interoperability layer may reduce the need for full centralization,
- or a functional authority may outgrow its narrow mandate and need a constitutional upgrade.

The archive should therefore ask not only **why was the default rebutted?** but also **what future change would make the default or another route safer again?**

## Compact override triad

| Override direction | Core reason | Typical use | Main danger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Downward** | intimacy, heterogeneity, rapid correction, lived context | neighbourhood stewardship, building-level witness, very local place management | rights lotteries, fiscal fiction, spillovers ignored |
| **Upward** | rights floors, solidarity, pooling, macro-risk, interoperability, security | equal protection baselines, redistribution, strategic infrastructures, treaty-level narrow waists | prestige centralization, local hollowing, permanent emergency ratchet |
| **Sideways** | real system footprint mismatches ordinary civic territory | basins, corridors, metro systems, grids, ports, referral networks, technical regimes | quango escape, weak legitimacy, hidden territorial government |

The archive should use this triad as a routing grammar, not as a substitute for field-specific packet work.

## Guardrails

- Do not rebut a routing default merely because a higher or lower tier is politically fashionable.
- Do not narrate local delivery presence as proof that the whole field belongs below its default home.
- Do not use a sideways body to avoid answering whether broad territorial power now exists.
- Do not move a function upward for uniformity when a common floor or interoperability rule would be enough.
- Do not move a function downward if the financing and guarantee model still depends on unnamed higher-tier rescue.
- Do not treat temporary emergency override as ordinary constitutional settlement.

## Failure modes

- **prestige routing** — the function moves up because upper tiers appear more serious.
- **intimacy camouflage** — a localist argument hides the higher-tier guarantees or pooled money it still depends on.
- **system denial** — a real basin, corridor, metro, or referral footprint is forced into the wrong territorial box.
- **quango escape** — a powerful in-between authority stays too narrow in law for the public power it actually exercises.
- **bundle confusion** — a mixed field is routed as one thing even though its lanes point in different directions.
- **emergency ratchet** — temporary upward control becomes the new normal without explicit constitutional reconsideration.

## Practical tests

A routing override is being handled honestly when a note can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Does it start from the default route rather than ignoring it?
2. Does it clearly classify the departure as downward, upward, or sideways?
3. Is the rebuttal reason load-bearing rather than rhetorical prestige or habit?
4. Has the note separated mixed lanes before claiming one override for the whole field?
5. If the move is sideways, is the body still narrow enough to match its justification, or has it become territorial government in disguise?
6. If the move is temporary, is the return or rereview path visible?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a later note says **the default route does not fit this function** but cannot also say **whether the honest correction is downward intimacy, upward guarantees, or sideways system-footprint routing — and what would reopen that choice later**, then it still has **scope exception without an override grammar**.
