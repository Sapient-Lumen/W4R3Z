# 721 — Notice packets for lane-typed government fields: service rules, proof models, clock starts, failed delivery, and no government by presumed receipt

## One-line thesis

Once a field already has service, case, and decision packets, it should also publish one narrow **notice packet** saying which communications are merely informational, which are consequential official notices, which acts require formal service, what address or notice-home hierarchy governs, which proof model attaches to each lawful channel, when clocks start, how failed delivery is repaired, and what continuity rule survives postal disruption, portal outage, representative change, or cross-border transmission — so the field does not decay into government by presumed receipt, channel ambush, or deadline folklore.

## Why this matters

The recent packet spine is now strong enough to describe much of a whole field constitution without making every later note rewrite miniature administrative law.

- `718` gives the service packet.
- `719` gives the case packet.
- `720` gives the decision packet.

That sequence can now say what the field promises, how a matter becomes a live case, and what a consequential determination must contain.

But one practical constitutional question still lacks a compact reusable object:

**how does a consequential act actually reach the affected person or organisation in a way the field can lawfully stand behind?**

The archive already has broader doctrine here.

- `424` separates front-door notice from later decision-specific explanation.
- `446` says durable state must outrank vanishing status messages.
- `472` says case lineage should survive withdrawal, supersession, and reopen.
- `482` says clocks must stay honest.
- `573` says permits and licenses need legible administrative procedure.
- `575` says official notice, service, and proof of delivery are a constitutional contact layer.
- `656` says pending matters need honest transition rules at cutover.
- `709` says challenge routes need visible entry and clocks.
- `716` says information packets must name the canonical case and record home.
- `720` says decision packets must separate decision date, notice date, operative date, and review linkage.

This note does not replace those. It compresses them into one reusable packet for the newer field-constitution stack.

Without a notice packet, fields drift in predictable ways.

First, they drift by **presumed receipt**: a stale address, dead inbox, or brittle portal becomes the field’s silent theory of legal effect.

Second, they drift by **channel ambush**: people discover too late that one route was merely informational while another carried the only legally consequential message.

Third, they drift by **deadline folklore**: nobody can clearly say whether the clock runs from dispatch, attempted delivery, secure-message availability, actual opening, or a separate deemed-service rule.

Fourth, they drift by **repair denial**: failed delivery is treated as the recipient’s problem rather than as a governed trigger for correction, reissue, alternate service, or hold.

A notice packet exists so a field’s contact layer becomes as explicit as its money, workforce, information, service, case, and decision constitutions.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Separate reminders, status updates, official notices, formal service, and public publication

A serious field should not use one word such as notification for all of these.

The packet should visibly distinguish:

- convenience reminders or alerts;
- ordinary status updates that do not by themselves change legal position;
- official notices that communicate consequential state or obligation;
- formal service acts where law requires a tighter transmission method;
- and public publication, which may support transparency or fallback but is not automatically the same thing as personal notification.

This matters because many fields quietly turn an email alert, portal banner, or generic dashboard update into the practical equivalent of legal service without ever saying so.

### 2. Name the notice classes and the serving lane

A normal packet should publish a short list of **notice classes** before discussing any one field’s detailed merits logic.

Typical notice classes differ by field, but most consequential systems need some subset of:

- receipt or acknowledgement notice;
- completeness, defect, or pause notice;
- hearing or interview notice;
- information or evidence request notice;
- provisional adverse or intent notice where the person may still respond before a final decision;
- final decision notice;
- payment, compliance, suspension, or enforcement-linked notice;
- transfer, merger, or successor-home notice;
- correction, replacement, or withdrawal notice for a prior act.

The packet should also name the **serving lane**: the lane that legally owns service even where a shared service center, clerk’s office, municipality, contractor, or platform physically transmits the message.

### 3. Publish one notice-home hierarchy and one address-for-service rule

A field should not leave people guessing which inbox, mailbox, representative account, or portal actually counts.

The packet should therefore say, in order of authority:

- what counts as the primary notice home for the case or person;
- how an address for service or representative address is declared and changed;
- when digital delivery may replace paper, and when it may not;
- what fallback applies if the primary notice home fails;
- and which changes in representation, custody, housing, or legal status trigger a mandatory notice-home refresh.

This is where the field stops treating contact data as generic profile trivia and starts treating it as part of due process.

### 4. Bind each lawful channel to a proof model instead of pretending every dispatch proves the same thing

A notice packet should not merely list channels. It should say what each channel can actually prove.

For each lawful method, the packet should name the proof model, for example:

- proof of dispatch only;
- proof of delivery attempt;
- proof of delivery to a physical or electronic address;
- proof that the message became available in a secure notice home;
- proof of acceptance or signature;
- or proof sufficient only when paired with a deemed-service rule created elsewhere in law.

The packet should also say what the field does **not** infer. An email alert that says “new mail is available” is not the same thing as proof that the consequential notice itself was read, understood, or even delivered by the same channel.

### 5. Keep clock anchors explicit: dispatch date, notice date, deemed date, actual receipt evidence, and operative date are not interchangeable

A field should not need ombuds or court intervention before anyone can tell which date matters.

The packet should therefore say, for each consequential notice class:

- when the message was created or dispatched;
- what counts as the notice date;
- whether any deemed-service rule exists, and when it applies;
- whether rebuttal or non-receipt evidence can displace the deemed date;
- what challenge, response, payment, appearance, or compliance clock starts from which anchor;
- and whether the decision or obligation becomes operative on notice, later than notice, or only after another interval expires.

This is where notice discipline docks directly to `719` and `720`.

### 6. Treat failed delivery as a governed repair event, not as evidence that the person no longer matters

A returned letter, dead portal account, bounced secure message, wrong representative, or inaccessible format is not merely an operations annoyance. It is a constitutional event.

A normal packet should therefore say:

- what counts as failed delivery;
- when ordinary service must stop and repair begins;
- who may correct contact data and on what proof;
- when the field must reissue, resend by another lawful method, or extend a clock;
- when substitute or alternative service is allowed;
- and what minimum record of the failure and repair remains in the case lineage.

Fields that skip this end up governing by convenience: the administration moves on because the system says sent.

### 7. Keep secure notice homes and convenience alerts visibly distinct

Digital delivery usually needs two layers.

One layer is the **authoritative notice home**: the secure inbox, portal, or registered-delivery surface where the actual rights-bearing communication lives.

The other is the **convenience alert**: email, SMS, app push, or phone reminder telling the person new official correspondence is available.

A good notice packet should say which layer carries legal weight, what the alert can and cannot prove, and what fallback applies when alerts fail but the authoritative notice home still exists — or vice versa.

Otherwise fields collapse into portal-shell government on one side or screenshot-folklore government on the other.

### 8. Make representation, custody, and cross-boundary situations first-class parts of the packet

Many notice failures happen not in ordinary same-address delivery, but when the person is represented, detained, hospitalized, displaced, abroad, or in another institution.

The packet should therefore say:

- when notice goes to the person, the representative, or both;
- how substitute recipients are recorded;
- what extra rules apply to cross-border transmission;
- when publication or public posting is a last resort rather than a convenience;
- and what accessibility, language, or assisted-delivery obligations survive even when the route changes.

A field that treats these as edge cases is usually designing for the easiest recipients and governing everyone else by exception handling.

### 9. Keep the packet small enough that later field notes can cite it instead of rebuilding notice doctrine from scratch

A normal notice packet should fit in one compact block.

If a later note needs pages of prose to explain what counts as service, which inbox or mailbox matters, what proof a channel yields, when clocks start, how failed delivery is repaired, and how a representative or successor home changes notice routing, then the field still lacks a reusable notice constitution.

## Compact notice packet

A normal notice packet should usually fit in one table-like block:

| Field | Notice classes and serving lane | Notice-home hierarchy and proof model | Clock anchor and failed-delivery rule | Drift trigger and consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Income support** | benefit lane serves acknowledgement, evidence request, provisional adverse, final decision, payment-change, and reconsideration notices | packet names secure account or declared paper route as the notice home, distinguishes convenience email from authoritative mail, and records representative routing where present | packet distinguishes dispatch, notice, deemed, and payment-effect dates; returned mail, dead digital account, or accessibility failure triggers reissue or assisted route rather than silent clock burn | repeated disputes about whether a person ever received the decision opens repair, oversight, and notice-form redesign |
| **Planning and permits** | permitting lane serves completeness, consultation, hearing, decision, condition, compliance, and enforcement-linked notices | packet names applicant address for service, representative service, site-posting or public notice only where law allows, and channel-specific proof requirements | packet names when appeal clocks run from the decision notice, when compliance clocks run from service, and when substitute service needs explicit authorization | routine confusion between public posting and personal service opens redesign and legal-floor clarification |
| **Tax and public debt collection** | revenue lane serves assessment, correction, payment, review-rights, and collection-escalation notices | packet names correspondence preference rules, secure account mail versus paper mail, and proof retained for each route | packet states exactly when objection or payment clocks run, what happens during postal disruption, and when non-delivery evidence pauses or reroutes collection steps | if enforcement routinely outruns explainable service, the field opens challenge-led repair and control-plane review |

The exact field content will vary, but the packet form should stay recognizable.

## Failure modes

- **government by presumed receipt**: the field acts as though dispatch alone proves fair notice.
- **channel ambush**: one surface looks official but another hidden surface carries the only consequential message.
- **deadline folklore**: offices and users infer different anchors for the same clock.
- **portal-shell notice**: the secure portal exists, but nobody can say when availability, alert, access failure, and legal effect diverge.
- **returned-mail denial**: the system records failed delivery but no governed repair follows.
- **representation drift**: notice keeps going to the wrong person, former representative, or dead address because the field lacks a visible hierarchy.
- **publication laundering**: the field uses public posting or generic website publication as a shortcut for personal notice.
- **postal-disruption amnesia**: ordinary paper assumptions survive even when the route is materially broken.
- **cross-border fiction**: the field acts as though foreign transmission itself proved domestic legal effect.

## Anti-escape-hatch tests

A notice packet is not complete unless it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Can the field distinguish informational alerts, ordinary status updates, official notices, formal service, and public publication?
2. Is there one visible notice-home hierarchy for people, organisations, and representatives?
3. Does each lawful channel carry an explicit proof model rather than a vague assumption of receipt?
4. Can the field say which date starts each material clock and how deemed service can be rebutted, corrected, or repaired?
5. Does failed delivery trigger a governed repair route before the field escalates consequence?
6. Are digital notice homes, convenience alerts, and paper fallback kept visibly distinct?
7. Can the field explain how notice works when a person is represented, displaced, detained, hospitalized, or abroad?
8. Is the packet small enough that later notes can cite it instead of rebuilding notice law in prose?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a consequential public act can change a person’s position before the field can honestly say **what was served, where it was served, what proof exists, and which clock lawfully started**, the institution is still governing by **presumed receipt**.
