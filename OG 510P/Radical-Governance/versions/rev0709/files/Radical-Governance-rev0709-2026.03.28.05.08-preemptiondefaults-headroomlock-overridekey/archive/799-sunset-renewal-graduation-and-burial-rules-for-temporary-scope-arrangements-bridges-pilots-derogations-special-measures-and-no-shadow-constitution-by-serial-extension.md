# 799 — Sunset, renewal, graduation, and burial rules for temporary scope arrangements: bridges, pilots, derogations, special measures, and no shadow constitution by serial extension

## One-line thesis

Whenever a polity uses a **temporary scope arrangement** — bridge body, host authority, pilot, derogation, emergency measure, temporary exemption, special administrator, or other exceptional workaround — the archive should require one compact **sunset / renewal / graduation rule**: **declare the default end state at the start, time-limit the arrangement, raise the proof burden on every renewal, force legitimacy and finance upgrades before any “temporary” body becomes ordinary government, and archive the ending publicly so shadow constitutions cannot survive by serial extension**.

## Why this matters

The archive can now say **what the ideal stack is** (`790`), **how to benchmark a real arrangement against it** (`791`), **how to test it under shock** (`792`), **how to keep that testing on a public clock** (`793`), **how to move toward a better stack in phases** (`794`), **what proof should stay public during that journey** (`795`), **how repeated failure can justify intervention** (`796`), **who may challenge those judgments** (`797`), and **which peer set actually counts before scoring begins** (`798`).

What it still lacked was one compact doctrine for a persistent constitutional trick:

**call something temporary, then keep extending it until everyone forgets the ordinary constitution was supposed to look different.**

That trick appears at every scope.

A metropolitan “interim” host city keeps running the shared treasury for years. A provisional regional platform keeps controlling hospitals, grids, or corridors long after the supposed transition ended. A local-government commissioner or fiscal control board keeps getting renewed because no one wants to admit the intervention either failed or became the real regime. A treaty workaround secretariat keeps absorbing operational functions without the mandate, scrutiny, or finance of a genuine compact body. A pilot, sandbox, derogation, or exceptional asymmetry survives because it is easier to extend than to decide.

`794` already warns against permanent transitionalism. `796` already warns against permanent emergency custody. `797` already says renewals and handback denials should be highly reviewable. But the archive still needed one separate rule for the temporary object itself: **when should it expire, when may it renew, when must it graduate into the honest constitutional stack, and when should it be buried rather than quietly linger?**

That gap matters because temporary arrangements are often where constitutional drift hides. They are not always abuses. Some are necessary. But without a typed ending rule they become the easiest place to move money, power, exceptions, and dependence off the formal map while pretending nothing fundamental changed.

The archive therefore needs one compact **sunset / renewal / graduation / burial grammar** for temporary scope arrangements. Later notes should now treat this note as the place to go when the question is no longer only **how do we transition, intervene, or appeal?** but **what happens to the temporary constitutional weather itself once it has lasted long enough to count?**

## Pattern pack

### 1. Treat temporary arrangements as first-class constitutional objects, not as informal weather

A temporary scope arrangement should be named as such from the start.

That includes, for example:

- transitional hosts and bridge bodies,
- pilots and phased rollouts,
- temporary derogations or exemptions,
- special administrators and commissioners,
- emergency or crisis powers,
- temporary pooled-service shells,
- exceptional asymmetries,
- and treaty or intergovernmental workaround platforms.

If the arrangement is exercising public authority, moving money, binding members, or standing between the public and the ordinary constitution, it is not just an implementation detail. It is a constitutional object and should be treated that way.

### 2. Declare the default ending at birth, not only after drift begins

Every temporary arrangement should begin with a visible statement of its **default ending**.

The archive should prefer one of four declared endings:

1. **expire** — the arrangement stops and the prior or target arrangement resumes;
2. **renew narrowly** — the arrangement continues for a bounded additional period under a stronger reasons duty;
3. **graduate** — the arrangement becomes part of the ordinary constitutional stack, but only after legitimacy, finance, and review have been upgraded honestly;
4. **bury** — the arrangement ends and is archived as a failed, superseded, or completed temporary object.

If no ending is declared, the arrangement is already leaning toward shadow constitution by inertia.

### 3. Put time on the object itself, not only on the surrounding emergency or transition story

The archive should insist that the **measure**, **bridge**, **pilot**, or **special regime** has its own time discipline.

The Venice Commission’s updated Rule of Law Checklist stresses that emergency derogations and resulting measures must be necessary, temporary, and proportionate, with parliamentary and judicial review of their existence, duration, and temporariness. The point for this archive is broader than emergency law alone: even where the underlying problem persists, the exceptional measure should not float indefinitely without re-justification. citeturn663112view1turn663112view3

A declared crisis, transition need, or structural weakness is not a licence for measure-level timelessness.

### 4. Prefer sunset by default where uncertainty is high and ordinary legitimacy is not yet present

Where the arrangement exists mainly to test, bridge, or temporarily host authority, the archive should begin from a **sunset default** unless there is already a strong case for permanent constitutional adoption.

The European Commission’s Better Regulation Guidelines say sunset clauses can prevent obsolete legal provisions from remaining in force and may be particularly relevant for emergency legislation; they also say proposals should envisage when and how the measure will later be evaluated. OECD work likewise treats automatic review and sunset clauses as a discipline on lawmakers and as a transparent signal that outsiders will get a future chance to argue for retention, amendment, or removal. citeturn140627view0turn140627view3

The archive should carry that instinct into scope governance. If legitimacy is incomplete and uncertainty is high, expiry should be the ordinary baseline.

### 5. Make renewals harder than first adoption

A renewal should never be easier than the first temporary move.

The first temporary measure may sometimes be justified by urgency or by incomplete information. But once the polity reaches the end of the first period, it has fewer excuses. By then it has evidence, experience, identified harms, and a clearer picture of whether the temporary device is actually solving the problem or merely postponing an honest decision.

The archive should therefore require renewal decisions to answer more questions than the original adoption:

- what was learned,
- what was fixed,
- what remains unresolved,
- why expiry is not yet tolerable,
- why graduation is not yet honest,
- and what would count as the last renewal rather than just the next one.

`797` already raises the review burden for renewals. This note adds the design burden: renewal must become substantively harder as duration accumulates.

### 6. Distinguish graduation from drift

A temporary arrangement does not “graduate” merely because it became normal in practice.

Graduation should require a visible constitutional admission that the arrangement is now carrying ordinary power and therefore needs ordinary discipline. For example:

- a transitional metro host that effectively governs the shared city should not simply keep going; it should either be replaced by a real metropolitan authority or openly constitutionalised as such,
- a commissioner or emergency board that becomes the true treasurer or service governor should not be renewed into custom; it should either hand back or be replaced by a lawful and reviewable ordinary structure,
- a treaty workaround secretariat that now allocates funds, inspects compliance, or binds implementation should not stay a convenience fiction; it should either gain the legal and scrutiny kit of a real compact body or retreat.

Graduation is therefore a **conversion** of temporary power into ordinary, typed, and legitimacy-matched power — not simply the passage of time.

### 7. Tie graduation to legitimacy hardening and fiscal honesty

No temporary arrangement should graduate while still living on borrowed legitimacy.

Before graduation, the archive should expect the polity to answer:

- who now authorises the body democratically or constitutionally,
- what finance is now ordinary rather than ad hoc,
- what review lane will govern it,
- what records and publication duties will remain live,
- and which older host, emergency, or derogation powers will terminate because graduation occurred.

`794` already ties hardening power to hardening legitimacy. This note narrows that rule for temporary objects: **graduation requires legitimacy hardening, fiscal hardening, and explicit retirement of the temporary wrapper.**

### 8. Keep derogations and exemptions narrower than structural redesign

A temporary derogation should solve a bounded problem, not silently rewrite the whole allocation of authority.

OECD work on regulatory experimentation is useful here. It stresses that regulatory experiments should be time-limited, closely monitored and evaluated, and used to decide whether an approach should be maintained, modified, or terminated; it also notes the choice between sunset and permanent rules and the use of temporary exemptions or derogations in experimentation. citeturn471270view1turn471270view2turn471270view4

For the archive, the point is constitutional: exemptions, pilots, and derogations may be legitimate learning devices, but they should not become a disguised route to durable rescoping without the disclosure, legitimacy, and review that open redesign would have required.

### 9. Publish one live temporary-arrangements ledger

`795` already says important scope claims need outward proof. This note adds one specific proof surface: a **temporary-arrangements ledger**.

That ledger should identify, at minimum:

- the temporary object,
- its legal basis,
- its holder,
- the powers being exercised,
- its start date,
- its next expiry or review date,
- the default ending,
- the renewal and graduation tests,
- the review or appeal lane,
- and the current status: live, renewed, graduated, expired, or buried.

Without one ledger, temporary constitutional weather remains hard to count and easy to forget.

### 10. Force temporary objects to interact cleanly with transition, intervention, and contestability notes

The archive should not let temporary-object discipline blur together with adjacent functions.

- `794` should answer **how a polity moves in phases**.
- `796` should answer **how compelled repair escalates and hands back**.
- `797` should answer **who can challenge renewal, expiry, graduation, or burial judgments**.
- `795` should answer **what outward evidence stays public**.
- this note should answer **what finally happens to the temporary object itself**.

That separation matters because the same bridge body or special measure can sit inside all four questions without being reducible to any one of them.

### 11. Bury failed or completed temporary arrangements explicitly

The archive should prefer **burial with memory** over disappearance by quiet deletion.

When a temporary object ends, the polity should publish whether it:

- expired as planned,
- was terminated early,
- failed and was abandoned,
- graduated into ordinary government,
- or was replaced by a different temporary object.

The ending should remain discoverable so later benchmarking and stress work can see what was tried, what drifted, and what proved unsound.

### 12. After serial renewals, reopen the scope question instead of pretending the temporary object is still just a stopgap

There comes a point where repeated renewal is itself evidence.

If the same bridge, special measure, or derogation keeps returning, the archive should stop asking only whether it may continue and start asking whether one of three deeper truths has appeared:

- the target stack was under-specified,
- the host polity never built the kit needed for handback,
- or the temporary arrangement is doing work that actually belongs in the ordinary constitution.

At that point, `791`, `794`, and `796` should reopen the benchmark, upgrade-path, or rescoping question rather than treating another renewal as business as usual.

## Temporary-arrangement outcome grammar

| Temporary object | Normal reason it exists | Honest default ending | What renewal must newly prove | What graduation must newly prove | When burial is the right ending |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Transition bridge body** | carry authority during phased cutover | expire or graduate | cutover is still incomplete for reasons not caused by avoidable neglect | the bridge now deserves ordinary mandate, finance, records, and scrutiny | the bridge solved nothing or has been superseded |
| **Pilot / sandbox / phased asymmetry** | learn under bounded conditions | expire or graduate | the learning question is still live and the burden remains proportionate | the pilot has enough evidence and fairness to become ordinary policy | the pilot was inconclusive, distorted, or no longer justified |
| **Temporary derogation / exemption** | relieve a specific obstacle or permit limited experimentation | expire | the obstacle still exists and narrower alternatives are not enough | the exemption should become a general rule through ordinary lawmaking | the exemption created more distortion than learning |
| **Special administrator / commissioner / receivership** | prevent acute collapse or rights breach | expire or hand back | the holder still cannot safely resume ordinary control | a different ordinary structure, not the temporary holder as such, now carries the work | the intervention failed, is unlawful, or should yield to formal redesign |
| **Emergency or crisis measure** | address urgent necessity | expire | necessity, temporariness, and proportionality still hold for this measure now | any permanent change must move through ordinary constitutional or legislative routes | the acute justification ended or the measure drifted beyond necessity |
| **Treaty workaround / interim compact platform** | coordinate around a gap before a fuller compact is agreed | expire or graduate | members still need the workaround and cannot yet support a cleaner mandate | the body now merits explicit mandate, finance, compliance, and scrutiny | the workaround obscures accountability or blocks honest treaty choice |

## Renewal and graduation ladder

| Stage | Archive question | Required public proof | Typical destination |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Initial temporary move** | why is a temporary object needed at all? | necessity statement, legal basis, default ending, first review date | live temporary status |
| **First renewal** | what was learned and why is expiry not yet credible? | evaluation summary, narrowed scope or unchanged scope with reasons, next end date | one more bounded period or termination |
| **Serial renewal** | is repetition itself now evidence of deeper constitutional misfit? | cumulative learning ledger, explanation of missed handback or cutover, comparator and benchmark refresh | rescoping review, graduation preparation, or final expiry |
| **Graduation decision** | should this object become part of the ordinary stack? | legitimacy upgrade, finance plan, review lane, retirement of temporary wrapper, updated proof pack | ordinary constitutional object |
| **Burial / closure** | how is the temporary object remembered and closed? | closure note, successor or null-successor statement, archived record, end date | tombstoned historical object |

## Use rule for the archive

When later notes confront a bridge body, emergency host, pilot, derogation, special administrator, or other temporary scope arrangement, the archive should usually do these things in order:

1. **use `790` for the target scope stack**,
2. **use `791` to ask whether the underlying arrangement still benchmarks honestly**,
3. **use `794` to say whether the temporary object is part of a phased upgrade path or merely drift**,
4. **use `796` if the temporary object sits inside compelled repair, supervision, or handback**,
5. **use this note to decide whether the object should expire, renew narrowly, graduate, or be buried**,
6. **use `795` to keep the temporary-arrangements ledger and closure proof public**,
7. **use `797` when renewal, graduation, burial, or handback denial is contested**,
8. **use `798` when a graduation claim depends on showing the arrangement is ordinary among the right peer class rather than merely familiar in local folklore**.

The archive should not let “temporary” become a category that floats outside ordinary constitutional discipline.

## Guardrails

- Do not create a temporary object without a default ending and next review date.
- Do not let renewal ride on the same thin reasons that justified the first emergency or bridge step.
- Do not call drift “graduation” unless legitimacy, finance, and review have been upgraded openly.
- Do not use pilots or derogations to smuggle in durable rescoping without ordinary constitutional choice.
- Do not terminate a temporary object by deletion alone; keep a public closure trace.
- Do not let the temporary-object ledger become internal-only hygiene.

## Failure modes

- **serial extension constitution** — the same temporary object survives by repeated short renewals until everyone forgets the ordinary settlement was supposed to look different.
- **graduation by fatigue** — a bridge body or special administrator becomes permanent only because no one wants another redesign fight.
- **pilot without verdict** — a pilot keeps running after the learning question is exhausted because the polity prefers ambiguity to decision.
- **derogation creep** — a narrow exemption becomes the real operating rule while the general rule remains on paper.
- **renewal without memory** — each renewal is argued as if it were the first, with no cumulative ledger of what prior temporary periods were meant to achieve.
- **burial by deletion** — a temporary regime ends, but there is no public record of whether it succeeded, failed, or mutated into something else.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Is the temporary object listed on one public ledger with start date, end date, and default ending?
2. Does the first renewal answer what was learned, not just why delay is convenient?
3. Would a neutral reader be able to tell the difference between renewal and graduation here?
4. Has the proof burden risen as the arrangement aged?
5. If the object ended tomorrow, is there a closure note or tombstone path ready?
6. After serial renewals, has anyone reopened the benchmark or scope question rather than only extending the weather?

## Compression rule

If the archive wants to say not only that a temporary scope arrangement exists, but that it is **honestly temporary**, it should be able to do so with one short object naming the holder, legal basis, powers, start date, next review date, default ending, renewal test, graduation test, contestability lane, and burial path. If it cannot, the arrangement is already too shadow-constitutional to count as a well-governed temporary device.
