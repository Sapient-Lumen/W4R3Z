# 743 — Tombstone challenge and correction packets for retired government bodies, scopes, and shells: standing, material error, evidence chain, and no historical repair by helpdesk fiat

## One-line thesis

Once the archive can leave a dead authority with a tombstone (`741`) and later reroute that tombstone when successors drift (`742`), it should also keep one compact **tombstone challenge and correction packet**: one bounded review object for claims that the dead-page story is materially wrong, incomplete, or misleading, so successor maps, record homes, archive homes, aliases, and legacy-question lanes can be repaired with standing, evidence, dated reasons, and visible correction chains rather than by helpdesk fiat or silent rewrite.

## Why this matters

The archive now has a decent post-death grammar.

It can retire a scope lane (`642`) or terminate a residual estate (`740`). It can leave one durable tombstone stating that the thing is dead, where the records went, and where one bounded legacy-question lane still lives (`741`). It can also keep that tombstone honest when the first successor, archive home, record home, or lookup route later changes again (`742`).

What it still lacked was the smallest object for the next hard case: **what happens when somebody says the tombstone itself is wrong**.

That objection can arise in several ways. A successor body may claim that the tombstone overstates or understates what it inherited. A parent department may discover that the record home was misdescribed. An archivist may discover that a live archive link now points to the wrong preserved collection. A member of the public may arrive with an old identifier, office name, or case prefix that the tombstone omitted. A disclosure team may realize that the legacy-question lane named on the dead page cannot actually answer the class of requests people are being told to send there.

Without one compact packet for those claims, the same bad patterns recur. Corrections happen through helpdesk email and vanish. The newest holder edits the dead page as if its preferred version had always been true. Old aliases disappear because they look untidy. The original death statement gets overwritten in the name of clarity. Or the dead page remains visibly wrong because nobody knows who is allowed to alter a memorial object without accidentally reopening the dead authority.

The archive therefore needs one compact packet for **contesting and correcting the tombstone itself**. This packet should not be a broad appeal tribunal for every political disagreement about a past institution. It should be a bounded review lane for material errors in the dead-page memory object: successor claims, archive and record routing, identifier maps, legal basis labels, and the public explanation of what died and when. It should preserve the original statement, preserve the later rerouting chain, and still let the polity correct a misleading dead page before error hardens into official folklore. Without that packet, the archive still permits **historical repair by helpdesk fiat**.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Require a challenge-and-correction packet when a tombstone is plausibly material to rights, routing, records access, or public memory

Not every typo needs a packet. But whenever the tombstone could materially misroute a person, misstate a successor, misdescribe record custody, misidentify archive access, or erase a legacy identifier people still use, the archive should require one explicit review object rather than ad hoc editing.

This is especially important where the dead page still influences disclosure requests, case lookup, public inspection, audit trails, pension or benefit history, land or permit references, or the interpretation of which body inherited a legal burden.

### 2. Distinguish editorial maintenance from material correction

The packet should classify the alleged problem before doing anything else.

At minimum it should distinguish:

- **editorial maintenance** — typo, formatting, broken non-material link, or minor clarity change;
- **routing correction** — the public is being sent to the wrong successor, record home, archive home, or legacy-question lane;
- **identifier correction** — an omitted or wrong alias, acronym, URL, programme name, office name, or case prefix materially affects lookup;
- **status or scope correction** — the tombstone misdescribes what ended, when it ended, or whether there was a successor at all;
- **evidence correction** — a historic statement remains supportable only if source or archive references are repaired;
- **mixed correction** — several of the above travel together.

Without this classification, every change either becomes bureaucratically heavy or dangerously casual.

### 3. State who has standing to ask for correction

A tombstone challenge packet should name who may trigger review.

At minimum standing should extend to:

- the named successor or parent body;
- the record or archive custodian;
- the bounded legacy-question lane;
- an oversight, audit, ombuds, or disclosure office affected by the routing error;
- and any member of the public who can show that the tombstone creates a plausible misrouting, access, or historical-reference problem.

Standing should be broader than formal ownership, because dead-page errors often surface first through users, archivists, or wrong-door handlers rather than the current constitutional winner.

### 4. Demand an evidence chain, not just a preferred narrative

A material tombstone correction should travel with one compact evidence chain.

That chain should point, as relevant, to:

- the retirement or termination act;
- any earlier rerouting packet (`742`);
- record-transfer, archive, or custody decisions;
- public explanations previously shown on the tombstone;
- and the concrete wrong-door, wrong-link, or wrong-identifier evidence that triggered the challenge.

The correction packet should be lighter than a court judgment, but heavier than “we think the page should say this now.”

### 5. Preserve the original statement and mark the challenged field, not the whole dead page, as under review

The tombstone should remain legible while review is happening.

The archive should therefore prefer field-level review marks such as: **successor route under review**, **record home under review**, or **identifier map incomplete**, rather than collapsing the whole dead page into generic uncertainty. This keeps the public from losing the honest parts of the tombstone while one disputed piece is being checked.

### 6. Give every correction a dated reason and a visible before/after chain

If the correction is adopted, the packet should say:

- what the tombstone said before;
- what it says after;
- what evidence justified the change;
- who approved the correction;
- and whether the change is purely corrective, interpretive, or partially uncertain.

The point is not cosmetic version history. The point is to stop the dead page from becoming a smooth retrospective myth.

### 7. Separate correction of memory from resurrection of authority

A corrected tombstone remains a tombstone.

The packet should therefore block changes that would quietly turn a correction into reactivation, such as:

- reopening live intake through the dead page;
- replacing the death statement with present-tense service language;
- broadening a bounded legacy-question lane into a general front door;
- or letting a successor edit the memorial page until it functions as a live service hub.

The archive is correcting memory and routing, not reversing death.

### 8. Give wrong-door users a fairness rule during challenge windows

If a tombstone challenge concerns routing, archive access, or the legacy-question lane, good-faith users who contact the previously published route should not lose their place merely because the polity is correcting itself.

The packet should therefore state:

- whether prior addresses still forward during the review window;
- whether clocks pause or preserve filing dates for good-faith arrivals;
- and who is responsible for notifying any newly identified correct holder.

A polity should not become less accountable while it is repairing its own dead-page memory.

### 9. Update both the human-facing tombstone and the machine-readable lookup surfaces

A correction that fixes only the page and not the lookup map is unfinished.

The packet should require synchronized updates to:

- the public tombstone text;
- the machine-readable crosswalk or lookup map;
- any alias or identifier tables;
- and any internal wrong-door routing aids or records-access scripts that depend on the old statement.

Otherwise the archive preserves an official contradiction between surfaces.

### 10. Close every challenge with one of four explicit dispositions

The challenge-and-correction packet should end in one explicit disposition:

- **accepted correction** — the tombstone changes and the correction chain is published;
- **partial correction** — some fields change and others remain as they were;
- **no correction, clarified note** — the original statement stands but explanatory context is added;
- **referred onward** — the issue is really a broader legal or constitutional dispute and cannot be settled inside tombstone maintenance alone.

This prevents endless “under review” dead pages and stops tombstone maintenance from impersonating a forum for every larger fight.

## Guardrails

- Do not let the current holder rewrite a dead page without preserving the prior statement and the reason for change.
- Do not treat every typo like a constitutional review, but do not treat material successor, record-home, archive-home, or identifier errors as mere copy edits.
- Do not let correction windows suspend good-faith routing fairness for users arriving through the previously published route.
- Do not broaden a tombstone correction into live service restoration.
- Do not let machine-readable crosswalks drift out of sync with the human-facing dead page.
- Do not use “historical clarity” as a pretext for erasing ambiguity that genuinely existed at the time.

## Failure modes

- **historical repair by helpdesk fiat**: staff quietly edit the dead page without a dated reason, evidence chain, or preserved prior statement.
- **memorial capture by successor**: the newest holder rewrites the tombstone so completely that the dead page now serves the successor’s preferred narrative more than the historical record.
- **eternal disputed tombstone**: a materially wrong route remains marked “under review” for so long that users still get lost and rights-bearing questions keep bouncing.
- **field confusion inflation**: one disputed item causes the whole dead page to be treated as unreliable, wiping out useful information that was never contested.
- **cross-surface divergence**: the visible page is corrected but internal lookup tables, alias maps, or records scripts still send users somewhere else.
- **reactivation by correction**: the supposed fix reopens intake, general contact, or present-tense promises through a page that should remain a memorial object.

## Practical tests

A tombstone challenge-and-correction packet is honest when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Does it distinguish editorial maintenance from material correction?
2. Does it say who had standing to trigger review?
3. Does it preserve the prior statement and identify the exact field under challenge?
4. Does it carry one evidence chain rather than only a preferred new narrative?
5. Does it give a dated reason and visible before/after correction chain if the page changes?
6. Does it preserve fairness for users who contact the previously published route in good faith?
7. Does it update machine-readable lookup surfaces as well as the human-facing tombstone?
8. Does it correct memory and routing without reviving live authority through the dead page?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a polity can say **this authority is dead** (`741`) and can also keep its later reroutes historically legible (`742`), it should still be able to show one compact packet for **who may challenge a materially wrong tombstone, what counts as a correction, what evidence and reason chain must travel with the fix, how good-faith users are protected during the review window, and how the page is corrected without rewriting history or reviving live authority by memorial maintenance**. Otherwise it still permits **historical repair by helpdesk fiat**.
