# 735 — Rollback packets for ideal-government scope shifts: failed stabilization, return authority, pending matters, record lineage, and no reversal by headline alone

## One-line thesis

Once the archive can say how a function should be routed (`726`-`728`), ratified (`729`), kept continuous (`730`), witnessed at cutover (`731`), temporarily hosted (`732`), cleanly closed (`733`), and tested through a bounded independent-live window (`734`), it should also keep one compact **rollback packet** for the case where the successor does not hold: classify the rollback type, name the interim principal and public route, preserve the legal and evidentiary state of pending matters, move money/staff/systems/keys with the reversal rather than by headline alone, and refuse both automatic restoration folklore and disguised permanent recentralization.

## Why this matters

The recent ideal-government scope-shift cluster now answers a substantial chain of questions.

The archive can say **where a function should usually live** (`726`), **when a default should be overridden** (`727`), and **what minimum constitutional kit a sideways authority needs** (`728`). It can say **who must ratify a move across scopes** (`729`), **what continuity has to survive the move** (`730`), **what contemporaneous witness should freeze the handoff moment** (`731`), **how to govern a bounded temporary host** (`732`), **how to prove that host actually ended** (`733`), and **how to test whether the successor is genuinely holding the function on its own** (`734`).

What the cluster still lacked was the next practical object: **when stabilization fails, what exactly is the constitutional and operational packet for rolling the function back, in whole or in part, without losing cases, records, clocks, remedies, money, or blame?**

That gap matters because failed scope shifts are easy to narrate badly. A ministry says the transfer has been “paused,” but nobody can tell who now holds the live front door. A predecessor quietly resumes incident response while the public status still says the new body is in charge. Cases started under the successor are re-entered manually into the old system, breaking lineage and appeal rights. Records are moved back informally, but no one states whether the reversal is a temporary rescue, a partial lane handback, or a deeper admission that the new settlement was wrong. The result is a function that has effectively moved again, but only through rumor, emergency discretion, or operator improvisation.

The archive therefore needs one small packet that later notes can cite instead of re-describing the same failure every time: **a rollback packet for ideal-government scope shifts**. This packet should not romanticize reversal. It should simply force the polity to say what is being reversed, on what authority, for how long, with which protections for pending matters and historical proof, and through what next lane if the rollback itself becomes more than a short rescue. Without that object, the archive still permits a serious fiction: **reversal by headline alone**.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Trigger rollback through typed failure, not embarrassment language

A rollback packet should start by classifying the event that forced it. The polity should not hide behind vague phrases such as pause, temporary support, operational adjustment, or pragmatic bridge.

At minimum, the archive should distinguish:

- **contained stabilization failure** — a narrow successor failure that can be corrected without moving the whole function back;
- **renewed-hosting failure** — the predecessor or another host has already resumed meaningful runtime, control, or evidence support;
- **full rollback event** — the successor can no longer honestly count as the live principal for the relevant function or lane;
- **redesign trigger** — the failure suggests not just a bad cutover, but a misfit in the chosen settlement itself.

If the failure type is blurred at the start, every later claim about who holds the function and what must happen next will also blur.

### 2. Classify what is actually rolling back

Rollback is not one thing. A packet should say whether the reversal concerns:

- the **whole function**;
- a **typed lane** such as intake, case handling, records custody, approvals, payments, or incident command;
- a **territorial subset**;
- or the **public front door only**, while legal title temporarily stays elsewhere.

This matters because many bad reversals happen when the polity says “the function returned” even though only one lane returned, or says “the transfer is still on” even though the old holder again controls the only meaningful route.

### 3. Name the interim principal and the public route immediately

At the moment rollback begins, the packet should state two things without euphemism:

- **who is now the interim principal for consequential action**;
- and **which public route is authoritative while reversal is underway**.

These are not always the same body as the pre-transfer holder. A rollback may return work to the predecessor, move it into a bounded host arrangement, or re-route it into a third authority while the wider settlement is reconsidered. But the polity should never leave residents, firms, courts, auditors, or frontline staff guessing which door is real.

### 4. Preserve legal state separately from operating state

A failed stabilization period often tempts institutions to treat legal state and operating state as the same thing. The rollback packet should resist that. It should say whether the reversal is:

- a **temporary operating rescue** under the existing legal settlement;
- a **temporary legal and operating handback** under a bounded exception lane;
- a **partial legal unwind** affecting only named competences or territories;
- or a **full abandonment** of the attempted settlement.

Without that split, the archive cannot later tell whether the polity merely borrowed capacity again or actually changed the governing allocation a second time.

### 5. Keep one pending-matters schedule for every rollback

A rollback packet should force explicit treatment of live matters already inside the successor lane. At minimum it should state:

- which pending cases stay with the successor despite rollback;
- which transfer back or onward;
- which are paused and under what notice duty;
- which clocks continue, stop, or restart;
- and how rights to review, reconsideration, complaint, or judicial challenge remain reachable.

No polity should be allowed to fix a failed transfer by dumping pending matters into manual triage without a published state model.

### 6. Preserve record lineage and identifier continuity during reversal

Rollback should not destroy the proof that the failed transfer happened. The packet should therefore preserve:

- prior identifiers and crosswalks;
- state history across both directions of movement;
- transfer logs for records, decisions, and evidence bundles;
- and retrieval routes showing where the historical file now lives.

Where records must be recopied, rehydrated, or reindexed, the packet should say so. Reversal should not become a second provenance break layered on top of the first.

### 7. Move budgets, staff, systems, keys, and supplier instructions with the rollback

A rollback packet should be blocked unless it says what happens to the real operating spine:

- budget authority and payment controls;
- staff reporting lines, duty rosters, and privileged roles;
- access keys, credentials, certificates, and publishing rights;
- system hosting and monitoring responsibility;
- supplier instructions, contract call-off rights, and commercial signoff;
- and complaint, ombuds, review, and audit contact routes.

Otherwise the polity does not have a rollback. It has a press statement sitting on top of unchanged runtime reality.

### 8. Separate emergency rollback from renewed settlement

Some reversals are genuinely short emergency measures. Others quickly become the new practical settlement. The archive should force the distinction.

A rollback packet should therefore include:

- the maximum initial rollback window;
- the renewal lane and burden of proof;
- the trigger for reopening transitional hosting (`732`) if the reversal is really just renewed hosting by another name;
- and the trigger for reopening the wider redesign or rescoping lane (`729`, `631`, `641`) if the rollback is no longer a rescue but a new constitutional choice.

This blocks two symmetrical mistakes: pretending a real constitutional unwind is only “temporary,” and pretending a short rescue automatically restores the old constitution in full.

### 9. Keep blame, liability, and duty-of-repair legible across the reversal

Rollback should not let either holder launder responsibility. The packet should say:

- who remains accountable for acts already taken by the successor;
- who answers for harms caused by rollback itself;
- who maintains the historical file and response duties for old cases;
- and who pays for repair, replay, notification, or compensation where the failed transfer broke service or rights.

Otherwise every failed shift becomes an accountability shell game in which power moves faster than obligation.

### 10. Force one short end-state statement for the rollback itself

A rollback packet should end with one explicit next-state statement. It should say whether the reversal finished as:

- **successful temporary rescue with repaired successor relaunch to follow**;
- **bounded renewed hosting**;
- **partial permanent handback through proper ratification**;
- **full abandonment of the attempted settlement**;
- or **routing into a different scope or sideways authority after redesign**.

Without that closure statement, rollback becomes a second unbounded transitional zone layered on top of the first one.

## Guardrails

- Do not describe a material return of predecessor control as a mere pause or support adjustment.
- Do not let legal title, operating title, and public-route title collapse into one ambiguous status word.
- Do not reverse live work without a pending-matters schedule.
- Do not allow re-entry, rekeying, or ad hoc migration to destroy case lineage or record provenance.
- Do not claim rollback while budgets, credentials, suppliers, or monitoring still sit with the displaced holder.
- Do not let short rescue rhetoric hide a de facto new settlement.
- Do not let reversal launder liability for predecessor acts, successor acts, or rollback harms.

## Failure modes

- **reversal by headline alone**: the polity announces a rollback, but real control, systems, or public routes remain elsewhere.
- **case amnesia on return**: pending matters are re-entered or re-triaged without preserving state, clocks, or appeal lineage.
- **double provenance break**: the original transfer damaged the record chain and the rollback damages it again.
- **temporary-again forever**: rollback remains “short term” indefinitely because nobody reopened the proper hosting or redesign lane.
- **automatic restoration folklore**: institutions act as if the pre-transfer constitution simply reappears intact, even though staff, systems, suppliers, and legal states have materially changed.
- **blame laundering by reversal**: both old and new holders claim that the other now owns the harms.

## Practical tests

A rollback regime is honest when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Does the packet classify the failure type rather than hiding it in vague adjustment language?
2. Does it say exactly what is rolling back: whole function, named lane, territory, or front door?
3. Does it name the interim principal and the authoritative public route immediately?
4. Does it separate operating rescue from legal unwind?
5. Is there a pending-matters schedule covering transfer, pause, clocks, and remedies?
6. Are identifiers, records, and evidence chains preserved across the reversal?
7. Do money, staff, systems, keys, suppliers, and oversight routes move with the rollback rather than after it?
8. Does the packet distinguish temporary rescue from renewed hosting, permanent handback, or wider redesign?
9. Are blame, liability, and duty-of-repair allocated visibly across both directions of movement?
10. Does the rollback end in one explicit next-state statement rather than another unbounded transition?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a polity can say **the transfer failed** and **the function has moved back**, it should also be able to show one compact packet proving **what failed, what exactly rolled back, who now holds the live route, how pending matters and records survived, what operating spine moved with the reversal, and whether the rollback is a short rescue or a new settlement**. Otherwise it still permits **reversal by headline alone**.
