# 763 — Caretaker-government and pre-election-restraint necessity tests for ideal governments: routine administration, incoming-government consultation, major appointments freeze, emergency carve-outs, and no mandate stretch between verdict and handover

## One-line thesis

Ideal governments should treat dissolution, hung-parliament bargaining, post-election transition, and imminent change of government as **a distinct continuity-with-restraint mode rather than business as usual**: outgoing governments should continue routine administration and urgent protection of the public, but major policy, appointments, contracts, publicity, and irreversible international or fiscal commitments should pause, narrow, or move only after consultation with the likely successor, so no one stretches an expiring mandate through the handover gap.

## Why this matters

The archive already says a great deal about **government formation and removal** (`752` in the intended sequence), **emergency powers** (`757`), **vacancy and acting-office rules** (`761`), **public appointments** (`760`), **electoral administration** (`552`), and **political competition** (`500`). What it still lacked as one compact doctrine was a cleaner rule for the period when a government remains legally in office but has lost, or may shortly lose, the full political basis for ordinary discretion:

**what should government be allowed to do between mandate uncertainty and lawful replacement?**

Many constitutional abuses happen in the narrow band between formal legality and democratic legitimacy. An outgoing government may still control ministries, communications staff, appointments, contracts, grants, and international representation. If no caretaker discipline exists, that gap becomes a shadow mandate: allies are installed, procurement accelerates, discretionary advertising surges, treaties or concessions are rushed, and successors inherit faits accomplis.

Ideal-government design therefore needs a doctrine of **caretaker restraint**. The live design question is not whether the state should stop operating; it is how the state keeps operating without letting a fading or uncertain political mandate harden into irreversible advantage.

## Necessity table

| Necessity test | What counts as proof | Honest design consequence | Default answer if proof is weak |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Need to preserve routine continuity** | services, salaries, safety, and statutory administration must continue despite dissolution or coalition bargaining | routine administration continues | government shutdown logic |
| **Need to avoid irreversible pre-emption by an outgoing government** | election loss, hung parliament, or imminent handover makes major new commitments democratically suspect | caretaker restraint on major policy, appointments, contracts, and major negotiations | business as usual until formal replacement |
| **Need to handle urgent public danger** | delay would create serious legal, fiscal, security, or humanitarian harm | emergency or necessity carve-out with reasons and later disclosure | blanket freeze regardless of harm |
| **Need to protect successor choice where likely change is visible** | an incoming government or likely parliamentary majority can be identified or consulted | consultation with successor or negotiating parties before major action | unilateral outgoing action |
| **Need to prevent incumbency abuse during campaign and transition** | ministers still control publicity, agencies, grants, and civil-service machinery | pre-election and caretaker guidance on announcements, advertising, and administrative neutrality | discretionary self-restraint only |

## Pattern pack

### 1. Name caretaker status as a real constitutional mode

The constitution, cabinet manual, statute, standing guidance, or equivalent should define when caretaker status begins, what triggers it, and who interprets doubtful cases.

### 2. Keep routine administration moving

Caretaker government is not paralysis. Ordinary service delivery, statutory compliance, payroll, routine procurement, emergency response, and the preservation of life, property, and institutional continuity must continue.

### 3. Freeze or narrow major policy decisions unless delay would clearly harm the public

Do not make major new policy choices when the democratic basis for making them is uncertain or about to transfer. Where action cannot wait, the government should record why delay would be harmful and why the chosen move is the minimum necessary.

### 4. Freeze major appointments and long-tail contracts unless necessity is genuine

One of the most common mandate-stretch tactics is to fill offices or sign deals that outlive the outgoing government. Senior public appointments, heads of regulators, major grants, major procurements, and long-tail public contracts should therefore presumptively freeze or narrow.

### 5. Require consultation with the incoming government or negotiating parties where large decisions cannot wait

Disaster relief, market stabilization, urgent security action, treaty deadlines, or critical continuity appointments may truly be non-deferrable. In these cases the outgoing government should consult the likely incoming government or relevant negotiating parties and explain the path chosen.

### 6. Keep the civil service, agencies, and public communications politically neutral

Pre-election and caretaker periods are especially vulnerable to misuse of administrative resources. Ideal governments should therefore restrict partisan announcements, promotional advertising, selective grant timing, and use of official channels for campaign advantage.

### 7. Make emergencies a narrow carve-out, not a blank cheque

Caretaker periods do not suspend the duty to protect the public. But emergency necessity must stay evidentiary and bounded, with written reasons, temporal minimization, follow-up where possible, and later transparency.

### 8. Publish the doctrine, the decisions, and the exceptions

A caretaker convention that only insiders understand is easy to abuse. Ideal governments should publish the governing rules and, where significant exceptions are taken, record that fact, the reason, and any consultation performed.

### 9. Align caretaker discipline with formation, vacancy, and appointments rules

Caretaker government overlaps with hung-parliament bargaining, vote-of-confidence failure, temporary officeholding, and succession. These doctrines should align so governments cannot bypass one restraint rule by relabelling the problem as another.

### 10. End caretaker status cleanly when the new government is lawfully ready

Caretaker status should not linger ambiguously after investiture, appointment, or confidence is secured. The archive should prefer a visible end point.

## Guardrails

- Do not confuse caretaker restraint with shutdown or incapacity.
- Do not let outgoing governments make major appointments or long-tail commitments by default.
- Do not use emergency language to justify ordinary partisan policy during transition.
- Do not let publicly funded communications become campaign advertising in official dress.
- Do not keep caretaker status unwritten, unpublished, or dependent on insider custom alone.

## Failure modes

- **mandate stretch**: an outgoing government uses the transition window to bind successors through appointments, contracts, or policy commitments.
- **advertising incumbency**: official communications and public resources quietly serve campaign advantage.
- **freeze panic**: routine administration is wrongly halted because officials do not know what caretaker rules actually allow.
- **emergency laundering**: ordinary political decisions are rebranded as urgent necessity.
- **caretaker drift**: a government operates in indefinite semi-mandated mode because the transition endpoint is unclear.

## Compression rule for the archive

If a polity can tell you **who is still formally in office during an election or handover** but cannot also show **which decisions are now routine only, which are frozen, which require consultation, which emergency exceptions exist, and how administrative neutrality is preserved**, then it still has **continuity without a caretaker constitution**.
