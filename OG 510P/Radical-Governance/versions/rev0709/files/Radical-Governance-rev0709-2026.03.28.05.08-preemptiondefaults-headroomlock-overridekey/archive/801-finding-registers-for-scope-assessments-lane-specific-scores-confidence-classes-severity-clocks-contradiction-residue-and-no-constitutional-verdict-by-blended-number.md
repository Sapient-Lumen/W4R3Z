# 801 — Finding registers for scope assessments: lane-specific scores, confidence classes, severity clocks, contradiction residue, and no constitutional verdict by blended number

## One-line thesis

The archive should pair its answer-key, benchmark, assessment-mission, proof-pack, and contestability notes with one compact **finding-register grammar**: **every serious scope review should express its result lane by lane, state confidence and severity separately, preserve unresolved contradiction residue, allow no-score where proof is thin, and attach next-action clocks, so constitutional judgments are not collapsed into one blended number or one breezy headline verdict**.

## Why this matters

The archive can now say **what the ideal stack is** (`790`), **how to benchmark a real arrangement against it** (`791`), **how to stress-test it** (`792`), **when those checks recur** (`793`), **how to move toward a better stack** (`794`), **what outward proof should stay public** (`795`), **how persistent failure can justify intervention** (`796`), **who may challenge those judgments** (`797`), **which peer class actually counts before scoring begins** (`798`), **what must happen to temporary constitutional objects once “temporary” has to mean something** (`799`), and **how a serious assessment mission should be run** (`800`).

What the archive still lacked was one short rule for the moment after a mission, benchmark, drill review, renewal review, or intervention check has actually happened:

**how should the result be stated so it is honest enough to trust and precise enough to use?**

Later notes should now also treat `802` as the place to go once the missing problem is no longer the verdict grammar itself but **how the host must answer each material finding with owned repair work, resource posture, and closure proof**. They should now also treat `803` as the place to go once the missing problem is not how to state the finding or answer it, but **how a later verification review decides whether the claimed repair is really closed, still fragile, or back on the docket**.

That gap matters because governance assessment often goes wrong at the verdict layer. One polity gets a blended score that hides which lane is actually broken. Another gets a severe headline label without any confidence statement showing that the evidence is partial or contradictory. Another gets a thick report with many observations but no typed register telling later actors which items were advisory, corrective, urgent, unresolved, or simply unjudgeable on the evidence available.

The official models already point away from that mistake. OECD evaluation guidance treats criteria as complementary lenses whose use depends on the purpose of the evaluation rather than as ingredients for one mandatory aggregate score. WHO’s Joint External Evaluation practice reports indicator-level 1–5 capacities together with priority actions rather than one flat national number. UNDRR’s city scorecard keeps indicator-level scoring and even expects assessment rationale and proof for verification. Council of Europe monitoring of local self-government produces observations, recommendations, and post-monitoring road maps rather than one simplified constitutional grade. The archive should compress that lesson into one finding-register note: **serious scope assessment needs typed findings, not just activity and not just one blended verdict**.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Separate the assessment mission from the finding register

`800` says **how a serious review is carried out**.

This note says **how the resulting judgment is stated**.

The archive should keep those jobs distinct. A good mission can still produce a sloppy verdict. A good benchmark card can still be ruined by a finding register that collapses everything into one synthetic grade.

### 2. Do not default to one overall score

The archive should begin from a **no-single-score default**.

A single overall score may occasionally be useful for a dashboard or threshold rule, but it should not be the first or main constitutional output. It hides where the problem actually lives: vocation mismatch, kit weakness, fiscal incoherence, bad review routes, failed stress performance, weak maintenance discipline, mis-sequenced transition, overlong temporary machinery, or disputed intervention posture.

The basic unit of judgment should usually be the **lane-specific finding**.

### 3. Use lane-specific findings that match the question being asked

The register should only score or classify the lanes that the note under review actually opened.

Typical finding lanes include:

- **vocation fit**,
- **institutional kit floor**,
- **fiscal congruence**,
- **review and redress posture**,
- **stress continuity**,
- **maintenance posture**,
- **transition readiness**,
- **intervention proportionality**,
- **temporary-object status**,
- and **public-proof sufficiency**.

A benchmark refresh should not pretend to answer a handback question it never examined. An intervention review should not quietly issue a full constitutional grade if the mission was only scoped to one failing lane.

### 4. State confidence separately from the substantive finding

The archive should never let **confidence** disappear inside the score.

A finding can be severe and still evidence-thin. A finding can be positive and still only moderately supported. A finding can be mixed because the lanes disagree.

The register should therefore attach an explicit confidence class to each material finding, such as:

- **high confidence** — multiple lanes align and contradictions are narrow,
- **moderate confidence** — the evidence is adequate but partial or uneven,
- **low confidence** — proof is thin, stale, or materially incomplete,
- **disputed** — the contradiction residue is still substantial enough that the archive should not narrate closure.

The exact labels may vary later. The discipline should not.

### 5. State severity separately from confidence

Confidence answers **how sure are we?**

Severity answers **how serious is it if true?**

The archive should not let those collapse into each other. A low-confidence signal of capture in a key guarantor function may still justify urgent provisional checking. A high-confidence finding of a modest paperwork defect may still be routine rather than constitutional.

A compact severity ladder is usually enough:

- **watch** — keep under review,
- **corrective** — requires repair inside ordinary cycles,
- **urgent** — requires accelerated action or temporary safeguards,
- **stop-and-repair** — the finding is serious enough that continuation without remedy should not be treated as normal.

### 6. Distinguish structural defects from temporary degradation

The finding register should say whether the issue appears:

- **structural**,
- **recurring**,
- **shock-related but temporary**,
- **transition-related**,
- or **unknown on current evidence**.

That matters because a flood, strike, cyber incident, election-period freeze, or transition host arrangement can make a polity look constitutionally broken when the actual problem is temporary degraded mode. The opposite mistake is just as common: a decades-long under-financed review route gets narrated as an unlucky one-off.

### 7. Preserve contradiction residue instead of polishing it away

The archive should expect the finding register to name the main unresolved contradiction classes carried over from `800`.

Examples include:

- law versus practice,
- host narrative versus downstream experience,
- budget authority versus actual spend control,
- formal autonomy versus supervisory choke point,
- benchmark score versus stress performance,
- temporary label versus effective permanence.

A finding may still be issued while one of those contradictions remains open. But the unresolved residue should stay visible in the register instead of being laundered into clean certainty.

### 8. Permit no-score or partial-score findings where evidence is not good enough

The archive should prefer an honest **no-score**, **partial score**, or **unjudgeable** finding over a decorative number.

That is especially important when:

- the wrong territorial unit was initially used,
- comparator class is still disputed,
- key fiscal or legal records are missing,
- site verification was blocked where operations mattered,
- or the contradiction residue is still too large for closure.

The archive should treat no-score discipline as a sign of seriousness, not as a failure of nerve.

### 9. Give each material finding a next-action clock

A finding that names a problem but no next step is half-finished.

Each material finding should therefore carry at least one typed clock:

- **recheck date**,
- **management-response deadline**,
- **repair milestone**,
- **appeal window**,
- **intervention review date**,
- **renewal or expiry date**,
- or **follow-up mission trigger**.

That keeps the register docked to `793`, `794`, `796`, `797`, and `799` instead of becoming a shelf artifact.

### 10. Allow short synthesis verdicts only after the lane findings are visible

The archive may sometimes still want a short synthesis line for communication.

That is acceptable only after the lane findings are shown. A compact synthesis such as **fit**, **strained**, **failing**, or **temporarily unjudgeable** may be useful for orientation, but it should never replace the lane register, confidence classes, severity classes, and contradiction residue.

### 11. Publish the finding register as part of the proof pack

Later notes should now also treat `806` as the place to go once the missing problem is not how to phrase the verdict, but how to keep the underlying support set, contradiction set, redaction status, and exhibit lineage stable beneath that verdict. And they should treat `808` as the place to go once the missing problem is not verdict wording either, but how lane scores, confidence classes, and severity thresholds stay calibrated across teams rather than drifting by reviewer style.

`795` should not only publish that an assessment occurred. It should publish the smallest public-safe version of the finding register that lets outsiders see:

- which lanes were judged,
- what the finding was,
- how confident the reviewers were,
- how severe the issue was,
- what contradiction residue remained,
- and what happens next.

Otherwise the public gets the headline without the anatomy.

### 12. Let contestability attach to each material finding, not only to the overall verdict

`797` should be able to bite at the lane level.

A polity may accept the broad direction of a report while disputing one fiscal finding, one intervention-severity class, one no-score decision, or one claim that a temporary arrangement has become effectively permanent. The archive should therefore keep findings granular enough that objections can target the real disputed object.

## Finding-register minimum fields

| Field | What it tells later actors | Minimum form | Typical failure if missing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Finding lane** | what part of the scope arrangement is actually being judged | named lane tied to the review question | one report tries to answer every question at once |
| **Substantive finding** | what the reviewers concluded | short lane-specific statement or score | vague praise or alarm with no usable object |
| **Confidence class** | how secure the evidence base really is | high / moderate / low / disputed or equivalent | false precision and overclaiming |
| **Severity class** | how much constitutional or service harm is at stake | watch / corrective / urgent / stop-and-repair or equivalent | all problems look equally serious |
| **Temporal posture** | whether the issue is structural, recurring, temporary, or unclear | one short status label | one-off shocks become destiny, or chronic defects look episodic |
| **Contradiction residue** | what material disagreement remains open | one short contradiction note or none | disagreement disappears into the prose |
| **Next-action clock** | what happens after the finding | deadline, trigger, or follow-up rule | report shelf without closure discipline |

## Minimal finding-register example grammar

| Lane | Substantive finding | Confidence | Severity | Temporal posture | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Municipal kit floor** | elected council and treasury are real, but records and complaint handling remain underbuilt | high | corrective | recurring | management response in 60 days |
| **Metropolitan systems governance** | shared transport and flood functions are still coordinated by grant forum rather than democratically legible authority | moderate | urgent | structural | upgrade-path gate under `794` |
| **Regional platform capacity** | hospital and watershed platforms are real, but finance and data authority are split incoherently | moderate | urgent | recurring | focused review and fiscal repair plan |
| **Temporary intervention object** | special administrator remains in place beyond original rationale and now requires renewal-or-handback verdict | high | urgent | temporary drifting toward permanence | renewal review under `799` and contestability window under `797` |

## Use rule for the archive

When later notes need to say not just **how a scope arrangement was assessed**, but **how the resulting judgment should be stated so others can act on it without being misled**, the archive should usually do these things in order:

1. **use `790` for the target stack**,
2. **use `798` to declare the right peer class and territorial unit**,
3. **use `791`, `792`, `793`, `794`, `796`, or `799` to state the substantive question**,
4. **use `800` to run the assessment mission and evidence lanes**,
5. **use this note to express the finding register lane by lane**,
6. **use `808` when the missing problem is not evidence or wording but keeping the meaning of the score bands, confidence labels, and threshold calls consistent across reviewers and across time**,
7. **use `802` to convert those findings into a management response and corrective-action ledger**,
8. **use `803` when the missing question becomes whether a claimed fix may honestly count as closed or must reopen**,
9. **use `795` to publish the public-safe outward version of both the register and the response posture**,
10. **use `797` if one or more findings are contested**.

The archive should not let careful review end in careless verdict language.

## Guardrails

- Do not collapse lane findings into one blended number by default.
- Do not let a severe finding masquerade as certain when the evidence is thin.
- Do not let a high-confidence minor defect consume the same attention as a major structural failure.
- Do not hide unresolved contradiction residue to make the register look cleaner.
- Do not score what the mission never actually examined.
- Do not force a score when the honest answer is partial, provisional, or unjudgeable.
- Do not publish a headline verdict without the lane anatomy underneath it.

## Failure modes

- **blended-number constitutionalism** — one overall score hides where the real defect lives.
- **certainty laundering** — low-quality or contradictory evidence is narrated as a clean verdict.
- **severity collapse** — minor hygiene issues and major structural failures are treated as equivalent.
- **temporary/structural confusion** — degraded mode gets misread as constitutional design, or chronic weakness gets excused as one bad week.
- **registerless publication** — the public sees a headline or colour band without finding anatomy, clocks, or caveats.
- **appeal fog** — disputes are directed at the report in general because no material finding was typed sharply enough to challenge.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Does each material finding name the lane actually under judgment?
2. Can a reader tell the difference between confidence and severity for each important finding?
3. Is any major contradiction residue still visible rather than silently resolved by prose?
4. Where proof is thin, does the register allow no-score, partial score, or disputed status?
5. Does every urgent or corrective finding have a next-action clock?
6. If a synthesis verdict appears, can a reader still see the lane-level anatomy underneath it?

## Compression rule

If the archive wants to say that a scope arrangement is **fit**, **strained**, **failing**, or **temporarily unjudgeable**, it should be able to show one short register naming the judged lanes, substantive findings, confidence classes, severity classes, contradiction residue, and next-action clocks. If it cannot, the archive is still too close to **constitutional verdict by blended number**.
