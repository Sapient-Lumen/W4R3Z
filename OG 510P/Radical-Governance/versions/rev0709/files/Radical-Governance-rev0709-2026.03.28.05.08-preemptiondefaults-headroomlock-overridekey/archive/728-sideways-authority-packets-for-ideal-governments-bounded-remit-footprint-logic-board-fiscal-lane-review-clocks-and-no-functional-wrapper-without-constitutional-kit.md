# 728 — Sideways authority packets for ideal governments: bounded remit, footprint logic, board, fiscal lane, review clocks, and no functional wrapper without constitutional kit

## One-line thesis

When the archive routes a function **sideways** into a bounded functional authority rather than into an ordinary territorial tier, it should default to one compact **sideways-authority packet** naming the real system footprint, the exact remit and exclusions, the principal public lane that still owns the wider constitutional bargain, the legitimacy and fiscal model, the review and participation routes, and the upgrade-or-exit triggers — so basins, corridors, grids, ports, referral networks, and similar in-between institutions stay governable without pretending to be full territorial governments or dissolving into quango fog.

## Why this matters

`725` gives the archive one mixed stack from block to planet. `726` gives a default function-routing table. `727` gives a routing override triad that says when the honest correction is downward, upward, or sideways.

What the archive still lacked was the smallest reusable object for the **sideways case itself**.

That gap matters because the phrase **everything in between** is only useful if later notes can specify what an in-between body must look like without reopening the whole theory of special-purpose authorities every time. A watershed authority, freight-corridor body, grid operator, port system, trauma network, or cross-border technical regime should not require a fresh essay on each appearance. But neither should the archive let sideways routing become a vague permission slip for creating powerful boards with unclear remit, blurred democratic anchoring, hidden debt, and no visible trigger for constitutional upgrade.

The archive therefore needs one compact packet for the sideways institution: not a substitute for `543`, `596`, or the territorial-upgrade notes, but a small bridge between them. The point is to make sideways routing **typed, inspectable, and compressible**.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Use packet form whenever the honest route is sideways

If a later note says the right answer is neither the ordinary municipal, metropolitan, regional, nor national lane, but a **bounded functional authority** keyed to a basin, corridor, grid, port, airport zone, referral network, ecological landscape, or treaty-bound technical system, it should usually fill out one sideways-authority packet.

This keeps the archive from re-arguing the whole stack each time a real system footprint outruns ordinary map lines.

### 2. Start with the system footprint, not the institution’s branding

The first field in the packet should say what real footprint the body exists to govern.

Examples include:

- river basin or aquifer,
- metropolitan commuter shed,
- freight or logistics corridor,
- electricity or water network,
- port complex,
- hospital referral or trauma network,
- cross-border inspection, standards, or registry regime.

The archive should force the body to justify itself by the footprint it matches, not by a prestige label such as authority, district, corporation, agency, commission, or union.

### 3. Declare the remit and exclusions in the same breath

A sideways body is legitimate only if its remit is exact enough to keep the institution narrow.

The packet should therefore say:

- what the body governs,
- what it may decide,
- what it may merely coordinate or recommend,
- and what remains with the territorial governments above, below, or around it.

This matters because many in-between bodies fail not by choosing the wrong footprint but by drifting into adjacent powers through implication.

### 4. Name the principal public lane that still owns the wider constitutional bargain

Most sideways bodies should not be treated as self-grounding sovereigns. They remain embedded in a wider constitutional order.

The packet should therefore identify which territorial or treaty-level lane still owns the wider public bargain for:

- rights and due-process floors,
- redistribution or solidarity,
- broad land-use and territorial settlement,
- criminal or coercive authority,
- major constitutional amendment,
- and final review or escalation where the functional authority runs out of legal room.

This keeps the archive from mistaking a system operator for the whole state.

### 5. Match the legitimacy model to the severity of the powers exercised

A bounded technical body with narrow planning or operating duties can often rely on delegated representation, member-government appointment, or a mixed expert-public board.

But once the body can impose compulsory charges, incur substantial debt, condemn land, adopt binding plans with broad distributive effects, or repeatedly choose which communities bear growth, disruption, or hazard first, the packet should treat legitimacy as a constitutional question rather than a board-design afterthought.

The archive should therefore prefer one explicit legitimacy field saying whether the body is:

- advisory,
- delegated and board-governed,
- regulator-like with protected independence,
- double-legitimated through participating governments,
- or nearing the threshold where a territorial constitutional upgrade is more honest.

### 6. Declare the fiscal lane before praising technical efficiency

Sideways bodies often look efficient because their fiscal constitution is hidden.

The packet should therefore state:

- whether the body lives on tariffs, levies, member contributions, formula transfers, or grants,
- whether charges are voluntary, user-based, compulsory, or tax-like,
- whether debt is permitted and who stands behind it,
- and what capital, reserve, and transparency rules keep the body from becoming an off-map treasury.

A body whose public power depends on hidden guarantees, emergency subsidy, or debt parked outside the visible budget spine is not just technical. It is fiscally constitutional.

### 7. Give every sideways body one review lane and one participation lane proportionate to its role

A sideways authority should not become unreachable merely because it is not a full territorial government.

The packet should therefore identify at least:

- one review, complaint, appeal, ombuds, tribunal, or judicial lane proportionate to the kind of decisions the body makes;
- one participation lane proportionate to its real impact, such as hearings, consultation windows, stakeholder panels, petition routes, or public board access;
- and one public records or publication discipline that keeps agendas, plans, tariffs, rules, and performance visible.

This does not mean every functional authority needs an entire parliamentary ecology. It means no consequential in-between body should survive on operator helpdesks and annual reports alone.

### 8. Keep data, interfaces, and operating seams explicit

Most sideways bodies exist because several jurisdictions or institutions must share one system. The packet should therefore say where the authoritative shared facts live and how seams are governed.

That can include:

- shared indicators,
- basin or corridor maps,
- queue, dispatch, or network telemetry,
- interconnection rules,
- service or safety thresholds,
- data-sharing limits,
- and escalation rules when one member or operator fails.

Otherwise the authority will look bounded in law but unbounded in practice because whoever controls the common dashboard, protocol, or dispatch rail becomes the real governor.

### 9. Publish constitutional-upgrade triggers in advance

The archive should not wait until an in-between body is obviously overgrown before asking whether it has become real territorial government in disguise.

The packet should therefore list the triggers that would reopen the constitutional question, such as:

- repeated broad distributive bargaining across a territory,
- significant compulsory revenue or borrowing power,
- multi-sector territorial planning,
- direct rulemaking over daily place government,
- emergency powers that become routine,
- or a stack of several functional bodies now together governing one territory.

Once those triggers are crossed, the packet should point toward `606`, `608` through `626`, or other territorial-upgrade notes rather than pretending the body is still just a harmless wrapper.

### 10. Keep exit, merger, and handback thinkable

Some sideways bodies are durable because their system footprint is durable. Others are transitional, overbuilt, or later absorbed by a stronger metro, regional, or treaty regime.

The packet should therefore include a small exit field naming whether the likely long-run fate is:

- continued bounded operation,
- merger into a stronger territorial tier,
- split into narrower authorities,
- handback to component governments after capability repair,
- or dissolution once the problem class changes.

The archive should reject functional authorities that can explain how they start but not how they end or what would justify redesign.

## Sideways-authority packet

| Field | What the note should say | Default archive anchor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **System footprint** | the real basin, corridor, grid, port, network, or cross-border technical field that justifies sideways routing | `725`, `727` |
| **Problem class** | what kind of shared-system problem this authority exists to solve | `579`, `726` |
| **Remit and exclusions** | exact powers held, powers coordinated, and powers left elsewhere | `543`, `596` |
| **Principal public lane** | which territorial or treaty-level lane still owns the wider constitutional bargain | `725`, `727` |
| **Legitimacy model** | advisory, delegated board, regulator-like, double-legitimated, or territorial-upgrade-nearing | `543`, `606`, `612` |
| **Fiscal lane** | tariffs, levies, contributions, formula transfers, grants, debt rules, guarantee map | `581`, `596`, `608` |
| **Operating and data spine** | which shared maps, registries, telemetry, dispatch, or interface rules make the system work | `703`, `704`, `708`, `716` |
| **Review and participation lanes** | complaint, appeal, ombuds, hearing, petition, publication, and records routes | `709`, `711`, `723` |
| **Upgrade triggers** | what conditions would force constitutional upgrade or stronger legitimacy | `606`, `725`, `727` |
| **Exit or merger route** | continued bounded authority, merge, split, handback, or dissolve | `631`, `707` |

## Packet example grammar

A later note should usually be compressible into something like this:

| Field | Example entry |
| --- | --- |
| **System footprint** | multi-municipal flood basin with one downstream urban core and upstream storage dependencies |
| **Problem class** | basin-scale resilience, allocation, and spillover control that municipalities cannot internalize alone |
| **Remit and exclusions** | basin planning, flood works sequencing, emergency coordination, and ecological monitoring; no general land-use sovereignty or social-policy power |
| **Principal public lane** | municipalities keep local development control subject to basin rules; national level keeps rights floors, major funding, and final legality review |
| **Legitimacy model** | delegated board with municipal and state representation plus public hearings |
| **Fiscal lane** | formula contributions plus project grants and capital borrowing under published guarantee rules |
| **Operating and data spine** | one basin map, one telemetry spine, one warning protocol, one works register |
| **Review and participation lanes** | plan-hearing route, complaint route, judicial review of binding acts, open records |
| **Upgrade triggers** | reopen design if the authority starts making broad territorial growth bargains or imposing major compulsory levies |
| **Exit or merger route** | merge into metropolitan or regional government if the basin authority becomes the main territorial trade-off forum |

The archive should be able to read the in-between institution at this length before deciding whether more prose is needed.

## Guardrails

- Do not call an authority sideways if it is really doing general territorial government.
- Do not use branding, enterprise form, or technical language as a substitute for footprint logic.
- Do not leave the wider rights, solidarity, and legality bargain owner unnamed.
- Do not hide tariffs, guarantees, debt, or levy authority behind the language of efficiency.
- Do not let functional authorities become participation-free because they are not full legislatures.
- Do not leave constitutional-upgrade triggers implicit.
- Do not create a new note for every sideways case if the packet plus one local delta would do.

## Failure modes

- **functional-wrapper drift** — the body stays narrow in rhetoric while broad territorial power accumulates in practice.
- **board-first reasoning** — the institution is designed around appointments before the real footprint and remit are settled.
- **fiscal camouflage** — debt, guarantees, or compulsory charges hide behind technical-body language.
- **operator sovereignty** — whoever controls the common dashboard, dispatch rail, or protocol becomes the real governor without open constitutional recognition.
- **rights orphaning** — no territorial lane is clearly responsible for floors, remedies, or distributive correction around the functional authority.
- **immortal sideways body** — an authority explains why it was created but never what would justify merger, upgrade, split, or handback.

## Practical tests

A sideways-routing note is using packet discipline honestly when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Does it begin from the real system footprint rather than from the body’s label?
2. Does it say exactly what the authority governs and what remains elsewhere?
3. Does it name the principal public lane that still owns the wider constitutional bargain?
4. Is the legitimacy model proportionate to the actual severity of the powers exercised?
5. Is the fiscal lane visible enough to expose tariffs, levies, guarantees, debt, and dependency?
6. Can an affected person tell where to look for records, hearings, complaints, and review?
7. Does the note say what would force constitutional upgrade, merger, or handback later?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a later note says **the honest route is sideways into a bounded functional authority** but cannot also provide **one packet naming the system footprint, remit, principal public lane, legitimacy model, fiscal lane, review and participation routes, operating and data spine, and upgrade or exit triggers**, then it still has **an in-between institution without a reusable constitutional object for the archive**.
