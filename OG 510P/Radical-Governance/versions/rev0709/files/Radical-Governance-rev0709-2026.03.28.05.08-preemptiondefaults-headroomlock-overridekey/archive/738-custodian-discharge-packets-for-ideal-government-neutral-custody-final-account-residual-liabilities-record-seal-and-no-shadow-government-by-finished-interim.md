# 738 — Custodian-discharge packets for ideal-government neutral custody: final account, residual liabilities, record seal, and no shadow government by finished interim

## One-line thesis

Once the archive can place named surfaces into temporary neutral custody (`736`) and release them out again (`737`), it should also keep one compact **custodian-discharge packet** for the moment the custodian itself stands down: prove every custodial surface has been released, lawfully retired, or explicitly rehomed; freeze one final account of residue, liabilities, and records; preserve lookup and challenge routes after the custodian disappears; and refuse to let a finished interim body linger as hidden operator, archive gatekeeper, or excuse sink.

## Why this matters

The recent ideal-government scope-shift cluster can now say almost everything about the difficult middle interval between ordinary settlement and honest next settlement.

The archive can already say **where a function should ordinarily live** (`726`), **when routing defaults can be overridden** (`727`), **how sideways bodies should be constituted** (`728`), **who must ratify a move** (`729`), **what continuity must survive it** (`730`), **how to witness cutover** (`731`), **how transitional hosting works** (`732`), **how hosting actually ends** (`733`), **how post-closure stability is tested** (`734`), **how failed stabilization is rolled back** (`735`), **how named surfaces can enter temporary neutral custody when neither ordinary holder is yet honest** (`736`), and **how those surfaces can leave custody into a named next holder or narrower bridge** (`737`).

What still sat open was the last proof object in that sequence:

**how does the polity prove that the custodian itself can go away?**

That gap matters because neutral custody is often easier to start, and easier to partially unwind, than to truly finish. A custodian may release the public front door while retaining the only usable audit bundle. It may hand back live queues but continue to answer statutory queries because nobody named the successor complaint route. It may stop taking new action but remain the only body with authority to explain what happened during custody. A funding facility may survive after transfer to settle residual costs, legal claims, or wind-down obligations. A records repository may need to keep sealed logs, but the public should no longer treat it as an active governing front door. If none of that is typed, the archive leaves room for an interim body to survive after its constitutional purpose has ended.

Without a compact discharge object, three different confusions recur. First, **zombie custody**: the interim is declared over, but the custodian still operates one or more consequential surfaces because nobody proved they were truly gone. Second, **residual-liability fog**: liabilities, audits, FOI-style access routes, legal holds, or complaints survive the custodian, but nobody states who inherited them. Third, **shadow-government drift**: staff, vendors, regulated parties, or the public continue using the interim body because it still holds the best records, the best phone number, the safest permission set, or the only intelligible memory of the crisis.

The archive therefore needs one further anti-fiction object after `737`: a **custodian-discharge packet**. It is not a release packet. It is not a settlement certificate. It is the narrower proof object for standing down the neutral custodian itself after surfaces have been released, retired, archived, or reallocated. Without that packet, the archive still permits **shadow government by finished interim**.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Treat custodian discharge as a typed constitutional end-state, not as administrative fade-out

A discharge packet should never say only that the interim body “has now completed its role”. It should classify what kind of discharge is actually happening.

At minimum, it should distinguish:

- **full discharge** — every custodial surface has been released, retired, archived, or lawfully terminated, and the custodian ceases to operate;
- **discharge with residual estate** — live custodial work is over, but a tightly bounded residue remains for final accounts, claims, or record stewardship;
- **partial discharge** — one arm or surface of the custodian is discharged while another remains alive under a narrower mandate;
- **converted discharge** — the custodian ends, but one named residue function is reassigned into a successor archive, tribunal, department, or service operator;
- **failed discharge attempt** — the polity tried to stand the custodian down, but proof failed and custody, rollback, or renewed hosting remains necessary.

If discharge type is not named, public language will overclaim finality and hide the residue.

### 2. Require one disposition ledger for every surface that ever entered custody

A custodian should not disappear until the archive can show what became of each consequential surface it held.

At minimum, the discharge packet should classify every custodial surface as:

- released to an ordinary holder;
- released into a narrower bridge;
- retired and no longer operative;
- deposited into archival or sealed evidentiary custody;
- destroyed or disposed of under lawful authority;
- or still retained as bounded residue with a named review date.

The packet should not permit generic claims that “all relevant matters have been handed over”. It should preserve a typed disposition ledger for the public front door, rights-bearing queues, record spine, keys and credentials, payment or trust lanes, supplier instruction lanes, public status channels, and any extraordinary review or freeze surfaces that existed during custody.

### 3. Freeze one final account of money, contracts, claims, and wind-down costs

The custodian’s operating life should end with one final account rather than with fading memory. That final account should state:

- what funds the custodian received or controlled;
- what costs were incurred during custody and wind-down;
- what contracts, subcontracts, escrow arrangements, or hosted services remain open or are closed;
- what claims, liabilities, or contingent exposures survive;
- and who owns each remaining payment, reimbursement, recovery, or audit route.

A polity should not let the interim disappear while the residual estate is discoverable only through old invoices, supplier memory, or emergency appropriations.

### 4. Name the post-discharge home for residual duties that outlive the interim

Even when live custody ends, some obligations may continue. The packet should therefore identify who holds each residual duty after discharge.

That includes, where relevant:

- access-to-records and disclosure requests;
- litigation holds, subpoenas, or evidentiary production;
- audit follow-up and external assurance;
- complaint, challenge, or review routes tied to custody-era acts;
- pension, payroll, tax, or closeout obligations for the interim workforce;
- contract closeout, warranty, and indemnity issues;
- and statutory reporting, retention, or archival-transfer duties.

The archive should refuse discharge if the only honest answer is still “the custodian will deal with that later”.

### 5. Seal one final custody-era record package before the custodian disappears

A discharge packet should require a final sealed record package for the custody interval itself. The package should not reproduce the whole archive. It should preserve the minimum proof needed to reconstruct custody after the custodian is gone.

At minimum, it should point to:

- the custody instrument and later amendments;
- the surface schedule and any release packets;
- the event log for custody-era transfers, freezes, overrides, and releases;
- the final residue statement;
- the final account and sign-offs;
- the crosswalk from custody-era identifiers to post-custody homes;
- and the authoritative repository or repositories where these records now live.

If the custodian can disappear without sealing that package, then later review will depend on personal recollection and screenshot folklore.

### 6. Preserve one lookup route and one challenge route after discharge

The custodian’s disappearance should not make custody-era action practically unreviewable. The discharge packet should therefore preserve:

- one lookup path for matters touched during custody;
- one route for seeking the custody-era record package or its lawful successor;
- one complaint or challenge route for contesting custody-era conduct, where such challenge remains legally open;
- and one notice-home for residual official communications after discharge.

The route does not need to remain with the custodian. But it must exist somewhere named and durable enough that the interim’s disappearance does not erase legal memory.

### 7. Revoke or rehome interim-only powers, credentials, and delegations

A finished custodian should not keep operating power by technical inertia. The discharge packet should therefore require a named disposition for:

- emergency or superuser credentials;
- signature devices and delegated authorities;
- publication rights over official status channels;
- supplier-instruction rights;
- escrow triggers or decryption rights;
- and any extraordinary powers granted only because neutral custody existed.

Each such power should be revoked, reissued, archived, or rehomed explicitly. No polity should accept “nobody is using that account anymore” as proof that interim authority ended.

### 8. Put hard clocks on residual estates and require a next review if any residue survives

Residual estates may be honest, but they should remain obviously temporary. If any function, fund, archive, claim pool, or complaint route survives after discharge, the packet should name:

- the exact residue class;
- who runs it;
- what authority permits it;
- what date or event should terminate it;
- and what fresh review lane opens if the residue still exists when that clock arrives.

This prevents the archive from quietly replacing one unbounded custodian with a different unbounded residue shell.

### 9. Publish one short public discharge statement that is narrower than celebration rhetoric

The public statement for discharge should say:

- that the neutral custodian is standing down;
- whether discharge is full, partial, residual, converted, or failed;
- where live public routes now sit;
- where custody-era records and challenge routes now sit;
- whether any residual estate remains and until when;
- and what next review date or next packet applies.

It should not say “fully resolved” unless the packet actually proves there is no consequential residue left.

### 10. Reopen review rather than forcing discharge through unresolved ambiguity

If the polity cannot answer where the final account lives, who inherited custody-era liabilities, where the record package sits, or how a custody-era decision can still be challenged, then discharge is not ready.

In that case the archive should prefer one of three honest outcomes:

- continue the custodian under a narrower, explicit residual mandate;
- convert the residue into a named successor home;
- or reopen the release, rollback, or wider settlement lane rather than pretending the interim has vanished cleanly.

## Guardrails

- Do not let a custodian disappear before every custodial surface has a typed disposition.
- Do not collapse final accounting into generic closeout language.
- Do not leave disclosure, audit, litigation, or challenge duties floating after discharge.
- Do not let sealed logs or custody-era evidence become inaccessible because the interim body is gone.
- Do not tolerate interim credentials, delegations, or vendor permissions that simply “age out” without explicit revocation or rehoming.
- Do not let residual estates survive without clocks, review triggers, and named owners.
- Do not let the public lose the lookup or challenge route for custody-era acts.

## Failure modes

- **shadow government by finished interim**: the custodian is supposedly gone but still controls one or more consequential surfaces because nobody typed their final disposition.
- **residual-liability fog**: audits, legal claims, or disclosure requests survive discharge but no successor home is named.
- **sealed-memory disappearance**: custody-era records technically exist, but nobody can now find or request them.
- **credential afterlife**: interim-only powers, tokens, or delegations remain active because nobody explicitly revoked or rehomed them.
- **wind-down without ledger**: final costs and obligations survive in scattered invoices or oral memory rather than one signed account.
- **residue forever**: a “temporary” closeout shell persists for years because no termination clock or rereview lane was attached.
- **public-route amnesia**: the public is told the interim has ended without being told where the real route now is.

## Practical tests

A custodian-discharge regime is honest when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Is the discharge typed as full, residual, partial, converted, or failed rather than narrated vaguely?
2. Does the packet contain one disposition ledger for every surface that entered custody?
3. Is there one final account of funds, costs, contracts, liabilities, and wind-down obligations?
4. Are all residual duties given a named post-discharge home?
5. Is there one sealed custody-era record package with a durable repository path?
6. Do lookup and challenge routes for custody-era acts still exist after discharge?
7. Are interim-only powers and credentials explicitly revoked, archived, or rehomed?
8. Are any residual estates bounded by clocks and next-review lanes?
9. Does the public statement accurately describe the discharge class and new routes?
10. If proof is incomplete, does the archive keep the interim narrow and visible rather than letting it disappear into ambiguity?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a polity can say **the neutral custodian is standing down**, it should also be able to show one compact packet proving **what kind of discharge is occurring, what happened to every custodial surface, where the final account and custody-era record package live, who inherited the residual duties, what lookup and challenge routes survive, what interim powers were killed or rehomed, and what residual clocks remain**. Otherwise it still permits **shadow government by finished interim**.
