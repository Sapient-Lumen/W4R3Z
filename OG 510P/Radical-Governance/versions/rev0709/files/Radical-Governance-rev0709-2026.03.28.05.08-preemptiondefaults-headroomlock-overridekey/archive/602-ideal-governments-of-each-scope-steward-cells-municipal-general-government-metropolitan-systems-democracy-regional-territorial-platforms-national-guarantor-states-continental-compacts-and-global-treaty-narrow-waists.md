# 602 — Ideal governments of each scope: steward cells, municipal general government, metropolitan systems democracy, regional territorial platforms, national guarantor states, continental compacts, and global treaty narrow waists

## One-line thesis

An ideal multi-level polity should give each scope a distinct governing vocation and a minimum complete kit: micro-local units as visible stewardship and signal cells; municipalities as the first full general-purpose governments of ordinary life; metropolitan governments as democratically legitimated operators of shared urban systems; regional, provincial, or state governments as territorial-platform governments for landscapes, hospitals, grids, universities, and shared rule; nations as guarantor states for equal citizenship, redistribution, macro-stability, and constitutional settlement; continental bodies as bounded compact governments for cross-border mobility, markets, and infrastructures; and global institutions as treaty-bound narrow waists for peace, commons, alerting, and standards rather than as a fake world domestic state.

## Why this matters

The archive already contains a scope ladder, a subsidiarity rule, a constitutional-kit note, and a widening set of sector-by-scope notes. What it still lacked was the most compressed direct answer to the user-facing question: **what is the ideal government at each scale, in plain constitutional terms?**

That gap matters because multi-level systems often fail in one of three recurring ways. They either **clone one institutional imagination at every scale**, as if a block, a city, a metro, a province, a nation, and the planet all need the same democratic and administrative machinery. Or they **collapse the stack**, expecting either the municipality or the nation to do everything while metropolitan, regional, continental, or global layers remain weak, informal, or decorative. Or they **mis-size legitimacy**, giving some level the power to tax, zone, inspect, borrow, or bind several territories without a matching public forum, review route, or revenue model.

A sharper answer starts from vocation. The ideal government of a scope is the one whose authority, democratic channel, professional depth, finance, and review routes match the class of problems that live there. That means the right design is neither one sovereign resized many times nor a romantic localism that denies spillovers, solidarity, and shared infrastructures. It is a differentiated constitutional stack.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Begin from governing vocation, not institutional symmetry

Each scope should be designed around the class of problems it can actually see, bind, finance, and repair. The smallest scales can notice breakdown early and coordinate stewardship, but they cannot reliably pool macro-risk or sustain complex technical systems. Municipalities can govern everyday place. Metropolitan bodies can govern shared urban systems. Regions can hold together large territorial platforms. Nations can guarantee equal citizenship and redistribution. Continental and global bodies can solve selected cross-border coordination problems.

The archive should therefore reject **institutional symmetry as an ideal in itself**. The question is not whether each level has a parliament-like room, a mayor-like executive, or a ministry-like bureaucracy. The question is whether each level has the **minimum complete kit** for the work that actually belongs there.

### 2. Make the micro-local layer a stewardship-and-signal cell, not a tiny sovereign

The ideal micro-local unit is the building, block, village, ward, neighborhood, or small settlement institution that remains close enough to lived reality to notice danger, exclusion, neglect, and commons breakdown before larger systems do. Its value is density of knowledge, legibility, and early warning.

Its ideal form is therefore a **steward cell**: an assembly, ward committee, village council, block board, or similarly reachable institution with a very small budget, explicit record-keeping duties, and strong escalation rights. It should help allocate some discretionary money, steward tiny commons, convene residents, route failures upward, and preserve visibility around who asked for what and what happened next.

What it must never become is a miniature sovereign with broad policing, heavy borrowing, insulated patronage, or a myth of self-sufficiency. Micro-local government should be intense in voice and light in coercive ambition.

### 3. Treat the municipality as the first full general-purpose government of ordinary life

The ideal municipality is the first level that should clearly count as a **general-purpose government** rather than as a participatory adjunct, service outpost, or intergovernmental convenience. It is where public authority first becomes fully civic and fully administrative at the same time.

A serious municipal government should have an elected council, public committees, a blameable executive, a professional administration, ordinary public records, an intelligible budget, real front-door service capacity, and enough own-source revenue or stable formula finance to avoid permanent dependency theatre. This is the natural home of streets, sanitation, routine permitting, libraries, parks, public-space maintenance, local land-use control, and many ordinary access points into care, safety, and civil administration.

What it must never become is an empty democratic shell that holds elections but lacks the staffing, records, finance, and rulemaking room needed to govern ordinary place.

### 4. Give the metropolitan layer systems democracy rather than mayoral cartelism

The metropolitan scale exists because real cities are larger than their municipal shells. Labour markets, housing pressures, trunk transport, freight, air quality, flood systems, resilience planning, and some emergency capacities live in the **functional urban region**, not inside one council boundary.

The ideal metropolitan government is therefore a **systems democracy**: a directly elected metropolitan assembly or another comparably public-legible body, paired with a specialist executive and administration capable of governing shared urban systems across municipal borders. If a metropolitan authority can bind land use, transport, utility coordination, housing obligations, capital planning, or system-wide revenue decisions, it should have legitimacy proportionate to those powers.

What it must never become is a summit of incumbent mayors, a grant cartel, or a quango cluster that exercises metropolitan power without a clear electorate, chamber, or appeal route.

### 5. Use regional, provincial, or state government as a territorial platform and shared-rule layer

The ideal region is not a decorative map tier. It earns its place when it governs systems too large for the municipality or metro, too place-sensitive for the nation, and too operationally dense for a thin treaty body. Basins, corridors, hospitals, tertiary education, electricity or water platforms, regional development, territorial equalization inside federations, and some forms of plurinational self-government often live here.

Its ideal form is a **territorial platform government**: a constitutional or hard-law subnational government with a legislature, executive departments, administrative depth, substantial finance, and review routes proportionate to the systems it carries. In federal or plurinational settings it should also function as a **shared-rule** layer, not just a local-service scaler.

What it must never become is a patronage waiting room, a duplicate ministry shell, or an ornamental province that can neither govern major systems nor bargain constitutionally.

### 6. Reserve the nation for guarantor-state functions

The nation remains indispensable, but ideally for reasons narrower and more constitutional than generic sovereignty theatre. National government should be the **guarantor state**: the layer that secures equal citizenship, rights floors, redistribution, macroeconomic stabilization, social insurance, national infrastructure backbones, and the constitutional settlement of how powers are assigned, reviewed, and corrected.

That means the national level should own parliament, treasury, apex regulators where nation-scale coherence matters, independent election administration, supreme public audit, constitutional review, human-rights and equality guarantees, and the fiscal tools needed to keep territorial inequality from hollowing out citizenship.

What it must never become is an everything-government that treats every municipality, metro, and region as a branch office. A nation that cannot tolerate meaningful lower-tier discretion is not protecting subsidiarity; it is crowding out the stack.

### 7. Use continental or plurilateral bodies as bounded compact governments

Some coordination problems are too integrated for a single state but too specific for universal institutions. Cross-border mobility regimes, common markets, basin compacts, shared grids, customs spaces, regional rights machinery, industrial and research compacts, and peace-security arrangements may all justify a layer between nation and globe.

The ideal form here is a **continental compact government**: treaty-based institutions with named competences, bounded revenue, visible parliamentary or assembly scrutiny, review routes, and withdrawal or amendment rules. Their strength should come from solving real cross-border problems without pretending to be a general domestic government for all subjects.

What they must never become is ambient competence accumulation: a level that slowly takes on general state-like powers because elites prefer to legislate one floor higher than the public can easily follow.

### 8. Keep the global layer real but narrow: treaty narrow waists, not world domesticity

The global layer matters where no state, region, or continent can solve the problem alone: peace and war restraint, planetary commons, disease alerting, some shipping and aviation rules, some financial and technical standards, and other genuinely transboundary risks.

The ideal global form is therefore a **treaty narrow waist**: assemblies of states, secretariats, scientific panels, inspectorates, registries, dispute channels, and specialized agencies whose mandates are explicit and problem-bound. Their legitimacy comes from treaty basis, transparency, review, expertise, and the fact that the coordination problem is real.

What global government must never become is a fake world domestic state with a vocabulary far broader than its legal basis, fiscal reach, or democratic density.

### 9. Connect the stack with floors, equalization, review, and handback

No scope is ideal in isolation. The whole constitutional stack needs connective tissue:

- local and regional headroom under higher-tier rights and safety floors,
- formula finance and equalization so solidarity is not postcode luck,
- appeal and judicial review when one level injures another or injures a person,
- explicit burden-of-proof for upward transfers of power,
- expiry and handback rules for emergency concentration,
- and interoperable records and reporting that make responsibility traceable across tiers.

Without those connectors, even a well-described scope ladder turns into blame circulation.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Micro-local** | steward cell for voice, upkeep, and escalation | direct reachability and open attendance | micro-budgets, formula micro-grants, tiny discretionary funds | tiny sovereign theater |
| **Municipal** | general-purpose government of ordinary life | elected council plus visible executive accountability | own-source taxes/fees plus equalization and disciplined capital finance | democratic shell without administrative muscle |
| **Metropolitan** | systems democracy for the functional city-region | directly elected metro assembly or equally public-legible mandate | shared tax base, formula contributions, user revenue where appropriate, asset-matched borrowing | mayoral cartel or quango sprawl |
| **Regional / provincial / state** | territorial platform and shared-rule government | constitutional subnational legislature and executive | substantial tax share plus intergovernmental transfers and equalization | decorative map tier |

### 10. Allow asymmetry, but only for real differences in function, geography, or constitutional settlement

The ideal stack will not look identical everywhere. Capital districts, islands, borderlands, Indigenous or treaty-grounded autonomies, sparsely populated territories, and giant city-regions may all need special institutional kits. But asymmetry should be justified by real difference rather than by prestige deals, partisan carve-outs, or administrative improvisation.

The archive should therefore prefer **principled asymmetry**: visible criteria, rights floors, finance rules, and review routes that explain why a place differs and when that difference should persist.

## Guardrails

- Do not confuse micro-local stewardship with full local sovereignty.
- Do not leave municipalities elective but administratively hollow.
- Do not govern metropolitan systems through opaque bargaining among incumbents.
- Do not preserve regions that cannot name their constitutional job.
- Do not let the national executive absorb rights, facts, elections, audit, and territorial discretion into one command chain.
- Do not let continental bodies drift from compact to ambient government without explicit reauthorization.
- Do not answer genuine global coordination problems with either world-state fantasy or nationalist denial.

## Failure modes

- **scope mimicry**: every level copies the same institutional form regardless of problem fit.
- **micro-sovereignty cosplay**: neighborhood or village bodies are overloaded with powers they cannot finance, review, or lawfully exercise.
- **municipal shell government**: cities hold elections but lack the records, staff, revenue, and ordinance capacity needed for ordinary governance.
- **metro by cartel**: metropolitan authority exists in practice, but only through opaque bargains among incumbents, operators, and ministries.
- **regional ornament**: provinces or states survive as symbols, not as real territorial-platform governments.
- **national pile-up**: the nation keeps every serious power because upward retention is easier than honest subsidiarity.
- **compact creep**: continental or plurilateral bodies accumulate purpose beyond their named treaty basis.
- **world domesticity fantasy**: global institutions are asked to imitate a state they cannot honestly be.

## Practical tests

A design for ideal governments of each scope passes when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Can each level state its governing vocation in one sentence that differs materially from the levels below and above it?
2. Does each level have a minimum complete kit — forum, executive spine, money model, review route, and participation path — sized to its actual job?
3. Are metropolitan and regional layers real wherever the underlying systems are real?
4. Does the national level guarantee rights and solidarity without swallowing all lower-level discretion?
5. Are continental and global bodies tightly enough bounded that their mandate, capability, and legitimacy still line up?
6. Can the constitution explain how powers move upward, how they are reviewed, and when they move back down?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a polity can list its levels of government but cannot say **what each level is for, what minimum kit makes it honest, what path corrects its failure, and what prevents it from impersonating the next level up**, then it still has **a jurisdictional ladder without a theory of ideal governments by scope**.
