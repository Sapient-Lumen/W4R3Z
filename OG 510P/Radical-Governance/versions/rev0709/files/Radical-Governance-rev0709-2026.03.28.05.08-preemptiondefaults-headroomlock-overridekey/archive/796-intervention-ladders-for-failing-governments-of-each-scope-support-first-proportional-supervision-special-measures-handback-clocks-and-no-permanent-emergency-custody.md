# 796 — Intervention ladders for failing governments of each scope: support first, proportional supervision, special measures, handback clocks, and no permanent emergency custody

## One-line thesis

When a government-by-scope arrangement keeps failing its benchmark, stress, maintenance, or proof duties, the archive should not jump straight from diagnosis to permanent takeover. It should use one compact **intervention ladder**: **support first, escalate by law and proportionality, separate own-competence supervision from delegated-task control, keep any special measures temporary and reviewable, and pair every intervention with a handback or formal rescoping test so rescue does not harden into quiet permanent custody**.

## Why this matters

`790` now gives the archive a compact answer key for what ideal governments at each scope should generally be. `791` gives a short benchmark card for ordinary-time fit. `792` asks whether the arrangement remains honest under shock. `793` puts those checks on a recurring public maintenance rhythm. `794` says how a polity should move voluntarily and in phases toward a better stack. `795` says what outward evidence should stay public while all of that happens. `799` now adds the separate rule for what should happen to temporary commissioners, hosts, derogations, and other exceptional objects once intervention has lasted long enough to raise the question of expiry, renewal, graduation, or burial. `632` gives trigger conditions for reopening the scope question, and `631` already guards against one-way recentralisation by requiring credible handback lanes.

What the archive still lacked as one compact rule was the hard middle: **what to do when repeated failure is real enough that someone must compel repair rather than merely score, drill, schedule, or publish it**.

That gap matters because multilevel systems often fail in one of two bad ways.

The first is **romantic non-intervention**: everyone can see the municipality, metro body, region, ministry field office, or delegated authority is missing payroll control, procurement discipline, continuity capacity, review routes, or rights compliance, yet higher authorities refuse to intervene because any intervention is treated as betrayal of autonomy.

The second is **quiet permanent custody**: a genuine failure triggers support, supervision, co-signature, emergency management, fiscal control, central scripting, or temporary hosting, but the supposed rescue never develops a real handback clock, public closure test, or honest rescoping decision. Temporary administration becomes the real constitution by inertia.

The archive should reject both pathologies. A serious multilevel order needs one visible ladder that says when support is enough, when supervision is justified, when special measures become unavoidable, when handback must begin, and when the evidence is strong enough that the scope itself should be redesigned instead of living under endless intervention.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Separate failure detection from intervention choice

A bad benchmark, failed stress drill, repeated maintenance default, or missing proof pack is not yet an intervention form. It is a finding that opens the intervention ladder.

The archive should therefore separate:

- **diagnosis** under `791`-`795`,
- **trigger activation** under `632`,
- **intervention choice** under this note,
- and **durable redesign** under `794` when lighter intervention will not honestly close the problem.

This prevents every alarming result from being treated either as a reason for panic takeover or as a reason to do nothing because the full redesign case has not yet been proven.

### 2. Start with support before seizure

Where the live allocation is still substantively right but the holder lacks kit, money discipline, technical capacity, or surge depth, the first move should usually be support rather than dispossession.

That can include:

- technical assistance,
- shared service support,
- temporary fiscal or treasury help,
- procurement or records repair,
- peer support from stronger authorities,
- mandatory recovery planning,
- and capacity funding tied to visible milestones.

The archive should reject intervention ladders that treat local, metro, regional, or delegated weakness as automatic proof that power must move upward forever.

### 3. Distinguish own competences from delegated tasks before escalating

Intervention should be stricter where the failing authority is carrying out a delegated higher-level task than where it is exercising its own competence.

Where the task is **delegated**, higher authorities may legitimately insist on performance, legality, and even stronger expediency control because the upper tier remains responsible for the function.

Where the task is **own competence**, the archive should presume a narrower supervision philosophy: legality, rights protection, fiscal integrity, minimum service continuity, and proportionate corrective measures rather than open-ended hierarchical command.

Without this distinction, delegated-task discipline becomes a laundering route for swallowing local or regional self-government as such.

### 4. Escalate by typed ladder, not by vibes

A serious intervention ladder should name its steps in advance.

A usable default sequence is:

1. **notice and recovery plan**,
2. **targeted support and monitored milestones**,
3. **supervised repair** for named lanes such as treasury, procurement, payroll, records, or legality review,
4. **temporary special measures** for named functions or fiscal controls,
5. **handback and monitored stabilization** if the holder becomes credible again,
6. **formal rescoping review** under `632` and `794` if repeated intervention proves the scope settlement itself is dishonest.

The ladder may skip steps in acute emergencies, but it should never pretend that skipping the ladder makes the ladder unnecessary.

### 5. Keep intervention proportionate to the protected interest

Intervention should be no broader than needed to protect legality, rights, essential-service continuity, fiscal solvency, or the integrity of a delegated task.

That means:

- fixing payroll does not automatically justify control of planning,
- curing procurement failure does not automatically justify seizure of council agenda-setting,
- fiscal co-signature does not automatically justify political blackout,
- and continuity hosting does not automatically justify constitutional absorption.

The archive should reject rescue arrangements whose scope silently widens each month because no one wrote down the protected interest and the outer boundary of the measure.

### 6. Keep democratic organs and review lanes alive whenever possible

Intervention should repair or constrain, not erase political visibility by default.

Even under serious supervision, the archive should prefer keeping some combination of:

- the elected forum,
- public minutes,
- resident complaint lanes,
- judicial review,
- audit access,
- and a visible legislative or assembly scrutiny route.

A polity may temporarily narrow local discretion without abolishing the local public sphere. Otherwise administrative rescue becomes a convenient way to suspend democratic witness right when it matters most.

### 7. Treat special measures as temporary constitutional weather, not as a second ordinary regime

Where conditions justify emergency managers, commissioners, host authorities, mandatory approvals, spending freezes, or function-specific receivership, the intervention should remain explicitly temporary.

That means naming:

- the legal basis,
- the specific functions under special measure,
- the powers withheld from the ordinary holder,
- the evidence threshold for continuation,
- the maximum review interval,
- the public reporting duty,
- and the exit route.

If these are missing, the archive should presume the polity is drifting into permanent emergency custody.

### 8. Put the handback clock on the table at the start, not at the end

Every serious intervention should open with an exit grammar.

The intervention order should say:

- what must be repaired,
- what proof shows repair is real,
- who certifies readiness,
- what staged powers return first,
- what stabilization window follows,
- and what happens if the holder relapses.

This keeps the burden on the intervening authority to justify continued custody, instead of forcing the lower tier to prove an abstract metaphysical worthiness to govern itself again.

### 9. Distinguish temporary support from structural proof that the map is wrong

Repeated intervention is evidence. It is not self-interpreting evidence.

A sequence of failures may show:

- the scope is right but the kit was incomplete,
- the scope is right but the fiscal design was dishonest,
- the holder is too small and needs durable pooling,
- a metro or regional platform is missing,
- a delegated task should be reclaimed,
- or the function has become national or cross-border in fact.

The archive should therefore require the intervention ladder to end with an explicit question: **does this case call for handback after repair, or has repeated failure now become proof for formal rescoping?**

### 10. Keep intervention public, canonical, and challengeable

A serious intervention should have one canonical public surface that shows:

- the failing arrangement,
- the intervention level currently in force,
- the reason for escalation,
- the powers affected,
- the named officials or bodies responsible,
- the next review date,
- the handback conditions,
- and the challenge route.

That public surface is the proof-pack counterpart of this note and should usually be implemented through `795`.

### 11. Use asymmetry and partial lanes before whole-body seizure

Often the honest intervention is not takeover of the whole authority but temporary control or support in one lane:

- treasury,
- debt issuance,
- procurement,
- payroll,
- records,
- permitting backlog clearance,
- or one shared-system function.

The archive should prefer partial and typed measures where they are sufficient. Whole-body seizure should be reserved for cases where partial repair cannot protect the relevant interest.

### 12. Do not let intervention become a one-way upward ratchet

Some interventions should end in stronger pooling, metro government, regional platforming, delegated-task retrieval, or national guarantee. Others should end in restored local or regional autonomy with a better kit.

The archive should therefore treat intervention as a **repair and decision lane**, not as a disguised presumption that power only moves upward. `631` remains the standing anti-ratchet safeguard.

## Intervention ladder grammar

| Stage | What it is for | Typical tools | Default public question | Honest closure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Notice** | establish that failure is real and typed | benchmark failure notice, drill failure notice, proof-pack default notice, trigger activation | what exactly failed and in which lane? | recovery order or closure if false alarm |
| **Supported repair** | fix the holder without removing the function | technical support, recovery plan, peer support, conditional aid, training, shared back-office help | is the scope still right if the kit is repaired? | return to ordinary maintenance |
| **Supervised repair** | protect legality, fiscal integrity, or continuity in a named lane | co-signature, mandatory approval, reporting, controller, backlog protocol, records supervision | can a bounded lane intervention contain the risk? | staged relaxation and monitored stabilization |
| **Special measures** | stop acute collapse or rights breach | commissioner, emergency manager, host authority, spending freeze, function-specific receivership | what must be suspended or hosted to protect the public now? | handback plan, renewed review, or rescoping test |
| **Handback / stabilization** | restore ordinary holder with proof, not sentiment | staged return of powers, witness review, post-exit monitoring, relapse triggers | is the repaired holder actually credible in live conditions? | full restoration or reopened intervention |
| **Formal rescoping** | redesign the map when repeated intervention proves structural misfit | transfer, merger, metro upgrade, regional platform, delegated-task retrieval, treaty or compact redesign | is this still rescue, or is the constitution wrong? | new steady-state arrangement under `794` and `793` |

## Scope-sensitive defaults

| Failing scope | First honest intervenor | Default first move | Harder move if failure persists | Anti-cosplay limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Micro-local steward body** | municipality or equivalent local government | facilitation, records repair, tiny-budget guardrails, meeting continuity help | temporary hosting of the steward function by the municipality | do not treat block-level failure as proof that local voice itself is dispensable |
| **Municipality** | metro, county, region, state, or nation depending on the constitution | supported repair of treasury, records, procurement, staffing, and continuity lanes | fiscal control, host support for named services, or formal pooling / metro upgrade review | do not use routine support as camouflage for permanent abolition without a real redesign decision |
| **Metropolitan authority** | region, state, nation, or member governments by treaty | corrective plan on shared finance, command clarity, or service continuity | temporary commissioner, host of named network functions, or reconstitution of the metro body | do not let mayoral clubs reclaim the shared city by collapse theater |
| **Region / province / state** | federation, nation, or constitutional guarantor | monitored recovery in fiscal, legality, or delegated national-program lanes | temporary fiscal supervision, function-specific receivership, or constitutional review of the regional platform | do not turn national guarantee into standing ordinary administration of the region |
| **National** | constitutional court, legislature, guarantor institutions, treaty or compact peers as applicable | legal correction, fiscal recovery plan, independent oversight, emergency-law sunset enforcement | special constitutional measures, bounded trusteeship, or treaty compliance mechanisms | do not treat international or treaty intervention as a general licence for ambient upper-state drift |
| **Continental / global compact body** | member states under the treaty, courts, compliance mechanisms, or assemblies | remedial plan, reporting discipline, capability support, contribution repair | temporary suspension of powers, special administrator for a narrow mandate, or mandate redesign | do not answer every implementation failure with a fantasy world executive |

## Use rule for the archive

When later notes need to say how a failing government-by-scope arrangement should be compelled back toward honesty, the archive should usually do ten things in order:

1. **use `790` for the target stack**,
2. **use `791` for the ordinary-time diagnosis**,
3. **use `792` for shock-weather failure evidence**,
4. **use `793` for the missed maintenance or overdue follow-up pattern**,
5. **use `795` for the outward intervention board and public evidence surface**,
6. **use `632` to state whether the case stays within repair or reopens the scope question**,
7. **use `631` when the main problem is how intervention ends and power is handed back rather than how it starts**,
8. **use this note to state the intervention ladder itself: support, supervision, special measures, handback, or formal rescoping**,
9. **use `799` when the open question is no longer which intervention rung applies but what should happen to the temporary intervention object itself once it reaches renewal, expiry, graduation, or burial judgment**,
10. **use `797` when the open question is who may challenge the intervention, renewal, stay, or denial of handback**.

The archive should not force itself to choose between two bad defaults: pretending every failing arrangement can heal through polite benchmarking alone, or treating every failure as an excuse for immediate permanent takeover.

## Guardrails

- Do not confuse repeated diagnosis with actual intervention design.
- Do not jump straight to seizure when bounded support or supervised repair could honestly work.
- Do not use delegated-task failure as a pretext for swallowing own competences without a separate legal basis.
- Do not let special measures run without review intervals, public reporting, or handback conditions.
- Do not erase elected forums, complaint lanes, or review routes more broadly than the protected interest requires.
- Do not treat repeated intervention as self-proving evidence for permanent recentralisation.
- Do not hand powers back on sentiment alone; require proof and stabilization.

## Failure modes

- **autonomy fatalism** — obvious collapse is tolerated because any intervention is treated as illegitimate in principle.
- **ladder skipping** — the polity jumps from first alarm to maximum seizure without testing lighter tools or writing down why they are insufficient.
- **lane creep** — a fiscal or legality intervention quietly expands into general command because nobody polices the boundary.
- **permanent emergency custody** — temporary administration becomes the true constitution by serial renewal and lack of handback tests.
- **democratic blackout rescue** — support or supervision is used to extinguish the elected forum and public witness rather than to repair a named defect.
- **rescoping by stealth** — repeated intervention changes the real constitutional map without a formal redesign or ratification path.
- **handback romanticism** — powers are returned too early and without proof, creating the next predictable collapse.

## Practical tests

An intervention ladder is constitutionally honest when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Is the failure clearly typed before the intervention form is chosen?
2. Has the polity distinguished own competences from delegated tasks?
3. Did it try or explain why it skipped lighter support measures?
4. Are the protected interests, legal basis, and outer boundaries of the intervention written down?
5. Does the elected forum, complaint route, or review lane remain alive as far as possible?
6. Is there a public intervention board with next review date and handback conditions?
7. Is there an explicit test for whether the case ends in handback or formal rescoping?
8. Can the polity show that intervention is not simply a one-way upward ratchet?

## Compression rule for the archive

If the archive can say **what ideal government by scope should look like**, **how to benchmark it**, **how it performs under stress**, **how it is maintained**, **how it should transition**, and **what evidence should stay public**, but still cannot say **what happens when failure persists and someone must legally compel repair without turning rescue into permanent custody**, then it still has **scope diagnosis without a doctrine of intervention**.
