# 707 — Sunset packets for obsolete government lanes, bodies, and temporary machinery: review triggers, successor homes, residue registers, closure proof, and no immortal institutions by inherited backlog

## One-line thesis

Once a field has repair packets, it should also publish one **sunset packet** whenever review shows that a lane, body, or temporary governance machine no longer deserves to exist in live form — naming the trigger, the successor home or true extinction rule, the rights and service floors that still bind during wind-down, the residue register for cases, contracts, assets, staff, records, data, debt, and public-facing channels, the closure proof, and the reactivation rule — so obsolete institutions do not stay alive merely because they still have backlog, leases, websites, or logos.

## Why this matters

The archive already has strong nearby doctrine.

`543` explains that special-purpose authorities should face periodic review, merger, constitutionalization, or sunset rather than becoming immortal by habit. `586` explains how functions move when a transfer is honest. `625` explains that provisional and pilot institutions need review, succession, and lawful end states. `642` explains that a scope layer should leave behind a retirement certificate instead of constitutional ghosting. `705` and `706` now explain how a field changes mode under stress and when repeated stress should convert into a governed repair programme.

What the archive still lacked was one compact object for the next question:

**what happens when the right answer is not another repair cycle, but retirement of the lane, body, or temporary machinery itself?**

That gap matters because public systems are good at starting institutions and bad at ending them. Temporary taskforces keep issuing instructions after the emergency is over. Special-purpose authorities survive because they still hold debt, contracts, or data. A review concludes that a body no longer needs to exist at arm’s length, yet no one publishes the practical constitution for bringing it home. Websites, seals, procurement powers, complaint channels, and legacy files remain live enough to confuse the public, while insiders quietly know that the institution is dead in principle but undead in operation.

A repair packet is not enough for that. Repair assumes the field should continue in recognizably live form. A sunset packet begins from the opposite premise: the polity has decided that this lane, body, or machine should **stop being a live constitutional object**, and now needs a clean way to extinguish or absorb it without losing rights, service continuity, records, liabilities, or public memory.

This note therefore adds one compact doctrine for **sunset packets**.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Distinguish sunset from repair, pause, merger, and handback

The archive should not treat every institutional change as the same thing.

- **repair** means the lane or body remains necessary but needs structural correction;
- **pause** means it stops exercising some powers temporarily while remaining constitutionally alive;
- **handback** means extraordinary or borrowed control returns to the ordinary holder;
- **merger** means several live objects become one successor object;
- **sunset** means a lane, body, or temporary machine stops being a live governing object in its current form.

A sunset packet may end in merger, absorption, reserve status, or true extinction. What matters is that the polity says so explicitly instead of hiding the answer inside the word *reform*.

### 2. Open a sunset packet only on a named necessity trigger

A sunset packet should not open because a minister is impatient or because an institution has become unfashionable.

The packet should name the trigger, such as:

- the body’s purpose has been completed;
- a temporary emergency or transition machine has reached its handback condition;
- review shows the function no longer needs a separate live lane or arm’s-length form;
- repeated repair cycles show the current object is the wrong constitutional vessel;
- the function is being lawfully absorbed into another general-purpose or special-purpose home;
- or the function itself is ending and should not leave a zombie shell behind.

The question is not simply whether something is underperforming. It is whether it should remain alive **as a distinct governing thing**.

### 3. Decide the successor home before declaring the sunset

The archive should reject closure language that does not say where the live residue goes.

A serious sunset packet should say whether the object is:

- being **absorbed** into a ministry, municipality, metropolitan body, region, or other standing lane;
- being **merged** into a successor authority;
- being reduced to a **reserve or dormant capability** that has no ordinary live authority between activations;
- or being **fully extinguished**, because the function itself is ending.

No institution should be declared obsolete while its live obligations are still wandering around looking for a host.

### 4. Stop new inflow cleanly before the residue becomes immortal

Many dead institutions stay undead because the public keeps feeding them new work after the sunset decision.

The packet should therefore state:

- the last day on which the old object may accept new matters;
- the day from which new matters must go to the successor route;
- what short transition window still honors filings or notices sent to the old route;
- who remains answerable if a new matter is misrouted during wind-down;
- and which emergency fallback applies if the successor route is not fully ready.

A sunset packet should close the front door before it begins counting the backlog in the basement.

### 5. Keep rights floors and service floors alive during wind-down

The fact that an institution is leaving does not suspend the public’s rights.

The packet should therefore name:

- the minimum service floor that still binds until the final closure date;
- the complaint, reconsideration, appeal, or safeguarding routes that remain open;
- the notice standard that applies to affected users, staff, and counterparties;
- any temporary priority rules for burning down inherited stock;
- and the threshold at which delay or confusion requires escalation to the successor lane.

A polity should not get to sunset a body by making the public absorb the closure shock in silence.

### 6. Maintain one residue register rather than many half-lists

Sunset work is usually hidden across legal schedules, budget spreadsheets, HR trackers, procurement closeout lists, archives plans, and communications decks.

The archive should instead prefer one visible **residue register** that can name, for each live residue item:

- the class of residue — case, contract, asset, staff, data, record, debt, grant, litigation, public web surface, or other liability;
- the current holder;
- the successor holder;
- the cutover or closeout date;
- the governing legal instrument;
- the public route if outsiders still need to interact with it;
- and the closure test.

A sunset packet without a residue register is usually just an announcement followed by administrative folklore.

### 7. Kill live authority, not just the nameplate

Public institutions often survive operationally after their formal abolition because nobody turned off the real switches.

The packet should explicitly identify the authority kill points:

- appointment powers;
- signing authority;
- delegated decision powers;
- spending or procurement authority;
- enforcement or licensing powers;
- official seals, forms, and branded communications;
- digital admin access, domains, APIs, and inboxes;
- and any statutory or contractual power that outsiders might still treat as live.

The archive should treat **authority shutdown** as a first-class closure task, not as housekeeping.

### 8. Preserve records and public memory without preserving live power

To retire a body honestly is not to erase it.

A sunset packet should therefore distinguish between:

- the end of live governing authority,
- the preservation of records, datasets, websites, and public notices,
- the custody of historical and evidentiary material,
- and the ongoing discoverability of where the old body went and who now answers for its residue.

The archive should be especially strict here: public memory should survive, but no stale record, legacy domain, or archived page should be allowed to impersonate live authority.

### 9. Treat workforce, assets, contracts, and liabilities as closure work, not annexes

A sunset decision is false if it kills the body in law but leaves the expensive and rights-bearing parts to drift.

The packet should therefore say:

- what happens to staff, including transfer, severance, redeployment, pension, and line-management residue;
- how leases, equipment, estates, and digital systems are transferred, sold, mothballed, or retired;
- how contracts are novated, closed, or allowed to expire;
- how debt, contingent liabilities, litigation, and indemnities are assigned;
- and what budget lane funds the closeout itself.

No polity should claim closure while still outsourcing the most consequential residue to improvisation.

### 10. Closure should require proof, and reactivation should require a fresh act

A sunset packet should not close because a minister announced it or because the sign on the door came down.

Closure proof should usually include at least all of the following:

- no new matters can lawfully enter through the retired route;
- all live residue items have either been closed or assigned to named successor holders;
- public-facing routes, websites, contact points, and forms now direct users accurately;
- official records, datasets, and evidentiary material are preserved and reachable through named custodians;
- and no officer can still exercise the retired body’s powers by habit, stale delegation, or surviving admin access.

The archive should also force a **reactivation rule**. A sunsetted object should come back only through a fresh creation, delegation, or emergency activation lane with current proof, not through nostalgic reuse of old paperwork.

## Compact sunset packet

A normal sunset packet should usually fit in one table-like block:

| Object being sunsetted | Trigger | Successor home or extinction rule | Live floor during wind-down | Residue register focus | Closure proof | Reactivation rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Emergency procurement cell** | emergency mode has ended and two recovery reviews show ordinary procurement lanes can carry the field | absorbed into ordinary procurement and finance lanes; incident playbook retained only as dormant reserve capability | supplier-payment integrity, fraud controls, live complaint route, visible emergency-contract notices until final novation or closeout | open emergency contracts, claims, audit files, admin credentials, stockpile records, public guidance pages | no new emergency purchase orders through the old cell; all live contracts novated or closed; old admin rights removed; archived guidance clearly marked non-live | may be reactivated only through a fresh emergency declaration and named incident lead |
| **Special transit authority** | review shows the function still exists but no longer requires a separate body because metropolitan government can carry it directly | merged into the metropolitan transport lane with one successor budget, board route, and records custodian | service continuity, safety reporting, fare refunds, accessibility floor, live complaint route | franchises, debt, depots, staff transfer, safety cases, procurement pipelines, public maps and apps | no duplicate signing authority remains; passengers face one live route map; old board dissolved; accounts and contracts sit with the successor lane | a new authority would require a fresh necessity and form test, not reuse of the retired charter |
| **Pandemic coordination taskforce** | handback clocks have expired and ordinary health, care, and civil-protection lanes have resumed command | fully extinguished as a live command object; archives, datasets, and reserve protocols preserved under named custodians | public archive of orders and notices, residual compensation route, safeguarding of open claims | orders, datasets, websites, stockpile logs, after-action record, remaining grants, litigation residue | no live command orders, no active inboxes or dashboards presented as current, and all residue sits with named standing lanes | future reactivation requires a new incident declaration, not silent resurrection of the taskforce |

The archive should treat this as the normal length unless the sunset is unusually complicated.

## Guardrails

- Do not announce closure before naming the successor home or true extinction rule.
- Do not keep accepting new matters through a route that the polity says is ending.
- Do not let backlog alone justify immortal institution status.
- Do not preserve live authority accidentally through stale seals, domains, delegated powers, or admin credentials.
- Do not erase records, datasets, or public memory just because live power is ending.
- Do not let budget, HR, contracts, or liabilities drift outside the sunset packet.
- Do not let a sunsetted object return through custom, remembered workflow, or emergency nostalgia.

## Failure modes

- **immortal institution by inherited backlog** — the body is supposedly ending but keeps living because nobody closes or reallocates the residue.
- **nameplate abolition** — the title disappears while the same powers continue through the same people and systems.
- **ghost authority** — old websites, forms, domains, or seals still look live enough to mislead the public.
- **residue exile** — contracts, debts, archives, or claims are left offstage while the polity congratulates itself on abolition.
- **merge-without-home** — the old body is closed before the successor route is genuinely able to receive its work.
- **rights blackout** — complaint, appeal, and service routes disappear during wind-down.
- **archive amputation** — historical memory is lost because closure is mistaken for deletion.
- **zombie reactivation** — the old object returns through habit, crisis folklore, or leftover delegations rather than a fresh lawful act.

## Practical tests

A polity is using a sunset packet honestly when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Has it distinguished sunset from repair, pause, merger, and handback?
2. Is there a named trigger explaining why the lane, body, or temporary machinery should stop existing in live form?
3. Has it named the successor home or true extinction rule before the closure announcement?
4. Are new inflow rules, transition windows, and misrouting protections stated?
5. Are rights floors, service floors, and complaint or appeal routes preserved during wind-down?
6. Is there one residue register covering cases, contracts, staff, assets, records, data, debt, and public-facing surfaces?
7. Have the real authority kill points been identified rather than just the organization chart change?
8. Are records, datasets, websites, and public notices preserved in a way that keeps memory but does not impersonate live authority?
9. Does closure require operational proof instead of a speech, repeal, or rebrand alone?
10. If future conditions change, does reactivation require a fresh lawful act rather than ambient resurrection?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a later note can tell you **when a lane, body, or temporary governance machine should stop being live** but cannot also tell you **what opened the sunset packet, where the live residue goes, what rights and service floors still bind during wind-down, which authority kill points shut off the old object, what residue register tracks the remaining stock, what closure proof ends the process, and what fresh act would be required to reactivate it**, then it still has **a retirement intuition without an honest sunset constitution**.
