# 805 — Sampling frames and case-selection discipline for scope assessments: risk bands, ordinary and failed routes, negative cases, and no benchmark by showcase tour

## One-line thesis

The archive should pair its assessment-mission and tracer-route notes with one compact **sampling-frame grammar**: **every serious scope review should declare which sites, records, routes, stakeholders, and time slices are in frame, which are being sampled representatively or purposively, what is excluded, and what claims the chosen sample can honestly support, so benchmark work cannot drift into prestige tours, complaint-only anecdote, or constitutional overclaim from a thin showcase set**.

## Why this matters

The archive can already say **what the ideal scope stack is** (`790`), **how to benchmark it** (`791`), **how to stress it** (`792`), **when to revisit it** (`793`), **how to move toward it** (`794`), **what proof must stay public** (`795`), **how persistent failure can justify intervention** (`796`), **who can challenge those judgments** (`797`), **which peer class counts before scoring begins** (`798`), **what must happen to temporary constitutional objects** (`799`), **how a serious assessment mission should be run** (`800`), **how the resulting judgment should be stated** (`801`), **how findings become owned repair work** (`802`), **how claimed fixes are later verified** (`803`), and **how one real path through the stack can be traced in motion** (`804`).

What the archive still lacked was one compact rule for a quieter but decisive question:

**which evidence objects get picked in the first place, and what can the review honestly claim from that pick?**

That gap matters because multilevel-governance reviews often go wrong at the selection layer rather than the scoring layer. A municipality shows the best service counter, not the ordinary one. A metro authority offers the route that recently received an intervention, not the route people usually experience. A ministry supplies compliant files, not the missing or contradictory ones. Reviewers hear from the central ministry, the flagship city, and the friendliest civil-society partner, then speak as if they have seen the stack. Another mission goes the other way and treats a handful of complaints as if they automatically represent the whole polity. Neither approach is honest.

Official practice already points toward the missing discipline. WHO’s SARA treats service-readiness measurement as a **systematic survey**, distinguishes between a census for service availability and representative sampling for readiness, and relies on tracer indicators rather than narrative alone. World Bank PETS and QSDS methods map the flow of funds and frontline service realities and then collect information from facilities and other agents **on a sample basis** rather than trusting top-line accounts. GAO guidance treats evidence choice as a **risk-based** exercise and distinguishes between generalizable and nongeneralizable sampling, which is another way of saying that a review must know what kind of claim its sample can support. OECD DAC peer review methodology also resists capital-only review by using headquarters work, partner-country visits, stakeholder consultations, and project visits where they add value. The archive should compress that lesson into one note: **serious scope review needs declared sampling frames and claim limits, not showcase tours or drift-by-anecdote**.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Separate the comparator cohort from the sampling frame

`798` says **who counts as a like-with-like peer**.

This note says **which evidence objects are examined inside the chosen case**. Later notes should now also treat `806` as the place to go once the missing problem is not which objects entered the frame, but how each selected object is then typed, versioned, and linked to findings inside the evidence room.

The archive should not let those collapse into each other. Choosing the right peer class does not yet decide which municipalities, routes, facilities, records, or actors inside the reviewed arrangement will actually be seen.

### 2. Declare the review universes before selecting from them

Every serious mission should name the main **universes** from which evidence objects may be drawn, such as:

- sites or facilities,
- territorial subunits,
- live or closed case files,
- tracer-route classes,
- transfer or payment records,
- stakeholder groups,
- time slices,
- and temporary objects or handback events.

If the universe is not named, the sample cannot later be judged for coverage or omission.

### 3. Name the selection logic for each universe

The archive should expect each universe to carry one declared selection logic, such as:

- **census**,
- **representative sample**,
- **stratified sample**,
- **systematic sample**,
- **risk-based purposive sample**,
- **sentinel / edge-case sample**,
- or **convenience sample with declared limits**.

The point is not methodological vanity. It is to stop the review from speaking one way and sampling another.

### 4. Mix ordinary, failed, and edge cases

A serious scope review should usually avoid both extremes:

- not only flagship successes,
- and not only complaints or disasters.

A compact sample often works best when it includes at least some mix of:

- one ordinary path that is supposed to work,
- one failed, delayed, or disputed path,
- one high-risk or high-stakes path,
- and one boundary or handoff case where responsibility changes hands.

That mix keeps the review from becoming either tourism or grievance curation.

### 5. Sample the seam, not just the room

Where the constitutional question lives at the handoff, the sample should include the **boundary object** itself.

That may mean:

- transfer records between tiers,
- referral packets,
- joint-board decisions,
- delegated-task reporting,
- payment pass-throughs,
- shared-system control changes,
- or one route that crosses from ordinary administration into special measures.

The archive should not let a review sample only within offices when the defect lives between them.

### 6. Keep at least one non-prestige and one negative case in frame

A clean anti-theater rule is simple:

- at least one **non-prestige site or route**,
- and at least one **negative or contradictory case**

should usually appear whenever the mission is large enough to support more than a minimal desk review.

That does not mean manufacturing failure. It means refusing a sample that contains only things the host would have shown anyway.

### 7. Record exclusions, refusals, and blind spots as part of the result

A sample is not honest if it only lists what was seen.

The mission should also preserve where:

- access was denied,
- records were unavailable,
- a stakeholder group could not be reached,
- privacy or legality blocked a desired route,
- the time window excluded relevant shocks,
- or the sample could not cover one morphology or one territorial band.

The archive should treat those absences as part of the evidence surface, not as backstage trivia.

### 8. Bind the verdict to what the sample can support

The archive should distinguish at least three kinds of claim:

- **pattern claim** — this appears in the sampled routes or files and is serious enough to matter,
- **coverage claim** — this appears across enough of the declared frame to support a broader judgment,
- **illustrative claim** — this case is revealing but not enough to generalize from by itself.

Without that discipline, one complaint becomes “the system,” or one well-run site becomes “proof of reform.”

### 9. Refresh or expand the sample when contradiction accumulates

A sample plan is not sacred.

If the mission sees repeated contradictions — for example between the host narrative and sampled routes, or between sampled capital-city files and downstream accounts — the archive should permit a **declared sample refresh**.

That can mean adding one more territorial band, one more route class, one more negative case, one more time slice, or one more stakeholder group. The key is to log the change rather than quietly widening the frame.

### 10. Reuse the same frame for close-out where possible

`803` already says claimed fixes should be verified before closure.

This note adds a selection rule: where the original defect lived in a sampled route, file class, transfer path, or site type, the close-out check should usually revisit that **same class or frame**, not an easier substitute. Otherwise the repair check becomes a new showcase sample.

### 11. Publish the sample rationale without exposing private records

`795` should not dump case files. But it can usually say:

- what the universe was,
- how objects were chosen,
- which strata or risk bands were covered,
- what exclusions remained,
- and what sort of claim the sample supports.

That is enough for outsiders to know whether the review saw the actual stack or only its showroom.

### 12. Keep the sample small, but coverage-bearing

The archive is not trying to write a social-science dissertation every time it reviews a polity.

A small frame is often enough if it is typed honestly. The rule should be to sample **just enough to touch the main seams, one ordinary path, one stressed path, one contradictory or negative case, and the main morphology or territorial bands that matter to the question**.

## Minimum fields for a sampling-frame register

| Field | What it tells later actors | Minimum form | Typical failure if missing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Universe** | what pool the sample came from | sites / routes / files / stakeholders / time window | nobody can tell what was omitted |
| **Selection logic** | how objects were chosen | census / representative / stratified / purposive / convenience | selection silently drifts into theater |
| **Strata or risk bands** | whether ordinary and stressed cases were both covered | one short list | only safe middle cases get seen |
| **Included objects** | what was actually examined | short count or named set | later readers cannot reproduce the frame |
| **Exclusions and access failures** | what the review could not see | one short note | blind spots are laundered into silence |
| **Claim scope** | what the sample can honestly support | illustrative / pattern / broader coverage | anecdote becomes verdict |
| **Reuse rule** | how the frame will be revisited later | retest same class / expand / sunset | close-out swaps in easier proof |

## Minimal sampling-frame grammar

| Universe | Selection logic | What must be in frame | Claim limit | Next use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Municipal service counters** | stratified purposive sample | flagship site, ordinary site, high-burden site | pattern claim about front-door variation, not citywide perfection | benchmark finding under `801` |
| **Grant transfers** | risk-based file sample | on-time transfer, delayed transfer, exception payment | pattern or coverage claim depending frame size | fiscal repair under `802` |
| **Complaint routes** | typed route sample | one resolved case, one stalled case, one escalation case | illustrative unless repeated across strata | tracer-route follow-up under `804` |
| **Handback events** | census if few; purposive if many | all live handbacks or all high-risk handbacks | can support closure only for the sampled class | close-out review under `803` |

## Use rule for the archive

When later notes need to say not just **how a mission runs** or **which route was traced**, but **how the reviewed polity, routes, files, and sites were selected for inspection in the first place**, the archive should usually do these things in order:

1. **use `798` to choose the right comparator cohort, territorial unit, and morphology band**,
2. **use `800` to declare the review question and main evidence lanes**,
3. **use this note to define the universes, selection logic, strata, exclusions, and claim scope**,
4. **use `806` when the missing task is to give the selected objects stable exhibit IDs, provenance, version state, and handling notes**,
5. **use `804` where one sampled object is a real route through the stack**,
6. **use `801` to state findings in language that matches the sample’s actual inferential reach**,
7. **use `802` to assign repair work when the sample reveals a defect**,
8. **use `803` to retest the same class or frame when closure is later claimed**,
9. **use `795` to publish the public-safe rationale for what was looked at and what was not**.

The archive should not let a thin evidence pick quietly become a constitutional verdict.

## Guardrails

- Do not choose only flagship sites, recent pilots, or capital-city surfaces.
- Do not choose only complainant cases and then generalize without saying so.
- Do not hide access failures, missing records, or unvisited strata.
- Do not switch from purposive to “representative” language after the fact.
- Do not retest repair work using an easier frame than the one that revealed the defect.
- Do not let privacy or legality constraints disappear from the description of what the sample could not prove.

## Failure modes

- **showcase-tour constitutionalism** — the host’s best rooms quietly become the constitution.
- **complaint monoculture** — only failure cases are sampled, then treated as if they describe the whole stack.
- **middle-band blindness** — reviewers see ordinary cases and crisis cases but miss the chronic high-burden edge where the state actually frays.
- **seam evasion** — offices are sampled, but the transfer objects between them are not.
- **inferential laundering** — a nongeneralizable sample is written up as if it were representative.
- **closure substitution** — the follow-up review swaps in friendlier sites or files than the original frame.

## Anti-theater tests

1. Does the mission name the universes from which cases, sites, routes, records, or stakeholders were drawn?
2. Is the selection logic for each universe stated plainly enough that a reader can tell whether it was representative, purposive, or convenience-based?
3. Does the frame include at least one ordinary case and one negative or contradictory case where the mission is bigger than a desk review?
4. Where the defect likely lives at a seam, did the sample include the handoff object itself?
5. Are exclusions, access failures, or blind spots visible as part of the result?
6. Do the resulting findings stay within what the sample can honestly support?
7. If repair is later claimed, is the retest using the same class or frame rather than an easier substitute?

## Compression rule

If the archive wants to say that a scope assessment **saw enough of the real system to justify its claim**, it should be able to show one short sampling-frame register naming the universes examined, the selection logic for each, the strata or risk bands covered, the key inclusions and exclusions, and the maximum claim the chosen sample can honestly support. If it cannot, the archive is still too close to **benchmark by showcase tour**.
