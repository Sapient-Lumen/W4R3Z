# 457 — Source-backed summaries, round-trip checks, and no authority by recap

## One-line thesis

Summaries, excerpts, dashboards, FAQs, compiled dossiers, and public-facing recaps for consequential public AI should remain explicitly source-backed, round-trip-checkable, and non-authoritative by default, so compression does not silently create a new governing record.

## Why this matters

Consequential public-AI governance increasingly travels through smaller derivative surfaces: a public summary of an impact assessment, a FAQ excerpt from a canonical disclosure head, a briefing note compiled from logs and supplier material, a system-card paragraph quoted into procurement, a regulator packet assembled from several artifacts, a dashboard tile linked from a case record. Those surfaces are useful. They help people orient, route, and act.

But they also create a recurring governance failure: the recap becomes more readable than the source and quietly inherits the source's authority without preserving the source's caveats, scope, freshness, provenance, or unresolved tensions. A later reader sees one neat paragraph and assumes it carries the full force of the larger record. A staff team adopts a normalized summary while forgetting which parts came from a supplier, which from internal judgment, which from a raw witness, and which from a stale prior note. The archive should therefore refuse recap authority by default.

A derived surface may help. It may even be officially adopted. But it should not become governing merely because it is short, polished, or widely copied.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Distinguish source-native records from derived recaps

For each consequential summary-like artifact, keep visible whether it is:

- a source-native record,
- a frozen excerpt,
- a compiled summary,
- a briefing or routing aid,
- a public simplification of a larger record,
- or an officially adopted derivative surface.

The archive should make readers work less, not infer more.

### 2. Make every recap point back to exact governing sources

A derived surface should identify the exact source set it depends on, such as:

- canonical disclosure head,
- impact dossier,
- determination record,
- witness packet,
- supplier artifact,
- operator note,
- or review signoff.

Where practical, it should identify the version, date, and scope of the source basis, not merely the document family.

### 3. Require a round-trip or spot-check before official adoption

When a recap will inform consequential action, public explanation, oversight response, or durable recordkeeping, the institution should perform a review that asks:

- does the recap preserve the source's load-bearing caveats,
- does it keep disputed or unresolved points visible,
- does it preserve the source's audience and authority class,
- and can a reviewer move from the recap back to the source without guesswork?

The archive does not need a perfect reversible compression. It does need a compact round-trip discipline strong enough to catch recap drift before the recap becomes operational truth.

### 4. Do not let derived language silently inherit institutional adoption

A recap built from supplier material, generated text, operator prose, or mixed evidence should say whether it is:

- merely descriptive,
- adopted in part,
- adopted in full,
- or not an institutional position.

Polish is not adoption. A compiled note is not automatically a governing statement just because it looks official.

### 5. Preserve caveats, omissions, and unresolved tensions inside the recap

A trustworthy summary keeps enough of the original friction to remain honest. It should not erase:

- confidence limits,
- known disputes,
- audience boundaries,
- freshness uncertainty,
- evidence gaps,
- or conditions under which the underlying source is no longer safe to rely on.

The archive should prefer compact truthful residue over clean false closure.

### 6. Keep a replayable bridge from recap to raw evidence where needed

Where a summary depends on logs, witnesses, measurements, or mixed compiled records, the archive should preserve a bridge back to the raw or source-native layer. This can often be a compact pointer set rather than full duplication. The point is that later reviewers should be able to inspect the basis rather than only re-quote the recap.

### 7. Treat recap drift as a real governance change

If a public summary, queue explanation, briefing note template, dashboard label, or oversight digest changes what it says or omits about a consequential system, the archive should treat that as a governed change. Derived surfaces do not become exempt from change control merely because they are secondary.

## Guardrails

- Do not let a recap impersonate the canonical or source-native record.
- Do not sever a summary from the exact artifacts it depends on.
- Do not allow generated, supplier, or mixed compiled text to inherit institutional authority by formatting alone.
- Do not smooth away caveats that remain load-bearing in the source.
- Do not treat a portable normalized digest as a substitute for preserved source provenance.

## Failure modes

- **recap authority theft**: a short derivative artifact becomes the de facto governing record.
- **source severance**: later reviewers can read the summary but cannot locate the exact basis it summarized.
- **caveat laundering**: uncertainty, audience limits, or disputed points disappear during compression.
- **adoption by polish**: readable prose is treated as institutionally adopted without an actual adoption step.
- **portable digest drift**: a normalized packet survives while the source-native evidence lane is forgotten.

## Practical tests

A source-backed-summary discipline passes when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Does each consequential recap say whether it is source-native, derived, or officially adopted?
2. Can a reviewer identify the exact governing sources the recap depends on?
3. Is there a round-trip or spot-check step before the recap is used for consequential action or durable publication?
4. Does the recap preserve caveats, authority class, and unresolved points that remain load-bearing?
5. Can later reviewers move from the recap back to the source-native or raw-evidence lane without guesswork?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a summary cannot answer **what exact sources it stands on, what authority it has, and how a reviewer gets back to the basis**, then the archive is still letting a **recap** impersonate a **record**.
