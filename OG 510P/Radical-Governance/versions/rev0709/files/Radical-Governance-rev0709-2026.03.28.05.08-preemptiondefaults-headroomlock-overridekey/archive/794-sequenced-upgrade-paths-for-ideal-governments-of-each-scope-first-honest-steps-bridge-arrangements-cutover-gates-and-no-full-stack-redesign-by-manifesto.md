# 794 — Sequenced upgrade paths for ideal governments of each scope: first honest steps, bridge arrangements, cutover gates, and no full-stack redesign by manifesto

## One-line thesis

When a real polity is far from the archive’s ideal scope stack, reform should move through a sequenced upgrade path: **make the current arrangement honest first, repair the minimum complete kit and fiscal congruence next, constitutionalise any bridge institutions that are already exercising real power, transfer functions only with continuity proof, and attempt deeper territorial or constitutional redesign only after the operating spine is real**.

## Why this matters

`790` now gives the archive a short answer key for what ideal governments at each scope should generally be. `791` gives a reusable benchmark card for judging whether a real constitution or proposal matches that stack in ordinary time. `792` asks whether the arrangement remains honest under shock. `793` says how that judgment should be revisited on a public calendar instead of by sporadic panic. `795` says what minimum outward evidence should stay public while that journey is underway. `796` now separates voluntary or negotiated upgrade paths from harder intervention ladders for cases where repeated failure forces someone to compel repair. `799` now separates the sequencing question from the narrower ending rule for temporary bridges, pilots, derogations, and other exceptional objects once they have lasted long enough to count. Elsewhere, the archive already has deeper machinery for order of operations (`603`), minimum design packets (`700`), support and handback (`631`), drift triggers (`632`), function transfer (`586`), continuity (`730`), cutover witness (`731`), transitional hosting (`732`), and post-transfer stabilization (`734`).

What the archive still lacked was one compact rule for a practical question that follows naturally from all of that work: **how do you move from an actually existing misfit arrangement toward the ideal one without pretending you can redraw the whole constitution in one jump?**

That gap matters because reformers often swing between two bad instincts. One is **white-paper maximalism**: produce an elegant final-state diagram, announce a new metropolitan, regional, or localist settlement, and ignore the order in which money, staff, records, scrutiny, and public front doors must actually move. The other is **permanent transitionalism**: admit that the current arrangement is weak, create interim boards, host-city workarounds, delegated side deals, or emergency support bodies, and then leave them in place so long that the bridge becomes the constitution.

A better approach should accept that constitutional repair is often phased. But it should insist that phasing itself be typed, public, reversible where possible, and disciplined by explicit gates. The first job is usually not to make the polity perfect. It is to stop the polity from lying about what it already is.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Start from diagnosis, not from the end-state sketch

A sequenced upgrade path should begin with the archive’s existing diagnostic instruments.

- Use `791` to show what is weak in ordinary time.
- Use `792` to show what collapses under stress.
- Use `793` to show what can be repaired on the normal maintenance clock and what cannot wait.
- Use `632` when the evidence is serious enough to justify jumping the queue.

The point is to sequence from the real breakage pattern, not from a prestige model imported whole.

### 2. Make the current reality honest before moving more power

Before transferring functions upward, downward, or sideways, the polity should state honestly what the current arrangement already is.

If a host city is already acting as the real metro treasury, if a ministry is already rescuing a nominally devolved service every winter, if a regional board already sequences capital across municipalities, or if a treaty secretariat is already operating the common registry that members publicly rely on, the first repair is to stop describing that arrangement as something lighter than it is.

This may require an interim publication duty, a named control plane, a temporary legal wrapper, or a benchmark reclassification before any grand redesign occurs.

### 3. Repair minimum complete kit before expanding mandate

A level should not receive more work while it is still missing the minimum institutional kit needed for work it already has.

In most cases, the right first-phase repair is one of these:

- complete the forum, executive spine, records, and review lane;
- fix the fiscal lane and predictable operating money;
- repair basic staffing and procurement depth;
- make the public front door and responsibility map legible.

The archive should therefore prefer **kit completion before mandate expansion**. A polity that cannot yet govern honestly at its present scope usually should not be given a larger one as a motivational speech.

### 4. Sequence legal, fiscal, administrative, and democratic upgrades together

Scope repair is rarely just a legal act.

A function transfer that moves statute but not budget is fake. A transfer that moves budget but not staff and records is brittle. A transfer that moves operations but not scrutiny is shadow government. A transfer that moves power first and promises elections, audit, or appeal later is often a constitutional debt that never gets paid.

The upgrade path should therefore name, phase by phase:

- the legal act,
- the fiscal cutover,
- the staff and operating move,
- the records and systems move,
- and the democratic or review upgrade required by the power being acquired.

### 5. Use bridge arrangements only as typed constitutional objects

Some transitions genuinely require temporary machinery: a transitional metro host, a shared service shell, a joint implementation office, a neutral custodian, a phased fiscal bridge, or a staged transfer of live queues and records.

The archive should allow that. But it should require the bridge to be named as a bridge, not disguised as ordinary normality.

Every bridge arrangement should say:

- who the temporary holder is,
- what powers remain temporary,
- what the expiry or review clock is,
- what readiness test moves the next phase,
- what failure sends the matter to repair, handback, or reopened scope review.

Otherwise transition becomes a way of smuggling in permanent twilight government.

### 6. Move operating spines before symbolic constitutional ornament

The sequence should prioritise the parts that keep public authority continuous and intelligible.

In many cases that means moving or repairing, in roughly this order:

1. public front door and responsibility map,
2. budget and payment authority,
3. records and case state,
4. staff, contracts, and operational control,
5. review and complaint lanes,
6. only then the more ornamental or prestige-heavy constitutional surfaces.

The archive should distrust reform packages that foreground titles, logos, ceremonial officers, or chamber architecture while the real operating spine still depends on inherited host machinery.

### 7. Keep service continuity and rights protection live throughout the cutover

A sequenced upgrade path should not ask residents to absorb constitutional experimentation as service disappearance.

Every phase should therefore preserve:

- one visible public route,
- one named accountable holder,
- live complaint and review access,
- continuity for essential payments, permits, care, utilities, or emergency functions,
- and one clear notice of what changed and what did not.

If the transition cannot maintain those basics, the polity is not in orderly reform. It is in governance debt transfer.

### 8. Use asymmetry and pilots as reversible learning, not as a substitute constitution

Phased reform often needs pilots, differentiated rollouts, optional entry paths, or special-status territories that move first.

The archive should allow such asymmetry when it is treated as bounded learning. That means the polity should declare:

- why the pilot or asymmetry exists,
- what evidence would count as success or failure,
- what common floors still bind everyone,
- and whether the expected destination is wider rollout, stable asymmetry, or abandonment.

The archive should reject pilots that are really indefinite constitutional evasions.

### 9. Upgrade legitimacy when power hardens

A temporary body may begin as a co-ordination object and later become the real government of a field or territory.

When that happens, the sequence should force a legitimacy upgrade. If a metropolitan authority begins borrowing, setting distributive priorities, or binding member municipalities, the polity should move from summitry toward a real forum and stronger scrutiny. If a regional platform becomes the decisive steward of hospitals, grids, corridors, or higher education, the polity should stop pretending it is only a technical consortium. If a plurilateral compact begins operating shared funds, inspection, or compliance machinery, member-state and parliamentary scrutiny should deepen accordingly.

The archive should therefore tie **power hardening** to **legitimacy hardening** rather than leaving the democratic bill for later.

### 10. Separate immediate honesty repairs from deep territorial surgery

Not every benchmark failure requires immediate merger, abolition, or map redrawing.

Often the right sequence is:

- first, make the existing arrangement honest and operable;
- second, complete the missing kit and finance;
- third, test whether the arrangement still fails after honest repair;
- only then decide whether territorial, constitutional, or treaty redesign is truly necessary.

This protects the archive from mistaking every operational failure for proof that only the deepest possible rescoping can save the system.

### 11. Publish phase gates and stop-go consequences

A sequenced upgrade path should be public enough that outsiders can tell where the reform actually stands.

Each phase should therefore publish:

- the current phase,
- the gate conditions for the next phase,
- the owner of each condition,
- the proof object that will show completion,
- the fallback if the phase fails,
- and the next review date.

Without this, sequencing becomes headline choreography rather than governed transition.

### 12. End every path in one of four outcomes

The archive should not let reform pathways wander forever.

Every sequenced upgrade path should converge on one of four outputs:

- **stabilise in place** — the repaired arrangement is now honest enough to keep and maintain;
- **upgrade and settle** — the target scope and kit are now live and can enter ordinary maintenance under `793`;
- **hand back or step down** — temporary support or bridge machinery is no longer justified under `631`;
- **reopen scope** — the evidence still shows misfit serious enough to return to `632`.

That keeps the pathway from becoming a permanent constitutional halfway house.

## Upgrade-path grammar

| Field | What the pathway should say | Typical outputs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Starting condition** | what is wrong in the current arrangement | shell municipality / invisible metro / decorative region / overabsorbing center / thin compact with hidden operational load |
| **Target archetype** | which scope-form the polity is moving toward | stronger municipality / real metropolitan government / typed regional platform / repaired national guarantor / bounded compact |
| **Immediate honesty repair** | what must be publicly admitted or typed before bigger redesign | name the real host / publish the real control plane / reclassify delegated dependence / expose hidden rescue |
| **Phase-1 kit repair** | which missing forum, fiscal, staffing, record, or review elements must be completed first | records spine / predictable revenue / complaint lane / staff core / audit route |
| **Bridge arrangement** | what temporary object carries the transition and on what expiry logic | transitional host / shared implementation office / phased transfer shell / neutral custodian |
| **Cutover gate** | what proof is required before moving the next live surface | budget authority live / records migrated / staff appointed / public route switched / review lane active |
| **Legitimacy upgrade trigger** | what increase in real power requires stronger scrutiny or democracy | borrowing / binding standards / distributive power / taxation / enforcement / common fund control |
| **Fallback or handback** | what happens if the phase fails | pause / repair / rollback / support-with-handback / reopen scope |
| **Steady-state landing** | where the arrangement goes once transition ends | ordinary maintenance under `793` / continued benchmark watch under `791` / reopened scope review under `632` |

## Typical sequencing by scope

| Scope problem | Usually honest first move | Later move only if the first repair holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Micro-local body that is overburdened** | shrink sovereign theater; keep voice, witness, tiny upkeep, and upward escalation | only add bounded micro-fiscal or stewardship powers if records and escalation discipline are real |
| **Weak municipality** | complete books, staff, front door, review lane, and predictable operating money | only then widen mandates or devolve more complex service responsibilities |
| **Invisible metropolitan reality** | publish the real shared-system ledger and typed interim metro bridge instead of mayoral folklore | only then move to shared finance, binding powers, and stronger democratic forum |
| **Questionable regional tier** | prove the real platform or shared-rule vocation; strip decorative duplication where necessary | only then constitutionalise deeper regional authority or, if proof fails, collapse or reroute functions |
| **Overabsorbing national center** | separate guarantor duties from ordinary place operations; publish delegated and rescue dependence honestly | only then hand functions down or sideways with full transfer packs and equalization discipline |
| **Continental or plurilateral compact drifting upward** | restate mandate, reporting, compliance, and member implementation boundaries | only then deepen common funds, inspection, or dispute machinery with stronger scrutiny and amendment discipline |
| **Global regime under pressure to overclaim** | clarify the thin narrow-waist function, reporting lane, and implementation boundary | only then consider any mandate deepening through explicit treaty or review-conference route |

## Use rule for the archive

When later notes ask not only **what is the ideal government here?**, **how does it benchmark?**, **how does it hold under shock?**, or **how is it maintained?**, but also **how should a real polity get from here to there?**, the archive should usually do ten things in order:

1. **use `790` for the target stack**,
2. **use `791` to diagnose ordinary-time weakness**,
3. **use `792` to identify shock-weather breakage**,
4. **use `793` to distinguish routine maintenance from transition work**,
5. **use `631` and `632` to distinguish handback lanes from reopened scope review**,
6. **use this note to state the phased upgrade path and gate order**,
7. **use `795` when the issue is what public phase board or outward proof surface should stay live during the transition**,
8. **use `796` when the path is no longer mainly voluntary or negotiated and the question becomes what support, supervision, temporary special measures, or handback discipline are legitimate before or instead of durable rescoping**,
9. **use `799` when the missing rule is not the phase order itself but what should happen to a temporary bridge, pilot, derogation, or transition host once it reaches its stated end date: expire, renew, graduate, or be buried**,
10. **add only the domain-specific transfer or continuity packet that the case actually needs**.

The archive should not keep pretending that every misfit government must choose between immediate perfection and indefinite limbo.

## Guardrails

- Do not transfer more power to an arrangement that has not completed its current minimum kit.
- Do not treat bridge institutions as harmless if they already exercise real governing power.
- Do not move statute first and promise money, staff, records, or scrutiny later.
- Do not use pilots or asymmetry without declaring the learning question and exit rule.
- Do not confuse emergency support with permanent constitutional settlement.
- Do not jump straight to map surgery when honest repair has not yet been attempted.
- Do not let transition hide the public front door, accountable holder, or review route.

## Failure modes

- **full-stack redesign by manifesto** — the reform announces the final map without sequencing money, staff, records, or review.
- **permanent bridge government** — interim hosts, joint boards, or rescue structures quietly become the real constitution.
- **mandate before kit** — powers move first and the receiving level spends years governing as a shell.
- **symbolism before operating spine** — titles, logos, and ceremonial offices change while the actual runtime remains elsewhere.
- **pilot without destination** — differentiated rollout becomes permanent ambiguity because no success, failure, or rollout rule was declared.
- **hidden cutover harm** — residents discover only through delay, outage, or misrouting that the authority map changed.
- **democratic debt rollover** — a body acquires real distributive or coercive power but legitimacy upgrade is deferred indefinitely.
- **deep surgery as first instinct** — merger, abolition, or treaty deepening is attempted before simpler honesty and kit repairs are tested.

## Practical tests

A sequenced upgrade path is being stated honestly when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Does the pathway begin from an actual benchmark or stress diagnosis rather than a prestige model alone?
2. Does the first phase make the current arrangement more honest before making it grander?
3. Are legal, fiscal, administrative, records, and scrutiny changes all named rather than hidden inside one reform headline?
4. Is every temporary bridge typed, timed, and linked to an exit or failure lane?
5. Can the public still identify the live front door, accountable holder, and review route during every phase?
6. Are legitimacy upgrades tied to increases in real power rather than postponed indefinitely?
7. Does the pathway name whether failure leads to repair, rollback, handback, or reopened scope review?
8. Is there a clear landing into ordinary maintenance under `793` once the transition is complete?

## Compression rule for the archive

If the archive wants to say not only that a government-by-scope design is desirable, but that a real polity can **reach it without constitutional fantasy**, it should usually be able to do so with one short upgrade-path object naming the starting condition, target archetype, immediate honesty repair, phase-1 kit repair, bridge arrangement, cutover gate, legitimacy-upgrade trigger, fallback lane, and steady-state landing. If it cannot, the reform pathway is still too atmospheric to count as an honest archive judgment.
