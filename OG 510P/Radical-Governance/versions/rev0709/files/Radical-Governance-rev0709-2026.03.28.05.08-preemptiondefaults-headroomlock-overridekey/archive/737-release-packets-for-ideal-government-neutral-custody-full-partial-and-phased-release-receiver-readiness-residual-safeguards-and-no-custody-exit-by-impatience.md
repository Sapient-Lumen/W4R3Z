# 737 — Release packets for ideal-government neutral custody: full, partial, and phased release, receiver readiness, residual safeguards, and no custody exit by impatience

## One-line thesis

Once the archive has a temporary neutral-custody packet (`736`), it should also keep one compact **custody-release packet** for the moment named surfaces leave custody and re-enter ordinary government: classify the release type, prove receiver readiness surface by surface, preserve case and record lineage, state what safeguards remain temporarily in force, and refuse to end custody merely because a deadline, press statement, or impatient principal wants the appearance of settlement.

## Why this matters

The recent ideal-government scope-shift cluster now reaches the hardest middle cases.

The archive can say **where a function should normally live** (`726`), **when defaults should be overridden** (`727`), **how sideways authorities should be constituted** (`728`), **who must ratify a move** (`729`), **what continuity must survive it** (`730`), **how to witness cutover** (`731`), **how bounded hosting works** (`732`), **how hosting actually ends** (`733`), **how post-closure stability is tested** (`734`), **how failed stabilization is rolled back** (`735`), and **how a bounded neutral custodian can hold named surfaces when neither ordinary holder is yet an honest constitutional home** (`736`).

What still sat open was the next compact proof object:

**how does the polity prove that a surface can leave neutral custody and enter a named ordinary holder without merely reenacting the confusion that made custody necessary?**

That gap matters because custody is easier to declare than to end. A court, ministry, or emergency board says the queue, records spine, keys, or public route are in neutral hands for now. Then pressure builds to end the embarrassing interim. The successor says it is ready. The predecessor says the files are incomplete. A supplier is willing to hand over credentials but not documentation. One portal is live while notices still issue from another mailbox. Payments can resume, but appeals and audit lookups still depend on the custodian’s crosswalk. In public, leaders want to say the crisis phase is over. In reality, only some surfaces are ready, others are not, and nobody has produced one compact object stating exactly what is being released, to whom, on what proof, with what residue still bounded.

Without that object, the archive still permits several dangerous fictions. Neutral custody can end because political patience ran out, not because surfaces are actually releasable. The receiving holder can inherit legal title without receiving enough operational, evidentiary, and public-route coherence to hold the function honestly. Keys can move while audit trails, pending-matter ledgers, or notice-home crosswalks remain stranded. A "full release" can be announced while several consequential surfaces are in fact still shared, mirrored, or informally mediated by the custodian.

The archive therefore needs one smaller anti-fiction object after `736`: a **custody-release packet**. It is not a new ratification packet. It is not a settlement certificate (`641`). It does not decide the ultimate constitutional design question by itself. It does one narrower job: it proves, surface by surface, that custody can end for this named release class and that the next holder is receiving something capable of being governed rather than a ceremonial shell. Without that packet, the archive still permits **custody exit by impatience**.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Treat release from neutral custody as a typed event, not a mood of relief

The end of custody should never be described only as "normal service resumes" or "the matter is handed back." The packet should classify what kind of release is actually occurring.

At minimum, it should distinguish:

- **full release** — all named custodial surfaces move to one ordinary holder and custody ends;
- **partial release** — only some surfaces leave custody while others remain in custody or under another bounded bridge;
- **phased release** — surfaces leave in a declared sequence with hold points between phases;
- **conditional release** — release is effective only while named safeguards, monitors, or residue controls remain in force;
- **failed release attempt** — the release window was opened, but proof failed and custody or rollback remains necessary.

If release type is not stated, public language will overclaim finality.

### 2. Name the target next state before naming the receiving body

A custody-release packet should not merely say who receives the surfaces. It should say **into what constitutional state they are being released**.

Examples include:

- release to the designated successor as ordinary principal holder;
- release back to the predecessor under a rollback packet (`735`);
- split release into two or more ordinary holders under a lane split or redesign;
- release into a bounded sideways authority under a packet of the kind described in `728`;
- or release into a narrower temporary bridge such as transitional hosting (`732`) where full ordinary holding is still not yet honest.

This matters because the same named institution may receive surfaces under very different legal and operational conditions.

### 3. Require receiver-readiness proof for each released surface

No surface should leave custody merely because the receiving body is the politically preferred destination. The packet should require a short readiness statement for every released surface.

For each surface, the packet should say whether the receiver can:

- lawfully hold it;
- actually operate it;
- preserve its record and audit obligations;
- answer public queries through the authoritative route;
- and absorb the resulting liabilities, clocks, and review duties.

The archive should prefer a release schedule that names separate readiness for the public front door, rights-bearing queues, records spine, keys and credentials, payment lane, supplier-instruction lane, and public status lane. A body that can receive the website but not the appeal ledger is not ready for full release.

### 4. Force the custodian to produce one residue statement before release

Release should not be validated by receiver claims alone. The custodian should have to say what remains unresolved, what has been delivered, and what is no longer in its hands.

That residue statement should cover:

- items fully transferred;
- items copied but not yet re-homed;
- items retained temporarily under explicit authority;
- items still disputed or under seal;
- items destroyed or disposed of under lawful authority;
- and any residual dependencies that would make the release class less than full.

Without one residue statement, the polity cannot tell whether custody really ended or merely became harder to see.

### 5. Preserve lineage across queues, records, and identifiers at the moment of release

A custody-release packet should insist that release preserves lookup integrity. A later user, reviewer, auditor, or court should be able to start from the custody state and trace forward to the successor home without folklore.

The packet should therefore name:

- the canonical identifier crosswalk from custody-era references to successor references;
- which historical identifiers remain valid for lookup;
- how pending matters are mapped, split, merged, or retired;
- where evidence of custody-era interventions remains available;
- and how notice, review, and payment history remain discoverable after release.

No polity should allow custody to end in a way that breaks the evidentiary spine.

### 6. Release high-risk controls through validated transfer, not ambient access decay

Keys, signatures, admin privileges, repository rights, payment authorities, publication controls, and other high-risk powers should leave custody only by an explicit transfer method.

At minimum, the packet should say for each such control whether it is:

- revoked and reissued;
- transferred with validation and witnessed acceptance;
- converted into split control for a bounded residue period;
- or kept in escrow because ordinary release is still unsafe.

The archive should reject the fiction in which old access simply times out, shared passwords keep circulating, or a copied token is treated as proper constitutional release.

### 7. Keep one authoritative public route during and immediately after release

Release from custody should reduce public confusion, not generate another multi-door interval. The packet should say what the public sees at the moment of release and during any residue period.

That means naming:

- the authoritative route for new submissions;
- the status route for matters created during custody;
- the notice-home for consequential communications after release;
- the redirect logic for custodian-era channels;
- and the correction path if predecessor, successor, custodian, or supplier surfaces continue to publish conflicting instructions.

If the public cannot tell which door is real on release day, custody has not honestly ended.

### 8. State which safeguards survive release, and for how long

Some releases are honest only if a narrow residue of safeguards remains in force for a short time. The packet should state those residual controls openly rather than pretending they do not exist.

Examples include:

- enhanced logging or monitoring;
- dual signoff on exceptional actions;
- holdbacks on disposal or deletion;
- continuing escrow of source code or encryption material;
- temporary restrictions on bulk export or schema changes;
- special review triggers for service-floor breaches;
- or a rapid re-entry trigger back into custody or rollback.

Residual safeguards do not make a release dishonest. Hidden safeguards do.

### 9. Dock release to the next review lane immediately

A custody-release packet should not leave the post-release future undefined. It should say which lane opens next.

Depending on the release type, that may be:

- an independent-live stabilization window of the kind described in `734`;
- a rollback completion lane under `735`;
- a bridge-closure or transitional-hosting lane under `732`-`733`;
- or the wider settlement lane under `641` if the released arrangement is already mature enough and no separate stabilization period is needed.

This prevents custody exit from becoming its own silent destination.

### 10. Require one short public statement that is narrower than settlement rhetoric

The public statement accompanying release should be accurate, short, and less ambitious than celebratory settlement language.

At minimum it should say:

- which surfaces left custody;
- who now holds them;
- whether release is full, partial, phased, or conditional;
- what routes the public should use now;
- whether any safeguards or phased steps remain;
- and what the next review date or next packet is.

The archive should not let custody-release communications say "resolved" or "fully transferred" unless the packet actually proves those stronger claims.

## Guardrails

- Do not end custody merely because the interim has become politically inconvenient.
- Do not treat the legally preferred receiver as ready without surface-by-surface readiness proof.
- Do not transfer keys, records, queues, or notice homes in ways that break identifier lineage.
- Do not announce full release while residual controls still give the custodian or predecessor consequential hidden power.
- Do not let copied access, mirrored databases, or informal hotline help count as honest release.
- Do not let public messaging outrun the typed release class.
- Do not leave the post-release review lane implicit.

## Failure modes

- **custody exit by impatience**: interim custody ends because leaders want normality theater, not because the surfaces are releasable.
- **receiver shell release**: the receiving body gets title, branding, or a portal while the real evidentiary or operational substrate remains elsewhere.
- **split-brain release**: several actors continue to hold overlapping control because the packet never named which surfaces truly left custody.
- **lineage fracture on exit**: pending matters, historical identifiers, or record-trace paths stop working once the custodial crosswalk disappears.
- **residual-power laundering**: the custodian, predecessor, or supplier still holds hidden approval, publication, or recovery powers after a supposed full release.
- **celebratory overclaim**: public language describes settled normality while the release is only partial, phased, or conditional.
- **review vacuum after release**: custody ends but nobody states whether the next honest lane is stabilization, rollback completion, renewed hosting, or settlement.

## Practical tests

A custody-release regime is honest when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Is release from custody classified as full, partial, phased, conditional, or failed rather than described vaguely?
2. Does the packet name the constitutional next state, not only the receiving organisation?
3. Is there receiver-readiness proof for each consequential released surface?
4. Has the custodian produced a residue statement saying what did and did not leave custody?
5. Do queues, records, identifiers, notices, and payment histories preserve lookup lineage after release?
6. Are high-risk controls transferred through validated methods rather than ambient access drift?
7. Is there one authoritative public route during and immediately after release?
8. Are any residual safeguards stated openly, with clocks and triggers?
9. Does the packet dock immediately into the next review lane?
10. Is the public statement narrower than settlement theater and consistent with the actual release class?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a polity can say **neutral custody is ending for named surfaces**, it should also be able to show one compact packet proving **what kind of release is occurring, what next state those surfaces are entering, why the receiver is ready, what the custodian is truly relinquishing, what lineage and safeguards survive, and what next review lane opens immediately after release**. Otherwise it still permits **custody exit by impatience**.
