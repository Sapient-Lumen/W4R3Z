# 736 — Temporary neutral-custody packets for ideal-government scope shifts: contested handoffs, rights-bearing queues, record spines, credential escrow, and no settlement by possession

## One-line thesis

Once the archive can say how a function should be routed (`726`-`728`), ratified (`729`), kept continuous (`730`), witnessed at cutover (`731`), temporarily hosted (`732`), cleanly closed (`733`), tested through an independent-live window (`734`), and rolled back when stabilization fails (`735`), it should also keep one compact **temporary neutral-custody packet** for the harder interval where neither old holder nor new holder can honestly be treated as fully settled: appoint a bounded custodian for named control surfaces, preserve the public route and evidentiary spine, freeze or escrow high-risk powers, keep pending matters legible, and refuse to let mere possession of live systems, records, or credentials masquerade as constitutional settlement.

## Why this matters

The recent ideal-government scope-shift cluster now answers most of the ordinary transfer story.

The archive can say **where a function should normally live** (`726`), **when defaults should be overridden** (`727`), **how a sideways authority must be constituted** (`728`), **who must ratify a move** (`729`), **what continuity has to survive it** (`730`), **how to witness the handoff moment** (`731`), **how a bounded temporary host works** (`732`), **how to prove that host really ended** (`733`), **how to test whether the successor is independently holding the function** (`734`), and **how to reverse or re-route a failed settlement** (`735`).

What the cluster still lacked was the next emergency-grade proof object: **what should exist when neither side can honestly be treated as fully settled yet**.

That case is easy to recognize in practice. A successor has legal title, but the predecessor still controls the only working credentials. A rollback has begun, but handing live queues straight back would break benefits, hearings, payments, or safety-critical dispatch. Two bodies both claim authority over the same records spine, and neither can be trusted to rewrite or trim it without prejudice. A critical supplier is failing, so the public system needs a neutral release of code, keys, or technical documentation before either operating side can safely proceed. A court, regulator, cabinet, or treaty body has frozen the larger settlement question, but the public lane still needs one honest door, one evidence spine, and one temporary custodian for the live estate.

Without a compact object for that interval, institutions fall back on dangerous folklore. Whoever physically controls the mailbox, portal, API key, or database starts acting as the “real” government. The old holder claims emergency necessity. The new holder claims legal title. Vendors wait for contradictory instructions. Residents see two phone numbers, two websites, or two appeal addresses. Records and queues keep moving, but nobody can later prove under whose custody they moved or what changes were made while title was contested.

The archive therefore needs one small packet that later notes can cite instead of improvising this scene every time: **a temporary neutral-custody packet for ideal-government scope shifts**. The packet should not create a hidden third government. It should only establish a bounded custodian for named surfaces and a short period: enough to preserve continuity, rights, records, and public legibility while the polity decides whether the next honest state is settlement, rollback, split routing, or deeper redesign. Without that object, the archive still permits **settlement by possession**.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Trigger neutral custody only when ordinary principal-holder logic is no longer honest

A neutral-custody packet should not be the default answer to operational difficulty. It should be triggered only when the archive can no longer say, without fiction, that one ordinary holder fully controls the consequential surfaces that matter.

At minimum, the packet should distinguish:

- **contested-title custody** — law, regulation, or review has made the principal holder uncertain or actively disputed;
- **unsafe unilateral-control custody** — one side still possesses keys, systems, funds, or records that it should not be allowed to exploit as settlement proof;
- **rights-preservation custody** — immediate return or onward transfer would foreseeably damage rights-bearing queues, notices, payments, hearings, or remedies;
- **evidence-preservation custody** — records, audit logs, or decision traces need a protected custodian before either side can safely rewrite, reindex, redact, or dispose of them;
- **supplier-failure or dependency-failure custody** — a neutral escrow, release, or interim operator is needed so public continuity does not depend on the failing party’s continued goodwill.

If the trigger class is not named, neutral custody will sprawl into vague “special measures” rhetoric.

### 2. Classify exactly which surfaces move into neutral custody

Neutral custody should be surface-specific. The packet should never say merely that “the function is under neutral administration.” It should say which named surfaces are temporarily held or mediated.

At minimum, the archive should allow separate custody for:

- the **public front door**;
- the **rights-bearing intake and queue spine**;
- the **canonical records and evidence spine**;
- the **credential and key lane**;
- the **payment, trust, or escrow lane**;
- the **supplier instruction lane**;
- and the **public status and notice lane**.

Some crises require neutral custody of only one or two of these surfaces. Others require several. But the packet should never allow symbolic appointment of a custodian while real control surfaces stay elsewhere.

### 3. Name the custodian, appointing authority, and basis of legitimacy

Every neutral-custody packet should say:

- **who the custodian is**;
- **which authority appointed it**;
- **what instrument or order created the custody**;
- and **why that custodian is entitled to hold only these surfaces for only this interval**.

The custodian may be a court-appointed administrator, archivally recognized custodian, escrow agent, special administrator, statutory officer, jointly supervised board, or other bounded trustee-like body. But the packet should refuse any arrangement where the custodian’s legitimacy rests only on operational convenience or on unilateral appointment by one of the disputing holders.

### 4. Separate neutral custody from substantive government

The custodian should preserve the lane, not silently become the new sovereign. A packet should therefore say what the custodian **may not** do except under explicit emergency floor rules.

At minimum, the custodian should be blocked from:

- rewriting the long-run constitutional settlement;
- adopting major new policy;
- changing eligibility or rights floors except where an emergency preservation rule expressly allows it;
- disposing of records, assets, or code beyond the packet’s preservation logic;
- and extending its own remit by reference to backlog, convenience, or superior runtime knowledge.

Without these limits, neutral custody quickly mutates into unratified government.

### 5. Keep one canonical ledger for pending matters while custody lasts

The core reason for neutral custody is usually to stop rights-bearing work from vanishing into a jurisdiction fight. The packet should therefore require one canonical pending-matters ledger covering all live matters inside the custodial surfaces.

That ledger should state:

- what class of matter it covers;
- whether the matter is merely preserved, actively worked, or frozen;
- which clocks continue, stop, or are tolled;
- who may take emergency action and on what basis;
- what notice duty exists to the affected person or institution;
- and how the matter will later be released, remapped, or crosswalked to the next honest holder.

No one should be allowed to claim that “nothing changed” when the only live queue has in fact moved into neutral custody.

### 6. Preserve the record spine under custody-grade chain-of-custody rules

Where records or evidence enter neutral custody, the packet should make the custodian responsible for preserving the canonical spine rather than for paraphrasing it.

That means naming:

- the authoritative record home during custody;
- the exact change controls that apply to ingestion, annotation, redaction, export, and disposal;
- the event log for every movement or transformation;
- the lookup path for historical identifiers and crosswalks;
- and the release rule for later handback, transfer, or archival deposit.

If the polity cannot later show what changed in the file while custody lasted, then neutral custody has failed its central purpose.

### 7. Freeze, split, or escrow high-risk powers rather than leaving them ambient

Credentials, publishing rights, code repositories, signature devices, payment rails, vendor-admin privileges, and other high-risk controls should not remain ambient while settlement is disputed. The packet should say whether each such control is:

- **frozen**;
- **held by the custodian**;
- **split across more than one actor**;
- or **placed in escrow with clear release events**.

This is where the packet blocks one of the most common constitutional frauds of transition: the side that still has the key claims that key possession itself proves that it remains the real holder.

### 8. Keep one authoritative public route and one notice-home while custody lasts

A neutral-custody packet should immediately collapse public confusion. Residents, firms, frontline staff, courts, and oversight bodies should be able to find one authoritative route for status, notices, submissions, complaints, and emergency queries.

The packet should therefore name:

- the temporary public route;
- the notice-home hierarchy during custody;
- the redirect rules for displaced channels;
- the public status language that honestly describes custody without overselling settlement;
- and the correction route if other bodies continue to publish stale or conflicting instructions.

The archive should not permit dual-front-door government during a custody interval.

### 9. Preserve accountability, liability, and review despite the temporary custodian

Neutral custody should not become an accountability sink. The packet should say:

- who remains responsible for past acts of the predecessor and successor;
- who answers for harms caused during custody itself;
- which review, ombuds, tribunal, audit, or court routes remain open;
- and who pays for preservation, replay, escrow, notification, or emergency servicing.

The custodian may hold the lane, but it should not become a laundering device that erases responsibility for the events that made custody necessary.

### 10. Force an exit lane, review clock, and anti-permanence statement

Neutral custody is only honest if it is visibly temporary. Every packet should therefore include:

- an initial maximum custody window;
- periodic review points;
- the proof burden for extension;
- the typed exit lanes available — release to successor, release to predecessor, split handback, onward transfer to a sideways authority, archival deposit, or deeper redesign;
- and one anti-permanence statement that says custody does not itself settle the constitutional question.

Without that exit grammar, neutral custody becomes permanent government by fiduciary fog.

## Guardrails

- Do not appoint neutral custody where an ordinary named principal can honestly hold the relevant surfaces.
- Do not let the custodian’s operational possession become evidence that the larger settlement is resolved.
- Do not move records, queues, code, keys, or funds into custody without a surface-by-surface schedule.
- Do not let the custodian silently exercise policy powers that were never delegated.
- Do not leave notice, complaint, appeal, or status channels split across rival bodies.
- Do not allow escrow, archive, or special-administration language to hide who may still take consequential action.
- Do not extend custody by inertia, backlog, or the custodian’s superior familiarity with the system.

## Failure modes

- **settlement by possession**: whoever still controls the live system, mailbox, or key is treated as the real government without proper settlement.
- **custodian drift into hidden sovereignty**: the neutral holder starts making substantive policy or allocation choices because its preservation remit was never bounded.
- **dual-front-door confusion**: the public sees several active channels and cannot tell which one is authoritative during custody.
- **queue limbo**: rights-bearing matters sit in “temporary” status without a canonical ledger, clock logic, or release rule.
- **evidence laundering under protection rhetoric**: records are altered, reindexed, or selectively withheld while said to be “under safeguard.”
- **escrow without release discipline**: code, keys, or data are notionally protected but the release triggers, validation rules, and version discipline are absent.
- **temporary forever by fiduciary fog**: the custodian remains because no one stated the exit lane or the burden for extension.

## Practical tests

A neutral-custody regime is honest when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Has the polity shown why ordinary principal-holder logic is not currently honest?
2. Does the packet name exactly which surfaces move into neutral custody?
3. Is the custodian named, properly appointed, and bounded by a visible legal or constitutional basis?
4. Does the packet separate preservation custody from substantive government?
5. Is there one canonical ledger for pending rights-bearing matters during custody?
6. Are the records spine, identifiers, and event logs preserved under chain-of-custody rules?
7. Are keys, credentials, code, funds, and supplier instructions frozen, split, or escrowed rather than left ambient?
8. Is there one authoritative public route and one notice-home while custody lasts?
9. Are accountability, liability, and review routes preserved rather than submerged into the custodian?
10. Does the packet contain a real review clock and exit lane so neutrality cannot become permanent rule?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a polity can honestly say **neither ordinary holder can safely be treated as fully settled yet**, it should also be able to show one compact packet proving **which surfaces moved into temporary neutral custody, who appointed the custodian, what the custodian may and may not do, how queues, records, keys, and public routes are preserved, and how the polity exits custody into a named next state**. Otherwise it still permits **settlement by possession**.
