# 674 — Shared-regime records and publicity: public/private sessions, document registers, meeting records, vote records, authentic texts, corrections, and no governance by missing papertrail

## One-line thesis

Shared regimes should publish a **typed documentary constitution** that separates session publicity, agenda and paper circulation, official document registers, meeting-record formats, vote records, authentic texts, correction notices, and retention or access rules — so public authority does not vanish into missing minutes, unofficial drafts, or document folklore.

## Why this matters

The archive now has notes on representation (`671`), decision rules (`672`), and officer-and-secretariat power (`673`).

But even a regime with sound entry rules, legitimate delegates, clear voting thresholds, and bounded officer powers can still become unaccountable if its documentary spine is weak.

Many regimes fail not because no decision was taken, but because no one can later answer:

- whether the meeting was public or private,
- which draft was actually before the body,
- whether the relevant text was circulated in time,
- what objections, abstentions, or reservations were recorded,
- which language version is authoritative,
- and what correction or rectification process applies if the text proves faulty.

Without a typed documentary constitution, the regime drifts into three familiar pathologies.

The first is **missing-papertrail governance**: real choices are made, but later reviewers can recover only fragments.

The second is **draft-to-law slippage**: working papers, chairs’ summaries, press releases, and secretariat compilations blur into one another until nobody can say which text is operative.

The third is **publicity theater**: meetings are labeled open, yet documents, records, and vote traces remain too incomplete or too late to support real scrutiny.

This note therefore adds one compact doctrine for **records and publicity discipline**.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Type public and private session status explicitly

Publicity is not an ambient vibe. It is a decision about who may observe what and when.

The archive should require regimes to say for every body and session type:

- whether meetings are presumptively public or private,
- what test authorizes closure,
- who can close a meeting,
- what parts of the record remain public after closure,
- and when decisions taken in private must be announced publicly.

### 2. Maintain one official document register and symbol system

A regime cannot have document discipline without a reliable way to identify what exists.

The archive should therefore prefer one official register that gives each agenda paper, draft decision, report, amendment, meeting record, and final act a stable identifier, publication date, version history, and status.

Without that, the paper trail becomes a scavenger hunt.

### 3. Bind circulation to minimum notice and version visibility

A body should not be asked to deliberate on moving paper without knowing when it was distributed and whether it changed.

The regime should publish:

- minimum circulation periods,
- late-paper rules,
- who may table revisions,
- how consolidated drafts are labeled,
- and how superseded versions remain traceable.

Members and the public should not have to infer whether a text arrived early enough to matter.

### 4. Distinguish record types rather than pretending one paper can do everything

Different record forms do different constitutional work.

The archive should prefer explicit separation among:

- verbatim or near-verbatim records where warranted,
- summary records,
- agenda and document lists,
- vote tallies and roll calls,
- formal decisions and resolutions,
- and background or explanatory papers.

A press release is not a meeting record. A meeting record is not an authentic text. An authentic text is not a searchable index.

### 5. Record objections, reservations, abstentions, and vote pathways visibly

A regime that publishes only the headline outcome hides the real constitutional story.

The documentary constitution should say whether it records:

- consensus without objection,
- objections or reservations,
- abstentions,
- recorded votes and roll calls where taken,
- and explanations of vote or position where the regime allows them.

The public record should show not only that a decision passed, but **how** it passed.

### 6. Separate authentic texts from translations, convenience copies, and explanatory summaries

Shared regimes often operate in several languages and through many summary surfaces.

The archive should insist that the regime say:

- which language versions are authentic,
- how official translations are designated,
- who certifies the final text,
- and what priority applies if a convenience summary differs from the adopted act.

This is especially important where a regime’s public communications are faster and more readable than its legal output.

### 7. Give correction and rectification a named lane

Texts can contain real errors. A serious regime needs a way to correct them without either pretending they do not matter or reopening the whole settlement by stealth.

The archive should therefore require a visible correction lane that says:

- who may propose a correction,
- how participants are notified,
- what objection window applies,
- what distinguishes typographic correction from substantive amendment,
- and how corrected texts, correction notices, or rectification instruments are linked to the original.

### 8. Preserve private-session traces even where publication is limited

Some matters may justify private session. That does not justify documentary amnesia.

The archive should prefer a rule under which even private meetings still generate:

- a date and attendance trace,
- an agenda trace,
- a custody-controlled internal record,
- and a public announcement of any formal decision once disclosure is lawful.

Closure should limit exposure, not erase existence.

### 9. Make the documentary spine searchable and durable

The official record should not depend on insiders knowing old folder names.

A serious regime should provide durable access to:

- agendas,
- official documents,
- meeting records,
- adopted acts,
- vote records where applicable,
- and correction notices.

The archive should especially prefer systems where records remain discoverable by symbol, body, session, agenda item, and date.

### 10. Publish one publicity-and-records map by body and document type

The archive should prefer one public map showing, for each body and document type:

- default publicity status,
- who controls publication,
- circulation minimums,
- what record form is produced,
- what counts as the authentic text,
- and how corrections and supersessions are displayed.

If users must reconstruct the official archive from press briefings, screenshots, and delegated memory, the documentary constitution has failed.

## Working matrix

| Documentary question | Honest rule | Why | What to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| open or closed meeting status | explicit default plus closure test | makes secrecy a visible exception | informal closure by habit or discomfort |
| agenda paper and draft circulation | stable identifiers, timestamps, and version trace | shows what was actually before the body | late-paper government by surprise |
| record of debate | verbatim or summary form by rule | preserves a reviewable trace of discussion | substituting press products for official records |
| record of outcome | formal decision text plus vote or objection trace where applicable | distinguishes debate from operative act | one-line result with no pathway shown |
| authentic multilingual text | named authentic versions plus certified translations | prevents language drift from changing law | treating convenience copies as definitive |
| corrections and rectifications | separate correction lane linked to original text | fixes real errors without silent rewrite | stealth amendment through “cleanup” |

## Guardrails

- Do not call a meeting public if the paper trail is inaccessible, unstable, or too thin to support review.
- Do not let working drafts, chair summaries, and adopted acts share the same documentary status.
- Do not publish outcomes without enough vote or objection trace to understand the route taken.
- Do not close meetings without a visible legal basis and post-session disclosure rule.
- Do not correct authentic texts through informal reposting alone.
- Do not allow official records to become undiscoverable by body, date, or document symbol.

## Failure modes

- **missing-papertrail governance**: decisions exist, but no recoverable official record connects discussion, text, and result.
- **draft slippage**: non-operative papers start functioning as if they were adopted law.
- **publicity theater**: meetings are nominally open but documentary access is too weak for real scrutiny.
- **language drift**: summaries or translations quietly outrank the authentic text in practice.
- **silent correction**: real text changes are made without a traceable rectification lane.
- **private-session erasure**: confidentiality is used as a pretext for deleting institutional memory.

## Practical tests

A regime passes this note when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Does it distinguish public and private sessions with an explicit closure test?
2. Does it maintain one official document register with stable identifiers and dates?
3. Are circulation minimums and version changes visible?
4. Are record types separated into debate records, vote records, and final acts?
5. Can a reader tell which text is authentic and which texts are translations or summaries?
6. Is there a named correction or rectification lane distinct from amendment?
7. Do even private meetings leave behind a controlled documentary trace?
8. Is there one public map of publicity and record rules by body and document type?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a shared regime can say **that a meeting happened and that a decision exists** but cannot say **what papers were before the body, what record was made, which text is authentic, how objections or votes were recorded, and how later corrections are traced**, it still has **institutional output without an honest documentary constitution**.
