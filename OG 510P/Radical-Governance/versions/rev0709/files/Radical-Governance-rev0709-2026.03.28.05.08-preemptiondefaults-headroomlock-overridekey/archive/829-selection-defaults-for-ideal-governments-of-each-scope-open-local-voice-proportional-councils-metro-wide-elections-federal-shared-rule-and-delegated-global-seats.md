# 829 — Selection defaults for ideal governments of each scope: open local voice, proportional councils, metro-wide elections, federal shared rule, and delegated global seats

## One-line thesis

The archive should answer the question **how should offices and chambers at each scope actually be chosen?** with scope-matched selection defaults rather than one ballot fetish: open assemblies, simple ward contests, recallable delegates, and rotation where the unit is genuinely face-to-face; proportional or semi-proportional municipal councils plus merit administration where everyday city government begins; whole-territory metropolitan elections where metro power is real; regional and national lower chambers chosen by proportional rules strong enough to reflect plural publics, with territorial second chambers only where shared rule is real; and continental or global bodies chosen mainly through treaty delegation, indirect parliamentary composition, and merit-based secretariat appointment rather than fake world-state plebiscites.

## Why this matters

The archive already has a stronger answer to **what each scope is for** (`790`), **which regime form usually fits it** (`825`), **from where authority should draw its mandate** (`826`), **how long that mandate should usually last** (`827`), **how it should ordinarily end** (`828`), **how representation should differ by tier** (`490`), **which electoral systems often fit which scale** (`566`), **how legislatures should be structured** (`548`), **when second chambers are actually necessary** (`753`), and **how non-elective offices should be filled** (`760`). What it still lacked was one compact answer-key note for the narrower selection question:

**once the archive knows what a scope is for, what form fits it, and from where authority should come, how should the people who occupy those roles actually be chosen?**

That missing layer matters because constitutional design often cheats on selection. Tiny local bodies are trapped inside party-list machinery better suited to national coalitions. Cities with deep social diversity are governed through winner-take-all municipal rules that misdescribe the electorate. Metropolitan authorities hold real power over transport, land use, or housing but are still composed through congresses of incumbents rather than a public metropolitan vote. Regional or federal chambers duplicate one another instead of dividing popular representation from territorial shared rule. Global bodies borrow the emotional language of democratic election while their real authority still rests on delegated treaty power and professional administration.

The archive should therefore add one doctrine of **selection fit by scope**. Selection should track the size and density of the public, the kind of authority being exercised, the need for durable plural representation, the difference between political and professional office, and the honesty of delegation when the governing body is not, in fact, a sovereign demos.

## Scope table

| Scope | Default selection lane | Why this fit is usually best | What it must not become |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Micro-local** | open assembly, simple low-cost contest, rotation, or recallable delegate | keeps steward authority intimate, legible, and reversible | imported party machinery for problems small enough to see directly |
| **Municipal** | proportional or semi-proportional council election; manager or chief administrator selected through council confidence plus merit discipline | reflects durable urban diversity while keeping daily administration professional | winner-take-all city rule or plebiscitary municipal cosplay |
| **Metropolitan** | direct whole-territory metro election for any body with real tax, land-use, housing, transport, or trunk-service power | gives the actual urban system a visible demos | real metro power governed only by incumbent brokerage |
| **Regional / provincial / state** | proportional or mixed lower chamber; territorial second key only where shared rule is real | preserves plural representation without erasing federal or regional bargains | duplicated chambers or ornamental regional presidentialism |
| **National** | lower chamber proportional enough to reflect the country; government then formed through confidence or constitutionally matched executive rules | aligns electoral translation with governable responsibility | false majorities, dual-command collision, or decorative bicameral duplication |
| **Continental / plurilateral / global** | delegated member-state seats, bounded parliamentary complements where treaties create them, and merit appointment for secretariat/referee offices | matches delegated authority to delegated selection | pretend world-state democracy without a real global demos |

## Pattern pack

### 1. Separate popular election, representative-body choice, professional appointment, and treaty delegation as different constitutional lanes

The archive should not let every selection question collapse into the phrase **“who gets elected?”** Ideal governments should distinguish at least four selection lanes:

- **direct popular choice** for assemblies or executives that honestly claim a public democratic mandate;
- **representative-body choice** where executives are responsible to an elected chamber or where tiny bodies choose stewards from within the group;
- **professional appointment** for administrative and referee offices that should not turn on partisan campaigning;
- and **treaty or member delegation** where the body is exercising shared-rule authority on behalf of constituent governments rather than claiming a single undivided demos.

Those lanes solve different legitimacy problems and should not be smuggled into one another. A constitution that treats them as interchangeable will eventually over-elect what should be professional, under-elect what should be public, or disguise delegation as popular sovereignty.

### 2. Keep micro-local selection open, cheap, and reversible

At neighborhood, village, block, ward, or other face-to-face scale, the archive should usually prefer selection routes that keep entry cheap and correction easy.

That usually means some mix of:

- open assemblies or neighborhood meetings;
- simple ward or block contests with very low nomination barriers;
- rotation or short steward terms for convenors and committee leads;
- recallable delegates where a larger body must be formed from smaller units;
- and only as much party or ballot complexity as the locality truly needs.

The archive should therefore distrust any micro-local design that requires expensive campaigning, elaborate list engineering, or quasi-national party mediation just to choose people who mostly witness problems, steward shared space, escalate needs, and manage tiny common resources.

### 3. Make municipalities the first serious electoral scale, and keep the operating state out of campaign logic

Municipalities are usually the first scale where a broad everyday portfolio becomes honest: land-use administration, streets, sanitation, permits, parks, records, inspections, and many user-facing services. Their councils therefore need to represent real city diversity rather than simply manufacture a fake civic unanimity.

The archive should usually prefer **proportional or semi-proportional council elections** in medium and large municipalities — open-list PR, flexible lists, STV, or mixed forms that are honest about plural publics — while allowing simpler arrangements in genuinely small towns where the social field is still small and visible.

At the same time, the archive should keep the operational administrative state out of the campaign lane. Municipal managers, chief administrative officers, finance directors, engineers, records leads, and other durable operators should be chosen through merit and council-accountable appointment routes, not by direct popular election.

### 4. If a metropolitan body can bind the region, it should be chosen by the region

A body that can tax, issue metropolitan plans, allocate housing obligations, run trunk transport, or govern shared utilities should not be composed only by bargaining among mayors, governors, or ministers. That may be acceptable for a light co-ordination forum, but not for a government with real urban-system power.

The archive should therefore prefer **direct whole-metro elections** for metropolitan assemblies or councils once the threshold dossier shows real shared power is needed. Electoral design should let the whole functional territory appear in the selection lane while avoiding simple capture by the core city or veto confederacy by every edge municipality. Proportional or mixed systems will often be the honest default because metropolitan politics usually combines territorial diversity with region-wide coalition formation.

### 5. Regional and national selection should distinguish population representation from territorial shared rule

At regional and national scale, the archive should usually start from a clear division of labour:

- the **main lower chamber** should be elected by rules proportional enough to reflect the actual plurality of the people;
- **government formation** should then follow the regime form — usually assembly-confidence at regional scale and often parliamentary confidence at national scale;
- and a **territorial second chamber** should exist only where a federal, devolved, or asymmetric constitutional bargain genuinely requires shared rule by constituent units.

This matters because many systems confuse three different goals: representing people, representing territories, and forming a responsible government. The archive should not accept duplicated bicameralism where both chambers are chosen on nearly the same logic, nor should it accept heavy malapportionment in the name of territorial balance unless the constitutional bargain truly requires it.

### 6. Keep directly elected presidencies and mayors as exceptions that must prove systemic fit

Direct election is not the same as democratic honesty. It is only one selection lane, and it becomes dangerous when paired with a legislature or party system that cannot govern alongside it.

The archive should therefore treat directly elected presidents, governors, or strong mayors as **exceptional selection choices that must prove executive-legislative fit**. If the office is supposed to govern independently, the constitution must also show how budgets pass, deadlock is repaired, appointments are made, and removal occurs without turning constant collision into the main operating system.

Where that proof is weak, the archive should prefer chamber-centered government and treat direct election as ceremonial, bounded, or unnecessary.

### 7. Across all scopes, professional and referee offices should usually be chosen by appointment class, not by ballot class

This note is about selection defaults by scope, but one cross-scope principle must stay explicit: not every public office should be chosen by voters.

Across municipalities, metros, regions, nations, and upper-tier bodies, the archive should usually prefer:

- **merit appointment** for durable administrative and service-delivery offices;
- **mixed or widened-coalition appointment** for auditors, judges, election referees, ombuds, and other independence-bearing roles;
- **political appointment** only for a named and bounded strategic layer that honestly rises and falls with the government;
- and **anti-vacancy discipline** so acting service does not become a hidden appointment constitution.

That keeps the answer-key note compatible with `760` rather than pretending every problem is solved by another election.

### 8. Continental, plurilateral, and global bodies should usually be selected through delegation plus bounded complements, not counterfeit mass democracy

Where authority rests on treaties, charters, or pooled delegation among states, the selection lane should say so openly.

The archive should therefore usually prefer:

- member-state delegation or appointment for intergovernmental councils;
- parliamentary complements only where treaties create them and define their role;
- merit-based selection for secretariat, monitoring, and referee offices;
- and clear public visibility about who is representing a government, who is representing a population, and who is serving as a professional officer.

The archive should resist the temptation to treat every upper-tier body as incomplete unless it imitates a nation-state election. A global narrow-waist order is honest precisely because it does **not** pretend there is already one thick global demos for all purposes.

## Diagnostic questions

1. Is the selection lane honest about whether the office represents a face-to-face community, a citywide public, a metropolitan territory, a federal bargain, or a delegated treaty order?
2. Does the main electoral rule at that scale reflect durable pluralism without manufacturing either false unanimity or permanent deadlock?
3. Are administrative and referee offices kept out of the campaign lane unless there is a positive reason to elect them?
4. If there is a second chamber or directly elected executive, can the archive name the distinct constitutional work that selection lane performs?
5. Would ordinary residents be able to tell who they directly choose, who is chosen by an elected chamber, who is appointed professionally, and who is delegated by another government?
6. Does the selection design preserve lower-tier self-government and higher-tier shared rule instead of flattening every scale into the same ballot ritual?

## Guardrails

- Do not treat direct election as a default proof of democratic seriousness.
- Do not import national party machinery into genuinely micro-local steward institutions without a real need.
- Do not let diverse cities be governed through durable winner-take-all rules merely because they are familiar.
- Do not tolerate metropolitan bodies with real binding powers that are selected only by incumbent brokerage.
- Do not create second chambers without a real shared-rule or differentiated-review vocation.
- Do not elect what should be appointed professionally, or appoint what should carry a public democratic mandate.
- Do not let treaty bodies counterfeit a global demos they do not actually possess.

## Failure modes

- **ballot fetish**: every office is forced into election whether or not election is the honest lane.
- **micro-local overengineering**: tiny steward bodies inherit party complexity better suited to national politics.
- **municipal minority rule**: winner-take-all rules let a thin plurality govern a diverse city as if it were the whole public.
- **metro proxy rule**: metropolitan power is exercised by a congress of incumbents instead of by a visible metropolitan electorate.
- **territorial blur**: upper chambers duplicate lower chambers rather than protecting real shared rule.
- **dual-mandate collision**: directly elected executives and fragmented legislatures are combined without a workable repair grammar.
- **delegation denial**: upper-tier bodies pretend to mass democratic authority while actually operating through delegated state power.

## Compression rule for the archive

If the archive can name **what authority exists at a scope** but cannot also name **who chooses it, by what lane, for which public, and why that lane fits that scope better than the obvious alternatives**, then the design still has **authority without an honest selection constitution**.
