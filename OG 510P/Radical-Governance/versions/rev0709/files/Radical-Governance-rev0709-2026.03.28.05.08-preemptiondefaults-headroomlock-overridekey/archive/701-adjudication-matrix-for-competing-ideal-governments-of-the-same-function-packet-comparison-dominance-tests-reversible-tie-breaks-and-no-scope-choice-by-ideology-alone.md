# 701 — Adjudication matrix for competing ideal governments of the same function: packet comparison, dominance tests, reversible tie-breaks, and no scope choice by ideology alone

## One-line thesis

When the archive faces several plausible claims about which scope should govern the same function, it should compare the rival **design packets** through one compact adjudication matrix — vocation fit, boundary honesty, minimum kit completeness, fiscal and capability proof, spillover handling, solidarity and rights-floor fit, democratic proportionality, reversibility, and machinery cost — so the archive can choose or bracket the better packet without collapsing into ideology, naming habit, or essay-length restatement.

## Why this matters

`699` gives the archive one reusable scope spine. `700` gives it one reusable packet for any later claim about ideal government by scope.

The next compression problem is predictable.

Once the archive starts using packet discipline seriously, it will often have **more than one plausible packet for the same function**. Housing may plausibly sit municipally with metropolitan coordination, or metropolitan by default with municipal implementation. Flood governance may plausibly sit regionally, or nationally with regional operating arms, depending on basin shape and solidarity claims. Public-health surveillance may plausibly live regionally, nationally, or globally depending on whether the problem is ordinary service intelligence, guarantor-state capacity, or treaty-bound alerting.

Without one compact comparison rule, the archive risks three forms of drift.

First, it risks **ideological adjudication**: local-first by romance, national-first by comfort, global-first by aspiration, or regional-first by map nostalgia.

Second, it risks **packet inflation**: each competing claim re-expands the whole constitutional ladder instead of showing the decisive differences between the rival options.

Third, it risks **false certainty**: one packet wins by style, prestige, or familiar labels even though the real choice is close and should remain reviewable.

What the archive therefore needs is not another scope theory. It needs one small **adjudication matrix** for comparing rival packets in a disciplined, mergeable way.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Compare packets, not labels or slogans

When two or more scope claims compete, the archive should compare the rival **packets** from `700`, not the public labels alone and not the political slogans attached to them.

The live objects under comparison should therefore be packet-length claims such as:

- municipal with metropolitan coordination,
- metropolitan with direct metro forum and municipal operating arms,
- regional with basin-defined boundaries,
- national guarantor ownership with delegated local delivery,
- or global treaty narrow waist with domestic execution.

If a proposal cannot be stated as a packet, it is not yet ready to compete.

### 2. Eliminate packetless or dishonest candidates before scoring the rest

A packet should be dropped before detailed comparison if it fails any threshold honesty test already stabilised in `628`, `629`, `660`, or `700`.

Immediate disqualifiers include:

- no named archetype,
- no intelligible boundary logic,
- no minimum complete kit,
- no fiscal or capability proof,
- hidden reliance on a collapsed-stack or shared-regime exception without naming it,
- or obvious dependence on another tier’s staff, money, records, or legitimacy while pretending to be self-standing.

The archive should not adjudicate among packets that are not yet institutionally honest enough to count.

### 3. Start with vocation fit rather than political taste

The first comparative question is the simplest: **which packet best matches the class of problem?**

The archive should ask whether the function is mainly:

- ordinary place government,
- a shared urban system,
- a territorial platform,
- a guarantor-state duty,
- a bounded cross-border compact problem,
- or a genuinely planetary coordination problem.

A packet with the wrong vocation can still be politically attractive, but it should begin behind.

### 4. Compare boundary honesty, not just nominal scale

The second question is whether the proposed unit matches the real geography, membership rule, or problem field.

The archive should favour packets whose boundaries track the actual labour market, watershed, corridor, service catchment, equal-citizenship community, treaty membership set, or exposure field that the function must govern. It should penalise packets that inherit a map mainly because it already exists.

A familiar county, province, ministry district, or international club should not beat a better packet merely by being present on the map already.

### 5. Prefer the smallest packet that remains complete for the problem

Where several packets are plausible, the archive should usually prefer the **smallest scale that can still field a complete and honest kit**.

That means the lower or narrower packet should win only if it can still show:

- a real decision forum,
- an executive and operating spine equal to the task,
- predictable finance,
- review and redress,
- and enough professional density to avoid shell government.

This is not local romanticism. It is a burden-of-proof rule against unnecessary upscaling.

### 6. Penalise spillover blindness and unpriced co-ordination dependence

A lower-tier packet should lose ground when it relies on constant bargaining, informal club governance, or repetitive rescue by adjacent governments in order to handle spillovers it cannot honestly contain.

A higher-tier packet should likewise lose ground when it imposes a unified scale that erases meaningful local variation or operational intimacy without proving that the spillovers are genuinely large enough to justify the loss.

The archive should therefore compare not only **what each packet governs directly**, but also **what co-ordination debt each packet creates elsewhere**.

### 7. Test solidarity and rights-floor fit separately from service convenience

Some functions are not merely technical or operational. They concern equal citizenship, redistribution, portability of rights, macro-risk pooling, or constitutional guarantees that cannot be allowed to vary too far by postcode or province.

In those cases, the archive should ask whether a packet can honestly uphold:

- equal treatment floors,
- social-insurance solidarity,
- rights portability,
- due-process minimums,
- anti-discrimination duties,
- or other guarantor-state obligations.

A municipally or regionally convenient packet should not win if it cannot carry the solidarity or rights-floor burden the function actually requires.

### 8. Test democratic proportionality against the real power exercised

A packet should also be compared on whether its decision forum, scrutiny routes, and participation channels are proportionate to the power it would exercise.

If one packet requires real distributive, borrowing, rulemaking, or territorial-shaping power but still depends on opaque summitry, delegated proxies, or borrowed scrutiny, that packet should lose ground unless it includes a visible constitutional upgrade path.

The archive should not treat operational convenience as a reason to under-democratise a real governing scale.

### 9. Score reversibility and path dependence openly

When two packets appear close on first-order fit, the archive should compare their **reversibility**.

A packet deserves caution when adoption would create high path dependence through:

- expensive territorial surgery,
- irreversible platform migration,
- debt structures that are hard to unwind,
- treaty commitments with deep domestic rewiring,
- or administrative fusion that would later make handback politically or technically impossible.

In close cases, the archive should usually prefer the packet that leaves a cleaner review and handback lane under `631` and `632`.

### 10. Count machinery cost as a real cost, but not as a trump card

The archive should count institutional machinery cost honestly.

Every extra tier, forum, agency, connector, and fiscal lane adds friction, staffing demand, and public legibility costs. That matters.

But machinery cost should be treated as **one comparative cost among others**, not as an automatic reason to centralise or collapse. A simpler packet is not better if its simplicity is purchased by spillover denial, democratic mismatch, or guarantor failure.

### 11. Use three outcomes rather than forcing false finality

The adjudication matrix should permit three archive outcomes.

- **Preferred packet**: one packet clearly dominates the others on the decisive criteria.
- **Conditional packet**: one packet is currently preferred but only with named upgrade, review, or reversibility triggers.
- **Bracketed rivalry**: the archive keeps two packets live because the case is genuinely close, path dependent, or jurisdiction-specific, and it names what future evidence would settle the choice.

This keeps the archive from pretending that every scope question already has one eternal answer.

## Adjudication matrix

| Criterion | What the archive should ask | Typical winner when the answer is strong | Typical loser when the answer is weak |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Vocation fit** | does the packet match the real class of problem: ordinary place, shared system, territorial platform, guarantor duty, cross-border compact, or planetary problem? | the packet whose archetype fits the problem class cleanly | the packet chosen mainly by ideology or naming habit |
| **Boundary honesty** | do the boundaries track the real labour market, basin, corridor, demos, treaty membership, or exposure field? | the packet whose unit matches the real problem field | the packet that inherits a map for convenience alone |
| **Minimum complete kit** | can the packet name a real forum, executive spine, fiscal base, review lane, and participation channel? | the packet that is institutionally complete at its claimed scale | the packet that depends on borrowed machinery while pretending to govern |
| **Fiscal and capability proof** | can the packet actually finance and staff the mandate? | the packet with predictable money and enough professional density | the packet that survives only on discretionary rescue or shell staffing |
| **Spillover handling** | can the packet contain the relevant externalities without chronic side-bargaining? | the packet that internalises the main spillovers honestly | the packet that offloads co-ordination debt onto everyone else |
| **Solidarity and rights-floor fit** | can the packet uphold equal treatment, portability, redistribution, and guarantor duties where needed? | the packet that can protect the relevant floors without fiction | the packet that leaves rights or solidarity to postcode luck |
| **Democratic proportionality** | are legitimacy, scrutiny, and participation proportional to the power exercised? | the packet with a forum and review lanes equal to its real power | the packet that governs by proxy, summit, or technocratic shell |
| **Reversibility** | if the packet proves wrong, can the polity review, upgrade, split, or hand back without constitutional wreckage? | the packet with cleaner review and handback lanes | the packet that locks in through hard-to-undo fusion or treaty rewiring |
| **Machinery cost** | does the packet add machinery that is justified by real gains rather than theory clutter? | the packet whose extra machinery earns its keep | the packet that adds tiers or connectors without decisional payoff |

## Tie-break rules

When two packets remain close after matrix comparison, the archive should apply tie-break rules in this order.

1. Prefer the packet that preserves the lower tier’s role **without leaving the upper-tier spillover unsolved**.
2. Prefer the packet with the cleaner fiscal and capability proof.
3. Prefer the packet with the more proportionate democratic chain for the real power exercised.
4. Prefer the packet with the easier review, upgrade, split, or handback path.
5. If still close, keep a **bracketed rivalry** rather than forcing false closure.

## Packet comparison grammar

A compact comparison object should usually be readable in a few lines:

| Candidate | Why it might win | Why it might lose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Municipal with metropolitan co-ordination** | preserves place intimacy and local accountability while using metro connectors only where spillovers are narrow | loses if co-ordination debt becomes chronic, distributive, or system-wide |
| **Metropolitan default with municipal operating arms** | internalises urban spillovers and makes shared regional tradeoffs visible | loses if it centralises ordinary place work or lacks legitimacy equal to its power |
| **National guarantor ownership with local delivery** | secures equal floors and solidarity while keeping delivery close to residents | loses if the center crowds out local discretion for functions that are mostly ordinary place |

The archive should treat this as the normal length for rivalry comparison unless the case is unusually hard.

## Guardrails

- Do not compare packets that have not yet met the archive’s honesty threshold.
- Do not let a familiar public label substitute for vocation or boundary fit.
- Do not award victory to the packet that merely sounds most principled.
- Do not treat low machinery cost as a free good when it really means hidden co-ordination debt.
- Do not force one winner where a bracketed rivalry with named review triggers is more honest.
- Do not let democratic underbuild hide behind technical competence or emergency rhetoric.

## Failure modes

- **scope choice by ideology alone** — the preferred packet mainly reflects localist, nationalist, regionalist, or cosmopolitan identity rather than problem fit.
- **map inheritance bias** — an inherited county, province, ministry district, or treaty club wins because it already exists.
- **cheap-localism** — a lower-tier packet is praised for intimacy while its spillovers are socialised upward.
- **cheap-centralism** — a higher-tier packet is praised for coherence while it strips away place-sensitive discretion it does not need.
- **shell comparison** — rival packets are compared without naming their forums, fiscal bases, or review routes.
- **false finality** — a close case is declared settled when the archive should keep two packets live pending better evidence.

## Practical tests

A comparison note is using the adjudication matrix honestly when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Are the rival objects packet-length claims rather than bare labels?
2. Has each packet passed threshold honesty before comparison begins?
3. Is the decisive difference about vocation, boundaries, kit completeness, solidarity, legitimacy, reversibility, or machinery cost rather than about rhetorical taste?
4. Could an outsider see why one packet wins or why the rivalry remains open?
5. If the case is close, has the archive named what future evidence or constitutional change would settle it?

## Compression rule for the archive

If a later note contains **two or more plausible scope packets for the same function** but cannot say **which criteria decide between them, which packet presently dominates, or why the rivalry should remain bracketed and reviewable**, then it still has **scope options without an archive-grade adjudication rule**.
