# 810 — Threshold dossiers for ideal governments of each scope: metro triggers, county pooling, regional-platform proof, fiscal floors, legitimacy thickness, and no tier thickening by anecdote

## One-line thesis

When the archive reaches a **live threshold question** rather than a settled scope doctrine question, it should require one compact **threshold dossier**: a declared threshold claim, the right territory and comparator cohort, a small set of named evidence lanes, visible contradiction residue, and a provisional confidence statement, so judgments such as **when metro democracy is now required, when sparse territories need pooled county-scale service backbones, when a region is a real platform, or when a lower tier is fiscally non-hollow** are made by typed evidence rather than by population fetish, prestige case imitation, or crisis anecdote.

## Why this matters

`790` gives the archive its answer key for what ideal governments at each scope are. `791` gives benchmark cards for scoring a real arrangement against that answer. `798` says how to choose an honest comparator cohort. `800` through `808` give the archive a serious assessment method, evidence spine, mission-integrity rules, and calibration discipline. `809` now makes a crucial distinction: some parts of the scope answer are settled doctrine, while others remain **live threshold questions**.

What the archive still lacked was one compact object for a more specific task:

**how should a frontier threshold claim actually be assembled before the archive says that a metro tier is now necessary, that county-style pooling is now the honest answer, that a region has earned thick territorial-platform status, that fiscal autonomy is still hollow, or that a legitimacy upgrade is now required?**

That gap matters because threshold debates routinely collapse into bad substitutes for evidence.

One substitute is **population fetish**: a round number becomes a constitutional trigger even when the real system footprint, service catchments, and fiscal conditions point elsewhere.

Another substitute is **prestige borrowing**: one admired capital city, federation, or metro is treated as a universal proof object for unlike places.

A third substitute is **crisis ratchet**: an emergency coordination workaround is treated as if it automatically proved the permanent need for thicker upper-tier government.

A fourth substitute is **dataset monoculture**: one commuting measure, one revenue ratio, or one map is treated as if it settled the whole question.

The archive should be stricter than that. The point of `809` is not merely to say that some thresholds are still open. It is to force the archive to handle them in a way that is empirical without becoming pseudo-scientific. A threshold dossier is the missing bridge between **live frontier** and **usable constitutional judgment**.

Official guidance supports that move. The European Charter of Local Self-Government says public responsibilities should generally be exercised by the authorities closest to citizens, while allocation upward should weigh the nature and scale of the task; it also requires local financial resources to be commensurate with assigned responsibilities and protects equalization without erasing local discretion. OECD work on functional urban areas defines metropolitan reality through economic connection and commuting rather than inherited municipal borders. OECD work on multi-level public investment repeatedly stresses coordination and delivery at the relevant geographical scale, especially where fragmentation harms shared systems. OECD work on regional governance likewise shows that regional arrangements vary with asymmetry, capacity, identity, and metropolitan specificity rather than following one rigid ladder. World Bank metropolitan-governance work similarly treats metropolitan institutional choice as a response to functional economic areas and labor markets, not merely to ceremonial map categories. The archive should compress those lessons into a method rule: **do not use one universal trigger number; require a typed threshold dossier matched to the problem class.**

## Pattern pack

### 1. Use a threshold dossier only when the open question is genuinely threshold-shaped

A threshold dossier is not needed every time the archive talks about scope.

If the question is structural — for example, whether municipalities are the first full governments of ordinary life, or whether global institutions should remain narrow-waist treaty architectures — the archive should use settled doctrine.

A threshold dossier is for a narrower class of question:

- whether an already-recognised functional city now requires metro democracy;
- whether sparse settlements now require county-style pooling or intermunicipal backbone capacity;
- whether a region has become a real territorial platform rather than decorative duplication;
- whether a lower tier is fiscally substantial enough to count as more than a grant shell;
- whether a pooled authority has become consequential enough to need thicker democratic scrutiny;
- or whether failure or overload has crossed from repairable underbuild into honest rescoping.

The dossier exists to discipline these edge questions, not to reopen the whole constitutional stack from zero.

### 2. State the threshold claim in decision form, not essay form

Every threshold dossier should begin with a short decision sentence.

Examples:

- **Does this urban region now require a metropolitan government with direct democratic legitimacy?**
- **Does this sparse territory now require pooled county-style service operation rather than stand-alone municipal provision?**
- **Does this regional tier now count as a real territorial platform requiring a thicker constitutional kit?**
- **Does this municipality still fail the archive’s fiscal-floor test?**
- **Has this indirect joint board become consequential enough to require a public chamber, stronger borrowing scrutiny, or clearer review routes?**

The point is to prevent dossiers from becoming scenic literature. A dossier should answer one threshold claim at a time.

### 3. Lock the territorial unit and comparator cohort before reading the evidence

A threshold cannot be judged honestly if the territory keeps changing during the argument.

The archive should therefore require the dossier to declare:

- the territorial unit under study,
- the functional territory that may differ from the legal boundary,
- the morphology class,
- and the comparator cohort.

For metropolitan threshold questions, that will often mean a functional urban area, commuting zone, labor-market area, or equivalent city-region footprint rather than the legacy municipal shell. For county-pooling questions, it may mean catchments for emergency response, hospitals, schools, utilities, roads, or revenue administration rather than one nostalgic county line. For regional-platform questions, it may mean the footprint of real grid, basin, corridor, tertiary-care, university, disaster-logistics, or constitutional shared-rule systems.

The archive should use `798` before pretending a threshold claim is comparable across unlike territories.

### 4. Use a small fixed set of evidence lanes

A threshold dossier should not become a shopping cart of whatever metrics happen to be available. It should work through a small fixed set of lanes and explain which of them are decisive in the case at hand.

A strong default set is:

1. **Functional-integration lane** — commuting, labor-market connection, travel times, daily movement, freight or service interdependence, cross-boundary use of core systems.
2. **Service-topology lane** — whether the relevant service or infrastructure naturally operates on a footprint larger or smaller than the current jurisdiction.
3. **Fiscal-capability lane** — own-source room, transfer predictability, equalization fit, reserve capacity, borrowing room where relevant, and whether the current mandate is fiscally non-hollow.
4. **Legitimacy-thickness lane** — whether the power already being exercised is now consequential enough that indirect composition, ad hoc boards, or intergovernmental summits have become democratically too thin.
5. **Rights-and-coercion lane** — whether the scale question materially affects equal-rights protection, due process, or exposure to coercive power.
6. **Failure-and-continuity lane** — repeated breakdowns, handoff failures, delivery fragmentation, emergency command confusion, or chronic dependency on informal workarounds.

Not every lane will matter equally in every dossier. But every dossier should explain which lanes were checked and which ones actually drove the conclusion.

### 5. Permit multiple indicators inside each lane and refuse one-metric monopoly

The archive should explicitly reject one-metric thresholding.

A metropolitan trigger should not ride on population alone. A fiscal-floor claim should not ride on one revenue ratio alone. A regional-platform claim should not ride on map size alone.

Inside each lane, the dossier may use several indicators or forms of proof: official statistics, network maps, audited budgets, service coverage maps, route traces, waiting-time distributions, capital plans, equalization formulas, or repeated incident logs. The rule is not that more indicators are always better. The rule is that **the dossier should not pretend that one convenient metric exhausts the constitutional question.**

### 6. Keep boundary cases and negative cases in view

Threshold claims become unreliable when the archive sees only obvious success stories.

A good dossier should therefore include:

- one or more **boundary cases** where the threshold appears close but unresolved,
- one or more **negative cases** where the threshold has not yet been crossed,
- and where useful, one **anti-pattern case** where a tier was thickened without enough underlying proof.

This matters because thresholds are easiest to distort at the margin. Boundary cases show whether the archive is really distinguishing crossed from not-yet-crossed conditions, rather than merely narrating an intuition.

### 7. Separate threshold-crossing proof from institutional-design choice

Crossing a threshold does not automatically settle the exact institutional model.

A dossier may show that metropolitan governance is now required without settling whether the best response is a two-tier metro, a consolidated city-region, or a stronger special-purpose metro with upgraded democratic controls. It may show that sparse territories need pooled backbone capacity without settling whether that should take county form, a mandated consortium, or another bounded platform. It may show that a region is real enough to exist without settling every chamber, electoral, or executive design choice.

The archive should therefore separate two questions:

1. **has the threshold been crossed?**
2. **what exact constitutional design best answers that crossing?**

The first is dossier work. The second belongs to `812`, benchmark, packet, and rivalry work elsewhere in the archive.

### 8. Express provisional cut-points as bands and confidence classes

A serious archive should still be able to say more than “it depends.”

But where thresholds remain frontier work, the dossier should prefer **bands and confidence classes** over fake universal precision. For example, it may conclude that:

- the threshold is **clearly crossed**,
- **probably crossed but model choice remains open**,
- **borderline / frontier**,
- **probably not crossed**,
- or **clearly not crossed**.

It should then add a confidence statement such as **high**, **moderate**, or **provisional** confidence and name the principal uncertainty. This keeps the archive decisive without pretending to have discovered timeless constitutional constants.

### 9. Record contradiction residue and what would change the conclusion

A threshold dossier should always say what evidence cuts the other way.

Examples:

- a commuting pattern suggests metro integration, but hospital and school catchments remain mostly local;
- a region carries real corridor and grid functions, but fiscal autonomy remains weak and indirect legitimacy still thin;
- a municipality has strong own-source revenue, but its reserve position and borrowing access are too fragile for the mandate it carries;
- repeated coordination failures point upward, but the failures may still be repairable through a cleaner concurrency settlement rather than a thicker tier.

The dossier should then state what additional evidence would most likely change the conclusion. This prevents the archive from treating a provisional threshold judgment as if it were already beyond revision.

### 10. Pair the dossier with an explicit review clock

Threshold claims should not become permanent merely because they were once made under uncertainty.

A dossier should therefore name:

- when it should be refreshed,
- which signals jump the queue for early refresh,
- and which downstream objects depend on it.

A metropolitan threshold dossier may need refresh after new transport integration, major municipal mergers, or repeated flood-command failures. A fiscal-floor dossier may need refresh after a transfer reform, equalization change, debt shock, or new own-source powers. A regional-platform dossier may need refresh after new devolution, corridor build-out, grid integration, or formal constitutional bargaining.

### 11. Require a compact outward summary when the dossier is used for live reform

If a threshold dossier is used in a real reform process, the archive should prefer a short outward-facing summary rather than a purely internal technical memo.

That summary should say:

- the threshold question,
- the territory studied,
- the comparator cohort,
- the decisive evidence lanes,
- the confidence class,
- the principal contradiction residue,
- and the next design step.

This does not mean publishing every raw artifact. It means the public should be able to see why a scope-thickening, pooling, rescoping, or fiscal-floor claim is being made at all.

### 12. Use one minimum dossier table before accepting a frontier threshold claim

| Dossier component | What it must name | Common failure if absent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Decision question** | one threshold claim in yes / no / borderline form | essay drift |
| **Territory** | legal unit plus functional unit where different | boundary gaming |
| **Comparator cohort** | matched peers and exclusion logic | benchmark by postcard |
| **Evidence lanes** | which lanes were checked and which were decisive | metric shopping |
| **Contradiction residue** | strongest evidence cutting the other way | confidence theater |
| **Confidence class** | clearly crossed / probably crossed / borderline / probably not / clearly not, plus confidence | fake precision |
| **Review clock** | refresh trigger and expiry or revisit date | immortal provisional judgment |
| **Downstream consequence** | what design or review step this dossier unlocks, if any | threshold talk with no governance effect |

## Guardrails

- Do not use one population number as a constitutional trigger by itself.
- Do not let a famous capital, metro, region, or federation substitute for a declared comparator cohort.
- Do not treat emergency co-ordination during crisis as automatic proof of permanent upper-tier thickening.
- Do not confuse proving that a threshold is crossed with proving one exact institutional design.
- Do not let one available dataset monopolise the dossier if the underlying constitutional question is multi-lane.
- Do not let a fiscal equalization discussion silently become a full argument for recentralization.

## Failure modes

- **population fetish** — round numbers pretend to settle a multi-lane constitutional judgment.
- **commuting monoculture** — travel-to-work data is treated as the whole metro question even where utilities, risk, freight, or service catchments point differently.
- **decorative regionism by map size** — a large territory is treated as a real region even though the platform work is thin.
- **fiscal shell normalization** — hollow lower tiers are treated as autonomous because transfers continue to arrive, even though meaningful discretion is absent.
- **crisis ratchet** — temporary command improvisation hardens into permanent scope inflation without a proper dossier.
- **glamour borrowing** — admired cases are copied across unlike morphologies and capacities.
- **threshold theater by repetition** — a provisional cut-point starts looking settled only because several authors reused it.

## Practical tests

A threshold dossier is honest when it can answer yes to all of the following:

1. Has it stated one threshold question rather than several mixed together?
2. Has it fixed both the legal territory and the functional territory being studied?
3. Has it named the comparator cohort and why tempting prestige cases were excluded?
4. Has it checked a small fixed set of evidence lanes rather than shopping opportunistically for one convenient statistic?
5. Has it kept boundary or negative cases in view?
6. Has it separated threshold-crossing proof from exact institutional-model choice?
7. Has it recorded contradiction residue and the main evidence that would change the conclusion?
8. Has it expressed the answer as a band and confidence class rather than a false universal cutoff?
9. Has it attached a review clock and said what downstream design or assessment object depends on it?

## Compression rule for the archive

If the archive can say **that a threshold has been crossed** but cannot also show **which territory was studied, which peers counted, which evidence lanes were decisive, what evidence cut the other way, and how confident the judgment really is**, then it is still trying to thicken or rescope government **by anecdote, slogan, or map prestige rather than by a serious threshold dossier**.
