# 726 — Default function routing table for ideal governments: everyday place, shared systems, guarantor duties, compact unions, and treaty narrow waists

## One-line thesis

The archive should keep one compact **default routing table** for common public functions: route witness, stewardship, and micro-commons to micro-local units; ordinary daily place government to municipalities; shared urban systems to metropolitan government or narrow utilities; basin, hospital, university, corridor, and ecological platforms to regions or typed functional authorities; equal citizenship, redistribution, macro-stability, and constitutional settlement to nations; bounded cross-border markets, mobility, and corridors to compact unions; and genuinely planetary peace, alerting, interoperability, and commons baselines to treaty-bound global narrow waists — while forcing mixed fields to declare lead scope, support scopes, fiscal logic, and upgrade tests instead of choosing a level from scratch every time.

## Why this matters

The archive now has a serious constitutional grammar for ideal government by scope: a **scope spine** (`699`), a **minimum design packet** (`700`), a way to compare rival packets (`701`), a way to split mixed fields before scope choice (`702`), interface and control-plane packets for distributed stacks (`703`-`704`), and a stronger **mixed-scope constitution** (`725`) that says more clearly when a polity needs full territorial government, bounded functional authority, or compact-and-treaty machinery.

What it still lacked was one compact surface that later notes can cite when they need to answer a more practical question:

**for the common functions of government, what is the default lead scope before exceptions, lane-splitting, and local constitutional detail?**

That missing surface matters because multilevel argument often becomes expensive in a predictable way. Every new field starts by reopening the full theory of municipality, metro, region, nation, compact union, and global regime even when the answer is mostly ordinary. The result is not only duplication. It is drift. One note quietly routes a function to the city, another to the region, another to a special authority, and each repeats just enough constitutional language to sound plausible.

The archive should therefore keep one **starter routing table**. It is not a substitute for judgment. It is the default object that says where ordinary presumption lies, what support scopes usually remain in play, and when the analyst must escalate into exception ladders, field splitting, or democratic upgrade tests.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Treat routing as a default presumption, not as automatic truth

A routing table should shorten argument, not end it. It names the **default lead scope** for a function family and the usual support scopes that continue to matter. It does not abolish the archive’s later tests about spillovers, rights floors, equalization, capability congruence, asymmetry, or collapsed-stack exceptions.

The point is to stop reopening first principles where the default answer is already strong enough to begin from.

### 2. Distinguish four questions every time: lead, support, finance, and review

A function is not fully routed just because the archive has named one lead tier. Later notes should still be able to answer four distinct questions:

1. who leads the ordinary function;
2. which other scopes support, constrain, or co-produce it;
3. where the durable fiscal base sits;
4. and what review or backstop lane corrects failure, capture, or drift.

This matters because many fields are constitutional hybrids. Local government may lead daily execution while national government funds rights floors. A metropolitan authority may lead transport planning while municipalities still own streets and the nation co-funds capital. A global body may set reporting or technical baselines while implementation remains national or regional.

### 3. Keep everyday place functions close to the municipality unless spillovers plainly outrun it

Ordinary place government should still default to the municipal archetype. That includes most local streets, parks, libraries, routine permitting, public-space care, basic code enforcement, neighborhood-facing maintenance, ordinary customer-facing service design, and much of the civic front door for residents and firms.

Micro-local units matter here as witness, stewardship, and escalation devices, but they should not be mistaken for full sovereign governments of daily life.

### 4. Upgrade to metropolitan government when the real system is already urban-region wide

The archive should default shared urban systems upward once they clearly outrun municipal borders.

That includes metropolitan transit, trunk roads, region-scale housing balance, trunk utilities, air-shed and congestion management, major land-use coordination, and other bargaining spaces where one municipality can impose costs on the rest of the urban system. When the real market, commute shed, or infrastructure network is metropolitan, routing should stop pretending that interlocal diplomacy alone is enough.

### 5. Use regions for major territorial platforms, not merely for map symmetry

The regional archetype should lead when the function depends on a larger territorial platform rather than on a single urban system or a fully national guarantee.

That includes hospital and referral platforms, university and skills systems, basin-scale planning where a permanent territorial platform is warranted, regional economic development, ecological landscapes, disaster logistics, and constitutionally meaningful self-rule inside federations or plurinational settlements.

### 6. Reserve the nation for guarantor duties, solidarity, and constitutional settlement

National government should not be the default lead for every hard problem. It should, however, remain the default lead where equal citizenship, redistribution, macro-stability, common legal status, national rights floors, defense, migration regime, national tax capacity, social insurance, and constitutional arbitration plainly require it.

In those domains the nation is not merely a coordinator among places. It is the guarantor tier.

### 7. Use typed functional authorities where the footprint is real but full territorial government would be too much

Some functions are best routed neither to a full territorial government nor to the center. Watersheds, grids, ports, corridors, air traffic systems, market operators, referral networks, and similar infrastructures often need one body aligned to the system footprint.

The archive should continue to prefer narrow functional authorities where the problem is genuinely system-bounded, the mandate can stay typed, and democratic upgrade remains available if the body starts making broad territorial bargains.

### 8. Use compact unions for recurrent cross-border systems, not as decorative middle worlds

Between nation and globe, many functions belong neither to one state nor to a world government. Common markets, customs spaces, mobility frameworks, shared energy or transport interconnection, basin treaties, and some common regulatory platforms fit better as continental or plurilateral compacts.

The routing table should therefore treat compact unions as real but bounded homes for recurrent cross-border functions whose problem class is durable and whose legal basis is treaty or equivalent constitutional compact.

### 9. Keep the global layer narrow, real, and implementation-aware

The global layer should usually lead only where the public good is genuinely planetary or where safety depends on internationally shared rules, reporting, or technical baselines.

That includes peace and conflict restraint, communicable-disease alerting, certain shipping and aviation baselines, selected telecommunications and interoperability standards, scientific assessment, and other common-commons functions that no one state can settle for everyone else.

Even there, the routing table should remember that global institutions often lead through treaties, reporting frameworks, or technical standards while national and regional institutions still perform most implementation.

### 10. Force mixed fields to split before pretending one level owns the whole thing

Some major policy areas are not single functions at all.

Policing is split across neighborhood safety, ordinary patrol, detention interfaces, serious crime, intelligence, standards, and oversight. Schooling spans local school stewardship, regional service platforms, teacher labour markets, and national finance or rights-floor design. Health spans local prevention, regional hospitals, national financing, and global alerting. Land, identity, and business registries often mix local capture with national trust frameworks.

The routing table should therefore say where the **default lead** sits, but it should also tell the archive when a field probably needs **lane-splitting** rather than one-tier closure.

## Guardrails

- Do not reroute an ordinary municipal function upward merely because the center has more staff.
- Do not leave a genuinely metropolitan system governed only by interlocal bargaining once it is making binding territorial tradeoffs.
- Do not create a region unless it carries real territorial platforms or meaningful self-rule.
- Do not use a functional authority when the problem is really a full territorial bargain that needs democratic upgrade.
- Do not assign national lead merely because a field has a rights dimension; ask whether the nation is guarantor, operator, or both.
- Do not romanticize compact unions or global bodies without naming implementation, finance, and review lanes.
- Do not force mixed fields into one-tier answers when lane-splitting would be more honest.

## Failure modes

- **scope choice from scratch**: every new field reopens the entire ladder and quietly contradicts earlier notes.
- **central defaultism**: anything politically difficult is routed to the nation because it is the strongest actor on paper.
- **metro denial**: an urban-region system is left to municipal veto trading long after the real territory has outgrown that frame.
- **province by wallpaper**: a regional tier exists mainly for symmetry, patronage, or inherited map nostalgia rather than platform work.
- **authority sprawl**: each spillover problem gets a new special body even when territorial government or intergovernmental coordination would do.
- **global theater**: treaty institutions are imagined as world government even where their real role is narrow standard-setting or alerting.
- **composite-field collapse**: policing, health, education, or justice is narrated as one function when it is actually several lanes with different scale logics.

## Practical tests

A routing choice is probably honest if the answer to each question is yes:

1. Can the archive say whether this is an ordinary place function, shared urban system, territorial platform, guarantor duty, bounded cross-border compact, or global narrow waist?
2. Has it named the lead scope separately from support, finance, and review scopes?
3. If the field is mixed, has it admitted that and split the field where necessary?
4. If a special authority is proposed, is the system footprint real enough to justify it?
5. If a metro or region is proposed, does it carry actual distributive, spatial, or platform work rather than decorative map symmetry?
6. If national or global leadership is claimed, is the argument based on genuine guarantor or planetary necessity rather than administrative impatience?

## Compression rule for the archive

When future notes ask where a familiar function should live, start here. Cite the default routing row, then state only the local delta: the spillover, asymmetry, rights floor, exception ladder, or lane split that justifies departure. That keeps the archive compact without forcing each new note to relitigate the whole constitution of scale.
