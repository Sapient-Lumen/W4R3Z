# Boundary Change Checklist (Splits, Mergers, Metro Formation)

**Purpose:** offer a practical checklist so boundary changes preserve records, obligations, and remedy routes.

**Person served:** Residents whose services and representation change when boundaries change and who need continuity of records, remedy, and elections.

**From-below:** This checklist prevents boundary changes from quietly stripping your rights or moving accountability out of reach.

**EXP pointer:** counters `EXP-06` (Complexity) and `EXP-08` (Invisibility) by forcing boundary-change decisions to publish who loses access and how remedy routes remain reachable (see `98-persons-path-and-accessibility-invariants.md`).

Boundary changes are **high-risk constitutional moves**: they reshuffle authority, tax bases, services, elections, records, and remedies. This memo is a **one-page operational checklist** extracted from (and meant to be used alongside) `17-jurisdiction-formation-and-boundaries.md` and `54-subsidiarity-and-scope-assignment-test.md`.

**Anchor constraints:** consultation before boundary change and protection of local communities’ say in boundary changes ([BIB-COE-LOCALSELF-ART5]); territorial planning coordination across scales ([BIB-UNHABITAT-IGUTP-2015]).

## Named tensions (design must surface these)
- “Efficiency” claims vs democratic legitimacy and community consent.
- Consolidation vs representation (minorities can be diluted; peripheries can be ignored).
- Transition speed vs continuity and accountability (avoid grey authority and lost records).
- Fiscal discipline vs cruelty (no essential-service cliffs or hostage conditionality).

## A) Trigger test (why are we changing boundaries?)
Proceed only if at least one is true **and** alternatives are exhausted (compacts, functional authorities, shared services, metro coordination):
- **Capacity collapse:** essential services can’t be delivered reliably (persistent `CAD-*` failures) and the failure is structurally tied to jurisdiction size/fragmentation.
- **Spillovers dominate:** harms/benefits regularly cross the boundary (transport, housing markets, watersheds, grids), and compacts/functional authorities can’t solve it.
- **Fiscal mismatch:** the tax base/benefit incidence is systematically misaligned with service obligations and equalization/transfers can’t close the gap.
- **Representation distortion:** governance cannot represent the affected community or maintain electoral equality without structural change.

**Stop condition:** if the main goal is “efficiency” without a rights/service/fiscal rationale, treat as suspect and require an **assurance case** (`73-...`).

## B) Scope-and-interface requirements (what MUST be produced)
A boundary change proposal is incomplete unless it pre-commits to these joinable artifacts:

1) **`DRR-TYPE: SCOPE`** for the boundary decision itself (scope tests + effective date + remedy continuity). (`31-...`, `34-...`, `54-...`)
2) **Competence ledger update**: old → new mandate ownership and the transition plan pointer. (`34-...`)
3) **Finance alignment package**:
   - updated revenue assignment + debt/liability allocation,
   - equalization/transfer updates (`TRF-*`) with formula and conditions,
   - a “no service cliff” backstop for essential services (see `82-...`, `18-...`, `35-...`).
4) **Records + continuity plan**:
   - record custody/retention continuity, FOI/RTI routing, and no-loss guarantees,
   - publication integrity for the competence ledger and any constitutional registers. (`31-...`, `53-...`)
5) **Remedy continuity plan**:
   - what happens to pending cases/appeals,
   - which `AL-*` lanes apply before/after effective date,
   - standing rules during transition. (`36-...`, `08-...`)
6) **Election/representation continuity plan** (where applicable): interim districts, election timetable, franchise continuity, and challenge lane.

## C) Process checklist (tight, testable)

### C1) Pre-decision diligence (required before the vote)
- Apply the **scope assignment test** (`54-...`) and publish the one-screen scope docket (or attach it to the `DRR-TYPE: SCOPE`).
- Publish an **alternatives analysis**: why compacts (`19`), functional authorities (`15`), metro coordination (`16`), or shared services (`87`) are insufficient.
- Publish a **service impact sheet** for the top critical services (`SRV-*`): access points, staffing, service standards, and continuity risk.
- Publish a **fiscal impact sheet**: revenue, equalization, debt/liabilities, and any one-off transition costs.

### C2) Consultation and consent discipline
- Define the affected “local communities” and how they are consulted in time and appropriately (consistent with boundary-protection norms) ([BIB-COE-LOCALSELF-ART5]).
- Publish a consultation `ENG-*` entry and a duty-to-respond plan (`41-...`, `21-...`).

### C3) Transition governance (avoid “grey authority”)
- Name a time-bounded **transition authority** (or compact) with explicit powers, constraints, and publication duties.
- Require delegation/acting authority receipts for transition signatories (`78-...`).
- For enforcement/custody and emergency powers, pre-commit to continuity of receipts/logs and serious-incident protocols (`43-...`, `24-...`, `23-...`).

### C4) Implementation gates (don’t flip the switch without readiness)
Boundary changes SHOULD have **gates** that can delay the effective date if readiness isn’t met:
- records migration + FOI routing tested,
- payroll/HR continuity and critical vacancy plan,
- essential service continuity test for `ESS-1` services,
- hotline/complaints capacity and lane discoverability (`AL-*`).

### C5) Post-change review and reversibility
- Set a **review date** (e.g., 12–24 months after effective date) and define success criteria.
- Use sunsetting/deprecation discipline: if the change fails, specify the reversible path (or a compensating compact) (`74-...`).

## D) Minimal metrics (re-use existing packs)
Track the change as a program:
- **[MCL-4] Scope-change auditability** (scope decisions logged and ledger updated)
- **[CAD-2] Response/queue times** for affected `SRV-*` services (watch for transition spikes)
- **[IPM-10] Transfers predictability & legibility** (if transfers/withholding events change)
- **[LRR-4] Time-to-remedy** for grievances/appeals during transition
