# Resilience Finance: Risk Pools, Catastrophe Transfer, and Solidarity Rails

**Purpose:** make disaster and shock response *fast, fair, and hard to loot* by pre‑committing funds, triggers, and accountability across scopes.

**Person served:** people hit by a disaster (flood, fire, quake, pandemic, infrastructure collapse) who need *cash + services now* without begging, corruption, or arbitrary rationing.

**From-below:** this memo is designed to stop “aid theater”: slow relief, hidden eligibility rules, political favoritism, and post‑disaster land grabs.

## Design goals (non‑negotiables)
- **Speed with legitimacy:** pre‑agreed triggers + automatic disbursement windows; contested cases route to a fast grievance lane (see `08-remedy-and-grievance.md`).
- **Equity:** benefits keyed to *harm and vulnerability*, not media salience or political alignment.
- **No silent swaps:** don’t trade immediate relief for long‑run asset stripping (land grabs, debt traps, privatization under duress).
- **Anti‑looting by design:** publish flows; tighten procurement/benefits integrity rails (see `110-budget-procurement-integrity.md`, `163-integrity-stack-anti-corruption-rails.md`).

## A scope map: who holds what kind of buffer?
Ideal systems use **layered buffers** (many small, a few big), with clear handoffs:

### Micro‑local (block / neighborhood / mutual aid)
- **Mutual‑aid caches:** pre‑positioned supplies + a lightweight registry of vulnerable households (opt‑in; privacy protected).
- **Micro‑grants:** small rapid cash (or vouchers) for the first 72 hours; audit by receipts sampling + community verification.
- **Delegation rule:** micro‑local never becomes the *only* safety net; it triggers escalation when thresholds are exceeded.

### Municipal / local government
- **Contingency reserve & surge contracting:** standing contracts for debris removal, temporary housing, logistics; open price books.
- **Parametric “rapid relief” module:** simple, explainable triggers (e.g., rainfall + river gauge; grid outage duration) for automatic household payments where feasible.
- **Recovery zoning moratorium:** short, automatic pause on evictions/foreclosures + anti‑speculation guardrails (link: `136-land-housing-and-commons-integrity.md`).

### Regional / state / province
- **Shared service corps:** interoperable emergency management, engineering, and health surge teams.
- **Risk pool / reinsurance layer:** spreads correlated risks across municipalities; requires mitigation standards as a condition of coverage.
- **Interoperable benefits:** displaced residents keep eligibility across jurisdictions (see `109-portability-and-cross-jurisdiction-continuity.md`).

### National
- **Social insurance for shocks:** unemployment, health, housing stabilization, and business continuity support with pre‑declared eligibility logic.
- **Sovereign risk transfer:** use capital‑market and (re)insurance instruments where appropriate (cat bonds / capital‑at‑risk notes), but treat them as *complements* to prevention, not substitutes.
- **Debt discipline for recovery:** publish post‑event borrowing terms; prevent collateralized “fire sale” sovereign debt that undermines future fiscal autonomy.

### Global / plurilateral
- **Mutualized facilities:** rapid response windows and contingent finance for low‑capacity states, with governance that prevents donor capture.
- **Data commons:** shared hazard, exposure, and loss data standards; open methods for triggers and payouts.

## Instruments and when to use them (rails-first)
- **Contingency funds** (fast, flexible): best for frequent events; requires strict transparency and replenishment rules.
- **Parametric insurance** (fast payout, clear triggers): best when triggers can be measured reliably; requires *appeals path* for edge cases.
- **Catastrophe bonds / capital-at-risk notes** (risk transfer): best for rare, severe events; high transaction costs mean they’re not universal—use pooled issuance where possible.
- **Contingent credit lines** (bridge finance): helps liquidity but can become a debt trap; publish terms and cap collateralization.
- **Solidarity levies / windfall clawbacks** (replenishment): earmark recovery replenishment to prevent “rebuild by austerity.”

## Governance rails (what makes it trustworthy)
### 1) Triggers you can contest
- Publish triggers, data sources, and decision logic **before** events.
- Add a **trigger dispute lane**: if your claim is outside the parametric model, you can request a fast review.

### 2) Benefit integrity without humiliation
- Prefer **ex post audits** (random sampling, anomaly detection) over invasive upfront gatekeeping.
- Protect against identity theft; treat identity as a utility with minimized claims (see `160-digital-identity-credentials-privacy-utility.md`).

### 3) “Build back better” without “build back captured”
- Tie reconstruction to **open contracting + price transparency** and anti‑cartel enforcement.
- Require **land and housing anti‑speculation** measures during recovery (see `150-land-commons-stewardship-anti-speculation.md`).

### 4) Incentives that don’t punish honesty
- Avoid tying local payouts solely to reported losses (invites inflation or suppression).
- Separate **harm measurement** from **eligibility** when possible; use independent loss estimation and publish methods.

### 5) Prevention is the default
- Coverage and transfers should be conditioned on mitigation and maintenance standards, but never used to abandon people mid‑risk transition.
- Use disaster‑risk evidence to justify prevention spend (see [BIB-UNDRR-GAR-2025], [BIB-UNDRR-GAR-2025-COSTS]).

## Minimal “implementation kit” (starter spec)
- A **Resilience Finance Charter** with:
  - buffer ladder by scope (who pays first, second, last),
  - pre‑declared triggers + appeals lane,
  - publishing rules (flows, vendors, prices, geography),
  - replenishment rule (who refills reserves; how),
  - anti‑speculation and anti‑eviction automatic clauses.
- A **public dashboard**: payouts, procurement, and performance—aligned with the archive’s metrics integrity rules (`03-metrics-and-evidence.md`).
