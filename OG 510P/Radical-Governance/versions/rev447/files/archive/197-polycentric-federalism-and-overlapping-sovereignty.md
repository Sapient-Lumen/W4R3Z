# Polycentric Federalism & Overlapping Sovereignty (Cross-Scope Design)

**Purpose:** make multi-level governance *work in practice* when authority is shared, overlapping, and contested.

**Person served:** someone harmed by buck‑passing or domination across levels (local, regional, national, supranational) who needs a **legible, enforceable path** to identify responsibility and force correction.

**From-below:** this memo is an antidote to “call a different office” governance: it defines how shared sovereignty stays contestable and how coordination stays visible.

This memo is **not** a theory of federalism. It is a **design pattern pack** for polycentric systems: many centers of decision-making that can coordinate, compete, and correct one another while preserving rights floors. (Anchor: Ostrom on polycentric governance: see [BIB-OSTROM-POLYCENTRIC-2010].)

See also: `14-scope-ladder.md` (what belongs where), `54-subsidiarity-and-scope-assignment-test.md` (scope test), `19-compacts-and-cooperative-governance.md` (compacts), `18-intergovernmental-finance.md` (money matches mandates), `114-interjurisdictional-dispute-and-coordination.md` (disputes), `117-exit-voice-fork-federation.md` (exit/voice/fork).

## Named tensions (design must surface these)
- **Local autonomy vs rights floor:** experimentation and cultural legitimacy vs equal protection.
- **Coordination vs domination:** solving spillovers without central capture or preemption-by-stealth.
- **Fiscal discipline vs solidarity:** preventing soft-budget bailouts without abandoning people.
- **Overlaps vs clarity:** plural authorities can improve resilience but amplify confusion.

## What “ideal” looks like in a polycentric system

An ideal polycentric system is **not** “decentralized everything” or “centralized everything”. It is:

1. **Clear default ownership + explicit overlap rules.**
   - Every major function has a *default owner* and explicit circumstances for shared authority.
   - Overlaps are treated as **interfaces**, not accidents (publish the join-keys).

2. **A rights-and-remedy floor that cannot be bargained away.**
   - Local variation is allowed only above a minimum rights/procedure baseline (rule-of-law + remedy) (see `01-principles.md`; anchors: [BIB-UDHR], [BIB-WJP-ROL]).

3. **Visible intergovernmental bargaining.**
   - “Deals” (transfers, waivers, conditional funding, delegation) are published as compacts with reasons, terms, and review dates (`CMP-*` + `DRR-*`).

4. **Fiscal federalism that matches mandates.**
   - If a higher scope imposes duties, it must fund them or explicitly reassign the duty. Unfunded mandates are treated as integrity violations (anchor concepts: [BIB-OATES-1999-FISCALFED], [BIB-IMF-IGF-2018]).

5. **A credible dispute path between governments.**
   - A neutral forum for intergovernmental disputes (jurisdiction conflicts, transfers, preemption, externalities) with published decisions.

6. **Graceful failure modes.**
   - When coordination fails: temporary trusteeship/backstop, surge capacity, and continuity plans that preserve local legitimacy and restore autonomy after repair (`112-exception-control-and-emergency-powers.md`, `109-portability-and-cross-jurisdiction-continuity.md`).

## Minimum Viable Polycentric Stack (MVPS)

This is the smallest “federalism spine” that makes overlap *legible and contestable*.

1) **Competence ledger coverage**
- Every jurisdictional unit has a Unit ID; mandates and funding obligations are explicit.

2) **Scope-test docket (`DRR-TYPE: SCOPE`)**
- When authority is pulled upward (preemption, harmonization, emergency centralization), the decision must be filed with reasons, evidence, and a review date (subsidiarity/proportionality discipline; anchors: [BIB-TEU-A5], [BIB-EURLEX-PROTOCOL2]).

3) **Compact register (`CMP-*`)**
- All cross-scope agreements (delegations, shared services, joint procurement, conditional transfers, shared enforcement) are registered and versioned.

4) **Intergovernmental finance traceability**
- Transfer formulas and conditions are published; equalisation is explicit; bailouts have rule-bound triggers (soft budget constraints are a known failure mode: see [BIB-RODDEN-SBC-GERMANY-2006]).

5) **Intergovernmental dispute resolution**
- A tribunal/arbitration path for scope/transfer conflicts, with standing rules and time-bounds.

6) **Portability guarantees**
- People must not lose essential status/benefits/services when boundaries shift or authorities conflict (`109-portability-and-cross-jurisdiction-continuity.md`).

## Design patterns (use as modules)

### Pattern A: “Default owner + override tests”
**Use when:** multiple levels plausibly claim authority.

- Define a **default owner** at the lowest competent scope.
- Allow override only if a written test passes (spillovers, scale economies, rights-floor protection, coordination failure) and is filed to the scope docket (`DRR-TYPE: SCOPE`).

### Pattern B: Dual legitimacy intergovernmental council
**Use when:** regions/municipalities must coordinate without erasing local legitimacy.

- A council with “**people + places**” representation (avoid dominance by large units while retaining population legitimacy).
- Meetings, votes, and compacts are published; closed sessions require a recorded justification (protective legibility applies).

### Pattern C: Equalisation with conditionality that preserves appeal
**Use when:** transfers are required for equity and basic services.

- Publish the formula, the data sources, and the re‑calculation schedule.
- Conditional funding must be rule-bound and appealable; discretionary patronage is treated as a capture vector.

### Pattern D: “Preemption with reasons + sunset”
**Use when:** national harmonization is needed (rights floors, market integrity, crisis).

- Preemption must specify: the harm being prevented, why lower scopes cannot solve it, and what autonomy remains.
- Include a review date and a deprecation/sunset plan (`74-sunsetting-and-deprecation-discipline.md`).

### Pattern E: Nation-to-nation / indigenous sovereignty compacts
**Use when:** polities are not merely “local governments” but self-determining nations.

- Treat as **first-class units** in the competence ledger.
- Use compacts for shared stewardship (water, land, education, policing interfaces) while preserving self-governance.
- Rights and remedy floors still apply to people affected by decisions (person’s path invariants).

### Pattern F: Functional overlays (special districts) with hard anti-creep
**Use when:** a network good crosses boundaries (transit/water/energy/schools).

- Narrow mandate + transparent tariffs + independent audit.
- Clear appeal lanes for billing/access/enforcement.
- Mandatory sunset/review to prevent “shadow state” accumulation (see `15-functional-authorities.md`; data anchor: [BIB-USCENSUS-SPECIALDIST-2022]).

## Anti-patterns (name them so they’re stoppable)

- **Unfunded mandates:** duties imposed without money or realistic capacity.
- **Opaque intergovernmental deals:** closed-door bargaining that reallocates power or money without publication.
- **Soft budget constraints:** repeated discretionary bailouts that reward fiscal risk-taking.
- **Preemption-by-stealth:** central overrides via procurement rules, grants, or technical standards without a scope docket.
- **Jurisdictional confusion as defense:** complexity used to defeat remedy (EXP-06).

## Minimal success metrics (≤ 8)
- % of major intergovernmental agreements published as `CMP-*` with review dates [LRR-8]
- time-to-resolution for jurisdiction disputes (median/90p) [LRR-4]
- share of mandates with explicit funding responsibility (coverage) [LRR-8]
- equalisation adequacy proxy (variance in core service outcomes after transfers) [LRR-7]
- portability failures per 10k moves/boundary events (status/benefit continuity incidents) [CAD-3]
