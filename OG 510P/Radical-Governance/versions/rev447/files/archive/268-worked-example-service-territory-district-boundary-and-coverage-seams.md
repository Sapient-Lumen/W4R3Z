# Worked Example Service Territory, District Boundary, and Coverage Seams

**Purpose:** make boundary-based eligibility, routing, and priority decisions legible so a hidden map or stale territory file does not become an unreviewable gate.

**Person served:** someone whose access depends on which district, catchment, service zone, circuit, territory, or coverage area the institution thinks they are in.

**From-below:** people usually encounter boundary systems as “wrong door” pain long before they ever see the map or rule that pushed them there.

---

## Use this memo when
- a school, utility, health network, shelter system, or other service says the person is out of district or territory;
- the boundary or service-area file is contested, stale, or poorly aligned with lived place;
- a boundary change silently reshuffles queues, obligations, or pricing.

## Required artifacts
- **Boundary-basis receipt** naming the map version, rule class, and appeal lane.
- **Coverage-seam packet** when more than one provider or territory plausibly applies.
- **Grace routing rule** so the person is not bounced or dropped while the seam is under review.

## Safe defaults
- Boundary disputes should preserve continuity before they reassign responsibility.
- A hidden map is not a legitimate gate.
- When the institution cannot explain which boundary rule controlled, it should not harden the denial.
