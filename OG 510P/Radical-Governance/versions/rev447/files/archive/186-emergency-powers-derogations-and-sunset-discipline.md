# Emergency Powers, Derogations, and Sunset Discipline

**Merge relation:** narrower companion to `165-emergency-powers-derogation-sunsets-rails.md`. Use `165` as the canonical entry; use this memo when you specifically need the ledger/renewal/sunset-discipline emphasis.


**Purpose:** make “emergency” a **tightly bounded legal mode** with *visible triggers*, *time limits*, *oversight*, and *automatic rollback*—so exceptional powers can respond to real crises without becoming a durable governance substrate.

**Person served:** a person whose rights, movement, work, property, or health choices are being constrained “because emergency”.

**From-below floor:** **no emergency without receipts** (who declared it, why, under what legal basis, what rights are constrained, what evidence, what end-date, and what review path).

---

## 1) First principle: emergency is a *mode*, not a blank check

Emergency powers must be:

- **Officially proclaimed** and grounded in **law** (no informal “state of exception”).
- **Necessity + proportionality** constrained (strictly required by the exigencies of the situation).
- **Non-discriminatory** (no emergency as pretext for targeted suppression).
- **Time‑bounded** with renewal thresholds and escalating scrutiny.

Anchors: ICCPR Article 4 (derogations) + HRC General Comment 29 + Siracusa Principles. See `[BIB-ICCPR]`, `[BIB-HRC-GC29]`, `[BIB-UNHRC-GC29-2001]`, `[BIB-SIRACUSA]`.

---

## 2) Emergency Declaration Receipt (EDR)

Every emergency declaration must generate an **Emergency Declaration Receipt**:

- **EDR-ID**, timestamp, declaring authority, jurisdiction, scope.
- **Trigger claim:** what event qualifies as “public emergency threatening the life of the nation” (or local statutory analogue).
- **Evidence docket:** citations to the public facts and measures used to justify declaration.
- **Rights map:** which rights are affected; whether by *ordinary limitation* or *derogation*; what is non‑derogable.
- **Measures list:** what powers are activated (and explicitly what is *not* activated).
- **End date / sunset:** default expiry (short), renewal requirements (strict).
- **Oversight path:** parliamentary ratification clocks, judicial review availability, independent monitor/ombuds route.
- **Notification duties:** international notification where applicable (ICCPR/ECHR contexts) and public notice requirements.

(Join to: `31-records-foi-and-government-memory.md`, `39-rulebook-and-instruments-registry.md`, `51-release-registry.md`.)

## 3) Two-lane doctrine: limitation first, derogation last

Emergency governance should implement a strict ordering:

1. **Use ordinary limitation clauses** where possible (narrow, right-specific, less legally destabilizing).
2. **Derogate only** if ordinary limitations are insufficient, and only to the extent strictly required.

This mirrors the logic in HRC GC29 (Article 4 as exceptional) and the Siracusa interpretive constraints. `[BIB-UNHRC-GC29-2001]` `[BIB-SIRACUSA]`

---

## 4) Sunset + renewal discipline

**Default sunset:** 14–30 days (jurisdiction-specific), with renewal requiring:

- updated evidence docket,
- explicit measure-by-measure justification,
- impact report (harms + distribution),
- and higher thresholds over time (e.g., supermajority, independent certification).

**No evergreen emergency:** renewals must be legally treated as *new declarations* with new receipts.

Research anchor on sunsets: `[BIB-SUNSET-CLAUSES-2016]`.

---

## 5) Oversight tripwires (anti-capture rails)

Emergency powers are a capture magnet. Require:

- **Parliamentary ratification clock** (short); failure to ratify auto-terminates.
- **Courts remain operational**; emergency does not suspend the right to challenge measures.
- **Independent monitor** (inspector general / ombuds / rights commissioner) with:
  - subpoena or access powers,
  - mandatory publication cadence,
  - protected whistleblowing channels.
- **Elections protection:** emergency cannot be used to unilaterally alter election timing/rules without elevated cross‑party thresholds and judicial review (see Venice Commission benchmarks). `[BIB-VENICE-COVID-EMERGENCY-2020]`

(Also see Venice Commission compilation/benchmarks pages; add to bibliography if reused.)

---

## 6) Data, surveillance, and “emergency tech” constraints

If emergency measures involve data, surveillance, identity checks, or mobility controls:

- **Purpose lock:** emergency-only use; explicit ban on repurposing for routine policing, immigration, or political monitoring.
- **Minimization:** collect least data necessary; avoid centralization where possible.
- **Deletion + rollback:** automatic deletion schedules; “emergency dataset” must have a destruction receipt.
- **Public register:** publish what systems are used, vendors, model versions, and oversight contacts.

## 7) Exit + after-action discipline

Emergency mode must end with:

- **Termination Receipt (ETR):** date/time of termination; measures rolled back; remaining tail risks.
- **After-Action Review (AAR):** what worked, what harmed, what should be pre-authorized next time.
- **Remedy lane:** fast-track compensation / restoration when measures were wrongly applied.
- **Archive of exceptions:** emergency waivers/variances must be logged and joinable. (See `85-waivers-variances-and-exceptions-discipline.md`.)

## Minimal tests (hook into `107-governance-test-suite.md`)

- **T — Emergency boundedness:** Can an affected person retrieve EDR + rights map + end date + review path within minutes?
- **T — Sunset reality:** Do emergency powers auto-expire unless renewed with new receipts?
- **T — Oversight continuity:** Are courts/ombuds/inspectors operational with real access powers?
