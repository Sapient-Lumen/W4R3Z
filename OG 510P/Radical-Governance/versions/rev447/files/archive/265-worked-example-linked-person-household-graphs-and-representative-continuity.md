# Worked Example Linked-Person, Household Graphs, and Representative Continuity

**Purpose:** make household, dependent, caregiver, authorized-representative, and linked-person state legible so institutions stop spilling one person’s data error or compliance event onto everyone connected to them.

**Person served:** a person whose eligibility, safety, access, or obligations depend on how the institution draws the edges between people.

**From-below:** linked-person graphs are power. When those edges are wrong, the governed should not have to discover the mistake only after food, care, transport, or representation breaks.

---

## Use this memo when
- a household composition change, custody status, or caregiver relationship is disputed;
- an authorized representative, interpreter, or confidential contact fails to carry forward;
- a debt, sanction, attendance problem, or compliance flag spills across household members or dependents.

---

## Required artifacts

### 1) Linked-person / household graph receipt (`LGR-*`)
Every rights-affecting change to household composition, dependent status, caregiver link, or representative assignment emits a receipt naming:
- the current relationship graph as the institution sees it;
- the disputed edge or changed link;
- who can speak for whom, and under what confidentiality constraints;
- what protections stay live for dependents while review runs.

### 2) Spillover rule card
Where one person’s status can affect another’s benefits, obligations, or queue position, publish a compact rule card stating:
- when spillover is allowed;
- when it is prohibited;
- what individualized findings are required before a linked person is harmed.

### 3) Representative continuity packet
If a case has an authorized representative, advocate, counsel, caregiver, or confidential contact, transfers MUST carry:
- contact method;
- authority scope;
- expiry/review date if any;
- receiving-side acknowledgment before adverse clocks restart.

---

## Safe defaults
- **Dependents do not lose continuity because an adult link is under review.**
- **Household spillover must be explicit, not inferred by convenience.**
- **Confidential and safety-sensitive relationships survive handoffs unless a fresh receipt changes them.**
- **Representative continuity breaks pause binding events.**

---

## Minimum tests
- Can the person see the household or linked-person graph the institution is actually using?
- Is there a bounded rule for when one person’s noncompliance can affect another?
- Do representative and safety-contact states survive transfers?
- Can a reviewer reconstruct why the institution believed a household edge existed?
