# Service-level governance and redress operations

**Purpose:** make “government works” an enforceable interface: **service promises, observable performance, and fast redress** when reality diverges.

This doc connects three existing threads in the archive:
- state capacity and service delivery
- remedy/grievance
- observability/audits

## The service interface (what people can rely on)
For each *essential* public service, publish a short “service card”:
- eligibility + required inputs
- time-to-decision (median + P95)
- error classes and how to contest
- escalation route and deadlines
- contact channels (including accessible/offline)

Treat service cards as *public contracts*.

## SLOs for public services
Define SLOs (service-level objectives) that are human-meaningful:
- time-to-first-response
- time-to-decision
- time-to-remedy (when government is at fault)
- uptime for essential digital services
- exclusion/denial rate (especially for identity/benefits)

All SLOs must include:
- measurement method
- reporting cadence
- ownership (named unit)
- what happens when SLOs fail (automatic escalation)

## The redress pipeline (minimum viable)
A grievance redress mechanism (GRM) should provide:
- multiple intake channels (online, phone, in-person, assisted)
- clear processing rules and service standards
- monitoring + feedback to complainants
- appeal path when the GRM itself fails

OGP’s review of GRMs is a useful evidence-oriented entry point. citeturn1search1

## Independent redress for rights claims
Where grievances implicate rights (not merely service errors), route to independent bodies:
- ombuds / mediator institutions
- national human rights institutions (NHRIs) designed for independence and broad mandate

The UN “Paris Principles” are a strong baseline for independence, pluralism, and effectiveness in such institutions. citeturn1search2turn1search6

## “Remedy-first” defaults (anti-bureaucratic cruelty)
When evidence is uncertain but harm is clear:
- prioritize temporary relief + fast review
- avoid irreversible penalties pending appeal
- compensate for wrongful denials/delays when government fault is found

## Failure escalation ladder
When local service delivery fails persistently:
1. internal escalation (manager + audit flag)
2. external escalation (independent inspector / auditor)
3. scope failure declaration (see Failure Migration Rule in scope synthesis)
4. temporary takeover/assistance compact
5. restore authority after capability proof

## Metrics that matter
Publish a minimal public dashboard:
- median + P95 decision times
- denial rates + top denial reasons
- appeal volume + reversal rate
- time-to-remedy
- repeat-complaint rate (signals systemic defects)

Avoid vanity metrics. Optimize for *trust, speed, and correctness*.

## Procurement + staffing hooks
Most chronic service failure is capacity failure:
- require postmortems for recurring SLO breaches
- tie procurement/staffing requests to measured bottlenecks
- prohibit “new tech” deployments that worsen exclusion or appeal load
