# Illicit Finance & Kleptocracy Defense (Make “Who Benefits” Joinable, Verifiable, and Contestable)

**Purpose:** prevent public power and public money from being quietly routed through hidden owners, intermediaries, and off‑ledger influence.

**Person served:** residents and honest vendors harmed when contracts, licenses, and exemptions are captured via shell entities, bribery, or hidden interests.

**From-below:** gives ordinary people (and honest insiders) a way to *trace, contest, and stop* corrupt routing without needing insider access.

**EXP pointer:** counters `EXP-01` (Opacity), `EXP-06` (Deference), and `EXP-05` (Fear) by (a) making beneficiaries legible, (b) binding decisions to explainable trails, and (c) protecting those who report. (`04`, `31`, `83`, `99`)

**Material floor (one sentence):** assumes MVF staffing and assisted/offline access; in degraded mode, preserve salvage‑core obligations: receipting, time bounds, and continuity of records. (See `07` MVF; `80` Phase −1; `98`; `31`; `101-claude-rev142-normative-requirements.md` (NR-13).)

**Assistance & representation:** provide protected, assisted, and offline reporting + inquiry lanes; allow third‑party/collective filings when retaliation risk is real; publish who can act on behalf of someone. (`98`, `36`, `83`, `77`)

**Authority (must bind):** this stack is theater unless it can *pause/nullify*, *debar*, *claw back*, *discipline*, and *refer* (prosecution/anti‑corruption bodies) with auditable closure loops. (`22`, `32`, `55`, `84`)

---

Illicit finance governance is not just “law enforcement.” It is a **data + decision interface problem**: when beneficiaries are hidden, integrity controls cannot bite.

This memo defines a portable **anti‑kleptocracy join** that connects:
- **Who got what** (contracts, grants, licenses, exemptions),
- **Who benefited** (beneficial owners / controllers),
- **Who influenced** (lobbying, gifts, conflicts, intermediaries),
- **What the state did** (decisions, waivers, enforcement),
- **What remedies exist** (contest lanes, clawback, debarment, pattern correction).

## Minimal stack (start here)

1) **Beneficial Ownership (BO) register with verification**
- Publish an entity’s *beneficial owner / controller* data using a standard schema and a **verification policy** (not just self‑reporting).
- Make BO data **joinable** to the contracting and influence registers and to decision receipts via stable identifiers.
- Provide privacy‑safe disclosure tiers (e.g., public vs accredited access) to avoid creating a targeting surface. (`77`, `99`)
- Evidence hooks: BODS + verification guidance. See [BIB-BODS], [BIB-OPENOWNERSHIP-VERIFY-2020], and FATF R.24 guidance [BIB-FATF-BO-2023].

2) **Contracting + spending trails that don’t “silent swap”**
- Require contracts/grants/subsidies to land in joinable registers with *change history* and *beneficiary joins*. (`38`, `49`, `22`, `110`)
- Ban “silent vendor swap” through affiliates/subcontractors without a logged approval + conflict check + receipt. (`110`, `31`)
- Enforce debarment/eligibility checks as a **hard gate** (with auditable override discipline). (`84`, `85`)

3) **Influence + interests disclosure that connects to decisions**
- Join BO and vendor entities to **influence and interests** (lobbying, meetings, gifts, outside roles, family ties where legally relevant) with safe redaction for targets. (`46`, `79`, `77`, `99`)
- Evidence hooks: OECD lobbying integrity recommendation [BIB-OECD-LOB] and public integrity baseline [BIB-OECD-PI].

4) **Asset declaration / interest statements for high‑risk roles (portable pattern)**
- Where officeholders control high‑value allocations or enforcement discretion, require periodic declarations and event‑triggered updates (e.g., new outside role; close relative hired by a major vendor).
- Bind it to recusal, waiver discipline, and sanctions. (`79`, `78`, `85`)

5) **Cross‑border cooperation primitives**
- Publish what the jurisdiction can and cannot verify alone; define “verification dependencies” and escalation paths (mutual legal assistance, cross‑registry joins, trusted exchange networks).
- Keep this narrow and rights‑respecting; joins must name a corrective action and contest lane. (`70`, `36`, `77`)

## Verification, dispute, and privacy (do not skip)

- **Verification is an operating function, not a one‑time check:** define sampling, triggers (e.g., rapid ownership churn), and escalation to investigation. (See [BIB-OPENOWNERSHIP-VERIFY-2020], [BIB-FATF-BO-2023].)
- **Disputes must be receipted:** if BO data is challenged or corrected, emit a correction receipt and propagate to affected registers/decisions. (`31`, `37`, `52`)
- **Protective legibility:** publish enough to enable contestation while minimizing retaliation and defamation risk; default to aggregation or scoped disclosure where necessary. (`99`, `77`, `83`)

## Where this becomes mandatory (scope fit)

- **High‑value allocation:** procurement, large grants/subsidies, capital projects, natural resources, and monopoly/utility regulation. (`22`, `97`, `13`)
- **Discretion + opacity:** licensing, inspections, waivers, emergency procurement, and enforcement settlements. (`85`, `81`, `32`)
- **Cross‑border rent channels:** shell entities, intermediaries, and “consultancy” routing.

## Implementation notes (tight)

- Start by wiring **three joins**: `38` contracting register ↔ `46` influence register ↔ BO register (this memo). Everything else becomes easier after that.
- Ship with **two contest lanes**: (a) “bad data / hidden beneficiary” challenge; (b) “integrity gate failure” challenge (pause + review). (`36`, `22`)
- Use **pattern remediation** when you find a routing pattern (same intermediaries, same ownership churn style): treat it as a system bug with a fix + backfill. (`76`, `84`)
