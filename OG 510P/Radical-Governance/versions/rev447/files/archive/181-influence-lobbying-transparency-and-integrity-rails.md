# Influence, lobbying transparency, and integrity rails

**Purpose:** prevent “quiet capture” by making influence legible, contestable, and bounded.

This doc defines a minimal **influence transparency interface** that any scope can adopt without redesigning its entire constitution.

---

## A. Requirements (the non-negotiables)

1. **Influence visibility:** meetings, gifts, sponsorships, and paid advocacy are disclosed.
2. **Identity & interest clarity:** who is represented, by whom, and for what objective.
3. **Decision traceability:** significant policy changes link to influence disclosures.
4. **Enforcement:** penalties are real, fast, and publicly reported.
5. **Parity:** rules apply to *all* influence channels (industry, unions, NGOs, consultants).

---

## B. The “Influence Ledger” (minimum data model)

Publish, in a searchable public registry:

- participant identity (person + organization)
- represented interest(s) and client(s)
- topic / bill / procurement / regulation referenced
- meeting date/time, format, and attending officials
- any transfers: gifts, travel, hospitality, political donations (where legal)
- written materials submitted (or hash + retrieval link)
- conflict-of-interest declarations for public officials
- cooling-off / revolving-door status

**Operational note:** disclose quickly (e.g., within days), not quarterly; otherwise the registry becomes archival theater.

---

## C. Gates (when influence must be escalated)

Certain actions require a higher scrutiny tier:
- emergency powers and fast-track legislation
- large procurement awards / change orders
- regulatory exemptions, waivers, and “special cases”
- monopoly / platform governance decisions
- constitutional or electoral rule changes

**Gate effect:** at scrutiny tier, decision-makers must publish:
- justification memo
- alternatives considered
- risk assessment (incl. capture risks)
- dissenting views
- compliance checklist linking to influence ledger entries

---

## D. Anti-capture tripwires

Trigger an automatic review if any occur:
- a single actor accounts for outsized share of meetings on a topic
- repeated closed-door meetings without minutes
- “consultant laundering” (same interest via rotating representatives)
- last-minute legislative text swaps
- procurement change orders exceeding thresholds

When tripped: initiate an independent audit and publish findings.

This doc is designed to couple with:

---

## F. Tests (how to verify it works)

Minimum verification:
- the registry is complete, searchable, downloadable
- disclosure deadlines are met
- enforcement actions are published
- policy documents link to influence disclosures
- capture tripwires run automatically and generate public reports
