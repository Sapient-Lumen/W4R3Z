# Constitutional Review Observability & Precedent Ledgers

**Purpose:** ensure constitutional review is not just a court—it's a **publicly inspectable system** with legible inputs/outputs, consistent reasoning, and correction channels.

This memo complements `156-judicial-systems-and-constitutional-review.md` by focusing on the **information and accountability rails** around review, not the institutional design itself.

---

## 1. Precedent ledger (what it is)

A **Precedent Ledger** is a public index (searchable) of:
- decisions (with reasons)
- dissents / concurrences
- cited authorities
- remedies ordered
- compliance status (implemented? pending? ignored?)
- links to case files / briefs where publishable

**Rule:** every decision gets a stable pointer and a compact “Decision Card”.

### Decision Card (minimum fields)
- case id + date + chamber
- question presented (one paragraph)
- holding (one paragraph)
- test/standard applied (bullets)
- remedy (bullets)
- implementation owner + deadline (if any)
- citations (statutes, constitutional clauses, precedent)
- publication exceptions (what is withheld + why + expiry)

---

## 2. Review observability: inputs → process → outputs

### Inputs
- docket transparency: filings count, standing disposition, admission criteria
- amicus disclosure: who filed, conflicts, funding signals (when relevant)

### Process
- scheduling clocks: time to hearing / time to decision targets
- recusal disclosures: reasons and replacements (ties to influence integrity rails)

### Outputs
- publish reasons by default
- publish remedy + compliance plan
- publish follow‑up: whether remedy was executed, delayed, or resisted

---

## 3. Compliance loop (review without enforcement is theater)

Every constitutional remedy must have:
1. **Implementation owner** (ministry/agency/legislature)
2. **Plan receipt** (what steps, what date)
3. **Progress receipts** (milestones)
4. **Completion receipt** (what changed)
5. **Non‑compliance escalation** path (sanctions, budget holds, or political triggers)

This integrates with:
- audit & inspection integrity (`130`)
- rulemaking/change control (`118`)
- sanctions integrity (`131`)
- redress systems (`195`)

---

## 4. Standing and access (make reachability explicit)

Publish:
- standing rules in plain language
- costs/fees and fee waiver rules
- legal aid availability
- alternative channels (ombuds, administrative review) when standing is narrow

**Test:** an ordinary person can determine in <30 minutes whether they can bring a claim and how.

---

## 5. Special handling: emergency review

During emergencies, constitutional review must be:
- **fast** (accelerated clocks)
- **public** (reasons published)
- **time‑bounded** (review again at fixed intervals)
- **post‑mortem required** (what was suspended, why, and what is restored)

See `23-emergency-governance-and-exceptions.md`.

---

## 6. Failure modes and guards

### Failure: secret law by sealed decisions
Guard: publish redacted reasons + expiry date; independent review of sealing.

### Failure: selective constitutional enforcement
Guard: compliance ledger + public escalation when ignored.

### Failure: capture by appointment
Guard: appointment packets, cooling‑off rules, disclosure of conflicts; publish dissent patterns and recusal data.
