# Monetary & Financial Stability Governance (Money as Public Utility + Systemic Risk Control)

**Purpose:** make monetary policy, financial stability, and payments infrastructure *legible and contestable* while preserving the operational capacity to act fast in crises.

**Why it matters in this archive:** fiscal governance can be transparent yet still fail people if the monetary/financial layer (credit creation, crisis backstops, payment rails) is opaque, politically captured, or distribution‑blind.

See also: `07-fiscal-and-budgetary-governance.md`; `135-taxation-and-revenue-integrity.md`; `110-budget-procurement-integrity.md`; `144-data-stewardship-and-data-trusts.md`; `156-judicial-systems-and-constitutional-review.md`.

---

## 1) Scope fit (who should do what)

**Micro-local / local:** consumer protection, community finance access, local public banking (if any), last‑mile cash/branch service standards.

**Regional / national:** monetary policy; prudential supervision; resolution regimes; deposit insurance; lender-of-last-resort; payments regulation.

**Supranational / global:** cross‑border standards and coordination (banking supervision baselines; macroprudential coordination; payment interoperability; crisis liquidity lines).

**Design rule:** treat this as **polycentric**: multiple bodies with distinct mandates, shared interfaces, and explicit handoffs (see `197-polycentric-federalism-and-overlapping-sovereignty.md`).

---

## 2) From-below non‑negotiables

1. **Explainability of decisions:** decision summaries in plain language + technical annexes + dissenting views.
2. **Transparency on governance and operations:** publish who decides what, with what tools, and what data is used.
3. **Rights and remedies:** clear complaint paths for consumer harms; due process for enforcement; appealable administrative actions.
4. **Distributional visibility:** publish a *distributional impact docket* (who bears costs, who benefits, and why that is acceptable).
5. **Crisis legitimacy:** emergency powers must have time limits, reporting duties, and post‑incident review (with measurable “exit criteria”).

(Transparency and accountability patterns draw on the IMF’s Central Bank Transparency Code and BIS work on central bank governance.) [BIB-IMF-CBT-2020] [BIB-BIS-CBG-2009]

---

## 3) Institutional architecture (minimal viable, high-integrity)

### A. Mandate layering
Use **layered mandates** rather than vague “do everything” mandates:

- **Primary:** price stability (and/or inflation target) and systemic financial stability.
- **Secondary:** maximum employment / real‑economy support *within* primary constraints (explicit hierarchy).
- **Guardrails:** legality, proportionality, and “least harmful effective intervention.”

(Example of statutory dual mandate language exists in major central banks; the U.S. Fed publicly describes the dual goals assigned by Congress.) [BIB-FED-DUALMANDATE-FAQ]

### B. Independence *with* accountability
Independence is not “no oversight”; it’s **insulation from day‑to‑day political micromanagement** paired with:

- published decision frameworks
- auditability (including for unconventional tools)
- legislative testimony obligations
- conflict-of-interest and revolving-door constraints
- clear crisis powers and review

(IMF and BIS materials explicitly frame transparency and accountability as supporting legitimacy and independence.) [BIB-IMF-CBT-2020] [BIB-BIS-CBG-2009]

### C. Macroprudential council (systemic risk steering)
Create (or formalize) a **macroprudential authority** with:
- a clear mandate, powers, and toolkit ownership
- access to systemwide data (including non-bank exposures)
- publishable risk assessments and action rationales
- “act or explain” requirements

(IMF/FSB/BIS and IMF policy work emphasize institutional foundations and governance for effective macroprudential policy.) [BIB-IMF-FSB-BIS-MPP-2016] [BIB-IMF-MPP-2013]

### D. Prudential supervision baseline
Regulation and supervision should meet an internationally recognizable baseline; treat **Basel Core Principles** as the “floor”, not the ceiling, with adaptations for local context. [BIB-BCBS-CORE-PRINCIPLES-2012]

## 4) Decision protocol (monetary + stability as a control loop)

Use a **versioned policy loop** (see `104-governance-control-loops.md`):

1. **Signal:** publish indicators, models, and uncertainty bands (what would change your mind?).
2. **Deliberate:** structured options analysis; record dissent.
3. **Decide:** policy statement with rule references; specify intended transmission channel(s).
4. **Monitor:** publish outcomes vs. forecast; identify confounders and distributional effects.
5. **Correct:** pre-committed review triggers; sunset unconventional measures by default.

Add a **Goodhart check** (see `03-metrics-and-evidence.md`): if a metric becomes a target, log likely gaming vectors and mitigation.

---

## 5) Crisis playbooks (legitimate speed)

### A. Lender-of-last-resort and liquidity facilities
- predefine facility types and eligibility logic
- publish collateral rules and haircuts (or publishable ranges with delayed release if needed)
- require post‑event disclosure and independent review

### B. Resolution and deposit protection
- credible bank resolution regimes (speed + fairness)
- depositor protection rules that reduce panic without subsidizing reckless risk-taking
- “no silent swap”: emergency measures must be recorded as temporary, with sunset and audit

(Basel’s supervision principles explicitly stress early intervention and effective supervisory powers.) [BIB-BCBS-CORE-PRINCIPLES-2012]

---

## 6) Payments as public infrastructure (including CBDC)

Treat payments as **critical civic infrastructure**:
- universal access and reliability standards
- privacy and data minimization defaults
- interoperable rails; open standards where feasible
- anti‑fraud and sanctions compliance with due process

For CBDC specifically, central banks have articulated **foundational principles** (do no harm to monetary/financial stability; coexist with cash and existing money; support innovation/efficiency). Use those principles as explicit test criteria and publish design tradeoffs (interest bearing, caps, intermediated vs direct models, offline use, privacy architecture). [BIB-BIS-CBDC-PRINCIPLES-2020]

---

## 7) “Join rails” between fiscal and monetary (avoid blame games)

Create explicit coordination interfaces:
- fiscal‑monetary coordination logs (what was requested; what was refused; why)
- crisis financing protocols (who funds what; when; with what repayment logic)
- a public ledger of guarantees, backstops, and contingent liabilities

Goal: prevent “shadow fiscal policy” (large quasi-fiscal effects without budget process).

---

## 8) Minimal implementation checklist (starter kit)

- [ ] Mandate hierarchy written + published
- [ ] Decision minutes + dissent + model registry policy
- [ ] Macroprudential authority with “act or explain”
- [ ] Basel‑floor supervisory assessment plan
- [ ] Crisis facility registry with sunset + post‑mortem
- [ ] Payments access, uptime, and privacy standards
- [ ] CBDC (if any) evaluated against published foundational principles
- [ ] Distributional impact docket published for major interventions
