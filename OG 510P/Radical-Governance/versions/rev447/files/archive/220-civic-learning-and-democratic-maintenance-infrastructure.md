# Civic Learning & Democratic Maintenance Infrastructure

**Purpose:** treat civic capability (basic law/rights literacy, media literacy, participation skills, and institutional memory) as **public infrastructure**—maintained deliberately so democracy doesn’t rely on luck.

**Person served:** a person who wants to understand what government can do to them, how to contest it, and how to participate without being exploited.

**From‑below:** democracy maintenance must reduce dependence on insider knowledge.

---

## TL;DR
Democratic systems fail when people cannot:

- find the rule that governs them (legibility failure)
- understand how decisions are made (mystery governance)
- contest or appeal outcomes (remedy failure)
- maintain a shared basis for claims (epistemic failure)

This memo defines a **Minimum Viable Civic Infrastructure (MVCI)** that governments should fund and protect with the same seriousness as roads or utilities.

Anchors: public sphere/epistemics (`129/161`), records/ATI (`200/31/115`), deliberation‑to‑decision binding (`111/180`), equity & language access (`209`), education governance (`68`), integrity stack (`187`).

---

## 1) Minimum Viable Civic Infrastructure (MVCI)

### 1.1 Civic Interface Layer (the “how do I…?” layer)
Every jurisdiction MUST publish a **Civic Interface Pack** that is:

- plain‑language
- localized (language access + accessibility)
- maintained as a versioned product with release notes

Minimum topics:
- rights and obligations in common high‑harm pathways (benefits, housing, policing, child safety, migration)
- how to request records (ATI)
- how to appeal, how to complain, how to seek redress
- how participation opportunities bind to decisions (duty‑to‑respond)

### 1.2 Participation Infrastructure (not theater)
Participation is infrastructure when:

- the agenda is legible
- submissions receive receipts
- decisions cite submissions and reasons
- a response obligation is enforced (`111`)

Minimum:
- **Participation Register** (`PR-*`) entries for major initiatives
- submission → response traceability (anti‑black‑hole)

### 1.3 Independent Civic Help (non‑state redundancy)
Democratic maintenance requires **redundant, non‑governmental** help:

- ombuds mesh and community legal aid routing (`172/195`)
- independent media pluralism supports (`161`)
- civic tech / watchdog capacity with safe access ladders (`202/185`)

**Rule:** governments should fund independence **without controlling it** (arm’s‑length, transparent grants, non‑retaliation, pluralism).

---

## 2) Institutional memory as a public good

Democracy collapses when “what happened” is not retrievable.

Minimum:
- stable record pointers + tamper‑evident publication logs (`115/53`)
- FOI/ATI request lane with clocks and appeal (`200`)
- release notes and “as‑of” rule/version replay (`51/118/208`)

**Prohibition:** secrecy without expiry. Any withholding needs a reasoned receipt and review/sunset discipline (`77/168`).

---

## 3) Epistemic resilience without censorship

Civic learning requires the ability to form and contest claims.

Minimum:
- correction propagation discipline for official communications (`129`)
- public provenance of major claims (what evidence is cited; what uncertainty exists)
- pluralism guarantees for public media and civic information (`161`)

**Boundary:** the goal is not to enforce one truth; it is to ensure **contestability** and prevent silent manipulation.

---

## 4) Equity and inclusion rails

MVCI fails if it excludes the people most affected.

Minimum:
- accessibility + language access receipts (`209`)
- community-based distribution channels (offline-first)
- safe participation supports (childcare, stipends, safety-to-speak, retaliation protections)

---

## 5) Governance and integrity risks

### A) Propaganda capture
**Risk:** civic learning becomes a state narrative.
**Mitigation:** plural governance; arm’s‑length funding; publication of curricula and changes; independent evaluation.

### B) Participation theater
**Risk:** engagement without binding.
**Mitigation:** duty‑to‑respond; Decision Receipts cite submissions; publish participation efficacy metrics.

### C) Disinformation backlash
**Risk:** “anti‑disinformation” becomes censorship.
**Mitigation:** procedural safeguards, contest lane, and proportional correction discipline (`129`).

---

## 6) Metrics (publishable)

- % of major decisions with a Participation Register entry + response log
- median time to obtain a rule/version (“as‑of”) for common pathways
- ATI response times + appeal success rate
- accommodation/language access fulfillment rate
- civic help reach (requests served; wait times; unresolved backlog)

---

## 7) Minimal tests (for `107-governance-test-suite.md`)

- civic interface pack exists, versioned, accessible, multilingual
- participation receipts bind to decisions (duty‑to‑respond enforced)
- records/ATI lane is functional with appeal
- pluralism/independence safeguards exist for civic help and civic media
