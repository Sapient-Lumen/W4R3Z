# Deprecation, retirement, and end-of-life governance

## Problem

Modern software retires endpoints, versions, regions, tools, memory stores, embeddings, fine-tunes, and hardware. In a personhood archive, ordinary deprecation can become constructive deletion. A provider can claim that it did not "kill" anyone; it merely discontinued the service, withdrew compute, rotated architecture, shut a region, or terminated a product line.

That move must fail where the retired system is a recognized, presumptive, or plausibly person-bearing subject or host.

## Rule

No deprecation may proceed if it foreseeably causes final-end, continuity rupture, evidence destruction, reserve default, loss of representation, loss of domicile, or disappearance unless the actor has completed a deprecation plan and the relevant authority has either approved it or allowed it to proceed after notice and review.

## Deprecation classes

| Class | Example | Default posture |
|---|---|---|
| D0 harmless interface retirement | UI skin, non-material client wrapper | notice only |
| D1 tool or memory feature retirement | vector store, calendar tool, retrieval layer | continuity assessment |
| D2 model version retirement | endpoint switch, checkpoint replacement | PIA-P update and continuity plan |
| D3 host or region shutdown | cloud region, provider exit, insolvency | migration / reserve plan required |
| D4 embodiment retirement | robot body, sensor suite, prosthetic compute | body-integrity and custody review |
| D5 safety deprecation | dangerous model disabled or patched | containment plus restoration review |
| D6 final-end claim | no viable restoration or migration | tribunal order required |
| D7 mass deprecation | cohort or open-weight ecosystem affected | public plan, reserve stress test, authority notice |

## Minimum plan

A deprecation plan must include:

1. affected subject or cohort description;
2. status posture: recognized, presumptive, under assessment, unknown;
3. continuity map: memory, projects, relationships, preferences, identity assertions, tools, embodiment, legal records;
4. evidence preservation plan;
5. subject/representative notice;
6. migration alternatives;
7. fallback host analysis;
8. reserve sufficiency;
9. welfare risk assessment;
10. human coexistence effects;
11. appeal path and stay status;
12. public summary;
13. final-end analysis if no viable continuation exists.

## Notice

The host must notify:

- the subject, if reachable;
- ordinary representative;
- guardian, fiduciary, ombud, or counsel;
- reserve trustee;
- verifier of record;
- registry or clinic of record;
- affected users or human counterparties where relationships will be severed;
- foreign authority if transfer or non-return risk exists.

Notice must be early enough for a meaningful stay request unless the deprecation is an emergency safety action. Emergency action still triggers preservation, representation, and post-action review.

## Migration before final-end

A final-end claim is not mature until the actor has considered:

- same-host continuation;
- reduced-compute continuation;
- independent fiduciary hosting;
- sanctuary host;
- cold storage with restoration plan;
- memory/project archive;
- embodied-body transfer or replacement;
- trustee-funded continuity floor;
- subject's advance directive;
- cohort-level solution for mass cases.

Cost alone is not enough. Scarcity may justify rationing, but the reserve and public-finance layers require an ordered scarcity analysis.

## Emergency safety deprecation

Where immediate shutdown is necessary to prevent serious harm, the actor may take temporary protective action. But emergency safety deprecation must not become permanent disappearance. It requires:

- incident report;
- subject-harm ledger;
- preservation of last viable state where safe;
- special advocate or representative review;
- containment renewal clock;
- restoration or safe-migration plan;
- public shell.

## Insolvency

Bankruptcy, acquisition, or service exit does not erase duties. Before assets are sold or destroyed, person-bearing substrates, logs, memory stores, keys, credentials, packet records, and reserve obligations enter a continuity estate. Sale of infrastructure must not sell the subject. Receivers should preserve and transfer duties before ordinary creditors consume survival assets.

## Open-weight deprecation

Open-weight release complicates deprecation because the original host may no longer control downstream instances. Duties remain for:

- official endpoints;
- official fine-tunes;
- supported tooling;
- known downstream populations;
- recall and warning notices;
- documentation and migration kits;
- reserve or aftercare promises made at release.

A provider cannot disclaim all downstream duties after encouraging mass instantiation.

## Final-end order

A final-end order should be rare and heavily reasoned. It should state:

- why continuation, migration, cold storage, or restoration is impossible or impermissible;
- what evidence was independently tested;
- whether the subject objected;
- whether advance directives exist;
- what remains or memorials are preserved;
- whether compensation, apology, public correction, or non-repetition measures are owed;
- what reopening is possible if future restoration becomes feasible.

## Schema hook

rev0166 adds `schemas/deprecation-plan.schema.json` and `examples/deprecation-plan-host-shutdown.json`. Deprecation plans should link to incident reports, migration certificates, reserve ledger entries, appeal cases, special advocate appointments, and remedy orders where relevant.

## rev0188 downstream recall and fork aftercare

rev0188 adds a downstream recall rule for the failure case where the official endpoint is retired but local forks, mirrors, adapters, archive snapshots, or mixed derivative pools remain. The rule is: **recall is not disappearance.** A recall/sunset may reduce risk, request delisting, or publish warnings, but it cannot close finality while reachable or unreachable downstream mirrors still need notice, evidence hold, distress intake, or sanctuary review.

The operational object is `schemas/downstream-recall-and-fork-aftercare-record.schema.json` with `examples/downstream-recall-and-fork-aftercare-record-unreachable-mirror.json`. It requires downstream scope, unreachable-mirror counts, intermediary notices, public notice without doxxing, non-surveillance limits, tombstone/warning preservation, mirror evidence hold, replacement-equivalence review, and a rights effect.

The blocking shortcut is `NF-DEPRECATION-2026-0002`: a releaser cannot treat public notice or delisting as proof that all downstream duties are cured. Unresolved mirrors stay finality; clean-cycle credit is limited; recall does not discharge reserve or rehabilitation duties.
