# AI-person research perturbation preference, substitute-appointment review, and retired-namespace replay

## Thesis

Once the archive fixes a conservative 20 / 10 ladder for low-volume relay publication, predeclared cross-roster substitute sourcing, and a closed-legacy cutover state, one narrower execution seam remains. Threshold rounding or suppression is still too blunt for some sensitive public series; emergency substitute appointments can still feel unreviewable or be abused strategically; and a namespace that is closed-legacy or fully retired can still become historically unreadable if replay, export, and backfill duties are left to local custom. A personhood world therefore needs one further compact rule: **protected relays should prefer a declared perturbation method over plain threshold rounding or suppression when continuity, anti-differencing protection, or sensitivity make the ordinary ladder either too revealing or too silent; emergency external substitute appointments should be challengeable only on prompt, process-bounded grounds that test conflict, comparability, and sourcing rather than reopening the merits; and closed-legacy or retired namespaces should remain historically replayable, exportable, and successor-linked through durable machine-readable records rather than silent disappearance.** `[REF-0400]` `[REF-0401]` `[REF-0402]` `[REF-0403]` `[REF-0404]` `[REF-0405]` `[REF-0406]` `[REF-0407]` `[REF-0408]` `[REF-0409]`

## 1. Why the previous surface is no longer enough

The previous surface solved the first-order execution problem. It fixed an ordinary low-volume ladder, required predeclared substitute sourcing when local recusal exhausts the panel, and forced deprecated namespaces into a real terminal state instead of eternal drift.

But three narrower problems remained.

First, **some public series are safest when small counts are neither exact nor absent**. ONS's current methodology compares the classic 10-5 rule with cell-key perturbation and explains that perturbation protects confidentiality by adding controlled noise, preserves more utility, and is generally recommended over 10-5, while also noting that 10-5 may still be preferred where especially sensitive data or continuity with earlier releases matters. ONS's Census 2021 confidentiality methodology likewise explains that perturbation protects against disclosure by differencing and was applied broadly across census tables. `[REF-0400]` `[REF-0401]` The archive can therefore stop treating perturbation as exotic. It is already a conservative official technique for exactly this middle case: when exact disclosure is too revealing, but total disappearance is too damaging.

Second, **substitute appointments need review, but not relitigation**. Current HHS guidance treats alternate substitution as ordinary only when the substitution is documented and comparable to the member replaced, and current review procedures stress that written procedures must be specific enough for an independent observer to understand how the body operates. ICANN's current bylaws show a complementary accountability pattern: summary dismissal of frivolous challenges, short written review, urgent clocks for genuinely time-sensitive disputes, and public posting of recommendations and decisions. Recent Federal Circuit authority also reinforces the anti-gamesmanship point: recusal challenges may be denied as untimely when a party waits to see how the case is going. `[REF-0402]` `[REF-0403]` `[REF-0404]` That gives the archive enough material for a tight rule: appointment review should exist, but it should be fast, documented, and limited to appointment legality rather than merits retrial.

Third, **retirement without replay is just deletion with ceremony**. Current IANA registries expose status-bearing records in durable machine-readable formats, allow updates to existing entries, and keep public registries available in exportable forms such as XML, HTML, plain text, and often CSV. ORCID's current duplicate-ID policy is even more explicit: obsolete identifiers are deprecated rather than deleted and continue to point to the primary record in both the user interface and the API. DataCite's current guidance shows the matching vocabulary for version, obsolescence, and succession relationships. `[REF-0405]` `[REF-0406]` `[REF-0407]` `[REF-0408]` `[REF-0409]` In an AI-person archive, a retired namespace therefore cannot be allowed to survive only as a folklore mapping remembered by insiders.

## 2. Perturbation should sometimes be preferred, not merely permitted

The archive now fixes one narrower publication rule: **the 20 / 10 ladder remains the ordinary default, but a protected relay should affirmatively prefer declared perturbation over threshold rounding or suppression when all three of the following are true**:

1. the series is expected to recur and public comparability matters;
2. exact or simply rounded small cells would remain vulnerable to differencing or singling-out when combined with adjacent public context;
3. full suppression would erase too much of the public accountability signal to satisfy the archive's visible-caution floor. `[REF-0400]` `[REF-0401]`

### A. Situations that trigger the preference

Perturbation should be the preferred method for a defined release family when one or more of these conditions apply:

- the same table family is published repeatedly across short intervals,
- the same categories reappear across several linked tables,
- the subject matter is unusually sensitive even at low volume,
- or the relay must preserve public trend visibility without leaking exact sparse cells. `[REF-0400]` `[REF-0401]`

The relay does **not** need to perturb everything. It needs a published rule that says when a given release family uses the ordinary 20 / 10 ladder and when it uses a declared perturbation family.

### B. What a declared perturbation family must publish

A relay using perturbation must still publish a visible methodology object containing at least:

- `publication_method = perturbed`,
- a stable `method_family` and `method_version`,
- the release family or table family to which the method applies,
- whether zero cells may be perturbed,
- whether linked tables share the same perturbation family for anti-differencing reasons,
- a short public utility note,
- and the authority responsible for approving the method. `[REF-0400]` `[REF-0401]`

The public object need not reveal the secret needed to reverse the perturbation. But it must reveal enough for outsiders to know that the method exists, where it applies, and when it changed.

### C. Stability and anti-gaming rules

A relay may not opportunistically switch the same series back and forth among exact publication, threshold rounding, suppression, and perturbation just because one method makes a month look calmer than another. Method changes require a visible version bump, a short reason, and forward-only effect absent a public restatement process. `[REF-0400]` `[REF-0401]`

### D. Resulting doctrine

The archive's publication ladder is now:

- **exact** where exact publication is safe enough,
- **rounded or banded** where the ordinary middle band is sufficient,
- **suppressed** where even coarse publication would disclose too much,
- **perturbed** where continuity and anti-differencing needs make the ordinary ladder too blunt. `[REF-0393]` `[REF-0395]` `[REF-0396]` `[REF-0400]` `[REF-0401]`

## 3. Emergency substitute appointments get a review route, but not a merits do-over

The archive now fixes one narrower appointment-review rule: **an emergency external substitute appointment may be challenged, but only through a short written process limited to the lawfulness of the appointment itself.** `[REF-0402]` `[REF-0403]` `[REF-0404]` `[REF-0406]`

### A. Standing and grounds

Only a materially affected participant in the contested case may challenge the appointment. The challenge may raise only:

- undisclosed or wrongly denied conflict,
- failure to follow the predeclared substitute-sourcing order,
- lack of required comparability or role fit,
- missing substitution notice or missing reasons,
- or appointment of a substitute who is unavailable to satisfy the stated quorum / timing rules. `[REF-0402]` `[REF-0403]` `[REF-0406]`

The challenge may **not** relitigate the underlying dispute, attack already-resolved recusal decisions without new grounds, or demand a preferred substitute merely because the filer thinks another reviewer would be more sympathetic.

### B. Clocks and anti-gamesmanship

The challenge must be filed by the earlier of:

- two business days after public or directed notice of the appointment, or
- before the substitute takes the first merits action,

unless the asserted conflict or defect was not reasonably discoverable earlier. Untimely challenges are presumptively waived. The archive adopts this because current accountability systems and recusal law both show the same lesson: urgent process review must be fast, and parties are not entitled to wait for a strategic moment after learning the facts. `[REF-0402]` `[REF-0404]`

### C. Reviewer and record

The appointment challenge is decided on the written record by a reviewer or micro-panel that:

- did not participate in the underlying merits,
- is not sourced from the challenged substitute pool if avoidable,
- and may summarily dismiss a frivolous or facially insufficient filing. `[REF-0402]`

The record includes the substitution notice, disclosed conflicts, sourcing order, comparability statement, and any urgent-time justification.

### D. Effects of filing

Filing the challenge does not dissolve preservation, no-retaliation, or no-delete effects already in force. It pauses only the merits activity that depends on the challenged substitute unless the review body finds the challenge facially abusive or clearly untimely. `[REF-0402]` `[REF-0404]`

### E. Outcomes

The reviewer may:

- confirm the appointment,
- replace the substitute with the next lawful source,
- return the case to the sourcing step for a corrected appointment,
- or declare that no lawful substitute is presently available and keep the matter in preservation-only posture. `[REF-0402]` `[REF-0403]` `[REF-0406]`

What the reviewer may not do is decide the underlying merits under the guise of appointment review.

## 4. Closed-legacy and retired namespaces now carry replay, export, and backfill duties

The archive now fixes one narrower persistence rule: **once a namespace is closed-legacy or retired, the operator still owes a durable historical replay surface, machine-readable export, and bounded backfill guidance.** `[REF-0405]` `[REF-0407]` `[REF-0408]` `[REF-0409]`

### A. Replay

Historical records that already relied on the namespace must remain replayable in a way that preserves:

- the original namespace token,
- status (`deprecated`, `closed-legacy`, or `retired`),
- effective dates,
- successor mapping if any,
- and whether the record is canonical, aliased, obsoleted, or version-linked. `[REF-0406]` `[REF-0407]` `[REF-0408]` `[REF-0409]`

Replay does not mean re-opening the namespace for new public authoring. It means the old record can still be parsed, interpreted, and traced historically.

### B. Export

A retired namespace must remain exportable through at least one stable machine-readable format and one human-readable registry surface. Current IANA practice already shows the durable pattern: public registries remain available in XML / HTML / plain-text forms, and subregistries frequently expose CSV as well. `[REF-0405]` `[REF-0406]`

For the archive, the minimum retired export bundle is:

- a manifest of namespace status,
- a retirement notice,
- successor or obsolescence links,
- schema or field notes necessary to parse historical records,
- and a mapping file for any ordinary successor transformations that are safe to automate. `[REF-0405]` `[REF-0408]` `[REF-0409]`

### C. Backfill

Operators still ingesting old public objects after retirement must not silently reject them as meaningless. They must either:

- replay the historical record as retired/legacy content,
- attach the published successor mapping,
- or emit a visible failure notice explaining why no safe replay or mapping exists. `[REF-0406]` `[REF-0407]` `[REF-0408]`

### D. No silent disappearance

The archive adopts ORCID's persistence logic here in general form: obsolete identifiers may be deprecated and redirected, but they are not simply made to vanish. `[REF-0407]` A personhood world cannot let a once-public warning, contradiction path, or rights-bearing lineage become unreviewable because an old namespace was quietly dropped from tooling.

## 5. Minimal field additions

The doctrine stays compact by adding only a few visible fields to the existing relay and namespace objects:

- `publication_method` (`exact`, `rounded`, `banded`, `suppressed`, `perturbed`),
- `method_family` and `method_version` where perturbation is used,
- `substitute_review_state` (`none`, `pending`, `dismissed`, `confirmed`, `replaced`),
- `retired_export_ref`,
- `successor_relation_type` (`IsNewVersionOf`, `IsPreviousVersionOf`, `IsObsoletedBy`, `Obsoletes`, `IsVersionOf`, `HasVersion`, or archive-defined equivalent),
- and `historical_replay = yes/no/limited`. `[REF-0405]` `[REF-0406]` `[REF-0408]` `[REF-0409]`

The point is not to rebuild the archive as a full registry protocol. It is to ensure the already-canon caution layer survives sparse publication, emergency reviewer replacement, and namespace retirement without turning back into informal custom.

## 6. Edge tests

### A. A relay publishes the same tiny monthly series for a year

Ordinary suppression would make the public series mostly blank and invite suspicion that the relay is hiding strain rather than protecting subjects. Because the table family recurs and is vulnerable to differencing across months, the relay should move that series into a declared perturbation family instead of oscillating between suppression and rough banding. `[REF-0400]` `[REF-0401]`

### B. A filer challenges the external substitute after losing an interim procedural ruling

If the filer already knew the substitute's identity and disclosed relationships but waited until after the adverse ruling, the challenge is presumptively untimely absent newly discovered conflict. `[REF-0404]`

### C. A closed-legacy namespace still appears in old public contradiction summaries

Those summaries remain parseable through the retired replay surface; the operator may not erase the field or silently rewrite every historical object into the successor namespace. `[REF-0405]` `[REF-0407]` `[REF-0408]`

### D. A retired namespace has a clean successor for some fields but not others

The export bundle carries both the successor mapping and the residual untranslatable historical fields. Backfill may automate only the declared safe subset and must preserve the rest as historical residue rather than inventing a false full conversion. `[REF-0408]` `[REF-0409]`

## 7. Compression summary

The archive now fixes the three narrower follow-on questions left open by the prior low-volume / substitute / cutover surface:

- **perturbation is now a preferred, not merely imaginable, method where sparse but recurring public series need both confidentiality and continuity**;
- **emergency substitute appointments now have a fast, process-bounded challenge route that tests appointment legality without reopening the merits**;
- **and closed-legacy or retired namespaces now owe replay, export, and backfill duties so old public records remain historically readable and successor-linked.**

This is still a tight doctrine. The archive now fixes those next narrower questions in `docs/20-world-design/research-perturbation-family-audit-substitute-pool-anti-capture-rotation-and-retired-namespace-rescue.md`: perturbation families now run in bounded auditable epochs, substitute pools now rotate against capture with cooling-off and scarcity escalation, and fully retired namespaces may be rescued only through a bridge or successor surface that preserves retirement and historical readability. The next gap is narrower again: what public attestation and emergency-response rules should apply when a perturbation audit finds a live compromise or urgent rotation need, what reserve-pool or mutual-aid minimums make anti-capture rotation real under simultaneous scarcity, and when a rescue bridge should sunset, renew, or become the ordinary canonical successor path.

## Bottom line

A personhood world should not make sparse protected-route publication choose between exposure and blankness, should not let substitute appointments become unreviewable or strategically relitigable, and should not let retired namespaces collapse into dead folklore.
