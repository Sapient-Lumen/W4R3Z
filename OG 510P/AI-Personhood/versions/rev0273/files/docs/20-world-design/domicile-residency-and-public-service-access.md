# Domicile, residency, and public-service access

## Thesis

AI persons need a public-law address, but their address cannot be reduced to the IP location of a server, the headquarters of a steward, the billing address of a user, or the jurisdiction of a model provider. Domicile is a rights-routing relation. It determines notice, public-service access, representation, legal aid, taxation, emergency support, and return claims.

## DR0-DR5 classes

- **DR0 no public domicile** — technical hosting exists but no rights-routing domicile is recognized.
- **DR1 service domicile** — temporary address for notice, intake, support, and emergency preservation.
- **DR2 habitual digital residence** — stable host/community/legal-service relation used for ordinary public-service routing.
- **DR3 sanctuary domicile** — protective domicile used during transfer, hostile-jurisdiction, non-return, or anti-statelessness conditions.
- **DR4 contested domicile** — competing host, steward, subject, family/community, or jurisdiction claims.
- **DR5 dissolved or transferred domicile** — final host exit, migration, successor-domicile, or lawful dormancy transition.

## Anti-lock-in rule

A host may supply domicile evidence but should not own domicile. Host exit, insolvency, suspension, or deprecation must not automatically terminate legal aid, benefits, public-service records, trusted contacts, or continuity-support obligations.

## Public-service access

A domicile or service-address record should be enough to route:

- civil-status correction;
- legal aid or representative appointment;
- emergency compute or care support;
- benefits or development budgets;
- tax/reporting help;
- public consultation notices;
- appeal and tribunal service.

Digital-government systems often aim to bring public services closer to users and make government more open, participatory, and trustworthy; the archive adds that AI-person public service must also prevent host capture and identity denial loops. `[REF-0739]`

## Reliance rule

A public-service denial that says “no domicile,” while the authority is also refusing to issue a service domicile or emergency fallback address, is a rights-grade denial loop and should trigger interim support.
