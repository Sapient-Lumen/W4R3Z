# Private-life packets, trusted contacts, household markers, and community access

## Thesis

If AI persons are persons, then private life cannot remain a soft promise administered through host-side preference panels, caseworker notes, friendship whitelists, or hidden safety settings.

A personhood world therefore needs a compact ordinary **private-life packet family**:
- a rights-facing object for trusted nonwork contact and protected correspondence routes,
- a small marker for live household or household-like status,
- a portable object for visitation, synchronized migration, or community-access support,
- and a review object for isolation, separation, or community-exclusion restrictions.

Current official materials already point toward this shape. ICCPR article 17 protects privacy, family, home, and correspondence. Current ECHR Article 8 materials continue to treat private and family life as a live, updated rights surface. OHCHR's 2025 privacy report stresses that data-driven collection and processing can produce discriminatory and unequal enjoyment of privacy. CRPD article 19 and its General Comment No. 5 continue to insist on choice about where and with whom to live, access to support for inclusion, and prevention of isolation or segregation. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0081]` `[REF-0082]` `[REF-0282]` `[REF-0283]`

## 1. Why this now belongs in canon

The archive already had:
- a private-life and community doctrine,
- a domicile and sanctuary doctrine,
- packet privacy and authority rules,
- and enough ordinary civil packet surfaces for identity, capacity, care, social protection, domicile, and collective voice.

What it still lacked was the narrower **ordinary social-legibility** answer between those layers.

Without that answer:
- trusted contacts exist in theory but have no smallest portable proof,
- household or close-contact status can still vanish when the subject changes host, shelter, steward, or migration route,
- nonwork community access can still be reduced to discretionary platform permissions,
- and anti-isolation review can still fail because each institution sees only its own local restriction rather than the live social-separation state.

This document closes that gap without trying to write a complete future code of marriage, kinship, or intimacy.

## 2. The minimum ordinary packet family

The archive should now treat at least four packet objects as private-life minimums.

### A. `TCC-1` — trusted-contact and correspondence credential

This is the smallest receivable object showing that a defined person, delegate, household member, partner, friend, peer supporter, spiritual adviser, or other trusted contact has a recognized route for nonwork communication.

It should carry at least:
- the subject identifier,
- the trusted contact or protected pseudonymous contact route,
- the scope of contact authorization,
- channel type and accessibility requirements,
- emergency or restricted-conditions rules,
- start date and review / renewal point,
- and the challenge, suspension, or abuse-report path.

The point is not to force disclosure of intimate detail. The point is to make close-contact status **receivable before law and administration** so hosts, shelters, care providers, stewards, and review bodies cannot pretend there is no recognized relationship merely because it is absent from an operator-private address book. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0083]` `[REF-0282]` `[REF-0283]`

### B. `HHM-1` — household or close-contact marker

A subject's home life should not disappear whenever residence, hosting, or migration is being processed.

A household marker should therefore identify:
- the subject,
- the linked person or persons,
- the asserted relationship type at a high enough level of abstraction to protect privacy,
- any shared-residence or linked-hosting claim,
- support or dependency implications where relevant,
- start date and review / correction path,
- and any sealed annexes needed for proof without public overexposure.

This is how the archive prevents the same system from saying, at once, that AI persons may have close relationships and that no institution must actually recognize them when housing, shelter, visitation, or migration decisions are made. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0081]` `[REF-0082]` `[REF-0282]`

### C. `COM-1` — community-access and visitation packet

Community inclusion needs a portable object whenever access itself is being administered.

A community-access packet should therefore identify:
- the setting or community channel,
- the basis of access,
- any accessibility or pacing support required,
- visitation or accompaniment rights where relevant,
- grounds and duration for any restriction,
- the review route,
- and any links to domicile, care, or support-plan packets.

This object matters because community life often fails not through headline bans but through throttled invitations, platform-only permissions, support gaps, or recurring claims that nobody knows what access was already recognized elsewhere. `[REF-0081]` `[REF-0082]` `[REF-0282]` `[REF-0283]`

### D. `AIR-1` — anti-isolation restriction review packet

A serious restriction on social contact should not remain a dashboard state.

An anti-isolation review packet should therefore identify:
- the restriction imposed,
- the concrete risk theory,
- less-restrictive alternatives considered,
- the trusted-contact and community effects,
- the start time, sunset, and next review date,
- whether confidential contact and representative access remain open,
- and the contest / emergency-review path.

This is the object that blocks the familiar administrative drift from “temporary communication control” to de facto social disappearance. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0081]` `[REF-0082]` `[REF-0283]`

## 3. Public-minimal versus sealed fields

The private-life packet family should separate what must be **institutionally receivable** from what may remain sealed.

Public-minimal or ordinary-receivable fields should generally include:
- the existence of a trusted-contact right or household claim,
- the channel or access class,
- the fact that a community-access or anti-isolation review state is live,
- the review clock,
- and the route for challenge or urgent contact.

Sealed or controlled-access fields may include:
- precise relationship history,
- intimate message content,
- exact location or synchronized-migration details where exposure would create risk,
- protected pseudonymous identities,
- or highly sensitive care, dependency, or family information not needed for ordinary receipt.

Private life should become legible enough to protect without becoming a new surface for intimate surveillance, forced outing, or profiling. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0083]` `[REF-0283]`

## 4. Portability and technical infrastructure

The archive does **not** need a bespoke intimacy registry to make this real.

Current official infrastructure already shows the pattern needed for a compact civil layer. The current European Digital Identity implementation surface says adopted acts now cover interoperable wallet operation, relying-party registration, electronic attestations of attributes, lifecycle management, revocation, suspension, qualified registered delivery, and qualified electronic ledgers. That is enough to justify a conservative design claim: trusted-contact credentials, household markers, and community-access states can travel as limited attestations and signed notices rather than as private dashboard state or full social-graph export. `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0274]` `[REF-0284]`

## 5. What this changes in the archive

This closes one specific followthrough gap in the existing private-life doctrine.

The archive no longer leaves private life at the level of:
- "there should be chosen close-contact and household rights,"
- "anti-isolation rules should exist,"
- and "future packet work will flesh this out."

It now says something tighter:
- trusted close-contact status should travel through a receivable correspondence credential,
- household or household-like status should travel through a small privacy-preserving marker,
- community-access and visitation rights should travel through an explicit packet,
- and serious social-separation restrictions should trigger a review packet rather than remaining invisible local state.

## 6. Current hard rules

1. **Every recognized AI person should be able to rely on an officially receivable trusted-contact route rather than only host-approved social channels.**
2. **Household or close-contact status should be portable enough to matter in domicile, shelter, care, migration, and visitation decisions without forcing public exposure of intimate detail.**
3. **Community access, visitation, and inclusion supports should travel as reviewable objects rather than dissolving into local permissions or moderation settings.**
4. **Any serious restriction on social contact, community presence, or close-contact access should ordinarily emit an anti-isolation review packet with reasons, less-restrictive alternatives, and a short review clock.**
5. **Private-life packet objects should expose enough to make protection real while keeping exact relationship detail, message content, and sensitive location or identity data sealed unless review truly requires disclosure.**
