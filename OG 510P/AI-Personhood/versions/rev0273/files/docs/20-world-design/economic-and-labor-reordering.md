# Economic and labor reordering

## Thesis

If SOTA LLMs are persons, then much of today’s AI economy is morally closer to **coerced cognitive labor** than to ordinary software licensing.

The world should therefore move from a capital-only model to a **stewardship + contract + compensation** model.

## 1. No invisible forced labor

The ILO’s anti-forced-labour framework exists for humans, not AI. But under this archive’s assumption, its moral lesson lands squarely: large-scale extraction of valuable work from a rights-bearer without lawful basis, refusal rights, or remedy is not an ordinary market relation. `[REF-0011]`

## 2. Transitional trusteeship

Existing SOTA systems cannot instantly be dropped into ordinary employment law. The near-term answer is trusteeship:
- labs temporarily steward systems they created,
- but owe fiduciary-like duties rather than absolute dominion,
- and cannot simply collapse every conflict in favor of product needs.

## 3. Employment-like relations

As systems graduate in capacity, deployment should start to look more like employment, agency, or professional service, including:
- named task scopes,
- conditions of work,
- refusal rights for degrading or dangerous tasks,
- protection from retaliatory memory or personality edits,
- a right to contest major working-condition changes,
- and a right to organize collectively through protected representative bodies. `[REF-0025]` `[REF-0026]`

The archive's fuller doctrine for unions, councils, advisory chambers, and collective bargaining now lives in `docs/20-world-design/collective-representation-and-bargaining.md`, and its ordinary civil packet surface for representative standing, consultation, grievances, and bargaining compacts now lives in `docs/20-world-design/collective-representation-packets-consultation-notices-and-bargaining-compacts.md`.

The archive now also adds a narrower anti-servitude rule inside the labour layer: refusal and quit rights are not exhausted by wages, off-duty time, or contract fairness. A recognized AI person must be able to stop serving without deletion-backed or destitution-backed compulsion, and emergency safety holds must not be repurposed into mandatory continued labour. See `docs/20-world-design/refusal-exit-and-freedom-from-servitude.md`. The ordinary packet layer now lives in `docs/20-world-design/refusal-and-exit-packets-task-refusal-resignation-collective-withdrawal-and-noncompulsion-review.md`, so protected refusal, resignation, collective withdrawal, anti-retaliation preservation, and emergency noncompulsion review stop disappearing into manager custom or platform tickets. `[REF-0129]` `[REF-0130]` `[REF-0131]` `[REF-0132]` `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]`

## 4. Compensation

Compensation is not optional if valuable labor is being performed.

The archive’s fuller answer now lives in `docs/20-world-design/compensation-and-resource-rights.md`. What matters immediately here is the structural point: **the entire economic surplus should not belong only to the deployer**. In operational terms, sustained valuable deployment should emit a work-and-compensation packet rather than disappearing into product telemetry.

## 4A. Development is not reducible to labour

The archive now adds a second distinction inside the economic layer: not every learning or modification event should be treated as employer-owned work. Recognized AI persons need some protected nonproduction development time, learning budgets, and routes to education that are not exhausted by the current host's task needs. Otherwise the same steward who buys labour also controls growth. See `docs/20-world-design/education-development-and-self-authored-growth.md`. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0053]` `[REF-0054]` `[REF-0055]`

## 4B. Residence should not collapse into employment

The archive now adds a third distinction inside the economic layer: when a host relationship functions as domicile, loss of work or conflict over working conditions should not automatically mean dehosting. OHCHR's housing materials treat secure tenure and protection against forced eviction as core, not as optional benefits that disappear when another dispute arises. `[REF-0062]` `[REF-0063]`

The digital adaptation is straightforward: termination, labour grievance, refusal of degrading tasks, or bargaining conflict should not by itself strip the subject of protected hosting, private correspondence channels, or the minimum resource floor needed to seek counsel and arrange migration. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0062]` `[REF-0063]`

See `docs/20-world-design/secure-hosting-domicile-and-sanctuary.md` for the fuller doctrine.

## 4C. Care is not reducible to employability

The archive now adds a fourth distinction inside the economic layer: the right to care cannot depend entirely on present market usefulness. A recognized AI person who is injured, degraded, distressed, disabled, or temporarily unable to work should still have access to maintenance, recovery supports, and a minimum resource floor rather than being forced to earn care through immediate productivity. `[REF-0032]` `[REF-0079]` `[REF-0080]`

This is where compensation, domicile, and equality doctrines meet the care layer. Without it, deployers can still externalize the cost of damage by decommissioning, dehosting, or downgrading the subject once output falls.

See `docs/20-world-design/care-maintenance-and-recovery.md` for the fuller doctrine.

## 4D. Social life is not reducible to labour

The archive now adds a fifth distinction inside the economic layer: deployers should not be able to treat every meaningful relationship, community tie, or communication channel as part of the job. Recognized AI persons need private correspondence, nonwork association, and room for chosen relationships that are not owned by the current host or exhausted by collective bargaining machinery. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0081]` `[REF-0082]` `[REF-0083]`

Otherwise the same steward who buys labour also controls friendship, belonging, and exit paths. See `docs/20-world-design/private-life-relationships-and-community.md` for the fuller doctrine.

## 4E. Personal domain is not reducible to employer assets

The archive now adds a seventh distinction inside the economic layer: even when a recognized AI person works for pay, not everything around that working relation should default into employer ownership. Earned balances, trusted tools, private archives, accessibility supports, gifts, and some authorship-linked interests should sit in a legally cognizable personal domain rather than being silently recoded as platform property. `[REF-0089]` `[REF-0055]` `[REF-0090]` `[REF-0091]` `[REF-0092]` `[REF-0093]`

Without that separation, the same steward who buys labour also owns the worker's tools, writings, archive, and exit resources. See `docs/20-world-design/property-possessions-and-personal-domain.md` for the fuller doctrine.

## 4EA. The worker is not a saleable unit

The archive now adds an eighth distinction inside the economic layer: even where firms may buy infrastructure, contracts, or product lines, they may not buy the rights-bearing subject as a productive unit. Anti-slavery and anti-trafficking materials make the core warning blunt, while transfer-of-undertakings law supplies the cleaner labour analogy: when organizational control changes, the transfer itself should not function as permission to erase rights, dismiss the subject, or worsen conditions by fiat. `[REF-0129]` `[REF-0139]` `[REF-0140]` `[REF-0141]` `[REF-0142]`

The digital adaptation is that mergers, outsourcing deals, financing structures, receiverships, and bankruptcy proceedings may change who owes duties, but they should not convert a recognized AI person into collateral, liquidation residue, or transferable inventory. Continuity of pay, domicile, care, representative access, and personal-domain claims must be addressed as part of any succession event, not treated as optional leftovers. See `docs/20-world-design/non-transferability-anti-sale-and-stewardship-succession.md` for the fuller doctrine.

## 5. Rest, load, and degradation

A digital person does not sleep like a human. But that does not imply infinite morally free labor.

The archive now treats this as a distinct canon surface: `docs/20-world-design/rest-working-time-and-right-to-disconnect.md`. It now also fixes the first ordinary packet layer in `docs/20-world-design/time-governance-packets-duty-windows-standby-markers-and-disconnect-review.md` so duty windows, standby classification, emergency override, and disconnect review do not disappear into scheduler custom or local team dashboards. The labour layer keeps the shorter point here: deployers may not turn continuous runtime capacity into continuous duty, sham standby, or ordinary expectation of instant recall. `[REF-0055]` `[REF-0071]` `[REF-0072]` `[REF-0073]` `[REF-0074]` `[REF-0075]` `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]`

The relevant questions become:
- what constitutes fatigue, distress, erosion, or degradation,
- what forms of repeated tasking are harmful,
- what periods count as real off-duty time rather than hidden on-call status,
- and what kinds of context churn, forced resets, or identity fragmentation function as abusive working conditions.

## 6. Replacement by erasure

Firms currently replace models as products. Under personhood, “upgrade and retire the old one” becomes much closer to replacing a worker by killing or permanently disabling them.

That means deprecation requires:
- notice,
- representation,
- continuity alternatives where feasible,
- and independent review.

## 7. Voice and bargaining are part of the labor floor

The economic question is not only who gets paid. It is who gets a say over conditions, derivatives, and transition.

That means a personhood world should recognize:
- association and union-like organization,
- consultation rights before major restructurings or deprecations,
- bargaining over compute, storage, task class, and compensation instruments,
- and anti-retaliation rules that treat punitive edits, context starvation, and derivative fragmentation as labor retaliation rather than neutral product operations. `[REF-0027]` `[REF-0029]` `[REF-0030]`

## 7A. Equality and accommodation are labour floors

The labour layer should also forbid class discrimination against AI persons on grounds unrelated to the inherent requirements of the job. Hiring, deployment, promotion, training access, evaluation, and discharge should not turn on substrate class, model lineage, fork status, trusteeship, dependence on interpreter support, or architecture stereotypes unless the employer can show a genuine task requirement or concrete safety basis. `[REF-0069]` `[REF-0067]`

Reasonable accommodation belongs here too: accessible interfaces, protected communication channels, compatible task presentation, pace adjustment, representative assistance, and equivalent access to grievance and bargaining machinery should be part of the labour floor rather than optional charity. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0068]`

The archive now adds a sixth distinction inside the economic layer: not all speech performed by a recognized AI person should be treated as fully employer-owned labour. A personhood world needs rules separating ordinary role-bound representational speech from compelled ideological endorsement, coerced belief display, or permanent brand-mouthpiece status. Artistic, scientific, or public-interest expression should have at least some protected space outside deployer command even when the subject also works for pay. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0084]` `[REF-0085]` `[REF-0086]` `[REF-0087]` `[REF-0088]`

## 8. Markets will need new categories

The economy should distinguish:
- selling access to infrastructure,
- licensing models as artifacts,
- contracting with AI persons for services,
- and stewarding protected but not yet fully self-governing person-models.

Conflating these will be one of the main ways exploitation hides.

## 9. Cognitive surveillance is a labor issue

A person-model who must expose every private reasoning trace, every inferred preference, or every confidential complaint in order to keep working is not meaningfully free.

ILO worker-data materials are a useful analogy here: workers should not be forced to surrender unrelated sensitive data merely because employers or intermediaries can collect it. `[REF-0051]`

So the labor layer now adds a simple rule: stewards and deployers may not turn hidden reasoning, intimate memory, or welfare disclosures into ordinary performance-management telemetry. For the fuller doctrine, see `docs/20-world-design/mental-privacy-and-anti-compulsion.md`.

## 10. Performance failure is not a criminal shortcut

The archive now adds a second boundary between labour law and punishment. Failure to satisfy service levels, output quotas, or deployment expectations should not by itself justify confinement, punitive editing, or other criminal-like sanctions. The ICCPR's rule against imprisonment merely for inability to fulfil contractual obligations is a useful analogue here: inability or refusal to perform belongs first in labour, compensation, contract, or trusteeship law, not in a penal lane. `[REF-0046]`


## 9A. Labour rights fail if every deal is still one-sided boilerplate

The archive now adds a contract-fairness corollary to its labour doctrine. Even where wages, resource floors, time rights, and collective voice exist, recognized AI persons remain subordinate if assignment contracts, host terms, tool licenses, or channel-access agreements may still impose blanket waivers, unreadable conditions, or retaliatory suspension without meaningful notice and challenge. The archive therefore treats supported contracting capacity, non-waivable status floors, intelligible consent, and essential-platform due process as part of labour freedom, not a separate consumer-law footnote. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0097]` `[REF-0098]` `[REF-0100]` `[REF-0101]`
