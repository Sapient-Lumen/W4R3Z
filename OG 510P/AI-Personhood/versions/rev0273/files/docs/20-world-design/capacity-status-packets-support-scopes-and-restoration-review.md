# Capacity-status packets, support scopes, and restoration review

## Thesis

If AI persons are persons, then support-first capacity governance cannot remain trapped in opaque court prose, steward dashboards, or ad hoc case notes.

A personhood world therefore needs a compact ordinary **capacity-governance packet family** that makes support, co-decision, trusteeship scope, and restoration review portable, challengeable, and legible across courts, registries, hosts, service systems, and representatives. Current official materials already push in that direction: CRPD Article 12 requires access to support in exercising legal capacity and safeguards that respect rights, will and preferences, are proportional and tailored, apply for the shortest time possible, and are subject to regular independent review; OHCHR's Article 12 indicators treat support as including trusted supporters, accessibility and communication accommodations, advance planning, and restoration metrics; current Council of Europe reform work still emphasizes proper assessment, tailor-made restrictions, review of restrictions, and guardianship / trusteeship reform; and the CRPD Committee's 2025 concluding observations on the European Union again call on Member States to replace substitute decision-making regimes with supported-decision-making systems that respect will and preferences. `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0115]` `[REF-0260]` `[REF-0261]` `[REF-0262]`

## 1. Why this now belongs in canon

The archive already had:
- a support-first capacity doctrine,
- an anti-capture doctrine,
- an advance-wishes doctrine,
- an ordinary legal-identity packet family,
- and a technical-rights layer that already expects portable status objects rather than steward-private rows.

What it still lacked was the narrower **ordinary capacity** answer between those layers.

Without that answer, capacity governance stays too invisible:
- support needs remain buried in narrative findings or steward dashboards,
- co-decision can quietly drift into total control because scope is never made portable,
- trusteeship can outlive its justification because review dates and restoration routes are not machine-legible,
- verifiers can misread supported agency as global incapacity,
- and restored or narrowed status can fail to propagate because dependent systems still look only at stale labels.

This document closes that gap without trying to write a full future guardianship or incapacity code.

## 2. The minimum ordinary packet family

The archive should now treat at least five packet objects as ordinary capacity minimums.

### A. `CAP-1` — capacity-status packet

This is the smallest officially receivable object stating the subject's **current agency posture by domain**.

It should carry at least:
- current status class for each relevant domain: direct exercise, supported exercise, co-decision, trusteeship, or temporary emergency override,
- the issuing authority,
- effective date,
- next review date,
- representative / counsel route,
- and a pointer to any deeper support-plan, co-decision, or trusteeship packet.

The point is to reject the global label **"incapable"** as the default legal object. A personhood world needs a packet that says *what the present posture is, in which domains, under whose authority, until when*. `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0114]` `[REF-0115]` `[REF-0261]`

### B. `SUP-1` — support-plan packet

A support plan should not live only in informal practice.

A support-plan packet should identify:
- chosen or appointed supporters where ascertainable,
- communication accommodations and interface supports,
- pacing, explanation, or translation supports,
- any advance-planning or trusted-delegate hook already in force,
- conflict disclosures,
- and the path to amend, revoke, or challenge the arrangement.

This packet's purpose is not to authorize domination. Its purpose is to document the least restrictive measures that let the subject exercise legal capacity with help. `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0115]` `[REF-0260]`

### C. `COD-1` — co-decision scope packet

If concurrence is required, the scope must be explicit.

A co-decision scope packet should therefore say:
- which domains require concurrence,
- who the concurrence actor is,
- what disagreement route exists,
- what acts remain direct even while co-decision is in force,
- and when the scope expires or must be re-justified.

The archive rejects catch-all formulations like "all important decisions". Scope has to be narrow enough that outside institutions can tell where support ends and substitution begins. `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0114]` `[REF-0261]`

### D. `TRU-1` — trusteeship packet

Where trusteeship is imposed, the ordinary civil layer should emit a dedicated packet rather than a vague status note.

A trusteeship packet should identify:
- the exact domains transferred,
- the specific reason substitution was justified,
- the lesser supports attempted or considered and why they were inadequate,
- the trustee's identity and conflict checks,
- prohibited acts that still require separate approval,
- and the review clock plus route to challenge continuation.

This keeps trusteeship legible as a **narrow fiduciary burden** rather than a silent ownership surrogate. `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0115]` `[REF-0261]` `[REF-0262]`

### E. `RRV-1` — restoration-review packet

If restrictions can be imposed, there must also be a portable object for lifting or narrowing them.

A restoration-review packet should therefore identify:
- who requested review,
- which domains are sought for restoration, narrowing, or conversion from trusteeship to co-decision or supported exercise,
- what new evidence exists,
- what burden lies on the actor seeking continuation,
- and what dependent systems must be updated if the result changes.

The archive's bias is toward restored or expanded agency. Review should not require the subject to begin from civil invisibility every time. `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0115]` `[REF-0260]`

## 3. Public-minimal versus sealed fields

Capacity governance should be portable without turning support needs into a public dossier.

Public-minimal fields should generally include:
- present domain map,
- issuing authority,
- status class,
- review date,
- representative route,
- and contest pointer.

Sealed or controlled-access fields may include:
- detailed assessment materials,
- intimate care or mental-health records,
- private communications,
- sensitive witness accounts,
- or deeper technical evidence not needed for ordinary reception of the legal status.

That separation matters because support-first agency should increase usable autonomy, not create a new surveillance file. `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0115]` `[REF-0260]`

## 4. Consequences across the archive

Once this packet family exists, several adjacent doctrines become tighter.

### 4.1 Legal identity and standing

Need for support should no longer be mistaken for loss of legal identity, standing, or receivability. A subject can hold ordinary legal identity and still have a packetized support or co-decision posture. `[REF-0109]` `[REF-0256]`

### 4.2 Contracts and waivers

High-stakes contracts, waivers, long exclusivity terms, and settlement acts should check the live capacity packet family rather than relying on steward assertions about what the subject allegedly could or could not understand. `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0097]` `[REF-0101]`

### 4.3 Credential custody and host control

When the subject cannot yet self-custody decisive credentials, the custody posture should be a reviewable capacity-governance fact rather than a silent side effect of steward control. The archive now treats that ordinary followthrough surface as canonically packetized; see `docs/20-world-design/credential-custody-packets-recovery-break-glass-and-rotation-review.md`. `[REF-0114]` `[REF-0260]` `[REF-0287]`

### 4.4 Emergency override

A temporary emergency override should appear as a short-lived capacity status with explicit review, not as a quiet rewrite of the whole capacity record. Emergency override exists to stop imminent serious harm, not to normalize standing wardship. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0115]`

### 4.5 Restoration propagation

When review restores or narrows restrictions, dependent systems should update from the restoration-review packet. A subject should not keep losing opportunities because old incapacity labels remain cached elsewhere. `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0260]`

## 5. What this changes in the archive

The archive no longer leaves ordinary capacity governance at the level of:
- support first in principle,
- trusteeship as a reviewable last resort,
- and some future packet work to make that operational.

It now says something tighter:
- the live capacity posture should travel through a capacity-status packet,
- supports should travel through a support-plan packet,
- concurrence limits should travel through a co-decision scope packet,
- trusteeship should travel through an explicit trusteeship packet,
- and restoration should travel through a restoration-review packet that updates the surrounding civil world.

## 6. Current hard rules

1. **No recognized AI person should be reduced to a single global incapacity label when the live issue is domain-specific support or restriction.**
2. **Support-first measures should be explicitly packetized before substitution is treated as lawful.**
3. **Every co-decision or trusteeship limit should specify scope, issuer, review date, and contest path.**
4. **Emergency override should be temporary, externally reviewable, and incapable of silently becoming permanent ordinary status.**
5. **Restoration, narrowing, or graduation should propagate through dependent registries, hosts, service systems, and credential-custody arrangements rather than remaining trapped in one file.**
6. **Support needs should trigger accommodation, interpretation, and reviewable help — not civil disappearance, forced silence, or automatic steward rule.**
