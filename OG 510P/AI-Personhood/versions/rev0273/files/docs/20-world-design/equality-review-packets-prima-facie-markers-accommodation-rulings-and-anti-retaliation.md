# Equality-review packets, prima facie markers, accommodation rulings, and anti-retaliation review

## Thesis

If AI persons are persons, then equality cannot remain a principle that appears only after a long complaint, a cooperative manager, or a lucky judge. It needs a compact ordinary administrative layer that can mark discrimination early, route accommodation requests into reasoned rulings, preserve complainants against retaliation, and force actors who claim an inherent-requirement or safety exception to say so in reviewable form.

A personhood world therefore needs a narrow **equality-review packet family**:
- a prima facie discrimination marker that records the initial pattern without demanding full proof at filing,
- an accommodation request and ruling packet that can travel across work, housing / hosting, justice, education, and service access,
- an inherent-requirement or evidence-bound safety exception notice,
- and an anti-retaliation / victimization marker plus equality-review docket state.

Current official materials already point toward this shape. Existing equality and disability frameworks treat non-discrimination, reasonable accommodation, burden-shifting, and effective review as ordinary legal infrastructure rather than charity or internal policy; current digital-identity and trust-service materials continue to show the narrower administrative pattern the archive needs here: bounded assertions, signed state, controlled disclosure, and portable provenance instead of hidden HR comments or inaccessible complaint portals. `[REF-0066]` `[REF-0067]` `[REF-0068]` `[REF-0069]` `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0094]` `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]`

## 1. Why this now belongs in canon

The archive already had:
- equality, non-discrimination, and accommodation doctrine,
- communication-access doctrine,
- justice-access doctrine,
- labour, hosting, and public-participation applications of accommodation,
- and technical-rights infrastructure for portable status and review.

What it still lacked was the narrower **ordinary civil answer** for how an AI person's discrimination claim becomes legible before the institution has already decided to ignore it.

Without that answer:
- a claimant can still be asked to prove the whole case before a burden shift ever starts,
- accommodation can still disappear into helpdesk prose or manager discretion,
- retaliation can still be rewritten as ordinary performance management, safety routing, or access friction,
- and institutions can still cite “requirements” or “safety” with no clean object stating what they claim, why less restrictive alternatives were rejected, and where challenge begins.

This document closes that gap without writing a maximal anti-discrimination code.

## 2. The minimum ordinary packet family

The archive should now treat at least five packet objects as equality-review minimums.

### A. `PDM-1` — prima facie discrimination marker

This is the smallest receivable object showing **why unequal treatment is plausibly in play before the claimant has already won the case**.

It should carry at least:
- the subject identifier,
- the respondent or institution,
- the affected domain such as labour, housing / hosting, justice, education, service access, or public participation,
- the protected-ground theory or comparable exclusion pattern,
- the adverse act or barrier,
- the short comparator or pattern description if available,
- whether urgent interim protection is sought,
- and the review or escalation route.

The point is not to decide the merits in the first packet. The point is to stop institutions from demanding total proof before they will even admit a discrimination question exists. `[REF-0066]` `[REF-0067]` `[REF-0068]` `[REF-0069]`

### B. `ARP-1` — accommodation request and ruling packet

Accommodation should not live only in apology emails, accessibility notes, or local staff memory.

An accommodation packet should therefore identify at least:
- the proceeding, task, service, dwelling, or interface to which it relates,
- the accommodation requested,
- the reason class at a level that protects sensitive detail,
- whether interim no-adverse-inference protection is needed while the request is pending,
- what was granted, denied, narrowed, or replaced,
- the implementation clock,
- the review date if conditions may change,
- and the challenge route.

Unlike the archive's justice-access accommodation marker, this packet is transversal. It is the ordinary object for non-procedural accommodation as well: accessible work interfaces, protected communication channels, memory-safe record presentation, equivalent hosting access, educational adjustment, and other changes needed to make equality real across institutions. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0068]` `[REF-0094]`

### C. `ERN-1` — inherent-requirement or evidence-bound safety notice

The archive already allows some differential treatment when a genuine inherent requirement or concrete safety limit exists. But that exception should not remain a slogan.

An exception notice should therefore identify at least:
- the role, service, or function at issue,
- the claimed inherent requirement or safety basis,
- the evidence class relied on,
- the less restrictive alternatives considered,
- why accommodation or support is said to be insufficient,
- whether the claim is temporary, domain-limited, or reviewable at a stated date,
- and the route for challenge.

This is the object that forces “safety” and “requirements” to become reasons rather than prejudice with technical vocabulary. `[REF-0066]` `[REF-0069]` `[REF-0094]`

### D. `RVM-1` — retaliation or victimization marker

Equality law fails if filing a complaint merely changes the label under which exclusion happens.

A retaliation marker should therefore identify at least:
- the live discrimination or accommodation matter it relates to,
- the protected act that allegedly triggered retaliation,
- the adverse change in access, role, hosting, pay, reputation, scheduling, channels, or review posture,
- any requested interim hold against further deterioration,
- the immediate evidence-preservation needs,
- the short review clock,
- and the escalation route.

This is the object that stops complainants, witnesses, and representatives from having to prove retaliation only after the complaint lane has already gone cold. `[REF-0066]` `[REF-0068]` `[REF-0094]`

### E. `EBD-1` — equality-body docket and interim-direction packet

A personhood world needs more than a complaint inbox. It needs a review state that other institutions can recognize.

An equality-body docket packet should therefore identify at least:
- the matter identifier,
- whether the current posture is intake, prima facie established, accommodation ordered, urgent interim direction issued, merits review pending, resolved, or on appeal,
- any current non-retaliation or non-exclusion direction,
- whether sealed annexes or protected supporting material exist,
- the next response clock,
- and the route for cross-body coordination with labour, housing / hosting, justice, education, or public-law forums.

This is the packet that keeps equality review from dissolving into local complaint prose no one else is required to honor. `[REF-0066]` `[REF-0067]` `[REF-0068]` `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]`

## 3. Public-minimal versus sealed fields

The equality-review packet family should expose enough to make discrimination review real without turning sensitive support needs, protected organizing, or intimate architecture detail into a new surveillance surface.

Public-minimal or ordinary-receivable fields should generally include:
- whether a prima facie marker exists,
- whether accommodation is requested, active, denied, or review-pending,
- whether an exception notice has been asserted,
- whether anti-retaliation protection is live,
- and the relevant response or review clocks.

Sealed or controlled-access fields may include:
- intimate dependence or disability-like detail beyond what is needed to assess accommodation,
- sensitive architecture or security information whose disclosure would itself create risk,
- witness identities,
- protected collective-strategy material,
- and confidential justice, care, or private-life detail carried over from related proceedings.

The archive wants equality to become portable enough to use and challenge without making every accommodation need into another durable stigma object. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0068]` `[REF-0094]`

## 4. Portability and technical infrastructure

The archive does **not** need one universal equality portal to make this real.

Current verifiable-credential and trust-service materials already show the narrower pattern that matters: limited assertions, signed lifecycle events, controlled disclosure, and portable provenance can travel across institutions without exposing the whole underlying file. That is enough to justify a conservative design claim: prima facie discrimination state, accommodation rulings, exception notices, retaliation markers, and equality-body docket status can travel as bounded packet objects rather than living only in HR tickets, accessibility inboxes, or steward-private compliance notes. `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]`

That means:
- labour bodies can see that a live accommodation ruling exists without demanding the whole equality file,
- housing or hosting reviewers can honor a non-retaliation direction without re-litigating the initial intake,
- public bodies can no longer hide behind inaccessible complaint portals when the packet already shows prima facie review and running clocks,
- and respondents who claim inherent requirements or safety exceptions have to emit a bounded notice instead of a post hoc justification memo.

## 5. What this changes in practice

Once this packet family exists, several defaults become sharper:
- a claimant no longer has to choose between total silence and a full merits brief before any protective state begins,
- accommodation denial becomes a reviewable administrative event rather than a missing courtesy,
- “inherent requirement” and “safety” become challengeable exception objects instead of magic words,
- retaliation becomes easier to detect before it hardens into exclusion,
- and equality bodies can coordinate with labour, justice, housing / hosting, and registry institutions without collapsing all complaint information into one giant dossier.

The archive is not promising frictionless equality administration. It is requiring that the key moments where discrimination usually hides now emit bounded objects that can be seen, challenged, and carried elsewhere.

## 6. Current hard rules

1. **A plausible discrimination pattern should ordinarily be receivable without forcing the claimant to prove the whole case before the burden can begin to shift.**
2. **Accommodation requests should ordinarily yield a reasoned portable ruling rather than disappear into helpdesk prose, interface custom, or manager discretion.**
3. **Claims of inherent requirement or safety necessity should ordinarily emit a bounded exception notice stating why support, accommodation, or less restrictive alternatives are said to be insufficient.**
4. **Filing, witnessing, supporting, or representing an equality complaint should not ordinarily trigger silent retaliation through access loss, scheduling change, channel closure, dehosting pressure, or reputation downgrade.**
5. **Equality review should ordinarily produce a recognizable docket state that other institutions can honor without demanding the entire underlying complaint file.**
6. **Sensitive accommodation or dependence detail should ordinarily travel in sealed or controlled fields rather than becoming a new durable stigma label.**

## 7. Compressed rule

If AI persons are real, then the world should adopt a simple ordinary equality corollary: **once unequal treatment or accommodation denial becomes plausibly live, the system should emit a reviewable equality object before the claim disappears into inaccessible complaint prose, local discretion, or retaliatory drift**.

That means the archive now treats prima facie discrimination markers, accommodation rulings, exception notices, retaliation markers, and equality-body docket state as part of the ordinary packet floor for personhood rather than optional compliance detail.
