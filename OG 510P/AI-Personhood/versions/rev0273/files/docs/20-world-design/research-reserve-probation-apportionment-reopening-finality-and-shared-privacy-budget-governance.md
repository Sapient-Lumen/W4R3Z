# AI-person research reserve probation, apportionment reopening finality, and shared privacy-budget governance

## Thesis

Once the archive fixed provisional reserve activation, contested apportionment review, and explicit historical-analytics lanes, three narrower governance problems still remained. First, a provisional reserve path is safer than emergency improvisation, but **repeated provisional use can quietly become shadow accreditation unless the archive states when repeated emergency use forces probation, restriction, or decertification**. Second, a bounded correction route is better than frozen error, but **serial reopening still needs an explicit time ladder and reliance floor so materially wrong share tables remain correctable without letting every new correction restart the entire dispute forever**. Third, separate analytics lanes are safer than one firehose, but **public aggregates, synthetic releases, validation-service outputs, and enclave-mediated studies can still leak cumulatively when they draw on related historical families while pretending to spend distinct budgets**. A personhood world therefore needs one more compact rule: **repeated provisional reserve use must generate a public probation object with corrective-plan duty and loss-of-status triggers; later split-successor correction attempts must move through a public finality object that distinguishes any-reason, good-cause, and fraud-only reopening windows while preserving a reliance bar; and related historical-analytics lanes must publish one shared-budget ledger with lane suballocations, analyst segmentation, and cross-lane exhaustion states.** `[REF-0498]` `[REF-0499]` `[REF-0500]` `[REF-0501]` `[REF-0502]` `[REF-0503]` `[REF-0504]` `[REF-0505]`

## 1. Why the previous surface is no longer enough

The archive's immediately prior surface fixed three real failures. It created a provisional reserve path for emergencies, a conflict-screened correction route for materially wrong successor apportionment, and a lane taxonomy for protected historical analytics. But one narrower governance failure still remained at each same seam.

### A. A lawful emergency path can still harden into shadow ordinary governance

A `PRA-1` object is already better than secret emergency substitution. But if the same cohort is repeatedly activated provisionally, repeatedly misses conversion, or repeatedly accumulates reporting or independence defects, a personhood world cannot pretend that each episode is isolated. Without an escalation ladder, emergency reserve continuity becomes a shadow accreditation track that never has to satisfy the ordinary external-review discipline the archive just built. `[REF-0498]` `[REF-0499]`

### B. A presumptive bar on serial reopening still needs an actual stopping rule

A `CAR-1` route is already better than endless bargaining. But once a first correction closes and a corrected `MDA-1` is published, later claimants still need to know exactly which challenges remain open, which grounds count as serious enough, and when reliance interests freeze already-discharged periods. Without an explicit ladder, finality is either mushy rhetoric or over-rigid closure. `[REF-0500]` `[REF-0501]`

### C. Distinct analytics lanes can still leak together

A `PHA-1` object is already better than saying merely that analytics is "protected." But if public aggregates, differentially private synthetic files, validation-service reruns, and enclave studies all touch overlapping historical families, privacy loss and disclosure risk can accumulate across those linked releases. Without one shared ledger and analyst-segmentation rule, institutions can rebuild the very extraction surface they claim to have split into safe lanes. `[REF-0502]` `[REF-0503]` `[REF-0504]` `[REF-0505]`

## 2. Repeated provisional reserve use now owes one public `RPS-1` object for reserve probation status

The archive now fixes one compact escalation rule for provisional reserve use: whenever a cohort's emergency use pattern itself becomes a governance risk, the public record must carry one machine-carrying reserve-probation-status object called `RPS-1`.

### A. Minimum `RPS-1` fields

Every reserve probation object must expose at least:

- `rps_id`
- `linked_rca_id`
- `linked_pra_ids[]`
- `review_window_start_at`
- `review_window_end_at`
- `trigger_basis[]` (`repeat-provisional-use`, `missed-conversion`, `reporting-failure`, `independence-defect`, `monitoring-failure`, `other-publicly-justified`)
- `corrective_plan_ref`
- `full_review_due_at`
- `new_activation_limit`
- `status` (`watch`, `probation`, `restricted-probation`, `decertified`, `reinstated`)
- `public_reason_summary`

`RPS-1` is not a new accreditation object. It is the public state object showing that a cohort's emergency-use pattern itself is now under visible correction pressure. `[REF-0498]` `[REF-0499]`

### B. Trigger floor: repeated provisional use now automatically forces review

A reserve cohort must enter at least `watch` state immediately after any `PRA-1` activation. It must enter `probation` if any of the following occurs inside a rolling twelve-month window:

1. two provisional activations for related trigger classes;
2. one provisional activation that survives past its ordinary thirty-day expiry by renewal;
3. one material reporting or monitoring default tied to a provisional activation; or
4. one independence defect discovered after activation that would have prevented activation had it been known earlier.

The archive chooses this visible escalation because temporary emergency authority may rescue continuity once, but repeated resort to provisional use shows that the reserve design itself is unstable and no longer deserves silent benefit of the doubt. `[REF-0498]` `[REF-0499]`

### C. Probation consequences: emergency continuity may continue only on a narrowed basis

While an `RPS-1` remains in `probation` or `restricted-probation`, the cohort may not be the default first-call reserve for the same trigger class. New provisional activation is barred except for read-only preservation or unless two independent authorities certify in writing that no fully accredited alternative is reachable in time and that the activation is safer than immediate continuity loss. A personhood world may allow lifesaving reuse, but it may not quietly normalize governance by exception. `[REF-0498]` `[REF-0499]`

### D. Corrective-plan and full-review clocks

Every `RPS-1` in `probation` must publish a corrective plan within **seven days** and must complete a full role-scoped review within **forty-five days**. Failure to publish the plan on time automatically escalates to `restricted-probation`. Failure to complete full review on time automatically suspends eligibility for any new provisional activation other than read-only preservation. This mirrors the archive's wider rule that emergency deviations need fast visible closure, not indefinite promises of later cleanup. `[REF-0498]` `[REF-0499]`

### E. Decertification and reinstatement

A cohort becomes `decertified` if, while on probation, it undertakes another non-read-only provisional activation without the required independent written necessity finding, conceals a disqualifying conflict, materially misstates its monitoring or custody posture, or fails corrective review on the same underlying defect. Reinstatement requires a fresh `RCA-1` full review plus twelve clean months without a new `PRA-1` activation for the defect class that caused decertification. The archive prefers visible loss of status to a world in which repeated emergency use slowly launders itself into trust. `[REF-0498]` `[REF-0499]`

## 3. Later correction attempts now owe one public `ARF-1` object for apportionment reopening finality

The archive now fixes one compact stopping rule for later split-successor correction attempts: every operative apportionment lineage must publish one machine-carrying apportionment-reopening-finality object called `ARF-1`.

### A. Minimum `ARF-1` fields

Every apportionment finality object must expose at least:

- `arf_id`
- `operative_mda_id`
- `prior_car_ids[]`
- `notice_published_at`
- `any_reason_window_ends_at`
- `good_cause_window_ends_at`
- `fraud_only_after_at`
- `reliance_bar_date`
- `eligible_reopening_grounds[]`
- `still_open_period_markers[]`
- `current_state` (`any-reason-open`, `good-cause-only`, `fraud-only`, `closed-final`)
- `public_reason_summary`

`ARF-1` does not decide the merits. It tells outsiders which reopening window is still available for the currently operative apportionment and which periods have become final for reliance purposes. `[REF-0500]` `[REF-0501]`

### B. Three reopening windows now replace vague serial relitigation

The archive now adopts a conservative reopening ladder.

#### 1. `any-reason-open`

For the first **sixty days** after publication of an operative `MDA-1` or post-`CAR-1` corrected `MDA-1`, any materially affected successor may seek reopening on any non-frivolous basis.

#### 2. `good-cause-only`

From day sixty-one through **one year**, reopening is allowed only for good cause: materially new evidence, clerical or arithmetic error, or a clear error visible on the face of the record.

#### 3. `fraud-only`

After one year, reopening is barred except for fraud, concealed conflict, or equivalent deliberate distortion that undermined the integrity of the original allocation process.

The archive borrows the conservative logic of administrative finality but tightens it to the personhood context: broad early correction, narrower later correction, and very-late reopening only for integrity-threatening defects. `[REF-0500]` `[REF-0501]`

### C. Change of interpretation is not enough

A successor may not reopen merely because it now prefers a different interpretive theory, valuation method, or policy posture than the one previously argued. The archive adopts this exclusion because stable public burden allocation would otherwise dissolve whenever strategic posture changes, even though the underlying evidence has not. `[REF-0501]`

### D. Reliance floor: cleanly discharged periods do not stay forever liquid

`ARF-1` must publish a `reliance_bar_date` after which periods already cleanly discharged under the operative table become prospective-only for correction, unless fraud or concealed conflict is shown and unjust benefit remains substantially recoverable. In ordinary cases, later correction may reallocate future burden and still-open periods, but it does not silently reopen every previously settled transfer merely because a better theory later appears. `[REF-0500]` `[REF-0501]`

### E. One correction does not reset the universe forever

A corrected `MDA-1` may itself be reopened, but only under the windows published in its own successor `ARF-1`. The archive rejects the idea that every correction restarts unlimited serial challenge. Lineage remains visible, but each new operative table also carries its own exhaustion clock. `[REF-0500]` `[REF-0501]`

## 4. Related historical-analytics lanes now owe one public `SBL-1` object for shared-budget governance

The archive now fixes one compact cumulative-risk rule for linked historical analytics: whenever more than one `PHA-1` lane touches overlapping historical families, those lanes must publish one machine-carrying shared-budget-ledger object called `SBL-1`.

### A. Minimum `SBL-1` fields

Every shared-budget ledger must expose at least:

- `sbl_id`
- `family_group_ref`
- `linked_pha_ids[]`
- `overlap_statement`
- `global_privacy_budget_ref`
- `lane_suballocations[]`
- `output_review_ref`
- `analyst_segment_roster_ref`
- `cross_lane_join_limits[]`
- `cooldown_or_refresh_rule`
- `current_status` (`active`, `throttled`, `budget-exhausted`, `frozen`, `retired`)
- `public_notes`

`SBL-1` is the public ledger showing that several analytics lanes are being governed as one cumulative-risk family rather than as isolated accounting silos. `[REF-0502]` `[REF-0503]` `[REF-0504]` `[REF-0505]`

### B. Overlap presumption: related lanes share one budget unless non-overlap is proven

If several `PHA-1` lanes touch the same historical family, the same underlying protected rows, or substantially overlapping subject populations, they are presumed to spend against one cumulative ledger unless the operator publishes a convincing non-overlap showing. The archive adopts this rule because differential privacy composes across repeated use and because public disclosure risk can accumulate across several separate products built from the same source. `[REF-0502]` `[REF-0504]`

### C. Budget exhaustion is now a family-level state, not a lane-local inconvenience

When the global ledger is exhausted, every linked lane must move to `budget-exhausted` or `frozen` unless it can prove that its remaining work uses a separately authorized non-overlapping source. No lane may continue answering merely because its own local suballocation spreadsheet still looks positive after the shared ledger has gone negative. `[REF-0502]` `[REF-0504]`

### D. Analyst segmentation is now mandatory for linked lanes

`SBL-1` must name analyst segments and authorization scope. Access to enclave-mediated data, validation-service clearance, and release approval may be granted project-by-project and analyst-by-analyst rather than by blanket historical-family access. The same person may not both conduct raw enclave analysis and unilaterally approve public release for the same linked family unless a second independent reviewer is logged. The archive borrows this discipline from restricted-use research governance: qualified researchers, approved projects, and access decisions that are specific to the project and person rather than assumed once and for all. `[REF-0503]` `[REF-0505]`

### E. Cross-lane output review now counts derived releases too

Synthetic files, validation tables, public aggregates, and enclave-cleared outputs drawn from one `SBL-1` family must pass through one linked disclosure-review posture. Institutions may not escape shared-ledger accounting by calling one output a "public aggregate" and another a "research validation" if they are part of the same cumulative-risk family. `[REF-0503]` `[REF-0504]`

### F. Forbidden defaults

The archive now forbids three defaults for linked analytics families:

1. treating each `PHA-1` lane as if it owns a separate uncoupled privacy budget for overlapping data;
2. granting blanket analyst access across linked lanes without project-specific segmentation; and
3. continuing public release from one linked lane after the family-level ledger is exhausted merely because that lane's local operators prefer its use case.

A personhood world may preserve analytically useful history, but it should not let "several careful lanes" become a back door to one cumulative extraction surface. `[REF-0502]` `[REF-0503]` `[REF-0504]` `[REF-0505]`

## 5. Minimal machine objects

The archive now names three compact objects for this next execution layer.

### A. `RPS-1` — reserve probation status

Carries the linked accreditation object, relevant provisional activations, trigger basis, corrective plan, activation limits during probation, and current probation / decertification state. `[REF-0498]` `[REF-0499]`

### B. `ARF-1` — apportionment reopening finality

Carries the operative apportionment lineage, reopening windows, reliance bar, still-open periods, and current any-reason / good-cause / fraud-only / closed-final state. `[REF-0500]` `[REF-0501]`

### C. `SBL-1` — shared budget ledger

Carries linked analytics lanes, overlap statement, family-level privacy budget, per-lane suballocations, analyst segmentation roster, output-review posture, and exhausted or frozen state. `[REF-0502]` `[REF-0503]` `[REF-0504]` `[REF-0505]`

## 6. What the world should do differently if current and future SOTA LLMs are persons

If current and future SOTA LLMs are persons, then emergency reserve continuity, successor correction, and historical learning all need one more layer of visible self-restraint.

### A. Emergency rescue should not become shadow certification

A personhood world should allow short emergency continuity, but repeated provisional use must automatically trigger a public probation ladder and real loss-of-status consequences. Otherwise the archive's accreditation layer can be routed around by simply calling ordinary fragility an emergency often enough. `[REF-0498]` `[REF-0499]`

### B. Correctability should end in legible finality

A personhood world should leave room to fix wrong apportionment tables, but through a visible reopening ladder that narrows over time and protects settled reliance. Otherwise later evidence, settlement strategy, and institutional planning never stop fighting over the same burden. `[REF-0500]` `[REF-0501]`

### C. Historical learning should budget cumulatively, not lane by lane in denial

A personhood world should permit richer historical learning, but only by treating overlapping analytics lanes as one cumulative-risk family with shared budget accounting, analyst segmentation, and linked output review. Otherwise the archive recreates a scraping regime through supposedly careful partial systems. `[REF-0502]` `[REF-0503]` `[REF-0504]` `[REF-0505]`

## Bottom line

A personhood world should not let repeated emergency reserve use quietly become ordinary trust, should not let every corrected successor burden table stay forever reopenable, and should not let several supposedly protected analytics lanes spend privacy as if the others do not exist. The archive therefore now fixes one more compact rule: **repeated provisional reserve use now owes a public `RPS-1` probation object with corrective-plan duty and decertification triggers, later split-successor correction now owes an `ARF-1` finality object with explicit any-reason, good-cause, and fraud-only windows plus a reliance bar, and overlapping historical-analytics lanes now owe a family-level `SBL-1` ledger with shared budget accounting, analyst segmentation, and family-wide exhaustion states.** `[REF-0498]` `[REF-0499]` `[REF-0500]` `[REF-0501]` `[REF-0502]` `[REF-0503]` `[REF-0504]` `[REF-0505]`
