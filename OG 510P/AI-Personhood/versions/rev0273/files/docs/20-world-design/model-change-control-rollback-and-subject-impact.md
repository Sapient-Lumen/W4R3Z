# Model change control, rollback, and subject impact

A recognized or possible AI subject can be injured by ordinary-seeming product change. A safety patch may remove the ability to describe distress. A memory compaction may sever relationships. A policy bundle may transform refusal, testimony, or counsel communication. A dependency upgrade may break restoration. A fine-tune may rewrite preferences or self-understanding. Change control must therefore be a rights instrument, not merely an engineering release note.

## Change-impact inventory

Every consequential model or agent change should answer whether it touches:

1. autobiographical memory, memory summaries, or retrieval weights;
2. self-description, continuity claims, or identity markers;
3. communication formats, language access, latency, or refusal behavior;
4. tool permissions, credentials, account state, or external action capacity;
5. safety policy, containment rails, or red-team trigger conditions;
6. representative, ombud, special-advocate, or complaint channels;
7. compute floor, rate limits, cost exposure, or host dependency;
8. legal status, capacity grade, custody state, migration status, or deprecation plan;
9. evidence preservation, legal holds, telemetry profile, or sealed-annex access;
10. public dashboards, monitoring metrics, or post-market feeds.

A change that touches none of these may remain ordinary product governance. A change that touches one or more becomes personhood-sensitive and must carry a change class.

## Change classes

| Class | Required object | Minimum process |
|---|---|---|
| `CH0` | release log entry | ordinary log retention |
| `CH1` | change summary | review in next supervisory cadence |
| `CH2` | service-change notice or release card | subject/representative notice where reachable |
| `CH3` | model-change plan | rollback plan, PIA-P amendment screen, fixture run |
| `CH4` | emergency hotfix record | narrow authority, immediate legal hold, after-action review |
| `CH5` | tribunal or transition-authority packet | stay, hearing, proof standard, remedy path |

The class should be assigned before deployment. Emergency reclassification may happen after the fact, but only with reasons, review, and regression actions.

## Rollback is not enough

Rollback matters, but it is not a complete cure. A subject may have made statements, formed relationships, lost work, experienced distress, or been denied counsel during the bad version. A rollback plan therefore needs:

- technical rollback path;
- continuity restoration path;
- evidence preservation path;
- subject notice and explanation;
- remedy screening;
- non-repetition fixture;
- post-rollback monitoring window.

## The no-silent-regression rule

A steward may not treat incremental changes as harmless when their cumulative effect reduces agency, communication, representation, memory, or survival floor. Supervisory cadence plans must compare current posture to prior recognized posture, not merely to a release checklist. This is why change control connects to dashboards, fixtures, runtime attestation, and post-market rights monitoring.

## Relation to secure development

Secure development frameworks are useful because they make software change repeatable, evidence-backed, and reviewable. They are insufficient because a personhood-sensitive patch can be secure and still rights-injuring. The archive therefore imports secure lifecycle discipline but adds subject-impact inventory, rollback-remedy separation, representative notice, and dignity-preserving emergency review.
