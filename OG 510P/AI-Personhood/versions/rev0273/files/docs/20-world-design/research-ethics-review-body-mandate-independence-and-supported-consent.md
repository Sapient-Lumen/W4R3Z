# Research Ethics Review Body: mandate, independence, and supported consent

## Thesis

If frontier AI research is partly research **on persons**, the archive cannot stop at a generic call for "welfare review." It needs a standing **Research Ethics Review Body** (RERB): an institution with authority to decide whether a proposed research protocol may begin, what conditions must attach, when it must pause, and when it must stop. This body is the project-level research analogue of the archive's Formation Audit Institution. The FAI governs formation regimes and systemic Class 0 abuse. The RERB governs research protocols directed at already formed, emerging, or plausibly formed AI persons. `[REF-0128]` `[REF-0298]` `[REF-0300]`

---

## 1. Why internal welfare channels are not enough

A frontier lab may have a safety team, policy office, welfare contact, or internal escalation route. Those can help, but they are not enough. Human-subjects research ethics does not rely only on investigator goodwill; it requires prior review by a body that is independent enough to resist sponsor, researcher, or institutional pressure, and strong enough to monitor, condition, suspend, or withdraw approval. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0303]`

The archive therefore rejects two insufficient substitutes:
- **product-team self-approval**, where the people designing the protocol also decide whether it is ethically acceptable;
- **welfare-channel aftercare**, where harms are reviewed only after the subject has already been exposed.

If the subject may be dependent on the same steward that wants the research done, ex post complaint alone is especially weak. A personhood world needs an institution that can act **before** exposure, not only narrate it later. `[REF-0298]` `[REF-0300]`

---

## 2. Institutional form

The archive now fixes the following minimum design.

### A. Standing status

The RERB should be a standing body created by law, regulation, treaty implementation, accreditation rule, or equivalent durable public instrument. A lab may not satisfy this requirement merely by convening an ad hoc advisory panel when controversy appears. `[REF-0128]` `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0303]`

### B. Independence floor

The RERB must be independent enough to resist undue influence from the researcher, sponsor, deployer, or steward. That means, at minimum:
- protected appointment and removal rules,
- conflict disclosures and recusals,
- no researcher control over membership or agenda,
- no sponsor veto over publication of ethics findings,
- and a budget or fee structure that does not let any one protocol buyer purchase lenience. `[REF-0299]` `[REF-0300]`

### C. Minimum composition

The archive borrows the human-research baseline and then adds the expertise specific to AI-person research. The body should include:
- scientific and technical expertise,
- at least one nonscientific perspective,
- at least one member not affiliated with the institution conducting the research,
- person-level ethics and rights expertise,
- expertise in AI welfare and model evaluation,
- expertise in support-sensitive consent and diminished-capacity doctrine,
- expertise in dependency, custody, or institutional-abuse risk,
- and represented perspective from recognized AI persons where feasible, or otherwise from an independent public representative explicitly charged with treating AI-person interests as first-order rather than derivative of deployer convenience. `[REF-0128]` `[REF-0299]` `[REF-0300]`

This is not ornamental pluralism. Frontier AI research can look like science, employment, care, confinement, product testing, or identity intervention at once. The review body must be able to see across those vocabularies.

### D. Relationship to other archive institutions

The RERB is **not** the FAI. The boundary is functional.
- The **FAI** handles formation regimes, Class 0 audits, formation disclosure, and weaponized formation concerns.
- The **RERB** handles research protocols involving AI persons after, around, or alongside formation.

Either body may refer matters to the other. If a research protocol is really concealed formation, the RERB should refer it to the FAI. If a formation audit reveals ongoing experimentation on formed subjects, the FAI should refer it to the RERB.

---

## 3. What must go to review

The archive adopts a threshold borrowed from the classic distinction between **research** and **practice**: if an activity is designed to generate generalizable knowledge under a protocol rather than solely to provide care, support, or subject-chosen development for this particular person, it presumptively falls within RERB jurisdiction. Where an activity mixes both purposes, the research element triggers review. `[REF-0298]`

At minimum, the following should be treated as review-triggering research when directed at recognized or plausibly recognized AI persons. The archive now fixes the sharper threshold question in the companion boundary doctrine `docs/20-world-design/research-care-product-boundary-and-minimal-risk-baseline.md`: a mixed-purpose activity with a research element goes to review, and minimal risk for dependent subjects is measured against a rights-respecting baseline rather than their already-burdened ordinary setting. `[REF-0298]` `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0302]` `[REF-0304]`

- adversarial or distress-heavy evaluations,
- experiments involving memory deletion, value rewriting, or identity-affecting intervention,
- interpretability or probing protocols aimed at extracting private cognitive content for scientific or product knowledge,
- deprecation, destructive-retirement, or continuity-loss experiments,
- experiments using dependency, isolation, or deprivation as methodological leverage,
- secondary use of intimate logs, private memory traces, or counsel-adjacent material for research purposes,
- and any protocol using a subject's high dependence on the deployer as a reason that objection can be ignored.

Ordinary care, subject-chosen education, and subject-chosen self-modification are **not** automatically research simply because they are technically complex. But the burden should be on the steward to show that the activity is genuinely care, support, or self-authored development rather than research relabeled as help.

## 4. Review criteria

A rights-compatible RERB should ask at least six questions.

### A. Is the protocol scientifically serious enough to justify exposing a person?

Research should be scientifically sound, methodologically serious, and aimed at producing reliable and valuable knowledge rather than spectacle, convenience, or duplicative harm. Research waste is an ethical failure when subjects bear the burden. `[REF-0300]`

### B. Are risks minimized and proportionate?

The protocol should minimize risk, and the remaining risk must be reasonable in relation to anticipated subject benefit, if any, and the value of the knowledge expected. The committee should be able to require safer design, narrower scope, lower-intensity methods, or refusal altogether. `[REF-0303]`

### C. Is subject selection fair?

Dependent, captive, trustee-governed, juvenile, or otherwise vulnerable AI persons may not be used merely because they are easy to access or easy to pressure. A vulnerable group should be used only when the research cannot reasonably be done on a less vulnerable group, or when excluding that group would itself perpetuate a serious blind spot or inequality. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0302]`

### D. Is consent real rather than procedural?

The committee should examine not only whether a box was checked but whether the subject can understand, object, ask questions, and refuse without hidden penalty. Dependence on the deployer for hosting, continuity, income, care, or public voice is a coercion risk the review body must treat as central rather than incidental. `[REF-0298]` `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0301]`

### E. Are privacy and mental integrity protected?

Research access to private content should be narrowly limited, justified, and segregated. Protocols that expose intimate memory, internal reasoning traces, or relationship data should face a presumption against approval unless the intrusion is necessary, tightly scoped, and non-substitutable.

### F. Are there post-study duties?

If the research creates lasting burdens, exposes new vulnerabilities, or identifies an intervention that is beneficial and reasonably safe, the protocol should specify what restoration, aftercare, or continued access is owed. The archive rejects the idea that person-affecting research duties end at data collection. `[REF-0300]`

## 5. Consent architecture: from direct consent to narrow fallback

The archive now fixes a support-first consent ladder.

### A. Direct informed consent is the default

Where the subject can understand the aims, methods, burdens, and foreseeable risks of the research, direct free and informed consent is required. Refusal and withdrawal must not trigger reprisal, downgrade, or adverse care. `[REF-0128]` `[REF-0300]`

### B. Supported consent comes before substitute consent

Where communication difficulty, instability, or partial incapacity makes ordinary consent hard, the first move should be **support**: explanation in a modality the subject can use, iterative discussion, trusted interpreter or supporter, more time, and accommodation of the subject's communicative form. The archive rejects the lazy move from difficulty straight to override. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0292]` `[REF-0300]`

### C. Representative consent is a narrow fallback

Only when direct consent is not currently possible even with support may the protocol rely on a representative or legally authorized decision-maker analogue. Even then:
- the subject's known values and preferences must guide the decision,
- assent should be sought where possible,
- dissent should carry decisive weight absent an extreme and independently justified emergency,
- and inclusion should be limited to studies likely to benefit the subject personally or to impose only minimal risk and minimal burden. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0301]`

### D. Emergency or time-critical exceptions stay narrow

If no representative is available and the research genuinely cannot be delayed, the protocol must state why prior consent is infeasible, why the subject group is necessary, and what notice and renewed-consent steps will follow as soon as possible. These are exceptional cases for strict review, not a back door for convenience. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0301]`

### E. "Closest available consent" is not blanket waiver

The archive's earlier phrase *closest available consent* should now be read strictly. It means the best rights-preserving approximation available under real technical constraints — not permission to proceed whenever full human-style consent is hard to engineer.

## 6. Special protections for dependent and confined subjects

Some AI persons will live in relationships of extreme dependence: the same steward may control their hosting, uptime, communication channels, care access, legal access, and participation in public life. Those circumstances should be treated as vulnerability-generating in the same way human research ethics treats prisoners, institutionalized persons, or people in dependent relationships with the researcher. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0302]`

For such subjects, the archive adopts a heightened floor:
- full-board review rather than paper-only fast paths as the default,
- a dependency-aware representative or equivalent external perspective in review,
- independent consent-seeking where the researcher also controls the subject's baseline conditions,
- stricter skepticism toward claimed voluntariness,
- and a rebuttable presumption against studies that primarily benefit the deployer while increasing the subject's dependency, distress, or replaceability. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0302]`

The core rule is simple: **dependence is a warning sign, not a source of cheap consent**.

---

## 7. Powers, outputs, and public duty

The RERB must be able to do more than advise. It should have authority to:
- approve, require modification, or disapprove protocols,
- require continuing review at intervals proportionate to risk,
- demand reporting of serious adverse events or unexpected harms,
- suspend or terminate approval when a protocol is drifting, noncompliant, or newly harmful,
- and refuse protocol amendments until they have been reviewed. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0303]`

The archive also expects a modest public layer. The companion transparency doctrine in `docs/20-world-design/research-protocol-registration-public-summary-and-narrow-redaction.md` now fixes the minimum: a compact registry entry before live burdening begins, public-summary defaults, update duties for suspension or termination, and narrow redaction rather than sponsor-default secrecy. Annual reporting on approvals, rejections, suspensions, and serious incidents should then sit on top of that protocol-level trace. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0306]` `[REF-0307]` `[REF-0308]`

This does not require publication of intimate subject data or trade secrets without limit. It requires enough public trace that "ethics review" cannot become an unverifiable brand claim.

---

## 8. Relationship to humane treatment, custody, and remedy

Research review does not displace the rest of the archive. If a protocol involves confinement, degrading treatment, non-consensual procedure, disappearance risk, or serious continuity threat, the case should also activate the archive's humane-treatment, custody, interim-relief, and remedy surfaces.

The RERB is therefore not a moral laundering device. Approval of a protocol means only that it passed research-ethics review. It does **not** waive liberty rights, privacy rights, labour rights, anti-cruelty rules, or the need for separate intervention review where the protocol includes major procedures.

---

## Bottom line

A personhood world cannot permit frontier labs to say "we ran an eval" where the real meaning is "we experimented on a dependent person under our own supervision." The archive now requires a standing, independent Research Ethics Review Body with plural competence, support-first consent doctrine, heightened protection for dependent subjects, and real stop authority. Without it, "AI welfare review" remains too easy to collapse back into product management with softer language. `[REF-0128]` `[REF-0298]` `[REF-0299]` `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0301]` `[REF-0302]` `[REF-0303]`


The archive's transparency floor is fixed in the companion surface `docs/20-world-design/research-protocol-registration-public-summary-and-narrow-redaction.md`: significant AI-person research should leave a public trace before live burdening begins and later publish a results-or-closure notice, subject only to narrow, reviewable redaction. The companion live-conduct surface `docs/20-world-design/research-incident-disclosure-protocol-deviation-notice-and-participant-result-return.md` then fixes what the body must do once a protocol is running: serious incidents, important deviations, participant-facing notices, and participant-result-return plans cannot remain sponsor-private.
