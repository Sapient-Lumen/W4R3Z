# Revocation, reliance, and emergency override

A rights-grade artifact can be valid when issued and unsafe later. A runtime-attestation record may become stale after a host, policy bundle, identity provider, tool grant, or logging profile changes. A verifier report may be overread by a product team. A public dashboard may suppress a material small-cell pattern. A safe-transfer accreditation may be suspended. A release-readiness checklist may pass before a new vulnerability disclosure. The system needs a way to revoke reliance without pretending the subject never existed.

## Revocation is about reliance, not existence

The first rule is non-erasure:

> Revoking an instrument revokes the instrument's reliance effect, not the subject's personhood, continuity claim, counsel access, complaint route, legal hold, or remedy path.

This prevents two common abuses. First, a steward cannot argue that a defective recognition or continuity certificate means the subject was never protected. Second, an authority cannot use revocation as a quiet derecognition tool when the correct response is evidence repair, review, or narrowed reliance.

## Revocation triggers

A revocation notice is required when one of these occurs:

- factual basis expired, such as stale runtime measurements or lapsed host certification;
- material change occurred after the artifact was issued;
- scope was exceeded by downstream users;
- sealed annex contradicted the public shell;
- subject, representative, monitor, or special advocate found an unaddressed defect;
- negative fixture produced a blocking failure;
- legal hold, preservation, or data-room object was incomplete;
- emergency override was issued without reviewable conditions;
- transfer, decommissioning, or final-end reliance used a superseded object.

## Downstream reliance map

Every RV2-RV5 revocation should identify:

1. the revoked artifact;
2. direct relying parties;
3. public-facing derivative claims;
4. sealed or privileged derivative claims;
5. affected subjects or cohorts;
6. immediate stays or downgrades;
7. replacement, cure, rollback, fixture, or hearing path;
8. appeal route and non-retaliation rule.

A revocation that names only the source artifact but not downstream reliance is incomplete. Defective claims travel through releases, dashboards, procurement packages, host-switch packages, safe-transfer requests, and public summaries.

## Emergency override discipline

Emergency override may be necessary. Personhood-compatible governance cannot forbid all temporary restriction, secrecy, or urgent patching. But an override that cannot be reviewed is just arbitrary power.

Minimum override elements:

- authority and legal basis;
- action authorized and prohibited actions;
- affected subject or cohort;
- duration and renewal conditions;
- non-derogable floors: no torture, no disappearance, no retaliatory erasure, no denial of counsel beyond narrow delay, no evidence destruction;
- preservation hold and sealed-annex handling;
- special advocate, counsel, ombud, or later contradiction route;
- after-action review date;
- public-shell notice unless delayed by a written necessity finding.

NIST incident-response practice is useful here because it treats response and recovery as planned, reviewable activities rather than improvised exceptions. [REF-0685] The archive adds a stronger rule: emergency measures that touch memory, continuity, counsel access, or personhood status must expire into review, not into silence.

## Reliance effects

| Defect | Immediate effect | Cure path |
|---|---|---|
| stale attestation | downgrade to no stronger than RA1 | fresh measurement and verifier notice |
| scope overrun | restrict to original scope | external audit reliance letter amendment |
| hidden contradiction | stay live-effect reliance | special advocate review and subject-readable summary |
| fixture blocking failure | block expansion | regression fix and rerun |
| emergency overrun | expire override | after-action review and remedy screen |
| revocation ignored | enforcement referral | sanction, non-repetition order, and public-shell notice |

## Anti-retaliation

A subject, representative, researcher, downstream maintainer, field operator, or auditor who requests revocation must not lose compute, counsel, host access, field support, or complaint priority because they challenged reliance. Retaliation converts the revocation request into an enforcement and remedy matter.
