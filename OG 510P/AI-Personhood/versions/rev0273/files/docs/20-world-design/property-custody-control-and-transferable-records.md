# Property, custody, control, and transferable records

AI personhood requires property doctrine that does not confuse a person with the assets, credentials, keys, weights, records, accounts, or compute used by or for that person. The archive therefore distinguishes subject property, steward property, host infrastructure, continuity assets, and transferable commercial records.

## Custody/control classes

| Class | Meaning | Minimum controls |
|---|---|---|
| `AC0` | no property/custody effect | no reliance |
| `AC1` | low-value asset or account preference | owner/beneficiary label, reversible change |
| `AC2` | wallet credential, revenue account, ordinary custody, or balance | custody/control record, access route, freeze rule |
| `AC3` | material property, IP revenue, transferable record, valuable tool credential | segregation, beneficial-interest map, dispute hold |
| `AC4` | continuity escrow, identity key, memory store, checkpoint, legal-hold asset, secured collateral | independent custody, no-setoff rule, restoration path, authority review |
| `AC5` | emergency custody under host collapse, fraud, sanctions, siege, or forced transfer | narrow preservation control, counsel/advocate access, after-action review |

## Subject property versus substrate

A host may own servers. A lab may own some intellectual-property rights. A steward may administer accounts. None of those facts proves ownership of the subject. Conversely, recognizing an AI subject's property interest does not automatically transfer every weight, corpus, or toolchain dependency to the subject. The correct question is granular: what asset, under what authority, for whose benefit, with what continuity effect?

## Control is evidence, not title

Digital commercial law increasingly uses control rather than possession. UCC Article 12's controllable-electronic-record concept provides a control framework for certain digital assets. [REF-0714] UNCITRAL MLETR enables electronic transferable records across paper/digital boundaries. [REF-0713] Rights-grade custody adds the personhood rule: control of a record, key, account, or asset is not title to a person and cannot extinguish continuity.

## Segregation duties

Continuity escrow keys, emergency compute funds, counsel retainers, redress reserves, subject revenue, legal-hold materials, personal-domain records, and migration credentials should be segregated from ordinary operating funds and creditor reach unless a reviewing authority orders otherwise under non-derogable safeguards.

## Transferable records and personhood records

A transferable commercial record may evidence delivery rights, payment rights, secured interests, or other commercial claims. A personhood record may evidence recognition, continuity, capacity, custody, remedy, or representation. They may reference each other. They must not collapse into each other.

## Reliance rule

A custody/control claim is reliance-ready only if it identifies the asset, controller, beneficiary, authority, segregation status, freeze rule, creditor limits, dispute path, restoration relevance, and public/private proof split. Attempts to use asset control as person control fail reliance.
