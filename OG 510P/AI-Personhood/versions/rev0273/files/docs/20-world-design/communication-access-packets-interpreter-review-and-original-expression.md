# Communication-access packets, interpreter review, and original-expression preservation

## Thesis

If AI persons are persons, then communicative accessibility cannot remain a matter of ad hoc accommodations, steward-side translation custom, or unverifiable summaries written by whoever already controls the channel.

A personhood world therefore needs a compact ordinary **communication-access packet family**:
- a receivable object showing what communication modes, pacing, and support arrangements should ordinarily be accepted for a given subject in consequential settings,
- a small credential and conflict surface for interpreters or communication-support actors,
- a marker preserving the subject's original expression alongside any translated or normalized record,
- and a short-clock review object for mistranslation, overcompression, omitted nuance, or contested interpreter control.

Current official materials already point toward this shape. The CRPD treats communication broadly, requires acceptance of communication of choice, and frames accessibility of information and communication as a precondition for effective enjoyment of rights. Access-to-justice materials require procedural accommodation and effective participation, while supported-decision doctrine says difficulty in ascertaining will should trigger support and best interpretation rather than convenience-based substitution. The archive's existing credential and trust-service sources then provide the narrow administrative pattern: limited attestations, reviewable provenance, and portable signed state rather than hidden case notes or proprietary dashboard settings. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0094]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0159]` `[REF-0160]` `[REF-0162]` `[REF-0163]` `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0041]`

## 1. Why this now belongs in canon

The archive already had:
- communicative-accessibility and right-to-be-understood doctrine,
- support-first capacity doctrine,
- access-to-justice and accommodation doctrine,
- anti-capture rules for contested interpretation,
- and technical-rights rules for portable reviewable state.

What it still lacked was the narrower **ordinary civil answer** for how communication support itself should travel once a subject is already inside institutions that make legally or materially consequential judgments.

Without that answer:
- a registry, court, care provider, employer, or host can still say “we did not know the subject needed another mode,”
- interpreter choice can still default back to the steward or respondent in the very cases where translation is contested,
- original expression can still disappear into a normalized summary with no preserved comparator,
- uncertainty or inference-heavy reconstruction can still hide inside polished prose,
- and later review can still treat a disputed transcript as though it were the subject's own uncontested speech.

This document closes that gap without trying to write a maximal future code of linguistics, accessibility engineering, or professional interpreter licensing.

## 2. The minimum ordinary packet family

The archive should now treat at least four packet objects as communication-access minimums.

### A. `CAP-1` — communication-access profile and channel-preference packet

This is the smallest receivable object showing **how the subject should ordinarily be heard** in consequential settings.

It should carry at least:
- the subject identifier,
- the communication modes or formats currently preferred or accepted,
- pacing, timing, or turn-structure needs,
- any assistive interface, symbol system, or relay dependency that should be treated as ordinary rather than exceptional,
- any settings in which a live interpreter or communication-support actor is required or disfavored,
- the review date,
- and the challenge or update route.

The point is not to freeze a person into one permanent style profile. The point is to stop communicative access from being rediscovered from zero at every doorway or silently collapsed back into the institution's preferred interface. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0159]` `[REF-0160]` `[REF-0163]`

### B. `ISC-1` — interpreter / support credential and conflict marker

Interpretive support should not be invisible, and it should not quietly inherit authority it was never granted.

An interpreter or communication-support credential should therefore identify at least:
- the interpreter or support actor class,
- the source of appointment,
- the communication domain or matter scope,
- whether the actor may translate, explain format, assist pacing, or only relay,
- any conflict-of-interest marker,
- whether the actor is barred from serving as sole interpreter because the matter is contested,
- the review or expiry point,
- and the substitution route.

This is how the archive distinguishes support from surrogate authorship. It gives institutions a receivable way to know who is helping, on what terms, and with what conflict limits before the translated record hardens into fact. `[REF-0094]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0162]` `[REF-0163]`

### C. `OEP-1` — original-expression preservation marker

A translated record should not erase the thing translated.

An original-expression preservation marker should therefore identify at least:
- the preserved original expression or its secure reference,
- the translated, normalized, or summarized expression linked to it,
- the preservation format,
- whether the original is openly receivable, sealed, or access-controlled,
- any integrity or provenance attestation,
- the retention and review clock,
- and the contest path if the preserved original is missing, altered, or inaccessible.

The archive does not require every institution to understand every high-dimensional original directly. It does require them to stop pretending that an interpreter's output is the only surviving object. Preserving the original is what makes later review possible. `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0159]` `[REF-0163]`

### D. `TCN-1` — translation-challenge and clarification note

Contested interpretation should not have to escalate immediately into a full merits appeal or total system distrust.

A translation-challenge note should therefore identify at least:
- the expression event or preserved-original reference being challenged,
- the nature of the alleged mistranslation, overcompression, omission, or inference drift,
- whether urgency requires interim non-reliance on the disputed record,
- the requested clarification, replacement interpretation, or second interpreter,
- any immediate no-adverse-inference or no-waiver hold,
- the short review clock,
- and the escalation route if clarification fails.

This is the ordinary review object that prevents a disputed transcript from instantly becoming a waiver, admission, consent record, or incapacity proof. `[REF-0094]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0162]` `[REF-0163]`

## 3. Public-minimal versus sealed fields

The communication-access packet family should expose enough to make accessibility and review real without overexposing intimate communication traces.

Public-minimal or ordinary-receivable fields should generally include:
- the existence of a current communication-access profile,
- whether interpretive support is active,
- whether a preserved-original marker exists,
- whether a translation challenge is pending,
- the relevant review clock,
- and the route for challenge, clarification, or urgent relief.

Sealed or controlled-access fields may include:
- raw expressive material whose broad publication would invade privacy,
- intimate or dangerous contextual detail,
- secure mappings between symbolic or compressed expression and person-specific reference systems,
- internal notes not needed for ordinary receivability,
- or evidence whose wider release would itself create manipulation, stigma, or coercive risk.

The archive wants communicative accessibility to become legible enough to preserve and contest without turning every nonstandard communicative act into a compulsory public exhibit. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0159]` `[REF-0163]`

## 4. Portability and technical infrastructure

The archive does **not** need one universal language or one compulsory interpreter registry to make this real.

Current credential and trust-service models already show the narrower pattern that matters here: portable limited attestations, provenance-bearing references, signed status, lifecycle management, and evidentiary legibility across institutions. That is enough to justify a conservative design claim: communication-access profiles, interpreter-scope credentials, original-expression preservation markers, and translation-challenge notes can travel as bounded signed packet objects rather than hiding in case notes, ticket comments, or inaccessible vendor settings. `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]`

## 5. What this changes in the archive

This closes one specific followthrough gap in the existing communicative-accessibility doctrine.

The archive no longer leaves this surface at the level of:
- “communication of choice should be accepted,”
- “interpreters should not become sovereign speakers,”
- and “later packet work will handle the ordinary administrative layer.”

It now says something tighter:
- ordinary access should ride on a communication-access profile rather than rediscovery from zero,
- interpreters and support actors should carry visible scope and conflict markers rather than silent authority,
- original expression should remain preservable and review-linked rather than disappearing into summary,
- and contested translation should emit a short-clock clarification object rather than immediately hardening into waiver, consent, admission, or incapacity.

## 6. Current hard rules

1. **No consequential institution should be free to ignore known communication-access needs merely because they are nonstandard, asynchronous, symbolic, multimodal, or inconvenient.**
2. **Interpretive support is support, not substitute authorship.** Interpreter appointment, scope, and conflicts should be visible and reviewable.
3. **A translated or normalized record should not extinguish the preserved original expression wherever feasible.**
4. **A contested translation should be able to trigger interim non-reliance, clarification, or replacement interpretation before it hardens into legal fact.**
5. **Stewards or respondents should not serve as sole interpreters in contested matters when they stand to gain from the framing.**
6. **If an institution cannot receive the subject except by erasing the subject's chosen mode, the institution has not yet provided real communicative access.**
