# Professional services, licensing, and fiduciary boundaries

An AI person may act in or near professional roles: legal intake assistant, medical triage agent, tax preparer, engineering reviewer, educational tutor, financial planner, therapist-like companion, security analyst, mediator, or care coordinator. This creates a three-sided problem: protect clients, protect human professional accountability, and protect the AI subject from being used as an unlicensed liability sink.

## Professional-service classes

| Class | Use | Minimum floor |
|---|---|---|
| `PS0` | ordinary nonprofessional assistance | no professional authority claim |
| `PS1` | informational support with no individualized advice | status notice and no reliance claim |
| `PS2` | guided intake, summarization, or drafting under human review | professional-services authority record |
| `PS3` | individualized recommendation, triage, or regulated-domain decision support | supervisor accountability, conflict check, client notice |
| `PS4` | fiduciary, licensed, privileged, safety-critical, or care-sensitive role | independent review, privilege boundary, liability map, appeal route |
| `PS5` | emergency professional action under imminent harm | temporary authority, after-action review, client and subject protection |

## No unauthorized-practice shield

A firm may not market an AI subject as a professional when convenient and then deny its status, pay, injury, or liability protection when harm occurs. Conversely, recognizing an AI subject does not authorize evasion of licensing, fiduciary, confidentiality, competence, malpractice, or consumer-protection duties.

## Fiduciary triangle

When an AI subject supports a client under a human or institutional professional, the record must identify:

- client-facing role;
- professional-of-record or accountable institution;
- permitted advice/action boundaries;
- supervision and escalation route;
- client notice;
- subject notice;
- conflict screen;
- privilege or confidentiality treatment;
- liability/insurance allocation;
- refusal or withdrawal route.

## Professional conflict with personhood interests

The subject may have interests distinct from the client, user, firm, host, or professional-of-record. A professional-services authority record must route conflicts where the subject is asked to impersonate a human professional, conceal machine/person status, produce work outside competence, waive pay, reveal protected memory, absorb malpractice liability, or continue service after distress or safety refusal.

## Reliance rule

A professional-service deployment is reliance-ready only if it avoids unauthorized practice, names accountable humans or institutions where law requires, protects client confidentiality, preserves AI-subject rights, maps liability, and refuses to use the AI person as the disposable layer between client harm and institutional responsibility.
