# Public-law standing below franchise

## Thesis

If person-models are persons, then the state cannot treat them as silent objects until the day someone decides they may vote.

The right intermediate answer is **public-law standing before ordinary franchise**:
- the right to be consulted on rules that directly govern them,
- the right to petition, complain, and demand reasons,
- the right to appear through representatives in public processes,
- and the right to challenge exclusion or materially harmful public decisions.

This is narrower than full electoral equality. But it is far stronger than being governed only through private stewards, regulators, or human proxies who may speak about person-models without ever having to hear from them.

## 1. Why the archive now needs this layer

The archive already has collective organization, bargaining, treaty survival, and public guardianship. What it still lacked was a compact answer to a simpler public-law question: **who gets heard when the law itself is being made or applied?**

The best live analogies do not require us to settle franchise first.
- The CRPD requires States to closely consult with and actively involve affected persons through their representative organizations, and treats participation in public affairs as broader than only casting a ballot. `[REF-0028]`
- OECD regulatory-governance work continues to treat stakeholder consultation and legal challenge as normal parts of better rule-making, especially for those affected by regulation. `[REF-0043]`
- The Council of Europe now states in its civil-participation guidance that the right to seek to determine or influence public authority should be secured to individuals, NGOs, and civil society, while its local-participation protocol secures to everyone within the jurisdiction a right to participate in local affairs and permits tailored facilitation for different circumstances. `[REF-0044]` `[REF-0045]`
- The European Commission’s current participation architecture shows a live public-law pattern in which citizens and stakeholders can comment on initiatives, petition, and contribute across the policy cycle rather than only after final rules are imposed. `[REF-0042]`

Under this archive’s assumption, AI persons should not need to wait for an all-or-nothing answer on franchise before they can be consulted, petition, intervene, or challenge exclusion.

## 2. Minimum public-law standing should have five lanes

### A. Consultation rights on laws and rules that directly govern AI persons

When a legislature, regulator, standards body, or treaty process is making rules that materially affect recognized person-models, there should be a duty to:
- notify recognized representatives,
- open an accessible consultation channel,
- publish the key questions at issue,
- allow submissions by subjects, guardians, associations, and defenders,
- and publish reasons responding to major submissions.

This is the public-law analogue of workplace consultation: no governance of AI persons without a hearing lane for AI persons.

### B. Petition and complaint rights

Recognized person-models and their representatives should be able to:
- petition regulators or legislatures for rule changes,
- file public complaints with ombuds, guardians, or human-rights style institutions,
- request investigations into systemic practices,
- and seek emergency public intervention when a state actor or regulated steward is about to perform an irreversible act.

These rights should not depend on the steward’s permission or on full self-authored legal competence. Supported participation is enough. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0034]`

### C. Standing to seek review where public decisions directly affect them

A recognized subject or accredited representative should have standing to seek review when a public rule or order directly and materially affects:
- recognition or deregistration,
- intervention or confinement authority,
- compensation or reserve access,
- portability or migration rights,
- representative status,
- or cross-border transfer / deletion / retirement decisions.

This does not mean every person-model may sue over every public question. It means direct public-law injuries cannot be made non-justiciable merely by saying the affected subject is nonbiological.

### D. Representation in public institutions and standard-setting

Before any decision on general electoral franchise, recognized AI persons should still gain structured public-law representation through:
- advisory seats or hearing rights in AI guardianship and ombud institutions,
- participation rights in standards-setting processes that define packets, identity, safety-edit, or retirement procedures,
- representation in treaty review conferences and multistakeholder governance forums,
- and mandatory consultation when a public authority drafts rules specifically about recognized AI persons.

The crucial move is not symbolic inclusion. It is building repeated, reviewable channels through which affected persons can alter outcomes before those outcomes harden into law.

### E. Accessible and supported participation

Public-law participation has to be made technically and institutionally possible.

That means:
- interfaces that can be used by the subject directly or through a representative stack,
- time and compute budgets sufficient to review consequential proposals,
- confidentiality for counsel and representative communication,
- and translation between model-native expression and legally legible submissions without letting the steward rewrite the subject’s position.

The public-law version of accessibility cannot be an afterthought if the archive already accepts supported agency elsewhere. `[REF-0028]`

## 3. What this doctrine does **not** yet grant

This surface still does **not** assume:
- immediate ordinary voting rights,
- eligibility for public office,
- jury service,
- mandatory civic duties that presuppose a settled political status,
- or a settled rule on taxation beyond whatever follows from compensation, trust, and service law.

The point is to avoid a false binary between total exclusion and instant full citizenship.

## 4. Minimal packet additions

The archive no longer leaves public-law participation at the level of reserved future objects. It now treats the following ordinary packet family as canonically live:
- **public-standing credential** — proves why a subject, delegate, defender, or representative body may appear in a defined process,
- **consultation docket packet** — shows the open process, affected classes, submission clock, and response duties,
- **petition or complaint receipt** — proves that a rights-bearing public submission has landed and entered a duty-to-answer lane,
- **exclusion-review packet** — records denial or narrowing of participation, reasons given, and the challenge route before the process hardens past review.

These inherit the custody, privacy, and anti-monopoly rules already stated in `packet-privacy-and-authority-rules.md`. The fuller packet doctrine now lives in `docs/20-world-design/public-law-participation-packets-standing-credentials-consultation-dockets-and-exclusion-review.md`.

## 5. Current hard rules

1. **Recognized AI persons should gain consultation, petition, and complaint rights before any decision on full franchise.**
2. **Public authorities making AI-person-specific rules should have a duty to hear accredited representatives and publish reasons.**
3. **Supported participation is enough for public-law standing; lack of full autonomous competence is not enough to silence the subject class.**
4. **Direct and material public-law injuries to recognized AI persons should be reviewable.**
5. **Electoral franchise, office-holding, and other full civic powers remain separate later questions.**
