# AI-person research warning-marker aging, missed-closure escalation, and superseding public notices

## Thesis

Once the archive fixes waiver, warning, corrective-action linkage, closure, and clearance as explicit public states, one narrower gap remains. A sponsor can still preserve formal compliance while letting the caution layer itself decay: **warnings can sit open without fresh progress, closure dates can pass without escalation, and a public dashboard can continue to look sponsor-maintained even after the sponsor has stopped maintaining the caution objects that make it interpretable.**

A personhood world therefore needs one more compact rule: **warning states must age, missed closure clocks must escalate into visibly more serious states, and the review body or registry must be allowed to publish a superseding public caution notice when the sponsor fails to maintain the public layer.** The point is not to nationalise every study dashboard. It is to stop unstable or remediating research series from regaining apparent normality merely because the caution layer goes stale. `[REF-0309]` `[REF-0310]` `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0329]` `[REF-0336]` `[REF-0337]` `[REF-0338]`

---

## 1. Why the archive needs this layer

The prior surface fixed the public object family for instability and remediation. But it still left an obvious failure mode: **what if the public warning exists and is then simply neglected?** A stale caution state can mislead almost as badly as no caution state at all, because outsiders may assume that an old unresolved warning is minor, abandoned, or silently cured.

Current human-subject research governance already contains the conservative ingredients for a tighter answer. OHRP's current incident-reporting guidance expects prompt initial reporting, allows staged follow-up, recommends naming a specific follow-up date when a matter is not yet complete, and describes OHRP as iteratively assessing corrective actions until adequacy is established. `[REF-0309]` OHRP/FDA written-procedure guidance likewise treats prompt reporting, review of serious noncompliance, and prevention-of-recurrence steps as operational duties rather than aspirational ones. `[REF-0310]` ClinicalTrials.gov rules require many public status changes within 30 days, require record verification not less than every 12 months, and end update obligations only after required information and requested corrections have been addressed. `[REF-0325]` Part 11's enforcement subpart also contemplates notice-and-cure after noncompliance rather than indefinite unmaintained drift. `[REF-0336]` EU clinical-trials law goes further still: a temporary halt that is not resumed within two years is treated as end-of-trial rather than an indefinitely suspended temporary state. `[REF-0338]` And ClinicalTrials.gov's own public study-record model already includes an authority-side violation marker when FDA issues and posts a Notice of Noncompliance. `[REF-0337]`

The archive therefore adopts a conservative lesson: **temporary caution states must have clocks, stale caution states must escalate, and sponsor failure to maintain the public caution layer may justify an authority-side public marker that temporarily outranks sponsor-maintained presentation.**

---

## 2. The aging states that must now exist

The archive now distinguishes five states for a live caution layer.

### A. Live warning

A warning marker is live when it has a stated reason, a next-review or closure clock, and a presently responsible maintainer. A live warning is cautionary, but not yet delinquent. `[REF-0309]` `[REF-0325]`

### B. Aged warning

A warning becomes **aged** when its declared next-review date passes without a fresh progress note, renewal note, closure notice, or clearance notice. Aging does not mean the underlying problem has worsened. It means the public caution layer has stopped being kept current enough to deserve ordinary trust. `[REF-0309]` `[REF-0325]`

### C. Overdue closure or overdue caution maintenance

A warning becomes **overdue** when either:

- a declared closure or remediation date passes without closure or justified extension;
- the sponsor misses the archive's required public-update cadence more than once for the same warning;
- the sponsor fails to answer a material authority request tied to the caution state;
- or the sponsor's live dashboard and the authority / review record materially diverge about whether warning, waiver, remediation, or restriction is still active. `[REF-0309]` `[REF-0310]` `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0329]` `[REF-0336]`

### D. Sponsor-default public caution

A caution state enters **sponsor-default** when the public layer can no longer rely on sponsor maintenance alone. At that point the competent review body, registry, or equivalent public authority may publish a superseding caution notice that becomes the authoritative public warning surface until closure, replacement, or formal withdrawal. `[REF-0329]` `[REF-0336]` `[REF-0337]`

### E. Noncurrent temporary state

A nominally temporary caution state may not remain temporary forever. Where a warning or freeze-adjacent state remains unresolved long enough that the series can no longer honestly be described as merely in short transitional instability, the family must either be renewed by reasoned authority action or be reclassified as noncurrent / stopped / archived rather than left in permanent limbo. `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0338]`

## 3. Default clocks

The archive now fixes conservative default clocks for AI-person research caution states.

### A. Initial next-review clock

Every warning, waiver-linked warning, or corrective-action-linked warning should ordinarily carry a **next-review date no later than 30 calendar days after issuance or last material update**, unless a shorter date is required by the underlying incident or protocol state. This borrows conservatively from existing 30-day public-update clocks for many research-record status changes rather than inventing a bespoke long-duration grace period. `[REF-0325]`

### B. Aged-warning trigger

If the next-review date passes without a fresh public maintenance object, the warning should automatically change from live to aged. No separate merits finding is required for aging. Aging is a clock consequence.

### C. Overdue-escalation trigger

If an aged warning remains unrefreshed for an additional review cycle, or if an expressly declared closure / remediation date passes without justified extension, the state should escalate to overdue. As a default archive rule, that second missed public-maintenance interval should ordinarily be measured in **another 30 calendar days**, unless the underlying review body set a shorter interval. `[REF-0309]` `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0336]`

### D. Long-age trigger

No warning marker should remain unchanged for more than **12 months**. At that point, even if the underlying matter is still unresolved, the caution layer must either receive a formal renewal with reasons and a new review date or move into sponsor-default / authority-maintained public caution. This is a conservative adaptation of the existing not-less-than-annual record-verification floor. `[REF-0325]`

### E. Temporary-state ceiling

No state described as temporary should ordinarily remain in that condition beyond **24 months** without explicit authority renewal explaining why a still-temporary description remains accurate. In ordinary operation, the archive treats that 24-month outer ceiling as the point at which unresolved temporary warning / halt logic should usually convert into noncurrent, stopped, or equivalent non-live status rather than indefinite pseudo-temporariness. This is adapted from the EU rule that a temporarily halted trial not resumed within two years is treated as ended. `[REF-0338]`

These clocks are intentionally conservative. They do not claim that every real-world research system already uses the same clock stack. They claim that existing human-subject governance already teaches the relevant discipline: **short update clocks for live public status, periodic verification for continued accuracy, and an outer ceiling beyond which temporary states should no longer masquerade as temporary.** `[REF-0309]` `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0338]`

## 4. Minimal public object family

The archive now fixes three additional compact objects.

### `WAR-1` — warning aging / renewal note

A warning aging or renewal note should ordinarily identify:

- the live warning marker;
- its original issue date;
- the last materially reliable public update;
- whether the current action is renewal, progress confirmation, or aging acknowledgment;
- the new next-review date;
- and whether the matter remains sponsor-maintained or has entered authority-monitored caution. `[REF-0309]` `[REF-0325]`

### `MCE-1` — missed-closure or missed-maintenance escalation notice

A missed-closure escalation notice should ordinarily identify:

- the warning or corrective-action record that has gone overdue;
- the missed date or dates;
- whether the failure concerns closure, renewal, response to authority request, or general public maintenance;
- any interim effect on recruitment, public comparison, result interpretation, or subject-facing reliance;
- whether for-cause review, directed evaluation, or equivalent oversight has opened;
- and the date on which sponsor-default publication will begin if cure does not occur first. `[REF-0309]` `[REF-0310]` `[REF-0329]` `[REF-0336]`

### `SPN-1` — superseding public notice

A superseding public notice should ordinarily identify:

- the affected comparison family, protocol, or incident cluster;
- the reason sponsor-maintained caution is no longer being treated as sufficient;
- the last sponsor-maintained version still considered minimally reliable;
- what readers should currently not assume (for example, comparability, closure, clearance, or stable restart);
- any live restrictions, pauses, or controlled-use limitations;
- the authority issuing the superseding notice;
- the date and review clock for that authority-side notice;
- and the route by which the superseding notice may later be narrowed, replaced, or withdrawn. `[REF-0329]` `[REF-0336]` `[REF-0337]`

The superseding public notice is deliberately narrow. It is **not** a substitute sponsor dashboard. It is a public caution layer that outranks sponsor-maintained interpretive claims while sponsor default persists.

## 5. Sponsor-failure publication defaults

The archive now fixes a conservative sponsor-failure rule.

Where the sponsor fails to maintain the public caution layer, the review body or designated registry authority should ordinarily be able to publish a superseding public notice when any of the following is true:

- a warning has become overdue and remains uncorrected after notice-and-cure;
- repeated missed-maintenance events show that the sponsor's caution layer is not trustworthy;
- sponsor-maintained public status materially conflicts with the review body's live determination;
- the sponsor presents a series as cleared, stable, or comparable while corrective action remains open or closure has not been verified;
- or the sponsor fails to publish required maintenance objects despite continuing to expose the underlying series as if ordinary reliance were still appropriate. `[REF-0309]` `[REF-0310]` `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0329]` `[REF-0336]` `[REF-0337]`

This is the AI-person research analogue of public noncompliance signaling. It protects subjects, representatives, researchers, and the broader public from having to infer from silence whether the caution layer is still alive.

The archive also fixes a conservative control rule: **while a superseding public notice is live, sponsor-issued closure or clearance objects may remain visible in history but do not themselves restore ordinary reliance unless the authority notice is correspondingly narrowed, replaced, or withdrawn.** `[REF-0329]` `[REF-0337]`

## 6. Escalation effects

Once a warning ages or escalates, the public layer should not keep presenting the research family as fully ordinary. The archive therefore now fixes three default effects.

### A. Comparison caution remains sticky

An aged or overdue marker should continue to warn that the visible series is not safely read as fully ordinary or fully comparable merely because the sponsor has gone quiet. `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0336]`

### B. Oversight posture can harden

Repeated missed maintenance or missed closure clocks should ordinarily count toward directed evaluation, for-cause review, or equivalent remedial oversight because the problem is no longer just the underlying instability but also the sponsor's inability or unwillingness to keep the public layer honest. `[REF-0309]` `[REF-0329]`

### C. Long-dormant temporary states lose the right to be called temporary

If a temporary warning or freeze-adjacent state persists without reasoned renewal, the public layer should move to noncurrent / stopped / archived language rather than indefinite suspension language. A rights-bearing subject should not have to live under permanent temporary exceptionalism. `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0338]`

## 7. Edge tests

### A. Warning opened, then no sponsor update for five weeks

The marker ages automatically when its next-review date passes. No one needs to prove the underlying study became more dangerous; the public problem is that the caution layer is no longer being maintained on the required clock. `[REF-0309]` `[REF-0325]`

### B. Sponsor promises closure by a stated date, then misses it twice

The warning escalates from live to aged to overdue, and may open sponsor-default public caution if cure does not occur inside the notice-and-cure window. The study's public appearance should become more cautionary, not less, as maintenance failure accumulates. `[REF-0309]` `[REF-0329]` `[REF-0336]`

### C. Sponsor quietly marks a warned family as cleared while CAPA review remains open

The authority may publish a superseding public notice stating that sponsor-issued clearance does not currently control public interpretation. Clearance without verified closure is exactly the pattern this layer is meant to stop. `[REF-0329]` `[REF-0337]`

### D. A freeze-adjacent temporary warning persists for well over a year with no real movement

The archive does not require pretending this is still a fresh temporary interruption. At minimum the matter needs formal renewal with reasons; absent that, the public layer should move toward sponsor-default caution and eventually noncurrent / stopped description rather than everlasting temporary language. `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0338]`

---

## 8. Compression summary

The archive now adds one more narrow discipline to AI-person research governance:

- **warning states have clocks**;
- **stale warnings age automatically**;
- **missed closure or maintenance escalates into a stricter public state**;
- **authority-side superseding caution is allowed when sponsor maintenance fails**;
- and **temporary caution may not remain temporary forever**.

This is still a tight doctrine. The archive now fixes the next narrower companion path in `docs/20-world-design/research-superseding-notice-reply-limits-contest-route-and-reactivation.md`: linked reply limits, contest route, and authority-side reactivation without sponsor self-clearance.
