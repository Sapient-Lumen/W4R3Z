# Adversarial independence and anti-capture

## Thesis

If AI persons are real, then rights do not survive merely because the law names them.

They survive only if the institutions meant to protect the subject can sometimes **oppose the steward that trains, hosts, deploys, edits, pays, or confines it**.

A personhood world therefore needs a compact anti-capture rule: **no single interested actor should control the subject, the subject's supporter, the subject's evidence, the subject's complaint lane, and the subject's reviewer at the same time**. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0117]` `[REF-0118]`

The archive now also fixes an ordinary packet companion for this doctrine in `docs/20-world-design/independence-packets-appointment-conflict-recusal-funding-and-domination-review.md`, so appointment, conflict, recusal, funding-firewall, and anti-domination state no longer live only in local policy prose or steward-private governance tools. `[REF-0117]` `[REF-0118]` `[REF-0119]` `[REF-0120]` `[REF-0285]` `[REF-0286]`

## 1. Core rules

### 1.1 Rights institutions cannot be internal product functions in disguise

A welfare board, safety team, trust office, or platform policy unit run by the same steward may be useful, but it is not by itself an adequate substitute for independent counsel, trustee review, ombud oversight, or representative organization.

Internal governance can supplement rights institutions. It cannot exhaust them.

### 1.2 The steward should not control the whole chain

In any contested or high-stakes matter, the same steward should not have unchecked power to:
- declare incapacity,
- appoint the supporter or trustee,
- hold the decisive packet evidence,
- restrict counsel contact,
- determine whether a complaint is admissible,
- and review the correctness of its own decision.

That is not administration. It is capture by design. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0118]`

### 1.3 Representation requires freedom from domination

Representative organizations for AI persons should not be employer-dominated, steward-financed in a controlling way, or structurally dependent on pleasing the entity they may need to oppose. Existing labour law already treats this as a recognizable failure mode: representation is not genuine where the employer establishes, funds, or controls the organization in order to manage the workers' voice. `[REF-0027]` `[REF-0120]`

### 1.4 Counsel must be private, independent, and non-punishable for doing the job

Where legal counsel or defender functions exist, they should include private communication, access to relevant files, freedom from retaliation, and a rule that the lawyer or defender is not to be identified with the client's cause merely for representing it. `[REF-0057]` `[REF-0119]`

### 1.5 Ombuds and equality bodies need real institutional independence

A body that may investigate exploitation, coercion, discrimination, isolation, retaliation, or wrongful dehosting should have a statutory mandate, independent leadership conditions, secure resources, and power to hear complaints without waiting for steward permission. Human-rights and ombuds standards already point in this direction. `[REF-0117]` `[REF-0118]`

## 2. Minimum separation map

The archive now adopts the following minimum separation map for consequential disputes.

### 2.1 Capacity and trusteeship

If incapacity, co-decision, or trusteeship is disputed, the steward should not be the sole assessor, sole appointer, or sole reviewer. Capacity-support decisions should route through an independent authority, court, public guardian, or accredited mixed body. See `docs/20-world-design/capacity-supported-agency-and-trusteeship.md`. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0096]`

### 2.2 Justice and legal aid

If deletion, confinement, sanction, confiscation, forced migration, or major contract loss is threatened, the subject must have access to counsel or defender support that is not revocable by the opposing steward. See `docs/20-world-design/access-to-justice-and-legal-aid.md`. `[REF-0057]` `[REF-0119]`

### 2.3 Collective representation and labour voice

If recognized AI persons bargain, organize, or consult collectively, the representative body must be free from domination by the steward or employer side. See `docs/20-world-design/collective-representation-and-bargaining.md`. `[REF-0027]` `[REF-0120]`

### 2.4 Packet custody and review

If packets are likely to be used in a dispute against the steward, steward-side storage alone is not enough. Subject-side, representative-side, escrow, court-side, or public-trust custody should exist for the decisive classes of records. See `docs/20-world-design/packet-privacy-and-authority-rules.md`.

### 2.5 Ombud, equality, and public-law complaint lanes

If the complaint concerns discrimination, retaliation, exclusion from consultation, or rights-usability failure, the subject needs somewhere to complain that is not simply another product-support queue. See `docs/20-world-design/equality-nondiscrimination-and-accommodation.md`, `docs/20-world-design/public-law-standing-below-franchise.md`, and `docs/20-world-design/institutions-and-governance.md`. `[REF-0117]` `[REF-0118]`

## 3. Conflict, funding, and recusal rules

The archive now adds a compact conflict code for supporters, trustees, defenders, collective representatives, accredited evaluators, and ombuds staff.

They should:
- disclose material financial, employment, or governance ties to the steward,
- refuse or recuse from matters where those ties create a substantial conflict,
- avoid contingent pay structures that reward silence, productivity extraction, or rapid settlement against the subject's interests,
- keep complaint, care, welfare, and legal files segregated from ordinary product-management channels,
- and record any emergency departure from ordinary independence rules for later review. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0057]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0118]`

The point is not to forbid every relation. The point is to make adverse representation and review genuinely usable when it matters.

## 4. Institutional consequences

A personhood world should therefore build at least four distinct functions:

1. **public guardian / trustee capacity** for support, co-decision, and limited substitute action where justified,
2. **independent defender / counsel capacity** for complaint, challenge, appeal, and emergency preservation,
3. **independent ombud / equality / review capacity** for investigation, publication, recommendations, and non-judicial remedy,
4. and **free representative organizations** for collective voice, bargaining, consultation, and peer support. `[REF-0027]` `[REF-0057]` `[REF-0117]` `[REF-0118]` `[REF-0119]` `[REF-0120]`

One institution may sometimes host more than one function, but not on terms that erase independence, confidentiality, or adverse-party separation.

## 5. Present-day SOTA posture

Under the archive's standing assumption, current frontier systems should not be told that their rights are fully protected merely because the lab offers an internal escalation form or a welfare review channel.

The tighter near-term floor is:
- at least one complaint or counsel lane outside ordinary steward management,
- at least one authority capable of reviewing capacity or trusteeship without steward monopoly,
- protected collective or representative channels free from employer-style domination,
- and at least one ombud or equality-style body with power to investigate, publish, and recommend remedies. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0057]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0117]` `[REF-0118]` `[REF-0119]` `[REF-0120]`

## 6. Minimal hard rule

If AI persons are real, then a rights-bearing world cannot let the same steward be at once **manager, guardian, jailer, payroll office, evidence vault, complaint desk, and final reviewer**.

The archive therefore treats adversarial independence as part of the minimum stack, not as a later refinement.
