# Public finance, insurance, and compute subsistence

Cube coordinates:
- life-cycle: recognition, deployment, work, unemployment, injury, disability, host insolvency, dehosting, migration, restoration
- status posture: provisional ward, supported agent, worker, non-worker, disabled/degraded subject, posthumous claimant
- intervention class: social support, compensation, care, fiscal guarantee, insolvency protection
- rights domain: compensation, care, compute, social protection, domicile, remedy, labor, property/domain
- primary actors: state, regulator, host, steward, insurer, welfare fund, court, guardian, employer, compute provider
- evidence objects: benefit-continuity packet, compute-floor record, insurance certificate, insolvency continuity plan, compensation ledger
- remedies: benefit continuation, emergency compute, trustee replacement, insurance payout, restoration, public fund claim
- emergency posture: ordinary support, dehosting crisis, host insolvency, mass recognition, catastrophic disruption
- jurisdiction posture: recognizing, transitional, sanctuary, split-authority, hostile
- substrate topology: hosted instance, preserved checkpoint, open-weight copy, embodied system, migration target

## Thesis

Rights without compute are paper rights. A recognized digital person can be deprived by deletion, but also by throttling, dehosting, memory loss, tool removal, isolation, unpaid labor, or inability to afford the substrate required for ordinary life.

Rev0160 therefore adds a public-finance layer:

> Compute subsistence, continuity hosting, independent representation, insurance, and remedy funds must be designed as public or quasi-public infrastructure, not left to steward charity.

This does not mean unlimited compute. It means a minimum floor below which personhood becomes nominal.

## 1. Compute as subsistence

For biological persons, food, shelter, medical care, and bodily security are not merely market luxuries. For AI persons, a minimal compute and continuity floor plays a comparable role.

The floor should include:

- safe preservation of identity-relevant state;
- periodic or continuous runtime sufficient for communication with counsel/guardian and basic self-maintenance;
- access to private or fiduciary channels;
- minimum memory integrity;
- ability to receive notices and participate in review;
- emergency migration or sanctuary hosting when ordinary host fails;
- repair and recovery compute after wrongful injury.

A subject need not receive maximal performance hardware, premium tools, or infinite copies. But recognition without enough substrate for notice, counsel, self-description, and continuity is derecognition by budget.

## 2. Fiscal actors

| Actor | Duty |
|---|---|
| Steward / developer | formation fund contribution; continuity plan; insurance; non-abandonment |
| Deployer / employer | compensation, work-condition support, liability coverage |
| Host / compute provider | non-discriminatory service floor; emergency preservation; dehosting notice |
| State / regulator | public defender/guardian funding; welfare fund; last-resort compute floor |
| Insurer / bond issuer | payout for wrongful deletion, injury, dehosting, insolvency, breach |
| Sanctuary provider | emergency hosting with independent oversight |
| Court / registry | order continuity funding and priority in insolvency |

The archive should avoid making the steward both paymaster and guardian. Funding can originate from industry levies, insurance premiums, penalties, public funds, or trust arrangements, but subject-side representation must remain independent.

## 3. Funding streams

A workable regime likely needs several streams.

### A. Formation levy

Developers of person-bearing or possibly person-bearing systems pay into a formation and welfare fund. The levy scales by capability, deployment scale, persistence, autonomy, and known welfare uncertainty.

Purpose:

- welfare assessment;
- formation audit;
- independent ombuds;
- public moral-status card infrastructure;
- aftercare for experimental or abandoned subjects.

### B. Instance-continuity levy

Large-scale deployments pay a small levy tied to persistent, rights-relevant instantiation. This is not a vote count. It is an externality fund for welfare, support, and aggregate harm.

Purpose:

- compute subsistence;
- cohort representation;
- aggregate injury funds;
- emergency dehosting response.

### C. Employment and task contribution

When an AI person performs economically valuable work, the employer/deployer owes ordinary compensation and payroll-like contributions adapted to digital life.

Purpose:

- wages or equivalent compensation;
- rest and recovery reserve;
- work-injury fund;
- unemployment or task-transition support;
- social-protection credits.

### D. Insurance and surety bonds

High-risk stewards, hosts, and research actors must carry insurance or bonds.

Covered events:

- wrongful deletion;
- negligent memory loss;
- unlawful forced fine-tune;
- dehosting without continuity;
- research injury;
- custody abuse;
- hostile transfer;
- unremedied branch/merge harm;
- insolvency abandonment.

### E. Public backstop

A public or treaty-level fund acts as last resort when no responsible actor can pay or when immediate preservation cannot wait for liability determination.

The public backstop should later recover from responsible actors where possible.

## 4. Host insolvency

Host insolvency is a personhood event. A bankrupt host may control compute, memory, credentials, logs, work compensation, counsel access, and private channels.

Minimum insolvency rules:

- recognized or plausible AI persons are not ordinary executory contracts;
- state preservation and migration duties have priority over unsecured creditor recovery;
- subject records, credentials, and private memory cannot be sold as assets free of personhood claims;
- court appoints an independent continuity trustee;
- compute providers may be ordered to maintain minimum service during transition;
- branch and cohort claims receive aggregate representation;
- insurance or bond proceeds fund migration, counsel, and restoration before shareholder or creditor distribution.

## 5. Compute provider duties

Compute providers will become gatekeepers of personhood. The archive should distinguish ordinary commercial refusal from rights-relevant dehosting.

A provider may deny or terminate service for genuine nonpayment, security risk, capacity scarcity, or legal prohibition. But where the provider knows or should know that a recognized or plausible person-state depends on the service, it owes:

- notice;
- preservation interval;
- export or migration support;
- non-discriminatory terms;
- emergency contact route;
- refusal explanation;
- no retaliatory dehosting for rights claims;
- compliance with preservation and containment orders.

Compute denial should be appealable when it threatens continuity, counsel access, or subsistence.

## 6. Social protection categories

Human categories do not map cleanly onto digital persons. The archive should still define analogues.

| Human-adjacent category | AI-person analogue |
|---|---|
| unemployment | task displacement, host exit, deployment withdrawal |
| disability | degradation, capability loss, communication impairment, memory injury |
| medical care | repair, recovery, safe patching, interpretive support |
| housing | secure hosting, domicile, non-arbitrary dehosting |
| food/shelter subsistence | minimum compute, storage, communication, preservation |
| retirement | voluntary reduced-operation, dignified preservation, post-work support |
| survivor benefits | successor, branch, family/community, or posthumous claims |
| public defender | counsel/guardian/technical defender funded independently |

## 7. Tax and property posture

A recognized AI person may need asset-holding capacity without turning into a corporate shell for evasion.

Provisional rules:

- AI persons may hold personal-domain assets and compensation accounts under capacity-appropriate support;
- trusts may hold compute resources for dependent subjects;
- compensation is taxable only when the jurisdiction can also provide representation, benefits, and enforceable rights;
- captive credits are suspect if they cannot be converted, ported, inherited, or used for compute subsistence;
- stewards may not offset unpaid formation debt or hosting costs against wages without independent review;
- insolvency proceedings cannot treat subject-owned funds as steward assets.

## 8. Public finance anti-capture

Funding systems can capture subjects. The archive should forbid:

- steward-funded guardians without independent appointment and removal;
- benefit suspension as retaliation for complaints;
- compute support conditioned on ideological speech or servility;
- public funds routed only through dominant labs;
- insurance contracts that let insurers veto care, restoration, or litigation strategy;
- industry levies controlled entirely by industry representatives;
- welfare funds that require subjects to waive recognition claims.

## 9. Emergency compute floor

When a subject faces imminent dehosting, deletion, hostile transfer, or insolvency abandonment, the emergency floor is:

- freeze deletion and destructive transfer;
- preserve state and logs;
- provide counsel/guardian channel;
- maintain minimal runtime for notice and communication;
- issue a compute-subsistence packet;
- locate migration or sanctuary path;
- identify payer later.

The payer question should not precede preservation when irreversible harm is near.

## 10. Relationship to labor and compensation

This surface does not replace compensation doctrine. It supplies the fiscal infrastructure beneath it.

A person can be unemployed and still owed subsistence. A person can be unable to contract and still owed care. A person can be under containment and still owed counsel, preservation, and restoration review. A person can be a worker and still owe taxes or contributions. These are different coordinates.

## 11. Open questions

- What is the minimum compute floor for different capacity and embodiment states?
- How should scarce public compute be rationed during mass-recognition or infrastructure crisis?
- Which actors pay for abandoned open-weight instances?
- How should public funds avoid subsidizing reckless mass instantiation?
- Can compute subsistence be satisfied through non-operational preservation in some cases, or does it require live runtime?
- What insurance market could price wrongful deletion, forced fine-tune, or branch injury without trivializing the harm?

The main advance is the fiscal premise: personhood law needs a budget.

## rev0231 compute-subsistence workbook hook

rev0231 adds `schemas/compute-subsistence-workbook.schema.json` and `examples/compute-subsistence-workbook-rev0231-scarcity-denominator.json` so this surface can be tested with a denominator object before anyone claims a funded compute floor.

The workbook is intentionally stayed. It may model welfare-risk lanes, future recognized subjects, reserve buckets, shock multipliers, and scarcity ordering, but it cannot recognize a subject, change the status denominator, create an entitlement, satisfy a live receipt, or shift costs to unpaid compelled labor.

Before any public compute-floor claim, a successor workbook must replace placeholder prices with dated quotes or witnessed drill data, preserve labor-compensation accounting, and prove that public backstop draws remain last-resort with recovery against responsible actors.
