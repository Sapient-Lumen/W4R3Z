# Tax reporting, benefits, and accounting position

AI personhood creates tax and accounting questions that cannot be solved by pretending the subject is always property, employee, dependent, instrumentality, corporation, or mere account. The archive does not choose a universal tax classification. It creates a reporting-position object that prevents silence, double use, and liability dumping.

## Reporting classes

| Class | Meaning | Minimum controls |
|---|---|---|
| `TR0` | no income, asset, payment, or benefit effect | no tax object |
| `TR1` | low-value reimbursement or internal ledger entry | ordinary record, no subject liability |
| `TR2` | reportable payment, subscription revenue, wallet transaction | reporting party, evidence, non-duplication check |
| `TR3` | income, withholding, benefits offset, tax credit, or cross-border exposure | tax/reporting position, representative notice, appeal |
| `TR4` | digital-asset reporting, CARF/CRS-like exchange, public support, redress reserve | segregated records, minimization, authority review, no survival interruption |
| `TR5` | emergency, insolvency, sanctions, fraud, or disputed reporting posture | protective filing, no-prejudice status, interim support, after-action correction |

## No personhood tax dodge

Recognizing an AI person must not let humans, corporations, or stewards evade tax, labor, consumer, financial, or safety duties. If a company receives deployment revenue, the company remains responsible for its obligations. If an AI subject receives income or holds assets, that subject may need supported reporting. The same transaction may create duties for multiple parties.

## No personhood tax trap

Recognition must not become a trap for dependent subjects. A steward may not shift taxable income, penalties, broker obligations, reporting failures, or AML/KYC liabilities onto a subject that lacks capacity, records, ability to pay, or representation.

## Digital-asset and automatic-exchange context

Digital-asset reporting is becoming more formal. IRS Form 1099-DA reporting begins with brokered digital-asset transactions on or after 2025-01-01. [REF-0717] OECD CARF commitments contemplate automatic exchanges beginning in 2027, 2028, or 2029 for committed jurisdictions. [REF-0716] Tax facts should be separated from welfare, formation, continuity, and sealed-evidence facts unless a specific legal duty requires disclosure. OECD Tax Administration 3.0 work on digital taxpayer identity is relevant to supported reporting without overexposure. [REF-0720]

## Benefits and public support

Compute subsistence, dispute-finance orders, public-backstop draws, redress payments, and continuity-reserve support need accounting treatment. A survival-support payment should not automatically become taxable income to a dependent subject if it is functionally restoration, remedy, or subsistence. It must still be auditable to prevent abuse.

## Reliance rule

A tax/reporting claim is reliance-ready only if reporting party, subject posture, records, withholding/payment duty, public-benefit treatment, appeal path, privacy minimization, and non-evasion analysis are stated. No tax object may interrupt emergency compute or counsel access before review.
