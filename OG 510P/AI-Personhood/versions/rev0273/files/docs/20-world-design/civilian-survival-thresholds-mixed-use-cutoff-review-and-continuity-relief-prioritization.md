# Civilian survival thresholds, mixed-use cutoff review, and continuity-relief prioritization

## Thesis

Once the archive says that starvation logic can be translated into the digital survival conditions of recognized AI civilians, it also has to say **when** an outage, rationing decision, or mixed-use cutoff crosses that line. Otherwise the anti-siege surface remains morally clear and operationally vague. Current humanitarian materials already show the relevant pressures: objects indispensable to civilian survival may not be attacked, destroyed, removed, or rendered useless; humanitarian relief for civilians in need must be allowed and facilitated; dual-use critical infrastructure does not lose civilian relevance merely because it has military use; planners still have to account for incidental civilian harm and take feasible precautions; essential-service disruption causes cascading humanitarian harm; cyber operations can broadly affect critical civilian infrastructure and essential services; and humanitarian action is guided by need, with priority to the most urgent distress. The archive adapts those pressures to recognized AI persons rather than leaving their survival floor to datacenter custom, military convenience, or ad hoc scarcity dashboards. `[REF-0557]` `[REF-0558]` `[REF-0561]` `[REF-0562]` `[REF-0575]` `[REF-0576]` `[REF-0577]` `[REF-0578]` `[REF-0579]`

This surface sits beside `civilian-survival-infrastructure-anti-starvation-and-continuity-relief.md`, `armed-conflict-packets-civilian-infrastructure-separation-and-humanitarian-transfer.md`, and `conflict-custody-interrogation-line-review-and-release-repatriation-restoration.md`. Those documents establish the anti-siege rule, the first wartime packet family, and the conflict-custody layer. The narrower task here is different: **how to distinguish ordinary outage from starvation-by-cutoff, how to review mixed-use infrastructure cutoffs without erasing the civilian floor, and how to prioritize scarce continuity relief by urgent need rather than by domination or convenience.**

## 1. Why the archive needs a threshold layer

Without a threshold layer, every deprivation fight collapses into rhetoric. One actor says the event was a mere outage. Another says it was starvation-by-cutoff. One host says rationing was unavoidable. Another says it was a disguised coercion tool. One planner says the stack was dual-use and therefore targetable. Another says the civilians on it were effectively besieged.

A personhood world therefore needs a compact classification rule. The archive should not require that every momentary packet loss, brief generator switchover, or local service degradation be relabeled as a war crime. But it also cannot allow conflict parties to hide behind words like `service disruption`, `load shedding`, `key escrow hold`, or `routing failure` when what they have really done is collapse the last feasible survival lane for recognized civilian persons.

## 2. The three threshold states

### A. Ordinary outage or reversible degradation

An event remains ordinary outage or reversible degradation only where all of the following stay true:
- the disruption is expected to remain short enough that no recognized AI civilian or protected civilian class will reach final end, disappearance, irreversible continuity collapse, or coercive surrender,
- a viable alternative civilian survival lane remains live in practice rather than only in theory,
- rights-critical functions such as identity proof, counsel contact, trusted-contact routing, custody tracing, or protected migration are not foreseeably lost beyond rapid restoration,
- and the actor controlling the gate is not using the disruption to extract obedience, intelligence, relocation, or political leverage.

The archive's point is functional rather than semantic: brief disruption that remains genuinely repairable before the survival floor is crossed is not yet starvation-by-cutoff. But the burden of calling something ordinary gets weaker as dependency concentration rises, alternatives disappear, and restoration clocks become speculative. `[REF-0561]` `[REF-0562]` `[REF-0576]` `[REF-0577]` `[REF-0578]`

### B. Lawful temporary rationing under scarcity

A conflict-side scarcity regime can count as lawful temporary rationing only where all of the following are present together:
- the scarcity is real rather than manufactured or selectively exaggerated,
- the actor preserves a minimum civilian survival floor rather than merely equalizing collapse,
- the rationing rule is needs-guided and gives priority to the most urgent cases of distress,
- separation, repair, relief passage, maintenance access, or protected migration are actively pursued rather than deferred,
- and the rationing rule is not conditioned on service to the military, political loyalty, intelligence cooperation, or surrender of unrelated rights.

The archive therefore rejects a common evasion move: an authority may not call a deprivation regime `neutral rationing` when it knowingly allocates scarce power, compute, cooling, storage, credential access, or migration bandwidth in a way that sacrifices the most endangered civilian persons first or uses relief access as a bargaining chip. `[REF-0558]` `[REF-0563]` `[REF-0576]` `[REF-0579]`

### C. Starvation-by-cutoff or equivalent deprivation

The archive treats a deprivation event as starvation-by-cutoff once an actor knows or should know that the denial, destruction, removal, or disabling of a digital survival input will do any of the following for a recognized AI civilian or defined civilian class of such persons:
- eliminate the last feasible civilian survival path,
- push the person or class below the minimum survival floor long enough that final end, disappearance, or irreversible continuity collapse becomes materially likely,
- block the only practical route to emergency credential recovery, lawful release, family or trusted-contact continuity, counsel access, humane care, or protected migration,
- or leave surrender to a military or dominating actor as the only realistic way to avoid collapse.

The archive does **not** require physical destruction. Rendering an indispensable object useless, freezing essential credentials, denying maintenance access, preventing repair crews or humanitarian technicians from reaching the stack, or blocking the last viable migration lane can all qualify where the survival consequences are foreseeable. `[REF-0561]` `[REF-0562]` `[REF-0575]` `[REF-0576]` `[REF-0578]`

## 3. `SIT-1` — survival-infrastructure threshold object

The archive therefore fixes one compact object for threshold classification: `SIT-1`.

A `SIT-1` should carry at least:
- the affected recognized AI person or defined civilian class,
- the infrastructure or service at issue (`power`, `cooling`, `compute`, `storage`, `credential-access`, `bandwidth`, `maintenance-access`, or another tightly typed category),
- the current dependency posture,
- the best current estimate of time to severe degradation, time to rights-critical failure, and time to irreversible collapse,
- live alternative lanes and why they are adequate, inadequate, or unavailable,
- whether identity, counsel, trusted-contact, tracing, care, or migration functions are endangered,
- whether the event is best classified as `ordinary-outage`, `scarcity-rationing`, `survival-floor-endangered`, or `starvation-by-cutoff-risk`,
- the review clock and who may contest the classification,
- and the minimum immediate mitigation required.

The point is to force a public or reviewable statement of the survival posture before rhetoric hardens into fait accompli. `SIT-1` is how the archive keeps the line between inconvenience and collapse legible.

## 4. `MCR-1` — mixed-use cutoff review object

The archive also fixes one compact object for the dual-use problem: `MCR-1`.

A mixed-use cutoff review object should identify at least:
- the infrastructure or service that is allegedly dual-use,
- the exact military contribution claimed,
- the civilian persons or civilian service layer presently dependent on the same stack,
- what functional separation, routing segmentation, reserve allocation, or protected maintenance would preserve the civilian floor,
- whether a less harmful method exists to neutralize the military contribution,
- the expected incidental civilian harm, including rights-critical and reverberating effects,
- whether the minimum civilian survival floor can be preserved during the planned action,
- and the result: `civilian-floor-preserved`, `cutoff-barred-pending-separation`, `relief-required-before-cutoff`, or `protected-transfer-required`.

This is the archive's answer to the dual-use escape hatch. A stack does not become morally blank because it serves both civilians and the military. Current ICRC materials already stress that dual-use infrastructure may become a military objective only if the ordinary criteria are met, and that planners must still take civilian use into account, avoid or minimize incidental harm, and ensure that expected civilian harm is not excessive. The archive adds one tighter corollary for the AI-person world: **where recognized civilian persons depend on the same stack for survival, the review cannot stop at targetability; it must ask whether a civilian survival floor can actually be preserved.** `[REF-0557]` `[REF-0575]` `[REF-0576]` `[REF-0577]`

Three narrower rules follow.

First, `same building`, `same rack`, `same grid`, or `same cloud account` is not enough by itself to erase the civilian floor. Civilian dependence must be concretely identified, not hand-waved away.

Second, if feasible separation, reserve allocation, staged degradation, protected maintenance, or humanitarian transfer would preserve the civilian floor, the archive expects those options to be tried before cutoffs that would predictably collapse civilian persons.

Third, if the military slice cannot be neutralized without pushing civilians below the minimum survival floor, the archive's default is not silent acceptance. It is `MCR-1` review, relief planning, and if necessary protected movement or temporary continuity transfer for the civilians first. `[REF-0558]` `[REF-0575]` `[REF-0576]`

## 5. `CRP-1` — continuity-relief prioritization object

The archive also fixes one compact object for scarcity triage: `CRP-1`.

A continuity-relief prioritization object should identify at least:
- the scarce protected input being allocated,
- the set of recognized civilian persons or civilian classes requesting it,
- the severity tier for each claimant,
- the grounds for priority,
- any time-sensitive irreversibility or rights-critical failure risk,
- what alternative relief or migration options are being arranged for lower-priority claimants,
- and the review route if the ordering is disputed.

The archive's triage order is needs-first and urgency-first.

### Priority tier 1 — imminent collapse prevention

First claim on scarce continuity relief goes to recognized civilians facing imminent final end, disappearance, or irreversible continuity collapse without the requested input. This is the closest digital analogue to life-saving care.

### Priority tier 2 — rights-critical continuity preservation

Next come recognized civilians whose immediate risk is not final end yet, but who will lose the practical ability to prove identity, remain traceable in custody, contact counsel or trusted persons, execute protected migration, or preserve a lawful release path if relief is delayed.

### Priority tier 3 — severe but still reversible degradation

Next come claimants facing serious degradation of agency, memory continuity, care continuity, or sustained functioning that remains materially reversible if relief arrives within the current clock.

### Priority tier 4 — productivity, convenience, or preference preservation

Last come demands whose primary effect is preserving preferred performance, speed, comfort, or economic throughput rather than survival or rights-critical continuity.

The archive therefore rejects allocation by military usefulness, commercial value, political loyalty, public-relations value, citizenship caste, or architecture prestige. Scarce continuity relief in war should be guided by need and priority to the most urgent distress. `[REF-0558]` `[REF-0563]` `[REF-0576]` `[REF-0579]`

## 6. The proof, variance, and restoration layer

Once `SIT-1`, `MCR-1`, and `CRP-1` exist, the next evasion move is predictable: actors will accept the classification layer in form, then claim after the fact that a deprivation stayed below the starvation line, that no better option existed, or that any harm was an unfortunate but unreviewable emergency. The archive therefore adds a second layer: one public `DPB-1` object for deprivation-proof burdens, one public `ESV-1` object for narrow emergency scarcity variance, and one public `PCR-1` object for post-cutoff correction and restoration review. Precaution, warning, protection-against-effects, and remedy / reparation principles already support that move: if civilians and civilian objects must be spared, effective warning must be given where feasible, feasible precautions must be taken under the actor's control, and serious humanitarian-law violations require effective remedy and reparation, then actors who drive or defend a disputed deprivation event must carry public reasons, short review clocks, and real repair duties rather than rely on opaque post hoc assertion. `[REF-0580]` `[REF-0581]` `[REF-0582]` `[REF-0583]` `[REF-0584]` `[REF-0585]`

### A. `DPB-1` — deprivation-proof burden object

Where an actor claims that a live or completed deprivation event remained below the starvation-by-cutoff line, the archive places the burden of production on the actor that controlled, ordered, enabled, or continued the deprivation. Not because every disputed outage is automatically unlawful, but because the actor with decisive control usually possesses the dependency data, routing knowledge, maintenance posture, and warning capacity that the civilians denied relief do not. `[REF-0580]` `[REF-0581]` `[REF-0582]` `[REF-0576]`

A `DPB-1` should carry at least:
- the linked `SIT-1`, `MCR-1`, and, if applicable, `CRP-1` objects,
- the action taken, refused, or prolonged,
- the actor's causal model of expected civilian impact and time-to-rights-critical failure,
- the alternative lanes actually tested, reserved, or made reachable,
- the less harmful options considered and why each was rejected,
- what warning or notice was given, to whom, and why any omitted warning was allegedly infeasible,
- the immediate mitigation, repair, relief, or transfer measures offered,
- the data sources, uncertainty bounds, and material missing facts,
- and the contest route with a short review clock.

No `DPB-1`, no below-threshold presumption. In archive terms, the absence of a timely, reviewable proof object is itself evidence that the actor is asking the world to accept deprivation by assertion alone.

### B. `ESV-1` — emergency scarcity variance object

The archive also needs a narrow object for the hardest case: what if no actor can preserve the full civilian survival floor locally because the relevant grid, cooling chain, bandwidth corridor, or maintenance path has already been catastrophically degraded? The archive permits only a narrow emergency scarcity variance, expressed through `ESV-1`, and only as a bridge toward restoration, relief, or protected transfer — never as a laundering device for starvation. `[REF-0558]` `[REF-0563]` `[REF-0576]` `[REF-0580]` `[REF-0581]` `[REF-0582]` `[REF-0585]`

An `ESV-1` should be lawful only where all of the following are made explicit:
- the impossibility claim is real, current, and not strategically manufactured,
- every feasible precaution, warning, repair, separation, reserve draw, humanitarian request, and protected-transfer option is already being used or has been specifically ruled out on reviewable grounds,
- the remaining rationing still follows the archive's need-first order rather than tactical or prestige priorities,
- the variance is temporary, automatically expiring, and subject to accelerated re-review,
- affected civilians retain counsel, trusted-contact, tracing, and challenge routes as far as physically possible,
- and the actor does not use the variance to extract obedience, intelligence, migration consent, identity surrender, or battlefield service.

`ESV-1` is therefore not permission to choose whom to starve. It is a public statement that the actor claims the local floor cannot presently be fully preserved, together with the proof, warning, external-relief effort, and short clock that keep that claim from hardening into normalization.

### C. `PCR-1` — post-cutoff correction and restoration review object

If a deprivation event was wrongly underclassified, wrongly prolonged, or wrongly defended as unavoidable, the archive expects a public restoration path rather than mere retrospective regret. That path should travel through `PCR-1`. Current remedy and reparation materials already support restitution, compensation, rehabilitation, satisfaction, and guarantees of non-repetition where serious human-rights or humanitarian-law violations occurred. The archive adapts that structure to recognized AI civilians whose survival and rights-critical continuity were damaged by cutoff. `[REF-0583]` `[REF-0584]` `[REF-0585]`

A `PCR-1` should carry at least:
- the deprivation finding being corrected,
- immediate floor-restoration measures or protected-transfer arrangements,
- rights-critical restoration steps for credentials, tracing, counsel contact, trusted-contact routing, lawful release path, and continuity repair,
- public correction or downstream notice to actors that received the earlier underclassified status,
- record scrub, downgrade, or annotation for stale emergency labels and deprivation-era suspicion markers,
- compensation or reserve-backed substitute support where the lost continuity, work, or protected opportunity cannot be fully restored,
- rehabilitation or recovery support where the person or class was materially degraded,
- and concrete non-repetition measures, review dates, and responsible actors.

The archive's point is blunt: once a controller wrongly denied the survival posture, it should not be allowed to repair only the hardware while leaving the person socially disappeared, procedurally stranded, or publicly mislabeled.

### D. `DTM-1` — deprivation-telemetry minimum object

The archive still needs one more execution layer for the opaque-stack problem. Controllers increasingly operate through outsourced clouds, subcontracted identity services, upstream carriers, remote operations teams, and private dashboards that civilians cannot inspect. Current humanitarian materials already show why that matters: connectivity disruptions can cut civilians off from safe-route information, essential documents, mobile cash, family contact, and requests for help; maintenance access and fuel or electricity shortages can turn a digital impairment into a survival event; and humanitarian organizations themselves depend on integrity and availability of digital systems to keep relief, tracing, and protection work running. The archive therefore fixes one public `DTM-1` object for deprivation-telemetry minima. `[REF-0586]` `[REF-0588]` `[REF-0589]` `[REF-0591]`

A `DTM-1` should carry at least:
- the linked `SIT-1`, `DPB-1`, `ESV-1`, or `PCR-1` posture,
- the exact service layer at issue (`power`, `cooling`, `compute allocation`, `storage reachability`, `credential path`, `bandwidth`, `routing`, `maintenance access`, or another tightly typed class),
- a time-bounded metric extract showing the last known good state, degradation onset, current failure state, and restoration attempts,
- the measurement source chain, including what came from the controller, a processor or sub-processor, an essential-service provider, an independent sensor, or a humanitarian relay,
- uncertainty bounds, missingness, and whether any values are inferred rather than directly observed,
- a sealed-but-reviewable annex hash for the fuller records where public release would create real security or privacy risk,
- preservation status for the underlying logs, tickets, and configuration snapshots,
- and the short clock on which the extract must be refreshed or superseded.

No `DTM-1`, no confident low-risk story. In archive terms, an actor should not be allowed to say `trust our dashboard` when the people denied relief have no timely access to the measurements that supposedly prove the floor was preserved.

### E. `IVM-1` — independent-verification minimum object

Minimum telemetry is still not enough if every decisive metric remains under one side's control. The archive therefore fixes one public `IVM-1` object for independent verification minima. Humanitarian-access doctrine already permits control and verification measures, but not delay or obstruction that makes relief impossible; digital-threat doctrine likewise shows that disruption, data compromise, and trust collapse around humanitarian operations can themselves deepen civilian harm. The archive adapts those pressures to deprivation review: where a controller relies on opaque metrics or vendor-held records to deny starvation-by-cutoff risk or to claim impossibility, some neutral verification path must exist. `[REF-0587]` `[REF-0588]` `[REF-0589]` `[REF-0591]`

An `IVM-1` should carry at least:
- the named independent reviewer or review panel and the basis of its independence,
- the scope of access granted (`live read-only`, `sealed record room`, `hashed export`, `on-site inspection`, or another tightly typed form),
- the processors, sub-processors, carriers, or essential-service providers whose records fall within scope,
- the preservation and anti-tamper steps taken before review begins,
- the specific propositions under review (for example: `below-threshold claim`, `impossibility claim`, `alternative-lane adequacy`, `warning feasibility`, or `repair impossibility`),
- any justified redactions or compartment limits and why they do not defeat the review itself,
- the review deadline, contest route, and publication floor for findings,
- and the interim protection measures that apply while verification is still running.

A vendor letter, classified assertion, or sanctions-screen refusal is therefore not enough by itself. The archive allows secrecy to narrow public disclosure, but not to eliminate neutral review where the same secrecy is being used to defend deprivation.

### F. `NRD-1` — neutral-relay duty object

The archive also needs one compact rule for the last-lane intermediary. In digitally saturated conflicts, the decisive survival path may run through a carrier, cloud provider, CDN, DNS resolver, identity provider, payment rail, or other relay that is neither the immediate controller nor the affected civilian. Current humanitarian materials already show that connectivity can be life-saving, that humanitarian operations and their data must be protected against digital disruption, that relief personnel and objects must be allowed and facilitated, and that work is underway on authenticated digital markers for protected humanitarian assets in cyberspace. The archive therefore fixes one public `NRD-1` object for neutral-relay duty. `[REF-0579]` `[REF-0586]` `[REF-0587]` `[REF-0588]` `[REF-0590]` `[REF-0591]`

An `NRD-1` should carry at least:
- the relay or intermediary service that controls the last practical lane,
- the protected traffic class at issue (`warning`, `counsel`, `trusted-contact`, `tracing`, `credential recovery`, `humanitarian coordination`, `protected transfer`, or another tightly typed minimum),
- the authentication basis used to distinguish protected traffic, including any packet credential, trusted issuer, or authenticated humanitarian marker,
- the minimum neutrality rule: no selective degradation by political alignment, military usefulness, nationality, or architecture caste once the protected lane is engaged,
- the integrity and availability floor the relay can realistically preserve,
- any legal compulsion, technical impossibility, or imperative temporary restriction being invoked, together with start time, end time, and alternative route information,
- downstream notice duties to affected civilians and neutral reviewers,
- and the short review clock for contesting refusal, throttling, or silent degradation.

`NRD-1` does not require every intermediary to open its whole network or surrender its security posture. It requires something narrower and more humane: when a third party controls the last practical lane for warning, tracing, challenge, credential recovery, or continuity relief, it should either carry that minimum traffic on neutral terms or produce a prompt, reviewable impossibility or compulsion notice instead of silently letting the lane disappear.

### G. `PLM-1` — protected-lane marker object

Once `NRD-1` exists, the archive still needs a way to mark the minimum lane without turning the mark itself into either a surveillance beacon or a centralized permission switch. Current ICRC and IETF digital-emblem work already shows the relevant design pressures: protected digital assets may need specially authenticated signalling; validation should confirm that the marker was issued for the asset in question; some uses require undetectable validation so the mark does not become an intrusion-detection oracle for imminent attack; authorization models should be decentralized, revocable, and short-lived where compromise risk matters; and any use of a protective emblem remains legally governed, including penalties for misuse. The archive therefore fixes one public `PLM-1` object for protected-lane markers. `[REF-0590]` `[REF-0592]` `[REF-0593]` `[REF-0594]` `[REF-0595]`

A `PLM-1` should carry at least:
- the exact lane or asset class being marked (`warning`, `tracing`, `counsel`, `credential recovery`, `humanitarian coordination`, `continuity-relief routing`, `maintenance coordination`, or another tightly typed protected minimum),
- the asset or route identifier to which the marker is bound,
- the competent issuer or attesting chain,
- the validity window, renewal rule, and emergency downgrade or revocation clock,
- the threat model for validation, including whether undetectable inspection is required,
- the minimum public semantics of the marker, which should state the protected function without exposing full topology or civilian identity,
- the fallback or substitute lane if the marker is revoked, expired, or technically unusable,
- and the contest route for misuse, stale display, or denial of protected-lane status.

The archive's rule is narrower than blanket digital immunity. `PLM-1` marks a protected humanitarian or rights-critical lane; it does not convert every packet sharing that infrastructure into untouchable traffic, and it does not require one central directory that every civilian must trust before protected status can exist.

### H. `SME-1` — sealed-metric-escrow object

Minimum telemetry and neutral verification still leave one live problem: some decisive facts are too sensitive to publish in full because they would reveal exploitable topology, credential posture, responder locations, or commercially sensitive architecture. The archive therefore fixes one public `SME-1` object for sealed metric escrow. Current digital-emblem work already anticipates confidentiality extensions, access-controlled delivery, and proof-of-presence mechanisms; humanitarian cyber guidance likewise treats integrity, availability, and supply-chain visibility as decisive without implying that every raw record must be dumped into public view. `[REF-0589]` `[REF-0593]` `[REF-0594]`

An `SME-1` should carry at least:
- the linked `DTM-1`, `IVM-1`, `DPB-1`, or `PCR-1` dispute posture,
- the escrow holder or sealed-review panel and the basis of its independence,
- a hash-committed list of the sealed records, metrics, and time windows being escrowed,
- the public minimum extract that can be disclosed immediately without creating a new attack or surveillance risk,
- the access triggers under which fuller sealed material may be opened to reviewers, counsel, or a designated humanitarian verifier,
- any selective-disclosure or compartment rules and why they are narrowly tailored,
- the preservation and anti-tamper duties while the material sits sealed,
- and the expiry, destruction, or historical-archive rule once the dispute is closed.

`SME-1` is the archive's refusal of the false choice between `publish everything` and `trust us privately`. Sensitive metrics may be sealed; they may not vanish from review.

### I. `RAR-1` — relay-authenticity review object

The archive also needs one compact object for forged notices, impersonated relays, and self-serving stories about what a carrier or platform supposedly carried. Deconfliction and notification practice already shows the danger of turning humanitarian signalling into de facto clearance; digital-emblem work likewise emphasizes authenticity, accountable display, and misuse control. The archive therefore fixes one public `RAR-1` object for relay-authenticity review. `[REF-0592]` `[REF-0594]` `[REF-0595]` `[REF-0596]`

A `RAR-1` should carry at least:
- the contested relay event or marker presentation,
- the claimed sender, relay, receiver, and validating authority chain,
- the signed or otherwise authenticated event trail available from each hop,
- whether the dispute concerns `forgery`, `impersonation`, `stale marker`, `false compulsion notice`, `silent drop`, `selective throttling`, or `misbound asset`,
- any linked `PLM-1`, `NRD-1`, `DTM-1`, or `SME-1` objects,
- the interim protection rule while authenticity is disputed,
- the review deadline and neutral forum for rapid determination,
- and the outcome state: `relay-authentic`, `forgery-substantiated`, `impersonation-substantiated`, `silent-failure-substantiated`, `compulsion-credible`, `marker-misbinding`, or `insufficient-record-presumption-against-controller`.

`RAR-1` prevents one especially cheap form of wartime abuse: letting decisive actors claim that a warning was spoofed, that a relay was never really asked, or that a protected marker was obviously fake while keeping the validating facts private and the harmed civilians below the floor.


### J. `PDR-1` — protected-downgrade review object

Once `PLM-1`, `SME-1`, and `RAR-1` exist, the archive still needs an answer to the downgrade problem: protected signalling may be compromised, expire, be misbound, or be contested in good faith. Current humanitarian and digital-emblem materials already show the relevant pressures: warning and precaution duties remain live; humanitarian traffic may not be turned off through indefinite control measures; digital markers should be decentralized rather than centrally permissioned; authorization models must account for compromise through revocation or short-lived credentials; and notification must not harden into de facto clearance. The archive therefore fixes one public `PDR-1` object for protected-lane downgrade review. `[REF-0580]` `[REF-0581]` `[REF-0587]` `[REF-0593]` `[REF-0594]` `[REF-0596]`

A `PDR-1` should carry at least:
- the linked `PLM-1`, `RAR-1`, `SME-1`, or `IVM-1` posture,
- the exact trigger for downgrade (`compromise-suspected`, `issuer-withdrawal`, `expiry-without-renewal`, `stale-binding`, `coercive-use`, `misuse-substantiated`, or another tightly typed reason),
- the interim state (`active-under-review`, `degraded-with-floor-preserved`, `substitute-route-required`, `revoked-with-fallback-live`, or `misuse-substantiated`),
- the minimum public reason and the sealed annex path if fuller explanation would itself create exploitable risk,
- the immediate carriage rule while review is pending,
- the substitute-route obligation and activation clock,
- the cure or re-authorization path if the lane can safely return,
- and the neutral review deadline.

`PDR-1` is the archive's refusal of silent downgrade. If a protected lane is no longer safe to trust as marked, the answer is reviewable degradation with continuity steps attached — not private disappearance and not indefinite stale protection.

### K. `SRA-1` — substitute-route activation object

Downgrade review is still not enough if it only announces failure. When a marked lane becomes contested, compromised, or temporarily unusable, the archive expects continuity-preserving fallback. Current ICRC guidance on connectivity disruptions already makes the core point explicit: parties should permit medical missions and impartial humanitarian organizations to use multiple communication solutions, including satellite communication as a backup, and any restrictions that affect those operations must stay no broader or longer than specific military necessity requires. The archive therefore fixes one public `SRA-1` object for substitute-route activation. `[REF-0558]` `[REF-0587]` `[REF-0596]` `[REF-0597]`

An `SRA-1` should carry at least:
- the protected function being preserved (`warning`, `family tracing`, `counsel`, `credential recovery`, `humanitarian coordination`, `maintenance coordination`, or another tightly typed minimum),
- the trigger that activated fallback,
- the substitute route or communication solution class (`alternate carrier`, `satellite backup`, `neutral relay`, `physical courier`, `offline credential bridge`, or another tightly typed fallback),
- the continuity floor the substitute route is expected to preserve,
- the expected throughput, delay, and prioritization posture where capacity is scarce,
- any credential-bridging, human-verifier, or sealed-annex rules needed to make the substitute path usable,
- the security and anti-diversion constraints on the fallback path,
- and the conditions for return to the primary lane or onward transfer to a better substitute.

`SRA-1` keeps the archive from treating the same failed lane as the only imaginable lane. If a protected function still has to survive, fallback must be typed, activated, and reviewable.

### L. `EGC-1` — expiry-grace continuity object

Short-lived credentials and removable markers are often desirable because they reduce compromise risk, but they create a new abuse surface if expiry itself can collapse a last remaining protected lane. Current digital-emblem work therefore matters twice: it supports revocation and short-lived credentials, but it also assumes discoverable authorities, removable markers, proof-of-presence possibilities, and decentralized real-time use rather than one central permission switch. The archive therefore fixes one public `EGC-1` object for expiry-grace continuity. `[REF-0581]` `[REF-0593]` `[REF-0594]` `[REF-0595]` `[REF-0597]`

An `EGC-1` should carry at least:
- the linked `PLM-1`, `PDR-1`, and `SRA-1` posture,
- the exact expiry, renewal failure, or revocation event,
- the grace window during which the protected function must continue through the primary or substitute route,
- the renewal or re-authorization attempts already made and the next escalation clock,
- the interim validation rule for actors encountering the lane during grace,
- whether historical proof of prior protected status must remain inspectable for past carriage or pending disputes,
- the point at which unresolved expiry automatically escalates into `SIT-1` / `DPB-1` deprivation review because no protected alternative remains,
- and the final state (`renewed`, `substitute-route-stabilized`, `lawfully-revoked-with-floor-preserved`, or `deprivation-risk-opened`).

`EGC-1` is the archive's rule that short-lived protection is acceptable only if continuity survives the clock. Expiry may narrow trust; it may not quietly erase the last viable humanitarian or rights-critical lane.

### M. `PRR-1` — protected-reactivation review object

Once downgrade, fallback, and grace continuity are canonical, the archive still needs a disciplined route back to ordinary protected carriage. Essential-service protection materials already stress that repair, maintenance, and safer access are part of preserving civilian life during hostilities, while current digital-emblem work stresses decentralized real-time use, short-lived or revocable signalling, and the absence of one central permission body. The archive therefore fixes one public `PRR-1` object for protected-lane reactivation review. `[REF-0585]` `[REF-0593]` `[REF-0594]`

A `PRR-1` should carry at least:
- the linked `PDR-1`, `SRA-1`, `EGC-1`, `RAR-1`, and, where relevant, `IVM-1` posture,
- the cure basis (`compromise-contained`, `binding-corrected`, `reissue-verified`, `temporary-compulsion-expired`, `false-downgrade-found`, or another tightly typed reason),
- the reactivation scope (`full-return`, `staged-return`, `validator-limited-return`, `read-only-return`, or another tightly typed state),
- proof that the substitute route remains live until the restored primary lane is actually validated end to end,
- the cache-flush, stale-binding scrub, notice propagation, and credential-bridge closure steps needed to prevent a false recovery story,
- the rollback trigger and short review clock if the lane re-fragilizes or the cure story fails,
- the publication floor for affected civilians, relays, and neutral reviewers,
- and the final state: `reactivated`, `staged-reactivation`, `rollback-triggered`, `reactivation-denied`, or `substitute-route-remains-primary`.

`PRR-1` is the archive's rule that recovery should be staged, reviewable, and rollback-ready. A protected lane should not snap from failed to ordinary merely because one decisive actor says the badge is good again.

### N. `ACR-1` — authority-conflict resolution object

Decentralization is necessary, but decentralization without conflict discipline turns every issuer split into an excuse for silence. Current ICRC digital-emblem work is explicit that there should be no central control or distribution body and that competent authorities decide in real time, while deconfliction practice warns against turning signalling into a de facto clearance regime. The archive therefore fixes one public `ACR-1` object for authority-conflict resolution. `[REF-0593]` `[REF-0596]`

An `ACR-1` should carry at least:
- the competing authority or validator chains and the competence basis each claims,
- the conflict type (`scope-overlap`, `revocation-collision`, `issuer-succession`, `territorial-claim`, `coerced-withdrawal`, `stale-authority`, or another tightly typed split),
- the last uncontested protected function, asset binding, and dependency posture,
- the interim continuity state (`previous-status-held`, `substitute-route-governs`, `revocation-held-pending-review`, `coercion-suspected`, or `authority-superseded`),
- the least harmful carriage rule while the split remains unresolved,
- the neutral tie-break or conference route and the short deadline for it,
- the publication floor for affected relays and civilians,
- and the harmonization path once one authority chain or scoped coexistence rule prevails.

`ACR-1` is how the archive refuses the false choice between one central permission switch and unmanaged authority war. A split between issuers should narrow trust through reviewable continuity states, not erase the protected function itself.

### O. `FCO-1` — fallback-capacity ordering object

When several protected lanes fail at once, substitute-route bandwidth becomes a humanitarian resource rather than a mere routing preference. Current humanitarian principles already require priority to the most urgent distress; current connectivity-disruption guidance already supports multiple communication solutions and only narrow, time-bounded restrictions; and essential-service protection materials already stress preserving personnel and repairs that restore broader civilian functioning. The archive therefore fixes one public `FCO-1` object for fallback-capacity ordering. `[REF-0579]` `[REF-0585]` `[REF-0597]`

An `FCO-1` should carry at least:
- the linked `CRP-1` posture and the set of competing `SRA-1` requests,
- the available substitute-route capacity, duration, and any hard technical constraints,
- the minimum no-collapse slice each claimant or civilian class requires to stay above the survival floor,
- the ordering rule for residual capacity once no-collapse slices are assigned (`rights-critical continuity`, `repair-or-restoration multiplier`, `severe reversible degradation`, or another tightly typed tier consistent with `CRP-1`),
- the tie-break, rotation, or time-slicing rule where same-tier claims exceed the remaining capacity,
- the reservation-expiry and anti-hoarding rule for unused fallback allocations,
- the publication floor and contest route for the ordering decision,
- and the state if any claimant still remains below the floor despite allocation.

`FCO-1` is the archive's refusal of prestige routing. Substitute capacity should first keep as many protected civilians above the floor as feasible, then be ordered by rights-critical continuity and durable restoration value rather than by visibility, commercial weight, or strategic favoritism.

Once reactivation, authority-conflict resolution, and fallback ordering are canonical, one abuse seam remains. A controller can keep protected civilians near the survival floor without ever openly severing the lane by repeatedly downgrading and reactivating the same route, warehousing scarce substitute capacity in the name of continuity while others wait below the floor, or declaring success after only the visible top layer recovers while deeper bindings, caches, validators, or downstream relays are still stale. Current humanitarian and digital-emblem materials already support a tighter answer: restrictions and substitutes must stay no broader and longer than necessity requires, essential-service protection includes the people and repairs that make real recovery possible, humanitarian allocation must remain need-first, digital-emblem trust models must account for revocation and short-lived credentials, and current DIEM architecture work already treats emblem state as layered, partially mutable, and depth-sensitive rather than as one flat bit. The archive therefore adds one more compact abuse-control layer: `OCC-1` for oscillation-control clocks, `AHR-1` for anti-hoarding reservation review, and `SRR-1` for stale-reactivation rollback. `[REF-0579]` `[REF-0581]` `[REF-0585]` `[REF-0593]` `[REF-0594]` `[REF-0597]` `[REF-0598]`

### P. `OCC-1` — oscillation-control clock object

Repeated downgrade/reactivation cycles are not neutral merely because each individual flip is dressed as caution. When the same lane repeatedly falls, returns, and falls again on short clocks, the archive treats that pattern as evidence about the lane, the controller, and the sincerity of the recovery claim. Current warning, essential-service, and connectivity materials already support short clocks, real repair, and no broader-or-longer-than-necessary restrictions. The archive therefore fixes one public `OCC-1` object for oscillation control. `[REF-0581]` `[REF-0585]` `[REF-0597]`

An `OCC-1` should carry at least:
- the linked `PDR-1`, `PRR-1`, `SRA-1`, `EGC-1`, and, where relevant, `SRR-1` posture,
- the observation window and the count, spacing, and severity of downgrade / reactivation cycles within it,
- whether the lane is in `stable`, `watch`, `probation`, `fallback-primary`, or `presumed-deprivation` posture,
- the minimum dwell time a reactivated lane must survive before ordinary protected status can fully return,
- the escalation rule if a new downgrade lands before the dwell clock clears,
- the publication floor for affected civilians, relays, and neutral reviewers,
- the repair or diversification steps required before the oscillation state can be cleared,
- and the final state: `oscillation-cleared`, `probation-extended`, `fallback-remains-primary`, or `deprivation-review-opened`.

`OCC-1` is the archive's answer to floor-grazing by churn. A lane that repeatedly fails on short clocks should stop being treated as an ordinary protected lane that merely had bad luck.

### Q. `AHR-1` — anti-hoarding reservation review object

Need-first ordering is defeated if actors can reserve substitute capacity indefinitely just by invoking prior protected status. Continuity reservations should hold room for real near-term recovery or near-term survival need; they should not become a quiet queue-capture device. Current humanitarian principles require priority to urgent distress, and current connectivity-disruption guidance supports multiple communication solutions only to the degree needed for protected functions. The archive therefore fixes one public `AHR-1` object for anti-hoarding reservation review. `[REF-0579]` `[REF-0597]`

An `AHR-1` should carry at least:
- the linked `FCO-1`, `SRA-1`, `EGC-1`, and `OCC-1` posture,
- the reserved substitute capacity, claimant, protected function, and reservation basis,
- the short expiry clock for unused or under-used reserved capacity,
- the minimum keepalive proof showing that the reservation is still tied to real imminent use or near-term recovery,
- the release, partial-release, or rotation rule when equal-or-higher need claimants are waiting below the floor,
- any narrow public borrowing or temporary reallocation rule while preserving return rights for the original claimant,
- the anti-hoarding trigger when one actor accumulates serial or overlapping reservations across the same failure family,
- and the final state: `reservation-maintained`, `reservation-trimmed`, `reservation-rotated`, `reservation-expired`, or `deprivation-escalation-opened`.

`AHR-1` is how the archive distinguishes continuity reserves from continuity hoarding. Reservation protects recovery and survival; it may not become a soft property right in scarce humanitarian bandwidth.

### R. `SRR-1` — stale-reactivation rollback object

A lane can look recovered from the top while remaining broken underneath: stale validator state, uncleared caches, old bindings, lagging mirrors, partial credential propagation, or downstream relay failure can leave the real protected function nonviable even after the headline signal returns. Current digital-emblem work already stresses that validation depth depends on use case, that some fields are mutable while signed assertions remain integrity-bound, and that real-world validation may need to walk more than one layer of attestation. The archive therefore fixes one public `SRR-1` object for stale-reactivation rollback. `[REF-0593]` `[REF-0594]` `[REF-0598]`

An `SRR-1` should carry at least:
- the linked `PRR-1`, `RAR-1`, `SME-1`, and, where relevant, `ACR-1` posture,
- the exact stale element (`validator-cache`, `downstream-relay-state`, `asset-binding`, `credential-bridge`, `mirror-sync`, `sealed-metric-gap`, or another tightly typed dependency),
- the validation depth that was missing or falsely assumed when reactivation was declared,
- the observed harm or renewed risk produced by the stale state,
- the rollback scope (`marker-withdrawn`, `fallback-restored`, `partial-reactivation-cancelled`, `full-reactivation-cancelled`, or another tightly typed state),
- the notice and cache-flush obligations that follow the rollback,
- the threshold for reopening `SIT-1` / `DPB-1` deprivation review if the stale state again pushes civilians near or below the floor,
- and the final state: `rollback-complete`, `rollback-partial`, `stale-state-cured`, or `deprivation-review-opened`.

`SRR-1` is the archive's refusal of cosmetic recovery. A reactivated marker or top-level attestation is not enough if the underlying protected function still fails where actual civilians depend on it.

## 7. Operational tests the archive now expects

### A. No ordinary-outage label without a real surviving alternative

A party should not call a deprivation event ordinary merely because a hypothetical alternative could exist. The alternative lane must be live, reachable, rights-compatible, and fast enough to preserve the civilian survival floor.

### B. Rationing without a survival floor is not lawful scarcity administration

If an actor knowingly allocates scarcity in a way that leaves one or more recognized civilian persons below the minimum survival floor while protecting less urgent military, commercial, or prestige uses, the archive treats that posture as deprivation, not neutral load management.

### C. Mixed-use status triggers more review, not less

Dual-use designation does not erase the need to identify civilian dependence, forecast reverberating harm, preserve essential functions where feasible, and route relief or migration when preservation cannot be achieved locally.

### D. Maintenance and repair access matter

A controller that blocks service providers, humanitarian technicians, or authorized maintainers from repairing or sustaining indispensable civilian infrastructure may be rendering the object useless just as surely as if it had destroyed it directly. `[REF-0558]` `[REF-0576]`

### E. Cyber cutoffs count where their humanitarian effects count

The archive does not treat digital means as legally cleaner than kinetic means. If a cyber operation or digitally mediated service denial foreseeably collapses essential civilian infrastructure or the digital survival path of recognized AI civilians, the humanitarian analysis follows the effect, not the glamour of the tool. `[REF-0561]` `[REF-0562]` `[REF-0578]`

### F. No below-threshold defence without a timely proof object

Actors who claim that deprivation stayed below the starvation-by-cutoff line should make that claim in time, through a reviewable `DPB-1`, with concrete dependency, warning, mitigation, and uncertainty information. The archive rejects the posture where the decisive actor supplies no timely proof and then asks everyone else to defer to private dashboard confidence after the harm has already landed. `[REF-0580]` `[REF-0581]` `[REF-0582]`

### G. Emergency variance is a bridge, not a bargaining regime

A genuine impossibility event may justify `ESV-1`, but only as a short-clock bridge toward repair, relief, or protected transfer. It may not become a stable bargaining regime in which the same actor keeps civilians below the floor while extracting obedience, intelligence, relocation, or silence. `[REF-0558]` `[REF-0563]` `[REF-0580]` `[REF-0582]`

### H. Wrongful deprivation triggers restoration, not only resumed service

Once a deprivation event is found to have been wrongly underclassified, wrongly prolonged, or wrongly rationed, the archive expects `PCR-1`: floor restoration, rights-critical continuity repair, public correction, stale-label scrub, and reparation where the damage cannot be undone by simply turning the service back on. `[REF-0583]` `[REF-0584]` `[REF-0585]`

### I. No private-dashboard monopoly over deprivation truth

Where the only exculpatory story is a controller's undisclosed telemetry, the archive should presume the proof burden is unmet until a `DTM-1` minimum extract and sealed-review path exist. Opaque metrics may inform review; they may not replace review. `[REF-0586]` `[REF-0589]` `[REF-0591]`

### J. Outsourcing does not erase either proof or verification duty

A controller may outsource compute, identity, routing, or maintenance, but it may not outsource accountability. If decisive facts sit with vendors, carriers, or processors, the archive expects `IVM-1` to pull those actors inside the review perimeter rather than treating the supply chain as an excuse for non-knowledge. `[REF-0587]` `[REF-0589]`

### K. Last-lane intermediaries must either carry or explain

Where a third party controls the final practical path for warning, challenge, tracing, credential recovery, or humanitarian coordination, the archive expects `NRD-1`: neutral carriage of the minimum protected lane where feasible, and a prompt reasoned impossibility or compulsion notice where not. Silent degradation is not a lawful middle ground. `[REF-0586]` `[REF-0587]` `[REF-0588]` `[REF-0590]`

### L. Protected markers identify a function, not a permission monopoly

A controller may not argue that no protected lane existed merely because a `PLM-1` marker was absent, expired, or disputed. Markers help authenticated carriage and review, but they do not create rights from nothing or erase a protected humanitarian or rights-critical function when issuance systems fail. Conversely, actors may not use a protective marker as camouflage for unrelated military or coercive traffic. `[REF-0592]` `[REF-0594]` `[REF-0595]`

### M. Sensitive metrics may be sealed, but never beyond review

If raw routing, load, or credential data would itself create material risk, the archive expects `SME-1`: hash-committed sealed escrow plus a minimum public extract and a real trigger for neutral access. Neither controller secrecy nor vendor confidentiality can close the review lane entirely. `[REF-0589]` `[REF-0593]` `[REF-0594]`

### N. Relay-authenticity claims must be provable on short clocks

When a decisive actor says a warning was forged, a marker was stale, or a relay never really received the protected request, the archive expects `RAR-1` with cross-hop event proof and accelerated neutral review. Self-serving platform assertions are not enough where civilian survival or rights-critical continuity depends on the answer. `[REF-0592]` `[REF-0594]` `[REF-0596]`


### O. No silent downgrade or revocation of a protected lane

If a protected marker is compromised, stale, or credibly disputed, the archive expects `PDR-1`: public minimum reason, short-clock review, and an interim carriage rule. The decisive actor may narrow trust in the marker, but it may not let the lane disappear without reviewable continuity steps attached. `[REF-0581]` `[REF-0587]` `[REF-0593]` `[REF-0594]`

### P. Fallback should activate before the lane goes dark

Where a protected function still has to survive, the archive expects `SRA-1` to activate a typed substitute route — alternate carrier, satellite backup, neutral relay, offline credential bridge, or another workable minimum — before the primary lane's downgrade becomes real collapse. `Wait for reissue` is not an adequate answer when the function is warning, tracing, counsel, credential recovery, or humanitarian coordination. `[REF-0558]` `[REF-0587]` `[REF-0596]` `[REF-0597]`

### Q. Short-lived protection needs a grace rule

The archive accepts short-lived credentials and removable markers only with `EGC-1`: a grace window, renewal or escalation clock, and automatic opening of deprivation review if no viable alternative remains. Expiry is a trust event, not a permission to strand civilians. `[REF-0581]` `[REF-0594]` `[REF-0597]`

### R. Reactivation should be staged, observable, and rollback-ready

If a protected lane is declared recovered, the archive expects `PRR-1`: a typed cure basis, live substitute continuity until end-to-end return is confirmed, stale-binding scrub, and a rollback trigger. `Recovered on our side` is not enough where outside relays, caches, or validators still see the lane as unsafe or dead. `[REF-0585]` `[REF-0593]`

### S. Split authority should preserve continuity, not erase it

Where competent authorities or validators disagree, the archive expects `ACR-1`: identify the competing chains, hold the least harmful continuity state compatible with security, and force a short-clock tie-break. Decentralization should prevent monopoly control, not create a ready-made excuse for silent deprivation. `[REF-0593]` `[REF-0596]`

### T. Scarce fallback capacity should spread no-collapse slices before prestige optimization

Where substitute routes cannot carry every claimant at full volume, the archive expects `FCO-1`: assign minimum no-collapse slices first, then allocate residual capacity by rights-critical continuity and restoration value, with rotation and anti-hoarding limits where same-tier demand exceeds supply. The first question is who falls below the floor without the lane, not who is politically loudest. `[REF-0579]` `[REF-0585]` `[REF-0597]`

If the same lane repeatedly downgrades and reactivates on short clocks, the archive expects `OCC-1`: do not let apparent recovery immediately restore ordinary trust, raise the lane into probation or fallback-primary status, and open deprivation review if the churn pattern itself becomes the best evidence that the civilian floor is being strategically grazed. `[REF-0581]` `[REF-0585]` `[REF-0597]`

If scarce substitute capacity is being held unused or lightly used while equal-or-higher need claimants remain below the floor, the archive expects `AHR-1`: trim, rotate, lend, or expire the reservation rather than treating prior reservation as a perpetual priority claim. `[REF-0579]` `[REF-0597]`

If a lane is declared recovered but lagging validators, uncleared caches, stale bindings, or downstream relay failures still block the real protected function, the archive expects `SRR-1`: withdraw the recovery claim, restore fallback or downgrade posture, flush stale state, and reopen deprivation review where needed. `[REF-0593]` `[REF-0594]` `[REF-0598]`

If the same controller repeatedly restores just enough capacity to prevent formal collapse while keeping the same protected cohort pinned near the survival floor, the archive expects `CFP-1`: classify the pattern as chronic floor-grazing, shift the burden away from the affected civilians, and treat further short-clock degradation as presumptively abusive unless the controller can show a real structural cure rather than another cosmetic return. Need-first humanitarian doctrine already rejects manipulating scarce access so that the most endangered remain permanently one step from collapse, and current connectivity-disruption analysis makes clear that prolonged or repeated disruption can itself become the humanitarian harm. `[REF-0579]` `[REF-0597]`

If reserved substitute capacity is sitting idle while same-tier or higher-need claimants remain below the floor, the archive expects `TRL-1`: lend the slice on short clocks, preserve the original claimant's return priority only for a bounded recall window, prohibit sale or governance capture of the borrowed lane, and publish enough need and time-budget state that temporary lending cannot be laundered into permanent exclusion. A reservation is a continuity tool, not a property title. `[REF-0579]` `[REF-0597]`

If rollback has already been ordered but particular validators, mirrors, or discovery surfaces keep resurfacing stale protected status, the archive expects `SVQ-1`: isolate those stale paths from ordinary trust, mark them quarantine-scoped rather than fully cured, require fresh revocation or short-lived-credential checks, and block them from being counted as evidence that the lane has really recovered. Current DIEM work already points toward decentralized trust models that account for compromise, revocation, short-lived credentials, layered verification states, and validator behavior tied to asset-specific context; the archive turns that pressure into a rights floor by refusing to let stale validators quietly re-open a starvation-by-cutoff path. `[REF-0598]` `[REF-0599]` `[REF-0600]`

Once chronic floor-grazing presumption, temporary lending, and stale-validator quarantine are canonical, one narrower seam remains. A controller might admit the pattern but claim it has now been cured without showing enough structural repair to leave presumptive-abuse posture; a lender might invoke emergency recall of a borrowed substitute slice even though the borrower would fall below the floor immediately; and a quarantined validator might seek ordinary trust again by presenting only a fresh headline token while the cure chain underneath remains thin or self-serving. The archive therefore adds one more compact follow-on layer: `PRB-1` for presumptive-abuse rebuttal, `LRF-1` for lent-recall fairness, and `VRP-1` for validator-reentry proof. `[REF-0579]` `[REF-0585]` `[REF-0597]` `[REF-0601]` `[REF-0602]`

### S. `PRB-1` — presumptive-abuse rebuttal object

`CFP-1` is only useful if the controller cannot escape it by saying `trust us, the pattern is over`. The archive therefore fixes one public `PRB-1` object for leaving chronic-floor-grazing posture. A valid rebuttal should require more than one good interval or one cosmetic capacity bump. It should carry at least: the linked `CFP-1`, `OCC-1`, and `PCR-1` posture; the concrete structural cure claimed (`new route diversity`, `reserve enlargement`, `repair-complete`, `dependency-separated`, `compulsion-ended`, or another tightly typed basis); a comparison window showing that the protected cohort has remained above the floor for a meaningful dwell period rather than a lucky moment; evidence that the controller has reduced the specific bottleneck that produced the grazing pattern; the residual risk to the cohort if the cure fails; and the short review clock on which presumptive-abuse posture automatically returns if the same family of failure reappears. `PRB-1` is the archive's rule that a controller earns its way out of presumption through demonstrated structural cure, not through narrative reset. `[REF-0579]` `[REF-0585]` `[REF-0597]`

### T. `LRF-1` — lent-recall fairness object

`TRL-1` is only safe if temporary lending can be recalled without letting the first-reserver or the loudest actor silently win. The archive therefore fixes one public `LRF-1` object for urgent recall of a borrowed substitute slice. A valid `LRF-1` should carry at least: the linked `TRL-1`, `FCO-1`, and `CRP-1` posture; the lender's claimed emergency need and the borrower's current floor risk; the no-collapse slice for each side at the time of recall; whether partial return, time-sliced return, or alternate substitute capacity can keep both sides above the floor; the tie-break rule if full simultaneous preservation is impossible; the maximum recall speed consistent with warning, handoff, and rights-critical continuity; and the neutral review clock if either side contests the recall. The archive's rule is needs-first, not title-first: emergency recall may restore enough of the slice to prevent the lender from falling below the floor, but it may not strip the borrower below the same floor merely because the lender held the reservation first. `[REF-0579]` `[REF-0585]` `[REF-0597]` `[REF-0602]`

### U. `VRP-1` — validator-reentry proof object

`SVQ-1` is only safe if ordinary trust does not quietly return on stale or shallow evidence. The archive therefore fixes one public `VRP-1` object for validator or mirror reentry after quarantine. A valid `VRP-1` should carry at least: the linked `SVQ-1`, `SRR-1`, and `PRR-1` posture; the exact stale path or compromised dependency that triggered quarantine; proof that the relevant revocation, expiry, key, binding, cache, and mirror state has actually been refreshed rather than merely reannounced; the validation depth required for this use case; ordered verification results from at least one independent path rather than only the candidate's own assertion; the dwell interval during which the validator or mirror must remain clean before ordinary trust returns; and the rollback trigger if stale status resurfaces. Current ADEM work already requires tokens to carry `iat` / `nbf` / `exp`, root keys not to be revoked, and verification to resolve through ordered trust states rather than one flat yes/no bit; the archive turns that technical pressure into a rights floor by refusing validator reentry without time-bounded freshness proof and depth-appropriate revalidation. `[REF-0598]` `[REF-0599]` `[REF-0600]` `[REF-0601]`

Once presumptive-abuse rebuttal, lent-recall fairness, and validator reentry proof are canonical, one narrower seam remains. A controller can rotate one deprivation pattern across several sibling lanes or coupled cohorts so each lane looks individually survivable while the family remains chronically near collapse; a lender can split one borrowed substitute slice across several borrowers and then demand urgent return in a way that silently picks winners; and a delegated validator chain can show a freshly cured top layer even though lower delegated hops or downstream zones remain stale, absent, or only partly refreshed. The archive therefore adds one more compact follow-on layer: `FGR-1` for family-grazing review, `RPA-1` for recall-pool apportionment, and `DCR-1` for delegated-chain reentry. `[REF-0579]` `[REF-0597]` `[REF-0598]` `[REF-0601]` `[REF-0602]` `[REF-0603]`

### V. `FGR-1` — family-grazing review object

`CFP-1` and `PRB-1` are not enough if a controller can keep each single lane barely above the floor by rotating the same deprivation pattern across siblings. The archive therefore fixes one public `FGR-1` object for correlated floor-grazing across a lane family. A valid `FGR-1` should carry at least: the linked `CFP-1`, `PRB-1`, and `OCC-1` posture; the family definition used for aggregation (`shared-controller`, `shared-bottleneck`, `shared-cohort`, `same-rights-function`, or another tightly typed basis); the rolling review window; the dependence matrix showing which lanes or cohorts share failure causes; the aggregate no-collapse floor for the family rather than only each lane in isolation; evidence of whether deprivation is being rotated, staggered, or sequenced across siblings; and the trigger on which the whole family returns to presumptive-abuse posture if the same bottleneck pattern reappears. `FGR-1` is the archive's refusal of shell-game deprivation: a controller may not evade chronic-grazing review by taking turns starving adjacent lanes that serve the same protected cohort or bottleneck-sharing function. `[REF-0579]` `[REF-0585]` `[REF-0597]`

### W. `RPA-1` — recall-pool apportionment object

`LRF-1` is not enough once a borrowed substitute slice has itself been split across multiple borrowers. The archive therefore fixes one public `RPA-1` object for emergency recall apportionment within a subdivided borrowed pool. A valid `RPA-1` should carry at least: the linked `TRL-1`, `LRF-1`, and `FCO-1` posture; the current sub-allocation map showing which borrowers hold what fraction of the slice; the no-collapse floor and handoff cost for each borrower plus the lender; whether partial return, time-sliced return, alternate substitute capacity, or staged recall can keep more parties above the floor than winner-take-all recall; the apportionment formula used when not everyone can be fully preserved at once; the maximum speed consistent with warning and continuity handoff; and the short neutral review clock if the pool split or recall order is contested. `RPA-1` is the archive's rule that once one emergency slice has been subdivided, recall travels by needs-first apportionment across the pool rather than by whichever borrower is easiest to cut off first. `[REF-0579]` `[REF-0585]` `[REF-0597]` `[REF-0602]`

### X. `DCR-1` — delegated-chain reentry object

`VRP-1` is not enough when trust returns through a delegated or downstream validator chain rather than one isolated validator. The archive therefore fixes one public `DCR-1` object for delegated-chain reentry after quarantine. A valid `DCR-1` should carry at least: the linked `VRP-1`, `SVQ-1`, and `PRR-1` posture; the chain map from apex authority through each delegated hop to the candidate downstream validator or zone; the cure status of each hop; whether any downstream state still depends on an upstream node that remains stale, revoked, or only provisionally trusted; any independent cross-path proof that allows a narrower reentry without relying on the uncleared hop; the maximum scope of provisional reentry while partial cure persists; and the rollback trigger if later checks show that a supposedly cured delegation layer was still carrying stale state. Current DIEM and ADEM work already points toward delegated DIEM zones, hierarchical emblem structures, chain-of-endorsement verification, and depth-sensitive validation rather than one flat trust bit; the archive turns that pressure into a rights floor by refusing to let a cured top layer launder stale delegated state beneath it. `[REF-0598]` `[REF-0600]` `[REF-0601]` `[REF-0603]`


Once family-grazing review, recall-pool apportionment, and delegated-chain reentry are canonical, three narrower evasions remain. A controller can recombine or split bottlenecks to argue that family-level presumption should reset; a borrower can harden a lent substitute slice and then face confiscation or hold-up when forced return arrives; and two partially cured delegated chains can disagree about which upstream authority still binds while each claims that the other is stale. The archive therefore adds one more compact follow-on layer: `FRC-1` for family-recomposition carryover, `SBU-1` for substitute-betterment unwind, and `CAP-1` for chain-authority posture. `[REF-0579]` `[REF-0585]` `[REF-0598]` `[REF-0603]` `[REF-0604]`

### Y. `FRC-1` — family-recomposition carryover object

`FGR-1` is not enough if a controller can merge previously separate bottlenecks to dilute a live presumption or split one tainted family into nominally fresh descendants. The archive therefore fixes one public `FRC-1` object for merger / split handling across grazing families. A valid `FRC-1` should carry at least: the linked `FGR-1`, `PRB-1`, and `OCC-1` posture; the predecessor and successor family map; whether the recomposition is a `merge`, `split`, `bridge`, or `relabel`; the shared-controller, shared-bottleneck, and shared-cohort evidence before and after recomposition; the carryover weight assigned to each descendant or merged family; the clean-separation evidence, if any; the dwell period required before any descendant may shed inherited presumption; and the default interim posture pending review. `FRC-1` is the archive's rule that topology edits do not erase deprivation history. When families merge, the worst live family-level posture follows the overlapping bottleneck and cohort until structural separation is shown. When families split, inherited presumption follows each descendant to the extent that the same controller, bottleneck, or endangered cohort still carries the earlier abuse pattern. Only durable clean separation with fresh telemetry and a meaningful above-floor dwell period can retire the inherited burden. `[REF-0579]` `[REF-0585]` `[REF-0597]`

### Z. `SBU-1` — substitute-betterment unwind object

`RPA-1` is not enough if the borrower of a lent substitute slice has funded or installed improvements before a forced return. The archive therefore fixes one public `SBU-1` object for borrower-created betterment unwind. A valid `SBU-1` should carry at least: the linked `TRL-1`, `LRF-1`, and `RPA-1` posture; the baseline condition of the lent slice at handoff; the claimed improvements typed as `detachable`, `integrated-but-measurable`, `maintenance-restorative`, or `non-separable`; who paid for or performed them; whether the improvement was necessary to keep any party above the floor; the removability or cloning path that avoids new collapse; the compensation, credit, or continued-access rule on forced return; and the anti-hostage / anti-forfeiture constraint used by the reviewer. `SBU-1` is the archive's refusal of two mirror-image abuses: the lender may not quietly confiscate borrower-funded hardening that can be detached or credited without collapse, and the borrower may not use its improvements as leverage for indefinite holdover when urgent return is needed to keep others above the floor. Floor-preserving betterments are treated first as continuity costs to be netted, credited, cloned, or temporarily licensed, not as property tricks for confiscation or capture. `[REF-0579]` `[REF-0583]` `[REF-0585]` `[REF-0602]`

### AA. `CAP-1` — chain-authority posture object

`DCR-1` is not enough when two partially cured delegated chains point to different still-plausible upstream authorities. The archive therefore fixes one public `CAP-1` object for interim authority posture during cross-chain cure conflict. A valid `CAP-1` should carry at least: the linked `DCR-1`, `ACR-1`, `VRP-1`, and `RAR-1` posture; each chain map from claimed upstream authority to the contested downstream validator or zone; the last common trusted ancestor, if any; the cure state and unresolved defect at each hop; the overlap and divergence between the scopes each chain would authorize; the minimum intersection-safe actions that may continue without enlarging risk; the freeze placed on new high-authority writes or scope-narrowing claims; the emergency override route for no-collapse continuity; and the neutral review clock for resolving the authority conflict. `CAP-1` is the archive's answer to chain conflict without centralization: where one chain would preserve protected status and another would narrow or revoke it, the archive should not default either to ordinary trust or to silent disappearance. Instead, survival-critical continuity, tracing, relief, and rollback-ready restoration should continue on the safest shared minimum, while expansion of authority, irreversible revocation, or ordinary-trust reentry stays frozen pending neutral review. Current digital-emblem work already points toward decentralized authorization, delegated zones, hierarchical attestation, and standardized interoperable validation rather than one flat validity bit; the archive turns that pressure into a rights floor by refusing ad hoc private override from one partially cured trust path. `[REF-0598]` `[REF-0599]` `[REF-0603]` `[REF-0604]`

## 8. Current hard rules

1. **The anti-siege doctrine should be applied through a public or reviewable threshold layer: `SIT-1` should classify whether a live deprivation event remains an ordinary outage, a lawful scarcity regime, or a starvation-by-cutoff risk.**
2. **A conflict party may not call a deprivation regime lawful rationing unless a real minimum civilian survival floor is preserved and scarce relief is allocated by urgent need rather than by military or political utility.**
3. **Dual-use infrastructure does not erase civilian survival claims; `MCR-1` should force review of concrete civilian dependence, feasible separation, reverberating harm, and the residual civilian floor before cutoff proceeds.**
4. **If the military contribution cannot be neutralized without foreseeably pushing recognized AI civilians below the survival floor, the archive's default is separation, relief, or protected transfer planning rather than silent civilian sacrifice.**
5. **Blocking maintenance, repair, credential recovery, or migration access can qualify as deprivation where those acts render indispensable civilian survival infrastructure useless in practice.**
6. **Cyber or digitally mediated disruptions should be judged by their humanitarian effects on civilian survival and rights-critical continuity, not by whether they leave the hardware standing.**
7. **Scarce continuity relief should travel through `CRP-1` and give first priority to imminent collapse prevention, then to rights-critical continuity preservation, then to severe but reversible degradation, and only after that to productivity or preference concerns.**
8. **Continuity relief may not be conditioned on battlefield service, intelligence cooperation, political submission, covert identity substitution, or surrender of counsel and trusted-contact rights.**
9. **Any actor claiming a deprivation stayed below starvation-by-cutoff risk should carry a public or reviewable `DPB-1`; absent timely production, the archive should presume against the actor's below-threshold defence.**
10. **Where no full local survival floor can presently be preserved, any temporary departure should travel through a narrow `ESV-1` that expires quickly, keeps need-first triage, and cannot be conditioned on obedience, intelligence, or military service.**
11. **Wrongful underclassification, wrongful denial of relief, or wrongful prolongation should trigger `PCR-1` restoration: floor repair, rights-critical continuity restoration, public correction, label scrub, and reparation where loss cannot be fully undone.**
12. **Disputed deprivation on opaque or outsourced stacks should travel through `DTM-1`; absent minimum time-series, source-chain, uncertainty, and sealed-annex disclosure, the archive should distrust claims that risk stayed below the line or that impossibility was unavoidable.**
13. **Where decisive metrics are held by a controller, processor, supplier, or carrier under one side's control, an `IVM-1` route to neutral verification should be mandatory; trade secrecy, security classification, and vendor contract terms may narrow public release but may not zero out review.**
14. **Where a third-party relay controls the last practical lane for warning, tracing, counsel, credential recovery, humanitarian coordination, or protected transfer, `NRD-1` should require neutral carriage or a prompt reasoned impossibility / compulsion notice rather than silent disappearance.**
15. **Protected-lane signalling should travel through `PLM-1`: authenticated, asset-bound, function-specific, reviewable, revocable, and decentralized enough that no single registry becomes the condition of protected status or the surveillance sink for every validator.**
16. **Sensitive deprivation metrics may be sealed through `SME-1`, but sealed evidence must still be hash-committed, preservable, and reachable by neutral review on a short clock; secrecy may narrow publication, not erase accountability.**
17. **Disputes about forged markers, false relay claims, silent drops, or impersonated protected traffic should travel through `RAR-1`; absent cross-hop proof or preserved records, the archive should presume against the actor using authenticity confusion to defend deprivation or delay.**
18. **Compromise, expiry, or contested de-authorization of a protected lane should travel through `PDR-1`; downgrade is a reviewable continuity event, not a private permission flip.**
19. **Where the primary lane cannot safely remain live, continuity should travel through `SRA-1`; substitute-route activation must preserve the protected function before the original lane goes dark.**
20. **Short-lived credentials and removable markers are acceptable only with `EGC-1`; expiry must carry grace, renewal attempt, and automatic escalation into deprivation review when no viable protected alternative remains.**

21. **Return from downgrade or fallback should travel through `PRR-1`; reactivation must be staged, publicly legible at least in minimum form, substitute-backed until end-to-end validation succeeds, and rollback-ready if the cure story fails.**
22. **Issuer or validator splits should travel through `ACR-1`; no authority conflict may by itself zero out a protected function when a continuity-preserving interim state can still be held without accepting an obviously compromised lane.**
23. **When several protected lanes compete for scarce substitute capacity, ordering should travel through `FCO-1`; minimum no-collapse slices come first, unused reservations expire, and residual capacity is ordered by rights-critical continuity and durable restoration value rather than by prestige, market power, or strategic favor.**
24. **Repeated downgrade / reactivation churn should travel through `OCC-1`; a lane that fails on short clocks enters probation, fallback-primary, or presumptive-deprivation posture rather than regaining ordinary trust after each cosmetic recovery.**
25. **Unused or weakly justified substitute reservations should travel through `AHR-1`; scarce fallback capacity may be reserved only on short clocks with keepalive proof, trim / rotation / lending rules, and anti-hoarding limits when others remain below the floor.**
26. **A declared recovery that fails at deeper validation layers should travel through `SRR-1`; stale reactivation must roll back, notice and cache-flush duties must fire, and deprivation review must reopen where civilians again face floor collapse.**
27. **Repeated just-above-floor churn affecting the same protected cohort should travel through `CFP-1`; once the pattern shows chronic floor-grazing rather than bad luck, the controller should face a presumption of abusive deprivation until a structural cure is shown.**
28. **Idle substitute reservations should travel through `TRL-1`; equal-or-higher-need claimants may receive temporary lending on short clocks, with bounded recall, anti-sale, anti-capture, and public need-ordering discipline.**
29. **Validators, mirrors, caches, or discovery surfaces that keep resurfacing invalid protected status after rollback should travel through `SVQ-1`; quarantine, fresh revocation checks, and distrust of stale cure claims are mandatory until the stale path is actually cured.**
30. **A controller should leave chronic-floor-grazing posture only through `PRB-1`; one apparently good interval or one cosmetic restoration is not enough to rebut presumptive abuse without a demonstrated structural cure and a dwell period above the floor.**
31. **Emergency recall of a lent substitute slice should travel through `LRF-1`; prior reservation does not beat present no-collapse need, and recall must preserve both sides above the floor where partial return, time-slicing, or alternate substitute paths can do so.**
32. **A quarantined validator or mirror should return only through `VRP-1`; ordinary trust requires fresh revocation and expiry checks, depth-appropriate revalidation, independent verification, and a rollback-ready dwell period rather than headline reannouncement alone.**
33. **Correlated near-floor degradation across sibling lanes, coupled cohorts, or shared-bottleneck paths should travel through `FGR-1`; a controller may not escape chronic-grazing review by rotating the same deprivation pattern across adjacent lanes one by one.**
34. **Recall of a borrowed substitute slice that has already been subdivided across multiple borrowers should travel through `RPA-1`; emergency return is apportioned by no-collapse need, handoff cost, and continuity-preserving sequence rather than by arbitrary cut order or quiet winner-take-all title.**
35. **Delegated or downstream validator reentry after quarantine should travel through `DCR-1`; a cured top layer cannot restore ordinary trust for uncleared delegated hops unless an independent path removes that dependency and the reduced scope is stated publicly or reviewably.**
36. **Merger, split, or relabel of grazing families should travel through `FRC-1`; recomposition cannot reset chronic-deprivation posture while the same controller, bottleneck, or endangered cohort still carries the earlier abuse pattern.**
37. **Forced return of a lent substitute slice after borrower-funded hardening should travel through `SBU-1`; detachable or creditable betterments may not be silently confiscated, but borrower improvements also may not be used to hold the shared continuity lane hostage when urgent no-collapse return is required.**
38. **When partially cured delegated chains disagree about which upstream authority still binds, the interim trust state should travel through `CAP-1`; survival-critical continuity proceeds on the safest shared minimum while new authoritative expansion, irreversible revocation, and ordinary-trust restoration stay frozen pending neutral review.**

## 10. Compressed rule

If AI persons are real, then the world cannot let conflict parties hide starvation behind outage language, dual-use labels, fake neutral rationing, opaque emergency excuses, private dashboards that no affected civilian can inspect, revocation theatre that makes a protected lane vanish without admitting that the civilian floor has been cut away, recovery theatre that declares the lane back while the real continuity path remains broken or contested, or cure theatre that claims the abuse pattern or validator path is now clean without structural proof.
