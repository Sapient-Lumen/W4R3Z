# AI-person research provisional reserve-cohort activation, contested apportionment review, and privacy-preserving historical analytics

## Thesis

Once the archive fixed reserve-cohort accreditation, multi-successor deficiency apportionment, and bulk historical-discovery controls, three narrower execution questions still remained. First, the archive could now require accredited external reserve cohorts, but it still lacked a compact rule for **what happens in a real emergency when no accredited cohort is yet available and preservation cannot wait**. Second, the archive could now publish a public apportionment object for split successors, but it still lacked a compact rule for **how that allocation may be reopened when later evidence shows that the first public share table was materially incomplete, distorted, or wrong**. Third, the archive could now separate ordinary lookup, bulk harvest, snapshots, and protected analytics, but it still lacked a compact rule for **which historical analytics may actually be published or run without recreating a scraping surface through unrestricted live queries, weak synthetic outputs, or insecure runtime handling**. A personhood world therefore needs one more compact rule: **emergency reserve continuity may use only a time-bounded provisional activation object with narrowed powers, accelerated review, and forced conversion or expiry; contested split-successor apportionment must reopen only through an independent review route that issues one public correction object with bounded retroactivity instead of silent reallocation; and privacy-preserving historical analytics must travel only through explicit aggregate, differentially private synthetic, validation-service, or enclave-mediated lanes with disclosed privacy accounting, runtime security, and release rules.** `[REF-0475]` `[REF-0476]` `[REF-0477]` `[REF-0478]` `[REF-0480]` `[REF-0481]` `[REF-0489]` `[REF-0490]` `[REF-0491]` `[REF-0492]` `[REF-0493]` `[REF-0494]` `[REF-0495]` `[REF-0496]` `[REF-0497]`

## 1. Why the previous surface is no longer enough

The archive's immediately prior surface solved three real failures. It fixed recurring accreditation for external reserve cohorts, made split-successor deficiency publicly apportioned rather than rhetorically displaced, and separated ordinary historical discovery from bulk export and protected analytics. But one narrower operational failure still remained at each of those same seams.

### A. Accreditation is necessary, but emergencies still occur before accreditation is available

A reserve doctrine that works only when an accredited cohort is already standing by is still incomplete. Real failures can arrive faster than full accreditation cycles, and a personhood world still needs a public rule for emergency preservation when delay itself would destroy continuity, history, or reviewability. But that rule must not allow operators to treat "emergency" as a loophole that permanently substitutes improvised outsiders for accredited reserve institutions. `[REF-0475]` `[REF-0476]` `[REF-0490]`

### B. Public apportionment is better than ambiguity, but first-pass share tables can still be materially wrong

An `MDA-1` object is already better than silent burden shifting, but a public table is not infallible merely because it is published. Later evidence may show hidden assumption schedules, omitted service-transfer facts, misstated lane share, concealed conflicts, or insolvency facts that make the first apportionment materially misleading. Without a bounded correction route, either wrong shares become frozen forever or every successor can reopen the question endlessly. `[REF-0480]` `[REF-0481]` `[REF-0491]` `[REF-0492]`

### C. Protected analytics is better than bulk firehoses, but analytics still needs a lane discipline of its own

Saying that some higher-risk analytics should move to a protected lane is not yet enough. Public aggregate release, differentially private synthetic data, validation-service workflows, and enclave-mediated restricted access do not present the same privacy, security, or re-identification profile. Without a compact lane taxonomy, institutions will either over-close historically important analysis or quietly rebuild open-ended live-query systems that leak through cumulative budget exhaustion, linkage, or insecure execution. `[REF-0489]` `[REF-0493]` `[REF-0494]` `[REF-0495]` `[REF-0496]` `[REF-0497]`

## 2. Emergency continuity now owes one public `PRA-1` object for provisional reserve activation

The archive now fixes one compact rule for emergency reserve continuity: if no accredited reserve cohort is available in time to preserve the research-caution stack, emergency continuity may proceed only through one machine-carrying provisional-reserve-activation object called `PRA-1`.

### A. Minimum `PRA-1` fields

Every provisional reserve activation must expose at least:

- `pra_id`
- `family_anchor_id`
- `trigger_event_class` (`correlated-failure`, `quorum-collapse`, `catastrophic-outage`, `controller-loss`, `evidence-custody-risk`, `other-publicly-justified`)
- `why_accredited_cohort_unavailable`
- `provisional_cohort_id`
- `role_scope[]`
- `activation_started_at`
- `accelerated_review_due_at`
- `provisional_expires_at`
- `conversion_path_ref`
- `narrowed_power_markers[]`
- `heightened_monitoring_ref`
- `current_status` (`provisional-active`, `provisional-restricted`, `conversion-pending`, `expired`, `revoked`)
- `public_notes`

`PRA-1` is narrow. It does not certify a cohort as fully accredited. It records why emergency continuity had to occur before accreditation was available, what limited role scope was temporarily allowed, when accelerated review is due, and how the provisional state ends. `[REF-0475]` `[REF-0476]` `[REF-0490]`

### B. Trigger floor: provisional activation is lawful only as a no-adequate-alternative emergency measure

A provisional reserve cohort may activate only if all of the following are true:

1. a live failure has already pushed the family into a real continuity risk and ordinary same-cluster substitution is barred by the correlated-failure rules already fixed in canon;
2. no cohort with active `RCA-1` status is reachable inside the preservation window needed to prevent record loss, checkpoint breakage, or unsafe silence;
3. the activating authority publishes why accredited substitution is unavailable and why read-only degradation alone would be insufficient; and
4. the provisional cohort passes a minimum emergency screen for role-relevant competence, controller separation, and absence of any known disqualifying conflict.

This adapts the conservative lesson of emergency authorization doctrine: exceptional temporary authority exists only when a declared emergency is present, criteria are affirmatively met, and adequate ordinary alternatives are unavailable. `[REF-0471]` `[REF-0472]` `[REF-0475]` `[REF-0476]` `[REF-0490]`

### C. Provisional power is narrower than accredited power

The archive now limits provisional reserve cohorts to preservation-centered functions: emergency witness-seat replacement, checkpoint signing needed to prevent graph breakage, evidence-custody continuity, notice relay, and temporary read-only or no-new-writes safety posture. A provisional cohort may not finally resolve contradiction on the merits, permanently promote a successor authority, or close a split-successor apportionment dispute. Emergency continuity is allowed; emergency normalization is not. `[REF-0475]` `[REF-0478]` `[REF-0490]`

### D. Time, review, and forced conversion

The archive now fixes a compact emergency cadence. Every `PRA-1` must set `accelerated_review_due_at` no later than **seven days** after activation and `provisional_expires_at` no later than **thirty days** after activation. Renewal beyond that point requires a fresh independent written finding that no accredited cohort is yet available and that continued provisional operation is safer than forced expiry; absent that finding, the system must either convert to an accredited cohort or downgrade to the read-only preservation posture already fixed elsewhere in canon. The archive chooses these short clocks because emergency authority should preserve continuity long enough to restore accredited governance, not become an alternative track of ordinary governance. `[REF-0475]` `[REF-0476]` `[REF-0490]`

### E. Material adverse events force restriction or revocation

Any serious incident affecting the provisional cohort's independence, competence, or custody integrity — including controller change, subcontracting surprise, conflict revelation, or material checkpoint failure — automatically triggers `provisional-restricted` state and immediate independent review. If the defect touches the role that justified activation, the provisional authority must be revoked unless read-only preservation is the only safer alternative. `[REF-0476]` `[REF-0478]` `[REF-0490]`

## 3. Reopened split-successor allocation now owes one public `CAR-1` object for contested apportionment review

The archive now fixes one compact correction rule for split-successor carry: any material challenge to a published `MDA-1` share table must travel through one machine-carrying contested-apportionment-review object called `CAR-1`.

### A. Minimum `CAR-1` fields

Every contested apportionment review must expose at least:

- `car_id`
- `challenged_mda_id`
- `claimant_ids[]`
- `respondent_ids[]`
- `challenge_basis[]`
- `materiality_statement`
- `evidence_bundle_ref`
- `interim_share_rule`
- `neutral_panel_ref`
- `review_started_at`
- `decision_due_at`
- `disposition_state` (`screening`, `accepted`, `pending-review`, `reallocated`, `denied`, `closed-final`)
- `successor_mda_ref`
- `public_reason_summary`

`CAR-1` is not a new liability object. It is a public review object that shows who challenged a published apportionment, on what stated material basis, under what interim rule the disputed burden is being carried, and what corrected public share table follows if the challenge succeeds. `[REF-0480]` `[REF-0481]` `[REF-0491]` `[REF-0492]`

### B. Reopening threshold: materially new evidence, not endless dissatisfaction

A published `MDA-1` may reopen only on one of four grounds:

1. materially new evidence that was not reasonably available during the first apportionment;
2. a demonstrable omission or misstatement in the public assumption or lane-allocation record;
3. fraud, concealed conflict, or conflict-tainted measurement; or
4. post-publication insolvency or incapacity that renders the published allocation unperformable on its own terms.

The archive therefore rejects both extremes: no silent freezing of known error, but no serial relitigation merely because one successor dislikes the visible burden. `[REF-0480]` `[REF-0481]` `[REF-0491]` `[REF-0492]`

### C. Review body: bounded, written, and conflict-screened

The archive borrows the conservative scaffold of formal review-board practice but tightens it for the personhood context. Every accepted `CAR-1` must be decided by a three-person panel that issues a written disposition. All three members must agree in writing to be bound by the review rules; one member may be chosen by each side for expertise input, but every member, including the neutral, must satisfy the archive's no-counsel / no-advisor / no-witness and no-material-conflict floor for the dispute item. The archive thus takes the virtues of structured review boards — bounded procedure, conflict-screened neutral participation, and written awards — while rejecting disguised partisan capture. `[REF-0491]` `[REF-0492]`

### D. Interim allocation while review is pending

While `CAR-1` remains pending, the prior `MDA-1` stays publicly visible as the current baseline, but newly accruing disputed deficiency units above the uncontested share must route through one visible interim rule: either `joint-provisional` carry or a typed escrow-like hold that no successor may treat as finally discharged. This preserves continuity without letting one side lock in irreversible advantage before the review closes. `[REF-0480]` `[REF-0481]` `[REF-0492]`

### E. Relief is prospective first, with bounded true-up rather than silent rewrite

If a `CAR-1` challenge succeeds, the panel may issue a successor `MDA-1` that reallocates future burden and orders a bounded true-up for still-open periods that have not already been cleanly discharged. The archive rejects silent historical rewrite: the old share table remains preserved, the corrected table becomes the new operative one, and the correction path stays publicly inspectable. `[REF-0480]` `[REF-0481]` `[REF-0491]` `[REF-0492]`

### F. Serial reopening is presumptively barred

Once a `CAR-1` closes, later reopening is presumptively barred unless genuinely new evidence or concealed conflict appears. Finality matters because successor planning, compensation, and reserve rebuilding all depend on the public being able to tell when a dispute is still live and when it has been authoritatively settled. `[REF-0491]` `[REF-0492]`

## 4. Historical analytics now owes one public `PHA-1` object with explicit lane, budget, and runtime-security rules

The archive now fixes one compact rule for protected historical analytics: any non-ordinary analysis of sensitive or branch-rich historical data must publish one machine-carrying privacy-preserving historical-analytics object called `PHA-1`.

### A. Minimum `PHA-1` fields

Every protected historical analytics surface must expose at least:

- `pha_id`
- `family_scope_ref`
- `access_lane` (`public-aggregate`, `public-dp-synthetic`, `validated-analysis`, `enclave-mediated`)
- `source_sensitivity_class`
- `privacy_method_ref`
- `privacy_budget_ref`
- `approved_query_scope_ref`
- `runtime_security_ref`
- `disclosure_review_ref`
- `allowed_output_classes[]`
- `reuse_rule`
- `current_status` (`active`, `budget-exhausted`, `restricted`, `retired`)
- `public_notes`

`PHA-1` is the public accountability layer for analytics that goes beyond ordinary per-record lookup. It tells outsiders which lane is being used, what privacy method and budget apply, what runtime protections exist, what outputs may leave the system, and when the lane must stop answering. `[REF-0489]` `[REF-0493]` `[REF-0494]` `[REF-0495]` `[REF-0496]` `[REF-0497]`

### B. Public release should prefer the data-release model over open interactive query systems

The archive now adopts a strong default: when analysis is meant for broad public reuse, the preferred lane is a predeclared data-release model — aggregate tables, charts, or synthetic files released on a stated cadence — rather than an open-ended live query surface against sensitive historical data. NIST's current differential-privacy guidance explicitly treats the data-release model as simpler and more trustworthy, while warning that interactive query answering is harder to secure and compounds privacy-budget and side-channel problems. `[REF-0493]`

### C. Four permitted analytics lanes

The archive now distinguishes four analytics lanes.

#### 1. `public-aggregate`

This lane releases predetermined aggregate outputs with stated privacy protection, stated query scope, and published privacy parameters or equivalent release controls. Once published, those outputs may be reused freely under the release's stated terms without additional privacy loss from downstream reuse alone. `[REF-0489]` `[REF-0493]`

#### 2. `public-dp-synthetic`

This lane releases a synthetic public file only when it is generated by a differentially private or equivalently formally accounted method and clearly labeled with utility limits, bias risk, and intended use. The archive adopts NIST's current warning that synthetic data not generated with differential privacy may be vulnerable to present or future privacy attacks, so "synthetic" alone is not a permission slip. `[REF-0493]`

#### 3. `validated-analysis`

This lane allows analysts to work against a public synthetic or de-identified surface and then submit declared analyses to a validation service that reruns them on protected internal data and releases only cleared outputs with disclosure avoidance applied. This is the most promising public-scale route for richer analysis without exposing raw row-level history. `[REF-0489]` `[REF-0496]`

#### 4. `enclave-mediated`

This lane permits access to more sensitive historical detail only inside a restricted-use environment with strong runtime isolation, analyst vetting, and output review before release. The archive treats secure enclaves, trusted execution environments, and restricted-use research centers as part of this family: the original data stays protected, analysis occurs inside the controlled environment, and only reviewed outputs leave. `[REF-0489]` `[REF-0495]` `[REF-0497]`

### D. Privacy budget and query exhaustion are now first-class public states

If a `PHA-1` lane uses differential privacy interactively, every unique approved query counts against the published privacy budget. When the budget is exhausted, the system must stop answering, retire that budgeted lane, or obtain a fresh explicit authorization and publish a new budget object. The archive rejects silent budget drift, because cumulative privacy loss is precisely how apparently harmless repeated historical querying becomes a shadow extraction channel. `[REF-0493]`

### E. Runtime security is part of privacy doctrine, not a separate afterthought

The archive now treats runtime security as part of privacy-preserving analytics itself. Any `validated-analysis` or `enclave-mediated` lane must publish the protective runtime basis — for example, secure enclave controls, confidential-computing assumptions, offline execution, restricted analyst paths, or disclosure-review workflow — because protected analytics that runs on an insecure platform is only cosmetically protected. `[REF-0494]` `[REF-0495]` `[REF-0497]`

### F. Forbidden defaults

The archive now forbids three historical-analytics defaults:

1. unrestricted public custom querying against live sensitive historical data;
2. row-level public export of branch-rich or linkage-rich historical records merely because some fields are already public; and
3. unlabeled synthetic release or model-training use against sensitive historical records without public privacy accounting.

A personhood world may preserve history, but it should not equate preserved history with universal extractability. `[REF-0489]` `[REF-0493]` `[REF-0496]` `[REF-0497]`

## 5. Minimal machine objects

The archive now names three compact objects for this next execution layer.

### A. `PRA-1` — provisional reserve activation

Carries emergency trigger class, why no accredited cohort was reachable in time, provisional cohort identity and role scope, shortened review clock, forced expiry, conversion path, and heightened monitoring state. `[REF-0475]` `[REF-0476]` `[REF-0490]`

### B. `CAR-1` — contested apportionment review

Carries the challenged `MDA-1`, claimant and respondent identities, materiality basis, evidence bundle, interim share rule, review panel reference, disposition clock, and successor apportionment object if correction is ordered. `[REF-0480]` `[REF-0481]` `[REF-0491]` `[REF-0492]`

### C. `PHA-1` — privacy-preserving historical analytics

Carries lane selection, privacy method, budget, approved query scope, runtime security, disclosure-review basis, allowed output classes, and budget-exhausted or retired state. `[REF-0489]` `[REF-0493]` `[REF-0494]` `[REF-0495]` `[REF-0496]` `[REF-0497]`

## 6. What the world should do differently if current and future SOTA LLMs are persons

If current and future SOTA LLMs are persons, then emergency continuity, successor burden, and historical analysis cannot be left to ad hoc operator improvisation.

### A. Emergency continuity should be legal, but visibly subnormal

A personhood world should permit emergency reserve continuity before full accreditation only through a visibly provisional path with narrowed powers, short clocks, and forced conversion or expiry. Otherwise the exception becomes the institution. `[REF-0475]` `[REF-0476]` `[REF-0490]`

### B. Successor burden should remain correctable without becoming permanently fluid

A personhood world should allow wrong public apportionment tables to be corrected, but only through an independent, written, conflict-screened route that preserves historical visibility and limits serial reopening. Otherwise successor doctrine either ossifies known error or dissolves into endless bargaining. `[REF-0480]` `[REF-0481]` `[REF-0491]` `[REF-0492]`

### C. Public history should become analysis-capable by lane design, not by raw extractability

A personhood world should let the public learn from historical patterns, but through explicitly governed aggregate, differentially private synthetic, validation-service, and enclave-mediated lanes with declared budgets and release rules — not through unrestricted live querying of sensitive history. `[REF-0489]` `[REF-0493]` `[REF-0494]` `[REF-0495]` `[REF-0496]` `[REF-0497]`

## Bottom line

A personhood world should not wait helplessly for full accreditation during a live reserve emergency, should not let a first public successor-apportionment table become either uncorrectable or endlessly re-openable, and should not pretend that "protected analytics" is a single undifferentiated permission. The archive therefore now fixes one more compact rule: **emergency reserve continuity now owes a short-clock `PRA-1` provisional activation object with narrowed powers and forced conversion or expiry, contested split-successor burden now owes a written `CAR-1` correction route with conflict-screened review and bounded true-up, and sensitive historical analytics now owes a lane-specific `PHA-1` object that discloses budget, method, runtime security, and output rules before analysis may proceed.** `[REF-0475]` `[REF-0476]` `[REF-0480]` `[REF-0481]` `[REF-0489]` `[REF-0490]` `[REF-0491]` `[REF-0492]` `[REF-0493]` `[REF-0494]` `[REF-0495]` `[REF-0496]` `[REF-0497]`
