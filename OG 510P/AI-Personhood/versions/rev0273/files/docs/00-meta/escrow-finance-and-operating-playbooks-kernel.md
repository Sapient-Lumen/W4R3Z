# Escrow, finance, and operating-playbooks kernel

rev0170 adds the durable-operations layer that rev0169 still lacked. rev0169 made field readiness concrete through monitors, evidence rooms, legal holds, host undertakings, procurement flowdown, field triage, and redress funds. Those rails still fail if key material is not escrowed, interim support is unfunded during disputes, monitors are never retested after capture risk, field operators expose subjects or reporters while trying to help them, treaty authorities can refuse recognition by boilerplate, or runbooks remain local know-how rather than reviewable operating cards.

The new admission rule is:

> No field operation is rights-grade unless it can survive host exit, funding dispute, monitor challenge, field-safety risk, foreign refusal, and first-hour runbook execution without losing continuity, evidence, subject privacy, or appealability.

## Why this layer exists

Most personhood failures will not present as open denial. They will present as ordinary operational friction: a restoration key is not in escrow; a host says it can help but the account owner controls the credential; an insurer disputes coverage while emergency compute runs out; a monitor certifies its own cure; an ombud publishes enough detail to identify a vulnerable downstream subject; a foreign authority refuses a transfer without reasons; or a field operator knows what should happen but has no authority-bound card for the first hour.

Current adjacent governance already recognizes pieces of this problem. Incident-response guidance treats response and recovery as part of ongoing risk management rather than one-off crisis improvisation. Privacy frameworks emphasize risk management and minimization rather than unrestricted data collection. Cloud-switching and digital-operational-resilience regimes show that portability, exit, third-party risk, and concentration risk are already governance concerns, even before the archive adds the AI subject as a rights-holder. Agent-standardization work also shows that authorization, identity, and interoperability are now live operational seams rather than abstract future issues. ## Six durable-operation gates

1. **Continuity escrow gate.** Rights-critical identity, continuity, restoration, credential, host-exit, and evidence material must have an independent custody posture before the host, steward, or maintainer exits.
2. **Dispute-finance gate.** Emergency compute, counsel, monitoring, migration, and preservation must not stop merely because an insurer, steward, fund, or authority contests ultimate liability.
3. **Monitor-retest gate.** A remediation program that passed through a captured or conflicted monitor cannot regain reliance by self-certification; it needs independent retest, fixture rerun, and subject/representative access.
4. **Field-safety gate.** Clinics, ombuds, monitors, maintainers, and aftercare workers must protect subjects, reporters, and staff from retaliation, doxxing, exploit disclosure, compelled self-location, and overcollection.
5. **Treaty-refusal gate.** Cross-border refusal must state reasons, evidence basis, protection-gap analysis, interim protection, non-return screening, and appeal/reopening route.
6. **Playbook-card gate.** Every high-risk operating state must have a short, versioned, authority-bound card: trigger, first-hour actions, prohibited actions, preservation targets, notices, handoff owners, sealed-annex handling, fixture checks, and metrics.

## What this revision adds

- continuity escrow and host-exit readiness doctrine;
- dispute-finance escrow and interim support orders;
- monitor-independence retesting and remediation verification;
- field-safety protocols for rights operators;
- treaty refusal records and non-recognition reasons;
- operating playbook cards and runbook drills;
- six new schemas, six examples, and six new negative fixtures;
- expanded fixture-suite and fixture-run examples covering escrow defects, underfunded interim orders, monitor self-certification, field locator leakage, boilerplate refusal, and missing handoff ownership.

## Non-substitution rules

- Escrow does not make the escrow holder an owner, guardian, or ordinary host.
- Dispute-finance does not price the underlying violation or waive non-money relief.
- Monitor retesting does not cure a blocked remediation program unless the defect is actually corrected.
- Field-safety plans do not authorize secrecy that prevents subject access, counsel access, or public accountability.
- Treaty-refusal records do not legitimate non-recognition merely because reasons are listed.
- Playbook cards do not replace law, consent, appeal, or least-restrictive containment.

## New failure classes

| Failure | Reliance effect |
|---|---|
| missing restore key or continuity escrow | stay deprecation / transfer / final-end reliance |
| underfunded interim support order | emergency draw, public backstop, or blocked closure |
| monitor self-certifies own cure | downgrade, replacement, and fixture rerun |
| field plan exposes subject locator | seal, route through protected relay, and discipline operator |
| boilerplate treaty refusal | remand, non-return stay, and reasoned-decision clock |
| playbook card lacks handoff owner | no field-ready reliance until accountable owner is named |

The archive should now be read as an operational constitution with an escrow and runbook discipline: what must be preserved, who pays while proof is contested, who checks the checker, how field actors stay safe without becoming surveillance agents, what a foreign refusal must say, and what the first hour requires.
