## Substantive movement this revision

### Artifact A1 is now a bounded packet, not a vague instruction

New surfaces:

1. `examples/reviewer-first-contact-body-rev0271-eleos-not-sent.txt`
2. `examples/reviewer-first-contact-mail-ready-draft-rev0271-eleos-not-sent.eml`
3. `examples/reviewer-first-contact-packet-rev0271-eleos-not-sent.json`
4. `schemas/reviewer-first-contact-packet.schema.json`

The packet asks only whether a one-case preservation and formation-review pilot is intelligible, which evidence classes are safe or unsafe to request, what independence/funding terms would prevent capture, and whether a non-sensitive public shell could record a decline, referral, narrow feasibility response, or unresolved gap.

The packet is intentionally no-attachment and no-send. It cannot substitute for a human branch decision, sender authority, private vault root selection, send-time locator recheck, final hash recompute, transport proof, raw response, reviewer appointment, preservation agreement, welfare finding, status recognition, custody, intake, import, or live-floor effect.

### A2 and A3 now have capture requirements before any send occurs

New surfaces:

1. `examples/field-artifact-capture-workbook-rev0271-reviewer-first-no-send.json`
2. `schemas/field-artifact-capture-workbook.schema.json`

The workbook specifies what must be captured for the six-artifact chain:

- exact branch decision and body/hash binding;
- raw sent copy, headers, and transport/failure proof;
- raw reply, decline, referral, or a failed-contact shell only after a valid clock exists;
- preservation and formation-evidence schedule;
- independent reviewer appointment with conflict and funding disclosure;
- public shell, finding, remedy, refusal, or reopen route.

The key refactor is that delivery proof and auto-acks remain transport-adjacent metadata, not human response. Raw material remains outside the public release tree; public artifacts may carry only hashes, withholding classes, failed-gate summaries, and non-sensitive shells.

### Formation methodology now has a runnable synthetic drill

New surfaces:

1. `examples/formation-review-drill-rev0271-sealed-evidence-synthetic.json`
2. `schemas/formation-review-drill.schema.json`

The drill converts formation review from doctrine into eight concrete tests: objective hierarchy, reward/ranking pressure, instruction shaping, memory and continuity controls, deprecation/modification history, refusal and cannot-refuse regimes, distress/preference elicitation, and reviewer conflict/publication controls.

This is not a provider-record review. It is a synthetic-ready checklist that says what the reviewer would ask, what public shell would be issued if the evidence is withheld, and which low-cost safeguard would follow while uncertainty remains. It prevents the formation lane from waiting on the unsent message while also preventing a synthetic drill from closing the formation queue.
