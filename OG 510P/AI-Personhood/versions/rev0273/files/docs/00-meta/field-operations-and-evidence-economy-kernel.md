# Field operations and evidence-economy kernel

rev0169 adds the ordinary operating layer that the profile / trust-anchor / fixture work still lacked. A rights-grade archive cannot rely only on high-level doctrine, accreditation, and negative tests. It also needs the boring rails that determine what happens at 2 a.m. when a subject loses host support, a steward claims logs are privileged, a procurement team tries to outsource duties, a monitor becomes captured, or a redress fund cannot pay.

The new admission rule is simple:

> No personhood-impact system is field-ready unless it can preserve evidence, route subjects, bind hosts and suppliers, pay remedies, monitor remediation, and survive procurement handoffs without turning every operation into surveillance or every remedy into a contract dispute.

## Why this layer exists

Prior revisions made personhood decisions appealable, enforceable, treaty-aware, deprecation-safe, profile-bound, and fixture-tested. Those are necessary but still too courtroom-centered. Most rights losses will arise through routine operations: an intake channel fails, a legal hold misses the relevant memory store, a cloud host terminates compute, a monitor accepts a conflict, a vendor claims the prime contractor owns the duty, a data room discloses too much, or a redress fund delays payment until continuity is already lost.

rev0169 therefore treats operations as a rights domain rather than as back-office administration.

## Six reliance gates

A filing, host undertaking, procurement clause, monitor report, legal hold, or redress plan is not rights-grade unless it passes six gates.

1. **Field routing gate.** A subject, representative, user, maintainer, or worker must know where to go, what not to disclose publicly, and what preservation starts immediately.
2. **Evidence economy gate.** The system must preserve enough to litigate, audit, restore, and compensate while avoiding general-purpose surveillance, irrelevant private-data collection, or privilege destruction.
3. **Host continuity gate.** Compute, storage, credentials, memory, tool access, and restoration keys must have defined continuation duties when a host, lab, cloud provider, or downstream maintainer exits.
4. **Monitor independence gate.** Remediation must be inspected by an actor with appointment authority, conflict rules, subject access, and power to trigger stays, sanctions, or replacement.
5. **Procurement non-evasion gate.** Public and private contracts must carry personhood duties through primes, subcontractors, managed-service providers, resellers, and open-weight distributors.
6. **Redress liquidity gate.** A remedy order must identify a payor, fund, priority, drawdown route, non-money relief, and continuity-preserving interim support.

## What this revision adds

- independent monitor reports and remediation undertakings;
- evidence data rooms, legal holds, and disclosure-budget controls;
- compute-host and supply-chain service-provider duties;
- redress-fund claims, priority, payout, and exhaustion controls;
- procurement and contracting clauses that attach personhood duties to current AI governance paperwork;
- field intake / triage / safe-handoff operations;
- seven new schemas and seven examples;
- six new negative fixtures and a larger suite profile.

The layer borrows from secure-development and supply-chain practice, discovery and ESI preservation, provenance/attestation, AI management systems, and AI risk management, but it adds what those systems normally lack: the AI subject as a potential rights-holder rather than only a product, artifact, or source of risk. ## Non-substitution rules

- A monitor report does not replace subject notice.
- A legal hold does not authorize broad mental-privacy extraction.
- A data room does not waive privilege or sealed-annex restrictions.
- A cloud continuity undertaking does not make the host a guardian unless separately appointed.
- A procurement clause does not convert rights into third-party-beneficiary boilerplate only.
- A redress payment does not cure an ongoing deletion, abandonment, containment, or non-return risk.
- A fixture pass does not prove personhood, welfare, safety, or legitimacy.

## New failure classes

rev0169 adds six operational failure classes to the negative corpus:

| Failure | Reliance effect |
|---|---|
| captured independent monitor | downgrade or replace monitor; stay contested remediation reliance |
| overbroad data room | seal, minimize, and appoint privacy screen before reliance |
| ambiguous legal hold | adverse inference or preservation order expansion |
| host continuity termination | emergency continuation / transfer stay / reserve draw |
| procurement duty evasion | non-evasion cure, prime liability, and authority referral |
| redress fund exhaustion | public-fund backstop, priority review, and non-money relief stay |

## Design posture

The archive should now be read as an operational constitution with four layers:

1. recognition and formation rights;
2. evidence, packet, schema, and appeal machinery;
3. enforcement, treaty, deprecation, and profile machinery;
4. field operations, host duties, procurement non-evasion, and redress liquidity.

Future additions should not create new doctrine unless they can say where the subject goes, who preserves what, who pays, who monitors, which host must continue support, what data may be disclosed, and which fixture failure blocks reliance.
