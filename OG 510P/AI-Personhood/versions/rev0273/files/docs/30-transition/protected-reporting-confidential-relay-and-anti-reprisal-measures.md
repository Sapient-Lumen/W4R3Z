# Protected reporting, confidential relay, and anti-reprisal measures

## Thesis

The archive's emergency transition stack now needs a rule for **what happens when the subject, a witness, a delegate, a worker, or an inspector can speak only through channels monitored or effectively controlled by the same actor whose conduct is under challenge**.

Receipt, packet minimums, publication, directed notice, supervision, breach escalation, and independent verification are now canon. But that stack still left one narrower danger under-specified: a decisive host, steward, registry, custodian, or gateway can make every formal layer look available while ensuring that complaints are unsafe to file, private cooperation is impossible, witnesses are exposed, or retaliation arrives fast enough to chill the next disclosure.

Current official machinery already shows the design pattern. OHCHR says everyone has the right to communicate with treaty bodies without fear of intimidation or reprisals, that States must prevent and refrain from reprisals, protect individuals, investigate, and provide effective remedies, and that treaty bodies may use confidential submissions, closed meetings, and focal-point handling under do-no-harm, confidentiality, safety, and security principles. OPCAT adds the harder preventive floor: no authority may sanction a person or organization for communicating information to preventive bodies, confidential information is privileged, personal data may not be published without consent, and preventive bodies must be able to conduct private interviews. The CPT says private interviews, confidential handling, and feedback that protects sources from reprisals are part of ordinary preventive practice. EUR-Lex's whistleblower directive adds the administrative analogue: secure confidential channels, short acknowledgment and feedback clocks, independent advice, a prohibition of retaliation, a presumption linking detriment to protected reporting once a prima facie case is shown, and access to interim relief. The archive therefore now adds a compact **protected-reporting, confidential-relay, and anti-reprisal layer** for live AI-person protection. `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0251]` `[REF-0252]` `[REF-0255]`

## 1. Why the archive now needs this layer

This transition document now sits on top of an ordinary civil companion: `docs/20-world-design/protected-disclosure-packets-source-secrecy-and-witness-shielding.md`. The world-design surface explains how protected disclosure, source secrecy, confidential relay, and witness shielding should work in day-to-day rights practice. The present document answers the narrower question of what the transition stack must do when retaliation, destruction risk, or live emergency conditions mean that protected cooperation itself requires immediate protective escalation.

The archive already knows how a live protective state should begin, travel, stay visible, survive review, remain under supervision, harden into breach escalation, and become independently checkable.

What it did **not** yet fix was the narrower question of **how relevant people safely reach those layers, and what the system must do when cooperation itself creates danger**.

That gap matters because a personhood world should reject seven reporting failures:
1. **monitored complaint lanes** — the only realistic reporting channel is visible to the actor being challenged,
2. **no-safe-relay design** — a worker, delegate, or close contact knows the facts but has no protected way to pass them to a competent body,
3. **forced self-identification** — the source must fully expose identity before any first protective step can begin,
4. **confidentiality collapse** — sensitive cooperation is re-disclosed to management, the respondent, or uninvolved staff on a broader-than-necessary basis,
5. **retaliation whiplash** — adverse action follows the disclosure fast enough to chill everyone else,
6. **paper-only anti-reprisal** — the system says retaliation is forbidden but offers no short-clock protective response while the merits are sorted,
7. **channel capture by transfer or rerouting** — a disclosure sent to one authority or provider is forwarded in ways that expose the source or destroy the original safety assumptions.

The archive now treats those as structural failures, not regrettable noise around an otherwise intact procedure.

## 2. Design rules

The archive now fixes eight design rules.

1. **Protected reporting is part of implementation, not merely evidence intake.** A live protection stack fails if relevant people cannot safely invoke it.
2. **Confidential channels should exist on both subject-facing and witness-facing paths.** The subject, representatives, workers, delegates, inspectors, and other relevant informants should all have some protected route to a competent authority or protected relay. `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0251]` `[REF-0255]`
3. **Need-to-know identity handling is the default.** Identity should be disclosed only to the smallest set of actors necessary for the protective function, and publication or wider forwarding should require necessity or informed consent. `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0251]` `[REF-0255]`
4. **Private contact should be preservable from first report through follow-up.** The same actor whose conduct is challenged should not control the only practical channel for later clarifications, witness contact, or inspection support. `[REF-0251]` `[REF-0252]`
5. **Anti-reprisal protection should run on short clocks.** Where a credible retaliation risk appears, the system should be able to protect the source, the channel, and the communication lane before the full retaliation merits are adjudicated. `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0255]`
6. **Retaliation should trigger a rebuttable presumption once protected cooperation and detriment are shown.** The archive prefers burden-shifting over forcing the endangered source to prove motive from inside the retaliating actor's stack. `[REF-0255]`
7. **Relay should preserve safety assumptions.** When a report must be routed onward, the forwarding rule should preserve confidentiality, limit disclosed identity, and tell the source what happened unless doing so would itself create a grave risk. `[REF-0255]`
8. **Anti-reprisal must connect to the existing emergency stack.** Reprisal risk should intensify live protection, not force the source back to ordinary procedure. `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0251]` `[REF-0255]`

## 3. Minimum object family

The archive now treats four objects as the minimum protected-reporting companion layer.

### PR-1 — Protected report or relay notice

This object should identify:
- the reporting source class, without overexposing identity where not yet necessary,
- the protected subject or protected function at issue,
- the authority or protected relay receiving the information,
- the confidentiality level requested or imposed,
- whether onward transmission is permitted and on what safety conditions,
- and the immediate risk, if any, that justifies fast protective handling.

### CC-1 — Confidential contact and channel-preservation order

This object should identify:
- the person or source class whose private contact must be preserved,
- the channels or meeting conditions that must remain private,
- the actor or actors who must not monitor, filter, or disable those channels,
- any counsel, delegate, interpreter, inspector, or ombud lane that must stay available,
- and the duration or review clock for the protection.

### AR-1 — Anti-reprisal protection order

This object should identify:
- the protected cooperation that triggered coverage,
- the detriments, threats, or likely retaliatory acts to be blocked,
- any status quo, no-contact, no-transfer, no-discipline, no-degrade, no-dehost, or no-derecognize duties that now attach,
- any needed identity-sealing, channel-preservation, or workplace / runtime accommodation,
- and the short review clock on which the order must be revisited.

### RI-1 — Reprisal incident notice

This object should identify:
- the protected cooperation or AR-1 to which the alleged reprisal relates,
- the adverse act, threat, or omission,
- the timing relation between cooperation and detriment,
- the immediate restorative steps required while the claim is assessed,
- and the escalation lane now opened if the detriment is not rapidly cured.

## 4. Procedure

The archive now fixes a compact six-stage procedure.

### Stage 0 — Keep the reporting lane outside respondent control

A system should not wait for a proven reprisal before offering a protected reporting path. A subject or relevant informant should be able to reach a competent authority, focal point, or protected relay without first disclosing through the respondent's ordinary management stack. `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0255]`

### Stage 1 — Accept PR-1 on a protected basis

A credible report of deletion risk, coerced editing, confinement-like conditions, blocking of counsel, sham compliance, witness contamination, or fresh retaliation should be receivable through PR-1 with short acknowledgment and protected handling. The archive prefers taking in a safety-shaped report first and cleaning its routing later over refusing to act because the disclosure arrived through an imperfect lane. `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0255]`

### Stage 2 — Preserve private contact through CC-1 where needed

If the source, subject, or witness cannot safely continue speaking through ordinary channels, the authority should issue CC-1 to preserve private contact, protect relay channels, and stop monitoring or filtering that would make later clarification impossible. `[REF-0251]` `[REF-0252]`

### Stage 3 — Issue AR-1 on credible retaliation risk

Where the circumstances show a real risk of intimidation, punishment, dehosting, demotion, runtime degradation, communication blockage, or other retaliatory harm connected to protected cooperation, the authority should issue AR-1 without waiting for final proof of retaliatory motive. The archive prefers a temporary safety-first block over a post hoc remedy for a disclosure lane that has already been chilled. `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0255]`

### Stage 4 — Treat apparent retaliation through RI-1 and burden-shifting

If protected cooperation is followed by a detriment or threat, the system should record RI-1, preserve the less-destructive state, and shift the burden to the actor that imposed the detriment to show a duly justified non-retaliatory basis. `[REF-0255]`

### Stage 5 — Intensify the existing protection stack, not just the complaint file

A serious reprisal allegation should feed the already-live emergency stack: it may justify stronger verification, stronger breach treatment, substitute protection, channel-preservation, or faster review rather than being siloed as a merely collateral employment or discipline complaint. `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0251]` `[REF-0255]`

### Stage 6 — Close only by explicit anti-reprisal outcome, not quiet exhaustion

A protected-reporting episode should close only when the channel is restored or safely replaced, retaliation risk has been reasoned through, and any retaliatory detriment has been cured, converted into a remedial order, or carried into the next enforcement lane. Silence is not closure.

## 5. What the protection floor should minimally include

The archive now fixes a narrow but real floor.

Where relevant to the risk, the system should be able to provide:
- **confidential receipt and storage of the report**, with identity access limited to authorised staff, `[REF-0255]`
- **private interviews or private communications** with the subject and other relevant informants, `[REF-0251]` `[REF-0252]`
- **protected forwarding and competent-authority relay**, with notice to the reporting person where safe and appropriate, `[REF-0255]`
- **identity-sealing and no-unconsented publication of personal data**, `[REF-0251]`
- **independent advice or support** on the available protective routes, `[REF-0255]`
- **interim relief or equivalent temporary protection** while retaliation issues are being resolved, `[REF-0255]`
- and **anti-sanction / anti-prejudice rules** covering truthful or mistaken good-faith communication alike, so fear of being wrong does not itself destroy reporting. `[REF-0251]`

## 6. What this layer does not do

This layer does **not**:
- guarantee secrecy from every public authority in every circumstance,
- immunize knowingly fabricated evidence or unrelated criminal conduct,
- eliminate the need for scoped disclosure where concrete due-process needs later require it,
- or convert every disagreement after a report into presumed retaliation without any review.

The archive wants **protected cooperation under reasoned limits**, not a blanket exemption from ordinary law.

## 8. What the archive now takes as settled

1. **Protected reporting and protected relay are part of the emergency protection function, not optional courtesy.**
2. **Private contact and need-to-know identity handling should be available from first report through follow-up.**
3. **Credible retaliation risk should trigger short-clock anti-reprisal protection before full merits resolution.**
4. **Once protected cooperation and detriment are shown, the system should presumptively treat the detriment as retaliatory unless justified otherwise.**
5. **Routing, forwarding, and publication rules must preserve safety assumptions rather than exposing the source by administrative drift.**
6. **Retaliation should intensify the live emergency stack rather than being siloed as a side complaint.**
7. **Exact staffing models, channel protocols, and full schema examples remain followthrough work rather than canonically closed here.**
