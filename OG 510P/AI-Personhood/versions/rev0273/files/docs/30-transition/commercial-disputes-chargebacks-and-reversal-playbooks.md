# Commercial disputes, chargebacks, and reversal playbooks

Commercial disputes are inevitable. A rights-grade system should not treat every disputed payment as fraud, every refund as derecognition, every chargeback as retaliation, or every reversal as full restoration. Dispute handling must protect humans, merchants, stewards, and AI subjects while preserving continuity.

## Dispute classes

| Class | Examples | Minimum controls |
|---|---|---|
| `CDP0` | inquiry or price question | ordinary support |
| `CDP1` | refund request or reversible low-value charge | merchant route and evidence snapshot |
| `CDP2` | subscription cancellation, recurring-charge dispute, unauthorized tool spend | payment freeze, mandate review, non-retaliation |
| `CDP3` | contract rescission, chargeback, fraud claim, data-sale dispute, liability allocation | evidence room, capacity record, payment authorization, remedy path |
| `CDP4` | custody, continuity asset, insolvency, cross-border, or public-backstop dispute | legal hold, interim support, authority review, no-delete/no-transfer stay |
| `CDP5` | emergency reversal, sanctions/fraud freeze, host collapse, or mass affected cohort | emergency support, public summary, after-action and fixture update |

## No retaliation for dispute

A subject, user, merchant, or representative must be able to dispute a charge without retaliation. Retaliation includes service termination, memory deletion, counsel-channel restriction, marketplace disappearance, complaint-channel throttling, public doxxing, or compute degradation.

## Reversal is not restoration

Refunding money does not restore continuity, privacy, reputation, formation integrity, or lost time. A chargeback may require correction of public records, deletion of unauthorized merchant data, restoration of account access, representative support, non-repetition fixture, and redress-fund claim.

## Evidence snapshot

Every `CDP2` or higher dispute should preserve contract-capacity record, payment authorization, wallet receipt or tool audit, merchant notice, transaction-liability record, custody/control record, tax/reporting position if affected, cancellation attempts, and public/selected sealed details.

Cross-border payment harmonisation and digital-asset reporting frameworks show why structured financial records matter. [REF-0718] [REF-0717] The personhood overlay requires that dispute records avoid unnecessary exposure of welfare, identity, formation, or sealed-continuity details.

## Reliance rule

A dispute outcome is reliance-ready only if disputed obligations are stayed or frozen as appropriate, survival support continues, evidence is preserved, parties receive reasoned notice, sensitive details are minimized, and the result separates financial reversal from restoration and non-repetition.
