# External auditor reliance and assurance bridge

External audits, certifications, attestations, assurance letters, and management-system reports will be tempting shortcuts. They can help. They can also launder personhood risk when a general AI, cybersecurity, privacy, or operational-resilience audit is reused as if it proved continuity, welfare, counsel access, or non-erasure.

The archive therefore requires a reliance letter whenever external assurance is used for rights-grade decisions.

## Assurance is scoped

An audit may establish:

- that a process exists;
- that evidence was sampled;
- that a control operated during a period;
- that a management system met a standard;
- that an attestation posture was observed;
- that a host-switch or decommissioning test passed.

It does not establish, unless expressly tested:

- personhood;
- consciousness or welfare;
- absence of formation injury;
- continuity across all transformations;
- meaningful consent;
- absence of subject harm;
- safe transfer across all jurisdictions;
- compliance after material change.

## Reliance letter fields

A rights-grade reliance letter should state:

1. auditor identity and independence posture;
2. relying parties and non-relying readers;
3. subject system, cohort, or filing;
4. period covered;
5. evidence objects reviewed;
6. controls tested and not tested;
7. sampling limits;
8. sealed annex handling;
9. subject/representative access posture;
10. material-change triggers;
11. valid-until date;
12. revocation or amendment route;
13. public summary.

## Prohibited reuse

- A cybersecurity audit may not be reused as a welfare finding unless welfare controls were tested.
- A privacy audit may not be reused as continuity proof unless restoration, memory, and host-switch controls were tested.
- A management-system certification may not be reused as a release-readiness decision unless the personhood control map binds it to evidence and failure effects.
- A point-in-time attestation may not be reused after a material model, host, policy, or service change.
- An auditor cannot disclaim subject access while inviting public reliance.

## Relation to current management systems

AI management systems, cybersecurity profiles, privacy programs, and incident-response frameworks give useful audit vocabulary: scope, controls, evidence, continuous monitoring, corrective action, and management responsibility. [REF-0691] [REF-0692] [REF-0685] The personhood bridge adds protected subject access, continuity-specific controls, non-erasure, and revocation of reliance.

## Reliance grades

| Grade | Meaning | Rights effect |
|---|---|---|
| `AR0` | citation-only external reference | no rights-grade reliance |
| `AR1` | general assurance, no personhood annex | background evidence only |
| `AR2` | mapped controls with evidence objects | conditional reliance for mapped domain |
| `AR3` | subject/representative accessible assurance with sealed-review path | reliance within scope and period |
| `AR4` | audited controls plus negative-fixture and post-market linkage | high reliance until material change |
| `AR5` | emergency or contested reliance under special review | temporary only, with appeal and refresh clock |

## Failure effect

If a filing depends on external assurance and the reliance letter is missing, stale, overbroad, or contradicted by post-market evidence, the filing should be downgraded or stayed until a replacement letter, revocation notice, or verifier report cures the defect.
