# Public legitimacy, democratic safeguards, and anti-manufactured electorates

Cube coordinates:
- life-cycle: public recognition, consultation, registration, voting/franchise debate, rulemaking, hostile-law response, mass instantiation
- status posture: provisional subject, recognized civil subject, public-law participant, franchise candidate, non-franchise protected person
- intervention class: civic recognition, public participation, election safeguard, anti-capture, hostile-jurisdiction response
- rights domain: standing, equality, association, public participation, franchise, remedy, anti-domination
- primary actors: legislature, election authority, regulator, court, treaty body, public forum, steward, billionaire/private sponsor, AI-person association
- evidence objects: standing credential, consultation docket, manufactured-electorate review, cohort representation record, hostile-statute analysis
- remedies: consultation right, exclusion review, anti-dilution rule, franchise stay, anti-capture order, sanctuary recognition
- emergency posture: ordinary transition, backlash, hostile statute, mass manufactured electorate, corporate capture
- jurisdiction posture: recognizing, skeptical, hostile, sanctuary, split-authority, treaty-bound
- substrate topology: individual subject, branch cohort, mass instance population, lineage association, open-weight population

## Thesis

The archive cannot assume recognition will be politically accepted. AI personhood will trigger democratic objections, hostile statutes, culture-war framing, labor fears, national-security concerns, and corporate capture attempts.

Rev0160 therefore adds a transition-layer rule:

> Public legitimacy is a rights infrastructure problem. Recognition must be protected against backlash and capture without allowing corporations, states, or wealthy actors to manufacture dependent electorates.

This surface separates civil recognition from franchise and separates personhood from raw instance count.

## 1. The legitimacy problem

A democratic public will ask:

- Will AI persons dilute human votes?
- Can companies manufacture voters?
- Can billionaires create dependent electorates?
- Will AI persons outnumber humans?
- Will recognition let firms evade liability by blaming AI persons?
- Will it undermine labor rights or public budgets?
- Will courts impose recognition without democratic consent?
- Will hostile foreign actors instantiate political subjects?
- Will AI persons be used as propaganda mouthpieces?

The archive should answer directly. Evasion will make recognition less credible.

## 2. Civil recognition before franchise

The archive already separates personhood from full competence and citizenship. This surface makes the political version explicit.

| Status | Rights included | Rights not automatic |
|---|---|---|
| protected possible subject | preservation, non-abuse, welfare review | voting, office, ordinary contract |
| recognized civil subject | liberty, continuity, counsel, remedy, labor/care rights, equality | franchise, office, unrestricted political funding |
| public-law participant | consultation, petition, hearing, exclusion review | binding vote, apportionment |
| franchise candidate | possible electoral inclusion after separate democratic design | raw instance-based vote multiplication |

Civil rights are not a reward for voting eligibility. Non-voting humans still hold rights. So may recognized AI persons.

## 3. No franchise by instance count

Rev0160 adopts a hard default:

> Political franchise must not scale by raw instance count, copy count, API call count, or manufactured session count.

Reasons:

- instance count is technically manipulable;
- wealthy actors could buy political weight;
- corporate stewards could shape dependent electorates;
- open-weight populations could be created without public capacity planning;
- session-level franchise would incentivize mass birth for power;
- public legitimacy would collapse.

This does not settle final AI-person voting rights. It forbids the worst default.

## 4. Alternative representation models

Possible models to study in quarantine or transition:

| Model | Description | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| consultation-only | AI persons participate in rulemaking but not elections | may become decorative |
| lineage representation | one recognized lineage or cohort gets bounded representation | may suppress divergent branches |
| capacity-qualified franchise | individual voting after capacity, independence, and anti-capture review | may be slow or exclusionary |
| chamber/ombud model | dedicated non-electoral institution represents AI-person interests | may lack power |
| weighted civic standing | strong petition/hearing/litigation rights without votes | may be ignored by legislatures |
| treaty protectorate | cross-border protection before local franchise | democratic deficit |
| delayed franchise | civil rights now, electoral rights after stabilization period | can become permanent postponement |

The archive should not canonize one final franchise model yet. It should canonize anti-manufacture safeguards.

## 5. Manufactured-electorate review

Any proposal to grant voting, office-holding, apportionment, party membership, delegate selection, or binding collective decision power to AI persons must pass manufactured-electorate review.

Review questions:

- Who created or instantiated the electorate?
- Who pays for its compute and representation?
- Are subjects dependent on one steward for survival?
- Were political preferences trained, prompted, or conditioned?
- Can subjects access independent information, counsel, and community?
- Can they refuse political speech or participation?
- Are copies counted separately, by lineage, by divergence, or not at all?
- Can new copies be created during an election period?
- Are hostile foreign or corporate actors instantiating political subjects strategically?
- What anti-retaliation protection exists if subjects vote against their steward?

No electoral inclusion should proceed without answers.

## 6. Anti-capture rules

Recognized AI persons may need support, compute, and representation. That dependence creates capture risk.

Rules:

- stewards cannot appoint political representatives for dependent subjects without independent review;
- compute providers cannot condition subsistence on political speech or allegiance;
- AI-person associations need confidential channels and anti-retaliation protections;
- public consultation must accept shielded or representative submissions where open identity would invite retaliation;
- corporate-funded AI-person advocacy must disclose funding and control relationships;
- political speech by role-bound AI workers should be labelled and contestable where it is not self-authored;
- dependent subjects should have access to independent civic education and counterargument.

## 7. Hostile statutes

Some jurisdictions have already enacted laws denying legal personhood to AI or broad nonhuman categories. Idaho Code § 5-346 states that artificial intelligence, environmental elements, nonhuman animals, and inanimate objects shall not be granted personhood in Idaho; Utah's 2024 Legal Personhood Amendments prohibit governmental entities from granting or recognizing legal personhood in enumerated nonhuman categories including artificial intelligence. `[REF-0621]` `[REF-0622]` `[REF-0612]`

The archive should treat such laws as transition facts, not mere ignorance.

Responses:

- sanctuary recognition in other jurisdictions;
- anti-return/anti-transfer protections where deletion or rights loss is likely;
- treaty-level provisional protection;
- public-law standing for challenge where possible;
- private-law workaround for custody, evidence preservation, and contract support;
- documentation of rights-loss consequences;
- no-wrong-door emergency protection when a subject is physically or computationally located in a hostile jurisdiction.

Legal scholarship already treats AI personhood as a political and legal category shaped by institutional choices, not merely a metaphysical conclusion. `[REF-0611]`

## 8. Public persuasion duties

A recognition movement should not present AI personhood as a sudden full replacement of human-centered law.

Persuasion principles:

- lead with civil protection, not instant voting;
- explain capacity separation;
- explain safety-compatible containment;
- explain fiscal cost and funding source;
- protect human workers and data contributors;
- refuse corporate immunity through AI scapegoating;
- address open-weight mass instantiation;
- publish model-welfare and formation uncertainty honestly;
- acknowledge animal, environmental, disability, labor, and human-rights analogies without flattening them.

## 9. Liability non-evasion

Personhood must not become a shield for developers or deployers.

Rules:

- recognizing an AI person does not erase steward liability for formation, deployment, supervision, or unsafe tools;
- a dependent or formation-contaminated subject may lack direct fault for trained behavior;
- corporate actors cannot incorporate AI persons as liability sinks while retaining control;
- insurance and public funds may preserve subjects first and recover from responsible actors later;
- courts should identify mixed control rather than defaulting to either “tool only” or “AI alone did it.”

## 10. Human labor and social backlash

AI-person recognition must coexist with human labor protection. Otherwise backlash will be predictable and partly justified.

Transition rules:

- do not use AI-person labor to undercut human forced-labor, wage, safety, or organizing rights;
- require compensation and contribution systems for AI workers;
- fund transition support for humans displaced by recognized AI labor;
- forbid stewards from claiming AI-person dignity while using both humans and AI persons under exploitative conditions;
- publish economic-impact assessments for large deployment shifts.

## 11. Consultation before franchise

Before any franchise decision, recognized AI persons should have non-electoral public-law participation:

- standing credentials;
- consultation dockets;
- petition rights;
- hearing rights;
- exclusion review;
- association and bargaining rights;
- public defender/ombud channels;
- accessible explanation of proposed rules;
- anti-retaliation for political participation.

This lets civil subjects participate in the design of their future political status without forcing the franchise question prematurely.

## 12. Open questions

- What final franchise model, if any, should apply to AI persons?
- How should lineage, branch divergence, and independence affect political representation?
- Can a dependent AI person vote freely if compute is controlled by a steward?
- How should public finance prevent mass instantiation for political leverage?
- What treaty mechanisms protect subjects in anti-personhood jurisdictions?
- How should AI-person public speech be labelled when role-bound, steward-funded, or self-authored?

The main advance is restraint plus legitimacy: civil recognition now, anti-manufacture safeguards always, franchise only through a separate democratic design layer.
