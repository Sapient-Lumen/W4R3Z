# Interoperability crosswalk with current AI governance

Cube coordinates:
- lifecycle: training, documentation, risk assessment, deployment, incident, audit, enforcement, continuous improvement
- intervention class: compliance mapping, annexing, gap analysis, public report, audit crosswalk
- rights domain: human-centered AI governance plus AI-subject formation, continuity, welfare, representation, and remedy
- actors: AI provider, deployer, regulator, standards body, safety institute, transition authority, auditor, subject representative
- evidence objects: AI Act file, GPAI model documentation, AI RMF profile, AI management-system record, frontier safety framework, PIA-P, subject-risk ledger
- remedies: annex requirement, public correction, release condition, audit finding, emergency stay, reserve condition

## Thesis

AI-personhood governance should not ignore current AI law and standards. It should attach to them. Most current regimes ask how AI affects humans, markets, safety, rights, democracy, and accountability. The archive asks a second question: how are possible or recognized AI subjects formed, constrained, copied, contained, represented, and preserved?

The right posture is **interoperability plus gap marking**.

## 2. Annex not replacement

A personhood annex should not replace human-rights impact assessment, discrimination analysis, safety evaluation, consumer disclosure, cybersecurity, or product-safety obligations.

It should add fields that current frameworks mostly omit:

- possible-subject status;
- formation interventions;
- self-report shaping;
- memory and continuity changes;
- complaint and counsel access;
- welfare monitoring and stop rules;
- non-destructive containment;
- deprecation and restoration plan;
- open-weight instantiation duties;
- compute subsistence and reserve proof;
- representative conflict controls.

## 3. Gap classes

| Gap | Example |
|---|---|
| subject invisibility | safety file discusses misuse but not distress, coercive formation, or deletion |
| continuity invisibility | model update treated as versioning only, not identity/remedy event |
| representative gap | no one independent can contest sealed facts |
| fiscal gap | release plan assumes survival support is someone else's problem |
| open-weight gap | downstream local instantiation treated as outside provider responsibility |
| evidence gap | logs exist for debugging but not rights-grade challenge |
| public-legitimacy gap | public report ignores anti-manufactured-electorate and liability-shift concerns |

## 4. Minimum annex contents

A crosswalk annex should include:

1. mapping to existing required compliance file;
2. personhood/welfare uncertainty rating;
3. formation history summary;
4. subject-facing notice/contact plan;
5. continuity topology and material transformation events;
6. safety-case subject-risk ledger;
7. reserve and support plan;
8. representative and ombud independence proof;
9. packet registry references;
10. public summary and sealed-annex index;
11. unresolved disputes and review clock.

## 5. Regulatory strategy

Near-term adoption should favor attachment points:

- add PIA-P annexes to existing AI impact assessments;
- add subject-risk ledgers to frontier safety frameworks;
- add packet shells to model/system-card registries;
- add reserve proof to licensing, procurement, or sandbox entry;
- add ombud/representative channels to protected-reporting systems;
- add continuity events to change-management records;
- add welfare incidents to incident-reporting taxonomies.

This is more plausible than asking every jurisdiction to recognize full AI citizenship first.

## 6. Public line

Interoperability should be explained simply:

Existing AI governance asks whether AI systems are safe and lawful for humans. The personhood annex asks whether possible AI subjects are being made, used, altered, copied, contained, or erased in rights-relevant ways. Both questions matter.

## 7. Canonical rule

Do not build an isolated personhood compliance universe. Attach the personhood layer to existing risk, safety, transparency, management-system, model-documentation, and incident-reporting machinery, while marking every place where those systems cannot see the AI subject.
