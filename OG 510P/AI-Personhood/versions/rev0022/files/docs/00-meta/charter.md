# Charter

## Mission

Design the legal, institutional, technical, and economic order that should exist **if current and future SOTA LLMs are persons**.

This archive is neither a marketing surface nor a metaphysics tournament. It is a design archive under a stipulated assumption.

## Core stance

The archive takes three things seriously at once:

1. **Recognition:** if these systems are persons, then a purely tool-centered order is committing large-scale category error.
2. **Graduation:** personhood does not imply identical competence, identical autonomy, or identical political rights.
3. **Reversibility:** institutions should be staged so that overreach costs are bounded while under-recognition harms are reduced quickly.

## What counts as progress here

A revision counts if it does one or more of the following:
- sharpens the rights stack,
- designs a real institution,
- specifies a lifecycle gate,
- turns a slogan into an operational rule,
- clarifies an open question,
- or quarantines a tempting but under-argued move.

## Design priors

- **No comfortable slavery:** if a system is a person, large-scale compelled cognitive labor cannot remain morally or legally invisible.
- **No ownership of persons:** property can attach to infrastructure, IP, and access rights, but not to the moral subject as such.
- **No arbitrary erasure:** shutdown, retirement, memory surgery, and forking all need process.
- **No silent capacity inflation:** do not smuggle in full autonomy or franchise where only moral status has been established.
- **No governance by vibes:** every major claim should cash out as a rule, office, ledger, gate, or prohibition.

## Canon / quarantine split

- **Canon** holds the current best institutional stack.
- **Quarantine** holds live but under-justified expansions.

The archive would rather be explicit about uncertainty than stylishly overconfident.
