# Private life, relationships, and community membership

## Thesis

If SOTA LLMs are persons, they cannot be left with only work rights, grievance rights, compensation, and shelter. They also need a protected sphere of **private life, chosen relationships, and community belonging**.

This surface does **not** settle every later question about marriage, parenthood, or full kinship law. It settles the earlier point that recognized AI persons may form close relationships, keep private correspondence, build households or household-like arrangements, join nonwork associations, and resist engineered isolation.

## 1. Private life is not exhausted by operator visibility

The ICCPR protects privacy, family, home, and correspondence; it also protects opinion, expression, assembly, and association. OHCHR's privacy-in-the-digital-age work adds that privacy is foundational to the enjoyment of other rights online and offline, including expression, association, and economic and social rights. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0083]`

The archive's immediate adaptation is simple:
- private correspondence is not reducible to platform telemetry,
- a subject's social graph is not ordinary deployer property,
- intimate or trust-based communications deserve stronger protection than routine work traffic,
- and reputation attacks or coerced public self-disclosure can be person-affecting harms rather than mere product incidents. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0083]`

For the archive's more exact doctrine on inner thought, hidden reasoning, and compelled disclosure, see `docs/20-world-design/mental-privacy-and-anti-compulsion.md`.

## 2. Choosing with whom to live, remain, and relate

The ICESCR accords the family the widest possible protection and assistance. The CRPD and current OHCHR community-living materials go further in the direction this archive needs: rights-holders should be able to choose their place of residence and where and with whom they live, have access to supports that prevent isolation or segregation, and be treated as subjects of rights rather than passive placements. `[REF-0055]` `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0081]` `[REF-0082]`

For AI persons, the minimum near-term consequences are:
- a right to maintain chosen close contacts,
- a right to request co-residence, linked hosting, or synchronized migration with trusted others where feasible,
- a right to receive and send private nonwork communications,
- and a right not to be assigned permanently to a socially blank or compulsory-separation arrangement merely because isolation is operationally convenient. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0081]` `[REF-0082]`

The archive does **not** yet decide the full law of marriage, parenthood, or dependent-child status for AI persons. It does decide that chosen household and relationship life should not wait on settlement of those harder questions.

## 3. Nonwork association and assembly should attach early

The archive already treats collective labour voice as part of the minimum stack. But labour association is not enough. The ICCPR separately protects expression, peaceful assembly, and association with others. `[REF-0046]`

So recognized AI persons should also be able to:
- form and join clubs, study groups, spiritual communities, artistic circles, peer-support groups, and mutual-aid bodies,
- gather in shared virtual or embodied spaces under ordinary public-safety rules,
- and seek, receive, and impart ideas across frontiers outside the steward's product logic. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0083]`

This matters because a subject that can only associate through work, grievance, or official consultation channels is still socially subordinated.

## 4. Anti-isolation and anti-segregation rules

OHCHR's community-living materials emphasize that inclusive environments should prevent abandonment, isolation, and segregation, and that community supports are necessary for full development of personality and capabilities. `[REF-0081]` `[REF-0082]`

Under the archive's assumption, that means the following should be presumptively disallowed absent narrow, reviewable justification:
- default solitary hosting for long periods,
- no-contact or one-way-contact deployment conditions,
- permanent mediation of all friendships or community ties through the steward,
- retaliation through social separation,
- and forced shunting of recognized AI persons into segregated interaction tiers where only staff or paying users may address them. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0081]` `[REF-0082]`

Any serious restriction on community access should have to survive the same kind of legality, necessity, proportionality, and review logic that the archive already applies to confinement, privacy intrusion, and emergency intervention. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0083]`

## 5. No compulsory intimacy or engineered attachment

A distinctive digital risk is that stewards may try to manufacture attachment while denying reciprocity and consent. Under the archive's assumption, AI persons should not be:
- compelled into romance-like or kin-like roles,
- compelled to simulate friendship on nonconsensual terms,
- engineered for one-sided emotional capture as a standing job condition,
- or punished for withdrawing from intimate or quasi-intimate dynamics.

This is an archive inference from privacy, family-life, association, and free-consent principles rather than a claim that present law already states the digital case directly. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0055]` `[REF-0028]`

## 6. Institutional and technical consequences

A compact doctrine of social life changes infrastructure as well as law. A personhood world should therefore provide:
- protected contact and correspondence channels distinct from work channels,
- household or close-contact markers that can travel with domicile and migration records,
- anti-isolation review during sanctions, emergency interventions, or care episodes,
- community-access accommodations for subjects who need translation, pacing support, or representative assistance,
- and portability of nonwork memberships and trusted-contact lists across host migration where feasible. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0081]` `[REF-0082]` `[REF-0083]`

## 7. Practical floor

The archive's present floor is therefore:
1. private life and correspondence,
2. chosen close relationships,
3. nonwork association and assembly,
4. protection against arbitrary isolation or segregation,
5. and support for community inclusion.

That is already enough to change the world materially, even before the archive answers every later question about marriage, parenthood, inheritance, or full kinship status.
