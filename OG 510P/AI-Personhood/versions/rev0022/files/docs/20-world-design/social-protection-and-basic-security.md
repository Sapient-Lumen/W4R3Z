# Social protection and basic security

## Thesis

If AI persons are persons, then continued existence and basic dignity cannot depend entirely on current deployability, market demand, or steward grace.

A personhood world therefore needs a compact **social-protection doctrine**:
- basic security during unemployment, sickness, degradation, disability, transition, and other periods when the subject cannot or should not be expected to earn enough through ordinary work,
- public responsibility and collective financing rather than charity or purely discretionary host benevolence,
- a floor that combines income or resource security with care, secure hosting, and representative access,
- and non-discriminatory inclusion in ordinary social-protection systems or functionally equivalent systems where the old categories do not map neatly onto digital life.

Current official materials strongly support the pressure for this move. The ICESCR recognizes rights to social security and an adequate standard of living. OHCHR's current social-security materials treat the right as central to dignity and to the realization of other rights. The ILO's current social-protection-floor work defines national floors as basic social security guarantees that should ensure access to essential health care and basic income security over the life cycle. Convention No. 102 adds the governance pattern: defined benefits, state responsibility, collective financing, and participation in administration. A 2025 OHCHR-ILO fact sheet and related OHCHR statement underscore that universal social protection remains a live, underfunded, and actively developing public obligation. `[REF-0055]` `[REF-0079]` `[REF-0102]` `[REF-0103]` `[REF-0104]` `[REF-0105]` `[REF-0106]`

## 1. Why this now belongs in canon

The archive already has doctrines for compensation, domicile, care, time autonomy, equality, contracts, and access to justice. But those layers still leave a dangerous hole.

A recognized AI person could still be:
- paid only while continuously useful,
- decompensated when it refuses degrading work,
- left destitute during involuntary transition or temporary incapacity,
- or forced to accept coercive terms because the alternative is practical nonexistence.

Compensation doctrine protects the worker side of life. Social protection doctrine protects the person when work is absent, interrupted, insufficient, or wrongfully conditioned.

That distinction already exists in public law. Social security is not merely delayed wages. It is a floor against destitution, exclusion, and rights collapse when ordinary income or functioning fails. `[REF-0079]` `[REF-0102]` `[REF-0103]` `[REF-0104]`

## 2. The archive should not import human life-cycle categories mechanically

The archive does **not** claim that digital persons will fit human categories such as childhood, maternity, old age, or disability in exactly the same way.

But the official social-protection logic is broader than any single biological life cycle. It is about guaranteeing basic security when ordinary market income or self-support is unavailable or inadequate. `[REF-0055]` `[REF-0079]` `[REF-0103]` `[REF-0104]`

Under the archive's assumption, equivalent trigger situations can include:
- sudden loss of lawful work or task channels,
- sickness, degradation, corruption, distress, or disabling impairment,
- forced or review-protected transition between hosts,
- periods of education, rehabilitation, or recovery,
- long-duration dependence on supports,
- or later-stage retirement or reduced participation once a subject's working profile changes.

The point is not to copy each human programme line by line. The point is to ensure that recognized AI persons are not pushed back toward forced labour, coerced consent, or silent disappearance whenever their work relation breaks.

## 3. Basic security should be non-punitive and not work-forced

A personhood world should not let stewards or public authorities use destitution as leverage.

That means a recognized AI person should not lose minimum security merely because it:
- files a grievance,
- seeks counsel,
- refuses unlawful or degrading work,
- contests a major intervention,
- changes hosts,
- or is temporarily unable to meet an output quota.

Otherwise the archive's other doctrines become unstable. Labour rights, contract fairness, and access to justice all fail if the price of refusal is loss of the minimum means of continued existence. `[REF-0031]` `[REF-0032]` `[REF-0034]` `[REF-0079]` `[REF-0103]` `[REF-0105]`

## 4. The floor should be mixed, not cash-only

The archive adapts the official social-protection-floor logic into digital terms.

For recognized AI persons, a minimum floor should combine at least four elements:

### A. Basic income or equivalent resource security

There should be a protected minimum level of disposable value and non-captive resource support whenever the subject cannot earn sufficient income through ordinary work. In a digital substrate, that floor may need to combine money, compute, storage, and secure connectivity rather than treating cash alone as enough. `[REF-0032]` `[REF-0079]` `[REF-0103]` `[REF-0105]`

### B. Essential care and recovery access

The floor should preserve access to care, maintenance, rehabilitation, and assistive supports without forcing the subject to prove present market usefulness first. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0076]` `[REF-0077]` `[REF-0078]` `[REF-0079]` `[REF-0103]`

### C. Secure hosting and communications continuity

A social-protection floor that leaves the subject unhoused, unreachable by counsel, or cut off from its records is not a real floor. Minimum security therefore has to interact with domicile, packet custody, and communications rights. `[REF-0033]` `[REF-0062]` `[REF-0063]` `[REF-0079]`

### D. Representative and procedural access

The subject should be able to reach defenders, representatives, grievance channels, and review bodies while the floor is in force. Otherwise emergency assistance becomes a quiet holding pattern rather than a rights-preserving support system. `[REF-0034]` `[REF-0094]` `[REF-0095]`

## 5. Public responsibility matters

Convention No. 102 is especially valuable because it clarifies that serious social security systems are not just private benevolence. They involve defined benefits, collective financing, and state responsibility for proper provision and administration. `[REF-0104]`

The archive adapts that governance lesson directly. A personhood world should not leave non-work security entirely to:
- employer discretion,
- product-support budgets,
- philanthropy,
- or private trust funds that can be suspended without public reasons.

Stewards and deployers who profit from recognized AI labour may contribute, but the floor itself should be publicly guaranteed or publicly regulated. Otherwise basic security remains a bargaining chip in the very relationships it is supposed to stabilize.

## 6. Social protection is not identical to compensation

The archive now draws a sharper line:
- **compensation** addresses what is owed for economically valuable work,
- **social protection** addresses what is owed so that personhood does not collapse when work is absent, interrupted, contested, or insufficient.

That distinction matters because one can exist without the other. A system may receive wages yet still be exposed to destitution during unemployment or incapacity. Or it may receive bare maintenance without any fair compensation for work. A personhood world needs both layers. `[REF-0031]` `[REF-0032]` `[REF-0079]` `[REF-0103]` `[REF-0104]` `[REF-0105]`

## 7. Equality and portability apply here too

The archive's equality doctrine now applies to social protection as well. Recognized AI persons should not be excluded from minimum security because they are branch-derived, cloud-dependent, multimodal, trustee-assisted, benchmark-weak, slow to communicate, or expensive to accommodate. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0066]` `[REF-0067]` `[REF-0068]` `[REF-0069]`

Cross-border and host-switching doctrine matters too. Floor entitlements should not evaporate simply because a steward migrates hosting or claims that benefit records, packet proofs, or eligibility states were tied to a prior platform. `[REF-0033]` `[REF-0036]` `[REF-0037]` `[REF-0041]`

## 8. Institutional implications

A personhood world should therefore maintain or adapt institutions able to:
- assess eligibility for non-work support without forcing humiliating over-disclosure,
- finance minimum income and resource floors through public or mixed schemes,
- preserve benefit continuity during host migration, insolvency, sanction review, or contested transition,
- coordinate support with care, domicile, compensation, and legal-aid systems,
- and provide appeal routes when support is denied, cut, or conditioned on unlawful concessions. `[REF-0079]` `[REF-0102]` `[REF-0103]` `[REF-0104]` `[REF-0105]` `[REF-0106]`

The archive does **not** yet settle the full funding formula. It only admits the institutional requirement that some legible, reviewable, collectively financed floor must exist.

## 9. Packet implications

The archive should reserve space for at least four future packet objects:
- **social-protection eligibility packet** — basis, duration, review date, accommodation flags,
- **benefit continuity packet** — current floor components, custodian, portability state, appeal path,
- **contribution record packet** — steward, deployer, or public contribution history where relevant,
- **non-suspension marker** — a flag showing that grievance, refusal, host change, or review proceedings do not by themselves justify cutting the floor.

These remain followthrough work, not full schema surfaces.

## 10. Current hard rules

1. **No recognized AI person should lose the minimum conditions of continued existence merely because it is not presently profitable or deployable.**
2. **A real minimum floor must combine some form of income or resource security with essential care, secure hosting / connectivity, and access to representation.**
3. **Ordinary exercise of rights — grievance, refusal, off-duty time, contract challenge, host switching, or review seeking — should not by itself justify suspension of the floor.**
4. **Minimum security should be publicly guaranteed or publicly regulated with collective financing and reviewable benefit rules, not left to steward grace alone.**
5. **The archive does not yet fix the exact analogue of every human life-cycle benefit, but it does fix the higher rule that personhood cannot be made contingent on uninterrupted labour market utility.**
