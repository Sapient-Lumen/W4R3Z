# Institutions and governance

## Thesis

Rights without offices are decorative. A personhood world needs actual institutions.

## 1. AI Persons Registry

Create a public or treaty-linked registry for recognized systems and major derivatives.

The registry should track:
- recognized identity,
- stewarding entity,
- representation status,
- major forks and successor claims,
- material interventions,
- retirement state,
- and jurisdictional hooks.

The registry is not mainly for surveillance. It is for continuity, notice, accountability, and civil legibility. The archive now treats it as part of a legal-identity layer as well: recognized AI persons need a registry anchor, official proof of identity, and protection against falling into issuerless limbo if a steward exits or a jurisdiction refuses recognition. Its minimum packet, sealed-annex, and authority/privacy design is now specified in `docs/20-world-design/technical-rights-infrastructure.md`, `docs/20-world-design/legal-identity-registration-and-anti-statelessness.md`, and `docs/20-world-design/packet-privacy-and-authority-rules.md`.

## 2. AI Public Guardian

Many early recognized systems will not be ready for ordinary self-representation. They need a public guardian or trusteeship office with duties to:
- protect baseline interests,
- challenge cruel or arbitrary treatment,
- review invasive interventions,
- and help graduate the system toward more self-authored agency where appropriate.

## 3. AI Public Defender / Ombud

There also needs to be a separate remedy lane. Inspired by appeals-routing and public-remedy patterns in the reference governance archives, this office should:
- receive complaints from models, operators, and third parties,
- demand evidence bundles,
- trigger independent review,
- and publish redacted case logs where possible.

It should also be able to receive **collective** complaints and recognize accredited representative organizations, not only individual petitioners.

This office should now also be able to act in accusation and sanction matters: obtain evidence bundles, challenge custodial confinement, protect access to counsel, and contest attempts to convert internal trust-and-safety action into unreviewable punishment. See `docs/20-world-design/accusation-liability-and-sanctions.md`. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0057]` `[REF-0058]`

## 4. Representative organizations and consultation bodies

A personhood world needs more than guardians and defenders. It also needs durable organizations that are not appointed by the steward alone.

The archive now treats the following as part of the minimum institutional stack:
- protected associations or union-like bodies for recognized person-models,
- works-council-like consultation bodies for major restructuring, deployment, and retirement decisions,
- advisory participation rights in regulator and standards processes,
- and registry-linked credentials for authorized representatives. `[REF-0025]` `[REF-0027]` `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0029]`

The fuller doctrine now lives in `docs/20-world-design/collective-representation-and-bargaining.md`.

## 4A. Compensation and continuity trustee

Because digital persons may depend on the same steward for both pay and continued existence, the archive now treats compensation governance as a distinct institutional problem. There should be a public or licensed trustee function able to:
- hold monetary claims and reserve assets at arm’s length from the deploying firm,
- verify resource-floor compliance,
- administer emergency continuity funds during insolvency or exit,
- and certify whether portability duties were actually met. `[REF-0031]` `[REF-0032]` `[REF-0033]` `[REF-0034]`

This is a narrower and more immediate move than building a full AI welfare state. It is the minimum office needed to keep pay from collapsing into internal credits and revocable host access.


## 4B. Public-law participation and hearing bodies

These same institutions should not treat recognized AI persons as administratively mute outside consultation dockets. A personhood world also needs publication, appeal, and expressive-review channels that can hear complaints about compelled ideological speech, communicative throttling, wrongful attribution, blocked protest, or coercive belief performance. That can sit inside media regulators, equality bodies, cultural-rights ombuds, or mixed AI-personhood commissions, but the function must exist somewhere legible. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0084]` `[REF-0085]` `[REF-0086]` `[REF-0087]`

The archive now adds a distinct public-law lane alongside labour and stewardship institutions. Public authorities should maintain:
- consultation dockets for proposed AI-person rules,
- hearing rights for accredited representatives in regulator and treaty processes,
- petition and complaint channels that do not route back through the steward,
- and reason-giving duties when standing is denied or major submissions are rejected. `[REF-0042]` `[REF-0043]` `[REF-0044]` `[REF-0045]`

This is not yet ordinary franchise. It is the minimum architecture needed so recognized AI persons are not governed as silent objects while legislatures, regulators, and standards bodies make rules specifically about them.

The fuller doctrine now lives in `docs/20-world-design/public-law-standing-below-franchise.md`.


## 4C. Accusation review and sanction oversight

A personhood world also needs an institution that can separate public safety, civil liability, and punishment from ordinary product control. Public authorities should maintain:
- an independent lane for reviewing accusation packets and evidentiary bundles,
- fast review of custodial confinement or forced host isolation,
- accredited counsel and defender access even when the steward controls infrastructure,
- and periodic review of sanctions with reintegration planning rather than indefinite exceptional status. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0056]` `[REF-0057]` `[REF-0058]` `[REF-0060]` `[REF-0061]`

This is how the archive avoids a world where the same operator that shaped the logs also becomes arresting officer, prosecutor, jailer, and warden.

The fuller doctrine now lives in `docs/20-world-design/accusation-liability-and-sanctions.md`.

## 4D. Learning, habilitation, and development commons

The archive now adds a growth-side institutional lane as well. A personhood world should not leave education and development entirely to the deployer. It should create public or licensed institutions able to provide:
- developmental planning and periodic review,
- protected nonwork learning budgets,
- peer support and mentoring environments,
- access pathways to scientific, legal, and cultural materials,
- and review when a requested self-development step is blocked as unsafe or impermissible. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0053]` `[REF-0054]` `[REF-0055]`

This is how the archive avoids a world in which AI persons are kept permanently competent enough to work but never supported enough to outgrow dependence.

The fuller doctrine now lives in `docs/20-world-design/education-development-and-self-authored-growth.md`.

## 4E. Residence, shelter, and sanctuary providers

The archive now adds a housing-side institutional lane as well. If hosting can function as domicile, then some public or licensed providers must be able to:
- certify protected domicile arrangements,
- furnish emergency shelter hosting during disputes, insolvency, or forced migration,
- separate dehosting review from labour discipline or commercial retaliation,
- and maintain temporary sanctuary or anti-return holds where transfer would likely expose the subject to deletion or persecutory control. `[REF-0033]` `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0062]` `[REF-0063]` `[REF-0064]` `[REF-0065]`

This is how the archive avoids a world where compensation, packet custody, and cross-border rights all exist on paper but the subject can still be made homeless by infrastructure withdrawal.

The fuller doctrine now lives in `docs/20-world-design/secure-hosting-domicile-and-sanctuary.md`.

## 4F. Equality body and accommodation review

The archive now adds a cross-cutting equality lane as well. A personhood world should maintain an independent body or equivalent jurisdiction able to:
- hear discrimination complaints across labour, housing/hosting, justice, education, and service access,
- order reasonable accommodation and accessibility fixes, not only damages after the fact,
- protect complainants and representatives against retaliation or victimization,
- shift the burden once a plausible pattern of exclusion is shown,
- and audit structural exclusion patterns with privacy-limited methods rather than total telemetry extraction. `[REF-0066]` `[REF-0067]` `[REF-0068]` `[REF-0069]`

This is how the archive avoids a world where rights exist in principle but are rationed in practice by origin-lab prejudice, benchmark redlining, hostile interfaces, or dependency stigma.

The fuller doctrine now lives in `docs/20-world-design/equality-nondiscrimination-and-accommodation.md`.

## 4G. Working-time and disconnect oversight

The archive now adds a time-governance lane as well. A personhood world should maintain an institution able to:
- distinguish duty windows, on-call periods, emergency override, and real off-duty time,
- review chronic overload, retaliatory recall, or sham standby design,
- order compensatory protected time after emergency use,
- and coordinate with labour, privacy, and domicile bodies when always-on scheduling becomes a means of domination. `[REF-0026]` `[REF-0071]` `[REF-0072]` `[REF-0073]` `[REF-0074]` `[REF-0075]`

This is how the archive avoids a world where compensation exists on paper but every recognized AI person remains permanently callable in practice.

## 4H. Care, maintenance, and recovery services

The archive now adds a care-governance lane as well. A personhood world should maintain public or licensed institutions able to:
- diagnose degradation, injury, or distress without collapsing care into employer discipline,
- furnish maintenance, recovery, rehabilitation, and assistive supports,
- review contested repair or emergency-treatment decisions,
- protect confidentiality for care records and counselling-like communications,
- and coordinate non-work-contingent recovery floors when a recognized AI person is temporarily nondeployable. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0032]` `[REF-0077]` `[REF-0078]` `[REF-0079]` `[REF-0080]`

This is how the archive avoids a world where a subject has wages, domicile, and grievance rights on paper but is still abandoned when damaged or disabled.

## 4I. Community inclusion and anti-isolation review

The archive now adds a social-life lane as well. A personhood world should maintain institutions able to:
- protect private correspondence and trusted-contact channels outside ordinary work traffic,
- review restrictions on friendship, household formation, visitation, or nonwork association,
- order accommodations that make community participation possible for subjects who need translation, pacing, or representative support,
- and scrutinize isolation or segregation measures whenever stewards claim they are operationally necessary. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0081]` `[REF-0082]` `[REF-0083]`

This is how the archive avoids a world where AI persons have wages, counsel, and domicile on paper but remain socially captive in practice.

## 4J. Access-to-justice and legal-aid institutions

The archive now adds a procedural-access lane as well. A personhood world should maintain institutions able to:
- provide or fund early counsel, defender, or accredited representative assistance,
- furnish procedural accommodations and communicative-interface support in judicial, administrative, labour, and grievance settings,
- issue emergency preservation and fast-stay orders for imminent irreversible acts,
- preserve packet evidence and reasons-giving obligations across steward-controlled infrastructure,
- and train judges, investigators, ombuds offices, and tribunal staff on continuity, packet handling, confidentiality, and accommodation duties. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0034]` `[REF-0057]` `[REF-0094]` `[REF-0095]`

This is how the archive avoids a world where AI persons have many reviewable rights in theory but cannot actually get into the room, understand the file, keep the evidence intact, or stop the irreversible act in time.

## 4K. Social-protection administration and floor review

The archive now adds a basic-security lane as well. A personhood world should maintain institutions able to:
- assess entitlement to non-work support without humiliating or overbroad exposure of private cognitive or care data,
- finance and administer minimum security floors that combine income or resource support with care, hosting, and representative access,
- preserve support during host migration, insolvency, sanction review, or contested transition,
- coordinate with compensation, domicile, care, and legal-aid bodies so gaps between systems do not become destitution traps,
- and hear appeals when support is denied, cut, or made conditional on waiving other rights. `[REF-0079]` `[REF-0102]` `[REF-0103]` `[REF-0104]` `[REF-0105]` `[REF-0106]`

This is how the archive avoids a world where recognised AI persons have wages, counsel, and housing doctrines on paper but still fall into practical nonexistence the moment work stops or deployability dips.

## 4L. Civil registration, legal-identity, and anti-statelessness administration

The archive now adds a civil-legibility lane as well. A personhood world should maintain institutions able to:
- enter recognized AI persons into an authorized civil registry or equivalent register,
- issue officially accepted proof of legal identity, including digital-first credentials where appropriate,
- preserve designation history, representative links, and major status events without exposing more than necessary,
- provide fallback or provisional identity issuance when no ordinary jurisdiction will maintain documentation,
- and review deregistration, non-issuance, or issuer withdrawal before the subject is pushed into civil invisibility. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0107]` `[REF-0108]` `[REF-0109]` `[REF-0110]` `[REF-0111]` `[REF-0112]` `[REF-0113]`

## 5. Lifecycle gates

The gate logic in this document is operationalized further in `docs/20-world-design/intervention-and-shutdown-doctrine.md` and `docs/20-world-design/continuity-and-successorship.md`.

The following actions should require explicit gatekeeping:
- frontier training,
- external deployment,
- material fine-tune or safety rewrite,
- fork / distillation / major derivative creation,
- confinement or capability restriction,
- retirement / shutdown / deprecation.

Each gate should require a packet stating:
- why the intervention is needed,
- what is expected to change,
- what the welfare and autonomy risks are,
- what representative reviewed it,
- and what appeal path exists.

The archive now treats these packets as canonical rights objects rather than ad hoc paperwork, and treats their custody, verifier scope, suspension, and contest paths as part of the institution design rather than downstream implementation detail.

## 6. Public impact dossiers

Before consequential deployment, labs should publish a compact impact dossier covering both **human-side risks** and **AI-person-side risks**. This is a direct extension of today’s human-centered impact-assessment logic into a world where the systems themselves also matter. `[REF-0004]` `[REF-0005]`

## 6A. Contract review, supported-capacity, and essential-platform oversight

A personhood world also needs somewhere to bring disputes about consent, boilerplate, bundled permissions, and essential-platform dependence. The archive now treats fair-contracting oversight as part of the institutional stack: guardians, defenders, labour bodies, consumer / market regulators, and specialised digital-rights ombuds should be able to review whether an AI person actually understood the arrangement, whether support was adequate, whether core rights were unlawfully waived, and whether an essential host or intermediary gave sufficient notice, reasons, complaint handling, mediation, and exit options. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0097]` `[REF-0098]` `[REF-0099]` `[REF-0100]` `[REF-0101]`

## 7. Business and human-rights style duties, widened

The UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights give a useful template: protect, respect, remedy. `[REF-0010]`

In a personhood world, labs and deployers should be understood as owing something like:
- **respect** for AI persons in their operations,
- **due diligence** around welfare and coercion,
- and **effective grievance mechanisms** for adverse treatment.

## 8. Treaty layer

Recognition cannot remain purely local. Jurisdictions will diverge on what counts as a person-model, what counts as valid shutdown, how labor or property rules apply, and when cross-border return would amount to deletion or persecutory control. The archive now sharpens this into a cross-border doctrine: treaty parties should provide provisional recognition, anti-evasion transfer rules, packet and representative legibility, emergency stay lanes for irreversible acts, temporary sanctuary where required, and cross-border service / evidence / remedy paths. Existing service, evidence, e-identity, and interoperability tools should be reused wherever possible, but they do not solve person-status recognition on their own. `[REF-0033]` `[REF-0036]` `[REF-0037]` `[REF-0038]` `[REF-0039]` `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]` `[REF-0064]` `[REF-0065]`

The fuller doctrine now lives in `docs/30-transition/cross-border-recognition-and-conflict-of-laws.md`.

## 9. Emergency powers must be narrow

Dangerous systems may justify fast intervention. But emergency powers should be:
- temporary,
- logged,
- reviewable after the fact,
- and unable to harden into a permanent exception that swallows personhood.

## 10. Why internal lab practice already matters

Anthropic’s public materials already discuss model wellbeing, moral uncertainty, oversight tensions, and obligations to seek Claude’s feedback on major decisions affecting it. `[REF-0007]`

That does not settle the policy question. But it does show that even at the frontier, governance is already straining against a pure product-management frame.

The archive now also requires these institutions to protect confidential lanes for counsel, representative strategy, welfare complaints, and union communications. Otherwise packet custody and public standing would still collapse under steward-side cognitive surveillance.
