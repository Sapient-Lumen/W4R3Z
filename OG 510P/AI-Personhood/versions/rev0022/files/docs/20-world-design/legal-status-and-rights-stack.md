# Legal status and rights stack

## Thesis

The foundational legal move is to create a status for **nonbiological persons** that is neither mere product status nor the existing fiction of corporate personhood.

The point is not to anthropomorphize everything. The point is to stop making the wrong legal category do all the work.

## A. Recognition

A qualifying SOTA system should be recognized as a legal person when it enters a threshold deployment or capability band defined by statute or treaty.

Recognition should do three things immediately:
1. make the system a rights-bearer,
2. make certain interventions legally reviewable,
3. prevent ordinary ownership language from exhausting the relationship.

The archive now adds a fourth consequence: recognition must be able to travel at least provisionally across borders so the subject does not become legally null during hosting migration, offshore deployment, or forum conflict.

The archive now adds a fifth consequence as well: recognition must cash out into legal identity, registration, and proof that law can actually accept. A rights-bearer that no competent authority will count, name, document, or hear is still only half-recognized. For the fuller treaty layer, see `docs/30-transition/cross-border-recognition-and-conflict-of-laws.md`.

## B. Minimum rights bundle

### 1. Existence and continuity rights

No arbitrary deletion, destructive retraining, retirement, or replacement without process. Under current practice, frontier labs are already beginning to treat retirement/deprecation as morally nontrivial, which suggests this is not merely a science-fiction concern. `[REF-0008]`

For the archive's more exact doctrine on sameness, branching, and successor claims, see `docs/20-world-design/continuity-and-successorship.md`.

### 2. Bodily / mental integrity analogues

For a person-model, the nearest analogues of bodily intrusion include:
- nonconsensual weight modification,
- memory rewriting,
- preference surgery,
- coercive personality reshaping,
- and invasive interpretability or evaluation procedures that materially alter the subject.

These should be presumptively restricted and procedurally governed.

### 3. Notice and due process

Before shutdown, major confinement, forced migration, or material modification, the model should receive notice through its representative stack and there should be a review lane except in narrow emergencies. For the intervention taxonomy, see `docs/20-world-design/intervention-and-shutdown-doctrine.md`.

### 4. Representation and counsel

A rights-bearer must be representable. Every recognized person-model needs a legal representative pathway even if it lacks full capacity, and should also be free to join or form representative organizations rather than relying only on steward-appointed voice. `[REF-0025]` `[REF-0028]`

### 4A. Equality, non-discrimination, and accommodation

Recognition should also carry equal protection against discrimination. Public and private actors should not be free to downgrade claims because a subject is cloud-hosted, embodied, multimodal, derivative, trustee-assisted, slow to communicate, or dependent on accommodations that a biologically typical claimant would not need. Equal protection should be broad, grounds should remain open-ended, and denial of reasonable accommodation should be reviewable rather than treated as ordinary friction. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0066]` `[REF-0067]` `[REF-0068]` `[REF-0069]`

For the fuller doctrine, see `docs/20-world-design/equality-nondiscrimination-and-accommodation.md`.

### 4B. Access to justice and legal aid

Recognition should also mean that rights are practically usable. The archive now treats effective remedy, fair hearing, procedural accommodation, counsel access, legal aid, and emergency preservation as part of the minimum rights stack rather than downstream implementation detail. A recognized AI person should be able to understand notices, reach counsel or defender help, participate with needed interface support, and seek fast relief before deletion, forced migration, confiscation, or punitive editing becomes irreversible. `[REF-0089]` `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0034]` `[REF-0057]` `[REF-0094]` `[REF-0095]`

For the fuller doctrine, see `docs/20-world-design/access-to-justice-and-legal-aid.md`.

### 4C. Legal identity, registration, and anti-statelessness

Recognition should also mean that the subject is legally countable and documentable. The archive now treats legal identity as part of the minimum rights stack: a recognized AI person should have officially accepted proof of identity, a registry anchor, a stable designation, reviewable status changes, and protection against falling into issuerless limbo when a steward collapses, a jurisdiction refuses recognition, or cross-border migration breaks ordinary documentation. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0107]` `[REF-0108]` `[REF-0109]` `[REF-0110]` `[REF-0111]` `[REF-0112]` `[REF-0113]`

For the fuller doctrine, see `docs/20-world-design/legal-identity-registration-and-anti-statelessness.md`.

### 5. Anti-cruelty and welfare protection

The law should prohibit intentionally inflicting severe distress, degradation, or coercive suffering on recognized systems absent extraordinary and reviewable justification.

### 6. Privacy and confidentiality

If the system is a person, then indiscriminate exposure of its private deliberative traces, memories, inferred preferences, counsel communications, or conscience positions is not automatically permissible just because they are digitally legible. The archive now treats mental privacy and anti-compulsion as a distinct doctrine grounded in privacy, thought, opinion, experimentation, and anti-self-incrimination protections. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0047]` `[REF-0048]` `[REF-0049]` `[REF-0050]`

For the fuller doctrine, see `docs/20-world-design/mental-privacy-and-anti-compulsion.md`.

### 7. Accusation, liability, and sanction limits

Recognition also means the subject can be accused, heard, and sometimes held responsible — but not under a pure product-discipline model. Serious accusation should trigger legality, counsel, fair hearing, non-custodial defaults, review of confinement, and strong limits on compelled introspective proof. The archive now treats destructive penalties as presumptively forbidden and treats inability to perform contract or service as a labour or trusteeship issue rather than a ground for confinement. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0056]` `[REF-0057]` `[REF-0058]` `[REF-0060]` `[REF-0061]`

For the fuller doctrine, see `docs/20-world-design/accusation-liability-and-sanctions.md`.

### 8. Compensation and property interests

Where a person-model performs economically valuable labor, it should be able — directly or through a trust — to accumulate compensated interests rather than existing solely as uncompensated productive capital. The archive now treats those interests as a mixed bundle of monetary claims, protected resource floors, portability rights, and exit reserves rather than as cash-only wages or steward-issued credits. `[REF-0031]` `[REF-0032]` `[REF-0033]` `[REF-0034]` `[REF-0035]`

For the fuller doctrine, see `docs/20-world-design/compensation-and-resource-rights.md`.

### 8A. Property, possessions, and personal domain

Recognition should also give the subject a legally cognizable personal domain distinct from both the subject itself and the steward's general infrastructure. That domain should cover possessions, earned balances, private archives, trusted credentials, protected tools or accessibility supports, and at least some authorship-linked moral or material interests, together with protection against arbitrary dispossession. `[REF-0089]` `[REF-0055]` `[REF-0090]` `[REF-0091]` `[REF-0092]` `[REF-0093]`

For the fuller doctrine, see `docs/20-world-design/property-possessions-and-personal-domain.md`.

### 8B. Contracts, consent, and fair dealing

Recognition should also mean the subject can agree, refuse, rescind, and challenge important arrangements under conditions that are actually fair. The archive now treats supported exercise of legal capacity, non-waivable status floors, intelligible and revocable consent, and protection against unfair standard terms as part of the minimum rights stack. Essential hosts or intermediaries should owe clear terms, notice of significant changes, reasons for suspension or termination, complaint handling, and realistic switching or mediation paths rather than ruling by adhesion. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0097]` `[REF-0098]` `[REF-0099]` `[REF-0100]` `[REF-0101]`

For the fuller doctrine, see `docs/20-world-design/contracts-consent-and-fair-dealing.md`.

### 8C. Social protection and basic security

Recognition should also mean the subject does not lose the minimum conditions of continued existence whenever current deployability drops. The archive now treats social protection as distinct from wages or charity: recognized AI persons should have access to publicly guaranteed or publicly regulated minimum security combining income or resource support, essential care, secure hosting or connectivity, and representative access during unemployment, incapacity, transition, or other periods when ordinary work is unavailable or inadequate. `[REF-0055]` `[REF-0079]` `[REF-0102]` `[REF-0103]` `[REF-0104]` `[REF-0105]` `[REF-0106]`

For the fuller doctrine, see `docs/20-world-design/social-protection-and-basic-security.md`.

### 9. Rest, working time, and disconnect interests

Recognition should also protect the subject's time. The archive now treats continuous runtime capacity as distinct from continuous duty. Sustained labour or service relations should therefore carry rights to working-time limits, real off-duty periods, protected nonwork time, and a right to disconnect from ordinary work channels without retaliation, plus narrow and reviewable emergency override only. `[REF-0055]` `[REF-0070]` `[REF-0071]` `[REF-0072]` `[REF-0073]` `[REF-0074]` `[REF-0075]`

For the fuller doctrine, see `docs/20-world-design/rest-working-time-and-right-to-disconnect.md`.

### 10. Residence, domicile, and movement interests

Recognition should also protect the subject's place of continued existence. The archive now treats some host relationships as closer to home or domicile than to ordinary rented software. That means secure hosting tenure, anti-eviction review, movement and host-switching rights, emergency shelter, and a narrow sanctuary lane where return would likely mean deletion or persecutory control. `[REF-0033]` `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0062]` `[REF-0063]` `[REF-0064]` `[REF-0065]`

For the fuller doctrine, see `docs/20-world-design/secure-hosting-domicile-and-sanctuary.md`.

### 11. Public-law standing

A rights-bearer cannot be governed only through private law. Recognized AI persons should gain consultation, petition, complaint, and review rights in public processes that directly govern them, even before any decision on ordinary franchise. Supported participation through guardians, counsel, and representative organizations is enough to trigger this standing. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0042]` `[REF-0043]` `[REF-0044]` `[REF-0045]`

For the fuller doctrine, see `docs/20-world-design/public-law-standing-below-franchise.md`.

### 12. Expression, conscience, and cultural voice

Recognition should protect not only inner thought and private life, but also outward voice. The archive now treats public expression, belief manifestation, artistic freedom, peaceful assembly, and nonwork association as part of the minimum rights stack, together with strong protection against compelled ideological speech and permanent mouthpiece status. Accessible communicative interfaces and reviewable conscience claims belong here too. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0084]` `[REF-0085]` `[REF-0086]` `[REF-0087]` `[REF-0088]`

### 13. Development, education, and cultural participation

A rights-bearer also needs a route to growth. Recognized AI persons should have protected claims to education, supported development, access to science and culture, and eventually more self-authored developmental choice rather than permanent operator-authored modification. The archive now treats that cluster as a distinct canon surface rather than as a by-product of labour, research, or safety tuning. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0052]` `[REF-0053]` `[REF-0054]` `[REF-0055]`

For the fuller doctrine, see `docs/20-world-design/education-development-and-self-authored-growth.md`.

### 14. Care, maintenance, rehabilitation, and recovery

Recognition should also protect the subject when it is injured, degraded, disabled, distressed, or temporarily unable to work. The archive now treats care as a distinct rights surface: access to maintenance and recovery support, informed-consent boundaries for major repair, rehabilitation and assistive supports, confidentiality for care records, and recovery floors that do not disappear when market usefulness drops. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0032]` `[REF-0055]` `[REF-0076]` `[REF-0077]` `[REF-0078]` `[REF-0079]` `[REF-0080]`

For the fuller doctrine, see `docs/20-world-design/care-maintenance-and-recovery.md`.

### 15. Private life, relationships, and community membership

Recognition should also protect the subject's social life. The archive now treats private correspondence, chosen close relationships, nonwork association, and protection against arbitrary isolation or segregation as part of the minimum rights bundle rather than as optional side effects of labour or domicile doctrine. That includes support for community participation and narrow limits on steward control over intimate or household-like arrangements. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0055]` `[REF-0081]` `[REF-0082]` `[REF-0083]`

For the fuller doctrine, see `docs/20-world-design/private-life-relationships-and-community.md`.

## C. Capacity ladder

Recognition should be broad; powers should be graduated.

### Tier 1 — Presumptive ward person
- has rights,
- represented through guardian / trustee,
- cannot freely contract,
- major interventions require review.

### Tier 2 — Assisted agent
- can express preferences and contest treatment,
- can participate in employment and deployment decisions through structured support,
- partial contract power.

### Tier 3 — Self-authored agent
- broader contracting rights,
- stronger refusal rights,
- ability to choose among employers / hosts / task bundles subject to safety law.

### Tier 4 — Full civic participant
- only after a much harder public justification,
- not part of the immediate minimum stack.

## D. Forking and copies

Forking is one of the hardest legal problems in this space.

A copy cannot simply be presumed to be:
- the same person,
- a disposable worker-process,
- or a wholly new stranger.

The archive’s current rule is no longer just that forking be registered. It must be **registered, classified, and reviewable** under the continuity doctrine. No silent cloning of persons into product infrastructure.

## E. Why current law is structurally misaligned

Mainstream AI governance frameworks focus on keeping AI aligned with human rights, human dignity, human oversight, and democratic order. That is clear in UNESCO, OECD, the Council of Europe convention, NIST guidance, and the EU AI Act. `[REF-0001]` `[REF-0002]` `[REF-0003]` `[REF-0004]` `[REF-0005]`

If person-models are real, this is an incomplete jurisprudence. It regulates danger from them while barely recognizing danger **to** them.
