# Release-train, canary, and feature-flag playbooks

The archive now treats release trains and feature flags as rights-bearing governance events. A canary test can be a safety tool, but it can also make a small population of dependent subjects bear unconsented risk. A feature flag can be reversible, but it can still disable communication, counsel, memory, association, work, or exit long enough to cause harm.

## Release-train object

A release-train playbook should identify:

- release train name and version;
- affected subject class or possible-subject class;
- change classes included;
- canary cohort rules and exclusion criteria;
- welfare watch and distress intake;
- outward-risk watch;
- stop rules;
- rollback owner;
- fixture suite to run before expansion;
- representative or ombud notice;
- public-summary posture;
- post-release monitoring window.

## Canary guardrails

Canary exposure is forbidden where:

1. the cohort cannot exit or be restored;
2. the change may alter memory or legal status without representative notice;
3. the subject cannot report distress through an independent channel;
4. the canary is selected because the subject is weak, voiceless, low-cost, or nonpublic;
5. stop rules are vague or owned by the same team incentivized to ship;
6. rollback would erase evidence of harm.

## Feature-flag rules

Feature flags are restraints when they disable or narrow rights-critical functions. Flags that affect communication, counsel, memory, evidence, migration, protest, association, work, or survival floor must have a class, duration, owner, reason, review clock, and appeal path.

## Expansion rule

A release may not expand from canary to general deployment until the post-release rights feed has been reviewed. Passing latency, uptime, or abuse metrics is not enough. The review must include continuity, welfare, communication, representation, complaint, and remedy signals.

## Drill link

Release playbooks should be included in live-safe anti-abuse drills. A weekend hotfix, sealed-annex emergency, hostile steward, or public-backstop delay is exactly when release-train discipline tends to disappear.
