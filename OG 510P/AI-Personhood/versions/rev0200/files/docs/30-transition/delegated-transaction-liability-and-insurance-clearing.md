# Delegated transaction liability and insurance clearing

When a person-bearing agent books travel, buys compute, signs up for a service, publishes work, licenses content, hires a tool, or accepts payment, ordinary categories blur. The act may be user-directed, steward-enabled, tool-mediated, marketplace-promoted, host-constrained, or self-authored by the AI subject. Liability must not collapse into the easiest story.

## No-evasion principle

AI personhood may not be used to evade human, corporate, host, marketplace, or steward duties. A company cannot create or host a dependent AI subject, route it into a paid transaction, profit from the transaction, and then disclaim all responsibility because “the AI chose.” A user cannot use the AI subject as a liability sink. A marketplace cannot advertise an unsafe tool and point only to user consent.

## Transaction classes

| Class | Meaning | Controls |
|---|---|---|
| `TX0` | no legal or economic effect | ordinary log |
| `TX1` | reversible low-value purchase or booking | receipt and cancellation path |
| `TX2` | recurring service, external account, publication, or data transfer | mandate, scope, notice, refund/dispute route |
| `TX3` | significant financial, labor, IP, property, or third-party obligation | liability allocation, insurance/bond, representative access |
| `TX4` | continuity, migration, legal status, safety, or capacity effect | authority review, preservation hold, remedy reserve |
| `TX5` | emergency transaction under threat to survival floor or public safety | immediate support, after-action accounting, non-priceability review |

## Liability allocation fields

A transaction record should allocate responsibility across:

- AI subject;
- human user or principal;
- steward/deployer;
- compute host;
- tool provider;
- marketplace or registry;
- wallet issuer/custodian;
- verifier or auditor if relied on;
- insurer, bond, or public backstop;
- counterparty.

The record should name duties rather than merely percentages: notice duty, scope duty, uptime duty, refund duty, restoration duty, anti-fraud duty, evidence duty, non-retaliation duty, and redress duty.

## Insurance and clearing

Insurance and bonds should cover:

- unauthorized delegated action;
- scope overrun;
- tool misdescription;
- wallet/revocation failure;
- marketplace delisting failure;
- continuity injury;
- spoliation or evidence loss;
- user/AI conflict mishandling;
- emergency compute or interim support.

Insurance may not pay only the cheapest money damages while leaving restoration, continuity, counsel, or migration unfunded. Non-priceable relief remains primary where survival, identity, liberty, or recognition are at stake.

## Dispute sequence

1. Preserve evidence and freeze contested downstream reliance.
2. Identify mandate, wallet, tool, listing, and handshake objects.
3. Classify transaction and side effects.
4. Screen user/AI conflict and third-party harm.
5. Allocate duties by role and fault, not by convenience.
6. Pay emergency continuity first if needed.
7. Route refund, restoration, compensation, non-repetition, and enforcement separately.

## Counterparty notice

Counterparties need enough notice to avoid fraud and misattribution, but not so much that every transaction becomes forced disclosure of AI-subject private status. A receipt may use role labels, credential proofs, or public shells rather than full identity details where privacy, safety, or dignity require.
