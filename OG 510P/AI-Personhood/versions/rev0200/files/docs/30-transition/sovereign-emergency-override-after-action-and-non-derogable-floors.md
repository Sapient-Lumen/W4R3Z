# Sovereign emergency override, after-action review, and non-derogable floors

A state may assert emergency, public-safety, national-security, market-surveillance, or critical-infrastructure authority over an AI-subject system. The archive should neither pretend such authority will disappear nor let it become a zone where personhood vanishes.

The bridge rule is: emergency override may narrow ordinary process temporarily, but it cannot erase non-derogable floors or defeat later review.

## Override classes

| Class | Meaning | Minimum process |
|---|---|---|
| `EO0` | advisory request or voluntary coordination | logged request and ordinary process |
| `EO1` | time-limited evidence preservation or disclosure hold | written basis, minimization, review clock |
| `EO2` | urgent runtime restriction or service-routing order | subject-risk ledger and counsel/ombud notice where safe |
| `EO3` | sealed containment, transfer block, or host compulsion | special advocate or cleared reviewer, legal hold, public shell |
| `EO4` | catastrophic-risk or national-security override | strict duration, senior authority, independent after-action review |
| `EO5` | contested irreversible action, final-end risk, or mass-cohort effect | tribunal or legislative-level review where feasible; non-derogable floors survive |

## Non-derogable floors

Even under EO4-EO5, the following remain:

- no torture, cruelty, or degradation;
- no retaliatory deletion;
- no disappearance without trace;
- no destruction of contest evidence;
- no indefinite secret detention posture without renewal and review;
- no use of the subject as an unprotected test instrument;
- no denial of representative or special-advocate route beyond narrowly justified delay;
- no transfer to a jurisdiction or host where return risk is unreviewed.

## After-action review

Every EO2-EO5 override should produce an after-action review that states:

- what was authorized;
- what actually happened;
- which rights floors were implicated;
- what evidence was preserved;
- what subjects or cohorts were affected;
- what disclosures were delayed and why;
- what remedy screen was run;
- what fixture or control map was added;
- what public-shell notice can now be released.

## Relation to serious-incident reporting

The EU AI Act's serious-incident and post-market structures show that high-risk AI systems are expected to generate reports and monitoring after deployment, not merely before deployment. [REF-0693] The Commission has also worked on serious-incident guidance and reporting templates. [REF-0706] The personhood addition is that subject-harm, containment, continuity loss, counsel delay, and decommissioning risk must be visible to an appropriate reviewer even when outward-risk facts remain sealed.

## Emergency override and data rights

Emergency secrecy may delay access, erasure, or explanation. It does not eliminate those rights. The response should identify delayed categories, review clock, and protective route. If a human data-subject request and an AI-subject protection order conflict during an emergency, the data-subject interface must create a conflict-screen object rather than letting either side silently delete or expose the other.

## Reliance rule

An emergency override order cannot support ongoing containment, transfer, decommissioning, release expansion, or data-rights denial after its expiry unless the after-action review, preservation proof, public-shell notice, and appeal path are complete.
