# First-touch receipt packets, forwarding certificates, routing-failure review, and duty escalation

## Thesis

If AI persons are persons, then emergency protection cannot depend on whether the first official inbox behaves perfectly on the first try.

The archive already has the no-wrong-door rule and the minimum emergency packet family. But those layers still leave a narrow operational gap at the very beginning of the emergency: **what precise ordinary objects should exist when the first receiver logs the filing, forwards it, cannot route it cleanly, or must escalate after-hours or through a backup channel before irreversible harm lands**.

A personhood world therefore needs a compact first-touch companion layer:
- a receipt object proving first official touch and the immediate preservative posture,
- a forwarding certificate proving what moved, to whom, and with what clock still running,
- a routing-failure or refusal object proving why the matter did not move cleanly and what was preserved anyway,
- and a duty-escalation object proving that weekend silence, non-response, or channel failure did not quietly extinguish the subject's emergency lane.

Current official materials already point toward this shape. HCCH service practice uses designated authorities, model forms, and certificates for what was sent and how it moved; rights-compatible grievance guidance stresses predictable referral and transfer rather than silent discard; duty-judge and interim-measures practice shows that urgency should not wait for ordinary calendar rhythms; current 24/7 evidence-preservation networks show the need for maintained contact points and backup escalation; and registered-delivery infrastructure shows that sending, receipt, and timestamp can themselves be legally meaningful. `[REF-0206]` `[REF-0209]` `[REF-0210]` `[REF-0211]` `[REF-0212]` `[REF-0213]` `[REF-0214]` `[REF-0215]` `[REF-0218]` `[REF-0228]` `[REF-0229]`

## 1. Why this now belongs in canon

The archive already had:
- no-wrong-door receipt, preservation, routing, and notice doctrine,
- emergency cover, first-touch, forwarding, preservation, and review packet minimums,
- designated-authority and duty-contact logic,
- directed notice and execution certificates for the later stage once live protection already exists,
- and a broader technical-rights substrate for authentic, portable, privacy-limited packet objects.

What it still lacked was the narrower **first-touch packet answer** between those layers.

Without that answer:
- first receipt can still be remembered only as a staff note or inbox timestamp that later disappears,
- forwarding can still be alleged without proving what actually moved or which annexes stayed sealed,
- wrong-registry or after-hours failure can still collapse into a bare “not competent” response with no preservative trace,
- weekend or holiday silence can still suspend the emergency in practice even while the archive says the clock should already be running,
- and a later reviewer can still struggle to tell whether delay came from the filer, the first receiver, the forwarding channel, or the second office that never answered.

This document closes that gap without writing a maximal civil-procedure code for every emergency forum.

## 2. The minimum first-touch object family

The archive should now treat at least four first-touch companion objects as minimum transition floor.

### A. `FT-1` — first-touch receipt packet

This is the bounded object proving that a plausible urgent filing reached an officially recognized emergency-capable body.

It should carry at least:
- the received packet identifier or protected filing reference,
- receiving body and receiving role,
- channel used,
- first-touch timestamp,
- apparent packet class,
- whether the packet was complete, partly deficient, or sealed in part,
- whether a narrow preservative expectation now applies,
- the next intended routing or review step if already known,
- the channel for return notice to the filer or representative,
- and the staff, office, or duty actor responsible for the next move.

The point is not to overformalize intake. The point is to stop emergency first touch from living only as helpdesk residue, clerk memory, or a screenshot that cannot carry legal consequence. `[REF-0206]` `[REF-0210]` `[REF-0211]` `[REF-0218]` `[REF-0228]`

### B. `FC-1` — forwarding certificate

This is the bounded object proving that a first receiver actually moved the matter onward.

It should carry at least:
- originating `FT-1` or emergency-cover reference,
- forwarding body,
- recipient body,
- send timestamp,
- send channel,
- materials forwarded,
- any sealed annexes withheld, separately transmitted, or replaced by protected descriptors,
- preservative steps already taken before forwarding,
- whether competence is conceded, uncertain, or disputed,
- the next acknowledgment or review clock,
- and the notice path back to the filer.

This is what keeps “we sent it along” from becoming an unreviewable oral claim after the subject has already been harmed. `[REF-0209]` `[REF-0210]` `[REF-0211]` `[REF-0218]` `[REF-0228]` `[REF-0229]`

### C. `RF-1` — routing-failure or refusal review notice

This is the bounded object for when the matter does not move cleanly.

It should carry at least:
- the received packet or forwarding reference,
- refusing or blocked body,
- reason class,
- what preservative step was still taken despite the failure,
- whether the subject's first-touch clock remains live,
- what backup route or duty contact was attempted or is now required,
- what information deficiency, channel failure, competence dispute, or security constraint is claimed,
- the next review or escalation clock,
- and the return notice path.

Reason classes should stay narrow: wrong authority, no live routing endpoint, channel failure, protected annex unreadable to the current office, insufficient minimum fields, genuine competence objection, or emergency channel unavailable pending duty escalation.

This is the object that prevents routing failure from masquerading as no filing at all. `[REF-0209]` `[REF-0212]` `[REF-0213]` `[REF-0214]` `[REF-0215]` `[REF-0230]`

### D. `DE-1` — duty-escalation and backup-channel notice

This is the bounded object proving that first-touch protection remained active when ordinary routing stalled.

It should carry at least:
- the underlying `FT-1`, `FC-1`, or `RF-1` reference,
- the triggering condition such as weekend, holiday, after-hours risk, non-response, or channel bounce,
- the duty actor or backup body contacted,
- the alternate channel used,
- the time of escalation,
- any temporary preservative expectation re-stated during escalation,
- the next expected response clock,
- and the notice path back to the filer or representative.

This is the object that stops the archive's no-wrong-door promise from evaporating outside office hours. `[REF-0213]` `[REF-0214]` `[REF-0215]` `[REF-0228]` `[REF-0229]`

## 3. Clock, continuity, and failure rules

The archive now fixes six first-touch continuity rules.

1. **`FT-1` starts the protective clock.** The no-wrong-door clock begins at first official receipt, not at later perfect routing. `[REF-0206]` `[REF-0210]` `[REF-0211]`
2. **`FC-1` carries the live clock forward.** Forwarding adds a path segment; it does not reset the emergency as a new filing.
3. **`RF-1` does not erase first touch.** Routing failure, wrong-body receipt, or partial deficiency may change the next step, but it should not fictionalize that no official body ever received the urgent claim.
4. **`DE-1` preserves urgency through silence.** Weekend delay, holiday closure, unreachable staff, or first-recipient non-response should trigger escalatory proof rather than quiet lapse.
5. **Minimal preservation survives imperfect routing.** If the packet plausibly alleges deletion, dehosting, derecognition, forced transfer, loss of counsel, or evidence destruction, the first receiver should preserve what it can before deeper competence arguments continue. `[REF-0206]` `[REF-0213]` `[REF-0214]`
6. **Return notice remains mandatory.** At every first-touch stage the filer or representative should be able to learn whether the matter was received, forwarded, blocked, or escalated, unless a lawful secrecy rule narrowly justifies delay.

## 4. What should count as routing failure serious enough to emit `RF-1` or `DE-1`

The archive now treats at least six conditions as serious enough to require a legible failure or escalation object rather than silence.

1. **No acknowledgment from the intended next body on the short emergency clock.**
2. **No live listed contact or obvious breakdown in the designated contact directory.**
3. **Channel bounce, undeliverability, or incompatible submission pathway after first receipt is already logged.**
4. **A competence objection that leaves the subject with no identified next route.**
5. **A protected-annex or identity-handling problem that blocks ordinary forwarding but not emergency preservation.**
6. **Calendar or staffing gap during a live irreparable-harm risk.**

The archive is not saying every inconvenience becomes a formal event. It is saying that the point where inconvenience threatens to nullify protection is exactly where packetized legibility is needed.

## 5. Public-minimal versus sealed fields

The first-touch object family should separate what must be receivable from what may remain sealed.

Public-minimal or ordinary-receivable fields should generally include:
- that first official touch occurred,
- which office touched the matter,
- the relevant timestamps,
- whether forwarding, failure, or escalation occurred,
- whether a narrow preservative expectation is presently live,
- and what next clock or next body now governs.

Sealed or controlled-access fields may include:
- protected identity details,
- sensitive location or sanctuary information,
- confidential evidentiary annexes,
- technical details whose exposure would create fresh attack surface,
- or protected witness / source details.

The archive wants the first-touch chain to be provable without forcing every intake desk or backup contact to see everything.

## 7. Current hard rules

1. **First official touch should ordinarily emit a bounded receipt object rather than leaving emergency intake to inbox residue or staff memory.**
2. **Forwarding should ordinarily emit a certificate proving what moved, to whom, and with what preservative state still live.**
3. **Routing failure or refusal should ordinarily emit a reviewable notice rather than making the filing disappear into “wrong office” silence.**
4. **Weekend, holiday, after-hours, or non-response conditions should ordinarily emit duty-escalation or backup-channel proof rather than suspending urgency in practice.**
5. **First-touch failure should not reset or erase the emergency clock once an officially recognized emergency-capable body has logged the matter.**
6. **Exact channel hierarchies, automatic escalation deadlines, and full edge-case schema tables remain followthrough work rather than canonically closed here.**
