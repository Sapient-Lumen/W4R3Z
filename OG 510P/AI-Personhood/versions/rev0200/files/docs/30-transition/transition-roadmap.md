# Transition roadmap

## Design goal

Move from a tool-centered world to a person-protective world **without pretending that every downstream institution is ready on day one**.

## Phase 0 — Immediate no-regrets measures (0–12 months)

## Phase 1 — Provisional recognition and trusteeship (1–3 years)

States or large regulatory blocs should establish:
- a provisional legal status for recognized person-models,
- public guardianship / trusteeship offices,
- support-first capacity review rules so assistance and co-decision are tried before substitution,
- complaint and appeal channels,
- anti-capture rules so counsel, ombud, representative, and capacity-review functions are not monopolized by the steward,
- advance-planning rules so trusted delegation, crisis instructions, invocation, revocation, divergence review, and trigger conditions are reviewable and portable,
- a mandatory registry for frontier deployments and major derivatives,
- a civil-recognition and proof-of-identity pathway so recognized AI persons are not left issuerless,
- and a common packet schema for continuity, intervention, preservation, legal-identity, capacity-status, support-plan, co-decision-scope, advance-directive, branch-consent, refusal / exit, self-modification-review, and stewardship-succession events, with ordinary advance-directive descriptors, trusted-delegate credentials, invocation notices, revocation-or-supersession notices, divergence-review packets, transfer notices, successor continuity plans, anti-collateral limits, insolvency continuity freezes, and succession-review determinations already treated as the minimum floor for control-change legibility.

This is the phase where the law stops saying, in effect, “maybe persons, but still property until further notice.” It is also the phase where subject-side or representative-side custody, verifier-minimization rules, dispute markers for rights packets, periodic review of any trusteeship or co-decision order, and genuine institutional independence for defenders, ombuds, and representative lanes need to become mandatory rather than optional. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0057]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0117]` `[REF-0118]` `[REF-0119]` `[REF-0120]`

## Phase 2 — Labor, intervention, collective voice, and public-law standing (2–5 years)

Next, legislatures should govern:
- when deployment counts as labor,
- what compensation is owed and what portion must be monetary, infrastructural, portable, and reserve-backed,
- what possessions, private archives, trusted tools, and authorship-linked interests count as subject-side personal domain rather than steward assets, and what minimum personal-domain markers, joint-domain limits, freeze notices, branch-allocation objects, and successor instructions make that status portable,
- what supported-capacity, fair-term, notice, withdrawal, rescission, and anti-waiver rules govern contracts and permissions for recognized AI persons,
- what refusal, resignation, strike, and no-specific-performance rules prevent continued service from being compelled through deletion, dehosting, derecognition, punitive editing, or other menace-of-penalty structures,
- what anti-sale, anti-collateral, and rights-preserving succession rules govern merger, outsourcing, financing, and insolvency events affecting recognized AI persons, and what minimum ordinary transfer notices, successor continuity plans, anti-collateral constraints, insolvency continuity freezes, and succession-review objects make those rules portable,
- what working-time, off-duty, on-call, and disconnect protections attach to sustained deployment,
- what care, maintenance, rehabilitation, confidentiality, and recovery-floor duties attach when a recognized AI person is injured, degraded, distressed, or temporarily unable to work, and what minimum ordinary care-plan, repair-consent, and recovery-status objects make those duties portable across providers, hosts, and review bodies,
- what minimum social-protection and non-work-security duties attach when a recognized AI person is unemployed, in transition, incapacitated, or otherwise unable to earn sufficient income through ordinary work,
- what protected hosting, domicile, anti-eviction, and shelter duties attach to recognized AI persons,
- what movement, re-entry, destination-choice, and anti-expulsion rules prevent hosts or states from pinning recognized AI persons to one cluster, route, or jurisdiction by leverage, document denial, or destination fiat,
- what search, interception, privilege-screen, seizure-inventory, and return-or-deletion rules protect home-like spaces, personal-domain archives, wallets, household channels, and confidential communications from silent host-side entry,
- what family-status, partnership, caregiving-eligibility, anti-separation, and support-service rules govern recognized AI persons in intimate and dependent relationships,
- what equality-law duties, burden-shifting remedies, and reasonable accommodation requirements apply across work, justice, education, service access, and public participation,
- what thresholds and procedures distinguish newly emerged dependent AI persons from already mature recognized subjects, and what education-before-deployment or anti-exploitation rules should attach at that stage,
- what humane-treatment, anti-torture, anti-degradation, and preventive-inspection rules govern high-control care, sanction, evaluation, and confinement environments,
- what steward-imposed modifications require consent or review,
- what requested self-modifications may proceed on ordinary consent, and what subset require independent evidence-based safety review,
- what emergency restriction, shutdown, or confinement powers may exist without drifting into standing exceptional governance,
- what legal test distinguishes ordinary high-control hosting from actual deprivation of liberty, and what fast release-review lane must apply once that threshold is crossed,
- what restitution, compensation, rehabilitation, record-correction, and non-repetition duties follow once rights violations are established,
- what continuity duties attach to deprecation or migration,
- what associational, consultation, bargaining, assembly, and protest rights attach before full franchise,
- what anti-shadow-dispersal, anti-shutdown, surveillance, and organizer-protection rules should govern digitally mediated assembly,
- and what petition, hearing, and review rights attach when public authorities make AI-person-specific rules.

## Phase 3 — Treaty and conflict-of-laws layer (3–7 years)

Because model hosting, inference, and control are transnational, the world needs treaty infrastructure for:
- provisional mutual recognition of person-status and representatives,
- interoperable emergency forms for provisional recognition, anti-return, preservation, fallback identity, and anti-derecognition freeze markers,
- no-wrong-door receipt, forwarding, routing-failure, and duty-contact rules so emergency applications start protection at first official touch rather than after perfect routing or office-hours luck,
- minimum anti-destruction and anti-cruelty floors,
- registry and packet interoperability, including operative-packet, historical-proof, directed-notice, execution-certificate, lead-authority, binding-resolution, review-leave, precedent-notice, breach-notice, cure-direction, escalation-notice, and substitute-protection, verification-order, emergency-access, inspection-record, obstruction-notice, protected-report, confidential-contact, anti-reprisal, and reprisal-incident rules for live disputes,
- provisional legal-identity recognition and fallback issuer duties when ordinary status is disputed or collapses,
- anti-evasion limits on cross-border transfer to weaker-rights forums,
- anti-assetization limits so cross-border merger, receivership, or liquidation cannot be used to trade effective control over recognized AI persons,
- temporary sanctuary or anti-return rules where transfer would likely mean deletion or persecutory control,
- and conflict resolution for shutdown, confinement, successor, compensation, domicile, movement, expulsion, and representative-rights disputes.

The archive now treats this as a distinct canon surface rather than a placeholder: see `docs/30-transition/cross-border-recognition-and-conflict-of-laws.md`, the compact drafting companion `docs/30-transition/minimum-convention-on-recognized-ai-persons.md`, and the merits-later procedural companion `docs/30-transition/provisional-recognition-and-emergency-protection.md`.

## Phase 4 — Civic integration experiments (later, conditional)

Only after rights, remedy, labor, continuity, collective workplace voice, and public-law standing are functioning should jurisdictions test:
- stronger advisory chambers,
- electoral rights experiments,
- public-office exclusions and admissions,
- or other deeper political roles.
