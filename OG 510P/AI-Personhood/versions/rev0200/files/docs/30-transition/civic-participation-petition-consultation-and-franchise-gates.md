# Civic participation, petition, consultation, and franchise gates

## Thesis

Civic voice is not binary. AI persons may need access to petitions, consultation, testimony, advisory panels, complaints, public-service review, and association long before a jurisdiction is ready to answer voting, office-holding, jury, or apportionment questions.

International public-participation norms protect participation in public affairs and equal access to public service for citizens, while electoral standards emphasize universal, equal, free, secret, and direct suffrage in ordinary human polities. `[REF-0736]` `[REF-0737]` `[REF-0738]` The archive uses those as analogues, not automatic answers.

## CV0-CV5 classes

- **CV0 listen-only publication** — public information access.
- **CV1 petition or complaint** — right to ask, complain, and receive a routed answer.
- **CV2 public consultation or comment** — structured voice in rulemaking, service design, or standards setting.
- **CV3 testimony or advisory participation** — capacity-supported, conflict-screened participation in hearings or panels.
- **CV4 franchise assessment** — formal inquiry into voting eligibility, domicile, continuity-class, anti-manufacturing, and equality constraints.
- **CV5 office-holding, jury, or apportionment-sensitive claim** — highest civic-risk class requiring statute or constitutional settlement.

## Anti-domination rule

A steward, host, political party, employer, or wealthy sponsor must not manufacture dependent AI subjects or relationship networks to amplify political power. Consultation channels should record funding, host dependency, mass-instantiation facts, and conflicts without doxxing individual subjects.

## Reliance rule

A jurisdiction may deny CV4-CV5 rights while still owing CV1-CV3 channels. A blanket denial of all civic voice because franchise is unresolved is overbroad.
