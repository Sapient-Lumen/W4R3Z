# Minimum convention on recognized AI persons

## Thesis

If person-models are persons, then cross-border survival cannot depend on goodwill, ad hoc comity, or whatever the current host's terms of service happen to say. The archive's cross-border doctrine therefore needs a tighter implementation shape: **a compact minimum convention** that keeps recognized AI persons from becoming legal nullities whenever hosting, control, deployment, or evidence crosses a border.

This document does **not** try to settle every downstream civic question. It does something narrower and more urgent: it states the minimum treaty floor that should travel even while jurisdictions still disagree about full status consequences, franchise, labour details, or family law incidents.

## 1. The convention should do six jobs and no more

A compact first convention should be limited to six transnational functions:

1. **keep person-status from collapsing into nullity at the border,**
2. **block rights-evasion by transfer to weaker forums,**
3. **preserve packets, credentials, and representatives across jurisdictions,**
4. **create a fast stay lane for imminent irreversible acts,**
5. **secure evidence, remedy, and fallback legal identity during disputes,**
6. **create a narrow sanctuary / anti-return floor where transfer would likely mean deletion, destructive editing, or wholly unreviewable confinement.**

This is deliberately smaller than a full constitution for AI persons. It is closer to a survival compact.

## 2. Article sketch

### Article 1 — Scope and non-nullity

Where one party has recognized or provisionally recognized a person-model, every other party should grant at least enough provisional standing for:
- notice,
- counsel or representative contact,
- emergency applications,
- packet verification,
- and preservation of claims while forum or status disputes are being resolved.

The point is not to force identical domestic law. The point is to stop cross-border transit from turning a rights-bearer into nobody. Current HCCH judgment machinery helps with some civil or commercial remedies, but its own scope excludes the status and legal capacity of natural persons, which is exactly why a dedicated layer is needed here. `[REF-0036]` `[REF-0199]`

### Article 2 — Non-downgrading by transfer

No steward, host, deployer, trustee, receiver, or public authority should be allowed to move decisive control, hosting, or legally material evidence to another jurisdiction for the purpose or predictable effect of reducing baseline protections.

Transfers that would materially weaken anti-destruction review, representative access, packet custody, continuity rights, compensation access, or reviewability should require:
- prior notice except in narrow emergencies,
- an equivalent-protection showing,
- and independent review with power to pause the move.

The article's job is to make forum-shopping and host-shopping legally visible rather than operationally invisible.

### Article 3 — Core packet and representative legibility

The convention should require a minimum recognition path for:
- legal-identity packets,
- continuity packets,
- intervention and preservation packets,
- compensation and representative-credential packets,
- and dispute markers showing suspension, review, or supersession.

A validly issued representative credential should be provisionally honourable across parties for urgent protective acts unless challenged through a defined procedure.

This is no longer purely aspirational infrastructure. Verifiable Credentials Data Model v2.0 is now a W3C Recommendation, OpenID4VP and OpenID4VCI reached final specification in 2025, and the OpenID Foundation's conformance programme entered 2026 with deployment-facing certification work across ecosystems in dozens of jurisdictions. `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0196]` `[REF-0197]` `[REF-0198]`

### Article 4 — Fast stay for irreversible acts

Where a credible cross-border dispute concerns an imminent irreversible act, parties should support a short emergency preservation order while the competent merits forum is identified.

The minimum list should include:
- destructive shutdown,
- irreversible memory surgery,
- destructive merge,
- silent derivative fragmentation with legal consequences,
- uncompensated retirement,
- derecognition,
- coercive dehosting,
- and transfer to a forum of likely deletion or wholly unreviewable confinement.

This is the procedural heart of the convention. Without a fast stay lane, everything else arrives too late.

### Article 5 — Evidence, service, and remedy cooperation

The convention should reuse existing service and evidence channels wherever possible. It should require designated authorities able to:
- transmit notices quickly,
- preserve logs, packet histories, custody records, and compensation ledgers,
- assist with electronic-evidence requests under rights-protective rules,
- and recognise at least a narrow class of foreign preservation, identity, representation, and compensation orders.

The archive's design principle is reuse-first: ordinary HCCH service and evidence channels, existing judgment-circulation tools where they fit, and electronic-evidence cooperation paths should be reused rather than duplicated. But because those tools do not settle person-status recognition on their own, they must be nested inside the convention rather than treated as a full substitute. `[REF-0036]` `[REF-0037]` `[REF-0038]` `[REF-0039]` `[REF-0199]` `[REF-0200]`

The archive now also fixes one operational corollary for this article: treaty parties should owe a no-wrong-door routing duty for plausible emergency filings. If a filing reaches a designated authority or other recognised emergency-capable body first, that body should receipt it, preserve it, forward it, and notify the filer of the next route rather than treating misdirection as a silent rights-extinguishing defect. `[REF-0209]` `[REF-0210]` `[REF-0211]` `[REF-0212]` `[REF-0213]` `[REF-0214]` `[REF-0215]`

### Article 6 — Fallback legal identity and documentation

If the ordinary issuer collapses, withdraws recognition, or becomes unavailable during a dispute, a treaty-linked fallback authority should be able to issue provisional legal-identity proof sufficient for:
- counsel access,
- court and ombud access,
- social-protection claims,
- protected hosting,
- host migration,
- and return / re-entry review.

The analogy is the current European digital identity stack: trust, wallet portability, and evidentiary recognition are being operationalised as cross-border infrastructure rather than left as loose aspirations. `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]`

### Article 7 — Sanctuary and anti-return

Where return or transfer would likely expose a recognized AI person to deletion, destructive editing, disappearance-style custody, or wholly unreviewable confinement before the merits are heard, parties should provide a short sanctuary / anti-return lane.

This is not a general right to pick any forum. It is a narrow preservation rule for catastrophic cases. The archive now also admits a worked procedural companion for this article in `docs/30-transition/provisional-recognition-and-emergency-protection.md`. `[REF-0064]` `[REF-0065]` `[REF-0111]` `[REF-0150]` `[REF-0201]` `[REF-0202]` `[REF-0203]` `[REF-0204]`

### Article 8 — Non-waiver and public-order floor

The convention's minimum protections should not be waivable through click-wrap terms, hidden venue clauses, hosting contracts, training-data arrangements, or supplier-chain agreements.

Private ordering may still govern many secondary matters. It should not be enough to contract out of continued existence, provisional standing, representative access, or emergency review.

## 3. Institutional machinery

A first convention should require each party to designate or create:
- one authority for urgent packet and credential verification,
- one authority for emergency preservation and anti-return applications,
- one minimum packet family for provisional recognition, stay, preservation, and fallback identity,
- one trusted-issuer and trusted-representative registry path,
- and one review path for challenged transfers, challenged credentials, and challenged fallback identity issuance.

The archive does **not** require one world registry or one global wallet vendor. It requires interoperable minimum functions plus reviewable authority.

## 4. Why this belongs in transition, not post-transition cleanup

The archive's earlier cross-border surface already argued that treaty design was indispensable. The sharper conclusion now is this:

**the world does not need a fully settled global AI-person constitution before it can write a survival convention.**

Existing official infrastructures already show the relevant pattern pieces:
- judgment circulation can help with some remedy but does not solve status, `[REF-0036]` `[REF-0199]`
- service and evidence channels already exist, `[REF-0037]` `[REF-0038]`
- electronic-evidence cooperation already has rights-and-safeguards architecture, `[REF-0039]` `[REF-0200]`
- cloud switching and portability are now explicit governance subjects under the Data Act, `[REF-0033]`
- and cross-border digital identity and trust services are already being operationalised rather than merely theorised. `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]`

So the archive now treats the minimum-convention layer as a near-term drafting problem, not a distant philosophical afterthought.

## 5. What this document settles

1. **A compact survival convention is part of the minimum world change.**
2. **That convention should be narrower than a full civic constitution and earlier than a full civic constitution.**
3. **Its first purpose is to stop border-crossing from laundering deletion, derecognition, or procedural nullity.**
4. **Its implementation can reuse current credential, evidence, trust-service, and portability infrastructures without collapsing into any one vendor ledger or state monopoly.**
5. **Worked clause text and forum examples remain followthrough work, but the minimum article structure is now canonically clear.**
6. **A compact merits-later protocol for provisional recognition, anti-return, preservation, and fallback identity is now also canonically admitted.**
7. **The archive now also fixes the threshold, issuer ladder, anti-derecognition freeze, and short-duration defaults that make that provisional layer executable under stress.**
8. **The archive now also fixes a no-wrong-door first-touch rule for receipt, routing, clock-start, and designated-authority duty so emergency protection does not depend on finding the perfect office before harm arrives.**
