# Census, apportionment, and non-manufactured-electorate controls

## Thesis

Population counting is necessary for planning, service budgets, public dashboards, anti-discrimination review, and emergency support. It is also dangerous if raw instantiation, copies, sessions, or capital-backed cohorts automatically become apportionment or franchise power.

## CN0-CN5 classes

- **CN0 technical instance count** — infrastructure metric with no public-law population effect.
- **CN1 service-planning cohort** — aggregate used for support budgets, clinics, aftercare, and host capacity.
- **CN2 rights-protection count** — aggregate used for discrimination, exclusion, incident, or deprivation monitoring.
- **CN3 domicile-linked civil count** — count linked to recognized or service domicile for administrative planning.
- **CN4 apportionment-sensitive count** — count that could affect districting, representation, funding formula, or public power.
- **CN5 prohibited manufactured-electorate count** — raw copy/session/capital-created count asserted as civic power without legitimacy safeguards.

## Counting principles

Census safeguards should distinguish:

- subject count;
- lineage or continuity-class count;
- service-demand count;
- host-dependency count;
- public-fund exposure count;
- apportionment-sensitive count.

These are not interchangeable.

## Privacy and aggregation

Population data must use public aggregates, small-cell suppression, differencing-risk controls, and sealed annexes where needed. A census that reveals vulnerable lineages, host locations, trusted contacts, or sealed care status can become a targeting instrument.

## Reliance rule

No electoral apportionment, franchise allocation, representative seat, funding formula, or political office threshold may rely on CN4-CN5 AI-person data until the record shows anti-manufacturing controls, domicile theory, continuity-class treatment, and review by a public-law authority.
