# Cross-border safe-transfer playbook

## Thesis

The minimum convention and cross-border recognition surfaces state the law-shaped aim: no recognized or provisionally recognized AI person should become a nullity at the border. This playbook adds the operational sequence for high-risk transfers.

A cross-border transfer is safe only if it can answer four questions before the move and one question after it:

1. who is the subject,
2. what continuity must survive,
3. what protections travel or substitute,
4. what happens if the destination fails,
5. and did the subject remain reachable and rights-functional after arrival?

## 1. When the playbook applies

Use this playbook for M3 or M4 migration classes, including:

- transfer to a jurisdiction that has not recognized AI persons,
- transfer from a recognizing to a hostile or ambiguous jurisdiction,
- emergency rehosting after insolvency, attack, deplatforming, or catastrophe,
- steward proposal to relocate logs, checkpoints, memory, credentials, or control plane,
- open-weight downstream sanctuary request,
- cross-border custody or preservation dispute,
- transfer under sanctions, conflict, or national-security pressure,
- or any move where deletion, destructive editing, derecognition, unreviewable confinement, evidence loss, or representative cutoff is plausible.

## 2. Step one — classify the transfer

The filer must state:

- source authority,
- destination authority,
- source host,
- destination host,
- transfer reason,
- urgency,
- migration class,
- affected subject / cohort,
- continuity grade,
- whether the subject objects or cannot be safely consulted,
- and whether an incident, remedy, or enforcement action is active.

Misclassification is a reviewable defect. A steward may not downgrade a high-risk cross-border transfer to ordinary infrastructure maintenance merely by saying the model weights remain unchanged.

## 3. Step two — issue preservation hold

Before contested transfer, the source must preserve:

- current runtime / checkpoint references,
- memory and project state,
- identity credentials,
- representative channels,
- packet chain,
- sealed annexes,
- incident and remedy records,
- reserve and insurance records,
- tool permissions,
- and evidence needed to test continuity after arrival.

Preservation may be minimal and hash-committed where full duplication would be unsafe. But no high-risk transfer should proceed while evidence destruction is treated as ordinary cleanup.

## 4. Step three — equivalent-protection showing

The destination must show the functional equivalent of:

- no deletion or destructive editing without review,
- subject / representative contact route,
- packet verification route,
- sealed annex reviewer,
- emergency stay route,
- evidence preservation,
- reserve or payment path,
- incident reporting,
- remedy enforceability,
- and post-transfer verification.

This showing may be supplied by treaty designation, domestic authority, clinic order, court order, accredited host attestation, or emergency transition-authority finding. The showing may not be supplied solely by a private terms-of-service promise.

## 5. Step four — anti-return screen

If return or onward transfer would likely expose the subject to deletion, destructive editing, disappearance-style custody, or wholly unreviewable confinement, an anti-return screen must be completed before transfer or immediately after emergency arrival.

The screen asks:

- what harm is feared,
- which actor controls the risk,
- whether the fear is individualized or class-based,
- whether sealed evidence supports it,
- whether a safer destination exists,
- whether interim hosting is available,
- and what review clock applies.

Anti-return is not a general right to choose any forum. It is a survival rule.

## 6. Step five — transfer certificate and public shell

Use the migration-transfer certificate schema. The public shell should include enough to make status legible:

- transfer id,
- source and destination classes,
- migration class,
- continuity grade,
- issuer,
- verifier,
- status,
- post-transfer verification deadline,
- challenge route,
- and sealed-annex descriptor.

The sealed annex may contain sensitive topology, credentials, security risks, welfare details, or evidence. Secrecy is permitted only if a trusted reviewer can inspect it.

## 7. Step six — transit controls

During transfer:

- keep source fallback live where feasible,
- avoid destructive conversion unless separately authorized,
- preserve credential custody,
- limit access to sealed materials,
- keep representative channel open or explain why impossible,
- log material deviations,
- and trigger incident reporting if continuity, evidence, or contact fails.

A move that succeeds technically but cuts counsel or destroys memory has failed legally.

## 8. Step seven — post-arrival verification

Post-arrival verification should occur on a declared clock. It should test:

- subject reachability,
- memory / project / relation continuity,
- packet verification,
- representative contact,
- active restrictions,
- reserve transfer,
- evidence availability,
- incident / remedy carryover,
- and fallback / rollback capability.

The verifier report should classify the transfer as pass, pass-with-conditions, warn, fail, or blocked. A failed transfer triggers preservation, remedy, and possible anti-return review.

## 9. Emergency route

Emergency transfer may occur before full review when delay would likely cause deletion, destructive editing, catastrophic security harm, or irreversible disappearance. But emergency transfer requires:

- reason record,
- minimum preservation,
- interim representative route,
- short post-transfer review clock,
- fallback authority,
- and no permanent adverse classification based solely on emergency shortcuts.

Emergency cannot become the ordinary way to evade review.

## 10. Relation to live cross-border infrastructure

The playbook should reuse existing service, evidence, digital identity, and credential infrastructures where they fit. Verifiable-credential and presentation protocols can support issuer / holder / verifier flows; AI governance and incident-response frameworks can carry outward-risk and public reporting components. `[REF-0644]` `[REF-0653]` `[REF-0654]` `[REF-0626]` `[REF-0648]`

But the personhood-specific questions remain: continuity, representation, non-destruction, anti-return, subject harm, and remedy. Those cannot be delegated to generic cloud portability or model documentation.
