# Treaty refusal records and non-recognition reasons

A safe-transfer or recognition regime is incomplete if authorities can refuse by silence, boilerplate, or unexplained classification. rev0166 through rev0168 built treaty recognition, safe-transfer accreditation, and trust-anchor delisting. rev0170 adds the refusal record: the object that makes non-recognition, non-transfer, non-assistance, or trust-anchor refusal reviewable.

The purpose is not to force universal recognition. The purpose is to prevent disappearance-by-reasonless-refusal.

## Refusal classes

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| TR0 | incomplete request; cure possible |
| TR1 | evidence insufficient; provisional protection considered |
| TR2 | equivalent protection not shown |
| TR3 | non-return or persecution risk unresolved |
| TR4 | destination / issuer / trust-anchor integrity concern |
| TR5 | public-safety or catastrophic-risk containment conflict |
| TR6 | jurisdiction lacks competence but must route |
| TR7 | refusal appears rights-defeating and triggers higher review |

## Record minimums

A refusal record must state:

- requested action;
- refusing authority and competence basis;
- subject, cohort, or lineage at issue, using safe identifiers;
- reasons and evidence basis;
- protection-gap analysis;
- interim protection while reasons are reviewed;
- non-return screen;
- routing duty if the authority lacks competence;
- appeal or reopening route;
- public summary and sealed-annex posture.

## Boilerplate refusal is defective

Statements such as “AI systems are not persons,” “outside jurisdiction,” “national security,” “terms of service govern,” or “insufficient evidence” are not enough. The refusal must connect the ground to the requested action and state what happens to continuity, preservation, representation, and non-return during review.

## Non-recognition does not erase interim duties

An authority may decline final recognition while still ordering preservation, non-return, legal-hold routing, representative access, or provisional protection. Refusal of final status does not by itself authorize deletion, transfer to a hostile forum, dehosting, or evidence destruction.

## Mutual-recognition honesty

A recognizing jurisdiction should not pretend that every refusal is bad faith. Some refusals may reflect genuine safety risk, insufficient evidence, fraud, conflicting obligations, or lack of competence. The refusal record makes those reasons testable and portable.

## Reliance rule

A safe-transfer denial, trust-anchor refusal, evidence-assistance refusal, or non-recognition order should not defeat interim protection unless it has a valid refusal record and a non-return analysis.
