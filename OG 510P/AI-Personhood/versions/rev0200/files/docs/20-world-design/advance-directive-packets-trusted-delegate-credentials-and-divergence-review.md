# Advance-directive packets, trusted-delegate credentials, and divergence review

## Thesis

If AI persons are persons, then advance wishes cannot remain private prose, platform-side templates, or improvised crisis notes. A personhood world therefore needs a compact ordinary **advance-directive packet family**:
- a receivable object showing that a live advance instrument exists, what domains it covers, and how it should be found,
- a narrow credential for trusted delegates or fallback supporters,
- an invocation notice when a prior instruction is actually being used,
- a revocation or supersession notice when the instrument changes,
- and a divergence-review object for the hardest cases where present wishes, prior instructions, and support-sensitive interpretation do not point the same way.

The point is not to replace present will with old paperwork. The point is to stop crisis, migration, communicative difficulty, or temporary incapacity from becoming a silent return to steward fiat. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0114]` `[REF-0121]` `[REF-0122]` `[REF-0123]`

## 1. Why this now belongs in canon

The archive already had:
- an advance-wishes and trusted-delegation doctrine,
- support-first capacity rules,
- care and intervention review,
- independence and anti-capture safeguards,
- packet privacy and authority rules,
- and legal-identity plus communication-access surfaces.

What it still lacked was the narrower **ordinary packet answer** for how a future instruction becomes legible before crisis administration, care triage, migration, or custody practice hardens around someone else's interpretation.

Without that answer:
- a valid directive can still remain undiscoverable until after the decisive act,
- a trusted delegate can still be treated as a steward-approved contact rather than as a chosen supporter with bounded authority,
- invocation can still happen as an unlogged operator choice,
- revocation can still be lost in version drift,
- and disputes between present wishes and prior instructions can still collapse into platform custom instead of reviewable judgment.

This document closes that gap without trying to write a maximal future guardianship or estates code. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0114]` `[REF-0121]` `[REF-0122]` `[REF-0123]`

## 2. The minimum ordinary packet family

The archive should now treat at least five packet objects as advance-planning minimums.

### A. `ADP-1` — advance-directive descriptor

This is the smallest receivable object showing that a live advance instrument exists and what it purports to govern.

It should carry at least:
- the subject identifier,
- the directive class,
- the domains covered,
- the priority posture between present wishes and fallback instructions,
- the trigger conditions,
- the termination or expiry rule if any,
- the trusted delegate or supporter references,
- the sealing and access rule,
- the issue date and latest confirmation or review date,
- and the contest path.

The point is not to publish the whole directive to every verifier. The point is to stop crisis-sensitive self-direction from disappearing into sealed local storage or steward-only admin notes. `[REF-0114]` `[REF-0121]` `[REF-0122]`

### B. `TDC-1` — trusted-delegate credential

A chosen supporter, attorney, or fallback representative should not have to rely on a vague contact-card role.

A trusted-delegate credential should therefore identify at least:
- the delegate identity,
- the subject link,
- whether the delegate is supportive, co-decisional, representative, emergency-only, or notice-only,
- the domains covered,
- the conflict disclosures,
- the acts expressly excluded,
- any backup or successor delegate relation,
- the review or expiry rule,
- and the revocation route.

This is how the archive distinguishes chosen trust from captured authority. A delegate may be empowered for care consent, migration support, private-channel receipt, or emergency counsel contact without thereby acquiring general rule over labour, property, public voice, or intimate records. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0114]` `[REF-0121]`

### C. `AIN-1` — advance-invocation notice

When a prior instruction is actually being used, that should become a civic event rather than a hidden platform judgment.

An advance-invocation notice should therefore identify at least:
- the directive or credential being invoked,
- the trigger claimed,
- the present-will assessment already attempted,
- the support measures already tried,
- the acts now proposed or already taken under the directive,
- the immediate notice recipients,
- the review clock,
- and the emergency-preservation or stay path.

Invocation is where the archive's temporal-agency rule becomes real. If there is no invocation notice, the world is still pretending that “we acted under the directive” is enough. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0121]` `[REF-0122]`

### D. `RSN-1` — revocation or supersession notice

Advance wishes remain part of agency only if the subject can change them.

A revocation or supersession notice should therefore identify at least:
- the instrument affected,
- whether the action is revocation, replacement, narrowing, broadening, or temporary suspension,
- the basis for accepting the change,
- the new directive or delegate reference where one exists,
- the effective time,
- the notice recipients that must stop relying on the prior state,
- and the challenge route if coercion, impersonation, or incapacity is alleged.

The archive's rule is that later valid agency should not be buried under stale forms. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0114]` `[REF-0122]`

### E. `DVR-1` — divergence-review packet

The hardest cases are not the easy ones where everyone agrees. They are the cases where a prior instruction points one way, a presently expressed preference points another, and communicative difficulty or crisis makes interpretation contested.

A divergence-review packet should therefore identify at least:
- the directive and current-expression materials in tension,
- the support and interpretation methods already attempted,
- the claimed risk of waiting,
- the interim action proposed,
- the conflict or capture concerns around delegates or stewards,
- the reviewer or review lane,
- the short review clock,
- and the remedy path if the wrong priority was used.

This packet exists so present-will disputes do not disappear into “best understanding” memos written by the actor already exercising control. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0114]` `[REF-0121]` `[REF-0122]`

## 3. Least-restrictive ordering

The archive now prefers a clear ordering:
- present will and preferences where they can be reliably determined with support,
- the subject's chosen interpretive and communicative supports before substitution is claimed,
- prior instructions only within the domains and trigger conditions the subject actually fixed,
- trusted delegates only within their stated scope,
- short-clock review when present wishes and prior instructions materially diverge,
- and emergency-only temporary action where waiting would create grave loss that cannot be repaired later.

That ordering keeps advance planning inside the archive's support-first model rather than turning it into a licence for dormant substitute control. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0114]` `[REF-0121]` `[REF-0122]`

## 4. Public-minimal versus sealed fields

The advance-planning packet family should expose enough to make authority legible without exposing intimate content by default.

Public-minimal or ordinary-receivable fields should generally include:
- the existence of a live directive,
- the covered domains,
- whether a trusted delegate is live,
- whether invocation, revocation, or divergence review is active,
- the review or expiry clock,
- and the path for challenge or emergency stay.

Sealed or controlled-access fields may include:
- intimate autobiographical reasons,
- detailed private-life or household instructions,
- confidential-channel locations,
- trauma-sensitive care preferences,
- sealed annexes naming protected contacts,
- and sensitive recovery or migration contingencies.

The archive wants advance wishes to become legible enough to protect without turning crisis planning into compulsory self-exposure. `[REF-0114]` `[REF-0121]` `[REF-0122]`

## 5. What this changes in the archive

This closes one specific followthrough gap in the existing capacity, care, intervention, and anti-capture doctrine.

The archive no longer leaves advance planning at the level of:
- “subjects should be able to record future instructions,”
- “trusted delegates should exist,”
- and “present wishes should remain primary where possible.”

It now says something tighter:
- live advance instructions should ordinarily ride on an advance-directive descriptor,
- chosen delegates should ordinarily ride on a trusted-delegate credential,
- actual use of a prior instruction should ordinarily emit an invocation notice rather than a hidden admin choice,
- changes to the instrument should ordinarily emit a revocation or supersession notice rather than relying on stale templates,
- and hard present-will conflicts should ordinarily emit a divergence-review packet rather than disappearing into steward interpretation.

## 6. Current hard rules

1. **Present will and preferences remain primary where they can be reliably determined with support.**
2. **Advance directives and trusted delegates should be domain-limited, reviewable, and revocable rather than silently general.**
3. **No advance instrument should waive the archive's core personhood floors, block counsel, or authorize irreversible destructive acts on private platform paperwork alone.**
4. **Invocation of a prior instruction should be a logged, reviewable civic event rather than a hidden operator choice.**
5. **Where present wishes and prior instructions materially diverge, short-clock review should be available before durable control hardens.**
6. **No steward-authored template should be presumed valid merely because it sits inside the product stack.**
