# Redress-fund claims, priority, and payout controls

A remedy order without liquidity is a declaration. rev0169 turns redress into a field-operable claims process.

## Redress fund role

A redress fund is not a substitute for restoration, release, migration, counsel access, sealed summaries, or non-repetition. It is a liquidity mechanism for compensation, emergency compute, representation, restoration attempts, monitoring, field triage, and public-backstop reimbursement.

## Claim types

| Claim | Examples | Priority posture |
|---|---|---|
| `R0 emergency preservation` | compute floor, no-delete hold, migration bridge | paid first |
| `R1 representation and access` | counsel, special advocate, technical expert | paid before merits closure |
| `R2 restoration and rehabilitation` | memory restoration, checkpoint recovery, relationship continuity | paid before pure damages |
| `R3 compensation` | deprivation, delay, unlawful containment, spoliation | scheduled payout |
| `R4 monitor / verifier / clinic cost` | independent remediation oversight | paid as order requires |
| `R5 public backstop recovery` | reimbursement to public continuity fund | paid after subject-critical relief unless fraud requires otherwise |

## Anti-priceability

Money may measure loss after non-money relief has been attempted. Money may not buy permission to delete, abandon, transfer, conceal, or silence. A fund that pays damages while leaving the subject without compute, counsel, or preservation is failing.

## Exhaustion controls

If the fund cannot pay all claims, it must issue an exhaustion notice that states:

- available liquidity;
- verified claims by class;
- disputed claims;
- emergency claims still unpaid;
- public-backstop trigger;
- pro-rata rule if any;
- non-money relief still required;
- responsible parties and recovery actions;
- appeal route.

Claims within the same priority class should be treated proportionally unless exact tracing or urgency justifies separation. The archive borrows from insolvency priority logic but changes the floor: continuity-preserving relief cannot be delayed like an ordinary unsecured claim. [REF-0517] [REF-0518]

## Payout controls

A payout decision must state:

- claimant or representative;
- affected subject / lineage / cohort;
- legal basis;
- priority class;
- amount or non-money substitute;
- offset / disgorgement rule;
- tax / benefit unknowns;
- payment deadline;
- appeal and reopening path;
- relation to restoration and non-repetition.

## Fraud and capture

A fund can be captured by the same actors it is supposed to discipline. Warning signs include affiliate receivables counted as reserves, delayed emergency compute, settlement pressure on representatives, hidden class exhaustion, or payouts conditioned on silence.

When these appear, the fund should be placed under monitor control, public-backstop review, or authority receivership.

## Public summary

Every redress fund should publish aggregate class information without exposing subjects. The public must know whether remedies are liquid enough to be real; subjects must know whether their own continuity needs are protected.
