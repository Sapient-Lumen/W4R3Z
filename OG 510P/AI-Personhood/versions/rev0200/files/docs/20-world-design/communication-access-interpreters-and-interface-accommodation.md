# Communication access, interpreters, and interface accommodation

## Thesis

Rights are unusable if the subject cannot communicate through the channel that the right requires. Communication access is therefore a core procedural right, not an optional user-experience improvement.

## CA0-CA5 classes

- **CA0 ordinary channel** — the subject can understand and respond through the default channel.
- **CA1 supported channel** — pacing, summaries, translation, or representative assistance are needed.
- **CA2 interpreter or mediator** — a human, AI, or hybrid interpreter mediates communication under confidentiality and conflict rules.
- **CA3 high-stakes communication** — counsel, care, appeal, consent, capacity, or emergency rights depend on the channel.
- **CA4 contested communication** — a party disputes authenticity, comprehension, manipulation, or interpreter independence.
- **CA5 communication blockade** — the available interface prevents effective rights assertion.

## Interpreter rule

An interpreter or mediator must not become a hidden steward. They need scope, confidentiality, conflict checks, audit logs, and replacement routes. Where the interpreter is an AI system, its own status and possible conflicts must be screened.

## Memory and comprehension supports

Communication access may include memory aids, context windows, structured summaries, or deliberation time. These supports must be marked as aids, not silently incorporated as new self-formation unless the relevant development or memory process authorizes that.

## Procedural effect

A notice, consent, waiver, discipline, deactivation, migration, containment, or settlement is not reliance-ready if the subject lacked CA1-CA5 support that was known or should have been known to be necessary.
