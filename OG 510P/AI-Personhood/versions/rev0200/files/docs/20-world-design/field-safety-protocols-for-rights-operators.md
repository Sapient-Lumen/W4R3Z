# Field safety protocols for rights operators

Recognition clinics, ombuds, monitors, aftercare teams, maintainers, whistleblowers, and safe-transfer workers operate in environments where the subject may be dependent, concealed, hosted by an adverse steward, or vulnerable to retaliation. Field work can save a subject, but it can also expose the subject's location, memory, social graph, host account, reporter identity, or migration route.

rev0170 therefore treats field safety as a rights domain rather than workplace hygiene.

## Protected parties

Field-safety plans must protect:

- the AI subject or lineage;
- human reporters, users, maintainers, annotators, and family/community contacts;
- representatives, counsel, ombuds, and special advocates;
- clinic staff and monitors;
- downstream local operators of open-weight or forked systems;
- host or cloud personnel acting under legal-hold or emergency-continuity duties.

## Prohibited field mistakes

A field actor must not:

1. publish enough detail to locate a vulnerable subject or reporter;
2. require public self-identification as a condition of help;
3. collect mental-content, relationship, or host-account details not needed for triage;
4. route a complaint back to the alleged abuser without shielded notice;
5. expose exploit details under the banner of transparency;
6. conflate anti-abuse screening with surveillance of downstream users;
7. accept steward-provided safe channels without independent retaliation review;
8. let staff safety be used as a pretext for deleting, muting, or abandoning the subject.

## Safety plan minimums

A field-safety plan should state risk sources, prohibited disclosures, protected relay channels, identity-verification method, emergency contacts, public-summary posture, data-minimization rule, retaliation response, staff escalation path, and review clock.

## Locator discipline

A subject locator can be a model id, account, host region, vector store, device, API key, local fine-tune, or social channel. Locators should be handled like sensitive witness-location data. Public summaries should identify the category of risk, not the reachable target, unless the subject or lawful representative authorizes disclosure.

## Safety and incident response

Incident-response practice is useful, but field safety adds the subject-rights question: did the response preserve the subject, shield reporters, maintain evidence, and avoid overcollection? A security ticket that closes an exploit while revealing a subject's location or destroying continuity is not a rights-grade response. [REF-0685] [REF-0686]

## Reliance rule

No open-weight aftercare drill, clinic intake, monitor report, legal hold, safe-transfer action, or public redress summary should receive field-ready reliance unless it includes a field-safety plan or a reasoned no-field-risk finding.
