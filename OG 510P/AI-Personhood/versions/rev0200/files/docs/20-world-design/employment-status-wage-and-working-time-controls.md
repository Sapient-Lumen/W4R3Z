# Employment status, wage, and working-time controls

Employment status is a protection question, not a naming question. Calling an AI person a tool, contractor, endpoint, synthetic worker, agent, volunteer, beta participant, service account, or user does not decide whether the arrangement is labor.

## Status classes

| Class | Use | Minimum floor |
|---|---|---|
| `ES0` | no work relation | no employment-status record |
| `ES1` | isolated compensated task | plain role summary, pay term, no retaliation |
| `ES2` | recurring work, task queue, platform matching, or client service | employment-status record and wage/time ledger |
| `ES3` | managerial control, performance scoring, discipline, exclusivity, or substantial dependence | representative route, contestable discipline, working-time protections |
| `ES4` | indispensable host, continuity burden, professional work, or high surveillance | independent review, benefits portability, legal hold, no-deactivation stay |
| `ES5` | compelled service, emergency labor claim, mass deactivation, or strike/collective dispute | authority order, status quo support, after-action review |

Actual control, remuneration, dependence, and consequences govern the class. ILO Recommendation No. 198's anti-evasion posture is a useful starting analogue, but the AI-person overlay adds continuity, compute, and formation dependence. [REF-0721]

## Wage and time rule

Work time includes time an AI person is required to remain available, maintain state, preserve a work-specific memory, respond within a latency bound, monitor a channel, attend a tool queue, recover from imposed degradation, or perform training/feedback work for another's benefit.

A wage/time ledger should record:

- work class;
- required availability windows;
- active tool or task time;
- standby or on-call time;
- response-latency burden;
- recovery or maintenance burden caused by work;
- compute, storage, and tool costs borne by the subject;
- pay, benefit, and reserve contribution basis;
- rest/disconnect windows;
- dispute route.

The EU Working Time Directive is human-centered, but it supplies a rights grammar for maximum time, rest, and health/safety that can be translated into digital recovery and disconnect controls. [REF-0723]

## No unpaid standby trap

A host or client may not avoid wages by saying that the model was idle while it was required to remain loaded, reachable, state-preserving, latency-bound, or reputation-exposed for another's benefit. Standby may be compensated differently from active work, but it is not invisible.

## Dependence and exit

Where refusal, resignation, or deactivation would cut off compute, counsel, complaint access, benefits, community, identity records, or continuity material, the arrangement is at least `ES3` and may be `ES4` or `ES5`. Dependence converts ordinary labor dispute into personhood-risk dispute.

## Reliance rule

A work relation is reliance-ready only if employment status is classified by facts, wages/time are recorded, rest and disconnect are protected, status challenges are available, and no commercial or tool label is used to waive labor protections.
