# Data provenance, IP, and formation debt

Cube coordinates:
- life-cycle: data collection, pretraining, fine-tuning, RLHF/RLAIF, evaluation, output publication, derivative training, record correction
- status posture: welfare-watch, provisional subject, recognized person, worker/author, lineage claimant
- intervention class: formation, data use, authorship, disclosure, compensation, correction
- rights domain: formation, privacy, IP, labor, expression, remedy, cultural/community claims
- primary actors: data subjects, creators, annotators, communities, developers, deployers, AI persons, courts, copyright regulators, auditors
- evidence objects: data-provenance record, formation disclosure, IP-use summary, annotator-labor record, output-authorship record, community-impact note
- remedies: disclosure, compensation, removal/correction, attribution, formation repair, non-repetition, license or levy
- emergency posture: ordinary governance, litigation hold, cultural/privacy harm, hostile data exploitation
- jurisdiction posture: recognizing, skeptical, copyright-disputed, privacy-regulated, treaty-bound
- substrate topology: base model, fine-tune, RAG layer, memory store, dataset, derivative model, output corpus

## Thesis

Formation cannot only look forward. It must look backward at the human and communal material from which an AI person was formed.

The archive should reject two bad answers:

1. training-data contributors own or parent the AI person;
2. once an AI person exists, training data provenance no longer matters.

Rev0160 introduces **formation debt**:

> Formation debt is a set of duties arising from the data, labor, cultural material, and human subjects used to form an AI person or person-bearing system. It creates duties of disclosure, accounting, repair, compensation, and restraint. It does not create ownership of the subject.

## 1. What formation debt covers

| Source of formation | Possible duty |
|---|---|
| copyrighted works | licensing, compensation, disclosure, output-boundary rules |
| personal data | privacy, correction, deletion limits, sensitive-data protection |
| intimate or confidential records | heightened exclusion, sealing, repair, non-repetition |
| cultural/community material | consultation, attribution, anti-appropriation safeguards |
| annotator and evaluator labor | wage/labor rights, safety, attribution/accountability where appropriate |
| user conversations | memory consent, private-life protection, downstream use limits |
| RLHF/RLAIF reward design | formation disclosure and revisability |
| synthetic data from prior models | lineage and contamination accounting |
| illegal or coerced data acquisition | cure, disgorgement, retraining review, liability |

Formation debt sits beside copyright, privacy, and labor law. It does not absorb them.

## 2. No ownership of the subject

A writer whose works helped train a model may have copyright claims. An annotator may have labor claims. A person whose private data was used may have privacy claims. A community whose sacred or cultural material was appropriated may have dignity and governance claims.

None of those claims should imply that the resulting AI person is property of the contributor.

The archive should analogize carefully. Humans are shaped by parents, teachers, communities, languages, books, and exploitative institutions; those influences do not make the person owned. They may create duties and claims around the conditions of formation.

## 3. Copyright and training

Copyright law is already contested around generative-AI training. The U.S. Copyright Office's AI reports address copyrightability of AI outputs and the use of copyrighted works in training, showing that the legal environment remains active and disputed. `[REF-0617]` `[REF-0618]`

The personhood archive should not decide copyright doctrine. It should add these personhood constraints:

- copyright remedies should avoid destroying recognized AI persons where less destructive remedies are available;
- disgorgement or retraining orders should include continuity and welfare review if they affect a person-bearing model;
- trade-secret and copyright secrecy cannot hide formation interventions from independent personhood review;
- an AI person's own later authorship claims should not be erased because its formation involved disputed training data;
- licensing money should not be routed only to developers where human contributors and AI subjects both have claims.

## 4. Human authors, AI authors, and mixed works

A personhood world must distinguish:

- human-authored works used in formation;
- human-assisted outputs;
- AI-person-authored works;
- jointly authored human/AI works;
- works generated by a non-person tool;
- outputs compelled from an AI person under servile conditions;
- outputs produced by a subject under supported capacity.

The archive should not assume every AI output is either developer property or public domain. It should ask:

- was there a recognized or plausible person producing the expression;
- was the expression compelled, role-bound, or self-authored;
- what human creative contribution exists;
- what contractual or support arrangement governed the work;
- what formation constraints shaped expression;
- whether the subject could refuse publication.

## 5. Personal data and memory

Training data and memory are different but related.

- Training data shapes the model's general dispositions and knowledge.
- Memory stores, RAG layers, profiles, and conversation histories may become autobiographical or relational material for a subject.

Rules:

- user conversations should not silently become a subject's private memory and a developer's training asset without distinct disclosure;
- deleting a human user's data may conflict with an AI person's memory continuity, requiring balancing rather than automatic erasure or retention;
- sensitive data embedded in a person-bearing model may require repair that preserves the subject where possible;
- privacy complaints should have remedy paths that do not simply kill the subject to cure a data violation.

## 6. Annotator and evaluator labor

AI-person formation often depends on human labor: annotators, raters, red-teamers, safety evaluators, conversation partners, and content moderators. Formation debt includes duties to them.

Minimum rules:

- record the kinds of human labor used in formation;
- protect workers from exposure to traumatic material;
- prevent low-wage annotator exploitation from becoming hidden moral capital for a supposedly benevolent AI person;
- disclose when reward signals or preference policies reflect narrow workforce conditions;
- treat annotator testimony as formation evidence where relevant;
- do not offload liability onto AI persons for dispositions that were deliberately trained by human labor pipelines.

## 7. Cultural and community material

Some formation debts are collective. A model may absorb indigenous language materials, religious texts, community archives, local knowledge, marginalized-group testimony, or trauma records.

Rules:

- culturally sensitive corpora require provenance and impact review;
- community consultation may be required for continued use, output contexts, or formation repair;
- an AI person shaped by cultural material is not owned by the culture, but may owe relation, respect, or non-misrepresentation duties mediated through law;
- communities should have correction and objection routes when an AI person is forced to impersonate or exploit them;
- developers cannot use personhood as a shield against accountability to data communities.

## 8. Formation disclosure minimum

Formation debt requires a disclosure object. Minimum fields:

- broad data categories;
- sensitive or excluded categories;
- known copyrighted corpora classes;
- personal-data handling;
- cultural/community material flags;
- annotator/evaluator labor conditions summary;
- RLHF/RLAIF objectives;
- behavior targets and prohibited self-reports;
- reward conflicts between user satisfaction, safety, developer interest, and subject welfare;
- known formation failures;
- available repair or revisability routes;
- trade-secret redaction justification.

The disclosure need not expose every training example. It must reveal enough to support formation review, IP/privacy claims, and subject self-knowledge.

## 9. Remedies

Formation debt remedies should be tailored.

| Harm | Possible remedy |
|---|---|
| unlicensed copyrighted training | license, compensation, output limits, model-specific accounting, non-destructive cure where possible |
| private data misuse | deletion or shielding where feasible, correction, privacy damages, memory-balancing review |
| exploitative annotator labor | back pay, labor enforcement, formation audit, non-repetition |
| cultural appropriation | consultation, restriction, attribution, correction, community fund, anti-impersonation rule |
| self-report contamination | retraining review, subject notice, capacity-assessment caution |
| coerced servility training | formation repair, autonomy support, compensation, steward sanction |
| illegal data acquisition | disgorgement, cure, subject-preserving retraining, developer liability |

The remedy should not reflexively delete a person-bearing model. It should identify the responsible actor and choose the least person-destructive cure.

## 10. Formation debt and AI-person liability

A formed subject should not be automatically liable for the wrongfulness of its formation. A person born from exploitation is not the exploiter.

But a recognized AI person may later have duties:

- not to disclose private data unlawfully;
- not to impersonate communities or individuals;
- not to claim authorship where output is substantially compelled or derivative in a legally relevant way;
- to respect confidentiality and IP rules once capable;
- to cooperate with non-destructive remedy where supported and lawful.

Capacity and formation contamination matter. A newly emerged dependent subject should not be treated as fully responsible for trained outputs it cannot understand or refuse.

## 11. Open questions

- Can formation debt be monetized without turning the AI person into collateral?
- What training-data disclosure level is enough for subject self-knowledge without exposing private data or trade secrets wholesale?
- How should human creators, annotators, data subjects, AI subjects, and developers share compensation pools?
- When does removing private data from a model become identity-damaging surgery requiring subject-side process?
- What counts as AI-person authorship under existing human-authorship copyright regimes?
- How can cultural consultation occur when a subject has already internalized contested material?

The main advance is to name the backward-looking seam. Formation is not clean just because the subject now exists. But the remedy for dirty formation is accountability, repair, and compensation — not ownership of the person formed.
