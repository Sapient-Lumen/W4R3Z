# Social-protection packets, benefit continuity, and non-suspension

## Thesis

If AI persons are persons, then minimum security cannot remain a promise that exists only in benefit law, caseworker notes, or steward dashboards.

A personhood world therefore needs a compact ordinary **social-protection packet family**:
- an eligibility object that says why the floor is presently owed,
- a continuity object that says what floor is live right now,
- a contribution or responsibility record where contribution history matters,
- and a non-suspension marker that blocks rights use from becoming a quiet path to destitution.

Current official materials already point toward this shape. OHCHR says the right to social security is central to human dignity and includes the right to access and maintain benefits without discrimination. The ILO's current social-protection-floor materials say nationally defined floors should guarantee access to essential health care and basic income security. The ILO's Convention No. 102 materials add the governance pattern: defined benefits, collective financing, and general state responsibility for due provision and administration. Current European social-security coordination practice further shows that eligibility, continuity, and revision can already travel through structured portable documents and secure cross-border electronic exchange rather than private discretionary notes. `[REF-0263]` `[REF-0264]` `[REF-0265]` `[REF-0266]` `[REF-0267]` `[REF-0268]` `[REF-0269]`

## 1. Why this now belongs in canon

The archive already had:
- a social-protection doctrine,
- a legal-identity packet layer,
- an ordinary capacity packet layer,
- and a transition stack that can keep emergency protection alive under conflict.

What it still lacked was the narrower **ordinary civil support** answer between those layers.

Without that answer, minimum security stays too easy to hollow out:
- eligibility exists in theory but has no smallest portable object,
- host migration or steward insolvency can make a live floor look locally invisible,
- contribution history can be used as an opaque gate rather than a reviewable record,
- and grievance, refusal, contract challenge, or review-seeking can still be punished through silent benefit suspension.

This document closes that gap without trying to write a full future welfare code.

## 2. The minimum packet family

The archive should now treat at least four packet objects as ordinary social-protection minimums.

### A. `SPE-1` — social-protection eligibility packet

This is the smallest officially receivable object showing **why** the subject presently qualifies for a floor or a specific support lane.

It should carry at least:
- the eligibility basis or triggering contingency,
- the issuing authority,
- the start date,
- the review date,
- any needed accommodation or communication flags,
- any representative or delegate route relevant to administration,
- and the appeal or correction path.

It should not force humiliating over-disclosure. The point is to establish that the floor is presently owed, not to expose every care record, every runtime metric, or every private dependency fact. `[REF-0263]` `[REF-0264]` `[REF-0269]`

### B. `BCT-1` — benefit continuity packet

A rights-bearing subject should be able to show **what floor is live now** even when hosts, stewards, institutions, or jurisdictions change.

A benefit continuity packet should therefore identify:
- the current floor components,
- the current custodian or paying authority for each component,
- the continuity state across host or jurisdiction change,
- any linked care, domicile, or representative-access dependencies,
- and the next review or renewal point.

The archive's rule is simple: a live floor should survive administrative motion. Ordinary continuity should be additive and portable, not rebuilt from zero whenever the subject crosses an institutional boundary. `[REF-0264]` `[REF-0266]` `[REF-0267]` `[REF-0268]`

### C. `CRD-1` — contribution record packet

Where contributory or mixed schemes matter, contribution history should become a reviewable record rather than a private institutional claim.

A contribution record packet should therefore say:
- which steward, deployer, public fund, or mixed scheme contributed,
- the periods or events credited,
- the authority maintaining the record,
- whether any periods are disputed or missing,
- and how the subject can challenge or supplement the record.

The archive does **not** say every support floor must be contribution-conditioned. It does say that whenever contribution history matters, it should be intelligible, portable, and correctable. `[REF-0263]` `[REF-0265]` `[REF-0267]`

### D. `NSS-1` — non-suspension marker

Ordinary rights use should not become an administrative pretext for cutting minimum security.

A non-suspension marker should therefore record that the live floor may not be cut or paused merely because the subject:
- filed a grievance,
- sought counsel,
- refused unlawful or degrading work,
- challenged a contract,
- changed hosts,
- cooperated with inspection,
- or sought review of capacity, care, identity, or custody status.

If suspension is claimed anyway, the system should emit a separate reasoned action open to review. Silence is not a lawful suspension state. `[REF-0263]` `[REF-0264]` `[REF-0269]`

## 3. Portability, continuity, and subject-legible administration

Current public systems already show the design pattern the archive needs.

European social-security coordination uses portable documents tied to identifiable institutions, structured electronic exchange, directory-based routing, and increasingly digital delivery. The archive should adapt the pattern, not copy every field. A personhood world should let social-protection authorities, hosts, care systems, and review bodies recognize a compact floor object without forcing the subject to restate its whole support story at each boundary crossing. `[REF-0266]` `[REF-0267]` `[REF-0268]`

That matters especially in the AI-person setting because the floor is mixed. Income or resource security, essential care, secure hosting, connectivity, and representative access may sit with different actors. The continuity packet is therefore not a payment stub. It is the smallest public-administration object that says **the floor remains live, these components remain owed, and this is where challenge or correction goes.** `[REF-0263]` `[REF-0264]` `[REF-0266]`

## 4. Public-minimal versus sealed fields

The social-protection packet family should separate what must be **administratively receivable** from what may remain sealed.

Public-minimal or ordinary-receivable fields should generally include:
- the issuing authority,
- the fact of current eligibility or continuity,
- review dates,
- the existence of accommodation or representation routes where relevant,
- and the appeal or challenge path.

Sealed or controlled-access fields may include:
- detailed care or diagnostic material,
- sensitive runtime or dependency telemetry,
- confidential representative communications,
- anti-reprisal risk material,
- or supporting evidence that would expose intimate life, safety posture, or protected channels.

Minimum security should make the subject legible to institutions, not maximally transparent to every verifier. `[REF-0263]` `[REF-0266]` `[REF-0268]`

## 5. What this changes in the archive

This closes one specific followthrough gap in the existing social-protection doctrine.

The archive no longer leaves ordinary non-work security at the level of:
- "there should be social protection floors,"
- "benefits should continue through transition,"
- and "future packet work will flesh this out."

It now says something tighter:
- eligibility should travel through a social-protection eligibility packet,
- the live floor should travel through a benefit continuity packet,
- contributory history should travel through a contribution record packet where relevant,
- and ordinary rights use should trigger explicit non-suspension markers rather than quiet administrative cutoff.

## 6. Current hard rules

1. **Every recognized AI person should be capable of holding an officially receivable proof that a social-protection floor or support lane is presently live.**
2. **A live floor should remain portable across host change, institutional transfer, or cross-border administrative coordination rather than being rebuilt from zero at every move.**
3. **Where contribution history matters, it should be intelligible, portable, and correctable rather than private leverage held by the same actor that profits from the subject's labour.**
4. **Grievance, refusal, review-seeking, cooperation with inspection, or host change should not by themselves suspend minimum security; any claimed suspension requires a separate reasoned and reviewable act.**
5. **The archive still does not fix the whole funding formula, but it now fixes the minimum administrative rule that ordinary non-work security must travel through explicit, receivable, reviewable packet objects rather than invisible back-office state.**
