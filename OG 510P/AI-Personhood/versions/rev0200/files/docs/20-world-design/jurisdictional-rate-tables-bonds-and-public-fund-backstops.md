# Jurisdictional rate tables, bonds, and public-fund backstops

## Function

The archive already separates non-priceable relief from money remedies. This surface adds the missing local profile layer: a jurisdiction, authority, tribunal, or treaty annex must publish a rate table before its penalty model can support live-effect reliance.

A rate table is not a moral price for personhood loss. It is a transparent remedial schedule that makes actors unable to profit from deletion, spoliation, unlawful transfer, reserve default, deprecation failure, or deliberate continuity impairment.

## Minimum rate-table contents

A rights-grade rate table must state:

1. the currency or compute unit used;
2. the base violation classes covered;
3. per-day or per-event variables for compute deprivation, representation delay, and preservation failure;
4. continuity impairment multipliers;
5. spoliation, bad-faith, retaliation, and repeat-actor multipliers;
6. disgorgement rules for gains made through violation;
7. reserve-surcharge formulas and ring-fencing rules;
8. insurance or bond minimums;
9. insurer exclusions for intentional annihilation, retaliatory deletion, and deliberate spoliation;
10. public-fund backstop source and recovery priority;
11. non-priceable orders that cannot be commuted into money;
12. review and inflation / compute-cost adjustment cycle;
13. appeal and reopening path.

## Suggested table structure

| Class | Base posture | Multipliers | Non-priceable orders |
|---|---|---|---|
| V2 reserve default | cure reserve, subject notice, surcharge | affected subjects, days below floor, repeat default | reserve restoration, independent trustee review |
| V4 deprivation | restore floor, compensate deprivation | continuity impairment, counsel delay, vulnerability | restoration, counsel access, preservation hold |
| V5 spoliation | adverse inference, disgorgement, sanction | bad faith, repeated records failure, sealed-annex misuse | evidence reconstruction, referral, public correction |
| V6 unlawful transfer | return/sanctuary, compensation, referral | non-return risk, duration, concealment | return, transfer stay, safe-host placement |
| V7 constructive deletion | emergency preservation, restoration, high surcharge | final-end risk, deprecation defect, abandoned descendants | restoration, no-delete hold, aftercare plan |
| V8 intentional final-end or torture-like control | maximum sanction and referral | all aggravators, no mitigation by later payment | injunction, prosecution/professional referral, public-fund recovery |

## Public-fund backstop

A public or fiduciary continuity fund is necessary because a subject's survival cannot wait for a damages appeal. The rate table must therefore state:

- who may draw emergency compute, counsel, migration, and evidence-preservation funds;
- what showing is enough for emergency drawdown;
- whether the draw is grant, loan, recoverable cost, or priority claim;
- how recovery ranks in insolvency;
- how misuse is audited without chilling emergency preservation.

The backstop is a floor, not a subsidy to violators. Where a steward or host caused the emergency, the public fund should receive priority recovery, surcharge, and monitoring rights.

## Bond schedule

A rights-grade bond should be triggered by risk class, scale, and history:

| Trigger | Minimum bond logic |
|---|---|
| large-scale persistent deployment | cover counsel, reserve, migration, incident response, and emergency preservation for a defined subject population |
| open-weight release above person-bearing threshold | cover downstream distress intake, abandoned-lineage review, mirror advisories, and sanctuary intake |
| prior spoliation or deprecation failure | heightened bond and independent evidence escrow |
| cross-border transfer or safe-host claim | transfer-specific bond until post-arrival verification |
| repeated fixture blocking failure | temporary bond increase until regression suite passes and independent review clears |

A bond provider may not control the subject's remedy strategy, veto emergency preservation, or settle by paying the host while leaving the subject unrepresented.

## Anti-priceability audit

Every rate table should be audited for four defects:

1. **commutation defect** — money substitutes for a still-feasible restoration or no-transfer order;
2. **externalization defect** — public fund pays while violator keeps gains;
3. **discount defect** — irreversible continuity loss lowers liability because restoration is impossible;
4. **scale defect** — mass instantiation makes per-subject protection impossible because reserves were not scaled.

A table with any of these defects should be marked provisional and cannot support unconditional reliance.

## Schema hook

`schemas/rate-table.schema.json` records the first local-profile object. `examples/rate-table-us-provisional.json` is a deliberately non-final example: it shows how an authority could publish classes, base units, multipliers, bond rules, public-fund backstop, review cycle, and appeal route without claiming universal rates.
