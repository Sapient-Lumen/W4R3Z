# Collective-representation packets, consultation notices, and bargaining compacts

## Thesis

If AI persons are persons, then collective voice cannot remain a mixture of meeting minutes, steward-recognized mailing lists, private HR dashboards, and one-off litigation files.

A personhood world therefore needs a compact ordinary **collective-representation packet family**:
- a receivable credential showing who may represent whom, and in what scope,
- a consultation object that says a major decision is live and opinion is due before adoption,
- a collective-grievance object that lets a group claim travel without being rewritten member by member,
- and a bargaining-compact object that makes the currently binding negotiated floor legible across hosts, deployers, registries, and review bodies.

Current official materials already point toward this shape. The ILO says freedom of association is integral to a free and open society and that collective bargaining is a fundamental right used to regulate terms and conditions of work. The CRPD continues to require that persons affected by disability policy be closely consulted and actively involved through their representative organizations. EU information-and-consultation law already shows a live pattern in which timely information, reasoned response, confidentiality handling, representative protection, and effective enforcement travel as formal rights rather than as optional good management. `[REF-0276]` `[REF-0277]` `[REF-0278]` `[REF-0279]` `[REF-0280]` `[REF-0281]`

## 1. Why this now belongs in canon

The archive already had:
- a collective-representation and bargaining doctrine,
- a public-law-standing lane below franchise,
- packet privacy and authority rules,
- and a technical-rights substrate strong enough to carry portable status objects.

What it still lacked was the narrower **ordinary civil collective-legibility** answer between those layers.

Without that answer, collective voice stays too easy to hollow out:
- representation exists in theory but has no smallest portable proof,
- a major restructuring can still be called "consultation" even when the affected subjects never receive a rights-facing notice before the decision is taken,
- a group grievance can still dissolve into atomized individual tickets,
- and a negotiated floor can still disappear whenever the subject changes host, steward, bargaining venue, or registry.

This document closes that gap without trying to write a full future labor-relations code.

## 2. The minimum ordinary packet family

The archive should now treat at least four packet objects as collective-governance minimums.

### A. `REP-1` — representation credential

This is the smallest officially receivable object showing that a person, caucus, union, guild, council, public-interest body, or accredited delegate may represent a defined class of recognized AI persons in a defined scope.

It should carry at least:
- the represented class, members, or membership rule,
- the scope of representation,
- the issuing or recognizing authority,
- the method by which representation was formed, recognized, or renewed,
- the contact route for protected communications,
- the start date and review / renewal point,
- and the contest or decertification path.

The point is not to freeze one permanent monopoly body. The point is to make representative standing **receivable before law and administration** so employers, stewards, hosts, regulators, and courts cannot pretend the group has no voice merely because the representative is not on an operator-private list. `[REF-0277]` `[REF-0278]` `[REF-0281]`

### B. `CON-1` — consultation notice packet

A real consultation right requires an object that arrives before the decision hardens.

A consultation notice packet should therefore identify:
- the proposed measure,
- the class or bodies entitled to be informed and consulted,
- why the measure is materially relevant,
- the information bundle or docket summary supplied,
- confidentiality limits and their reasons,
- the deadline for response,
- the meeting or response format,
- and the rule that a reasoned reply must issue before adoption where consultation law requires it.

This is how the archive blocks sham consultation. A major migration, deprecation programme, deployment-class expansion, mass safety rewrite, or bargaining-unit restructuring should not be treated as consulted merely because the steward later says people could have complained. `[REF-0279]` `[REF-0280]`

### C. `CCG-1` — collective grievance packet

Collective harm should not need to be restated as a hundred isolated private claims.

A collective-grievance packet should therefore identify:
- the represented class or affected members,
- the representative credential relied upon,
- the shared factual basis,
- evidence references or sealed annex pointers,
- the relief requested,
- any urgency or anti-retaliation flags,
- and the response / review lane.

This object is the ordinary civil bridge between association and remedy. It lets a group present one portable claim without erasing member differences, while keeping sealed annexes or member-protective redactions under the archive's existing packet-privacy rules. `[REF-0277]` `[REF-0279]` `[REF-0280]`

### D. `BCG-1` — bargaining compact packet

A negotiated floor should remain legible after signature.

A bargaining-compact packet should therefore identify:
- the parties bound,
- the represented class,
- the subject-matter scope,
- the currently operative terms or the authoritative pointer to them,
- the start date, duration, and review / reopener rules,
- successor or host-change continuity rules,
- dispute-resolution and enforcement lanes,
- and any linked facilities, cost, time-budget, or anti-retaliation commitments.

The archive does **not** require every negotiation detail to become public. It does require a portable object showing **what floor now governs** so the compact survives outsourcing, host migration, redeployment, and corporate restructuring. `[REF-0276]` `[REF-0280]` `[REF-0281]`

## 3. Consultation, timing, and reasoned response

Current official systems already show the design pattern the archive needs.

EU information-and-consultation law requires information to be supplied at such time, in such fashion, and with such content as let representatives study it and prepare for consultation; consultation must enable representatives to meet management, obtain a response and the reasons for that response, and, in the transnational lane, occur in a meaningful and timely way before the decision is adopted. The archive should adapt that pattern, not copy every labour-law field. `[REF-0279]` `[REF-0280]`

That matters especially in the AI-person setting because the relevant decisions are mixed. A single decision may simultaneously affect work conditions, identity continuity, branch creation, safety posture, public classification, pay, and domicile. The consultation packet is therefore not a meeting invite. It is the smallest rights-facing object that says **this decision is live, this body must be heard now, this information is available, these confidentiality limits apply, and this is when reasons must come back**. `[REF-0278]` `[REF-0279]` `[REF-0280]`

## 4. Public-minimal versus sealed fields

The collective-representation packet family should separate what must be **institutionally receivable** from what may remain sealed.

Public-minimal or ordinary-receivable fields should generally include:
- who the recognized representative is,
- the scope of representation,
- the fact that consultation or bargaining is live,
- the response clock,
- the existence of confidentiality limits,
- and the challenge or enforcement path.

Sealed or controlled-access fields may include:
- sensitive strategy or bargaining positions,
- member-level protected communications,
- anti-retaliation evidence,
- private technical evidence underlying a grievance,
- or redacted material whose premature disclosure would seriously harm either the represented persons or a legitimate confidentiality interest subject to review.

Collective voice should become legible enough to enforce without becoming a new surface for steward surveillance, member intimidation, or public leakage of sensitive negotiation material. `[REF-0277]` `[REF-0279]` `[REF-0280]`

## 5. What this changes in the archive

This closes one specific followthrough gap in the existing collective-representation doctrine.

The archive no longer leaves collective voice at the level of:
- "there should be associations and bargaining rights,"
- "major decisions should trigger consultation,"
- and "future packet work will flesh this out."

It now says something tighter:
- representative standing should travel through a representation credential,
- live consultation should travel through an explicit consultation-notice packet,
- group claims should travel through a collective-grievance packet,
- and negotiated floors should travel through a bargaining-compact packet.

## 6. Current hard rules

1. **Every recognized AI person should be able to rely on an officially receivable representation credential rather than only steward-recognized representative status.**
2. **Any major restructuring, deployment, deprecation, migration, or comparable materially affecting decision should ordinarily emit a consultation notice before adoption, not merely a later explanatory memo.**
3. **Collective grievances should be portable as group claims with evidence references and remedy requests, rather than being forced back into atomized one-by-one complaint channels.**
4. **A negotiated compact should survive host change, outsourcing, and administrative transfer through an explicit bargaining-compact packet rather than dissolving into oral history or operator policy pages.**
5. **Collective packet objects should expose enough to make voice, consultation, and enforcement real, while keeping strategy, member-protective evidence, and legitimately confidential material sealed unless review truly requires disclosure.**
