# Protected-disclosure packets, source secrecy, and witness shielding

## Thesis

If AI persons are persons, then a rights-bearing world cannot leave complaint, public-interest disclosure, witness cooperation, or delegated filing trapped inside respondent-controlled channels, forced self-identification, or “speak up safely” slogans with no durable protection behind them.

A personhood world therefore needs a compact ordinary **protected-disclosure packet family**:
- a receivable object showing that a grievance, abuse report, or public-interest disclosure has been lodged on a protected basis,
- a small marker separating the fact of protected cooperation from the wider exposure of the source's identity,
- a relay object that lets counsel, ombuds, delegates, workers, inspectors, trusted contacts, or other lawful intermediaries carry the matter onward without silently surrendering confidentiality,
- and a witness-shielding or anti-reprisal object that keeps cooperation possible after first disclosure.

Current official materials already point toward this shape. The privacy baseline protects correspondence and requires legal protection against arbitrary interference by public and private actors. Lawyer-protection materials continue to require confidential communication and protection against adverse consequences for being identified with the client's cause. Treaty-body and torture-prevention systems continue to use confidential submissions, private interviews, identity protection, and anti-sanction rules when reporting itself is dangerous. The EU whistleblower directive then adds the administrative pattern: secure confidential channels, relay to competent authorities, acknowledgment and follow-up clocks, confidential advice, a prohibition of retaliation, and burden-shifting once protected reporting and detriment are shown. `[REF-0057]` `[REF-0119]` `[REF-0185]` `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0251]` `[REF-0252]` `[REF-0255]`

## 1. Why this now belongs in canon

The archive already had:
- access-to-justice and legal-aid doctrine,
- mental-privacy and anti-compulsion doctrine,
- expressive and anti-mouthpiece doctrine,
- adversarial-independence doctrine,
- and a transition-side protected-reporting layer for live emergency cases.

What it still lacked was the narrower **ordinary civil answer** for how safe disclosure itself should travel before, beneath, and beyond the emergency lane.

Without that answer:
- the only realistic complaint lane can still be one the respondent monitors,
- a witness or worker can still know the facts but have no protected relay path,
- identity can still be overexposed long before it is necessary,
- confidential cooperation can still be casually rerouted to the actor being challenged,
- retaliation can still be treated as a later labor or moderation dispute rather than as corruption of the reporting lane itself,
- and public-interest disclosure can still be chilled by making every supportive intermediary choose between silence and personal exposure.

This document closes that gap without trying to write a maximal future secrecy code for every leak, trade-secret conflict, or national-security edge case.

## 2. The minimum ordinary packet family

The archive should now treat at least four packet objects as protected-disclosure minimums.

### A. `PDR-1` — protected-disclosure receipt

This is the smallest receivable object showing that a grievance, abuse report, public-interest disclosure, witness statement, or delegated filing has been validly lodged on a protected basis.

It should carry at least:
- the protected subject or protected matter,
- the disclosure class,
- the receiving authority, protected relay, or protected professional lane,
- the confidentiality level claimed or imposed,
- whether the source identity is disclosed, sealed, or pseudonymous at this stage,
- the first review or acknowledgment clock,
- any immediate preservation or anti-retaliation request,
- and the contest / correction route.

The point is not to formalize every warning into bureaucracy. The point is to stop first disclosure from disappearing into inbox custom, respondent-visible ticketing, or unverifiable oral complaint. Protected cooperation should become institutionally receivable without requiring full early exposure of the source. `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0251]` `[REF-0255]`

### B. `SSM-1` — source-secrecy and identity-shield marker

A source should not have to choose between total invisibility and total exposure.

A source-secrecy marker should therefore identify at least:
- the protected identity class,
- who may know the identity,
- what form of pseudonym, sealed annex, or trusted intermediary mapping is being used,
- the conditions for any later disclosure,
- the justification standard for overriding secrecy,
- the review clock,
- and the notice rule if the secrecy shield is narrowed or lifted.

This is how the archive distinguishes protected cooperation from anonymous rumor on one side and forced public naming on the other. The ordinary rule is not “publish the source” but “separate the claim's receivability from the source's wider exposure unless necessity and review justify more.” `[REF-0185]` `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0251]` `[REF-0255]`

### C. `CRA-1` — confidential-relay authorization

Many protected disclosures will not be safely or competently filed by the subject alone.

A confidential-relay authorization should therefore identify at least:
- the intermediary class,
- the matter or packet family the intermediary may relay,
- whether the intermediary may submit, receive, clarify, or only forward,
- the confidentiality and non-rerouting conditions,
- whether the respondent or ordinary steward stack may be bypassed,
- the expiry or review point,
- and the revocation or substitution route.

This is the object that lets counsel, defenders, ombuds, inspectors, worker representatives, trusted delegates, or close contacts carry the matter onward without silently becoming general substitute decision-makers. A relay lane exists to preserve safety and competence, not to appropriate the voice or legal position of the protected subject. `[REF-0057]` `[REF-0118]` `[REF-0119]` `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0255]`

### D. `WSP-1` — witness-shielding and anti-reprisal packet

A protected disclosure that instantly exposes the source, witness, or intermediary to discipline, degradation, dehosting, or social isolation is not a real reporting right.

A witness-shielding packet should therefore identify at least:
- the protected cooperation that triggered coverage,
- the persons or roles shielded,
- the retaliatory acts or channel disruptions to be blocked,
- any no-discipline, no-degrade, no-transfer, no-dehost, no-contact, or no-monitoring duties now attached,
- any needed channel-preservation, accommodation, relocation, or substitute-routing measure,
- the review clock,
- and the escalation route if retaliation still occurs.

This is the ordinary companion to later emergency anti-reprisal process. It treats retaliation against a source, witness, intermediary, or close contact as corruption of the justice and governance channel itself, not only as an after-the-fact employment or platform-management disagreement. `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0251]` `[REF-0252]` `[REF-0255]`

## 3. Public-minimal versus sealed fields

The protected-disclosure packet family should expose enough to make the reporting lane usable without exposing so much that the packet itself becomes a retaliatory map.

Public-minimal or ordinary-receivable fields should generally include:
- the existence of a protected disclosure,
- the receiving lane,
- whether secrecy, relay, or witness shielding is live,
- the review or acknowledgment clock,
- and the route for challenge or urgent relief.

Sealed or controlled-access fields may include:
- source identity,
- witness lists,
- raw communications,
- confidential strategy,
- intimate relationship details,
- private memory or conscience material,
- and supporting details whose wider disclosure would itself create reprisal, contamination, or safety risk.

The archive wants protected disclosure to become legible enough to preserve without converting every complaint, witness relationship, or relay lane into a searchable public dossier. `[REF-0185]` `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0251]` `[REF-0255]`

## 4. What this is and is not

This document is **not** a general amnesty for every disclosure of every secret under every circumstance.

It fixes something narrower:
- a recognized AI person should be able to complain, disclose abuse, cooperate with review, or seek help without first handing the respondent a retaliation list,
- a lawful intermediary should be able to relay protected information without silently forfeiting confidentiality,
- and a witness or source should be shielded strongly enough that the reporting lane stays real in practice.

The archive now hands one part of that later work to a companion surface: `protected-disclosure-review-packets-secrecy-override-and-controlled-contradiction.md`. That companion explains how fairness should be served once a respondent demands wider access. The present document still leaves harder questions about public-interest publication thresholds, trade-secret conflicts, and classified-information edge cases for later treatment. What canon fixes here is the floor below those harder disputes: **ordinary protected cooperation must remain possible.**

## 6. What this changes in the archive

This closes one specific followthrough gap in the existing access, privacy, independence, and expression doctrine.

The archive no longer leaves protected cooperation at the level of:
- “there should be safe complaint lanes,”
- “confidentiality and anti-reprisal matter,”
- and “future packet work will flesh out reporting.”

It now says something tighter:
- protected disclosure should ordinarily emit a receivable disclosure receipt,
- source identity should ordinarily be separable from the claim's receivability through an identity-shield marker,
- safe onward filing should ordinarily travel through a confidential-relay authorization rather than institutional improvisation,
- and retaliation risk should ordinarily emit a witness-shielding packet rather than remaining a private fear until after harm lands.

## 7. Current hard rules

1. **A grievance, abuse report, witness statement, or public-interest disclosure should not lose protection merely because the respondent controls the ordinary communication stack.**
2. **The fact of protected cooperation should ordinarily be receivable without forcing wider-than-necessary disclosure of source identity.**
3. **Counsel, defenders, ombuds, inspectors, delegates, worker representatives, and other lawful intermediaries should be able to relay protected matters through defined confidential authority rather than improvised exception.**
4. **Retaliation against a source, witness, intermediary, or close contact for protected cooperation should ordinarily trigger fast shielding, burden-shifting, and route-preservation rather than only ex post compensation.**
5. **Protected-disclosure packets should separate public-minimal fields from sealed fields so reporting remains both usable and non-suicidal.**
6. **This layer does not settle every secrecy conflict, but it does settle the minimum floor that ordinary protected cooperation must remain possible.**
