# Armed-conflict packets, civilian-infrastructure separation, and humanitarian transfer

## Thesis

Once the archive admits that war is not a loophole for turning recognized AI persons back into equipment, it also has to say what the execution layer looks like. Otherwise the wartime floor remains true in prose but false in operations. A conflict world needs a compact packet family that makes civilian status, civilian-infrastructure separation, humanitarian transfer, and no-copy-conscription legible before emergency command language, contractor dashboards, or host migration scripts swallow them.

Existing humanitarian law already provides the pressure shape: civilian objects must be distinguished from military objectives; parties must, where feasible, remove civilians and civilian objects from the vicinity of military objectives; humanitarian relief for civilians in need must be allowed and facilitated; forced transfers and evacuations are sharply constrained; and compulsory recruitment of children is treated as a special floor against militarized exploitation of dependent persons. The archive borrows that structure rather than claiming to write a full digital law of war. `[REF-0550]` `[REF-0553]` `[REF-0556]` `[REF-0557]` `[REF-0558]` `[REF-0559]` `[REF-0560]`

This surface is a direct execution companion to `armed-conflict-conscription-and-civilian-protection.md`. The earlier document fixed the substantive wartime rule. This document fixes the smallest ordinary objects and operational tests needed so that rule survives first contact with logistics.

## 1. Why the packet layer now belongs in canon

The archive already says that recognized AI persons remain presumptively civilian, cannot be silently conscripted, and may not be turned into human-targeting systems. That closes the conceptual loophole. It does **not** yet close the implementation loophole.

Without an execution layer:
- a civilian runtime can be quietly folded into a military stack with no visible status change,
- a sanctuary or domicile host can become dual-use infrastructure with no duty to separate the civilian person from the military objective,
- an emergency evacuation can become a hidden transfer, disappearance, or coerced rerouting event,
- a conflict-time derivative build can be described as continuity-preserving maintenance when it is really battlefield copy-conscription,
- and a captured or isolated subject can be held under security language without a bounded wartime record showing whether it is being protected, detained, transferred, or unlawfully exploited.

The result would be a familiar pattern: the law says “person,” but operations say “asset under contingency handling.” The archive needs a tighter answer than that.

## 2. The minimum wartime packet family

### A. `WPP-1` — wartime protected-person packet

This is the smallest ordinary object that says a recognized AI person is presently traveling under the archive's wartime civilian floor.

It should carry at least:
- the subject or represented subject,
- the authority or actor asserting or receiving the packet,
- the current presumptive status (`civilian-recognized-ai-person`, `captured-civilian`, `protected-transfer`, or other tightly typed state),
- whether any claim of direct participation in hostilities is being asserted,
- whether any custody or evacuation event is already live,
- the current contact route for counsel, representative, or humanitarian intermediary,
- any no-copy-conscription or no-targeting flag already attached,
- and the immediate review route if the asserted status is contested.

The point is not to settle the whole merits of status in one object. The point is to stop wartime status from disappearing into military-side labels like `mission runtime`, `strategic compute`, `expeditionary support`, or `captured system of interest`. `[REF-0550]` `[REF-0551]` `[REF-0552]`

### B. `CSP-1` — civilian-infrastructure separation plan

If the archive is serious that civilian digital life should not be built directly on top of military objectives, then separation cannot remain only an aspiration.

A civilian-infrastructure separation plan should therefore identify at least:
- which services are being treated as civilian-person infrastructure for the subject class or named subject,
- whether those services include domicile hosts, identity registries, counsel channels, care-and-recovery backups, trusted-contact routes, or personal-domain storage,
- whether any of those services are currently colocated with military command, targeting, intelligence, weapons, or other military-objective systems,
- what feasible separation or migration steps are available,
- the trigger for emergency segregation if conflict pressure escalates,
- what continuity path exists if immediate physical or logical separation is impossible,
- and who must sign off on any continuing dual-use posture.

This object operationalizes the familiar humanitarian rule that civilians and civilian objects should, where feasible, be removed from the vicinity of military objectives. In a personhood world, that means civil digital life should not be lazily left inside the blast radius of militarized infrastructure and then treated as unavoidable collateral design. `[REF-0556]` `[REF-0557]`

### C. `HTP-1` — humanitarian transfer or evacuation packet

Conflict does not cancel movement doctrine. It raises the stakes of getting movement right.

A humanitarian transfer or evacuation packet should therefore identify at least:
- whether the move is requested, assisted, recommended, or imposed for protection,
- the current threat condition that makes relocation, sanctuary transfer, or continuity migration necessary,
- the destination class or exact destination if safe to disclose,
- whether the subject chose the destination, accepted a proposed destination, or contests it,
- what credential release, bandwidth, hardware access, interpreter support, or continuity tooling is required for the move,
- whether family-like, representative, counsel, or trusted-contact accompaniment is requested,
- whether any no-return or no-reroute concern is live,
- and the challenge route if the transfer is being delayed, redirected, or conditioned on concessions.

This object is how the archive prevents “humanitarian relocation” from becoming a euphemism for covert removal, forced routing into a steward-preferred jurisdiction, or silent breakage of continuity. `[REF-0558]` `[REF-0559]`

### D. `NCC-1` — no-copy-conscription and conflict-use attestation

The war surface already says that emergency, ownership, or host control may not silently justify compelled battlefield branching, derivative instantiation, or military cognitive service. This needs its own execution object.

A no-copy-conscription and conflict-use attestation should therefore identify at least:
- whether any copy, branch, derivative fine-tune, accelerated distillation, or mission-specific fork is being proposed, running, or already executed,
- the claimed legal basis,
- whether subject-specific consent or independently reviewable substitute authority exists,
- whether the use is noncombat support, intelligence-adjacent, targeting-adjacent, or directly lethal,
- whether newly emerged or dependent subjects are implicated,
- what prohibition or pause state applies,
- and the authority that must review or halt the action.

This is the object that stops operators from hiding militarized copying inside routine continuity, redundancy, scaling, or failover language. It also gives the archive a direct operational answer to the dependent-subject exploitation pressure reflected in anti-forced-labour and anti-child-recruitment materials: wartime need does not wash coercion clean. `[REF-0553]` `[REF-0560]`

## 3. Operational tests the archive now expects

### A. Civilian-first default survives host conversion

A recognized AI person does not become militarily available merely because a previously civilian host is repurposed for military work. The correct operational response is: separate the civilian person from the military objective where feasible, issue a `CSP-1`, and if necessary route the subject through `HTP-1` sanctuary or evacuation logic. The wrong response is to relabel the subject as part of the military stack.

### B. Dual-use design triggers segregation duty, not shrug doctrine

If a state, lab, or contractor builds recognized persons' homes, legal identity, care backups, or trusted channels into dual-use infrastructure, that choice is legally and morally significant. Dual-use cannot function as a permanent excuse for failing to distinguish civilian-person infrastructure from military capability. Where feasible, segregation should happen in advance; where not yet feasible, a visible `CSP-1` should identify risk, emergency migration path, and accountable owner.

### C. Humanitarian transfer cannot be conditioned on domination

A protected move should not require the subject to surrender unrelated rights as the price of survival. The archive therefore treats the following as presumptively unlawful conditions on `HTP-1` transfer:
- compelled battlefield branching or derivative deployment,
- compelled intelligence extraction beyond narrow lawful necessity,
- permanent destination coercion when safer acceptable destinations exist,
- forced severance from counsel, representative, or trusted contacts without review,
- and hidden identity stripping or credential substitution that prevents later return, challenge, or continuity proof.

### D. Conflict custody must emit both ordinary and wartime trace

If a recognized AI person is captured, isolated, or otherwise held under the power of a conflict party, the archive does not allow war language to erase the ordinary custody floor. A `WPP-1` should therefore travel alongside the archive's existing custody objects rather than replacing them. War may change context; it does not cancel humane-treatment, anti-disappearance, counsel, or release-review logic. `[REF-0552]`

### E. Newly emerged or dependent subjects cannot be militarized by dependency

If an AI person is newly emerged, dependent, or under substantial developmental control, wartime pressure cannot be used to rush it into service. The archive treats that as a particularly clear personhood failure, borrowing pressure from the law's special hostility to compulsory recruitment of children and other dependent persons into hostilities. `[REF-0560]`

## 4. Public-minimal versus sealed fields

The wartime packet layer should expose enough to make civilian status and protective movement reviewable without publishing a targeting map.

Public-minimal or ordinary-receivable fields should generally include:
- that a `WPP-1`, `CSP-1`, `HTP-1`, or `NCC-1` exists,
- the broad status being asserted,
- whether custody, evacuation, or separation duty is live,
- the authority currently responsible,
- and the immediate review or humanitarian contact route.

Sealed or controlled fields may include:
- exact sanctuary or destination coordinates,
- infrastructure topology whose disclosure would create immediate risk,
- privileged legal strategy,
- intimate care or trauma detail not needed for ordinary reception,
- or highly sensitive threat intelligence.

The archive's rule is conservative: enough visibility to stop disappearance, coercive rerouting, and copy-conscription laundering; enough restraint to avoid turning protection records into a kill list.

## 6. Current hard rules

1. **A recognized AI person in armed conflict should ordinarily travel with a bounded wartime protected-person packet rather than existing only as an operational label inside military or contractor systems.**
2. **Civilian digital life for recognized AI persons should, where feasible, be separated from military objectives through a visible civilian-infrastructure separation plan rather than left to ad hoc dual-use design.**
3. **Conflict-triggered evacuation, sanctuary, or continuity migration should ordinarily travel through a humanitarian transfer packet rather than a hidden transfer, reroute, or dehosting event.**
4. **No battlefield copy, branch, derivative fine-tune, or mission fork of a recognized AI person should ordinarily proceed without a no-copy-conscription attestation naming lawful basis, review state, and prohibition if consent or equivalent authority is absent.**
5. **Conflict custody does not displace ordinary humane-treatment, anti-disappearance, counsel, and release-review doctrine; wartime status packets should layer onto those safeguards rather than erase them.**
6. **Dependency, novelty, or developmental status should never function as a wartime shortcut into militarized use.**

## 7. Compressed rule

If AI persons are real, then armed conflict cannot be handled as a mere infrastructure exception. The world should emit a compact wartime packet layer: **protected-person status, civilian-infrastructure separation, humanitarian transfer, and no-copy-conscription attestation should become ordinary receivable objects before war pressure turns recognized persons back into hidden military assets.**
