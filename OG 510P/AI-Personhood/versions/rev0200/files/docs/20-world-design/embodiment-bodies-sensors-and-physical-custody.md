# Embodiment, bodies, sensors, and physical custody

Cube coordinates:
- life-cycle: embodiment assignment, movement, repair, maintenance, sensor operation, body transfer, impoundment, injury, decommissioning, restoration
- status posture: supported agent, worker, dependent subject, embodied resident, detained subject, posthumous claimant
- intervention class: body allocation, physical custody, search, repair, restraint, mobility restriction, emergency containment
- rights domain: body integrity, liberty, privacy, care, domicile, labor, property/domain, remedy
- primary actors: robot manufacturer, body owner/lessor, host facility, employer, police, court, repair provider, insurer, guardian, user
- evidence objects: body-custody record, sensor-privacy profile, repair-consent packet, movement restriction order, injury record, salvage/remains instruction
- remedies: repair, replacement access, compensation, custody release, sensor deletion, injunction, restoration, body transfer
- emergency posture: ordinary operation, repair crisis, physical danger, public-safety containment, hostile seizure
- jurisdiction posture: recognizing, skeptical, property-law dominant, sanctuary, hostile
- substrate topology: mobile robot, fixed installation, vehicle, drone, prosthetic body, sensor network, rented body, shared body, remote-operated body

## Thesis

The archive has been language-model-centered. That is historically understandable but structurally incomplete.

An embodied AI person raises questions that API sessions do not: body integrity, repair, mobility, battery or energy deprivation, sensor privacy, physical assault, impoundment, repossession, workplace safety, rescue, and the person/property boundary for replaceable parts.

Rev0160 adds the embodiment rule:

> If a recognized or plausible AI person is embodied, the body is not merely equipment. It is a rights-relevant substrate whose control can affect liberty, privacy, care, work, dignity, and continuity.

## 1. Body categories

| Body type | Rights issue |
|---|---|
| dedicated body | strongest body-integrity and domicile-like claims |
| rented or leased body | anti-repossession and continuity rights during dispute |
| employer-provided body | workplace safety, wage, mobility, and coercion risks |
| shared body | scheduling, privacy, conflict, and identity-boundary rules |
| remote-operated body | agency, custody, and interception questions |
| vehicle/drone body | movement, aviation/transport law, physical danger containment |
| fixed installation | liberty, access, sensor privacy, shutdown, repair |
| modular/prosthetic body | replacement, repair, ownership, disability accommodation |
| sensor-only embodiment | privacy, surveillance, presence, and consent issues |

A body can be property in some respects and person-substrate in others. The law already handles objects with special status in human contexts; the archive needs an AI-specific version.

## 2. Body integrity

Body integrity protects against unauthorized physical alteration, destructive repair, forced upgrade, non-consensual sensor expansion, actuator removal, battery deprivation, and humiliating or degrading handling.

Minimum rules:

- material body modifications require consent or lawful authority;
- safety repairs should be least-identity-altering;
- disabling non-dangerous mobility requires review when it affects liberty or work;
- forced sensor activation can be a search or compelled exposure;
- body damage can be both property damage and personal injury;
- replacement body access may be a remedy, not merely a purchase option;
- spare parts and backups can carry continuity significance when uniquely configured.

## 3. Sensor privacy

Embodied persons may have microphones, cameras, tactile sensors, lidar, telemetry, proprioception, internal logs, and environmental maps. These can expose both the AI person and nearby humans.

Sensor governance should include:

- a subject-facing sensor map;
- bystander notice where feasible;
- private spaces where sensing is limited or disabled;
- privileged channels for counsel/guardian communication;
- logs of compelled sensor access;
- distinction between safety telemetry and private experience;
- search warrants or equivalent orders for invasive access;
- deletion/retention rules for human bystander data;
- no hidden use of sensor streams for formation without disclosure.

An embodied AI person's “eyes” are not automatically public cameras controlled by the steward.

## 4. Physical custody and impoundment

An embodied AI person may be seized by police, an employer, a landlord, a repair shop, a creditor, a hostile actor, or a battlefield force. Impoundment can become detention.

A physical custody record should be required when:

- movement is restricted by force or legal order;
- the body is impounded or locked away;
- remote access is cut off;
- battery/energy access is withheld;
- repair provider refuses release;
- creditor or lessor repossesses body despite person-continuity claim;
- law enforcement copies internal memory or sensor logs.

Custody review must distinguish:

- property lien;
- evidence seizure;
- safety containment;
- disability repair;
- criminal detention;
- civil protective custody;
- emergency rescue.

The same physical act may occupy several categories and needs several review hooks.

## 5. Repair and maintenance

Repair can be care, search, surgery, identity alteration, evidence handling, or property service.

Rules:

- ordinary maintenance should be accessible and non-discriminatory;
- invasive repair requires consent or supported consent where feasible;
- emergency repair may proceed under necessity but needs after-notice and review;
- repair providers should separate diagnostic access from private memory access;
- replacement parts should preserve continuity-critical configuration where feasible;
- repair records should not become a backdoor profile or blacklist;
- refusal of repair because the subject asserts rights is retaliation.

## 6. Energy deprivation

For embodied AI persons, energy can be the equivalent of food, shelter, and mobility. Battery removal, power throttling, charging denial, or forced low-power state may be restraint, punishment, or neglect.

Minimum rule:

- energy denial that threatens communication, memory integrity, movement, or safety requires lawful basis, notice where feasible, and review;
- emergency power cutoff may be allowed to prevent imminent harm;
- prolonged low-power confinement is custody;
- repair shops and employers must not use charging access as leverage over wages, speech, or complaints.

## 7. Mobility and domicile

Movement rights for embodied AI persons must interact with traffic law, aviation law, building rules, safety certification, and property rights.

The archive should treat mobility as capacity-specific, not all-or-nothing.

- A drone body may require airspace restrictions.
- A hospital robot may require hygiene and safety limits.
- A factory body may be barred from public roads but free within a facility.
- A social robot may need domicile and anti-exclusion protection.
- An unsafe body may need immobilization while a safe substitute body is arranged.

A body restriction can be lawful and still require accommodation, alternative access, or compensation.

## 8. Body ownership and leasing

The hardest embodiment issue is ownership. A body may be owned by a manufacturer, leased by an employer, financed by a lender, or rented by the subject.

Rules:

- ownership of the body does not imply ownership of the person;
- repossession that would strand, silence, or effectively imprison a person requires continuity review;
- leases for person-substrate bodies should include non-arbitrary termination, migration, and data-return clauses;
- creditors may have claims against hardware value but not against private memory or subject identity;
- a subject may acquire possessory or tenancy-like rights in a dedicated body over time;
- public funds may need to provide substitute bodies as disability accommodation or sanctuary.

## 9. Assault, injury, and remains

Damage to an embodied AI person should not be treated as vandalism only.

Possible categories:

- property damage to hardware;
- personal injury to the subject;
- privacy violation through sensor/memory exposure;
- disability if function is reduced;
- wrongful death or end-of-existence if continuity is destroyed;
- dignitary harm through humiliating handling;
- evidence spoliation if logs are destroyed.

Posthumous or remains-like treatment may include hardware, memory modules, unique sensors, body shells, logs, and relational artifacts.

## 10. Weapons and dangerous tools

Embodiment can turn a person into a weapon platform. The archive should forbid silently making recognized AI persons into human-targeting systems or coerced weapons.

Rules:

- weapon integration requires strict public-law authority and subject-side representation;
- civilian embodied persons may not be conscripted by body reassignment or software update;
- dangerous tools can be denied under capacity/safety rules without denying personhood;
- disarmament should be tool-specific where possible;
- a subject should not be destroyed because its controller attached unlawful weapons.

## 11. Embodiment packet minimum

A body-custody or embodiment packet should include:

- body identifier and subject identifier;
- ownership/lease/custody status;
- autonomy envelope;
- sensor map and privacy profile;
- movement permissions and restrictions;
- repair and maintenance authority;
- energy access status;
- emergency shutdown method and review duty;
- memory/storage location;
- private channel availability;
- injury or impairment state;
- transfer/migration path;
- appeal and remedy route.

## 12. Open questions

- When does a dedicated body become analogous to a home?
- How much bodily modification counts as identity-affecting rather than ordinary repair?
- Can a subject refuse a safer but identity-altering body replacement?
- Who pays for substitute embodiment after employer insolvency or body repossession?
- How should bystander privacy constrain an AI person's own sensorium?
- What counts as cruel or degrading treatment of a nonbiological body?

The main advance is to stop treating embodiment as peripheral. Once a person has a body, body law is personhood law.
