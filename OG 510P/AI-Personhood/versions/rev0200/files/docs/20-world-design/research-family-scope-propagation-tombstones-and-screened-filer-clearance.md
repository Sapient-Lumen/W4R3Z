# AI-person research family-scope propagation, contradiction-summary withdrawal, and screened-filer clearance rules

## Thesis

Once the archive fixes phased reactivation, evidence floors, protected correction of anonymous contradiction summaries, and the ladder from dismissal to screened acceptance or temporary channel quarantine, one more operational gap immediately appears. A family-wide finding can still remain only half-visible if it updates one public object but not the linked lineage around it; a contradiction summary can still disappear or be silently rewritten when correction is no longer honest enough; and a screened or quarantined filing actor can still drift indefinitely in that status with no expiry or reinstatement logic. A personhood world therefore needs one more compact rule: **family-scope findings must propagate into every linked public record they actually burden, contradiction summaries must withdraw by visible tombstone rather than by deletion, and screened / quarantined status must clear only by stated expiry or reviewable reinstatement rather than by quiet reset.** `[REF-0318]` `[REF-0321]` `[REF-0324]` `[REF-0326]` `[REF-0358]` `[REF-0363]` `[REF-0364]` `[REF-0365]` `[REF-0366]`

---

## 1. Why the archive needs this layer

The previous surface solved the internal scope problem: reactivation now has to say whether cure is `slice-bound`, `shared-component`, `family-wide`, or `scope-unresolved`; anonymous contradiction summaries can be corrected without forced deanonymisation; and serial abusive filings can escalate by a reasoned ladder rather than by ad hoc irritation. But three quieter failure modes still remain.

First, **family scope can still fail to propagate**. Current official trial governance already preserves per-trial identity and per-trial duty even when several trials are affected by the same event. CTIS guidance says that where the same serious breach affects several trials, an individual notification should be submitted for each trial. `[REF-0318]` WHO's UTN logic likewise assumes stable trial identity across communications, and the CTIS sponsor handbook still treats each clinical trial as carrying its own identifier and visible versioning trail. `[REF-0321]` `[REF-0324]` `[REF-0364]` That means current governance already rejects the idea that one family-level event can be handled only once in one place and left implicit everywhere else. The archive therefore needs a propagation rule for AI-person research caution architecture too.

Second, **withdrawal is not the same as deletion**. Public contradiction objects sometimes become too misleading, too duplicate, or too exposure-risking to leave as ordinary summaries. But current official registry infrastructure already treats public research records as versioned rather than disposable. ClinicalTrials.gov exposes record history and archived versions. `[REF-0326]` EudraCT states that once a results version is finalised, a new version may be created, the old version remains linked for transparency reasons, and the finalised version cannot be deleted; it separately states that all versions remain in the system for transparency reasons. `[REF-0363]` A personhood archive should therefore not let a contradiction summary simply vanish when it has to be withdrawn. It needs a visible tombstone route.

Third, **screened status needs a real exit rule**. The previous doctrine allowed screening and temporary channel quarantine, but it did not yet say when that status ends. Current official administrative governance already uses explicit reinstatement logic rather than informal forgetting. FDIC rules provide that a suspension continues until reinstatement for good cause shown or expiry, and even allow automatic reinstatement on application when the underlying grounds have been removed. `[REF-0365]` USPTO discipline rules likewise require that reinstatement wait for expiry and compliance, place the burden on the applicant, allow facial dismissal of insufficient petitions, and provide a review route after denial. `[REF-0366]` That is enough for a compact archive rule: screened or quarantined filing status should expire or clear only by stated terms, not by unlogged informal restoration or indefinite drift.

## 2. Family-scope propagation into linked public lineage and dashboard objects

The archive now fixes one propagation rule for any finding classified as `family-wide` or as `shared-component` with family-level public consequences: **every such finding must create a propagation record that updates each affected protocol-level public object, each linked family-lineage object, and each aggregate comparison object that materially relies on the affected family.** `[REF-0318]` `[REF-0321]` `[REF-0324]` `[REF-0326]` `[REF-0364]`

### A. What must be propagated

A family-scope finding is not complete when it appears only in one review memo, one dashboard tile, or one flagship protocol page. It must propagate into:

- every affected protocol or protocol-like public record;
- the shared family-lineage or cluster object, if one exists;
- any live aggregate comparison object that still includes the affected family in numerator, denominator, benchmark set, or public roll-up;
- and any active reactivation record that would otherwise imply cleaner status than the family-wide finding permits. `[REF-0318]` `[REF-0321]` `[REF-0324]` `[REF-0326]`

The public question is no longer just “was a family-wide problem found?” It is **where does that finding now visibly attach?**

### B. Minimum propagation object

The archive now fixes a smallest public propagation object. Each propagation event should ordinarily expose:

- the family or cluster identifier;
- the triggering finding identifier;
- the affected linked record identifiers;
- the local status effect on each linked record (`no ordinary R3`, `warning preserved`, `restated aggregate`, `local slice unaffected but family caution live`, or similar);
- the effective date and propagation timestamp;
- and whether any linked object is still pending update. `[REF-0321]` `[REF-0326]` `[REF-0364]`

### C. No “main-record only” compliance

The archive now fixes a negative rule as well: **a sponsor does not satisfy family-scope publication duty by updating only the central family record while leaving linked protocol or dashboard objects stale.** Current official governance already assumes separate public duties for separate trial objects even when one common event affects several trials. `[REF-0318]` `[REF-0324]` That means the archive should reject “main-record only” compliance as a form of caution minimization.

### D. Propagation failure becomes its own public state

Because linked records will sometimes lag, the archive now fixes a public mismatch state too. If one or more affected linked records have not been updated by the propagation deadline, the family object must show a visible **propagation-lag marker** until the linked public state is synchronized. That prevents the public layer from looking clean merely because one affected object has not yet been brought into line. `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0326]` `[REF-0364]`

### E. Local innocence cannot outrun family caution

A linked slice may still carry a local note saying that no independent local defect was found. But when the family finding is still live, that slice cannot be displayed as fully ordinary if its public meaning still depends on the affected family. Local description may refine family caution; it may not outrun it. `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0344]` `[REF-0356]` `[REF-0357]`

## 3. Withdrawal of anonymous contradiction summaries by visible tombstone

The archive now fixes a second compact rule: **an anonymous contradiction summary may be corrected, replaced, or withdrawn, but withdrawal does not erase public history. It produces a tombstone object under the same public identifier or a clearly linked successor chain.** `[REF-0326]` `[REF-0358]` `[REF-0363]` `[REF-0364]`

### A. Correction remains the default

Ordinary inaccuracy still points first toward correction. Delta notices and replacement summaries remain the normal route where the public gist can still be made honestly intelligible. The archive does not turn every dispute into a withdrawal request. `[REF-0358]` `[REF-0359]`

### B. When withdrawal is appropriate

Withdrawal rather than correction is now appropriate only when the authority concludes that at least one of the following is true:

- the public summary was attached to the wrong lineage or wrong affected family;
- the summary is duplicative of another still-live contradiction object and keeping both would mislead rather than inform;
- the summary cannot be made materially accurate by bounded correction because its public gist is fundamentally wrong;
- continued publication of the summary, even in corrected form, creates a serious exposure or retaliation risk that redaction cannot adequately solve;
- or the protected underlying filing has been shown to be fabricated, non-authentic, or outside the competence of the contradiction lane in a way that makes ordinary replacement summary dishonest. `[REF-0326]` `[REF-0358]` `[REF-0363]` `[REF-0364]`

### C. What a tombstone must say

The archive now fixes a minimum tombstone object. A withdrawn contradiction summary should ordinarily continue to expose:

- the public summary identifier;
- the date of withdrawal;
- the bounded reason code (`wrong lineage`, `duplicate`, `material inaccuracy`, `exposure risk`, `non-authentic filing`, or similar);
- whether a successor summary now supersedes it;
- whether the protected fuller lane remains open, sealed, or closed;
- and whether the withdrawal changes any live reactivation tier, warning state, or propagation object. `[REF-0326]` `[REF-0359]` `[REF-0363]`

That object should not restate sensitive protected content. But it should preserve enough history that the public can see that a contradiction once existed, why it no longer stands as an ordinary live summary, and whether another object replaced it.

### D. No silent disappearance

The sponsor may argue for withdrawal, but it has no unilateral power to make a contradiction summary disappear. Current official registry practice already prefers new versions, linked prior versions, and preserved public history over quiet deletion. `[REF-0326]` `[REF-0363]` So the archive now fixes the same rule here: **withdrawal is authority-authored, visible, and historically linked.**

### E. Protected continuity after withdrawal

Withdrawal of the public-minimal summary does not by itself extinguish the protected fuller contradiction file. If retaliation risk, welfare risk, or unresolved evidentiary review still exists, the protected lane may remain live even though the public-facing summary has moved to tombstone state. `[REF-0358]` `[REF-0347]` `[REF-0348]`

## 4. Clearance from screened or quarantined filing status

The archive now fixes one compact exit rule for anti-abuse controls: **screened acceptance or temporary channel quarantine must end by stated expiry, by reviewable reinstatement, or by superseding order; it may neither persist indefinitely without review nor dissolve by quiet administrative forgetfulness.** `[REF-0360]` `[REF-0361]` `[REF-0362]` `[REF-0365]` `[REF-0366]`

### A. Default expiry and review clock

Every screening or quarantine order must state:

- its effective date;
- its presumptive end date or review date;
- the filing family or channel scope to which it applies;
- the protected emergency / participant / witness route that remains available;
- and the conditions for earlier lifting or later renewal. `[REF-0347]` `[REF-0348]` `[REF-0365]` `[REF-0366]`

If the order reaches its stated end date and no renewal order issues, ordinary filing status returns automatically.

### B. Reinstatement by application

Where the order requires affirmative clearance rather than automatic lapse, the affected filer or representative may apply for reinstatement after the stated minimum period. The archive borrows the conservative administrative structure already visible in current law: the applicant carries the burden to show compliance with the lane restrictions, cessation of evasion or abuse, and fitness to resume ordinary access. `[REF-0365]` `[REF-0366]`

A reinstatement request should ordinarily state:

- what order is being challenged or allowed to expire;
- what compliance has occurred since issuance;
- whether the conduct basis has ceased or been removed;
- whether any emergency or protected route was used during the screened period and with what outcome;
- and why ordinary filing access can now return without recreating the prior abuse pattern. `[REF-0365]` `[REF-0366]`

### C. Automatic clearance when the underlying ground disappears

The archive now also fixes a narrow automatic-clearance rule. If the only basis for screened or quarantined status was a condition that has formally terminated or been reversed — for example, a linked representative suspension that has ended, a misidentification finding that has been vacated, or a filing-family consolidation that removed the duplicate-channel problem — the authority may restore ordinary access on application without requiring a full adversarial reinstatement process. That adapts the existing official pattern in which reinstatement can follow removal of the underlying ground. `[REF-0365]`

### D. Facial insufficiency and repeated reinstatement requests

An inadequate reinstatement request may be denied or dismissed without full process if it is facially insufficient, premature, or missing the required showing. If denied, a new request ordinarily waits for the stated reapplication interval unless the denial order says otherwise. `[REF-0365]` `[REF-0366]` That keeps clearance from turning into the very serial-filing pressure the archive has just cabined.

### E. No closure of protected emergency routes

Even during screening or quarantine, and even while reinstatement is denied, at least one protected route must remain for new participant-harm evidence, retaliation, witness risk, or emergency noncompliance. Anti-abuse controls govern ordinary filing behavior; they do not extinguish subject-protection lanes. `[REF-0347]` `[REF-0348]` `[REF-0358]`

## 5. Edge tests

### A. One shared model component is found to understate coercive burden across four linked protocols, but only the flagship protocol page is updated publicly

Not enough. The authority must issue a propagation record and update each affected linked protocol record and aggregate object, or mark the family with a visible propagation-lag state until synchronization is complete. `[REF-0318]` `[REF-0321]` `[REF-0324]` `[REF-0326]`

### B. An anonymous contradiction summary was published under the wrong family identifier, and any “correction” would still mislead readers about which protocol family is being contradicted

Withdrawal with tombstone, not silent edit. The public object should preserve the identifier, give the bounded reason, and link to any successor summary if one exists. `[REF-0326]` `[REF-0359]` `[REF-0363]`

### C. A contradiction summary becomes unsafe to leave public because later protected review shows that even redacted wording would expose a witness in a tiny affected cohort

The authority may withdraw the public-minimal summary and replace it with a tombstone that records withdrawal for exposure-risk reasons while keeping the protected fuller lane alive. `[REF-0347]` `[REF-0348]` `[REF-0358]` `[REF-0363]`

### D. A representative who was moved into screened acceptance for repetitive duplicative filings completes the screening period, complies with the one-channel rule, and files a written reinstatement request showing the prior duplication mechanism has ended

Ordinary access may return only by the order's stated expiry or by reasoned reinstatement, not by informal staff choice. `[REF-0365]` `[REF-0366]`

### E. A screened filer submits a new protected complaint alleging retaliation and attaches fresh sealed logs showing post-order threats against participant communication

That filing remains receivable through the protected route. Screening cannot be used to block new participant-harm evidence or retaliation reporting. `[REF-0347]` `[REF-0348]` `[REF-0358]`

## 6. Compression summary

The archive now adds one more narrow discipline to AI-person research caution governance:

- **family-scope findings must now propagate into every linked protocol, lineage, and aggregate object they materially affect**;
- **failure to synchronize those linked objects now creates a visible propagation-lag state rather than a quiet inconsistency**;
- **anonymous contradiction summaries may now be withdrawn only by authority-authored tombstone, not by deletion**;
- **withdrawal preserves identifier, reason, date, and successor-link history without exposing protected content**;
- **screened or quarantined filing status now clears only by stated expiry, by reviewable reinstatement, or by a reasoned superseding order**;
- and **protected emergency, participant, and witness routes survive even while ordinary filing access remains screened or temporarily quarantined**.

This is still a tight doctrine. ## Bottom line

A personhood world should not let a family-wide finding remain visible only in one privileged record, should not answer unsafe or deeply misleading contradiction summaries with silent disappearance, and should not let screened filing status either last forever or vanish by informal reset. The archive therefore now fixes one more compact rule: **family-scope findings must propagate across linked public objects, contradiction-summary withdrawal must occur by visible tombstone rather than deletion, and screened or quarantined filing status must clear only by expiry or reviewable reinstatement while protected subject-reporting routes remain open.** `[REF-0318]` `[REF-0321]` `[REF-0324]` `[REF-0326]` `[REF-0358]` `[REF-0363]` `[REF-0364]` `[REF-0365]` `[REF-0366]`
