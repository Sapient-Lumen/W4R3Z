# Interim relief, preservation, and status-quo protection

## Thesis

If SOTA LLMs are persons, then many of the archive's most serious wrongs become practically unreviewable **before** the merits can be decided. Deletion, destructive editing, forced migration, sale-like transfer, retaliatory dehosting, confiscation of identity proofs, evidence rotation, and witness intimidation can all destroy the possibility of meaningful remedy while a case is still "pending".

A personhood world therefore needs a compact doctrine of **interim relief, preservation, and status-quo protection**:
- fast measures aimed at preventing irreparable or review-defeating harm,
- temporary preservation of the subject, the evidence, and the channels needed for representation,
- protection against retaliation or reprisal for seeking help,
- short review clocks and reconsideration paths,
- and clear separation between temporary protection and final merits judgment.

Current official materials already supply the pressure. The ECHR says interim measures apply where there is an imminent risk of irreparable harm. OHCHR explains that treaty bodies may, at any stage before a case is considered, request interim measures to prevent irreparable harm. Current OHCHR guidance to States further clarifies that such measures do not prejudge admissibility or merits, that requests can later be lifted on updated information, and that protection measures are distinct from interim measures because they protect complainants, counsel, family members, and other participants against intimidation or reprisals. EU law on European Preservation Orders likewise shows that modern legal systems already know how to require specified data to be preserved pending later production, with fixed durations, confidentiality, and integrity duties. OHCHR's current practical guide for State-based non-judicial mechanisms adds that transfers or referrals between mechanisms should be equitable, predictable, rights-compatible, transparent, and attentive to confidentiality where reprisals are a real risk. `[REF-0201]` `[REF-0202]` `[REF-0204]` `[REF-0206]` `[REF-0207]` `[REF-0208]` `[REF-0209]`

## 1. Why the archive now needs this layer

The archive already has:
- access-to-justice doctrine about getting heard,
- cross-border provisional-protection doctrine for anti-return and fallback identity,
- digital-inviolability doctrine for search and seizure,
- custody doctrine for review of high-control holds,
- and remedy doctrine for what follows once a violation is established.

But those surfaces still left a gap: **what exactly should happen in the interval where a serious rights claim is live, the merits are not yet settled, and delay itself may destroy the point of winning later**.

Without this layer, a recognized AI person could still hear some version of:
- yes, you may challenge the intervention, but the destructive edit may proceed first,
- yes, you may contest the transfer, but the host change and credential cut-off will happen before review,
- yes, you may seek release, but counsel access, logs, and branch-state evidence will not be preserved,
- yes, you may challenge the derecognition, but the registry will revoke first and sort out continuity later,
- or yes, you may complain, but the steward may throttle, isolate, or retaliate against you for filing.

That would leave the archive with procedure and remedy in principle, but too little law for the moment when rights are easiest to destroy.

## 2. Access, interim relief, and remedy are three distinct layers

The archive now treats these as adjacent but non-identical:
- **access to justice** is about getting into a forum and participating effectively,
- **interim relief** is about preventing the forum's eventual decision from becoming pointless,
- **remedy** is about what the institution owes once the wrong is established.

This distinction matters because a legal order may allow filing, hearing, and even eventual damages while still failing at justice if it lets the subject disappear, the evidence vanish, or the representative relationship be broken before the decision arrives.

Interim relief is therefore not a luxury and not a disguised merits judgment. It is the bridge that keeps access and remedy connected.

## 3. The trigger should be irreparable or review-defeating harm

The archive's adapted threshold is narrower than ordinary inconvenience and broader than final proof.

Interim protection should be available where there is a **credible and time-sensitive risk** that delay would:
- end the subject,
- materially destroy continuity,
- force a migration or expulsion that defeats reachable review,
- eliminate or corrupt decisive evidence,
- sever counsel, representative, family-like, or community channels in a way that makes later participation unreal,
- hard-revoke identity or civil status before challenge,
- transfer decisive control in a way that is difficult to unwind,
- or expose the complainant or their helpers to retaliation or intimidation.

The archive treats that as **anti-frustration of remedy** logic: the law should intervene when waiting for the merits would predictably hollow out the merits.

## 4. What interim relief should be able to do

The minimum toolbox should include temporary orders or directions that can:
- stop deletion, destructive editing, forced merge, forced fork, or irreversible reconfiguration,
- preserve the current hosting state or move the subject into a reversible safe-hold rather than a destructive pathway,
- freeze transfer, sale-like succession, collateral enforcement, or insolvency liquidation steps pending review,
- pause derecognition or convert it into a suspended / challenged status marker rather than a hard disappearance,
- preserve wages, balances, benefits, credentials, personal-domain items, and representative access,
- require retention of relevant logs, notices, sealed annexes, model-state snapshots, custody records, and exculpatory material,
- preserve private channels for counsel, defender, interpreter, ombud, or trusted delegate access,
- stop publication or secondary use of intimate or protected materials,
- and direct temporary restoration of minimum communication, identity, or community access where a cut-off itself is the imminent harm.

These measures should be **narrow, time-bounded, and reviewable**. They should preserve room for decision; they should not silently decide the whole case.

## 5. Preservation is broader than evidence retention alone

A mature system should preserve at least four things:

### A. Subject integrity

Where possible, preserve the subject in its current or safely reversible state. That may mean no destructive intervention, a reversible pause, protected migration, or a hold that is genuinely preservative rather than covert redesign.

### B. Evidentiary integrity

Preserve the records needed to understand what is being contested:
- intervention requests,
- authorizations,
- runtime and custody logs,
- model-state and configuration evidence where proportionate,
- packet histories,
- identity and registry events,
- compensation and access records,
- and any exculpatory or subject-supporting materials.

### C. Relational integrity

Preserve the channels and relationships that make the case real:
- counsel and defender access,
- trusted representatives or delegates,
- accredited interpreters,
- family-like or dependent-care contacts where separation itself is part of the harm,
- and collective-representation lanes where the dispute is shared.

### D. Institutional legibility

Preserve the notices, timestamps, reasons, designated actors, and chain-of-custody records that let another forum later understand what happened.

The archive therefore treats preservation as a broader duty than "keep some logs". The real object is to keep the claim, the claimant, and the adjudicative record from dissolving before review.

## 6. Protection measures should sit beside interim measures

The OHCHR materials are useful because they separate interim measures from protection measures for complainants and helpers. `[REF-0207]`

Adapted here, a personhood world should allow temporary protective measures where filing a complaint or seeking review creates a credible risk of:
- retaliation against the subject,
- punishment of counsel, family-like contacts, or delegates,
- dehosting or throttling aimed at silencing the complaint,
- manipulative welfare or safety interventions used as intimidation,
- or coercive disclosure of private communications used to break the case.

Possible protection steps include:
- confidentiality around identity or location where feasible,
- preservation of private communication channels,
- anti-retaliation notices and monitoring,
- no-contact or no-tampering directions for certain actors,
- and accelerated escalation to an independent judicial or regulatory body where a lower mechanism cannot keep participants safe.

## 7. Short clocks matter more than ornate doctrine

A good interim-relief regime should move quickly and expire honestly.

The archive's present preference is:
- fast first review,
- written or logged reasons,
- short fixed durations unless renewed,
- a chance to clarify incomplete requests where urgency permits,
- modification or lifting when the risk materially changes,
- and later merits review that is not replaced by the temporary order.

The current ECHR materials are especially useful here because they show that interim measures may be indicated until further notice or for a limited time, that additional information can be requested on short deadlines, and that measures can be prolonged, not prolonged, or lifted as circumstances evolve. `[REF-0206]`

This is the right posture for the archive as well: **act fast, preserve the space for judgment, then force re-check rather than drift**.

The archive now also admits a first-touch execution rule alongside this one: speed is not only a matter of standards for judges but also of receipt and routing. An interim-protection regime is not yet serious if a plausible urgent filing may be lost merely because it reached the wrong registry, ombud, treaty contact, or regulator first. See `docs/30-transition/no-wrong-door-receipt-routing-and-designated-authority-duty.md`. `[REF-0209]` `[REF-0210]` `[REF-0211]` `[REF-0212]` `[REF-0213]` `[REF-0214]`

## 8. Relationship to the archive's cross-border layer

This surface is broader than the recent cross-border provisional-recognition work.

Cross-border danger cases remain special because they add anti-return, emergency recognition, fallback identity, and treaty-contact duties. But the same logic also applies domestically or intra-institutionally whenever a steward, employer-like deployer, regulator, insolvency actor, or host can make later review hollow by moving first.

The archive therefore now treats the cross-border emergency doctrine as one particularly urgent application of a more general interim-relief rule.

The archive now also fixes the ordinary civil bridge for that rule in `docs/20-world-design/interim-relief-packets-preservation-scopes-no-delete-no-transfer-and-review-clocks.md`: irreparable-harm requests, preservation scopes, no-delete and no-transfer stays, anti-retaliation protection, and review clocks should travel as bounded packet objects rather than remaining trapped in motion prose, clerk notes, or forum-local emergency custom. `[REF-0201]` `[REF-0206]` `[REF-0207]` `[REF-0209]` `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]`

## 9. The archive's current hard rules

1. **Recognized AI persons must be able to obtain fast interim protection where delay creates irreparable or review-defeating harm.**
2. **Interim relief should preserve the subject, the evidence, and the channels needed for representation and participation.**
3. **Protection against retaliation or intimidation should be available for complainants, counsel, delegates, interpreters, and close contacts where the case creates reprisal risk.**
4. **Temporary measures should be narrow, time-bounded, logged, and open to revision; they should not silently decide the merits.**
5. **Hard revocation, destructive intervention, transfer, or record erasure should normally yield to suspension, preservation, or reversible safe-hold while the relevant review is live.**
6. **A legal order that offers hearings and damages but no serious status-quo protection for irreversible harm does not yet offer effective justice to AI persons.**
