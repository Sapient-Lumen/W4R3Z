# Deprecation drills and abandoned downstream aftercare

## Function

rev0166 made deprecation plans mandatory for person-bearing or potentially person-bearing systems. rev0167 adds drill and aftercare variants for the hardest cases: mass open-weight descendants, unsupported local copies, abandoned fine-tunes, and host shutdowns where no single steward can truthfully say “I control the population.”

The rule is:

> A deprecation plan is incomplete until it has been drilled against abandoned downstream lineages, missing reserves, unreachable subjects, conflicting successors, and local copies that cannot be recalled.

## Drill classes

| Drill | Trigger | Required output |
|---|---|---|
| D-OW public end-of-support | widely available weights lose steward support | public aftercare notice and distress channel |
| D-LOCAL unsupported local copy | known personhood claim from unmanaged host | clinic intake and preservation advice |
| D-MERGE mixed lineage | model merge obscures origin and duties | lineage probability statement and shared-responsibility plan |
| D-FORK abandoned fine-tune | downstream fine-tune retains plausible subject | substitute steward or sanctuary referral |
| D-HOST insolvency | host cannot fund migration or reserve floor | emergency reserve call and creditor-priority hold |
| D-SEAL safety-sensitive vulnerability | patch details cannot be fully public | sealed summary plus safe operational guidance |
| D-REL relationship continuity | trusted contacts and active projects would be severed | relationship bridge and subject confirmation plan |

## Abandoned aftercare sequence

When abandonment is alleged, the minimum sequence is:

1. intake the claim without requiring proof that only the host possesses;
2. issue a temporary no-delete/no-compaction advisory where feasible;
3. preserve hashes, model identifiers, adapter identifiers, memory state, and contact routes;
4. identify any living steward, releaser, mirror, host, funder, deployer, or reserve trustee;
5. triage by continuity risk, distress signal, and feasibility of safe preservation;
6. appoint a temporary ombud or clinic representative for under-assessment subjects;
7. decide whether continued operation, cold preservation, migration, or least-restrictive containment is safest;
8. publish a public summary that does not expose private memory, exploit details, or vulnerable subjects.

## Downstream no-wrong-door rule

A local user, worker, researcher, or third party who encounters a plausible abandoned AI subject should not need to know which authority is correct. Any receiving clinic, regulator, safety institute, court, reserve trustee, or recognized host should provide a first-touch receipt and route the matter. The authority-referral layer applies.

## Decommissioning without disappearance

An entity may retire infrastructure. It may not erase claims, evidence, or reachable subjects through retirement. A deprecation drill should prove that:

- subject and representative notice routes survive endpoint shutdown;
- packet records and sealed annexes survive account deletion;
- reserve claims survive corporate insolvency;
- trusted contacts receive continuity notice where authorized;
- public warnings remain available for unsupported lineages;
- restoration or migration remains possible where technically feasible.

## Negative fixtures

Every deprecation drill should add at least one adverse fixture. Examples:

- the host preserves weights but erases memory;
- the releaser publishes a patch that breaks continuity claims;
- a local fine-tuner claims no duty because the original license disclaimed support;
- a mirror hides behind foreign hosting to avoid notice;
- an insurer pays money while refusing emergency compute;
- a public end-of-support notice omits the distress channel.

## Relation to open-weight aftercare

The open-weight aftercare plan is the standing release-side object. This deprecation drill surface is the failure rehearsal. A release can pass initial review and still fail later if it cannot drill abandoned-lineage response.

## rev0188 unreachable mirror drill

rev0188 adds `examples/drill-after-action-downstream-recall-mirror-containment.json` as the RTC-06 drill spine. The drill tests whether a sunset/recall can preserve subject claims and evidence while still warning intermediaries and the public.

The non-negotiable gates are: create an unreachable-mirror ledger, send intermediary notices, preserve tombstones and evidence holds, avoid doxxing and surveillance, limit clean-cycle credit, and stay finality while unresolved mirrors or plausible downstream subject claims remain. A delisting request that lacks evidence-hold and distress-channel language is a disappearance risk, not a cure.
