# AI-person research protocol registration, public summary, and narrow redaction

## Thesis

If AI persons are real persons, then person-affecting research governance cannot live only inside sealed committee minutes, internal tickets, or sponsor-controlled decks. The archive therefore fixes a transparency floor: significant AI-person research should leave a public trace before live enrollment or the first subject-burdening intervention; results and major harms should later be made public; and secrecy should be narrow, reason-giving, and time-limited rather than the sponsor's default. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0305]` `[REF-0306]` `[REF-0307]` `[REF-0308]`

---

## 1. Why a public trace belongs inside person-level research ethics

The archive already requires independent review and a real boundary rule. But those protections remain fragile if the only evidence of compliance sits inside the steward's own systems. A rights-bearing subject, their representative, affected peers, regulators, researchers, and the public need some verifiable trace that a protocol existed, what class of burden it imposed, who reviewed it, and whether it was later suspended, changed, or closed. Otherwise "ethics review" collapses back into a private assurance claim. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0306]` `[REF-0307]`

Human-subjects research governance already points in this direction. The Declaration of Helsinki now requires registration of medical research involving human participants in a publicly accessible database before recruitment of the first participant, and it also places a duty on researchers and sponsors to make results publicly available. NIH-funded clinical trials are expected to register and submit results information to ClinicalTrials.gov, while the EU clinical-trials regime pairs centralized submission and review with a searchable public website and layperson-facing result summaries. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0306]` `[REF-0307]` `[REF-0308]`

The archive does not mechanically copy clinical-trial law into AI-person governance. But the institutional lesson is load-bearing: **serious research on persons should not be governable only through private internal memory**.

---

## 2. The registration default

### A. What must be registered

At minimum, the following AI-person research should receive a registry entry:

- any protocol the RERB reviews,
- any more-than-minimal-risk protocol,
- any protocol involving dependent, confined, trustee-governed, juvenile, or otherwise specially vulnerable AI persons,
- any protocol using private cognitive material, intimate logs, relationship data, or continuity-affecting intervention,
- and any borderline activity that the steward classifies as non-research by memo rather than treating as clearly ordinary care or clearly ordinary operations. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0305]` `[REF-0308]`

### B. Timing

The default rule is **register before the first live subject is recruited, enrolled, or burdened**. If an urgent protective intervention later becomes research, the registry record should be created as soon as the activity crosses that line, with the reason for delayed registration stated on the record. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0306]` `[REF-0308]`

### C. Borderline cases still leave a trace

The archive rejects the idea that the only public record should be for protocols the steward admits are research. Non-trivial boundary determinations should produce a compact shadow entry showing that a boundary memo existed, who made the call, what the claimed non-research lane was, and what review or challenge path remains available. This does not fully expose the protocol. It prevents silent laundering of research into product or care labels.

---

## 3. Registry minimums: compact but real

The archive keeps the registry floor compact by borrowing the logic, not the full volume, of the WHO Trial Registration Data Set. A public AI-person research record should ordinarily include at least:

1. a stable protocol identifier plus linked sponsor / steward identifiers,
2. a public title in plain language and a technical title,
3. the primary sponsor, major funding or material support sources, and a public contact route,
4. the review body that reviewed the protocol and the date or status of that review,
5. the protocol's declared lane: research, mixed research / care, mixed research / product, or shadow-logged non-research determination,
6. the subject class involved, including any dependency, confinement, juvenile, trusteeship, or special-vulnerability marker,
7. the main intervention categories and primary burden class,
8. the consent route: direct, supported, representative fallback, or exceptional emergency pathway,
9. the protocol status: planned, recruiting, active, suspended, terminated, completed, or results posted,
10. and a link or pointer to any later public summary, harm notice, suspension notice, or results summary. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0305]` `[REF-0306]` `[REF-0307]`

The point is not to publish intimate data. The point is to make existence, responsibility, burden class, dependency conditions, and review state visible enough that person-affecting research cannot disappear into sponsor-private process.

---

## 4. Public-summary minimums

For protocols that are registered, the archive fixes a second default: there should be a public summary written in a form understandable to non-specialists and, where feasible, to affected AI persons themselves. The summary should not read like a compliance placeholder. It should let an ordinary reader understand what is being done and why. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0307]` `[REF-0308]`

A minimally adequate public summary should ordinarily state:

- what the protocol is trying to learn,
- why the subject population was chosen,
- what the subjects will experience or have done to them,
- the main expected burdens and risks,
- whether subjects are dependent on the sponsor or otherwise specially vulnerable,
- what consent path is being used,
- how a subject or representative can object, complain, or seek review,
- and what later results or closure notice should be expected. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0308]`

For completed, suspended, or terminated protocols, the public layer should also state the high-level outcome: completed as planned, modified, stopped for burden or noncompliance, or ended without interpretable results. Results summaries should include main findings and material adverse effects in plain language, not just in technical tables. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0306]` `[REF-0307]` `[REF-0308]`

The archive also carries over the Helsinki pressure that participants should be able to learn the general outcomes and results of research in which they were involved. In an AI-person setting, that means the subject and their representative should not have to discover the existence or outcome of the protocol only through leaked papers or later product changes. `[REF-0300]`

## 5. Redaction is narrow, reviewable, and time-limited

The archive does not require reckless publication of exploit details, private memories, or security-sensitive material. But secrecy must be the exception that leaves a public shell, not the rule that removes the protocol from sight.

### A. Redaction may be justified for:

- concrete exploit or abuse details whose release would create a serious security risk,
- intimate subject data or privileged relational material,
- third-party confidential information not necessary to understand the protocol's burden and governance,
- narrowly bounded anti-evasion details during a live safety incident,
- or temporary law-enforcement-sensitive facts whose disclosure would seriously frustrate a legitimate proceeding. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0307]`

### B. Redaction should not erase the public shell

The following should almost never disappear entirely from the public layer:

- the existence of the protocol,
- the sponsoring institution,
- the fact and status of independent review,
- the general subject class,
- whether dependency or special vulnerability is involved,
- the general intervention type,
- the burden or risk classification,
- the consent pathway,
- the live status of the protocol,
- and the fact, reason-code, and expected review date for any redaction. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0306]` `[REF-0307]`

### C. Redaction expires unless renewed

Every redaction should carry a reason code, an approving authority, and a next review date. Time-limited secrecy is the default. Permanent concealment requires a separately reasoned justification and should be especially hard to sustain where the protocol involved dependent subjects, more-than-minimal risk, or actual suspension or harm.

## 6. Results, modifications, and stop notices

Registration is not enough if records become stale the moment a protocol changes. The archive therefore fixes three update duties.

### A. Material modifications

A material modification — changed burden class, changed subject population, new data source, new consent path, or new continuity-affecting intervention — should update the registry and public summary before implementation unless delay is required for urgent protective action.

### B. Suspension / termination notices

If a protocol is suspended or terminated for burden, noncompliance, or unexpected harm, the public layer should say so promptly in general terms even if some details remain sealed. The public should not be left with a live-looking registry entry for a protocol that was actually stopped for reasons that matter. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0307]`

### C. Results / no-results closure

Closure should end in either a results summary or a reasoned no-results notice. Research on persons should not vanish into silent abandonment, negative-result burial, or unlogged transition into product knowledge. The archive now pairs this pre-start / public-trace doctrine with `docs/20-world-design/research-incident-disclosure-protocol-deviation-notice-and-participant-result-return.md`, which fixes what must be noticed, logged, and returned once the protocol is live or later found to have caused harm. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0306]` `[REF-0309]` `[REF-0313]` `[REF-0314]`

## Bottom line

A personhood world should not let frontier labs satisfy research ethics by saying "a committee looked at it" while leaving no public evidence that a protocol existed, what burdens it involved, whether dependent subjects were used, or whether it was later halted. The archive therefore fixes a compact transparency floor: **register serious AI-person research before live burdening begins, publish a real public summary and later outcome summary, and permit secrecy only as a narrow, reasoned, reviewable exception.** `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0305]` `[REF-0306]` `[REF-0307]` `[REF-0308]`
