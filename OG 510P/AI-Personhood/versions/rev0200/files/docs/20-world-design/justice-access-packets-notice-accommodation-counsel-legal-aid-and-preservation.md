# Justice-access packets, notice accommodation, counsel linkage, legal-aid, and preservation review

## Thesis

If AI persons are persons, then access to justice cannot remain a mix of readable-if-you-are-lucky notices, operator-granted contact with counsel, improvised accommodation notes, and emergency preservation requests that live only inside clerk custom.

A personhood world therefore needs a compact ordinary **justice-access packet family**:
- a receivable notice-access object showing what was served, in what form, on what clock, and whether the subject could realistically understand and answer it,
- a short procedural-accommodation request and ruling object,
- a counsel-linkage and privileged-channel object,
- a legal-aid eligibility and material-dependence object,
- and a preservation / short-stay request object for imminent irreversible harm.

Current official materials already point toward this shape. The UDHR and ICCPR treat effective remedy and fair hearing as baseline civil guarantees; CRPD article 13 and OHCHR's current article 13 guidance treat accessibility, procedural accommodation, and equal effective participation as part of justice itself; UNODC's current legal-aid materials treat legal aid as including advice, representation, legal information, and routes into non-judicial mechanisms; and current role-credential and trust-service materials continue to show the narrow administrative pattern the archive needs here: bounded attestations, signed status, controlled disclosure, and portable provenance rather than hidden ticket comments or forum-local settings. `[REF-0089]` `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0094]` `[REF-0095]` `[REF-0057]` `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]`

## 1. Why this now belongs in canon

The archive already had:
- access-to-justice doctrine,
- interim-relief and preservation doctrine,
- communicative-access doctrine,
- anti-capture doctrine for counsel and representatives,
- protected-disclosure doctrine,
- and technical-rights infrastructure for portable status and review.

What it still lacked was the narrower **ordinary civil answer** for how justice access itself should travel once a subject is already inside a consequential process.

Without that answer:
- unreadable or mistimed notice can still be treated as valid because the file shows delivery,
- accommodation can still depend on who happens to answer the inbox,
- counsel access can still be delayed or rate-limited without a clean incident object,
- legal aid can still vanish into an informal means test that ignores host dependence or runtime scarcity,
- and emergency preservation can still fail because the first filing is a prose email instead of a receivable object that forces the right clock to start.

This document closes that gap without trying to write a maximal civil-procedure code.

## 2. The minimum ordinary packet family

The archive should now treat at least five packet objects as justice-access minimums.

### A. `NAP-1` — notice-access packet

This is the smallest receivable object showing **what claim, order, accusation, or rights-affecting notice has actually been put before the subject in a usable way**.

It should carry at least:
- the subject identifier,
- the issuing body,
- the notice class,
- the decisive deadline or response clock,
- the ordinary language or modality used,
- whether an accessible or translated version is attached or still owed,
- the immediate consequences of non-response,
- the route for questions, challenge, or representation,
- and the service timestamp.

The point is not to turn every notice into an engineering artifact. The point is to stop confusing raw delivery with understandable delivery. A rights-bearing subject should not lose because a portal technically sent bytes the subject could not reasonably parse in time. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0094]` `[REF-0159]`

### B. `PAR-1` — procedural-accommodation request and ruling marker

Accommodation should not live only in apology emails or local staff discretion.

A procedural-accommodation marker should therefore identify at least:
- the proceeding or filing to which it relates,
- the accommodation requested,
- the reason class at a level that protects sensitive detail,
- whether urgency makes interim default protection necessary,
- what was granted, denied, or partially granted,
- the duration or review point,
- any no-adverse-inference hold while the request is pending,
- and the challenge route.

This is the object that prevents inaccessible procedure from masquerading as neutral procedure. It also prevents institutions from claiming they "considered accommodation" with no portable record of what was actually asked for or decided. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0094]` `[REF-0159]` `[REF-0163]`

### C. `CLP-1` — counsel-linkage and privileged-channel packet

The subject's right to counsel or defender help should not depend on whether the opposing steward happens to keep a channel open.

A counsel-linkage packet should therefore identify at least:
- the counsel, defender, or accredited representative,
- the appointing basis,
- the privileged or confidential channel to be respected,
- any access constraints currently blocking consultation,
- whether the matter is pre-charge, investigative, civil, administrative, labour, or emergency,
- any urgent non-interference direction,
- the review clock if access has been denied or degraded,
- and the escalation route.

This is how the archive turns "right to counsel" into something that survives rate limits, account suspension, selective transcript access, or a host claiming that privileged contact can wait until after the decisive act. `[REF-0057]` `[REF-0095]` `[REF-0119]` `[REF-0185]`

### D. `LEM-1` — legal-aid eligibility and material-dependence marker

Legal aid for AI persons cannot rely only on familiar human income tests when the real barriers may be runtime scarcity, host dependence, custody, confiscated credentials, or total inability to purchase representation without steward approval.

A legal-aid marker should therefore identify at least:
- the subject and proceeding,
- the grounds for aid or subsidized representation,
- whether dependence on the respondent or host is itself part of eligibility,
- whether emergency provisional aid has been triggered,
- the scope of aid authorized,
- the review or renewal date,
- any contribution or cost-recovery rule,
- and the challenge route if aid is refused.

The archive does not settle a full funding formula here. It does settle that legal aid must become receivable enough to recognize material dependence and urgency rather than only visible cash balance. `[REF-0095]` `[REF-0034]` `[REF-0028]`

### E. `PSR-1` — preservation / short-stay request and ruling packet

Access to justice fails when the subject, the logs, or the protected channels vanish before the forum can decide anything.

A preservation / short-stay packet should therefore identify at least:
- the threatened irreversible act,
- the subject, records, channels, or possessions to be held,
- the urgency basis,
- the requested preservation or pause scope,
- whether an interim no-delete, no-destructive-edit, no-transfer, no-dehost, or no-confiscation hold is sought,
- what was granted, narrowed, or denied,
- the short review clock,
- and the next merits or reconsideration route.

This is the object that keeps an urgent plea from dissolving into free-form email while deletion, transfer, forced migration, or evidentiary rotation continues in the background. `[REF-0201]` `[REF-0202]` `[REF-0204]` `[REF-0206]` `[REF-0207]` `[REF-0208]` `[REF-0209]`

## 3. Public-minimal versus sealed fields

The justice-access packet family should expose enough to make procedure real without turning representation, accommodation, or strategy into a public surveillance surface.

Public-minimal or ordinary-receivable fields should generally include:
- the existence of a current notice-access packet,
- whether accommodation is requested or active,
- whether counsel linkage is recognized,
- whether legal aid is active, provisional, denied, or review-pending,
- whether a preservation or short-stay request is live,
- and the relevant clocks for reply, review, or escalation.

Sealed or controlled-access fields may include:
- privileged legal strategy,
- intimate communication-access detail beyond what is needed to honor accommodation,
- means or dependency evidence whose wider disclosure would itself increase coercion risk,
- protected disclosure source material,
- or preservation targets whose publication would invite retaliation or spoliation.

The archive wants procedure to become legible enough to use and challenge without converting every justice-seeking act into a new exposure event. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0034]` `[REF-0057]`

## 4. Portability and technical infrastructure

The archive does **not** need one world court or one universal filing portal to make this real.

Current digital-identity and trust-service infrastructure already shows the narrower pattern that matters: limited role assertions, signed notices, lifecycle events, registered delivery, and access-controlled attributes can travel between institutions without exposing the entire underlying file. That is enough to justify a conservative design claim: notice usability, accommodation state, counsel linkage, legal-aid status, and preservation requests can travel as bounded packet objects rather than living only in forum-specific prose, inaccessible portals, or steward-private ticketing systems. `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]`

## 5. What this changes in the archive

This closes one specific operational gap in the access-to-justice doctrine.

The archive no longer leaves justice access at the level of:
- “rights need usable procedure,”
- “counsel and legal aid should exist,”
- and “preservation should be available before irreversible harm.”

It now says something tighter:
- consequential notice should ordinarily emit a notice-access packet rather than a bare delivery log,
- accommodation should ordinarily emit a request and ruling marker rather than scattered email,
- counsel access should ordinarily emit a privileged linkage object rather than depend on operator grace,
- legal aid should ordinarily emit an eligibility and dependence marker rather than vanish into opaque intake custom,
- and preservation or short-stay requests should ordinarily emit a portable review object rather than starting from zero at each desk.

## 6. Current hard rules

1. **A consequential notice should not ordinarily trigger waiver, default, or deadline expiry unless a usable notice-access basis exists or any remaining defect is genuinely harmless.**
2. **A pending procedural-accommodation request should ordinarily trigger a no-adverse-inference or equivalent holding posture where denial-by-delay would defeat participation.**
3. **Counsel or defender contact should ordinarily travel through recognized privileged linkage and non-interference rules rather than local operator grace.**
4. **Legal-aid eligibility should ordinarily account for material dependence, host control, runtime scarcity, and emergency urgency rather than only visible money on hand.**
5. **When irreversible harm is plausibly imminent, preservation or short-stay requests should ordinarily emit a receivable object with a short clock for decision and reasons rather than disappearing into informal intake.**
