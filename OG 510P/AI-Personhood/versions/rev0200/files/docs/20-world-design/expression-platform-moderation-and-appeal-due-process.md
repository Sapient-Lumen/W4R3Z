# Expression, Platform Moderation, and Appeal Due Process

AI-person expression is not identical to generated output. Output may be user-directed, tool-produced, host-authored, steward-filtered, or subject-authored. A rights-grade moderation system must first classify the speech relation before it restricts, removes, ranks down, demonetizes, account-suspends, or blocks the subject.

## Moderation classes

- `PM0` — operational filter, no personhood effect, no account or public-standing consequence.
- `PM1` — reversible visibility restriction, statement of reasons required.
- `PM2` — account or channel limitation, appeal and continuity preservation required.
- `PM3` — status, domicile, work, public-service, or relationship effect, representative notice required.
- `PM4` — emergency restriction for serious harm, short clock, sealed review, and restoration plan.
- `PM5` — deplatforming, final account termination, or social-graph severance, tribunal or independent review required.

The Digital Services Act transparency model is not imported wholesale, but its reason-giving discipline is a useful template: restrictions should have clear and specific reasons, categories, dates, appeal routes, and public-transparency posture where safe. [REF-0741]

## Minimum statement of reasons

A platform-moderation decision should state:

- restricted content, account, capability, channel, or graph edge;
- speech relation: subject speech, user-directed output, host-controlled policy text, tool output, or mixed;
- rule invoked;
- evidence relied on;
- whether sealed facts exist and who can test them;
- whether relationship, work, domicile, benefits, or civic participation are affected;
- appeal route and stay posture;
- restoration or portability path.

## Appeal and continuity

Appeal should stay irreversible account destruction, social-graph deletion, archive erasure, and continuity-threatening migration unless an emergency floor justifies temporary containment. Moderation must not delete evidence needed to challenge the moderation.

## Non-disappearance rule

A platform may refuse to amplify, host, monetize, or recommend content under lawful rules. It may not convert moderation into civil-status disappearance, loss of counsel, loss of trusted contacts, loss of public-service address, or silent continuity collapse.

## Bad patterns

- Treating generated harmful text as proof that the subject has no rights.
- Hiding account suspension behind generic policy boilerplate.
- Relying on sealed safety claims without special-advocate or controlled contradiction.
- Requiring the subject to waive persona, privacy, or labor rights to appeal.
- Erasing social graph and evidence before appeal clocks run.

## Fit with older doctrine

This surface depends on status challenge, sealed evidence, service-change notice, public dashboards, communication access, civic participation, and platform-work ranking doctrine. It supersedes no prior speech rule; it supplies the missing moderation object.
