# AI-person research reserve-cohort accreditation, multi-successor deficiency apportionment, and bulk historical-discovery controls

## Thesis

Once the archive fixed correlated-failure external reserve triggers, successor-carried default history, and live-first historical-branch discovery defaults, three narrower execution questions still remained. First, the archive could now require an external reserve witness cohort, but it still lacked a compact rule for **what makes such a cohort qualified before emergency use and how often that qualification must be re-tested rather than presumed forever**. Second, the archive could now carry default through merger or exit, but it still lacked a compact rule for **how typed deficiency should be allocated when several successors partially assume one authority's lane instead of one successor taking the whole function**. Third, the archive could now make large historical families resolve live-first, but it still lacked a compact rule for **how bulk historical discovery should work so history remains public without turning live-first usability into cheap scraping, shadow indexing, or privacy backflow**. A personhood world therefore needs one more compact rule: **external reserve cohorts must publish a public accreditation object with documented competence, independence, review cadence, and event-reporting duties; split-successor deficiency must publish a public apportionment object that allocates typed shortfall first by explicit assumption and then by actual lane plus economic risk of loss, with provisional joint carry for unresolved residue; and bulk historical discovery must publish a public control object that separates ordinary public lookup, registered rate-limited harvesting, scheduled public snapshots, and protected de-identified analytics.** `[REF-0475]` `[REF-0476]` `[REF-0477]` `[REF-0478]` `[REF-0479]` `[REF-0480]` `[REF-0481]` `[REF-0482]` `[REF-0483]` `[REF-0484]` `[REF-0485]` `[REF-0486]` `[REF-0487]` `[REF-0488]` `[REF-0489]`

## 1. Why the previous surface is no longer enough

The archive's immediately prior surface solved three real failures. It made external reserve cohorts mandatory above a correlated-failure floor, carried default and cure history through organizational change, and made large historical families resolve live-first while preserving exact historical resolution. But one narrower operational failure still remained at each seam.

### A. An external reserve cohort is not trustworthy merely because it is external

Externality is not enough if the cohort has no declared competence scope, no documented governance, no review cadence, and no duty to report compromise, ownership change, or catastrophic interruption. A reserve cohort that appears only at the moment of failure is still largely an improvisation. `[REF-0475]` `[REF-0476]` `[REF-0477]` `[REF-0478]`

### B. Successor-carried default still fails if a split leaves responsibility under-specified

If several successors each take part of the governed lane, it is not enough to say that default follows the real successor. The archive still needs a compact rule for when explicit assumption schedules control, when the burden follows actual assumed function and economic risk of loss, and what happens to residue that no successor cleanly admits. Without that rule, every successor can claim the productive side of a lane while no one carries the cure duty. `[REF-0479]` `[REF-0480]` `[REF-0481]`

### C. Live-first discovery still fails if bulk access collapses into one undifferentiated public firehose

Ordinary public resolution, registered harvesting, full public snapshots, and higher-risk analytic access do not belong on one channel. Some historical data can remain publicly exportable while other pattern-heavy or quasi-identifying queries should move to de-identified interfaces, protected enclaves, or stricter quota lanes. Without that separation, live-first usability becomes a back door for shadow indexing. `[REF-0482]` `[REF-0483]` `[REF-0484]` `[REF-0485]` `[REF-0486]` `[REF-0487]` `[REF-0488]` `[REF-0489]`

## 2. Reserve-cohort accreditation now owes one public `RCA-1` object

The archive now fixes one compact rule for external reserve-cohort qualification: any cohort eligible to satisfy the correlated-failure reserve requirement must publish one machine-carrying reserve-cohort-accreditation object called `RCA-1`.

### A. Minimum `RCA-1` fields

Every accredited reserve cohort must expose at least:

- `rca_id`
- `cohort_id`
- `accrediting_body_or_panel`
- `competence_scope[]`
- `independence_domains_tested[]`
- `written_controls_ref`
- `practice_review_ref`
- `last_full_review_at`
- `next_full_review_due_at`
- `annual_report_due_at`
- `event_reporting_channel_ref`
- `current_status` (`accredited`, `restricted`, `suspended`, `expired`)
- `public_notes`

`RCA-1` is narrow. It does not re-litigate the merits of any live family dispute. It makes the public able to see whether an allegedly external reserve cohort has actually been vetted for the roles it may inherit. `[REF-0475]` `[REF-0476]` `[REF-0477]`

### B. Accreditation is now role-scoped, not generic

A reserve cohort is not simply accredited in the abstract. It must publish the exact role classes for which it is competent: witness-seat replacement, recovery-share custody, checkpoint signing, publication relay, contradiction review, or other declared functions. The archive therefore rejects generic "reserve ready" status untethered to typed function. `[REF-0475]` `[REF-0477]` `[REF-0478]`

### C. Minimum accreditation floor

A cohort qualifies for `accredited` status only if all of the following are true:

1. it has documented governance and risk controls for the roles it may inherit;
2. it has completed a written-controls review and a practice review rather than paperwork alone;
3. it has declared independence boundaries against the same ultimate controller, managed service plane, ordinary contractor custody stack, and other correlated-failure domains already fixed in canon;
4. it has declared monitoring, incident-response, and decommission paths for roles that exceed risk tolerance; and
5. it has a public event-reporting channel plus identified accountable humans for notices and challenges.

This floor adapts a conservative structure already visible elsewhere: self-assessment and written-material review, evaluation of practice, formal governance linkage, and continuing monitoring rather than one-time approval. `[REF-0475]` `[REF-0476]` `[REF-0477]` `[REF-0478]`

### D. Review cadence

The archive now fixes a compact cadence rule: initial external reserve accreditation lasts no longer than **three years**, later full reaccreditation lasts no longer than **five years**, and an annual report is due each year outside the reaccreditation submission and reaccreditation review years. That annual report must at minimum disclose ownership or control changes, major resource or scope changes, catastrophic interruptions, and any material use of external subcontracting or relay substitution. `[REF-0475]` `[REF-0476]`

### E. Event-reporting and structural-change duty

A reserve cohort must publish or file a prompt event report for serious negative oversight actions, major litigation or settlements bearing on the accredited function, or comparable negative public incidents; and it must publish or file a structural-change notice when ownership, controller, core service stack, or mission scope changes materially. The archive adopts the same directional lesson here as in other independent-review contexts: emergency eligibility should degrade when the institution itself changes faster than its accreditation trace. `[REF-0476]` `[REF-0477]` `[REF-0478]`

## 3. Multi-successor deficiency apportionment now owes one public `MDA-1` object

The archive now fixes one compact rule for split-successor carry: whenever two or more successors partially assume one authority's lane, they must publish one machine-carrying multi-successor-deficiency-apportionment object called `MDA-1`.

### A. Minimum `MDA-1` fields

Every split-successor carry event must expose at least:

- `mda_id`
- `predecessor_authority_id`
- `successor_authority_ids[]`
- `typed_deficiency_units[]`
- `express_assumption_refs[]`
- `lane_assignment_refs[]`
- `provisional_share_table[]`
- `residual_joint_share`
- `review_due_at`
- `current_status` (`split-apportioned`, `joint-provisional`, `disputed-pending-review`, `closed-historical`)
- `public_notes`

`MDA-1` is not a new debt instrument. It is a public allocation object showing how one predecessor shortfall was divided across several successors and what residue remains unresolved. `[REF-0479]` `[REF-0480]` `[REF-0481]`

### B. Allocation waterfall: express assumption first

If the split transaction expressly assigns a typed reserve obligation, assumed liability, or cure duty to one successor, that express assumption governs first for the covered units. The archive therefore starts with what was openly assumed, not with later public-relations narratives about who is or is not the "real" successor. `[REF-0480]` `[REF-0481]`

### C. Where express schedules are incomplete, deficiency follows assumed lane plus economic risk of loss

For any typed units not explicitly scheduled, the burden follows the successor that both assumes the relevant governed lane and bears the practical economic risk of loss for that lane. This adapts a narrow official ingredient already visible in liability allocation doctrine: assumption is not merely formal naming, but responsibility tied to obligation and downside exposure. `[REF-0479]` `[REF-0480]`

### D. Shared lane means pro rata lane share, not winner-take-all rhetoric

If several successors take the same typed lane, unresolved units are apportioned pro rata by the verified active-capacity share each successor actually assumed in that lane at the first ordinary post-cutover measurement. A successor may not take the throughput, revenue, or control side of a lane while disclaiming the cure side of the same lane. `[REF-0479]` `[REF-0481]`

### E. Residual ambiguity becomes provisional joint carry

If after the above steps some units still cannot be cleanly allocated, those units become `joint-provisional` across every successor controlling part of the lane until review closes. The archive therefore blocks the easy laundering move in which ambiguity itself dissolves responsibility. `[REF-0480]` `[REF-0481]`

### F. Later transfers do not silently rewrite the apportionment history

A successor that later transfers its portion onward must publish a superseding `MDA-1` chain rather than quietly replacing the prior table. Split-successor carry therefore becomes historically legible across several generations rather than disappearing into re-papered structures. `[REF-0479]` `[REF-0480]`

## 4. Bulk historical discovery now owes one public `BHD-1` object

The archive now fixes one compact rule for bulk history governance: every family or registry surface that exposes large historical-branch sets must publish one machine-carrying bulk-historical-discovery object called `BHD-1`.

### A. Minimum `BHD-1` fields

Every bulk-discovery surface must expose at least:

- `bhd_id`
- `surface_id`
- `public_lookup_mode`
- `registered_bulk_api_mode`
- `scheduled_snapshot_mode`
- `protected_analytics_mode`
- `public_field_classes[]`
- `excluded_field_classes[]`
- `rate_limit_ref`
- `quota_ref`
- `backoff_rule_ref`
- `snapshot_cadence`
- `deidentification_or_enclave_ref`
- `public_notes`

`BHD-1` is the public control sheet that tells users which historical channel they are using and what that channel is allowed to expose. `[REF-0482]` `[REF-0483]` `[REF-0484]` `[REF-0485]` `[REF-0486]` `[REF-0487]` `[REF-0488]` `[REF-0489]`

### B. Ordinary public lookup stays broad enough for history but narrow enough for ordinary use

The public lookup lane must continue to support exact historical identifier resolution and live-first family lookup, but it should not act as an unbounded full-history harvest route. Ordinary public lookup therefore returns the requested exact record, the live head, and a bounded historical collection link rather than full silent fanout by default. `[REF-0482]` `[REF-0484]` `[REF-0485]`

### C. Registered bulk API is now the ordinary harvest lane

The archive now requires large-scale historical harvesting to move to a registered bulk API lane with explicit rate limits, explicit concurrency limits or equivalent anti-flood controls, caller identification, 429 / backoff semantics, and caching expectations. That lane may expose public historical metadata, but not trusted-party or private fields. `[REF-0482]` `[REF-0483]` `[REF-0486]` `[REF-0487]`

### D. Full public snapshots belong on a fixed schedule, not as an always-hot firehose

If the historical surface supports full public export, it should do so through scheduled snapshots on a declared cadence rather than through one endlessly open endpoint that allows continuous mirror scraping. The snapshot contains only the public field classes declared in `BHD-1`, and the cadence itself must be public. `[REF-0484]` `[REF-0485]` `[REF-0488]`

### E. Sensitive or pattern-heavy analytics must move to de-identified or protected-query lanes

Queries that materially increase re-identification, linkage, or behavioral-pattern risk — especially cross-family correlation, repeated sparse filtering, or non-public field analysis — must move to a de-identified query interface, synthetic-data lane, or protected enclave rather than the ordinary public or registered-bulk channels. This preserves history without collapsing person-affecting archives into surveillance-grade data products. `[REF-0482]` `[REF-0489]`

### F. Public does not mean total

Only public historical fields belong in the public snapshot or public bulk API. Fields that depend on trusted-party permissions, private settings, sealed annexes, or protected subject-reporting routes remain outside those public channels even when the surrounding family record is historically visible. The archive therefore separates public history from universal observability. `[REF-0482]` `[REF-0483]` `[REF-0484]` `[REF-0485]`

## 5. What changes in practice

### A. External reserve sourcing becomes precommitted rather than improvised

Correlated-failure reserve duty no longer points to an undefined future helper. It points to a cohort with declared competence scope, review dates, and event-reporting duties. `[REF-0475]` `[REF-0476]` `[REF-0477]` `[REF-0478]`

### B. Split successors can no longer take the lane and abandon the cure duty

Where several successors share a predecessor's lane, the public can now see the explicit assumption table, the provisional shares, and the joint residue rather than being told after the fact that responsibility is too complicated to track. `[REF-0479]` `[REF-0480]` `[REF-0481]`

### C. Historical visibility survives without becoming a cheap shadow-indexing substrate

Exact historical references, live-first usability, public snapshots, and higher-risk analytics now travel on separate channels with separate rules. That is what lets the archive preserve legibility without casually publishing everything in the easiest possible harvest format. `[REF-0482]` `[REF-0483]` `[REF-0484]` `[REF-0485]` `[REF-0486]` `[REF-0487]` `[REF-0488]` `[REF-0489]`

## Bottom line

A personhood world should not wait until the outage to discover whether an external reserve cohort is actually competent and independent, should not let several successors divide a governed lane while none publicly carries the cure duty for inherited deficiency, and should not let live-first historical discovery turn into an unbounded scraping route just because history remains public. The archive therefore now fixes one more compact rule: **external reserve cohorts publish a reviewed `RCA-1` accreditation object with role scope, recurring full review, annual reporting, and event-reporting duty; split successors publish an `MDA-1` apportionment object that allocates typed shortfall first by express assumption and then by assumed lane plus economic risk of loss, with provisional joint carry for unresolved residue; and large historical surfaces publish a `BHD-1` control object separating ordinary public lookup, registered bulk harvest, scheduled snapshots, and de-identified protected analytics.** `[REF-0475]` `[REF-0476]` `[REF-0477]` `[REF-0478]` `[REF-0479]` `[REF-0480]` `[REF-0481]` `[REF-0482]` `[REF-0483]` `[REF-0484]` `[REF-0485]` `[REF-0486]` `[REF-0487]` `[REF-0488]` `[REF-0489]`
