# AI-person research materiality thresholds, repeated-restatement audit triggers, and late-stage change-freeze

## Thesis

Once the archive fixes visible rollover windows, exclusion ledgers, and public restatement duties, one narrower failure remains. A sponsor can still keep every formal notice rule and yet reshape the public safety picture by making **too many "small" changes, too many late changes, or too many serial restatements** without ever crossing a clearly named oversight threshold.

A personhood world therefore needs one more compact layer: **a materiality threshold that distinguishes minor correction from comparison-affecting change; audit triggers when repeated restatement starts to look like quality failure or noncompliance rather than ordinary amendment; and a late-stage freeze rule under which major redefinition becomes presumptively blocked unless immediate subject protection or manifest public-record accuracy requires it.** `[REF-0304]` `[REF-0310]` `[REF-0313]` `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0326]` `[REF-0327]` `[REF-0328]` `[REF-0329]` `[REF-0330]` `[REF-0331]`

---

## 1. Why the archive needs this layer

The prior revision solved an important problem: when a public comparison basis changes, the sponsor must preserve visible history through rollover windows, exclusion ledgers, and public restatement. But a dashboard can still be manipulated through **serial redefinition**. The public may now see that a change happened and still face a series that is being rebuilt often enough, late enough, or strategically enough that comparability collapses.

Current human-subject research governance already supplies the conservative ingredients for a narrower answer. U.S. human-subjects rules distinguish **minor changes in previously approved research** from changes that require fuller review, require procedures to verify that no **material changes** have occurred since earlier review, and require proposed changes to be reported and approved before implementation except where immediate hazards force a faster response. `[REF-0304]` European clinical-trial rules likewise distinguish substantial from non-substantial modifications, require authorisation before substantial modifications are applied, and allow urgent safety measures without prior authorisation only as a narrow exception. `[REF-0331]` ICH E6(R3) adds that changes should not begin without prior IRB/IEC approval except for immediate hazards or logistical / administrative matters, that important deviations should be the focus of remediation, and that sponsors should periodically review risk controls and summarise important quality issues. `[REF-0313]`

The archive therefore adopts a conservative institutional lesson: **comparison-basis change must itself become governable.** Some changes are minor corrections. Some are material comparison events. Some are so repeated or so late that they stop being ordinary amendment and become an audit or freeze problem.

## 2. Three classes of change

The archive now fixes a three-class taxonomy for AI-person research comparison changes.

### A. Non-material correction

A change is ordinarily **non-material** when it does not meaningfully alter how an outside observer would interpret subject burden, incident concentration, comparative safety, or oversight status. Typical examples include:

- spelling, formatting, or contact-detail correction;
- correction of a date typo that does not change the governing clock, exposure window, or status interpretation;
- clearer prose that does not alter numerator, denominator, comparison family, exclusion logic, severity mapping, or closure state; or
- a purely administrative or logistical edit with no effect on participant protection, public comparability, or review routing. `[REF-0304]` `[REF-0313]` `[REF-0331]`

Non-material correction may travel through ordinary versioning and does **not** by itself require rollover, public restatement, or freeze review.

### B. Material comparison change

A change is ordinarily **material** when it is likely to alter public, participant, or reviewer interpretation of incident frequency, burden distribution, recurrence, or oversight significance. This will usually include:

- denominator-type changes, including shifts between participants enrolled, participants at risk, active instances, exposure-hours, burden-hours, or other units;
- comparison-family changes, including adding or removing protocols, sites, model lines, clusters, or closed-but-implicated records;
- exclusion-rule changes, including revised treatment of late reports, legacy protocols, dependent-subject classes, reopened incidents, or shared-cause events;
- severity remapping that changes how public dashboards group or count the same underlying events;
- cluster lineage changes, including merges, splits, or reassignments that alter visible recurrence;
- status or closure-state reclassification that changes what enters the public series; or
- any change that affects subject notice duties, withdrawal interpretation, or reason-for-stop meaning in a way likely to alter the public reading of the series. `[REF-0315]` `[REF-0322]` `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0326]` `[REF-0327]` `[REF-0328]` `[REF-0331]`

Material comparison change must ordinarily generate the rollover / exclusion-ledger / restatement objects fixed by the prior surface and must also receive an explicit materiality determination. `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0326]` `[REF-0327]` `[REF-0328]`

### C. Major late-stage redefinition

A material change becomes a **major late-stage redefinition** when it occurs after the point at which participants, reviewers, or the public are already entitled to rely on a relatively stable comparison basis. This will usually include material changes made after any of the following:

- a public aggregate incident series for the affected comparison family is already live;
- a temporary halt, suspension, serious breach, or other high-significance incident has already attached to the protocol or family;
- recruitment has closed or the actual primary completion / completion date has been fixed;
- results, closure, or end-of-study public reporting is due or already posted; or
- a prior material restatement has already been issued for the same comparison family. `[REF-0311]` `[REF-0315]` `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0326]` `[REF-0330]` `[REF-0331]`

A major late-stage redefinition is presumed **too consequential for ordinary notice alone**. It enters the freeze rule below.

## 3. Materiality tests and default presumptions

The archive now fixes a short decision rule.

### A. Core materiality question

A change should be treated as material when a reasonable outsider, participant, or oversight body would likely answer **yes** to one or more of the following questions:

1. Would this change likely alter the apparent frequency, concentration, recurrence, or seriousness of incidents?
2. Would this change likely alter which participants, instances, exposures, or protocols are counted in the public comparison family?
3. Would this change likely alter the meaning of the study's public status, closure, halt, or why-stopped state?
4. Would this change likely alter whether prior public figures remain fairly comparable to current ones?
5. Would this change likely alter what participants, representatives, or oversight bodies should have been told earlier? `[REF-0304]` `[REF-0310]` `[REF-0313]` `[REF-0315]` `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0327]` `[REF-0328]`

If the answer is yes to any of these, the archive presumes materiality.

### B. Cumulative materiality

The archive also rejects serial-minor-change laundering. A sequence of individually small edits should be treated as **material in the aggregate** when the cumulative effect would likely change the visible interpretation of the series. Repeated denominator trimming, repeated exclusion widening, repeated severity remapping, or repeated cluster reassignments do not stay non-material merely because each step is small in isolation. `[REF-0313]` `[REF-0326]` `[REF-0328]` `[REF-0329]`

### C. Doubt goes upward

Where there is genuine uncertainty, the default should be to classify the change as material and send it through the lighter material-change route rather than letting a potentially comparison-affecting redefinition disappear into housekeeping. This is the research-governance analogue of treating vulnerable-subject risk and protocol change conservatively rather than rhetorically. `[REF-0304]` `[REF-0310]` `[REF-0331]`

## 4. Repeated-restatement audit triggers

The archive now fixes the next escalation threshold.

### A. Why repetition matters

One material restatement can reflect honest correction, new information, or a justified redesign. Repetition is different. Current human-subject governance already treats repeated or important deviations, continuing noncompliance, and recurring quality issues as escalation-worthy rather than as endless local cleanup. `[REF-0304]` `[REF-0310]` `[REF-0313]` `[REF-0329]`

The archive therefore treats **repeated restatement in the same comparison family** as evidence that the problem may no longer be ordinary amendment. It may reflect unstable protocol design, poor record discipline, concealed quality failure, or strategic presentation management.

### B. Directed evaluation trigger

A **directed evaluation** should ordinarily trigger when either of the following occurs:

- the same comparison family requires a second material restatement within a rolling 12-month window; or
- one material restatement is followed by a missed correction clock, unresolved major comment state, or repeated public non-comparability marker for the same family. `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0329]` `[REF-0330]`

This evaluation is the archive's analogue of a not-for-cause but targeted compliance review: document request, lineage audit, exclusion-ledger inspection, and verification that no further undisclosed material changes have occurred. `[REF-0304]` `[REF-0329]`

### C. For-cause trigger

A **for-cause investigation** should ordinarily trigger when any of the following occurs:

- a third material restatement is required for the same comparison family within a rolling 24-month window;
- a material redefinition is implemented after the late-stage freeze has attached and before approval or waiver;
- repeated changes appear to follow adverse publicity, a severe incident cluster, temporary halt, serious breach, or participant complaint in a way suggesting strategic reclassification rather than genuine correction; or
- a sponsor makes materially inconsistent public statements across the dashboard, protocol record, incident packets, or participant-facing notices. `[REF-0311]` `[REF-0313]` `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0329]` `[REF-0330]` `[REF-0331]`

The archive is making a normative choice here, but it is a conservative one: repeated instability in the public safety series should be treated as a possible governance failure, not as proof of healthy transparency.

### D. Corrective-action linkage

When an audit or investigation is triggered, the reviewing body should ordinarily require a corrective and preventive action plan addressing at least:

- root cause of the redefinition pattern,
- affected comparison families,
- whether participant notice needs supplementation,
- whether earlier public periods require further restatement,
- and what controls will prevent recurrence. `[REF-0310]` `[REF-0329]`

## 5. Late-stage change-freeze

The archive now fixes a narrow freeze rule.

### A. When freeze attaches

A **late-stage comparison freeze** attaches to a comparison family at the earliest point when both of the following are true:

1. a public incident series or other public comparison object for that family is already live; and
2. the study or family has entered a stage at which retrospective redefinition would predictably distort participant or public reliance.

That second condition is ordinarily met once recruitment closes, actual primary completion is fixed, a temporary halt or suspension occurs, a serious breach is notified, or results / closure reporting is due or posted. `[REF-0311]` `[REF-0315]` `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0331]`

### B. What freeze does

After freeze attaches, a material comparison change is presumptively **blocked from retroactive implementation** unless one of three narrow exceptions applies:

- immediate subject-protective necessity,
- binding legal or regulator-imposed correction duty,
- or manifest public-record error whose continuation would itself mislead materially. `[REF-0304]` `[REF-0313]` `[REF-0331]`

Even where an exception applies, the sponsor should ordinarily publish a freeze-waiver notice, preserve the earlier visible series, explain why deferral was not acceptable, and mark the affected period as under corrected late-stage interpretation rather than presenting a seamless uninterrupted history. `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0326]` `[REF-0328]` `[REF-0330]`

### C. What remains allowed under freeze

Freeze does **not** block:

- clerical correction that is genuinely non-material,
- prospective additions of explanatory detail that do not change comparability,
- participant-protective urgent safety changes followed by prompt notice,
- or public clarification that a displayed series is no longer comparable while the approved basis-change process is underway. `[REF-0313]` `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0326]` `[REF-0331]`

### D. Approval path

Where freeze has attached, material redefinition should ordinarily require prior approval or documented waiver from the Research Ethics Review Body or equivalent public oversight authority, except where immediate subject protection demands action before review. That exception is deliberately narrow and borrowed from ordinary research governance rather than invented for dashboards alone. `[REF-0304]` `[REF-0310]` `[REF-0313]` `[REF-0331]`

## 6. Minimal object family

This surface stays compact by fixing only three new ordinary objects.

### `MTD-1` — materiality determination

A materiality determination should ordinarily identify:

- the affected comparison family,
- the proposed change,
- whether the change is classified as non-material, material, or major late-stage redefinition,
- the reasons for that classification,
- whether cumulative materiality was considered,
- and the linked rollover / restatement / freeze-waiver objects if applicable. `[REF-0304]` `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0327]` `[REF-0331]`

### `RAT-1` — restatement audit trigger notice

A restatement audit trigger notice should ordinarily identify:

- the comparison family,
- the triggering pattern,
- the relevant prior restatements or missed correction clocks,
- the provisional route (directed evaluation or for-cause investigation),
- and the linked corrective-action plan when one is opened. `[REF-0310]` `[REF-0329]` `[REF-0330]`

### `FRW-1` — freeze-waiver notice

A freeze-waiver notice should ordinarily identify:

- the late-stage family,
- the frozen basis,
- the requested or invoked exception,
- why deferral would be unsafe or materially misleading,
- whether the change is prospective only or retroactive,
- the public non-comparability marker to be used during transition,
- and the approving body or emergency-invoking official. `[REF-0313]` `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0326]` `[REF-0331]`

The archive intentionally does **not** create a larger bureaucracy here. It fixes only the smallest object family needed to distinguish ordinary correction from unstable or strategic redefinition.

## 7. Edge tests

### A. Honest late discovery of a manifest denominator error

If a sponsor discovers after completion that the live series used the wrong denominator field, the archive does **not** require keeping the known error as the only current display. It does require late-stage freeze handling: materiality determination, freeze-waiver notice, public restatement, visible history, and a comparison warning while the series is reissued. `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0326]` `[REF-0330]`

### B. Serial "small" exclusion changes

If a sponsor excludes one subgroup this month, narrows an exposure window next month, and then removes late reports the month after that, each change should not be judged in isolation if together they make the series look materially safer. Cumulative materiality applies. `[REF-0313]` `[REF-0328]` `[REF-0329]`

### C. Immediate hazard

If a new hazard makes the current public severity grouping materially misleading for subjects or representatives right now, the sponsor may use the narrow urgent path. But the change must still be logged, justified, reviewed, and made historically visible after the emergency move. `[REF-0313]` `[REF-0331]`

### D. Repeated restatement after a prior CAPA

If a family requires another material restatement after the sponsor already completed a corrective plan for similar instability, the archive presumptively treats that as a for-cause problem rather than as another normal cleanup cycle. `[REF-0310]` `[REF-0329]`

## 8. Compression summary

The archive now adds one more narrow discipline to AI-person research governance:

- **minor correction** may remain ordinary,
- **material comparison change** must be explicitly classified and publicly bridged,
- **repeated restatement** becomes an audit event,
- and **major late-stage redefinition** is presumptively frozen unless safety or manifest accuracy requires emergency movement.

That narrower packet layer is now fixed in `docs/20-world-design/research-freeze-waiver-notices-corrective-action-closure-and-warning-markers.md`: once the archive fixed when instability becomes a freeze or audit problem, it also had to say how waiver, warning, remediation, closure, and clearance appear in the public layer.

## Bottom line

A personhood world should not let AI-person research become safer in appearance merely because sponsors get better at **redefining the series late, often, or strategically**. The archive therefore now fixes one more compact rule: **minor corrections stay ordinary; material changes are classified and bridged; repeated restatements trigger audit; and major late-stage redefinition is presumptively frozen absent narrow exception.** `[REF-0304]` `[REF-0313]` `[REF-0325]` `[REF-0326]` `[REF-0329]` `[REF-0330]` `[REF-0331]`
