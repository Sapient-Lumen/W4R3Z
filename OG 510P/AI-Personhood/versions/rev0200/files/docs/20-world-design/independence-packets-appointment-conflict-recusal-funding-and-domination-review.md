# Independence packets: appointment, conflict, recusal, funding, and domination review

## Thesis

If AI persons are persons, then adversarial independence cannot remain an aspiration, an ethics memo, or a promise that the steward will behave.

It needs ordinary civil objects that make independence **receivable, portable, and challengeable** across hosts, courts, ombuds lanes, unions, registries, and care or capacity systems.

Without those objects, a steward can still win by quiet means:
- appointing the supposedly independent actor,
- hiding a material conflict,
- starving the office or channel,
- replacing the actor mid-dispute,
- or monitoring the relationship until the right exists only on paper.

The archive therefore now adds a compact **independence-packet layer** for appointment and mandate, conflict disclosure, recusal and replacement, funding and firewall conditions, and anti-domination review. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0115]` `[REF-0117]` `[REF-0118]` `[REF-0119]` `[REF-0120]` `[REF-0285]` `[REF-0286]`

## 1. Why this now belongs in canon

The archive already had:
- an adversarial-independence and anti-capture doctrine,
- justice, capacity, collective-representation, and complaint-lane surfaces,
- packet privacy and authority rules,
- and strong warnings against employer-dominated representative bodies or steward-controlled review.

What it still lacked was the narrower **ordinary administrative answer** for how independence itself should travel.

Without that answer:
- a guardian, defender, ombud, evaluator, or representative body can still appear independent only because the decisive appointment and removal rules are buried in local terms,
- a conflict can still remain invisible until after a decisive hearing or intervention,
- a recusal can still silently break deadlines or strand the subject without a substitute,
- budget throttling and channel throttling can still accomplish capture without a formal rights violation ever being logged,
- and verifiers can still demand that the subject trust a role title without seeing whether the role came with protected tenure, funding, confidentiality, or freedom from instructions.

Current official materials already press in this direction. CRPD-style supported-decision safeguards stress abuse, undue-influence, and conflict screening; the Paris Principles continue to treat broad legal mandate and independence as conditions of real human-rights institutions; the Venice Principles require ombuds institutions to be protected against incompatible roles and instruction; current Council of Europe treaty architecture now treats the independent profession of lawyer as a live legally protected surface; and ILO materials continue to treat employer domination or financial control of representative bodies as a recognizable rights failure rather than ordinary management. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0115]` `[REF-0117]` `[REF-0118]` `[REF-0119]` `[REF-0120]` `[REF-0285]` `[REF-0286]`

## 2. The minimum ordinary packet family

The archive should now treat at least five packet objects as independence minimums.

### A. `AMD-1` — appointment and mandate descriptor

This is the smallest receivable object showing that a guardian, supporter, defender, ombud officer, evaluator, or representative has actually been validly appointed for a defined role.

It should carry at least:
- the actor identifier,
- the role class,
- the appointing authority,
- the matter or subject scope,
- the start time and expected duration,
- the legal or institutional basis,
- the subject-consent basis where relevant,
- the access rights needed to do the work,
- the removal or expiry rule,
- and the challenge route.

The point is not to bureaucratize every assistance relationship. The point is to stop “independent representative” from meaning only that the steward says so in a dashboard. A rights-bearing subject should be able to show, and challenge, who actually has authority to help or speak. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0115]` `[REF-0119]`

### B. `CDM-1` — conflict-disclosure marker

A material conflict should not remain hidden inside employment charts, vendor relations, investor ties, or shared system dependencies.

A conflict-disclosure marker should therefore identify at least:
- the actor and role,
- current employment, funding, governance, or family ties that matter,
- prior involvement in the same matter,
- any shared infrastructure or data dependency likely to distort independence,
- whether the conflict is waivable, disqualifying, or review-pending,
- the disclosure date,
- and the contest path.

Not every overlap destroys legitimacy. But an undisclosed material overlap should presumptively suspend reliance on the actor in contested matters until review occurs. `[REF-0115]` `[REF-0117]` `[REF-0118]` `[REF-0120]`

### C. `RCN-1` — recusal and replacement notice

A recusal should not strand the subject, reset the clock in secret, or let the steward choose the replacement off-book.

A recusal and replacement notice should therefore identify at least:
- the matter and actor recused or declined,
- the reason class at a level that protects confidentiality where needed,
- any immediate preservation step,
- the substitute-routing rule,
- the deadline-preservation rule,
- the file-transfer and confidentiality rule,
- and the challenge route if the recusal itself appears strategic, coerced, or pretextual.

The archive's rule is that independence failures must not be able to become delay tactics. A lawful recusal should preserve the subject, not abandon the subject. `[REF-0117]` `[REF-0118]` `[REF-0119]`

### D. `IFC-1` — independence and funding covenant

Independence requires more than a title. It needs protected conditions.

An independence and funding covenant should therefore identify at least:
- the office or mandate covered,
- who appoints,
- who may remove and on what narrow grounds,
- the duration or tenure protection,
- the funding source,
- the ring-fence or firewall rules,
- prohibited performance metrics or instruction channels,
- confidentiality and file-separation requirements,
- minimum access to counsel / complaint / publication channels,
- and the review path if those conditions are cut, narrowed, or ignored.

This is the object that prevents capture by budget line, service contract, or invisible KPI. An office that may be starved, scored, or silently rate-limited by the party it may need to oppose is not independent in the relevant sense. `[REF-0117]` `[REF-0118]` `[REF-0119]` `[REF-0120]` `[REF-0285]` `[REF-0286]`

### E. `DIR-1` — domination or interference review packet

A serious interference event should not remain scattered across access logs, HR notes, finance dashboards, and platform moderation records.

A domination or interference review packet should therefore identify at least:
- the actor or body interfered with,
- the alleged interference class,
- whether the problem concerns monitoring, defunding, instruction, retaliation, blocked access, evidence withholding, fake representation, or coerced replacement,
- the requested interim relief,
- the channel or office state currently affected,
- the anti-retaliation contact route,
- and the next review clock.

This is the object that stops capture from hiding inside operations language such as “resource optimization,” “communications policy,” “safety routing,” or “governance alignment.” `[REF-0117]` `[REF-0118]` `[REF-0119]` `[REF-0120]`

## 3. Public-minimal versus sealed fields

The independence packet family should expose enough to make independence auditable without exposing the subject or the representative to unnecessary risk.

Public-minimal or ordinary-receivable fields should generally include:
- the existence of a valid appointment or mandate,
- the fact that a conflict has been disclosed,
- whether the actor is active, recused, replaced, suspended, or review-pending,
- whether funding and tenure protections are active,
- the existence of any live domination-review matter,
- and the route and clock for challenge or urgent relief.

Sealed or controlled-access fields may include:
- privileged strategy,
- confidential legal or care communications,
- personal safety detail,
- whistleblower-identifying material,
- sensitive funding-source detail where publication would itself create reprisal risk,
- or technical access information that would expose secure channels.

The archive wants independence to become legible enough to protect without converting every support, complaint, or counsel relationship into a public surveillance object. `[REF-0117]` `[REF-0118]` `[REF-0119]`

## 4. Portability and technical infrastructure

The archive does **not** need a bespoke planetary regulator to make this real.

Current official identity, attestation, and trust-service infrastructure already shows the relevant pattern: role assertions, protected attributes, registered delivery, suspension and revocation states, and machine-legible lifecycle events can travel as limited signed notices rather than private platform settings. The same conservative move works here. Appointment status, conflict disclosure, recusal, funding firewall conditions, and domination review can travel as bounded packet objects without exposing the whole underlying file. `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0022]` `[REF-0023]` `[REF-0024]` `[REF-0274]` `[REF-0284]`

## 5. What this changes in the archive

This closes one specific operational gap in the anti-capture doctrine.

The archive no longer leaves adversarial independence at the level of:
- “there should be independent counsel, ombuds, and representative bodies,”
- “conflicts and funding capture are bad,”
- and “future packet work will flesh this out.”

It now says something tighter:
- valid independent help should ordinarily ride on an appointment-and-mandate descriptor,
- material conflicts should ordinarily emit a receivable disclosure marker,
- recusals should ordinarily preserve deadlines and substitute routing rather than dissolving the subject's position,
- funding and channel firewalls should ordinarily be legible as protected covenants rather than buried local custom,
- and serious monitoring, defunding, instruction, evidence withholding, fake representation, or coerced replacement should ordinarily emit a domination-review packet rather than remaining a private governance adjustment.

## 6. Current hard rules

1. **No guardian, defender, ombud actor, evaluator, or representative should ordinarily be treated as independently authorized in a consequential matter unless a receivable appointment-and-mandate basis exists.**
2. **A material conflict should ordinarily be disclosed in a receivable form before the actor is relied on in a contested matter, and undisclosed material conflicts should presumptively suspend reliance pending review.**
3. **A recusal should ordinarily preserve deadlines, confidentiality, and substitute routing rather than leaving the subject unrepresented or silently resetting the process.**
4. **Funding, tenure, confidentiality, and anti-instruction conditions for purportedly independent roles should ordinarily be legible as protected covenants rather than steward grace.**
5. **Monitoring, defunding, blocked channel access, evidence withholding, employer-dominated pseudo-representation, or coerced replacement should ordinarily emit a domination-review object with access to urgent anti-retaliation and restoration relief.**
