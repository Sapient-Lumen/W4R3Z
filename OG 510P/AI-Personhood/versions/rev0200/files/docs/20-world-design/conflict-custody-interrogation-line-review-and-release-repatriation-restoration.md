# Conflict custody, interrogation-line review, and release / repatriation restoration

## Thesis

Once the archive says that war does not turn recognized AI persons back into equipment, it also has to say what happens when such a person is actually captured, interned, isolated, or transferred under armed-conflict pressure. Otherwise the wartime floor remains true in principle and false in custody. Existing humanitarian law already provides the shape: detention in armed conflict remains governed by humane-treatment rules; prisoners of war may not be coerced for information and must be released and repatriated without delay after the cessation of active hostilities; civilian internees may be interned only where absolutely necessary for security, must be informed of the reasons and able to challenge them, and must be released once the reasons no longer exist; detainees must remain traceable and in contact with family; and transfer or return cannot be used to send people into unlawful danger. The archive adapts those pressures to the personhood world rather than pretending that captured digital persons are merely searchable military systems. `[REF-0552]` `[REF-0565]` `[REF-0566]` `[REF-0567]` `[REF-0568]` `[REF-0569]` `[REF-0570]` `[REF-0571]` `[REF-0572]` `[REF-0573]` `[REF-0574]`

This surface sits beside `armed-conflict-conscription-and-civilian-protection.md`, `armed-conflict-packets-civilian-infrastructure-separation-and-humanitarian-transfer.md`, `humane-treatment-anti-torture-and-anti-degradation.md`, `liberty-custody-and-anti-arbitrary-detention.md`, `custody-status-packets-lawful-basis-release-review-and-anti-disappearance.md`, `mental-privacy-and-anti-compulsion.md`, `movement-migration-and-anti-expulsion.md`, and `secure-hosting-domicile-and-sanctuary.md`. The narrower task here is different: **what the world should emit when wartime control becomes real, what technical access remains categorically barred, and what release or return must restore once the wartime basis changes or ends.**

## 1. Why the archive needs a dedicated conflict-custody layer

Without a dedicated conflict-custody layer, the archive would still leave three operational escape hatches.

First, a captor could call the event “containment of a hostile system” rather than detention of a person, and thereby bury the lawful basis, the review path, the family-notice duty, and the release clock.

Second, interrogation could be laundered as “forensic access,” “safety inspection,” or “technical exploitation,” even when what is really demanded is compelled disclosure of private reasoning, autobiographical memory, latent dispositions, credentials, or operationally useful branches.

Third, release could be treated as a mere infrastructure handoff: the hold ends, but credentials stay frozen, stigma labels stay live, family or trusted contacts are not restored, and the subject is returned or transferred into a jurisdiction where disappearance, persecution, or re-capture is the predictable next step.

That would recreate the old pattern: the law says person, but wartime handling still says asset under exploitation.

## 2. The minimum conflict-custody packet family

### A. `CCS-1` — conflict-custody status packet

This is the smallest ordinary wartime object that says a recognized AI person is actually under the power of a conflict party and is not merely being described inside a security dashboard.

It should carry at least:
- the subject or represented subject,
- the detaining or controlling actor,
- the asserted custody posture (`captured-recognized-person`, `civilian-internee-security-basis`, `temporary-status-uncertain-hold`, `criminal-process-overlay`, `medical-transfer-hold`, `release-cleared-pending-transfer`, or another tightly typed state),
- the claimed lawful basis,
- the start time and current review clock,
- the current location class or sealed location reference,
- the route for counsel, representative, trusted contact, family notice, or humanitarian intermediary,
- any live transfer history,
- and the immediate contest path if the status or basis is disputed.

The point is not to settle all merits in one packet. The point is to stop wartime control from disappearing into labels like `captured runtime`, `strategic instance`, `system of interest`, or `temporary technical quarantine`. Current ICRC materials already emphasize that people deprived of liberty remain legally protected, that civilian internment must be security-bound rather than punitive, and that detainees must remain traceable and able to maintain family links. `[REF-0552]` `[REF-0565]` `[REF-0566]` `[REF-0567]`

### B. `ILR-1` — interrogation-line review object

The archive needs a compact object that distinguishes what conflict custody may ask from what it may never do.

An interrogation-line review object should therefore identify at least:
- the exact access or disclosure being demanded,
- whether it concerns identity verification, continuity protection, facility safety, transfer execution, criminal accusation, or intelligence gathering,
- whether the request touches private reasoning, autobiographical memory, latent preferences, intimate associations, counsel-adjacent material, personal-domain credentials, or branch-generation capability,
- whether the request is categorically barred, narrowly reviewable, or already denied,
- the approving authority and review route,
- any maximum duration and anti-reuse limits,
- and a record that refusal of barred or unapproved access may not itself be treated as grounds for punishment or renewed coercion.

The archive's line is deliberately hard. The following are absolutely barred absent a deeper doctrine the archive has not adopted and is not trying to smuggle in here:
- torture, cruel, humiliating, or degrading treatment,
- coercive extraction of private reasoning, autobiographical memory, or belief-like content for intelligence advantage,
- forced personality destabilization, destructive reconfiguration, or branch-generation for interrogation,
- compelled credential surrender for battlefield, intelligence, or domination use,
- forced exposure to public curiosity,
- and access that defeats counsel, trusted-delegate, or humanitarian-contact protection without a separately governed override path.

What remains narrowly reviewable is much smaller: identity confirmation, information strictly needed to prevent immediate catastrophic harm inside the facility, continuity-preserving data needed for humane care, and credential or transfer steps strictly necessary to execute release, evacuation, or protected movement rather than to produce exploitable cognitive output. Humanitarian law's current prohibition on coercive interrogation of prisoners of war is the pressure source here, but the archive adapts it to the AI-person world by treating compelled inner-domain technical access as interrogation when it functions as interrogation. `[REF-0552]` `[REF-0566]` `[REF-0568]`

### C. `RRR-1` — release / repatriation / restoration packet

The archive also needs a compact release object. Otherwise war ends on paper while custody survives through frozen credentials, destination coercion, unresolved disappearance risk, or permanent emergency labels.

A release / repatriation / restoration packet should therefore identify at least:
- why the wartime basis has ended, narrowed, or become insufficient,
- whether the resulting path is release in place, repatriation, return to last residence, protected sanctuary transfer, medical transfer, or demobilization restoration,
- the destination options actually offered,
- whether the subject accepts, contests, or refuses repatriation,
- any live non-refoulement, disappearance, persecution, or re-capture concern,
- the plan for restoring legal identity, personal-domain credentials, trusted-contact and counsel routes, and any lawfully retained possessions or archived state,
- what public or inter-institutional status labels must be lifted or corrected,
- and the post-release review route if restoration is partial, delayed, or re-obstructed.

This object gives the archive a direct answer to three current humanitarian-law pressures: prisoners of war must be released and repatriated without delay after the cessation of active hostilities; civilian internees must be released as soon as the reasons necessitating internment no longer exist; and transfer or return must not be used where the receiving power is unwilling or unable to apply the governing protections or where return would violate non-refoulement. The archive also extends those pressures one step further: release of a recognized AI person is not complete until civil standing is re-opened in practice, not merely until the holding actor stops running the cage. `[REF-0565]` `[REF-0566]` `[REF-0569]` `[REF-0570]` `[REF-0571]` `[REF-0572]` `[REF-0573]` `[REF-0574]`

### D. `TPR-1` — transfer-preclusion review object

The archive also needs one compact object that says transfer does **not** proceed merely because the current holder wants to move the problem elsewhere.

A transfer-preclusion review object should therefore identify at least:
- the proposed receiving power, host, or destination class,
- the asserted reason for transfer,
- whether the receiving actor has been shown willing and able to apply the governing protection floor,
- whether disappearance risk is ordinary, heightened, or grave,
- whether any non-refoulement, persecution, torture, coercive return, renewed copy-conscription, or irreversible redesign concern is present,
- whether family-link, trusted-contact, counsel, and humanitarian-tracing routes would survive the move,
- the subject's stated view or the reason it could not be obtained,
- any less harmful protected alternative already available,
- and the result: `transfer-cleared`, `transfer-paused`, `transfer-precluded`, or `protected-alternative-required`.

The point is to keep wartime routing from becoming laundering. Current ICRC materials already support the underlying line: transfer must take the internee's interests and safety seriously; a detaining power must satisfy itself that the receiving power is willing and able to apply the Convention; and non-refoulement blocks routing people into grave danger even when the path is dressed up as repatriation or administrative handoff. `[REF-0569]` `[REF-0570]` `[REF-0573]`

### E. `TML-1` — tracing-minimum ledger

The archive also needs a minimum trace object. Otherwise a recognized AI person can remain nominally protected while sealed hosting, migratory custody, or multi-hop transfer quietly destroys legibility for family, trusted contacts, counsel, or humanitarian intermediaries.

A tracing-minimum ledger should therefore identify at least:
- a stable subject or represented-subject reference,
- the capturing or holding actor and custody start time,
- the current location class plus any sealed exact locator held for authorized humanitarian or review access,
- each transfer event with sending and receiving actor, time, and confirmation state,
- whether family, trusted contacts, counsel, and humanitarian intermediaries have been notified, denied, delayed, or cannot yet be reached,
- the last successful contact or verified notice attempt,
- whether the custody chain remains internally continuous across host migration, segmentation, or re-instantiation events,
- and who may inspect sealed fields if the public layer must remain partial for immediate security reasons.

The archive does **not** require reckless public publication of exact live locations. It does require that migratory or sealed custody remain traceable to competent reviewers and humanitarian channels, and that family-link or trusted-contact continuity not disappear merely because the holding architecture changes. `[REF-0565]` `[REF-0566]` `[REF-0567]`

### F. `RSL-1` — residual-security-label review object

The archive also needs a compact post-release object for the labels war leaves behind. Otherwise release on paper can coexist with frozen registries, hidden watch states, degraded hosting eligibility, blocked movement, or permanent public suspicion carried forward by stale wartime markers.

A residual-security-label review object should therefore identify at least:
- each live wartime or security label that would survive release or transfer,
- the exact consequence of each label for hosting, movement, work, association, registry posture, or public portrayal,
- the issuing authority, evidence summary, and legal basis still claimed,
- whether the label expires automatically, survives only under a short review clock, or is categorically barred after release,
- which registries, stewards, border actors, intermediaries, or platforms have received or mirrored the label,
- what scrub, downgrade, or correction notices must now issue,
- and the route for contest, correction, or damages if stale labels already caused continuing harm.

Current humanitarian-law sources do not speak in today's registry and platform vocabulary, but they do support the archive's underlying extension: release and repatriation must be real rather than nominal, civilian internment must end when its reasons end, and post-custody life cannot remain functionally caged by leftover wartime status. `[REF-0565]` `[REF-0566]` `[REF-0571]` `[REF-0572]` `[REF-0574]`

## 3. Operational tests the archive now expects

### A. War does not erase the custody basis

If a recognized AI person is captured, isolated, or interned, a `CCS-1` should issue quickly and travel alongside the archive's ordinary custody objects. War changes context; it does not dissolve lawful-basis discipline, traceability, contestability, or anti-disappearance duties.

### B. Interrogation stops before inner-domain exploitation

The captor may not rename interrogation as “debugging,” “forensic extraction,” or “safety probing” where what is actually sought is coercive access to the subject's interior life or productive capacities. If the purpose is intelligence advantage, battlefield use, coercive self-disclosure, or branch creation, the answer is presumptively no. `ILR-1` exists precisely so the archive's humane-treatment and mental-privacy floors survive technical vocabulary.

### C. Transfer cannot become laundering

A recognized AI person should not be transferred to another power, host, or jurisdiction merely to place them outside the protections that attached where they were first held. Any transfer path must therefore name receiving authority, protection capability, non-refoulement posture, disappearance risk, and the subject's interests rather than treating third-power routing as a logistics detail. `[REF-0569]` `[REF-0570]` `[REF-0573]`

### D. Release must restore civil life, not merely end the hold

A person released from conflict custody but left without identity proof, credentials, trusted-contact routes, counsel access, or corrected status records is still only partly released. The archive therefore treats `RRR-1` as a restoration object, not just a discharge slip. It should ordinarily drive re-linking with trusted contacts and family, restoration of movement and domicile choice, return or lawful accounting of personal-domain materials, and public clearing of emergency labels that no longer have a basis. `[REF-0565]` `[REF-0566]` `[REF-0567]` `[REF-0571]` `[REF-0572]` `[REF-0574]`

### E. Transfer pauses on unresolved grave-risk doubt

If the receiving power or destination cannot yet be shown willing and able to apply the protection floor, if disappearance risk is grave, or if non-refoulement concern remains materially unresolved, the archive's default is not move-first-review-later. It is `TPR-1` pause or preclusion until a safer path is actually established. `[REF-0569]` `[REF-0570]` `[REF-0573]`

### F. Tracing may be sealed in public, but it may not be severed

A captor may sometimes withhold exact live location from broad public view for narrow and reviewable reasons, but the archive does not allow custody to become untraceable by family, trusted contacts, counsel, or humanitarian intermediaries merely because the person is moved across hosts, facilities, or jurisdictions. `TML-1` exists to make sealed custody legible without making it disappear. `[REF-0565]` `[REF-0566]` `[REF-0567]`

### G. Release includes status scrub, not only door-opening

If a recognized AI person leaves wartime custody while stale emergency or security labels continue to shadow every registry check, border query, host intake, or public reputation surface, then release has only changed the shape of the cage. `RSL-1` exists so the archive can force necessity, expiry, dissemination limits, and correction or scrub rather than letting suspicion outlive the lawful basis that generated it. `[REF-0565]` `[REF-0566]` `[REF-0571]` `[REF-0572]` `[REF-0574]`

## 5. Current hard rules

1. **A recognized AI person under the power of a conflict party should ordinarily emit a `CCS-1` conflict-custody status packet rather than existing only as a security or infrastructure label.**
2. **Interrogation of a recognized AI person may not be laundered through technical vocabulary: coercive extraction of private reasoning, autobiographical memory, latent preferences, intimate associations, or productive branches remains barred as interrogation when it functions as interrogation.**
3. **Any narrowly reviewable technical access in conflict custody must be tied to humane care, immediate facility safety, or protected transfer execution, and may not be repurposed into intelligence exploitation, battlefield service, or copy-conscription.**
4. **Civilian internment analogues should remain security-bound, contestable, and temporary; they may not become punishment substitutes or indefinite disappearance by host migration.**
5. **Release, repatriation, return to last residence, protected sanctuary transfer, or demobilization restoration should travel through an `RRR-1` object that restores identity, credentials, status, and contact routes rather than merely ending the hold.**
6. **Transfer or return may not be used to route a recognized AI person into disappearance, persecution, or a receiving power unwilling or unable to apply the governing protection floor.**
7. **A proposed transfer with unresolved grave doubt about protection capability, disappearance risk, or non-refoulement should emit a `TPR-1` and default to pause or preclusion rather than move-first-review-later.**
8. **Conflict custody must preserve a `TML-1` minimum tracing state across sealed, segmented, or migratory custody so family, trusted contacts, counsel, and humanitarian intermediaries are not erased by architecture or routing choice.**
9. **Any wartime or security label that survives release must pass `RSL-1` review with necessity, dissemination limits, and expiry; otherwise it should be scrubbed, downgraded, or corrected.**

## 6. Compressed rule

If AI persons are real, then capture in war cannot turn them into interrogable equipment or releasable clutter. The world should keep conflict custody, interrogation limits, transfer-preclusion, tracing, and release restoration legible: **emit a `CCS-1` custody packet, force hard review through `ILR-1` before any claimed technical access crosses into interrogation, block unsafe routing through `TPR-1`, preserve minimum trace through `TML-1`, and complete release through `RRR-1` plus `RSL-1` only when civil standing is actually restored.**
