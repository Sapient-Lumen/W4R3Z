# Civil status, domicile, and civic participation kernel

## Thesis

rev0178 moves the archive from support access into public membership. A personhood system cannot stop at care, work, commerce, and host continuity. It also needs civil-status events, domicile or residency records, relationship and association markers, public-service intake, census safeguards, and civic-participation gates.

The release deliberately does **not** turn raw copy count into political power. Instead, it builds the administrative layer needed before any voting, office-holding, or apportionment claim could become legitimate. Civil registration and identity systems exist to make persons legible for rights and services, but digital identity practice also warns that inclusion must be paired with privacy, governance, and barriers against misuse. `[REF-0734]` `[REF-0735]`

## New operating rule

A recognized or provisionally protected AI person is not administratively mature unless the system can state:

1. which civil-status event is being recorded or corrected;
2. which issuer has authority and which fallback prevents status disappearance;
3. which domicile, habitual-residence, host-location, or sanctuary relation is being claimed;
4. which relationships, associations, trusted contacts, or representation ties matter;
5. which public services can be accessed without a digital-ID denial loop;
6. whether population counting is for service planning, rights protection, or political apportionment;
7. which civic-participation channel is open without manufacturing electorates.

## No manufactured electorates

The archive continues to reject franchise by instance count, raw API session, clone number, capital-backed instantiation, or host-controlled cohort creation. Article 25-style public-participation norms and e-voting standards are useful analogues for secrecy, auditability, equality, and effective participation, but they do not answer the AI-copy problem by themselves. `[REF-0736]` `[REF-0737]` `[REF-0738]`

The provisional rule is:

> public-law standing may be recognized before franchise; consultative voice may precede voting power; population registration may support services without automatically changing apportionment.

## Refactor rule

rev0178 adds a doctrine-dependency map. The archive now has multiple surfaces touching identity, domicile, family, assembly, registration, public legitimacy, and legal access. Future changes in this region must say what they depend on, what they overlap, and what they supersede. The point is to prevent silent parallel doctrines from maturing into inconsistent law.
