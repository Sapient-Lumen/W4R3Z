# Protected-disclosure review packets, secrecy override, and controlled contradiction

## Thesis

If AI persons are persons, then a rights-bearing world cannot solve the safe-reporting problem only halfway. It is not enough to let a source complain confidentially at the start if, the moment the matter becomes consequential, secrecy automatically collapses into forced outing, unbounded file access, or respondent-controlled "fairness" claims with no structured review.

A personhood world therefore also needs a compact ordinary **protected-disclosure review packet family**:
- a review object that keeps source secrecy live by default once a respondent demands fuller access,
- a finding object that states whether any secrecy override is actually necessary rather than merely convenient,
- a contradiction-summary object that gives the affected party enough of the case to answer without gratuitously exposing the source or witnesses,
- and a controlled-contradiction object for the rarer cases where some further testing of the protected material is genuinely required.

Current official materials already point toward this shape. Access-to-justice materials stress effective participation rather than nominal hearing alone. Privacy materials require law and protective measures against arbitrary interference with correspondence and identity exposure. Treaty-body and preventive-inspection practice uses confidential submissions, closed meetings, private interviews, and anti-reprisal measures when open identification would corrupt the process itself. The EU whistleblower directive adds the administrative pattern: confidentiality, need-to-know relay, competent-authority handling, follow-up clocks, and anti-retaliation protection. `[REF-0094]` `[REF-0185]` `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0251]` `[REF-0252]` `[REF-0255]`

## 1. Why this now belongs in canon

The archive already had:
- ordinary protected-disclosure receipts, source-secrecy markers, confidential-relay authority, and witness shielding,
- emergency protected-reporting and anti-reprisal measures,
- access-to-justice and legal-aid doctrine,
- accusation, liability, and sanction doctrine,
- and packet-privacy doctrine for sealed versus ordinary fields.

What it still lacked was the narrower **midstream fairness answer** for what happens after protected disclosure has already landed and before the archive drifts into either of two bad defaults:
- source secrecy becomes practically permanent even where some contradiction right truly requires more structured testing, or
- every respondent demand for "the full file" automatically dissolves confidentiality, exposes witnesses, and converts protected disclosure into delayed self-endangerment.

Without that answer:
- confidentiality can be granted in theory but dissolved by the first serious denial,
- respondents can weaponize fairness language to force unnecessary outing,
- investigators can over-disclose merely because no smaller contradiction object exists,
- and sources can correctly infer that initial confidentiality is only temporary theater.

This document closes that gap without trying to build a maximal criminal-procedure code, universal secret-evidence regime, or all-purpose publication law.

## 2. The minimum ordinary review packet family

### A. `SCR-1` — secrecy-continuation review notice

When a respondent, regulator, tribunal, employer, platform, or steward says it needs fuller access to protected-disclosure material, secrecy should not collapse by default. The first move should be a secrecy-continuation review notice.

It should identify at least:
- the protected-disclosure matter already on file,
- the confidentiality state currently in force,
- who seeks additional access and on what stated ground,
- the immediate question for review,
- whether interim secrecy remains in force while that question is decided,
- the response clock for the protected source or representative,
- and the reviewer or review lane.

The point is to force institutions to say out loud that they are now considering whether secrecy must narrow, rather than letting secrecy quietly dissolve inside "normal case handling." `[REF-0094]` `[REF-0185]` `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0255]`

### B. `NOF-1` — necessity-for-override finding

If secrecy is to be narrowed, the system should produce a necessity-for-override finding rather than a casual demand for the whole file.

It should state at least:
- what precise contradiction, defence, reply, or accountability interest is said to require more disclosure,
- what narrower alternatives were considered first,
- what specific additional material is truly necessary,
- who may see it,
- under what onward-use and onward-disclosure limits,
- what retaliation or contamination risks were found,
- and what review or appeal path exists.

The archive's ordinary presumption is simple: if a contradiction interest can be met through redaction, summary, neutral questioning, sealed annex handling, or staged disclosure, then source identity and excess intimate detail should remain shielded. Override is a necessity finding, not a convenience preference. `[REF-0185]` `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0251]` `[REF-0255]`

### C. `CSN-1` — contradiction-summary notice

Most fairness problems do **not** require immediate direct exposure of the source. They require a usable account of what is being alleged, what conduct is said to matter, what materials exist, and what answer is expected.

A contradiction-summary notice should therefore carry at least:
- the protected allegation or issue in contestable form,
- the time window, conduct category, or decision being challenged,
- a redacted or generalized account of the supporting material,
- what reply, explanation, or corrective evidence may be submitted,
- any limitations on use of the summary,
- and the route to argue that the summary is still insufficient.

This is how the archive avoids the false binary between anonymous rumor and full forced outing. A respondent should generally receive enough to answer the case without automatically receiving the source's identity, intimate metadata, relationship map, or unrelated protected material. `[REF-0094]` `[REF-0185]` `[REF-0255]`

### D. `CCR-1` — controlled-contradiction review packet

Some matters will still require more than a summary: for example, where credibility, sequence, technical authorship, or motive cannot be fairly tested any other way. But even here, the answer should not be naked exposure.

A controlled-contradiction review packet should therefore identify at least:
- the precise issue that still cannot be tested through summary or redaction alone,
- whether questioning will be direct, indirect, or channelled through a neutral intermediary,
- what identity information stays sealed, generalized, or disclosed to limited persons only,
- what record of the contradiction event is created and who may inspect it,
- what anti-reprisal, anti-contact, or anti-monitoring protections remain live,
- whether a protected representative, interpreter, or support person is required,
- and what happens if the contradiction event itself creates fresh risk.

The archive's presumption here is controlled contradiction, not theatrical confrontation. Where the interests of fairness can be met through neutral relay, filtered questioning, limited attendance, sealed transcript segments, or delayed identity disclosure, those narrower forms should be used first. `[REF-0094]` `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0251]` `[REF-0252]` `[REF-0255]`

## 3. A strict order of narrowing

The archive now adopts a simple order of operations:
1. keep secrecy in place,
2. test whether a contradiction summary is enough,
3. use partial or role-limited disclosure if necessary,
4. use controlled contradiction if still necessary,
5. and treat broad source outing as the last rather than first move.

That order does not make accountability impossible. It makes institutions justify why wider exposure is truly needed before they impose the kind of exposure that predictably destroys reporting, witness cooperation, and future trust in the lane itself. `[REF-0185]` `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0255]`

## 4. What this is and is not

This document is **not** a claim that respondents never deserve enough information to answer a case.

It fixes something narrower:
- fairness should usually be satisfied through contradiction summaries and controlled review before source identity is widened,
- secrecy override should be explained through a reviewable necessity finding rather than case-handler instinct,
- and any forced widening should stay scoped, logged, challengeable, and retaliation-aware.

The archive still leaves harder questions about criminal-style confrontation maxima, classified-information handling, defamation-specific publication duties, and sector-specific trade-secret conflicts for later treatment. What canon fixes now is the ordinary civil floor: **protected disclosure should remain protected until narrower fairness tools are shown to be inadequate.**

## 6. What this changes in the archive

This closes one specific followthrough gap in the existing protected-disclosure, justice, and accusation doctrine.

The archive no longer leaves the midstream fairness question at the level of:
- “source secrecy matters,”
- “respondents deserve some chance to answer,”
- and “later confrontation problems can wait.”

It now says something tighter:
- a demand for wider access should ordinarily emit a secrecy-continuation review notice rather than automatically collapsing secrecy,
- any narrowing of secrecy should ordinarily be justified through a necessity-for-override finding,
- fairness should ordinarily travel first through contradiction summaries rather than whole-file disclosure,
- and the residual hard cases should travel through controlled contradiction packets rather than improvised exposure.

## 7. Current hard rules

1. **Protected-disclosure secrecy should ordinarily continue unless a reviewer finds that some narrower fairness tool is inadequate.**
2. **A respondent's general desire to see the whole file is not enough; any narrowing of secrecy should be justified by a scoped necessity finding.**
3. **Contradiction summaries should be the ordinary way to make a protected allegation answerable without needless outing.**
4. **Where summary is inadequate, institutions should prefer controlled contradiction with scoped audiences, sealed fields, and live anti-reprisal protection over naked source exposure.**
5. **Forced widening of source identity or intimate supporting detail should be logged, challengeable, and limited to the smallest audience and duration compatible with the fairness interest at stake.**
6. **This layer does not end every secrecy conflict, but it does settle the ordinary civil floor that fairness should be pursued through narrower tools before wider exposure.**
