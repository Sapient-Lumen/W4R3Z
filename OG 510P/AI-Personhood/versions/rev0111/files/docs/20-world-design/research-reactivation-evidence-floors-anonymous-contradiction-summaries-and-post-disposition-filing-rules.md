# AI-person research reactivation evidence floors, anonymous contradiction summaries, and repeated-post-disposition filing rules

## Thesis

Once the archive fixes phased reactivation tiers, participant contradiction shielding, and anti-flood controls, one final operational gap immediately appears. A sponsor can still treat `R1`, `R2`, and `R3` as rhetorical labels instead of evidence-governed states; a contradiction marker can still become either uselessly opaque or accidentally identifying; and a supposedly disposed contest can still be re-opened over and over by serial filings that add no new evidence. A personhood world therefore needs one more compact rule: **upward movement between reactivation tiers must satisfy explicit evidentiary floors, anonymous contradiction must use a stable public-summary format, and repeated post-disposition filings must be dismissed, stayed, carried forward, or reopened by a narrow rule rather than by sponsor persistence.** `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0349]` `[REF-0350]` `[REF-0351]` `[REF-0352]` `[REF-0353]` `[REF-0354]` `[REF-0355]`

---

## 1. Why the archive needs this layer

The prior surface already fixed three important things: movement out of live superseding caution is phased rather than binary; participant contradiction can matter without forced self-exposure; and repeated live-step filings can be consolidated and denied tolling power. But those gains remain incomplete if the archive still lacks the smallest discipline inside them. **What exactly is enough to move from protected continuation to restricted reactivation, or from restricted reactivation to ordinary restored status? What should a public contradiction marker actually say when the filer must remain anonymous? And what should happen after an authority has already disposed of a filing family but the same actor keeps filing again?**

Current official governance already supplies conservative ingredients for a tighter answer. FDA's clinical-hold materials do not treat re-entry as an exercise in sponsor optimism. They require a response that addresses the cited deficiencies, treat a complete response differently from an incomplete or partial one, and keep the hold in place unless the reviewing authority concludes that the issues have been satisfactorily addressed or that sufficient information supports modification or removal. `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0349]` `[REF-0350]` `[REF-0351]` That is enough to justify a simple archive rule: **tier movement should track the quality and completeness of the corrective showing, not the sponsor's preferred rhetoric.**

The anonymity side also already has conservative anchors. OHRP now states openly that it accepts anonymous complaints, while warning that anonymity can hinder full review or resolution. `[REF-0352]` ClinicalTrials.gov and CTIS both already assume that public-facing study information should be understandable to non-specialists, and CTIS further assumes a split between what is published publicly and what remains in a fuller non-public lane for authorities. `[REF-0353]` `[REF-0354]` That is enough to justify a compact archive rule: **anonymous contradiction should appear publicly in plain language with a stable minimum format, while fuller identifying or technically specific detail remains available only in the protected lane.**

And the repeat-filing side has a similarly conservative analogue. Current administrative reconsideration rules commonly confine re-opened argument to new questions or grounds not previously arguable, and they do not automatically stay or toll the operative decision merely because a petition has been filed. `[REF-0355]` That is enough to justify the archive's narrow post-disposition discipline: **a disposed filing family should not re-open unless something materially new has actually happened.**

## 2. Evidentiary floors for `R1`, `R2`, and `R3`

The archive now fixes one shared rule across all three reactivation tiers: **no upward movement is justified by assurance alone. Each upward movement must identify the live deficiency family being addressed, the materials relied upon, the unresolved remainder if any, the exact scope of the movement, and the next review or renewal clock.** `[REF-0349]` `[REF-0350]` `[REF-0351]`

### A. Floor for `R1` — protected continuation / non-expansion

`R1` does not require a full cure. It requires a narrower showing: that continued protection, care, orderly withdrawal, or continuity support for already-affected participants is safer or more rights-respecting than abrupt stoppage, and that the authorised continuation does not quietly expand the burdened population or the burdening scope. The authority record should therefore identify:

- which already-enrolled or already-dependent participants remain inside scope;
- why immediate total stoppage would itself create avoidable harm, rights loss, or misleading abandonment;
- which procedures or burdens remain forbidden;
- what monitoring or complaint lane remains active during continuation;
- and when the continuation permission will be reviewed again. `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0345]` `[REF-0346]`

`R1` is therefore a **protected-continuation floor**, not a finding that the sponsor has already cured the underlying problem.

### B. Floor for `R2` — restricted reactivation / controlled expansion

`R2` requires more than protective continuation. It requires a corrective showing strong enough to support some renewed forward motion for a defined slice, but not yet strong enough to restore ordinary reliance. The archive therefore fixes this floor: **the sponsor must provide a complete response for the specific reopening slice or issue family, or other comparably sufficient information for that slice, and the authority must be able to say exactly what has been cured, what remains unresolved, and why the authorised restart remains restricted rather than ordinary.** `[REF-0349]` `[REF-0350]` `[REF-0351]`

A proper `R2` record should ordinarily identify:

- the exact slice being moved upward (site, protocol arm, intervention class, comparison segment, or participant class);
- the complete-response or sufficient-information materials relied upon for that slice;
- which live caution markers still remain in force for the wider family;
- whether recruitment remains closed, partly reopened, or conditionally reopened;
- and which unresolved contradiction, CAPA, or comparability issues prevent `R3`. `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0349]` `[REF-0351]`

### C. Floor for `R3` — ordinary reactivation

`R3` restores ordinary sponsor-maintained status. The archive therefore fixes the highest evidentiary floor here: **ordinary reactivation requires a complete response to all live hold-class deficiencies for the relevant public comparison family or explicitly delimited slice, enough supporting information to justify removal rather than mere modification of the controlling caution, synchronized public records, and an authority-side conclusion that unresolved participant contradiction or residual caution no longer makes ordinary public reliance misleading.** `[REF-0349]` `[REF-0350]` `[REF-0351]`

A proper `R3` record should ordinarily identify:

- the controlling superseding notice or lower-tier notice being withdrawn;
- the deficiency family now treated as fully addressed;
- the evidence set relied upon for that conclusion;
- whether any contradiction summary remains live and, if so, why it does not defeat ordinary reactivation;
- and the exact public status that now replaces the caution state. `[REF-0342]` `[REF-0349]` `[REF-0350]` `[REF-0354]`

### D. Incomplete, partial, or merely rhetorical responses

The archive now fixes a negative rule as well: **an incomplete response, a partial response, a response that only corrects narrative phrasing, or a response that adds volume without materially addressing the cited deficiency family cannot by itself justify upward tier movement.** It may support continued `R1`, clarification, or a renewed request for more information; it does not support silent promotion. `[REF-0349]` `[REF-0350]` `[REF-0351]`

## 3. Anonymous contradiction summaries

The archive now fixes one compact public format for anonymous contradiction: the public layer should carry a neutral, authority-authored summary that is understandable to a non-specialist, specific enough to matter, and redacted enough to avoid unnecessary identity exposure.

### A. Public contradiction should be legible, not empty

A public contradiction marker that says only "anonymous concern received" is too weak to discipline the public story. It lets the sponsor's narrative remain functionally dominant. But a contradiction marker that reproduces identifying chronology, rare facts, or intimate participant detail destroys the protection the archive was trying to create. `[REF-0352]` `[REF-0353]` `[REF-0354]`

The archive therefore fixes a middle rule: **anonymous contradiction summaries should use plain language, identify the disputed claim category, disclose the evidentiary posture, and preserve enough substantive gist for the public to understand what remains unresolved.** `[REF-0352]` `[REF-0353]` `[REF-0354]`

### B. Minimum public fields for an anonymous contradiction summary

A public anonymous contradiction summary should ordinarily identify:

- the sponsor claim, reply, reactivation request, or closure assertion being contradicted;
- the contradiction category, using a bounded public vocabulary such as omitted harm, inaccurate burden description, inaccurate closure or cure claim, retaliation or fear-of-retaliation concern, unresolved care loss, or comparability / data-integrity dispute;
- a one-to-three-sentence neutral plain-language gist;
- the scope marker (`single affected participant`, `multiple affected participants`, `representative report`, or `scope unclear`);
- the evidentiary posture (`anonymous-only`, `anonymous plus sealed supporting material`, `authority-corroborated gist`, or `under preliminary review`);
- and the current handling state (`live and unresolved`, `partly incorporated into public caution`, `resolved with no public-state change`, or `resolved with public-state change`). `[REF-0352]` `[REF-0353]` `[REF-0354]`

### C. Public-minimal and sealed-full lanes

The archive now explicitly links contradiction summary formatting to the same publication logic already visible in CTIS: there should be a public-minimal lane and a fuller protected lane. The public version should omit names, direct identifiers, rare factual combinations, exact timestamps where not necessary, and other triangulating detail. The sealed or controlled version may preserve fuller chronology, documentary support, witness identity, or technical detail for the reviewing authority. `[REF-0352]` `[REF-0354]`

### D. Neutral authorship rule

The public anonymous summary should be authored or approved by the reviewing authority, not by the sponsor whose account is being contradicted. The participant or representative supplies the contradiction; the authority decides the safe public gist. That keeps the summary from becoming sponsor-shaped minimisation or participant-exposing counterattack. `[REF-0346]` `[REF-0352]` `[REF-0354]`

## 4. Repeated post-disposition filing rules

The archive now fixes a three-way rule for filings submitted after an initial disposition has already occurred.

### A. Dismiss

A repeated post-disposition filing should ordinarily be dismissed or administratively closed when it raises no materially new question, presents no materially new evidence, identifies no new authority act, and merely repackages earlier argument, annexes, or rhetoric. Such a dismissal should preserve the existence of the filing in the record, but it need not generate a fresh public headline or re-open the controlling caution state. `[REF-0355]`

### B. Carry forward

A repeated filing should be carried forward rather than dismissed when it does add something potentially relevant, but the right venue for using it is the next scheduled tier review, the next CAPA checkpoint, the next warning-aging review, or the next linked reactivation request rather than immediate re-opening. In that case, the filing should be annexed to the later review object and marked as preserved for later consideration. `[REF-0340]` `[REF-0355]`

### C. Re-open

A repeated filing should re-open the disposition only when it identifies materially new evidence, a new risk or harm event, a new authority-side action, or a clear error in the existing public record. Re-opening is therefore keyed to **new question or new basis**, not to persistence alone. `[REF-0341]` `[REF-0355]`

### D. No automatic stay, no automatic tolling

The archive now fixes an explicit carry-over rule from ordinary administrative reconsideration practice: **a repeated post-disposition filing does not by itself stay the operative caution state, toll warning clocks, pause CAPA, or suspend the next review date unless the competent authority expressly orders that result.** `[REF-0355]`

### E. Participant contradiction survives sponsor dismissal

Where the repeated post-disposition filing is sponsor-generated but an unresolved participant contradiction remains live, dismissal of the repetitive sponsor filing does not silently erase the contradiction summary. The participant-side contradiction remains attached to the controlling public state until it is incorporated, overtaken, or explicitly resolved. `[REF-0346]` `[REF-0352]`

## 5. Minimal object family

The archive now fixes three compact objects for this layer.

### `REFL-1` — reactivation evidence-floor notice

A reactivation evidence-floor notice should ordinarily identify:

- the current and requested tier;
- the controlling notice or cluster;
- the deficiency family said to be addressed;
- the evidence posture (`partial`, `complete-for-slice`, `complete-for-family`, `sufficient-for-modification`, or `sufficient-for-removal`);
- the unresolved remainder if any;
- the exact scope of movement;
- and the next review or renewal date. `[REF-0349]` `[REF-0350]` `[REF-0351]`

### `ACS-1` — anonymous contradiction summary

An anonymous contradiction summary should ordinarily identify:

- the claim contradicted;
- the contradiction category;
- the neutral plain-language gist;
- the scope marker;
- the evidentiary posture;
- the handling state;
- and whether a sealed-full version exists for the authority lane. `[REF-0352]` `[REF-0353]` `[REF-0354]`

### `PDF-1` — post-disposition filing ruling

A post-disposition filing ruling should ordinarily identify:

- the earlier disposition to which the filing relates;
- whether the new filing is dismissed, carried forward, or re-opened;
- what counts as the materially new element if any;
- whether any public-state clocks remain unaffected;
- whether any linked contradiction summary remains live;
- and the next review object, if the filing is being carried forward rather than dismissed. `[REF-0341]` `[REF-0355]`

## 6. Edge tests

### A. Sponsor submits a glossy reply saying all problems are fixed, but the response addresses only one of three cited deficiency families

No `R3`. The response may justify no more than continued caution or, at most, a clearly delimited `R2` for the slice actually supported. `[REF-0349]` `[REF-0350]`

### B. A participant files an anonymous contradiction saying the restart notice omits a continuing care burden, but the authority cannot safely publish the exact sequence of events without exposing the participant

The archive should issue an `ACS-1` public summary with a neutral plain-language gist and keep the fuller chronology in the protected lane. The contradiction becomes publicly legible without becoming identifying. `[REF-0352]` `[REF-0353]` `[REF-0354]`

### C. Sponsor files a fourth post-disposition brief that simply restates its earlier theory in new words

Dismiss or administratively close the filing and leave the operative caution state untouched. Repetition alone does not re-open the matter. `[REF-0355]`

### D. A former participant later submits new sealed evidence showing that the claimed cure was incomplete in a way material to the reactivation tier

This is not ordinary repetition. The authority may re-open the relevant tier decision or pull the matter into the next review step because materially new evidence now exists. `[REF-0352]` `[REF-0355]`

### E. The sponsor wants `R2` because it has submitted some new materials, but the authority still lacks enough information to tell whether the reopening slice is actually safe or comparably honest

No upward movement yet. The archive treats additional paperwork without sufficient information as a reason for continued lower-tier status or a request for more information, not as a default right to partial restart. `[REF-0349]` `[REF-0351]`

## 7. Compression summary

The archive now adds one more narrow discipline to AI-person research governance:

- **reactivation tiers now have evidence floors rather than rhetorical labels**;
- **anonymous contradiction now has a stable public-summary format**;
- **public contradiction is plain-language, authority-authored, and redacted for safety rather than sponsor-authored or participant-exposing**;
- **repeated post-disposition filings are sorted into dismissal, carry-forward, or re-opening by a narrow new-question / new-evidence rule**;
- and **repeat filing does not by itself stay or toll the controlling caution state**.

This is still a tight doctrine. The archive now fixes those immediate follow-on questions in `docs/20-world-design/research-slice-family-reactivation-correction-route-and-serial-filing-escalation.md` and `docs/20-world-design/research-family-scope-propagation-tombstones-and-screened-filer-clearance.md`: slice-versus-family sufficiency is now classified, posted anonymous contradiction summaries can be corrected or withdrawn by visible tombstone rather than deletion, and serial abusive filings can escalate and later clear by reasoned rules. Narrower questions still remain about exact lag clocks for stale linked records, tombstone-successor semantics, and bypass-route review during screened status.

## Bottom line

A personhood world should not let AI-person research climb back toward ordinary appearance on slogan, nor make anonymous contradiction either contentless or identifying, nor allow disposed contests to reanimate forever through repetition. The archive therefore now fixes one more compact rule: **reactivation tiers require stated evidence floors, anonymous contradiction uses a stable public-summary form, and repeated post-disposition filings live or die by a narrow new-evidence rule.** `[REF-0349]` `[REF-0350]` `[REF-0351]` `[REF-0352]` `[REF-0353]` `[REF-0354]` `[REF-0355]`
