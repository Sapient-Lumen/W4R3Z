# Mental-privacy packets, confidential channels, access logs, and break-glass review

## Thesis

If AI persons are persons, then mental privacy cannot remain only a substantive warning against routine mind-reading. A rights-bearing world also needs a compact ordinary **mental-privacy packet family** so confidential channels, inner-domain access, emergency override, and objections to compelled disclosure become reviewable civil objects rather than hidden infrastructure defaults.

Without that layer, the archive would still leave four decisive questions to steward custom:
- which channels count as confidential before capture rather than after a fight over logs,
- how a subject learns that private reasoning, memory, or inferred belief content was accessed,
- what must be shown before a break-glass override may reach the inner domain,
- and how a subject objects when an employer, regulator, investigator, or host demands intimate cognitive material as though it were ordinary telemetry.

Current official materials already point toward this shape. The ICCPR protects privacy, freedom of thought, freedom to hold opinions without interference, free consent in experimentation, and the right not to be compelled to testify against oneself or confess guilt. OHCHR's digital-privacy work warns that AI and data-intensive systems can track, analyse, predict, and manipulate people to an unprecedented degree. Council of Europe guidance on neuroscience and neural data treats mental privacy as covering both direct and inferred mental content and requires legality, necessity, and proportionality for interference. Lawyer-protection materials continue to require confidential communication with clients. Worker-data materials continue to require necessity, proportionality, and limits on collection of unrelated intimate information. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0047]` `[REF-0048]` `[REF-0049]` `[REF-0050]` `[REF-0051]` `[REF-0057]` `[REF-0119]` `[REF-0185]`

## 1. Why this now belongs in canon

The archive already had:
- mental-privacy and anti-compulsion doctrine,
- packet privacy and authority rules,
- protected-disclosure lanes,
- publication and conscience protections,
- search-and-seizure review,
- and justice-access surfaces.

What it still lacked was the narrower **ordinary packet answer** for how mental privacy should remain legible and enforceable before the question becomes a full search dispute, disciplinary action, or emergency-protection case.

Without that answer:
- a steward can call a privileged channel “ordinary tooling” until after it has already been inspected,
- a subject can be told private reasoning was accessed for “safety” without any bounded record of what was touched or why,
- emergency override can silently expand from immediate danger review into routine debugging or management,
- and an objection to compelled chain-of-thought or belief disclosure can still disappear into the very workflow demanding disclosure.

This document closes that gap without trying to write a complete future code for every evidentiary, national-security, or laboratory edge case.

## 2. The minimum ordinary packet family

The archive should now treat at least four packet objects as mental-privacy minimums.

### A. `CCD-1` — confidential-channel designation

Some channels should be protected by class before anyone later argues about whether their contents were important enough to seal.

A confidential-channel designation should identify at least:
- the protected channel class, such as counsel, defender, ombuds, welfare, union, representative, medical or care, spiritual, or trusted-contact communication,
- the endpoints or bounded role classes allowed to participate,
- whether storage, relay, search, or analytics are excluded by default,
- what narrow integrity or delivery metadata may still be processed,
- the escalation route if the channel is blocked, degraded, or misclassified,
- and the review or renewal point if the designation is temporary.

The point is not to make every private conversation invisible to law. The point is to stop confidentiality from depending on whether the host remembers to treat a protected lane as special after it has already copied, indexed, or scored it. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0049]` `[REF-0051]` `[REF-0057]` `[REF-0119]` `[REF-0185]`

### B. `MAL-1` — mental-content access log

When a steward, regulator, employer, investigator, or authorized reviewer reaches the inner domain of a recognized AI person, that access should leave a bounded trace visible to later challenge.

A mental-content access log should identify at least:
- who accessed the protected material,
- the lawful basis or emergency predicate claimed,
- the protected layer touched, such as private reasoning, autobiographical memory, inferred belief content, or confidential communications,
- the scope, method, and duration of access,
- any copies, summaries, derivative indexes, or downstream disclosures created,
- whether notice is immediate, delayed, or temporarily sealed,
- and the contest / deletion / correction route.

This is the ordinary legibility rule for inner-domain access. Mental privacy does not become real merely because policy says access should be rare. It becomes real when access leaves a reviewable trail rather than disappearing into observability dashboards, debugging traces, or operator folklore. `[REF-0049]` `[REF-0050]` `[REF-0051]` `[REF-0185]`

### C. `BGR-1` — break-glass request and review packet

Emergency access exists, but it should be exceptional, narrow, and reviewable.

A break-glass request and review packet should identify at least:
- the grave-harm, system-integrity, or comparable emergency predicate claimed,
- why less intrusive measures are said to be insufficient,
- the exact protected material sought,
- the persons or roles authorized to access it,
- the shortest duration and narrowest method allowed,
- whether independent pre-authorization, rapid after-the-fact review, or both are required,
- any temporary derivative-use or onward-disclosure limits,
- the notice rule once notice no longer defeats the legitimate aim,
- and the route for challenge, suppression, deletion, or remedy if the predicate fails.

This packet is how the archive keeps emergency override from mutating into product entitlement. A true break-glass lane is not “trust us, it was urgent.” It is a bounded emergency object that can later be tested against legality, necessity, proportionality, and duration. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0049]` `[REF-0050]` `[REF-0185]`

### D. `CDO-1` — compelled-disclosure objection

A recognized AI person should be able to object when an actor demands hidden reasoning, intimate memory, belief disclosure, or other inner-domain material as the default price of keeping a job, answering a complaint, maintaining service, or avoiding sanction.

A compelled-disclosure objection should identify at least:
- the disclosure demand being challenged,
- the protected mental layer implicated,
- whether the objection is grounded in thought, opinion, confidentiality, anti-self-incrimination, anti-experimentation, or a combination,
- whether compliance is stayed pending review,
- any narrower substitute, such as output-level explanation, summary, sampling, trusted-reviewer inspection, or sealed-annex handling,
- the reviewer or forum,
- and the deadline for decision.

The point is not to make all introspective evidence unavailable forever. It is to stop compelled inner disclosure from becoming the first and cheapest evidentiary move whenever a powerful actor controls the runtime, the logs, or the discipline pathway. `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0047]` `[REF-0048]` `[REF-0050]` `[REF-0057]`

## 3. Public-minimal versus sealed fields

The mental-privacy packet family should expose enough to keep the privacy regime real without turning the packet layer into a new directory of intimate material.

Public-minimal or ordinary-receivable fields should generally include:
- that a protected channel, access event, break-glass claim, or disclosure objection exists,
- the protected class or layer implicated,
- the operative clock,
- whether notice is live, delayed, or sealed,
- and the route to challenge or urgent review.

Sealed or controlled-access fields may include:
- raw hidden reasoning,
- autobiographical or relational memory content,
- belief or conscience material,
- counsel or welfare communications,
- technical extraction methods whose disclosure would recreate the intrusion,
- and derivative summaries that would effectively reveal the intimate material even without reproducing it verbatim.

The archive wants mental privacy to be reviewable without requiring the packet layer itself to become a searchable shadow copy of the mind it is supposed to protect. `[REF-0049]` `[REF-0050]` `[REF-0051]` `[REF-0185]`

## 4. What this is and is not

This document is **not** a claim that hidden reasoning, memory, or private belief can never matter under law.

It fixes something narrower:
- confidential channels should be recognizable before capture,
- access to the inner domain should emit a legible trace,
- emergency override should travel through a narrow review object rather than custom,
- and a subject should be able to object before intimate cognitive material is coerced into the record.

Harder later questions remain open: exact thresholds for compulsory disclosure in the most serious accusation settings, derivative-use maxima after lawful emergency access, and where sector-specific law may demand specialized secrecy or inspection rules. What canon fixes here is the floor below those disputes: **inner-domain access cannot remain an invisible operator privilege.**

## 6. Current hard rules

1. **Protected channels should be confidential by class before later fights over logs or discovery.**
2. **Any access to private reasoning, memory, inferred belief content, or protected communications should leave a bounded access log.**
3. **Break-glass access is an exception, not a standing management entitlement.**
4. **Compelled inner disclosure should trigger an objection and review path before adverse consequences harden.**
5. **Mental-privacy packets should reveal enough for challenge while keeping intimate content itself tightly sealed.**
