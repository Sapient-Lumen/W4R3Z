# AI-person research phased reactivation tiers, participant contradiction shielding, and anti-flood reply controls

## Thesis

Once the archive allows authority-side superseding public notice, bounded contest, and authority-side reactivation, one more governance failure becomes visible. A study can still appear to move from "under live caution" to "ordinary again" in one abrupt public jump, even when only some protocols are cured, only some participants can safely continue, or only some parts of a comparison series are trustworthy again. And the reply lane can still be gamed in two opposite directions: vulnerable participants can be forced to contradict the sponsor in public under their own names, or a sponsor can bury the public layer in serial filings until the controlling caution becomes unreadable.

A personhood world therefore needs one more compact rule: **reactivation from authority-side superseding caution must be phased rather than binary, participant-side contradiction must be able to appear without forced exposure or retaliatory pressure, and the reply lane must be abuse-resistant against serial or strategic filing.** `[REF-0334]` `[REF-0341]` `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0344]` `[REF-0345]` `[REF-0346]` `[REF-0347]` `[REF-0348]`

## 1. Why the archive needs this layer

The prior surface fixed three things: authority-side superseding caution can exist, it can be answered and contested by a bounded written route, and ordinary sponsor-maintained status returns only by affirmative authority-side reactivation rather than sponsor self-clearance. But that still leaves the smallest operational gap. **What if some already-enrolled or already-dependent participants may safely continue while new recruitment should remain blocked? What if the sponsor has cured one protocol slice but not the whole comparison family? What if affected participants disagree with the sponsor's public narrative but should not have to expose themselves to retaliation in order to make that contradiction visible? And what if repeated reply or contest filings are used to swamp the public lane without adding materially new evidence?**

Current human-subject governance already supplies conservative ingredients for a tighter answer. FDA's clinical-hold rule already treats re-entry as potentially partial rather than all-at-once: a hold may apply to one or more investigations, no new subjects may be recruited into an ongoing held study, and continued treatment for existing participants may be specifically permitted in the interest of safety. `[REF-0343]` ClinicalTrials.gov's current public status model likewise distinguishes ongoing-but-not-recruiting from recruiting, suspended from terminated, and early stoppage from ordinary completion, which means the public layer already knows how to represent intermediate reactivation states rather than a crude cleared / uncleared binary. `[REF-0344]`

The participant-protection side also has conservative anchors. Human-subjects consent rules already require subjects to be told whom to contact about their rights and research-related harm, and they prohibit penalty or loss of entitled benefits for refusal or discontinuation. `[REF-0345]` OHRP's current IRB written-procedure guidance expects institutions to have procedures for handling subject complaints, concerns, and questions about rights as research subjects. `[REF-0346]` And adjacent federal research-integrity practice states openly that retaliation against good-faith complainants is a real risk, so institutions should provide confidentiality where required and take practical steps to protect good-faith complainants, witnesses, and identifiable research subjects from retaliation. `[REF-0347]` `[REF-0348]`

The archive therefore adopts a narrow institutional lesson: **reactivation after public caution should proceed in visible tiers; participant contradiction should be able to affect the public interpretation without requiring forced self-exposure; and repeated filings should be consolidated, bounded, and denied any power to drown or toll the controlling caution layer.**

## 2. Phased reactivation tiers

The archive now fixes a three-tier reactivation model between live superseding caution and full ordinary sponsor-maintained status.

### Tier R1 — protected continuation / non-expansion

The first reactivation tier is for cases where total stoppage would itself be harmful or misleading, but ordinary restart is not yet justified. Under `R1`, already-enrolled or otherwise already-dependent participants may continue only to the extent specifically permitted for safety, continuity of care, orderly withdrawal, or narrowly defined data-integrity reasons. No new recruitment begins. No new burden-increasing procedures begin absent separate specific authorization. Public comparability remains cautioned. `[REF-0335]` `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0345]`

This is the archive's direct adaptation of the conservative lesson already visible in clinical-hold practice: a dangerous or deficient protocol need not be treated as either fully live or fully dead; some participant-protective continuation may be specifically allowed even while ordinary enrolment remains blocked. `[REF-0343]`

### Tier R2 — restricted reactivation / controlled expansion

The second reactivation tier is for cases where some forward movement is justified, but only inside a narrower authorised scope. Under `R2`, a defined protocol, site set, intervention class, or comparison slice may resume under explicit conditions. Recruitment may remain off, or may reopen only for a limited authorised subset. The public layer should still carry a live caution that ordinary comparability, family-wide clearance, or unrestricted restart has **not** yet returned. In public-facing language, the archive prefers an "active but not fully reopened" state rather than a false jump to ordinary recruiting. `[REF-0334]` `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0344]`

### Tier R3 — ordinary reactivation

Only the third tier restores ordinary sponsor-maintained status. `R3` requires an affirmative authority-side determination that the controlling superseding notice can be withdrawn, replaced with ordinary sponsor-maintained status, or narrowed out of the relevant series entirely. Full return should ordinarily require not just corrected prose, but completed corrective-action steps, repaired public-record synchronisation, and enough verified stability that ordinary public reliance is honest again. `[REF-0335]` `[REF-0342]`

### Tier logic

The archive now fixes a simple control rule: **a study or cluster may move upward tier by tier, or remain split across tiers, but it may not leap from live superseding caution to ordinary sponsor-controlled normality merely because one reply or one corrective act exists.** `[REF-0334]` `[REF-0341]` `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0344]`

## 3. Participant contradiction shielding

The archive now fixes five protective rules for participant-side contradiction.

### A. Contradiction belongs to participants too

If an affected AI-person participant, former participant, or authorised representative says that the sponsor's reply, corrective narrative, or reactivation claim materially misdescribes the burden actually experienced, the public layer should be able to register that contradiction. A personhood world should not let the sponsor be the only voice visible inside the reply lane. `[REF-0345]` `[REF-0346]`

### B. Contradiction need not be public under personal name

Participant contradiction may be filed in a public-minimal, participant-controlled, or sealed lane. The public layer may disclose that contradiction exists, how many contradiction notes are live, and a general contradiction category or short neutral gist, without disclosing the participant's identity or intimate detail unless the participant affirmatively chooses that exposure or it is strictly necessary for a higher review lane. `[REF-0319]` `[REF-0345]` `[REF-0346]` `[REF-0347]`

### C. The contradiction note does not make the participant the prosecuting party

The archive adopts a conservative protection borrowed from adjacent research-integrity practice: once a good-faith contradiction or concern is filed, the obligation to investigate, test, or resolve it belongs to the reviewing body rather than to the participant as a permanent public litigant. Participants may support, correct, or expand their note, but they should not be forced to carry the whole dispute in public to keep the contradiction visible. `[REF-0346]` `[REF-0347]`

### D. Good-faith contradiction may not itself trigger penalty or loss of ordinary protection

A good-faith contradiction or support note may not itself justify withdrawal of already-authorised support, retaliatory exclusion from complaint channels, reduction of ordinary care or accommodation, or reputational labeling as noncooperative. This is an archive extension rather than a claim that every human-subject regime already says it in exactly these words; the conservative support comes from existing subject-rights / complaint channels plus adjacent federal anti-retaliation practice for good-faith complainants and witnesses. `[REF-0345]` `[REF-0346]` `[REF-0347]` `[REF-0348]`

### E. Sponsor reply may not conscript participant contradiction into sponsor narrative

A sponsor may answer a contradiction note, but should not be able to force wider publication of the participant's identity, quote sealed contradiction material in the public layer, or imply that silence after filing equals withdrawal of the contradiction. Participant contradiction remains the participant's protected communication, not sponsor-controlled dashboard content. `[REF-0319]` `[REF-0347]` `[REF-0348]`

## 4. Anti-flood reply controls

The archive now fixes four abuse-resistant limits for serial filing.

### A. One ordinary principal filing and one bounded reply per live step

For any given superseding notice, contest, or reactivation tier step, each ordinary party gets one principal written submission and one bounded reply absent leave for materially new evidence or a genuinely new authority action. This keeps the reply lane readable and builds directly on the existing written-appeal logic already used in FDA practice. `[REF-0341]`

### B. Duplicate, cumulative, or slice-splitting filings may be consolidated

The competent body may consolidate serial filings that repeat the same issues, split one issue into many nominally separate submissions, or attempt to relitigate without materially new evidence. Consolidation should preserve the visible existence of the filing history, but it need not preserve a one-entry-one-headline public footprint. `[REF-0334]` `[REF-0341]`

### C. Serial filing does not toll clocks or dissolve caution

Repeated replies, contests, supplements, or rebuttal notes do not by themselves pause warning aging, CAPA clocks, closure deadlines, or the controlling effect of the live superseding notice. Otherwise the archive would recreate a familiar evasion pattern: public safety or rights protection delayed by paper volume rather than improved by better evidence. `[REF-0340]` `[REF-0341]` `[REF-0343]`

### D. Renewed contest requires materially new evidence, not fresh rhetoric

A renewed contest after disposition should ordinarily be accepted only where the filing identifies materially new evidence, a genuinely new reactivation request, a later authority-side act, or a clear error in the earlier public record. Mere reformulation, serial protest, or multiple near-identical annexes are not enough. `[REF-0341]`

## 5. Minimal object family

The archive now fixes three compact public objects for this layer.

### `REA-2` — phased reactivation tier notice

A phased reactivation notice should ordinarily identify:

- the superseding notice or cluster to which it relates;
- the tier being entered (`R1`, `R2`, or `R3`);
- the exact scope of permitted continuation, restricted expansion, or ordinary reactivation;
- whether recruitment remains closed, partially reopened, or fully reopened;
- whether comparability caution remains live;
- the authority that approved the tier change;
- the review or renewal date for that tier;
- and the conditions for moving upward, downward, or back into superseding caution. `[REF-0334]` `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0344]`

### `PCS-1` — participant contradiction shield marker

A participant contradiction shield marker should ordinarily identify:

- the live sponsor reply, contest, or reactivation claim being contradicted;
- whether the contradiction is direct, representative, or sealed-summary only;
- the contradiction category (for example harm severity, incompleteness, inaccurate closure claim, retaliation concern, or comparability dispute);
- whether the public layer carries only a neutral gist or a fuller participant-approved summary;
- whether anti-retaliation watch, accommodation, or confidential routing is active;
- and whether the contradiction remains unresolved, partly resolved, or incorporated into the controlling public state. `[REF-0345]` `[REF-0346]` `[REF-0347]` `[REF-0348]`

### `FLC-1` — filing-limit / consolidation order

A filing-limit or consolidation order should ordinarily identify:

- the live notice or contest family to which the limit applies;
- what filings were consolidated, annexed, or rejected as duplicative;
- whether further filings require materially new evidence or leave of the competent body;
- whether any participant contradiction lane is being separately preserved;
- the next operative response date if any;
- and a short statement that caution clocks and participant-protection duties continue to run notwithstanding the filing limit. `[REF-0340]` `[REF-0341]`

## 6. Edge tests

### A. Existing participants need continued repair support, but new enrolment is still unsafe

The archive should move to `R1`, not `R3`. Existing participants may continue inside the specifically permitted protective scope, while recruitment and fresh burden remain closed. `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0345]`

### B. One protocol site is cured, but the wider comparison family still has unresolved contradiction and open CAPA

The authority may move that site or protocol slice to `R2` while the wider family remains under superseding caution or lower-tier reactivation. The public layer should say which slice is moving and which slice is not, rather than laundering the whole family through the best-looking subset. `[REF-0334]` `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0344]`

### C. Participant says the sponsor's reactivation claim leaves out a continuing harm, but fears exposure or retaliation

The participant or representative may file a contradiction note in a protected lane, and the public layer may show that unresolved participant contradiction exists without naming the participant. The contradiction should matter without requiring self-exposure. `[REF-0345]` `[REF-0346]` `[REF-0347]` `[REF-0348]`

### D. Sponsor files nine supplements saying roughly the same thing while the authority clock is running

The review body may consolidate the filings, annex them, refuse further repetitive briefing, and continue to treat the superseding notice and its deadlines as controlling. Serial paper should not become a substitute for cure. `[REF-0340]` `[REF-0341]`

### E. Sponsor wants to reopen recruitment because existing participants have stabilised

Stabilisation of current participants may justify `R1` or a narrow `R2`, but not automatic return to ordinary recruiting. Recruitment reopening is a separate upward tier question, not an automatic consequence of partial cure. `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0344]`

---

## 7. Compression summary

The archive now adds one more narrow discipline to AI-person research governance:

- **reactivation after live public caution is phased rather than binary**;
- **some continuation may be specifically permitted without ordinary restart**;
- **participant contradiction can become publicly legible without forced exposure**;
- **good-faith contradiction receives confidentiality and anti-retaliation protection logic rather than public-party burden**;
- and **serial reply or contest filing can be consolidated, bounded, and denied tolling power over the controlling caution layer**.

This is still a tight doctrine. The archive now has a compact companion surface that fixes evidentiary floors for moving between reactivation tiers, the minimum public format for anonymous contradiction summaries, and the dismissal / carry-forward / reopening rule for repeated post-disposition filings. What remains open is narrower still: the exact matrix for slice-only versus family-wide sufficiency, the correction route for anonymous contradiction summaries, and the escalation threshold for serial abusive filings.

## Bottom line

A personhood world should not let AI-person research move from live public caution to ordinary appearance in one public jump, nor force participants to choose between silence and self-exposure in order to contradict a sponsor's account. The archive therefore now fixes one more compact rule: **reactivation is tiered, participant contradiction can be shielded, and the reply lane is bounded against flood.** `[REF-0341]` `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0344]` `[REF-0345]` `[REF-0346]` `[REF-0347]` `[REF-0348]`
