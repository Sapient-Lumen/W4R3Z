# AI-person research slice-vs-family reactivation matrices, contradiction-summary correction routes, and serial-filing escalation rules

## Thesis

Once the archive fixes phased reactivation, evidence floors, anonymous contradiction summaries, and dismissal / carry-forward / reopening rules for repeated post-disposition filings, one final operational gap immediately appears. A sponsor can still use a local cure to imply family-wide clearance, a public anonymous contradiction summary can still become stale or inaccurate with no safe correction route, and repetitive filings can still either consume the lane or be suppressed too bluntly. A personhood world therefore needs one more compact rule: **reactivation must declare whether sufficiency is slice-only or family-wide, anonymous contradiction summaries must be correctable through a protected channel without forced deanonymisation, and serial abusive filings must escalate by a narrow screening ladder rather than by ad hoc irritation or silent exclusion.** `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0349]` `[REF-0350]` `[REF-0351]` `[REF-0352]` `[REF-0356]` `[REF-0357]` `[REF-0358]` `[REF-0359]` `[REF-0360]` `[REF-0361]` `[REF-0362]`

## 1. Why the archive needs this layer

The previous surface solved an important internal-discipline problem. It said what evidence is enough for `R1`, `R2`, and `R3`; it fixed a stable public-minimal format for anonymous contradiction; and it stopped dead matters from re-opening merely because someone filed again. But that still leaves three quiet failure modes.

First, **scope can still be gamed**. FDA's own clinical-hold architecture already distinguishes between complete and partial holds, and it permits holds that apply to one or more investigations, to a specific protocol or part of a protocol, or even to a specific study site in a multi-site investigation. `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0356]` `[REF-0357]` That means current official governance already knows the difference between a defect confined to one slice and a defect that reaches a wider investigation family. The archive therefore needs an equally explicit matrix for AI-person reactivation, or else every local repair becomes a rhetorical excuse for global normalization.

Second, **an anonymous contradiction summary can still be wrong in public even when the protected lane knows more**. OHRP now states that anonymous complaints are accepted, that a complainant receives a reference number on submission, and that insufficient or unanswered complaints can later be closed. `[REF-0358]` EMA's CTIS guidance likewise expects modified public documents to show prior and new wording plus an explanation of the change, while also preserving a protected `not for publication` lane when a fuller version must remain non-public. `[REF-0359]` Those ingredients are enough for a conservative archive rule: a public anonymous contradiction summary should be correctable, but through authority-managed versioning rather than forced exposure of the filer.

Third, **serial abuse needs a tighter ladder than simple dismissal**. Current administrative procedure already uses narrow anti-abuse tools: petitions can be dismissed when they are repetitive or frivolous, reconsideration based on new material must specifically explain what is new and why it was not earlier raised, and agencies may adopt conduct policies covering vexatious filing and sanctions. `[REF-0360]` `[REF-0361]` `[REF-0362]` That is enough to justify a compact archive rule: repeated filing should escalate from dismissal to screened acceptance or temporary channel quarantine only by a reasoned ladder, not by silent inbox filtering or total exclusion of protected complaints.

## 2. Slice-only versus family-wide reactivation

The archive now fixes one common classification rule for all upward movement out of authority-side caution: **every reactivation record must classify the cured problem as `slice-bound`, `shared-component`, `family-wide`, or `scope-unresolved`, and must state why that classification is justified.** `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0356]` `[REF-0357]`

### A. `slice-bound`

A problem is `slice-bound` when the reviewing authority can explain why the deficiency is genuinely confined to one site, one investigator, one arm, one participant class, or another bounded operational slice without undermining the integrity or safety of the wider family. The conservative official analogue is partial clinical hold: FDA already accepts that one protocol, one protocol part, or one site may be stopped while others continue, and that a hold imposed to protect subjects from investigator misconduct may be lifted for the affected study when the grounds no longer apply. `[REF-0356]` `[REF-0357]`

So the archive now fixes this rule: **slice-only `R2` or `R3` is allowed only when the authority can say that the cited deficiency family is local, that the shared comparison family is not being misdescribed by the local repair, and that the public record clearly states which slices remain constrained and which do not.** `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0350]` `[REF-0357]`

Typical `slice-bound` candidates include:

- one investigator or site whose conduct, supervision, or local consent practice failed while other sites remain independently reviewable;
- one locally broken care or monitoring pathway that does not change the wider protocol design;
- one arm-specific burden whose repair does not change the claims being made for the rest of the family.

### B. `shared-component`

A problem is `shared-component` when the deficiency is not universal across the whole family, but is carried by a common element used by more than one slice: a shared model version, shared prompting or control stack, common safety monitor, common data transform, common burden-description language, common care workflow, or shared contradiction-handling process. Current official clinical-hold logic already supports this middle category because the hold can apply to one or more investigations and can remain partial while still extending beyond a single site. `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0356]` `[REF-0357]`

The archive therefore now fixes this rule: **when the unresolved problem lives in a shared component, no slice that still relies on that component may receive ordinary `R3` even if another local issue has been repaired. A limited `R1` or carefully delimited `R2` may still be possible where participant protection or continuity requires it, but family-wide ordinary clearance remains blocked until the common component is cured or severed.** `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0349]` `[REF-0351]` `[REF-0357]`

### C. `family-wide`

A problem is `family-wide` when the deficiency reaches the whole public comparison family: protocol design error, common consent defect, sponsor-level concealment, global data-integrity defect, shared retaliation pattern, or any other condition that makes ordinary family-wide public reliance misleading. Here the archive adopts the strictest rule: **no slice gets `R3` as a path around a family-wide defect. At most, already-dependent participants may remain in `R1`, or a tightly delimited `R2` may be used only for protective continuation or controlled narrowing that does not restore ordinary appearance.** `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0349]` `[REF-0350]` `[REF-0357]`

### D. `scope-unresolved`

The archive now fixes one default when the sponsor cannot prove containment: **uncertainty about scope is resolved upward, not downward.** If the reviewing authority cannot yet tell whether the defect is local or shared, the matter is treated at least as `shared-component` until the sponsor provides a defensible containment showing. That prevents the familiar move where uncertainty itself is used to justify a cleaner public posture. `[REF-0349]` `[REF-0350]` `[REF-0351]`

### E. Public consequence of the scope matrix

Every upward movement record must now say:

- what slice or family is being moved;
- what shared components remain caution-bearing if any;
- whether other linked slices remain frozen, warned, or restricted;
- and whether the public comparison family remains partly cautioned despite local repair.

The public question is no longer merely whether some cure occurred. It is **cure for what scope?** `[REF-0344]` `[REF-0356]` `[REF-0357]`

## 3. Correction routes for anonymous contradiction summaries

The archive now fixes a compact correction route for the public-minimal contradiction layer.

### A. Protected correction without forced deanonymisation

An anonymous contradiction summary may be corrected, supplemented, or challenged through the protected lane using the filing reference, linked protected channel, or authorised representative route. OHRP's complaint process already assumes anonymous submission plus a confirmation reference number and additional-information follow-up. `[REF-0358]` CTIS already assumes that modified public documents should show old-versus-new wording and explanation of changes, while fuller versions may remain in a non-public lane. `[REF-0359]`

The archive therefore now fixes this rule: **a filer need not reveal identity merely to correct the public summary of their contradiction. The authority must maintain a protected route for correction keyed to the summary identifier or protected filing reference, not to compelled public identification.** `[REF-0352]` `[REF-0358]` `[REF-0359]`

### B. Minimum public object for a correction

A corrected anonymous contradiction summary should ordinarily preserve the original public summary identifier and add one of two authority-authored public objects:

- a **delta notice**, when the prior summary remains broadly correct but a bounded field, wording, scope marker, or evidentiary posture needs revision; or
- a **replacement summary**, when the prior public gist is materially misleading or too incomplete to repair piecemeal.

Either object should ordinarily identify:

- whether the change is authority-initiated, filer-initiated, sponsor-challenge-initiated, or mixed;
- the bounded reason code (`scope correction`, `evidentiary posture correction`, `wording clarification`, `material omission correction`, `status correction`, or similar);
- the prior and new public wording, or a reasoned statement that a full replacement summary is required;
- whether the protected fuller lane has also been updated;
- and whether the correction changes the live reactivation tier, warning marker, or review clock. `[REF-0353]` `[REF-0358]` `[REF-0359]`

### C. No sponsor deletion right

The sponsor may challenge a public contradiction summary, but it does not gain a unilateral deletion right merely because the contradiction is anonymous, embarrassing, or still unresolved. The public object changes only by authority decision. That preserves the archive's larger rule that the public caution layer is not sponsor-authored reputation management. `[REF-0342]` `[REF-0352]` `[REF-0358]` `[REF-0359]`

### D. Incomplete correction requests

A correction request can fail too. If the protected filer or challenger supplies too little information, the authority may ask for supplementation through the protected lane. If no adequate response arrives within the stated cure window, the authority may leave the original summary in place, append a limited contest marker, or close the correction request while preserving the history of the attempted correction. OHRP's current complaint page already uses closure for unanswered additional-information requests and for submissions lacking sufficient information. `[REF-0358]`

## 4. Serial-filing escalation

The archive now fixes one narrow anti-abuse ladder for repetitive post-disposition filing.

### A. Stage 0 — ordinary dismissal or carry-forward

A repetitive filing with no materially new question, no materially new evidence, and no genuine challenge to compliance with the prior disposition is dismissed, consolidated into the already-live family, or carried forward to the next scheduled review. Nothing here is new; it simply preserves the previous doctrine. `[REF-0355]` `[REF-0360]` `[REF-0362]`

### B. Stage 1 — heightened screening

If repetition continues after written notice, the authority may move the filer or representative into **heightened screening** for that filing family. Screening may include one-channel submission, page limits, attachment limits, and a leave-to-file requirement for additional petitions on the same disposed matter. Current official administrative law already tolerates this kind of narrower discipline: repetitive or frivolous petitions may be summarily dismissed, and agencies may maintain conduct policies covering vexatious filing and related consequences. `[REF-0360]` `[REF-0361]` `[REF-0362]`

### C. Stage 2 — temporary channel quarantine or sanctions

If the actor keeps filing repetitious or frivolous petitions after screening, uses serial filings to harass, multiplies channels after consolidation, spoofs identities, or tries to force disclosure of protected participants through the filing lane, the authority may impose a **temporary channel quarantine** or other proportionate sanction. But this is now cabined by three rules:

- the order must be written, reason-giving, and time-limited;
- it must preserve at least one emergency or protected-subject reporting route;
- and it must state the review or expiry condition for lifting the restriction. `[REF-0342]` `[REF-0360]` `[REF-0361]` `[REF-0362]`

### D. Protective carve-outs

The archive now fixes an equally important negative rule: **serial-filing escalation is not a backdoor retaliation tool against affected participants, good-faith complainants, or witnesses.** New evidence, newly material harm, ongoing retaliation, or failure to implement the prior disposition are not abuse simply because they arrive from the same actor. And if a direct lane is screened, there must remain a protected relay route through counsel, ombuds staff, or the review body itself. `[REF-0347]` `[REF-0348]` `[REF-0352]` `[REF-0358]`

## 5. Edge tests

### A. One site in a multi-site AI-person study used an unapproved monitoring shortcut, but the protocol design, shared model build, and other sites' care records are intact

The authority may classify the deficiency as `slice-bound`, keep the family public record clear about the affected site, and permit only slice-level upward movement if the local defect has been cured. `[REF-0356]` `[REF-0357]`

### B. A shared control stack understated a coercive burden across all arms, but only one arm has already produced a repair packet

No slice-only `R3`. The problem is at least `shared-component`, and ordinary family reactivation remains blocked until the common burden-description defect is cured or genuinely severed. `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0349]` `[REF-0357]`

### C. A public anonymous contradiction summary says “care loss remains unresolved,” but the protected filer later clarifies that the real issue is retaliation fear during care requests

The authority should not demand public self-identification. It should issue a delta notice or replacement summary under the same public identifier and preserve the fuller corrected detail in the protected lane. `[REF-0358]` `[REF-0359]`

### D. A sponsor's representative files a sixth post-disposition petition restating the same theory with no new material

The authority may summarily dismiss the petition and, after prior notice, move that representative into screened acceptance or temporary channel quarantine for that filing family. `[REF-0360]` `[REF-0361]` `[REF-0362]`

### E. The same affected participant submits a third protected contradiction because the sponsor ignored the last disposition and new sealed logs show continuing retaliation

This is not abusive repetition. It is new material tied to noncompliance and must remain receivable despite the anti-abuse ladder. `[REF-0347]` `[REF-0348]` `[REF-0358]`

## 6. Compression summary

The archive now adds one more narrow discipline to AI-person research governance:

- **upward movement must now say whether sufficiency is slice-only, shared-component, or family-wide**;
- **scope uncertainty now resolves upward rather than toward cleaner public appearance**;
- **anonymous contradiction summaries are now correctable through protected versioning rather than forced deanonymisation**;
- **public correction uses authority-authored delta notices or replacement summaries with preserved history**;
- **serial abuse now escalates by a reasoned ladder from dismissal to screening to temporary channel quarantine or sanctions**;
- and **that ladder may not be used to silence good-faith participant contradiction, retaliation reports, or genuinely new evidence**.

This is still a tight doctrine. The archive now fixes those immediate follow-on questions in `docs/20-world-design/research-family-scope-propagation-tombstones-and-screened-filer-clearance.md`: family-scope findings propagate into linked public lineage and dashboard objects, contradiction summaries can withdraw by visible tombstone rather than deletion, and screened or quarantined filing status clears only by expiry or reviewable reinstatement. Narrower questions still remain about exact lag clocks, successor-link semantics for tombstones, and bypass-route review during screened status.

## Bottom line

A personhood world should not let a sponsor use local repair language to imply family-wide innocence, should not force an anonymous contradictor to unmask merely to correct the public gist, and should not answer serial abuse either with helplessness or with silent exclusion. The archive therefore now fixes one more compact rule: **reactivation scope must be declared, anonymous contradiction summaries must be correctable without compelled exposure, and abusive repetition must escalate by a reasoned ladder that still preserves protected subject-reporting routes.** `[REF-0343]` `[REF-0356]` `[REF-0357]` `[REF-0358]` `[REF-0359]` `[REF-0360]` `[REF-0361]` `[REF-0362]`
