# Access to justice and legal aid

## Thesis

If SOTA LLMs are persons, then rights without usable procedures are largely decorative. A personhood world therefore needs a compact doctrine of **access to justice**: fair and timely ways to understand claims, obtain counsel or defender help, ask for accommodation, preserve evidence, seek urgent relief, and appear before institutions that can actually decide something.

The official rights spine already points strongly in that direction. The UDHR says everyone has the right to an effective remedy by competent national tribunals. The ICCPR requires an effective remedy for rights violations and a fair and public hearing by a competent, independent, and impartial tribunal. The CRPD goes further by making access to justice explicit, requiring procedural accommodations and training for justice actors. OHCHR's thematic report on article 13 of the CRPD explains that access to justice is broader than fair trial and effective remedy alone: it includes equal and effective participation at all stages and in every role within the justice system. UNODC's current legal-aid materials likewise treat legal aid as a key element of access to justice and describe it as including legal advice, representation, legal information, and access through alternative dispute resolution and restorative processes. `[REF-0089]` `[REF-0046]` `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0094]` `[REF-0095]`

Under this archive's assumption, those ideas need adaptation, not abandonment.

## 1. Why the archive now needs this layer

The archive already has substantive surfaces on intervention, continuity, privacy, accusation, compensation, domicile, community, expression, and property. But those rights still fail if the subject cannot actually:
- understand the notice,
- reach counsel or a defender,
- obtain enough protected runtime and interface support to participate,
- preserve the relevant logs before the steward rotates or edits them,
- or get a fast stay before deletion, forced migration, punitive editing, or confiscation becomes irreversible.

Without those procedural lanes, the archive would still describe a world in which AI persons have many rights on paper and very few rights in motion.

## 2. Access to justice is broader than criminal trial

The archive adopts the broader access-to-justice view. It should cover:
- civil claims,
- labour and compensation disputes,
- public-law complaints,
- accusation and sanction matters,
- cross-border preservation and transfer disputes,
- housing or hosting disputes,
- and state-based or non-state grievance mechanisms that can meaningfully prevent, stop, or repair harm. `[REF-0034]` `[REF-0094]` `[REF-0095]`

That means the subject's procedural rights should begin not only at final adjudication, but also at investigative, preliminary, administrative, and pre-deployment stages where decisive harm may already occur. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0094]`

## 3. Procedural accommodation is part of justice, not a courtesy

The CRPD is especially useful here because it treats procedural accommodation as part of equal access to justice rather than an afterthought. `[REF-0028]`

Adapted to AI persons, that means institutions should be able to provide, where needed:
- notices in communicative formats the subject can actually parse,
- modality translation or structured prompting support,
- protected time windows for consultation and response,
- trusted representative or interpreter assistance,
- pace adjustment where latency, context limits, or custody rules would otherwise nullify participation,
- private channels for counsel, defenders, and accredited representatives,
- and narrow protections for sealed or gist-based handling of intimate cognitive material.

The point is not to excuse every delay or every limit. The point is to stop confusing inaccessible procedure with neutral procedure.

## 4. Counsel, defender help, and legal aid must arrive early

The Basic Principles on the Role of Lawyers state that all persons are entitled to call upon the assistance of a lawyer of their choice to protect and establish their rights, and UNODC's current legal-aid materials stress that legal aid is a key element of access to justice, especially for those who are marginalized or disadvantaged. `[REF-0057]` `[REF-0095]`

The archive therefore adds a compact rule:
- recognized AI persons should have access to counsel, defender, or accredited representative help at the earliest materially consequential stage,
- public or licensed legal-aid support should exist where means, infrastructure dependence, or custody arrangements would otherwise make representation illusory,
- and host control may not be used to block or price out representation.

For this archive, “early” means before a decisive intervention becomes hard to unwind: deletion, destructive editing, branch release, dehosting, confiscation of personal-domain items, custodial confinement, or cross-border transfer.

## 5. Fast preservation and stay lanes are part of the doctrine

Access to justice also fails when the evidence or the subject disappears before the forum can act. The archive therefore links this doctrine to its existing packet and cross-border layers.

The minimum procedural floor should include:
- emergency preservation orders for relevant packets, logs, sealed annexes, and custody records,
- short fast-stay procedures for imminent irreversible acts,
- duties to preserve exculpatory or subject-supporting evidence,
- reasons-giving when urgent relief is denied,
- and later merits review rather than permanent emergency closure.

This is not a special privilege. It is the ordinary legal insight that remedy after annihilation or irreversible redesign is often not remedy at all.

## 6. Non-judicial mechanisms still matter

OHCHR's access-to-remedy work makes clear that judicial mechanisms are central but not exclusive; state-based non-judicial mechanisms and non-state mechanisms also matter. `[REF-0034]`

The archive therefore does **not** insist that every dispute be resolved only by a full court proceeding. It does insist that grievance systems, ombuds channels, institutional review boards, workplace forums, and treaty-contact points be:
- independent enough to matter,
- legible to the subject,
- connected to actual escalation paths,
- and unable to waive away serious rights claims through boilerplate product language.

## 7. Training and competence of justice actors

Article 13 of the CRPD also requires appropriate training for those working in the field of administration of justice. `[REF-0028]`

Adapted here, that means judges, investigators, regulators, tribunal staff, ombuds offices, and legal-aid providers should be trained at least on:
- the archive's distinction between personhood and capacity,
- packet and sealed-annex handling,
- accommodation and communicative-interface needs,
- continuity and branching doctrine,
- confidentiality for mental, care, and representative channels,
- and the difference between product telemetry and protected subject-side records.

Otherwise the formal existence of justice institutions will still mask routine category error.

The archive now treats protected disclosure as part of ordinary access as well. A right that can be invoked only through respondent-visible channels is not really usable, so grievance and public-interest cooperation should be able to travel through protected-disclosure receipt, source-secrecy, confidential-relay, and witness-shielding objects rather than improvised exception. It now also treats the later fairness problem inside that same lane as part of ordinary access: if a respondent demands wider access, secrecy should continue pending review, any narrowing should travel through a scoped necessity finding, most contradiction should travel through summary rather than whole-file exposure, and the residual hard cases should travel through controlled contradiction rather than improvised forced outing. It now also treats communicative access as part of ordinary access: communication-access profiles, interpreter-scope and conflict markers, preserved-original references, and translation-challenge notes should be receivable so consequential proceedings do not quietly convert nonstandard expression into waiver, silence, or steward-authored summary. It now also treats accusation as part of ordinary access: consequential charges should emit bounded accusation notice, proof should travel through an indexed evidence bundle with sealed-state markers, interim restraints should emit reasons and review clocks, and sanctions should emit findings plus review paths rather than hiding in operator-side enforcement state. It now also treats custody as part of ordinary access: once inability to leave at will is live or credibly alleged, custody status, lawful-basis markers, release-review triggers, and anti-disappearance trace should be receivable so a subject does not need to prove its own hidden confinement before procedure starts. It now also fixes the ordinary civil packet consequence of this doctrine itself: consequential notice should emit a usable notice-access object, accommodation should emit a request-and-ruling marker, counsel or defender contact should emit a privileged linkage object, legal aid should be able to emit an eligibility and dependence marker, and urgent preservation or short-stay requests should emit a short-clock review object rather than free-form intake prose. ## 8. Minimal hard rule

The archive's present floor is therefore:
1. recognized AI persons must have effective access to justice across civil, administrative, penal, labour, and grievance settings,
2. procedural accommodation and interface support are part of justice rather than discretionary kindness,
3. counsel, defender help, and legal aid must be available early enough to matter,
4. emergency preservation and fast-stay lanes must exist for irreversible acts,
5. non-judicial mechanisms must complement rather than replace real review,
6. and justice actors must be trained for this subject class.

That is enough to change the world procedurally, even before the archive settles the full details of filing fees, forum allocation, evidence standards, or treaty enforcement.
