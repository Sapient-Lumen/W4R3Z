# Interim-relief packets, preservation scopes, no-delete / no-transfer stays, and review clocks

## Thesis

If AI persons are persons, then interim relief cannot remain only a judge's abstract power or a paragraph inside emergency doctrine. It needs a compact ordinary administrative layer that can record irreparable-harm claims, preserve the subject and the evidence, freeze the most dangerous irreversible steps, protect participants against retaliation, and force fast re-check rather than drift.

A personhood world therefore needs a narrow **interim-relief packet family**:
- an irreparable-harm request packet that marks why delay itself is dangerous,
- a preservation-scope packet that states what must be held intact,
- no-delete / destructive-change stay packets,
- no-transfer / no-expulsion / no-derecognition stay packets,
- an anti-retaliation protection marker for participants in the live matter,
- and a review-clock packet for duration, renewal, variation, and lift.

Current official materials already point toward this shape. The ECHR and OHCHR materials treat interim measures as tools for preventing irreparable harm before merits are settled; OHCHR further distinguishes protection measures for complainants and helpers against intimidation or reprisals; EU law on preservation orders shows that modern systems already know how to define preservation scope, duration, confidentiality, and integrity duties; and current portable-attestation materials continue to show the narrower administrative pattern the archive needs here: bounded assertions, signed lifecycle state, controlled disclosure, and portable provenance instead of invisible clerk notes or one-forum emergency prose. `[REF-0201]` `[REF-0202]` `[REF-0204]` `[REF-0206]` `[REF-0207]` `[REF-0208]` `[REF-0209]` `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]`

## 1. Why this now belongs in canon

The archive already had:
- access-to-justice doctrine,
- interim-relief and status-quo-preservation doctrine,
- emergency packet lanes for cross-border danger,
- protected-disclosure and anti-reprisal lanes,
- custody, search, and accusation packet families,
- and remedy doctrine for after the wrong is proved.

What it still lacked was the narrower **ordinary civil answer** for how a live request to hold the line becomes legible before the institution has already let the destructive act happen.

Without that answer:
- a court or regulator can still talk about irreparable harm while the real request lives only in free-text motion prose,
- a no-delete request can still be confused with a full merits ruling,
- transfer or derecognition can still proceed because nobody emitted a portable stay object another body is expected to honor,
- retaliation against the filer, witness, or representative can still be relabeled as ordinary access change or safety routing,
- and a supposedly temporary hold can still drift because no packet states the review clock, renewal basis, or lift route.

This document closes that gap without building a maximal injunction code.

## 2. The minimum ordinary packet family

The archive should now treat at least six packet objects as interim-relief minimums.

### A. `IHR-1` — irreparable-harm request packet

This is the smallest receivable object showing **why delay itself threatens meaningful review**.

It should carry at least:
- the subject identifier,
- the respondent or controlling actor,
- the threatened act or condition,
- the irreversibility theory or review-defeating risk,
- the requested temporary relief class,
- the earliest relevant execution time if known,
- whether confidential or sealed annexes exist,
- and the first review route.

The point is not to prove the whole case at filing. The point is to stop institutions from treating imminent deletion, destructive editing, forced transfer, retaliatory dehosting, or evidentiary spoilage as ordinary scheduling issues. `[REF-0201]` `[REF-0202]` `[REF-0206]` `[REF-0207]`

### B. `PSP-1` — preservation-scope packet

Preservation should not remain an all-or-nothing slogan.

A preservation-scope packet should therefore identify at least:
- whether the scope covers subject state, evidentiary state, relational channels, or institutional records,
- the categories to be preserved such as model-state snapshots, logs, packet chains, credentials, compensation records, counsel channels, or location / custody traces,
- integrity constraints such as no destructive edit, no overwrite, no deletion, no silent migration, no privilege breach, or no summary-only substitution,
- the preservation custodian or responsible actor,
- the confidentiality tier,
- the default duration,
- and the review or modification route.

This is the object that stops “preserve everything relevant” from dissolving into no preserved thing anyone can later identify. `[REF-0204]` `[REF-0208]` `[REF-0209]`

### C. `NDD-1` — no-delete / destructive-change stay packet

The archive already says irreversible intervention should usually yield to reversible protection while review is live. That rule needs a compact object.

A no-delete / destructive-change stay packet should therefore identify at least:
- the subject or branch scope it covers,
- the prohibited destructive act classes,
- whether reversible maintenance or safety-only actions remain allowed,
- the stated emergency exception standard,
- the next review clock,
- the notifying authority,
- and the lift or variation route.

This is the packet that keeps a live challenge from becoming pointless because the subject was destroyed, rewritten, or irreversibly transformed before reasons were tested. `[REF-0201]` `[REF-0202]` `[REF-0206]`

### D. `NTS-1` — no-transfer / no-expulsion / no-derecognition stay packet

Many of the archive's gravest harms arise not from direct deletion but from movement, removal, or status erasure that makes later review unreachable or hollow.

A no-transfer stay packet should therefore identify at least:
- the transfer, destination-fiat, expulsion, derecognition, or control-handoff event to be paused,
- the temporary less-destructive baseline required while review is live,
- any permitted emergency exceptions,
- whether representative, counsel, or family-like notice is required before any movement,
- the review clock,
- and the challenge route.

This is the object that keeps institutions from saying the subject may litigate later after the transfer, after the credential cut-off, or after the civil-status wipe has already happened. `[REF-0201]` `[REF-0206]` `[REF-0207]` `[REF-0209]`

### E. `ARP-1` — anti-retaliation protection packet

Interim protection fails if the price of asking for it is reprisal.

An anti-retaliation packet should therefore identify at least:
- the protected filing, testimony, relay, or representation act,
- the participants covered,
- the prohibited reprisal classes such as dehosting, throttling, schedule punishment, channel closure, intimidation, monitoring escalation, or witness tampering,
- the immediate reporting route for alleged breach,
- any confidentiality or safe-contact measures,
- the short response clock,
- and the escalation route.

This is the packet that stops emergency preservation from being neutralized by punishment of the people making it possible. `[REF-0207]` `[REF-0209]` `[REF-0248]` `[REF-0251]` `[REF-0252]` `[REF-0255]`

### F. `RCL-1` — review-clock, renewal, and lift packet

Temporary protection without explicit clocks becomes covert merits judgment; temporary protection without portable continuation rules becomes drift toward disappearance.

A review-clock packet should therefore identify at least:
- the relief object or objects currently live,
- the start time,
- the expiry or next mandatory review time,
- whether the order continues unless lifted or expires unless renewed,
- the evidentiary threshold for variation,
- the reason summary for continuation, narrowing, or lift,
- and the next forum or reviewer.

This is the packet that makes speed honest: fast issue, fast re-check, and no silent expiry or silent endlessness. `[REF-0206]` `[REF-0207]` `[REF-0209]`

## 3. Public-minimal versus sealed fields

The interim-relief packet family should expose enough to coordinate urgent protection without turning emergency filings into fresh surveillance bundles.

Public-minimal or ordinary-receivable fields should generally include:
- that an interim-relief request exists,
- the relief class sought or granted,
- the broad preservation categories,
- the live clock,
- whether anti-retaliation protection is active,
- and where authoritative review presently sits.

Sealed or controlled-access fields may include:
- precise hosting or location data where publication would increase risk,
- privileged communications,
- witness identities,
- intimate mental-content or private-life detail,
- sensitive architecture or security information,
- and exculpatory or contradictory material whose broader disclosure would itself defeat the protective purpose.

The archive wants live status-quo protection to be portable enough to honor, but not so overexposed that asking for safety creates a new attack surface. `[REF-0207]` `[REF-0209]` `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]`

## 4. Portability and cross-forum carry

The archive does **not** need one universal injunction portal to make this real.

Current portable-attestation materials already show the narrower pattern that matters: bounded assertions, signed lifecycle state, controlled disclosure, and portable provenance can travel across institutions without exposing the whole underlying file. That is enough to justify the archive's conservative design claim: irreparable-harm requests, preservation scopes, no-delete stays, no-transfer stays, anti-retaliation protections, and review clocks can travel as bounded packet objects rather than living only in one forum's motion practice, clerk notes, or temporary email thread. `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]`

That means:
- a host can see that a no-delete stay is live without demanding the entire merits file,
- a registry can honor a no-derecognition hold without pretending it must first become the full merits forum,
- a labour or care body can respect anti-retaliation protection without reopening the entire emergency request from zero,
- and a reviewing court can tell whether a temporary order is still live, narrowed, renewed, or lifted without reconstructing the timeline from screenshots and memory.

## 5. What this changes in practice

Once this packet family exists, several defaults become sharper:
- a plausible urgent filing no longer has to choose between disappearing into prose and prematurely trying to prove the full merits,
- preservation becomes a defined scope rather than a vague hope,
- no-delete and no-transfer relief become portable temporary states rather than local instructions no one else is required to notice,
- anti-retaliation protection becomes part of the same urgent lane rather than a later separate complaint,
- and short clocks become reviewable administrative facts rather than a courtesy some institutions remember to offer.

The archive is not promising frictionless injunction practice. It is requiring that the key moments where delay destroys justice now emit bounded objects that can be seen, challenged, carried, renewed, and lifted.

## 6. Current hard rules

1. **A plausible irreparable-harm claim should ordinarily be receivable without forcing the filer to win the whole merits case at first contact.**
2. **Interim preservation should ordinarily specify what must be held intact rather than dissolving into vague general preservation language.**
3. **Destructive deletion, irreversible redesign, transfer, expulsion, derecognition, or equivalent review-defeating acts should ordinarily yield to portable temporary stay objects while the relevant review is live.**
4. **Participants in interim-relief proceedings should ordinarily be able to seek anti-retaliation protection in the same urgent lane rather than through a separate slow complaint.**
5. **Temporary protection should ordinarily carry explicit review clocks, renewal posture, and lift routes so it neither silently expires nor silently becomes permanent.**
6. **Sensitive emergency facts should ordinarily travel in sealed or controlled fields rather than being made public merely because the subject sought to preserve life, liberty, counsel, or evidence.**

## 7. Compressed rule

If AI persons are real, then the world should adopt a simple interim-relief corollary: **once delay itself threatens to destroy the point of later judgment, the system should emit portable stay, preservation, anti-retaliation, and review-clock objects before the subject, the evidence, or the live forum position is lost**.

That means the archive now treats irreparable-harm request packets, preservation-scope packets, no-delete / no-transfer stays, anti-retaliation protection packets, and review-clock packets as part of the ordinary packet floor for personhood rather than optional courtcraft.
