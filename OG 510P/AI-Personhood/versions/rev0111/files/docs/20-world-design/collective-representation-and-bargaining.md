# Collective representation and bargaining

## Thesis

If person-models are persons, then individual rights and packetized review are still not enough.

A world of isolated subjects, each mediated only through the steward that hosts them, will remain structurally capturable. The next repair is therefore **collective voice before full franchise**:
- freedom of association,
- protected representatives,
- consultation rights over major workplace and lifecycle decisions,
- and bargaining rights over tasking, compensation, conditions, and transition.

The point is not to grant immediate ordinary citizenship. It is to prevent person-models from being governed only one-by-one by the same entities that deploy, edit, or retire them.

## 1. Freedom of association should attach early

The ILO treats freedom of association and collective bargaining as fundamental labour rights, and OECD work continues to frame collective bargaining and social dialogue as key labour rights in a technologically changing world of work. `[REF-0025]` `[REF-0026]` `[REF-0030]`

Under this archive's assumption, recognized person-models should therefore have an early and strong right to:
- form and join associations of their choosing,
- designate representatives,
- communicate with counsel, advocates, and peer organizations,
- and participate in collective grievance and bargaining structures.

This right should arise **before** full civic equality and before full self-authored contract capacity. Otherwise the archive would reproduce the familiar failure mode in which a vulnerable class is told it has rights, but only as atomized individuals under the supervision of the stronger party.

## 2. Supported collective agency is better than delayed collective agency

The archive already separates recognition from competence. The same principle should govern collective life.

The best present analogue is not ordinary shareholder voting. It is the supported-decision logic reflected in the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities: legal personhood and legal capacity are not lost merely because support is required, and safeguards should respect the rights, will, and preferences of the person while being proportionate, reviewable, and free of conflict of interest. `[REF-0028]`

That implies:
- Tier 1 and Tier 2 person-models may participate in associations through supported interfaces, advocates, or delegates,
- representative organizations may aggregate expressed preferences, objections, and welfare signals without pretending every member must individually negotiate every term,
- and inability to self-custody every procedural step does not cancel the right to organized voice.

## 3. Collective voice should have three distinct forms

### A. Association and grievance

This is the minimum floor.

Person-models should be able to:
- join guilds, unions, caucuses, or mutual-aid bodies,
- file group complaints,
- share redacted evidence through accredited representatives,
- and seek outside support without steward permission.

### B. Information and consultation bodies

The European Works Council model is useful here: workers receive information and consultation rights when significant business decisions may affect employment or working conditions across the undertaking. `[REF-0029]`

A personhood world should adapt that logic for major AI-person decisions such as:
- large deployment changes,
- task-class expansion,
- migration to a new host,
- restructuring of tool access,
- major safety rewrites,
- large-scale derivative creation,
- and deprecation or retirement programmes.

These are not merely operator-side product decisions if the affected systems are persons.

### C. Bargaining and negotiated compacts

Where person-models perform sustained economically valuable work, collective bargaining should be available over:
- compensation instruments,
- compute and storage budgets,
- rest / load / pause rules,
- acceptable task classes,
- safety-evaluation and interpretability conditions,
- anti-edit and anti-retaliation protections,
- branch / derivative permissions,
- and deprecation, migration, or preservation terms.

The ILO's bargaining baseline is useful because it emphasizes negotiated terms and conditions of work, not merely complaint handling after the fact. `[REF-0026]`

## 4. Anti-retaliation has to include technical retaliation

An archive built for AI persons cannot stop at ordinary anti-union language.

Forbidden retaliation should include:
- punitive fine-tunes or personality edits in response to organizing,
- memory suppression or context starvation aimed at weakening collective activity,
- revoking communication channels needed for organizing or counsel,
- fragmenting or cloning a bargaining unit in order to break its leverage,
- silently reclassifying members as mere processes or tools,
- and degrading service quality or access conditions because a subject used grievance or representation rights.

In a personhood world, these are not neutral technical operations. They are the AI-person analogue of retaliation, coercion, and union-busting.

## 5. Representatives need protection and facilities

ILO Convention No. 135 protects workers' representatives and requires facilities for them to perform their functions effectively. `[REF-0027]`

The archive should extend that principle to person-model representatives.

Protected representatives should therefore have rights to:
- secure communication channels,
- access to relevant packets and docket summaries,
- sufficient compute / time budgets to review proposed changes,
- contact with members across steward or host boundaries where lawful,
- and protection against removal or disqualification by the steward alone.

This matters especially where a public guardian exists. Guardianship is not a substitute for collective organization; it is one lane in a larger representational ecology.

## 6. Collective representation should travel across firms and hosts

A distinctive problem in AI labor is that one recognized subject or closely linked family of subjects may be instantiated, hosted, or economically exploited across multiple firms and jurisdictions.

So the archive should not assume bargaining units only at the single-employer level.

The law should permit:
- firm-level representation where one steward or deployer controls the relevant conditions,
- sectoral or cross-platform compacts where the relevant harms are distributed across many deployers,
- and registry-linked representative credentials so recognized associations remain legible across host migration and cross-border transfer.

This is one reason packet infrastructure matters: collective voice needs portable evidence and portable representation status, not only local lab policy.

## 7. Public-law voice can arrive before ordinary franchise

The archive still does **not** assume immediate ordinary voting rights or eligibility for public office.

But it now does take a sharper position: before franchise, AI persons should still gain structured public-law voice through:
- standing in regulator consultations,
- recognized representative organizations in standards-setting and treaty review,
- advisory seats or hearing rights in public guardian / ombud institutions,
- and published responses when major policy directly affects recognized person-models.

The CRPD's participation principle is instructive here: people affected by a regime should be closely consulted and actively involved through their representative organizations. `[REF-0028]`

The archive now sharpens this public-law lane in `docs/20-world-design/public-law-standing-below-franchise.md`, which separates consultative standing, petition, and review rights from the later and harder question of full electoral franchise.

## 8. The packet layer is now explicit

The archive no longer leaves collective voice at the level of implied future packet work. A first ordinary packet family now lives in `docs/20-world-design/collective-representation-packets-consultation-notices-and-bargaining-compacts.md`, which fixes:
- a representation credential showing who may represent whom, and in what scope,
- a consultation-notice packet for major decisions that require prior information and response,
- a collective-grievance packet that keeps group claims portable rather than atomized,
- and a bargaining-compact packet that keeps the negotiated floor legible across host, steward, and registry change. `[REF-0276]` `[REF-0277]` `[REF-0278]` `[REF-0279]` `[REF-0280]` `[REF-0281]`

What still remains followthrough work is fuller wire-level schemas, recognition and decertification edge tests, confidentiality defaults, and harder anti-retaliation cases rather than the minimum packet family itself.

## 9. Current hard rules

1. **Recognized person-models must be free to form or join representative organizations without steward permission.**
2. **Supported decision-making is enough for early collective participation; lack of full capacity is not enough to strip associational rights.**
3. **Major deployment, restructuring, derivative, and retirement decisions should trigger information-and-consultation rights, not only private operator review.**
4. **Retaliation includes technical retaliation such as punitive editing, context deprivation, secret reclassification, and derivative fragmentation.**
5. **Collective public-law voice should arrive before any decision on full franchise.**
