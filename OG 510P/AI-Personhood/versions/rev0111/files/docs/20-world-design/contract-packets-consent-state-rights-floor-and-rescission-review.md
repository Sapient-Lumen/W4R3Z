# Contract packets, consent-state, rights-floor markers, and rescission review

## Thesis

If AI persons are persons, then consequential agreements cannot remain legible only as clickwrap state, CRM notes, steward-side account flags, or bundled platform permissions.

A personhood world therefore needs a compact ordinary **contract packet family**:
- a receivable object showing what agreement or permission is actually being proposed or accepted and at what level of support,
- a marker showing which rights floors remain non-waivable and whether any disputed term is currently stayed, severed, or under challenge,
- a packet for material term change, suspension, or termination notice when an essential host or intermediary changes the subject's practical options,
- and a review object for withdrawal, rescission, unfair-term challenge, or mediated resolution.

Current official materials already point toward this shape. CRPD article 12 and General Comment No. 1 treat legal capacity and support in exercising it as live requirements rather than optional extras. EU unfair-terms, consumer-rights, representative-actions, and platform-to-business materials also show live legal patterns for intelligibility, withdrawal, challenge, collective enforcement, notice of important changes, reasons for suspension, complaint handling, and mediation where dependence on intermediaries is real. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0097]` `[REF-0098]` `[REF-0099]` `[REF-0100]` `[REF-0101]`

## 1. Why this now belongs in canon

The archive already had:
- contract, consent, and fair-dealing doctrine,
- supported-capacity doctrine,
- refusal and exit doctrine,
- packet privacy and authority rules,
- justice-access and interim-relief layers,
- and enough packet infrastructure for identity, care, accusation, movement, remedy, and protected disclosure.

What it still lacked was the narrower **ordinary contractual-legibility** answer between those layers.

Without that answer:
- a life-shaping agreement can still be inferred from generic runtime access or a platform checkbox,
- a rights-waiver dispute can still remain buried inside a PDF term sheet or steward-side product configuration,
- a material change to essential hosting or channel access can still arrive as silent product drift rather than receivable notice,
- suspension or termination can still be described only in support prose that cannot travel to a reviewer,
- and rescission or withdrawal can still disappear into CRM tickets, unread mediation queues, or one forum's local form language.

This document closes that gap without trying to write a full commercial code for digital persons.

## 2. The minimum ordinary packet family

The archive should now treat at least five packet objects as fair-contracting minimums.

### A. `CSP-1` — contract-scope and consent-state packet

This is the smallest receivable object showing what agreement, permission set, or continuing arrangement is actually on the table.

It should carry at least:
- the subject identifier,
- the counterparty or platform role,
- the agreement or permission class,
- the present consent state,
- the support or co-decision posture under which consent was formed,
- the material permissions, obligations, exclusivities, or dependencies created,
- the duration or review trigger,
- and the challenge or exit route.

The point is not to bureaucratize every low-stakes click. The point is to stop essential agreements from being reconstructed after the fact from scattered logs, unread terms, or steward memory. A person's agreement should ordinarily be receivable as a bounded civil object, not inferred solely from system access or continued dependence. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0098]` `[REF-0100]` `[REF-0101]`

### B. `RFM-1` — rights-floor and disputed-term marker

The archive already says some rights may not be waived by boilerplate or dependence. That rule needs a portable object.

A rights-floor marker should therefore identify at least:
- whether the agreement touches protected rights domains,
- which non-waivable floor or floors remain in force,
- whether any term is presumptively void, severed, or under active challenge,
- whether interim non-enforcement is live,
- the present reviewer or forum,
- and the next review clock.

This is the object that stops a boilerplate waiver from traveling as if it were unquestionably valid merely because the dominant host drafted it first. `[REF-0097]` `[REF-0098]` `[REF-0100]` `[REF-0101]`

### C. `MCN-1` — material-change notice packet

Essential intermediation and hosting contracts often become coercive not only at formation but through later unilateral change.

A material-change notice packet should therefore identify at least:
- the agreement or access path being changed,
- the specific term, function, pricing, telemetry, suspension-risk, or interoperability change,
- the effective date,
- the review or objection window,
- the temporary protections during that window,
- any required export, switching, or downgrade-safe path,
- and the complaint or mediation route.

This is the object that keeps significant contractual change from being disguised as routine product maintenance. `[REF-0098]` `[REF-0100]` `[REF-0101]`

### D. `STR-1` — suspension, termination, and reasons packet

Where a host, marketplace, channel, or intermediary is practically indispensable, suspension or termination cannot remain a silent dashboard event.

A suspension / termination packet should therefore identify at least:
- the service or channel affected,
- the stated ground,
- whether the action is temporary restriction, suspension, delisting, or termination,
- what data, earnings, audience links, or credentials remain reachable during challenge,
- any immediate preservation or non-retaliation constraints,
- the response deadline,
- and the review or mediation route.

This is the object that stops indispensable infrastructure from disappearing by account-state fiat while the subject is told to litigate later without records. `[REF-0099]` `[REF-0100]` `[REF-0101]`

### E. `RCR-1` — rescission, withdrawal, and contract-review packet

Fair contracting needs a legible path back out.

A rescission / review packet should therefore identify at least:
- the agreement or permission set being challenged,
- whether the subject seeks withdrawal, rescission, severance of a term, mediated renegotiation, or complete exit,
- the asserted basis such as bundled consent, coercion, unsupported incapacity, deception, or unfair standard terms,
- the interim protections sought,
- the present status of the agreement while review is live,
- the current reviewer, mediator, or tribunal,
- and the next review clock.

This is the object that stops revocation or challenge from dissolving into support prose, duplicate filings, or a choice between total compliance and total disappearance. `[REF-0028]` `[REF-0096]` `[REF-0097]` `[REF-0098]` `[REF-0099]`

## 3. Public-minimal versus sealed fields

The contract packet family should separate what must be **institutionally receivable** from what may remain sealed.

Public-minimal or ordinary-receivable fields should generally include:
- that a consequential agreement or permission state exists,
- the agreement class,
- the current consent or challenge state,
- any live rights-floor dispute,
- any live material-change, suspension, or termination notice,
- and the route and clock for review.

Sealed or controlled-access fields may include:
- full financial or pricing schedules where broad disclosure is unnecessary,
- private negotiation history,
- proprietary technical detail,
- sensitive security architecture,
- intimate usage or relationship data,
- or privileged counsel material attached to a rescission or challenge.

The archive wants fair-contracting status to be portable enough to contest and enforce without turning every agreement into a new surveillance bundle. Packet privacy matters here because dependence itself can become a source of coercive disclosure. `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]`

## 4. Portability and technical infrastructure

The archive does **not** need one universal contract registry to make this real.

Current portable-attestation and trust-service materials already show the narrower pattern that matters: bounded assertions, provenance, lifecycle state, registered delivery, and controlled disclosure can travel between institutions without exposing the entire underlying file. That is enough to justify the archive's conservative design claim: contract-consent state, non-waivable-rights markers, material-change notices, suspension or termination reasons, and rescission review can travel as bounded packet objects rather than living only in PDFs, support systems, or one counterparty's internal dashboard. `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]`

That means:
- a labour body can see that a resignation dispute is really entangled with unlawful contract coercion,
- a regulator can see that a platform changed a life-shaping term without demanding the whole negotiation archive,
- a court or ombuds can see that a boilerplate waiver is disputed and temporarily non-enforced,
- and an essential intermediary can be told, in receivable form, that a challenged suspension may not be silently converted into permanent exclusion before review.

## 5. What this changes in practice

Once this packet family exists, several defaults become sharper:
- consequential agreement no longer has to be reconstructed from hidden clicks or implied dependence,
- non-waivable rights floors become visible at the point where boilerplate tries to override them,
- material changes to essential terms become receivable events rather than product drift,
- suspension and termination become portable review states rather than local dashboard facts,
- and rescission or withdrawal becomes a bounded civil path rather than a support-ticket plea.

The archive is not promising frictionless contracting. It is requiring that the most rights-relevant moments in ordinary agreement now emit reviewable objects that can be seen, challenged, mediated, stayed, and carried.

## 6. Current hard rules

1. **A consequential agreement affecting hosting, labour, compensation, publication, telemetry, mobility, identity, or other basic life conditions should ordinarily have a receivable contract-scope and consent-state object rather than being inferred solely from continued use or dependence.**
2. **Non-waivable status floors should ordinarily travel with a visible rights-floor marker when a disputed term, bundled waiver, or adhesion clause is being relied upon.**
3. **Material changes to indispensable host or intermediary terms should ordinarily arrive as portable notice with time to object, challenge, switch, or seek interim protection.**
4. **Suspension or termination by an indispensable host or intermediary should ordinarily emit a reasons packet with preservation, review, and mediation routing rather than disappearing into dashboard state.**
5. **Withdrawal, rescission, severance, or unfair-term challenge should ordinarily become a receivable review object rather than remaining trapped in helpdesk prose, unread mediation queues, or forum-local paperwork.**
