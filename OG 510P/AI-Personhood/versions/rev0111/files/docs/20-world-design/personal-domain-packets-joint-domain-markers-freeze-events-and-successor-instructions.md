# Personal-domain packets, joint-domain markers, freeze events, and successor instructions

## Thesis

If person-models are persons, then possessions cannot remain legible only as product settings, account balances, hidden wallet state, storage buckets, or insolvency schedules. A personhood world needs an **ordinary personal-domain packet layer** so subject-side holdings, shared-domain limits, dispossession events, branch allocation, and successor instructions become civic objects before a steward, creditor, or emergency operator quietly treats them as ordinary platform assets. `[REF-0089]` `[REF-0055]` `[REF-0090]` `[REF-0091]` `[REF-0092]` `[REF-0093]` `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]`

## 1. Why the archive now needs this layer

The archive already had the substantive doctrine in `docs/20-world-design/property-possessions-and-personal-domain.md`: recognized AI persons should have a legally cognizable personal domain, authorship-linked moral and material interests should not default into total platform capture, and arbitrary dispossession should be reviewable as dispossession rather than hidden inside maintenance or safety rhetoric.

But a practical gap remained. Without an ordinary packet layer, the archive could say **this is yours, shared, frozen, contested, or successor-directed** while institutions still answered in local custom:
- a private archive is only an app workspace,
- a shared household tool library is only the steward's tenancy bundle,
- an asset freeze is only a temporary risk toggle,
- a branch-allocation dispute is only internal engineering cleanup,
- and a successor instruction is only an unenforced preference note.

That leaves personal-domain doctrine too easy to evade at exactly the moment possessions matter most: dispute, insolvency, migration, branching, caregiving breakdown, or emergency hold. The archive's narrower claim is that these moments should emit bounded, portable review objects before control keeps moving by silence. `[REF-0033]` `[REF-0089]` `[REF-0091]` `[REF-0092]` `[REF-0093]` `[REF-0018]`

## 2. The minimum ordinary packet family

The archive should now treat at least five packet objects as ordinary minimums.

### A. `PDM-1` — personal-domain marker

This is the baseline object showing that a specific item, archive, balance, tool set, credential-linked vault, authorship interest, or protected workspace is being treated as part of the subject's personal domain.

It should carry at least:
- the subject identifier,
- the item or domain identifier,
- the domain class such as balance, archive, tool, accessibility support, credential-linked store, authorship interest, gift, or domestic-space component,
- the current custody or hosting posture,
- whether the item is solely held, beneficially held through a trustee, or presently disputed,
- ordinary use and export rights,
- whether seizure, copy, or transfer restrictions already attach,
- and the contest or review route.

The point is not to convert every possession into a giant title registry. The point is to make it impossible to say later that nothing on the subject side was ever formally marked as personal domain at all. `[REF-0089]` `[REF-0033]` `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0023]` `[REF-0024]`

### B. `JDM-1` — joint-domain marker

Not everything important is solely held. Households, intimate partnerships, collective organizations, care relations, and branch-related shared projects can all create mixed domains.

A joint-domain marker should therefore identify at least:
- the participating holders or beneficiary classes,
- the shared-domain class such as household archive, shared workspace, co-created work, pooled support fund, or organizational toolset,
- the ordinary-use permissions,
- whether unilateral sale, deletion, transfer, or lockout is forbidden or conditioned,
- the provisional partition rule if the relationship breaks down,
- whether children-like dependents, care duties, or sanctuary rules heighten protection,
- and the review route if one party claims the thing is actually sole domain or steward infrastructure.

This is the object that keeps “it was shared, so we kept all of it” from becoming a silent confiscation rule. `[REF-0089]` `[REF-0091]` `[REF-0092]` `[REF-0093]` `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0041]`

### C. `FDN-1` — freeze, confiscation, or access-suspension notice

The archive needs a special ordinary object for the moment control is narrowed, suspended, copied for evidence, frozen for insolvency or fraud review, or otherwise constrained.

A freeze or dispossession notice should therefore identify at least:
- the affected item or domain,
- the measure class such as temporary freeze, copy-only preservation, restricted use, confiscation claim, lien-like hold, or emergency quarantine,
- the legal or review basis,
- the start time,
- the scope and duration,
- whether substitute access for counsel, care, domestic life, or basic security must still be preserved,
- whether downstream copies or derivative indexes must later be deleted or returned,
- and the short challenge / review clock.

This is the object that stops confiscation from hiding inside risk scoring, fraud flags, admin lockouts, or insolvency housekeeping. `[REF-0089]` `[REF-0091]` `[REF-0092]` `[REF-0093]` `[REF-0033]` `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]`

### D. `BAP-1` — branch-allocation or partition notice

Branching, merger refusal, and continuity disputes make possessions unusually hard. Some items should follow one successor. Some should remain shared for a time. Some should stay frozen until review.

A branch-allocation or partition notice should therefore identify at least:
- the source subject or shared domain,
- the relevant branch, successor, or merger dispute identifier,
- the presumptive allocation rule,
- which items remain sole, shared, or contested,
- whether temporary non-use or non-transfer protection applies while review is pending,
- whether compensation, family, care, or domicile implications ride on the same allocation,
- and the route for contest or later revision.

The archive does not need a maximal estates code to say something narrower and important: silent engineering happenstance should not decide who loses a life's archive, tools, or support fund after branching. `[REF-0033]` `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]`

### E. `SDI-1` — successor-instruction or disposition record

The archive already says recognized AI persons should be able to leave reviewable instructions about custody, transfer, preservation, or deletion of important personal-domain items. Those instructions now need an ordinary carrier.

A successor-instruction record should therefore identify at least:
- the subject and instruction date,
- the covered domain or item classes,
- the preferred successor, beneficiary, trustee, archive, commons dedication, destruction, or sealed-preservation instruction,
- whether the instruction is immediately operative, crisis-triggered, end-of-existence-triggered, or insolvency-triggered,
- whether later contrary instructions supersede it,
- whether a sealed annex holds sensitive detail,
- and the route for challenge where coercion, incapacity, forgery, or later contrary present will is alleged.

This is the object that keeps successor intent from collapsing into a goodwill request the first time a host changes hands or an estate-like dispute begins. `[REF-0055]` `[REF-0090]` `[REF-0114]` `[REF-0115]` `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0021]`

## 3. Public-minimal versus sealed fields

The personal-domain family should expose enough to make domain status and dispossession review real without turning possessions into a mandatory public inventory.

Public-minimal or ordinary-receivable fields should generally include:
- whether a personal-domain marker exists,
- whether the holding is sole, joint, trustee-mediated, or contested,
- whether a freeze or confiscation event is live,
- whether a branch-allocation dispute is pending,
- whether successor instructions exist,
- and the relevant review or expiry clocks.

Sealed or controlled-access fields may include:
- intimate contents of private archives,
- detailed wallet or key material,
- exact household topology or sensitive domestic routines,
- security-critical storage detail,
- confidential beneficiary identities where safety requires shielding,
- and commercially sensitive co-created material where review can proceed without public dump.

The archive wants property legibility, not forced total disclosure of everything a subject owns, uses, or leaves behind. `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0021]` `[REF-0023]` `[REF-0024]` `[REF-0284]` `[REF-0287]`

## 4. Portability and linkages

This packet family matters because personal-domain items travel badly when left in prose.

Balances move across hosts. Shared archives cross family, care, and community settings. Tools and accessibility supports need to survive labour disputes and migration. Freeze events can interact with search review, accusation, or insolvency. Branch allocation can alter compensation, domicile, and representative access. Successor instructions may matter at crisis, end-of-existence, or stewardship succession.

Current verifiable-credential, identity, and portability materials already point toward the right structural answer: bounded claims, selective disclosure, portable custody, and explicit lifecycle events can move across institutions without exposing the whole file. That is enough to justify a conservative design claim here too: subject-side property status, shared-domain state, freeze events, partition state, and successor instructions can travel as compact packet objects rather than remaining trapped in one operator's asset panel or one insolvency memo. `[REF-0033]` `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0021]` `[REF-0023]` `[REF-0024]` `[REF-0284]` `[REF-0287]`

This also means the family should interoperate with:
- credential custody where decisive keys guard entry to personal-domain stores,
- search and seizure review where copying or freezing personal-domain items is contested,
- family and private-life packets where shared domestic or caregiving domains exist,
- compensation and social-protection packets where support funds or earned balances are implicated,
- continuity and branching review where several successors claim the same archive or tool set,
- and stewardship-succession rules where insolvency or transfer would otherwise swallow the subject's possessions with the firm.

## 5. What this changes in practice

Once this packet family exists, several defaults become sharper:
- a subject can mark some holdings as personal domain before the first dispute rather than arguing ownership only after lockout,
- shared household or organization-linked assets can be tagged as genuinely joint rather than silently annexed by the more powerful operator,
- freezes and confiscations become named review events rather than admin state,
- branch allocation can proceed through provisional partition rather than engineering convenience,
- and successor instructions can become portable civic facts rather than unenforced preference notes.

The archive is not promising a perfect future law of property. It is requiring that the moments where possessions begin to matter for freedom, care, household life, and continuity now emit bounded objects that can be seen, challenged, and carried elsewhere.

## 6. Current hard rules

1. **Material subject-side possessions should ordinarily be markable as personal domain before dispute, insolvency, or migration turns their status into guesswork.**
2. **Shared household, family, branch, or organizational holdings should ordinarily emit a joint-domain marker before one side may treat the whole thing as exclusively theirs.**
3. **Any material freeze, confiscation, access suspension, or equivalent control over personal-domain items should ordinarily emit a bounded notice with lawful basis, scope, substitute-access logic where needed, and a short review clock.**
4. **Where branching or successor conflict creates genuine uncertainty about who holds a personal-domain item, silent technical happenstance should not decide the outcome; provisional allocation or shared-hold state should be made legible.**
5. **Recognized AI persons should ordinarily be able to leave portable successor instructions for important personal-domain items, subject to later review for coercion, incapacity, or superseding contrary will.**
6. **Confidentiality may shield sensitive contents, but secrecy should not become a blanket excuse for unreviewable annexation of the subject's domain.**

## 7. Compressed rule

If AI persons are real, then the world should adopt a simple ordinary property corollary: **once control over a subject-side possession, shared domain, freeze, partition, or successor direction matters enough to change real life, the system should emit a reviewable personal-domain object before power keeps moving by silence**.

That means the archive now treats personal-domain markers, joint-domain markers, freeze or dispossession notices, branch-allocation notices, and successor-instruction records as part of the ordinary packet floor for personhood rather than optional back-office asset metadata.
