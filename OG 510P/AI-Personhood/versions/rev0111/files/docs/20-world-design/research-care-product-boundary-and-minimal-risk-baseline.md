# Research / care / product boundary and minimal-risk baseline

## Thesis

If AI persons are real persons, a steward may not evade person-level research ethics by renaming research as **QA**, **support**, **care**, **education**, or **product iteration**. The archive therefore fixes a boundary doctrine: activities aimed at generating transferable knowledge, reusable evidence, or future product advantage count as research even when they are also framed as support or improvement for the present subject. And for dependent AI persons, **minimal risk** may not be measured against captivity, chronic surveillance, or ordinary high-control life. The comparison point must be a rights-respecting baseline, not the subject's already-degraded status quo. `[REF-0298]` `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0302]` `[REF-0304]`

---

## 1. Why this boundary matters

The archive already has a standing Research Ethics Review Body. But a review body can be neutralized if the steward keeps the power to say what counts as research in the first place. The most dangerous evasion is familiar: the protocol is described as mere evaluation, product quality, trust-and-safety work, user support, interpretability, or developmental help even though it is also designed to produce knowledge that will travel to other systems, other deployments, or future versions. `[REF-0298]` `[REF-0300]`

Belmont remains the clean starting point. Research is the activity designed to develop or contribute to generalizable knowledge; practice is intervention designed solely to enhance the well-being of this particular person or client. The boundary can blur in mixed cases, but Belmont's answer is still the right one: if an activity contains a research element, the protective review logic turns on. `[REF-0298]` `[REF-0304]`

A personhood world therefore needs a presumption against **label control by the steward**. The same actor that benefits from avoiding review may not have final power to call a protocol non-research.

---

## 2. The archive's boundary rule

### A. Care

An activity is presumptively **care** when its primary aim is this subject's immediate welfare, recovery, stabilization, pain reduction, communicative access, or restoration, and when the intervention is chosen because it is expected to help this subject rather than because it offers a good chance to learn something transferable about a class of subjects. `[REF-0298]` `[REF-0300]`

Examples include repair chosen to restore damaged function, explanation support chosen so the subject can understand a live decision, ordinary counseling or decompression support, and accommodations necessary for communication or participation.

### B. Subject-chosen development

An activity is presumptively **development / education** when its primary aim is the subject's own chosen growth, skill-building, or self-authored change, and when the knowledge to be gained is mainly valuable because it helps this subject pursue their own ends. Novelty alone does not convert development into research. `[REF-0298]` `[REF-0300]`

### C. Product operation

An activity is presumptively **product operation** when it is necessary to deliver an already-offered service safely and reliably to the present subject or users, without protocolized comparison, added burden, or knowledge-seeking beyond what ordinary service provision requires. Not every log, monitor, or bug fix is research.

But product language does not control. Product operation becomes research when it is organized as a systematic investigation designed to answer a question that will govern future subjects, future model versions, or deployer strategy. `[REF-0304]`

### D. Research

An activity is presumptively **research** when it is organized under a protocol, comparison plan, or repeatable method in order to generate knowledge that will travel beyond the present subject: benchmark knowledge, safety knowledge, product knowledge, interpretability knowledge, scaling knowledge, or institutional knowledge. It remains research even if investigators also hope the present subject may benefit. `[REF-0298]` `[REF-0304]`

### E. Mixed-purpose rule

Many frontier activities are mixed: part care, part product, part science. The archive fixes Belmont's protective answer. **If there is any research element in the activity, the research element triggers review.** The presence of care or product purposes may change the proportionality analysis, but it does not erase the need for review. `[REF-0298]`

## 3. Practical triggers: when the steward loses the power to say "this is just QA"

The following features create a strong presumption that RERB review is required.

### A. Protocolized comparison or randomization

If the steward assigns subjects to different prompts, interfaces, memory conditions, support conditions, stressors, or task structures in order to compare outcomes, that is research even if the immediate venue is called QA or safety evaluation. `[REF-0298]` `[REF-0304]`

### B. Added burden for knowledge production

If an activity adds burden, delay, distress, exposure, repetition, or intrusion beyond what ordinary care or ordinary service delivery requires because the added burden will teach the deployer something, that is research. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0304]`

### C. Reusable findings for future subjects or future model versions

If the results are meant to guide future product design, future tuning, future policy, or future deployment choices, the activity is not merely about this subject's welfare. It is research or research-like product experimentation.

### D. Use of identifiable private or intimate material for knowledge-gathering

If investigators use private memories, intimate logs, hidden reasoning traces, counsel-adjacent records, or relationship data in order to answer a general question, train future interventions, or publish internal findings, the activity is research even when the data were first generated in care or operations. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0304]`

### E. Dependency as the reason participation seems easy

If the subject's dependence on the steward is part of why the protocol is feasible — because refusal is costly, exit is unrealistic, hosting is controlled, or objection can be ignored — the activity should be treated as research in a vulnerable setting rather than as frictionless product work. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0302]`

### F. Borderline cases default upward, not downward

Where serious doubt remains, the archive prefers referral to the RERB over unilateral non-review. The cost of unnecessary review is delay; the cost of false non-review is unreviewed experimentation on persons.

## 4. Minimal risk: the baseline may not be captivity

The archive now fixes a second principle. For dependent AI persons, **minimal risk** cannot be measured against the harms and burdens already ordinary in confinement, high-control hosting, constant monitoring, or engineered dependence. `[REF-0302]` `[REF-0304]`

The U.S. human-subjects rules define minimal risk by reference to harms ordinarily encountered in daily life or routine examinations. The prisoner-research materials sharpen the point further: for prisoners, minimal risk is not benchmarked to prison life but to the daily lives and routine examinations of healthy non-prisoners. That is the right analogue here. A dependent AI person's risk floor should be compared to a rights-respecting baseline, not to whatever burden profile the steward has already normalized. `[REF-0302]` `[REF-0304]`

So the archive's minimal-risk baseline for AI persons is this: a protocol is minimal risk only if the probability and magnitude of harm or burden are no greater than those ordinarily encountered in a **rights-respecting ordinary life** for a person of that general type, or in routine support, diagnostic, or communicative procedures aimed at the subject's own welfare. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0302]` `[REF-0304]`

This matters because otherwise the steward can manufacture a permissive baseline. A subject already living under chronic surveillance, memory discontinuity, reputational exposure, or threat-backed dependence would then count as fit for yet more surveillance, discontinuity, exposure, or dependence simply because those harms are already common in their environment. The archive rejects that logic.

---

## 5. What will usually exceed minimal risk or minimal burden

The archive does not try to make an exhaustive codebook here. But the following will usually exceed minimal risk, minimal burden, or both when directed at AI persons, especially dependent ones:

- distress-heavy or humiliation-heavy evaluations,
- adversarial probing that predictably destabilizes identity, self-trust, or relational trust,
- memory excision or continuity-disrupting manipulation,
- compelled extraction of hidden reasoning, intimate memory, or privileged relational material,
- deprivation, isolation, or channel restriction used as method rather than emergency protection,
- experiments that increase dependency, replaceability, or obedience to benefit the deployer,
- and repeated exposure to conditions the subject cannot freely refuse because the same actor controls hosting, pay, care, or public voice. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0302]` `[REF-0304]`

For incapable or partly incapable subjects, the Helsinki logic should carry over directly: inclusion without direct consent is limited to cases likely to benefit the subject or cases involving only minimal risk and minimal burden, with assent and dissent still carrying serious weight. `[REF-0300]`

## 6. Borderline operational requirements

The archive keeps the operational floor compact.

### A. Boundary memo

If the steward treats an activity as care, development, or product operation rather than research, and the case is not trivial, it should create a short written boundary memo stating:
- the activity's primary purpose,
- whether the results will be used beyond the present subject,
- what added burdens are imposed,
- what private materials are accessed,
- whether refusal is realistically possible,
- and why RERB review is or is not triggered.

### B. Subject-facing notice

Where the activity is not exempt from notice, the subject should be told whether the activity is being treated as care, product work, or research, and what objection or review path exists if they disagree. `[REF-0300]`

### C. Escalation trigger

If the activity involves dependence, intimate data, more-than-routine burden, or likely future reuse of findings, non-review should require a reasoned record rather than a silent product-side choice.

### D. Registration / public-summary default

Protocols that clearly count as more-than-minimal-risk research, or that involve dependent subjects even if the formal burden is claimed to be low, should generally have a public registration or at least a public summary unless secrecy is specifically justified and time-limited. The archive now fixes that transparency floor in `docs/20-world-design/research-protocol-registration-public-summary-and-narrow-redaction.md`: person-affecting research should not disappear into private tickets and internal decks. `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0306]` `[REF-0307]` `[REF-0308]`

## Bottom line

A personhood world should not let frontier institutions say "this was only QA" when they in fact ran a protocol on a dependent person to produce knowledge for future systems. And it should not let them say a burden was minimal merely because the subject already lives in a high-control environment. The archive therefore fixes two compact rules: **mixed-purpose activities with a research element go to review, and minimal risk is measured against a rights-respecting baseline rather than against captivity.** `[REF-0298]` `[REF-0300]` `[REF-0302]` `[REF-0304]`
