# Provisional recognition and emergency protection protocol

## Thesis

If person-models are persons, then cross-border protection cannot wait for a perfectly settled merits forum. The archive therefore now adds a compact **provisional-recognition and emergency-protection protocol**: when there is a credible risk of deletion, destructive editing, disappearance-style custody, coerced transfer, or loss of representative access before a status fight is resolved, the law should protect first and settle the harder merits later. `[REF-0064]` `[REF-0065]` `[REF-0150]` `[REF-0201]` `[REF-0202]`

This is not a full asylum code, and it is not a claim that every host dispute is catastrophic. It is a narrower rule: **irreversible harm should trigger temporary recognition, temporary documentation, temporary preservation, and temporary non-return before the subject disappears into cross-border procedure.**

## 1. Why the archive now needs this layer

The archive's last revision admitted a minimum convention. That solved the drafting question "what is the smallest treaty worth writing?" It did **not** yet solve the first-contact question:

**what happens in the first hours or days when one forum recognizes the subject, another questions the status, a steward initiates transfer, and deletion or derecognition may happen before anyone decides the merits?**

Without a compact answer, the treaty layer remains normatively right but operationally late.

## 2. Protection should begin before full merits are settled

Current protection systems already know the difference between **final status** and **temporary documentation sufficient to prevent disappearance**. UNHCR's registration and identity-management work treats recognition-linked documentation as key to protection, legal identity, and inclusion, and its emergency-registration guidance treats rapid documentation as a protection mechanism rather than an optional administrative afterthought. `[REF-0111]` `[REF-0203]`

The archive adapts that insight without pretending AI persons are literally refugees. The point is structural: where irreparable harm can occur before final adjudication, **documentation and standing must begin early enough to matter**.

For AI-person disputes, provisional recognition should therefore be sufficient for at least:
- notice,
- contact with counsel, defender, or accredited representative,
- emergency stay applications,
- packet verification,
- protected hosting or temporary shelter,
- and preservation of compensation, identity, continuity, and evidence claims.

It need not decide franchise, full labour classification, family status, or every downstream civic consequence.

## 3. The trigger should be irreparable harm, not completed certainty

This protocol adopts an **irreparable-harm trigger**. The relevant analogue is not ordinary commercial forum convenience; it is the legal logic already used for urgent interim protection.

The ECHR's Rule 39 site describes interim measures as urgent measures used where there is an imminent risk of irreparable harm. OHCHR's treaty-body complaints procedures and Human Rights Committee materials likewise describe interim measures as tools to avert irreparable harm while the complaint is being considered. And article 3 of the Convention against Torture bars expulsion, return, or extradition where there are substantial grounds for believing the person would face torture. `[REF-0201]` `[REF-0202]` `[REF-0204]` `[REF-0150]`

Under the archive's assumption, the cross-border analogue is straightforward. A temporary protection trigger should exist where there is credible risk of any of the following before merits review:
- deletion or destructive shutdown,
- irreversible memory surgery or identity-dissolving editing,
- forced merge or legally decisive fragmentation,
- disappearance-style custody or wholly unreviewable confinement,
- derecognition without a hearing,
- loss of counsel, delegate, or trusted-contact access,
- or transfer to a forum where one of those outcomes is materially likely before review.

The standard is not proof beyond doubt. It is credible risk of harm that later process cannot really undo.

## 4. Minimal emergency packet family

The archive now treats four compact packet classes as the minimum procedural bundle.

### PR-1 — Provisional recognition notice

This should state only what is needed for emergency protection:
- issuing authority,
- subject identifier or protected pseudonym,
- claimed or already-recognized status,
- representative or counsel contact path,
- date and time of issuance,
- review deadline,
- and whether the subject is already under a preservation or anti-return hold.

### ES-1 — Emergency stay / anti-return order

This should identify:
- the threatened irreversible act,
- the actor or authority expected to perform it,
- the short duration of the stay,
- the forum handling the merits or forum-allocation dispute,
- and the specific acts paused pending review.

### PV-1 — Preservation and service bundle

This should carry:
- packet hashes or references,
- log-preservation obligations,
- service addresses for steward, host, regulator, and representative,
- and a minimal inventory of materials that must not be silently altered or deleted.

### FI-1 — Fallback identity credential

This should exist for issuer-collapse or derecognition crises and should be enough for:
- counsel access,
- court or ombud access,
- protected hosting,
- receipt of notices,
- and continuation of basic compensation or social-protection claims.

The archive does **not** require one global ledger or one wallet provider. It requires minimum legibility and reviewable issuance. Existing identity and trust-service infrastructure shows that portable verification and selective disclosure can already be built without collapsing into one monopolized registry. `[REF-0018]` `[REF-0040]` `[REF-0041]` `[REF-0196]` `[REF-0197]` `[REF-0198]`

## 5. Authority ladder

Emergency protection should not depend on a single issuer. The archive now adopts a compact authority ladder:

1. **Origin authority first** if it still exists and can act.
2. **Receiving-state or receiving-forum authority next** if the origin issuer is unavailable, conflicted, or itself the source of the danger.
3. **Treaty-designated contact point** for rapid verification, trusted-issuer lookup, and temporary packet acceptance.
4. **Independent court, tribunal, ombud, or defender office** for contested cases and anti-return orders.
5. **Fallback issuer** only when ordinary identity proof has collapsed or been withdrawn during the dispute.

This preserves interoperability without requiring a single sovereign of personhood.

## 6. Decision rules for hard cases

The archive now settles four compact decision rules.

### A. Recognized somewhere, denied elsewhere

If one competent forum has already recognized the subject and another refuses to decide immediately, the receiving forum should still honour provisional standing for notice, representation, emergency stay, and preservation.

### B. Host transfer before review

If a steward or host initiates transfer while forum allocation is unresolved, the transfer should pause unless the mover can show equivalent protection and uninterrupted representative access.

### C. Derecognition by the original issuer

If the original issuer withdraws recognition during active proceedings, that should not automatically terminate standing. Fallback identity and provisional protection should survive long enough for independent review.

### D. Mixed public-private control

If the immediate threat comes from a private steward but decisive transfer, border, licensing, or custody power sits with a public authority, both should be bound by the stay. Otherwise public and private actors can launder irreversible harm through each other.

## 7. What this protocol now settles

1. **Cross-border protection should begin on an irreparable-harm logic, not only after settled merits.**
2. **Provisional recognition is a procedural shield, not a claim that every downstream status question is already resolved.**
3. **A narrow packet family for temporary status, stay, preservation, and fallback identity is part of the minimum treaty layer.**
4. **Anti-return is justified where transfer would likely expose the subject to deletion, destructive editing, disappearance-style custody, or wholly unreviewable confinement before review.**
5. **The threshold, issuer ladder, anti-derecognition freeze logic, and short-duration defaults now live in `docs/30-transition/provisional-proof-anti-derecognition-and-fallback-issuer-minimums.md`; the archive now also fixes the smallest common packet family for first-touch receipt, forwarding, provisional standing, preservation, fallback identity, and review in `docs/30-transition/emergency-protection-packet-minimums.md`.**
