What it still lacked was the shortest joined note for the next natural question:

**if the targeted weak signal is not actually improving after first corrections, what should a serious climate programme do next without drifting into churn, ceremony, or random escalation?**

This note is a compression note, not a new doctrinal branch.
Its job is to gather the archive's sequencing rule, boundary rule, diagnosis rule, remedy rule, verification rule, readiness rule, and off-normal rule into one first-stop second-pass map [S122][S123][S140][S141][S142][S143][S144].

## Fast rule

**If first corrections do not improve the targeted weak signal, do not declare partial success, do not keep widening the same remedy by habit, and do not jump straight to ideology. Re-open diagnosis, test whether the failure layer was misidentified, strengthen or change the correction at the actual bottleneck, tighten the proof gate, and decide explicitly whether the situation still belongs to routine correction or now warrants readiness downgrade, stronger tripwires, or entry into a narrower detour regime.**

## The compact canon

### 1. Re-open diagnosis before scaling the same fix

If the targeted weak signal does not improve, the first presumption should be that either:
- the failing layer was misidentified
- the remedy was aimed at the right layer but the wrong choke point
- the remedy landed but was too weak for the actual constraint
- the measured signal was noisier, slower, or dirtier than assumed
- or a boundary failure, burden problem, or detour leak is masking the intended gain

Do not respond by simply enlarging the same programme because it is already underway.
Re-open `142` and ask whether the first failure-location call still looks like the best explanation [S142][S144].

### 2. Distinguish “wrong layer” from “insufficient force”

The second pass should answer a sharp fork:

- **wrong layer:** the weak signal is mainly being driven elsewhere than first thought
- **right layer, insufficient force:** the first correction targeted the right place but did not push hard enough, fast enough, or narrowly enough

This distinction matters because the first case needs **diagnostic redirection**, while the second needs **stronger correction at the same point**.
Confusing the two produces both churn and underreaction [S139][S142][S143][S144].

### 3. Tighten proof and time windows before celebrating lagged or ambiguous movement

Many system signals move slowly or noisily.
That does **not** justify grading a correction as successful on anecdote, spend, headlines, or pipeline growth.
Instead, the second pass should tighten:
- the exact weak signal being watched
- the relevant time window for expected movement
- the population or place where the effect should appear
- and the confounders or measurement defects that might be hiding the effect

If those conditions still do not show improvement, routine optimism is not evidence.
The programme should either change the remedy or escalate the operating mode [S133][S138][S141][S144].

### 4. Escalate by layer, not by theatre

When the first correction fails, escalation should stay **layer-matched**:

- if truth is failing, increase comparability, audit, liability, and enforcement
- if throughput is failing, clear the actual queue, permit, network, or workforce choke point
- if burden is failing, shift more hassle, risk, and upfront cost off normal actors
- if protection is failing, harden exposure reduction and continuity faster where harm is concentrating
- if governance or readiness is failing, tighten ownership, clocks, drills, tripwires, and detour discipline
- if cross-border constraints are binding, lower capital cost, align standards, or secure supply before demanding more domestic heroics

Escalation should be **more specific and more binding**, not merely louder [S124][S131][S132][S133][S134][S135][S143].

### 5. Decide explicitly whether routine correction is still enough

The second pass must ask an operating-question, not only a policy-question:

**Is this still a routine underperformance problem, or has it become a readiness / detour problem?**

Move toward readiness downgrade, stronger tripwires, or live detour governance when:
- the weak signal is materially worsening rather than merely flat
- harms are accumulating faster than routine correction can plausibly catch up
- continuity, safety, affordability, or legitimacy are now at risk
- or temporary workaround behaviour is already becoming the de facto system

Do not keep the programme in routine mode out of optics if the operating reality has already crossed into stress governance [S103][S104][S109][S111][S112][S117][S118][S119][S120][S121][S122][S123].

### 6. Prevent churn by recording the new hypothesis and the new trigger

A proper second pass should leave a visible trace:
- what weak signal failed to improve
- what the revised diagnosis now is
- what stronger or redirected correction will run next
- what new evidence would count as success
- and what trigger would force another escalation rather than a third ceremonial retry

That keeps the archive aligned with real learning rather than repeating the same correction with new rhetoric [S120][S142][S143][S144].
