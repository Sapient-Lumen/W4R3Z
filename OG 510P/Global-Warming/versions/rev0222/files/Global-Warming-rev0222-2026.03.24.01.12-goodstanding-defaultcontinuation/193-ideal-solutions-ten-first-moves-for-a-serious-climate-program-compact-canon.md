## The ten first moves

### 1. Unblock grids, interconnection, and distribution first
The first move is to release the clean-electric backbone by expanding transmission, distribution, interconnection, and queue throughput.
This goes first because lack of grid capacity is now a binding bottleneck, with large queues already stalling renewables, storage, and major loads [S102][S103][S178].

House rule: **if clean projects cannot connect, many later climate promises become theatre.**

### 2. Build cheap clean power, storage, and flexibility faster than demand grows
Once the network can carry more, the next move is abundant clean generation plus storage and demand flexibility so electrification does not simply push stress around the system.
This matters because electricity demand is rising quickly from heat, cooling, electrification, and digital loads, while clean investment momentum only becomes climate success if the cleaner supply actually outruns the new demand [S100][S102][S106][S107][S111].

House rule: **do not electrify into a dirty or brittle system.**

### 3. Attack waste in buildings and cooling before paying to serve it forever
The third move is mass efficiency: better envelopes, passive cooling, efficient appliances, heat pumps, district solutions where suitable, and strong performance standards for new and existing buildings.
This belongs near the front because it lowers bills, reduces peak stress, shrinks required grid buildout, and directly protects people from heat [S104][S106][S143][S168][S169].

House rule: **the cheapest clean kilowatt-hour is the one the system never has to supply.**

### 4. Electrify ordinary mobility and shape cities so demand does not keep growing unnecessarily
The fourth move is road-transport electrification plus urban and freight design that reduces avoidable travel, vehicle size, and congestion.
That means EVs, buses, rail where useful, charging, freight efficiency, and city form that cuts compulsory car dependence rather than preserving it and trying to fuel it more cleanly [S30][S31][S32][S77][S79][S98][S106].

House rule: **clean vehicles help, but cleaner settlement patterns reduce the size of the problem itself.**

### 5. Run methane as a parallel emergency programme
The fifth move is not “later once CO2 is solved.”
It is a parallel emergency lane across oil and gas, coal, waste, livestock, rice, and refrigerant management where fast measured cuts can slow near-term warming and reduce obvious waste [S8][S11][S19][S21][S66][S67].

House rule: **fast warming pollutants get fast operational response.**

### 6. Stop new fossil lock-in and manage decline on purpose
The sixth move is explicit fossil decline: coal retirement schedules, no-new-lock-in screens, subsidy reform, cleanup liability, and worker-and-region transition policy.
Clean addition by itself is not enough if governments and firms keep adding or extending long-lived fossil assets, especially where mixed signals deepen later lock-in [S9][S23][S24][S191][S192][S193].

House rule: **build clean and deliberately shrink the dirty default.**

### 7. Protect ecosystems, water systems, and agrifood stability as core climate infrastructure
The seventh move is to treat forests, peatlands, wetlands, watersheds, agrifood methane, nitrogen losses, and food waste as part of the core solution rather than as environmental side issues.
This matters because these systems affect emissions, resilience, hydrology, disaster risk, and food security at the same time [S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S36][S37][S38][S39][S72][S73][S74][S75].

House rule: **do not solve carbon while allowing land, water, and food systems to keep unravelling.**

### 8. Treat adaptation, cooling, and exposure reduction as part of the solution itself
The eighth move is direct protection: cooling access, heat-health systems, resilient care and water services, early warning, stronger rebuilding rules, and dignified retreat where in-place defence stops being credible.
Even a perfect mitigation surge from now on would not erase already-banked risk, so protection is not a side lane or consolation prize [S12][S15][S16][S17][S40][S41][S81][S82][S83][S84][S85][S86][S87][S88][S89][S90].

House rule: **a climate programme that cuts future warming but leaves present exposure untouched is incomplete.**

### 9. Use industrial standards and procurement to clean materials while reserving scarce tools for true residuals
The ninth move is to decarbonize cement, steel, freight, shipping, aviation, and other hard sectors with material efficiency, common standards, procurement, and sharply rationed use of scarce clean molecules, biomass, carbon capture, and removals.
This belongs after the easier electrified backbone and end-use moves because premium tools should be saved for genuinely hard cases rather than spread thinly across uses that already have simpler substitutes [S26][S27][S28][S29][S33][S34][S35][S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S165][S166][S167].

House rule: **reserve premium tools for the hard remainder, not for avoidable delay in easier sectors.**

### 10. Build the finance, state capacity, and truth infrastructure that lets the package spread
The tenth move is not “administration later.”
It is lower cost of capital for poorer countries, guarantees and concessional finance where needed, local administrative staffing, workforce pipelines, simple public-facing delivery, and live measurement that separates physical outcomes from paper claims [S13][S91][S92][S93][S94][S95][S96][S113][S114][S143][S176][S178][S190].

House rule: **if the package cannot be financed, staffed, administered, and verified across ordinary places, it is not yet an ideal solution.**

## Ordering rule

The archive’s practical ordering rule is now simple:
- **backbone before mass electrification bottlenecks harden**
- **efficiency before oversized replacement**
- **methane and ecosystem protection in parallel, not after the power buildout**
- **fossil retirement on purpose, not by hope**
- **adaptation alongside mitigation, not after it**
- **scarce fuels and removals after easier substitutions are exhausted**
- **finance, staffing, and measurement as enabling architecture from the start rather than as late-stage support work**
