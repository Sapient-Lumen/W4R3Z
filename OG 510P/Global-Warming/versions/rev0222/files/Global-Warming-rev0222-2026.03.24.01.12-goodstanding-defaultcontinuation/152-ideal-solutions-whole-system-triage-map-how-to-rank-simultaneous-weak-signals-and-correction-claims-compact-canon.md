## Fast rule

**When many weak signals are live, rank them in this order: stop-harm urgency, irreversibility and lock-in risk, system leverage and bottleneck release, truth or integrity risk, and copyable correction potential. Put only a short list on the live correction queue. Park the rest visibly with owners, watch signals, and recheck dates rather than pretending they are being actively solved.**

## The compact triage map

### 1. Put stop-harm and continuity threats at the front of the queue
A weak signal goes to the front when delay would likely produce direct human harm, lifeline failure, severe affordability shock, or immediate protection loss.
Examples include:
- heat-health failure
- outage risk for critical services
- flood-protection failure
- water-security breakdown
- dirty fallback that is already injuring people or removing basic continuity [S103][S104][S105][S109][S123][S134].

This is not because these issues are rhetorically vivid.
It is because the archive already says stop-harm protection outranks routine comfort and routine delivery speed [S107][S123][S124][S134].

### 2. Next, pull forward anything that is creating lock-in, irreversibility, or repeated-loss traps
Even if a problem is not the most visibly painful today, it should move up the queue if delay is about to harden a dirty or dangerous state:
- new fossil lock-in
- rebuild to the wrong default
- repeated-loss rebuilding in exposed zones
- capital replacement cycles that will be missed
- governance drift that normalizes a temporary dirty fallback [S81][S82][S83][S84][S102][S108][S123][S141][S148][S149].

A serious programme should not let an apparently quieter issue sit in the backlog if waiting would make the later fix much harder or much more expensive.

### 3. Then rank by bottleneck release and cross-system leverage
After stop-harm and lock-in questions are surfaced, routine triage should ask:
**if we spend scarce correction capacity here, what else becomes easier?**

That usually moves up:
- a grid or connection blockage
- a permitting or procurement freeze
- a staffing or standards bottleneck
- a finance blockage that prevents many otherwise-good projects from moving
- a truth or data join whose repair clears many downstream decisions [S124][S132][S133][S138][S139][S140].

The point is not abstract leverage worship.
It is to use limited correction bandwidth on releases that unblock several queued gains at once.

### 4. Pull forward truth and integrity failures before they corrupt the rest of the queue
A weak signal should move up sharply when the programme can no longer trust its own evidence:
- performance claims are not comparable
- a dashboard is flattering but physically weak
- measured methane, delivery proof, or service continuity evidence is missing
- exception claims cannot be checked
- paper progress is outrunning real buildout or exposure reduction [S65][S66][S67][S68][S108][S123][S133][S138][S141].

If the truth layer is weak, many other queued decisions become guesswork.
So integrity failures often outrank seemingly larger but better-measured operational problems.

### 5. Among the remaining queue, prefer corrections that can be verified, copied, and hardened quickly
Once the front of the queue is no longer dominated by harm, lock-in, leverage, or truth failures, prefer correction work that can:
- produce a clear improvement soon enough to verify
- become routine rather than remaining heroic
- spread to similar contexts without bespoke redesign
- retire an inferior path rather than adding one more parallel programme [S121][S122][S147][S148][S149][S150].

This keeps the queue from filling with clever but non-verifiable one-offs that consume scarce senior attention without strengthening the base system.

## Queue discipline

A serious programme should not pretend that every live weak signal is on the active correction queue.
Use three visible states instead:

### Active now
For the short list that is currently receiving real governing attention, correction capacity, and verification effort.

### Parked with watch
For issues that matter but are not on the active queue right now. Keep:
- a named owner
- a watch signal
- a trigger for re-entry
- a recheck date
- and any containment rule needed so parking does not become silent acceptance [S102][S103][S123][S124][S151].

### Quarantined or refused
For claims that are low-quality, boundary-violating, paper-heavy, or dependent on unproven escape hatches. These should not compete with serious correction work until they clear proof gates [S141][S127].

## Ordered use

Use the mature triage map in this order:

1. **front-load stop-harm and continuity threats**
2. **pull forward lock-in and irreversibility risks**
3. **then prioritize the biggest bottleneck release or leverage gain**
4. **escalate truth and integrity failures because they corrupt the rest of the queue**
5. **among the remainder, prefer corrections that can be verified, copied, and hardened quickly**
6. **keep the live correction queue short and park the rest visibly**
