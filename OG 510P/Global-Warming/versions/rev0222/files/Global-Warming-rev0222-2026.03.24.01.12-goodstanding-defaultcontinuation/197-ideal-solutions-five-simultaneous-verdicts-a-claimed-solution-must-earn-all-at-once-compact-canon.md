# 197 — Ideal Solutions: Five Simultaneous Verdicts a Claimed Solution Must Earn All at Once Compact Canon

## Claim

The archive already has a shortest answer (`195`), a one-page answer (`58`), a hard-conclusions note (`192`), a first-moves note (`193`), a non-substitutions note (`196`), an objective-function note (`157`), and a longer acceptance-tests note (`97`).
What it still lacked was one very small front-door note answering a recurring practical question:

**What are the few things that any claimed ideal climate solution has to be able to say “yes” to at the same time?**

That gap matters because many climate proposals have one real virtue but fail somewhere else that is just as important.
A pathway can be ambitious but not buildable, protective but too slow, cheap-looking but paper-thin, or technically elegant but politically fragile.
This note is therefore a compression note, not a new doctrinal branch.
Its job is to state the smallest set of simultaneous verdicts a claimed solution must earn if it is to count as genuinely serious rather than partially attractive [S100][S101][S102][S103][S104][S106][S112][S113][S143][S145].

## Fast rule

**A claimed ideal solution is only serious if it can earn five verdicts at once: fast enough in physics, replacement-oriented rather than merely additive, protective and fair in ordinary life, buildable and spreadable through real institutions, and provable in measured outcomes strongly enough to survive correction, money pressure, and politics.**

## The five simultaneous verdicts

### 1. Is it fast enough in physics?
A serious climate solution must cut real warming pressure or real exposure on timescales that matter for overshoot, irreversible loss, and near-term harm.
That means it should privilege fast gross emissions cuts, methane action, efficiency, and direct protection rather than relying mainly on distant end-state stories or late repair [S100][S101][S105][S133].

### 2. Does it replace dirty dependence rather than merely accompany it?
A serious climate solution cannot merely add clean capacity while leaving fossil lock-in, fossil support, and dirty defaults largely intact.
It has to build the cleaner system and also discipline, retire, or stop extending the dirtier one, otherwise clean growth and dirty persistence simply coexist [S100][S101][S102][S145].

### 3. Does it protect people and allocate burdens fairly enough to last?
A serious climate solution should improve or secure essential service floors such as cooling, heat, mobility, health, and safety while avoiding the familiar pattern of stacking cost, hassle, proof, and risk on weaker households, poorer countries, and exposed regions.
Protection and fairness are not decorative ethics here; they are part of whether the climate programme is socially and politically durable at all [S104][S106][S108][S143].

### 4. Can it actually be built, connected, financed, staffed, and copied?
A serious climate solution must clear real bottlenecks: grids, queues, permitting, standards, procurement, workforce, administration, and affordable finance.
If it cannot move through those choke points, it is not yet an ideal solution in practice, no matter how elegant the abstract package looks [S102][S103][S106][S107][S112].

### 5. Can it prove itself in physical outcomes and survive correction?
A serious climate solution must be judged by lower gross emissions, measured methane cuts, reliable clean service, lower exposure, durable dirty retirement, and visible cost or wellbeing gains — not mainly by labels, targets, offsets, or announced money.
It should also be corrigible enough that weak performance triggers review, remedy, and stronger routines rather than ceremonial self-congratulation [S103][S113][S114][S145].

## Compression rule

**A claimed ideal climate solution does not count because one part of it is admirable. It counts only if it is fast enough in physics, replacement-oriented, protective and fair, buildable through real bottlenecks, and provable in measured outcomes strongly enough to survive correction and political time.**
