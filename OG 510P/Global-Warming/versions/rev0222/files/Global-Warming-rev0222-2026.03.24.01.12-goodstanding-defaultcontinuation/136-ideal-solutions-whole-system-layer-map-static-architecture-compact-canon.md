## The compact canon

### 1. Design layer: define what counts as a serious answer
Start with the joined design layer.
This is where the archive decides that the answer is a package, not a silver bullet; that it must cut fast, minimise overshoot, reduce dependence on later heroic cleanup, and satisfy hard acceptance tests before deeper attention is earned.
Without this layer, every later argument is vulnerable to being organised around the wrong object.

### 2. Physical-portfolio layer: decide what the programme actually does in the world
A serious climate programme still needs a physical answer.
That means the joined portfolio of clean buildout, waste-cutting, fossil retirement, industrial transition, land and ecosystem protection, and bounded residual management.
This layer answers the concrete question: **what actually gets built, retired, protected, or reserved?**

### 3. Protection layer: reduce exposure while warming is already arriving
The programme is not complete when it has a mitigation portfolio.
It also needs a joined protection layer for heat, water, health, lifelines, exposure discipline, ecosystem buffers, and dignified retreat where places are becoming unprotectable.
This layer answers: **how does the programme directly protect people from damage that is no longer avoidable?**

### 4. Political-economy layer: make the programme livable, legitimate, and financeable
Even the right physical answer fails if it arrives as a bill shock, a legitimacy collapse, or a permanently high cost of capital.
This layer turns climate strategy into a durable settlement around affordability, visible benefit-sharing, fiscal realism, distribution, and development space.
It answers: **why would people, firms, and states keep this programme running rather than revolt, defect, or starve it of capital?**

### 5. International layer: make many national programmes add up to a world-scale answer
Global warming is not solved by domestic architecture alone.
This layer extends the programme across borders through cheaper capital for poorer countries, common standards, supply-chain coordination, methane and fossil burden-sharing, and international regimes for sectors no single state can govern alone.
It answers: **how do many state programmes cohere rather than cancel one another out?**

### 6. Delivery layer: release the chokepoints that stop arrival
Correct design and legitimate finance are still not enough if grid queues, permitting friction, workforce shortages, brittle supply chains, and bespoke contracts block buildout.
This layer treats bottleneck release as part of the solution itself.
It answers: **what must be unblocked so the right portfolio can actually arrive at speed?**

### 7. Truth layer: keep claims measurable, comparable, and enforceable
A mature programme needs truth infrastructure.
That means measured methane where feasible, interoperable definitions, public ledgers for contested claims, physical-first scoreboards, hard MRV for removals and carbon management, and corrective ratchets when reality falls short.
It answers: **how do we stop paperwork, vague pledges, and accounting tricks from impersonating progress?**

### 8. Governing-kernel layer: steer routine public authority
Once the programme exists, routine public authority needs a stable operating kernel: execution lanes, steering levers, governing clocks, roles, decision rights, and precedence rules.
This layer answers: **who steers what, on what cadences, with what authority, during ordinary delivery?**

### 9. Readiness layer: keep capacity live before a bad day
Routine governance is not enough unless readiness is live, rehearsed, honestly classified, and evidence-backed.
This layer turns readiness into a state machine instead of a ceremonial label.
It answers: **how do we know whether the system is actually ready, partly ready, stale, or already slipping into detour conditions?**

### 10. Off-normal / detour layer: govern exceptions without normalising them
When tripwires fire, routine governance is no longer enough.
The system needs narrow proof-gated entry into escalation, fallback, or exception; live duties while the detour is open; explicit return-to-routine tests; and after-action hardening once it closes.
This layer answers: **how do we survive abnormal periods without letting temporary dirt, drift, or emergency governance become the new normal?**

### 11. Intake / admission layer: keep archive growth separate from programme growth
The archive itself needs a meta-layer that screens new proposals, routes them to the smallest useful note, and admits new main-canon files only for real net-new value or real compression.
This belongs outside the climate programme proper.
It answers: **how does the archive stay small and improve proportionately as the programme matures?**
