# 60 — Ideal Solutions by Context: One Common Spine, Five Place Types

## Claim

There is **no single hardware list** that is ideal everywhere.

There **is** a common climate spine, but the **front of the queue changes by place type**.
That is the practical meaning of the archive’s place-sensitive rule in `53-ideal-solutions-as-place-sensitive-systems-universal-principles-local-fit-and-context-adjusted-delivery.md`.
This note makes that rule operational.

The archive should therefore answer the question **“what are ideal solutions?”** in two layers:

1. a **common spine** that belongs almost everywhere, and
2. a **context translation** that changes emphasis by development stage, infrastructure condition, fossil dependence, and exposure profile [S13][S40][S41][S70][S91][S172][S173][S175].

## The common spine

Across most places, ideal portfolios still contain the same core logic:
- clean electricity, grid expansion, storage, flexibility, and selected clean firm capacity where needed [S4][S5][S6][S14]
- efficiency, passive design, better buildings, efficient cooling, and direct electrification where ready [S16][S17][S51][S52][S53][S54][S106]
- methane abatement in energy, waste, and agrifood systems [S8][S11][S19][S21][S22]
- managed fossil decline, including subsidy reform and retirement architecture [S9][S23][S24][S25]
- ecosystem protection, water security, and adaptation as civil protection [S12][S15][S36][S37][S38][S40][S41][S72][S73][S74][S75][S76]
- finance, delivery capacity, measurement, and affordability architecture so the package can scale honestly in the real world [S13][S59][S60][S61][S62][S65][S68][S69][S70][S71][S91][S94][S95][S96].

What changes by context is **not the physics**.
What changes is the order of operations, the binding bottleneck, the financing architecture, and the least-regret route through local constraints [S172][S173][S174][S175].

## Five place types

These are not rigid categories.
They are practical archetypes for choosing what belongs at the **front** of the solution queue.

### 1. High-income incumbent fossil systems

These places usually already have large grids, high per-capita energy use, old building stock, heavy car dependence, and enough institutional capacity to move faster than they do.

Here the ideal solution is **replacement and retirement at speed**.
The front of the queue is usually:
- retrofit and envelope improvement
- heat pumps and efficient cooling
- EVs, transit, and mode shift where possible
- faster clean-power and grid buildout
- industrial electrification, efficiency, and cleaner procurement
- deliberate retirement of coal, oil, and eventually gas dependence rather than treating clean supply as an optional add-on [S4][S6][S9][S16][S17][S28][S30][S31][S51][S52][S53][S54][S69][S103][S106].

These systems should be especially strict about **scarcity discipline**.
They should not waste scarce hydrogen, biomass, CCS capacity, or fiscal room on uses that can already be electrified or avoided more directly [S26][S30][S32][S33][S42][S112][S165][S166][S167].

### 2. Fast-growing middle-income industrialisers

These places often face fast demand growth, rapid urbanisation, manufacturing expansion, rising cooling demand, and still-forming infrastructure systems.

Here the ideal solution is **clean-first development rather than dirty build-then-clean-later development**.
The front of the queue is usually:
- clean power, transmission, and distribution built ahead of demand where possible
- cooling efficiency and appliance standards before inefficient stock locks in
- compact urban growth, mass transit, and better land-use choices before car dependence hardens
- industrial strategy for low-emissions materials and export competitiveness
- clean cooking, freight efficiency, and agrifood methane / nitrogen action where relevant
- concessional finance and cost-of-capital reduction because financing terms can dominate technology economics [S4][S5][S13][S16][S17][S18][S19][S22][S28][S77][S78][S91][S94][S95][S96][S172][S173].

The key rule here is: **do not import the rich world’s historical lock-in path and then promise to fix it later**.

### 3. Low-income, energy-poor, infrastructure-thin settings

These places may still face unreliable electricity, low appliance ownership, weak grids, informal housing, limited fiscal room, and thin administrative capacity.

Here the ideal solution is **access-first clean development**.
The front of the queue is usually:
- least-cost clean electricity access through the best local mix of grid extension, mini-grids, and distributed systems
- efficient appliances and cooling access so new demand does not become unnecessarily large
- clean cooking, safer buildings, basic water systems, and resilient essential services
- social protection, public-health protection, and climate-resilient basic infrastructure
- finance structures that reduce the cost of capital and avoid debt-stacking adaptation burdens onto already thin public balance sheets [S12][S13][S15][S16][S40][S41][S69][S70][S71][S91][S92][S93][S94][S95][S96][S104][S175].

The archive should be explicit here:
**it is not an ideal climate solution to withhold modern energy services from poor populations in the name of purity**.
The ideal path is to expand access with the cleanest, lowest-regret systems available while avoiding future lock-in where possible [S70][S71][S91][S94][S96].

### 4. Fossil-exporting and fossil-rent-dependent economies

These places are a special case because the climate problem is tied not only to domestic consumption but also to fiscal dependence, export structure, and political power.

Here the ideal solution is **managed dependence reduction**.
The front of the queue is usually:
- methane measurement, leak reduction, and flaring control immediately
- subsidy reform and better use of public revenue
- diversification of the fiscal base and the real economy
- worker, region, and pension transition planning
- stopping new dependence-building infrastructure that assumes long fossil demand tails
- using current rents, where they still exist, to finance the post-fossil platform rather than to deepen lock-in [S9][S11][S23][S24][S25][S40][S102][S172][S173].

The archive should reject a common evasion here:
**“green diversification later” is not a solution if present policy still expands the fossil dependency that makes diversification harder.**

### 5. Highly exposed heat-, water-, coastal-, and disaster-stressed places

Some places are so exposed that adaptation is not a side lane but a co-equal survival lane.
That can be true for small islands, deltas, heat-stressed cities, drought basins, wildfire zones, and fragile coasts.

Here the ideal solution is **protection plus discipline**.
The front of the queue is usually:
- early warnings, heat-health systems, and resilient care systems
- efficient cooling and reliable power for adaptation loads
- water security, watershed protection, drought planning, and flood management
- stronger building standards and resilient infrastructure
- open risk information, insurance realism, no-build rules in the worst places, and dignified retreat where protection is no longer credible [S12][S15][S16][S17][S40][S41][S72][S73][S74][S75][S76][S81][S82][S83][S84][S85][S86][S87][S88][S89][S90][S104][S105].

This does **not** demote mitigation.
It means mitigation and protection must be designed together because exposure is already arriving faster than many places can absorb [S3][S12][S40][S41][S104].

## What this note changes in archive use

When the archive evaluates a proposal, it should now ask two extra questions:

1. **Which place type is this really for?**
2. **Does the proposal move the front of that place’s queue, or does it import the wrong context’s priorities?**

A proposal should usually be demoted when it is “good somewhere” but is being sold as “first-best everywhere.”
That is one of the main ways climate strategy becomes bloated, expensive, and politically brittle [S172][S173][S175].

## Common translation errors

The archive should now reject several recurring mistakes.

### 1. Universalising rich-country sequencing
A package built for capital-rich, grid-dense, high-capacity systems should not be copied wholesale into energy-poor or infrastructure-thin settings [S13][S70][S91][S94][S96][S172].

### 2. Using place-sensitivity as a fossil excuse
Local fit does not mean every place gets a permanent exemption from methane control, efficiency, clean power, or managed fossil decline [S9][S11][S23][S24][S25][S172][S173].

### 3. Sending scarce clean molecules to places that mainly need wires, buildings, and appliances
Where the main problem is weak access, weak grids, hot buildings, or bad appliance stock, hydrogen- or removal-heavy storytelling is usually a displacement of attention rather than an ideal solution [S16][S17][S42][S70][S91][S96][S106].

### 4. Treating adaptation as optional in highly exposed places
Where heat, flood, drought, smoke, or coastal loss are already severe, mitigation-only packages are incomplete by design [S12][S15][S40][S41][S81][S82][S85][S104].

### 5. Treating EMDE finance as an afterthought
A solution that only works under rich-country financing conditions is not yet an ideal solution to a global problem [S13][S91][S92][S93][S94][S95][S96].

## Compression

There is no single ideal climate package in the sense of one identical to-do list for all places.
There **is** a single ideal **logic**.

**The archive should now treat ideal solutions as one common spine translated into a small number of place types: rich fossil incumbents should replace and retire fast; industrialisers should build clean-first growth; energy-poor settings should pursue access-first clean development; fossil exporters should manage dependence down; and highly exposed places should pair mitigation with protection and retreat discipline from the start.**
