# 169 — Ideal Solutions: Turnover-Aware Trigger Points and Never-Miss-the-Moment Compact Canon

## Claim

The archive already has strong notes for:
- turnover-aware systems (`50`)
- low-friction systems (`36`)
- short latency / early benefit capture (`41`)
- copyability / fast replication (`168`)
- and the early-wins / anti-whiplash canon (`164`)

What it still lacked was one joined first-stop note for a front-door timing question that keeps reappearing whenever serious climate packages move from abstract preference to real-world delivery:

**what should ideal solutions do when the decisive climate choice only becomes live at sale, rental, failure, renovation, procurement, renewal, refinance, or rebuild?**

This note is not a new doctrinal branch.
It is a compression note.
Its job is to gather the archive’s turnover-aware rule into one place so the programme does not keep treating replacement cycles, transaction moments, failure moments, rebuild windows, and stock turnover as separate implementation trivia when they are often the difference between a smooth clean switch and another long lock-in cycle.

## Fast rule

**Ideal solutions should usually attach themselves to real trigger points: sale, rental, refinance, renovation, equipment failure, procurement, fleet renewal, permit renewal, and post-disaster reconstruction. Where stock is already changing, the cleaner and safer option should be made to win at that moment by default or near-default. Waiting until after the moment passes often means paying later to undo another full cycle of dirty or fragile lock-in. But turnover-awareness is not an excuse for passivity: where natural turnover is too slow for climate safety or exposure reduction, the archive should promote earlier retirement, mandatory upgrade, or managed exit instead** [S161][S162][S163][S164].

## The compact canon

### 1. Treat trigger points as governing moments, not as implementation detail
A serious climate programme should not imagine that buildings, vehicles, appliances, industrial equipment, and public assets change continuously.
They usually change at concentrated moments: sale, rental, lease renewal, refinance, replacement, fleet renewal, procurement, major renovation, and post-disaster rebuilding.
That is where standards, finance, disclosure, default offers, and public support can move the outcome with less friction than a detached appeal to voluntary action [S161][S162][S163][S164].

House rule: **policy should meet the choice when the choice is actually live.**

### 2. Prefer measures that capture stock turnover instead of missing it
If an old boiler fails, an apartment changes tenant, a bus fleet renews, a roof is replaced, or a damaged school is rebuilt, the programme should be ready with the cleaner and safer path at that exact moment.
Missing that window often means another decade or more of lock-in, followed by a costlier corrective intervention later.
The archive should therefore rank higher the measures that are ready for the moment when stock is already moving [S161][S162][S163][S164].

House rule: **the cheaper clean transition often depends on not missing the already-moving moment.**

### 3. Use trigger points to lower disruption and target public money better
When the cleaner switch happens at an ordinary replacement or transaction moment, the programme can often reduce scrappage waste, user hassle, and political resistance compared with a badly timed forced swap.
That does not make the transition painless.
It means public effort can be concentrated where it changes the choice most, rather than spread thinly across periods when people are not already making a capital decision [S161][S162][S163].

House rule: **put the strongest push where timing makes it bite best.**

### 4. Prefer trigger points that also improve legibility, verification, and enforcement
Sale, rental, refinance, registration, permit renewal, procurement, and reconstruction are not only choice moments.
They are also paperwork, inspection, valuation, underwriting, and accountability moments.
That often makes climate requirements easier to verify and harder to evade than rules that float free of any administrative event [S161][S164].

House rule: **the best timing windows are often also the best proof windows.**

### 5. Treat rebuild and recovery as climate moments, not just repair moments
After fires, floods, storms, or other shocks, governments can either reinstall yesterday’s vulnerability or use reconstruction to move assets, settlements, and infrastructure onto safer and cleaner trajectories.
Recovery readiness matters because the post-disaster window is narrow; if the state is not prepared before the event, money and urgency can rush the system back into the old pattern [S164].

House rule: **recovery should be prepared early enough that rebuild does not become a fast route back to failure.**

### 6. Turnover-awareness is not “wait forever” dressed up as prudence
Some assets live too long, emit too much, or expose people to too much harm to justify waiting passively for natural replacement.
The archive should still promote accelerated retirement, mandatory upgrade, buyout, or managed exit where the lock-in or danger cost of waiting is too high.
Turnover-awareness is therefore a timing discipline, not an excuse for delay [S162][S163][S164].

House rule: **ride natural turnover where possible; override it where waiting is unsafe.**

### 7. The ideal package should be pre-positioned for the next trigger point
The programme should not merely have the right long-run preference.
It should have the operational pieces ready before the next stock-turnover moment arrives: approved technologies, standard offers, trained installers, finance paths, procurement templates, eligibility logic, and ready rebuild standards.
Otherwise even a correct policy intention keeps losing to the incumbent at the decisive moment [S162][S163][S164].

House rule: **the clean option must exist in usable form before the moment arrives.**

## Preferred use

Open this note first when the live question is any of the following:
- how ideal solutions should use replacement cycles, procurement windows, sale or rental events, or rebuild moments
- why climate policy should care about trigger points rather than only long-run average incentives
- whether a proposal is likely to miss the moment and lock in another full asset cycle
- when natural turnover is a virtue and when it is an excuse for delay
- how to rank two serious climate measures when one is much better positioned to capture decisions that are already about to happen

Then reopen the deeper note that matches the surviving sub-question.

## Compression rule

**Ideal solutions should usually arrive at the moment when stock is already changing: sale, rental, replacement, renovation, procurement, renewal, refinance, and rebuild. Missing those trigger points is one of the quiet ways societies lock in another generation of emissions, fragility, and later cleanup cost.**
