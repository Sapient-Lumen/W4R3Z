## Fast rule

**Do not stop at adoption. Once the cleaner pattern is verified, banked, and spread enough to carry continuity safely, retire the inferior path on purpose: remove its special supports, close its default status, shrink its exceptions, migrate users and contracts into the better path, decommission duplicate workarounds, and keep only narrow temporary carve-outs with named owners, proof gates, and visible sunsets.**

## The compact retirement map

### 1. Retire only after the replacement is real enough to carry continuity
A serious programme should not force retirement merely because a cleaner option exists in principle.
Retirement should start after the archive’s verification, stabilization, and propagation tests are sufficiently met:
- the better path is improving the targeted weak signals in governed data
- the gain has been written into ordinary standards, budgets, staffing, and accountability
- the copied package is real enough in similar contexts that retirement does not turn continuity risk into a public surprise [S75][S123][S124][S146][S147].

### 2. Target the inferior path itself, not only celebrate growth of the better one
Systems often add the cleaner path while leaving the older path fully alive:
- the dirty tariff survives
- the fossil backup becomes the quiet routine
- the waiver becomes the real standard
- the older procurement rule still wins by inertia
- the exception remains easier than the better default

Retirement therefore means naming the path to be removed:
- the asset class
- the contract form
- the subsidy
- the permit presumption
- the fallback rule
- the loophole
- or the duplicate institution [S84][S123][S130][S141][S147][S180].

A programme has not really shifted if the new path grows while the old path remains fully available, socially normal, and publicly protected.

### 3. Move defaults, contracts, and supports so the inferior path loses its automatic advantage
Retirement is rarely achieved by moral instruction.
It usually happens when the programme changes the operating furniture:
- remove privileged access, hidden subsidy, or preferred queue treatment for the inferior path
- make the better path the standard contract, default offer, planning baseline, or replacement presumption
- close reimbursement, proof, or procurement rules that still tilt users toward the older path
- keep any remaining carve-out explicit, narrow, and sunset-bound [S71][S75][S85][S123][S124][S141][S147][S150][S151].

The key move is to make the better path ordinary and the inferior path exceptional, not the reverse.

### 4. Shrink dual-running and exception space deliberately
Dual-running is sometimes necessary during transition.
But if it becomes indefinite, it drains money, staff attention, and political clarity.
A serious programme should therefore:
- set explicit criteria for when the inferior path loses general availability
- convert broad exceptions into named temporary cases
- narrow dirty fallback to the least-damaging backup role
- and require each remaining exception to carry an owner, a reason, a proof gate, and an exit date [S84][S85][S86][S123][S141][S147].

Retirement is complete when the older path is no longer the quiet default hiding behind “temporary” language.

### 5. Decommission the obsolete workaround while protecting people and service continuity
Retirement is not only policy withdrawal.
It often requires real closure work:
- migrate users, loads, or service obligations
- retrain or reassign staff where possible
- retire or repurpose obsolete assets
- preserve minimum continuity for vulnerable users during the handoff
- and keep protection and affordability stronger where the old path had been propped up because better service was missing [S75][S84][S86][S87][S130][S131][S134][S147].

The archive’s rule is simple: retire the inferior path, not the people who were stuck using it.

### 6. Score retirement by disappearance of the inferior path, not by growth of the better path alone
A programme should not call retirement successful merely because the cleaner path is bigger than before.
The truth and scoreboard rules still govern:
- is the old path’s share, subsidy, queue priority, exception use, or fallback frequency actually shrinking
- are dirty rebounds and quiet resets disappearing at handoffs and stress moments
- is service continuity holding while the older path loses status
- are remaining carve-outs visible enough to govern rather than hiding inside routine practice [S133][S138][S141][S146][S147].

If the better path grows but the inferior path remains sticky, retirement is incomplete and the system is still paying for parallel worlds.

## One-paragraph compression

**Once a cleaner path is verified, banked, and spread enough to carry continuity safely, the next ideal move is to retire the inferior path on purpose: name the asset, contract, subsidy, loophole, or fallback rule to be removed; move defaults and supports so the better path becomes ordinary; shrink dual-running and exception space; decommission obsolete workarounds while protecting people and service continuity; and judge success by the disappearance of the inferior path, not only by growth of the better one.**
