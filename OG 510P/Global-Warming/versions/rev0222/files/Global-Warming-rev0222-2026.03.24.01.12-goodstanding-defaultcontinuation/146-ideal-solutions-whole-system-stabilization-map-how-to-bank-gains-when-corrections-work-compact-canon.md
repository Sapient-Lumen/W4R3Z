## Fast rule

**If a correction works, do not merely celebrate it. Convert the gain into the new governed routine. Shrink the temporary detour, lock the successful correction into standards, contracts, budgets, operations, and accountability, update the scoreboard and tripwires to the improved baseline, and keep watching for relapse, burden-shifting, or rebound until the improvement proves durable.**

## The compact stabilization map

### 1. Treat success as provisional until the targeted weak signal improves in the governed data
A correction should not be stabilized because the programme feels calmer or because one pilot looks better.
It should first pass the archive's own verification rule:
the **targeted weak signal** should improve at the layer the programme said was failing.

That usually means:
- the queue, permit, commissioning, turnout, completion, retention, exposure, outage, audit, or cost signal that triggered the correction actually moves
- the move is visible in governed measurement, not only in narrative
- the improvement survives long enough to count as more than statistical noise or one-off luck

### 2. Shrink the temporary detour and move the winning correction back into routine
If the correction worked inside an exception, surge, detour, emergency queue, or temporary override, the next question is not whether to keep the exception forever.
It is whether the successful element can be **pulled back into normal governed operation**.

That usually means:
- end the broadest temporary carve-outs first
- keep only the narrow temporary device that is still strictly necessary
- restore routine rights, clocks, proof gates, and public visibility as soon as the stronger routine can carry the gain
- avoid letting emergency convenience become the new permanent loophole

### 3. Convert the successful correction into defaults, contracts, standards, budgets, and staffing
A correction is not stabilized when people merely know what worked.
It is stabilized when the system is rewritten so the improvement happens **without renewed heroics**.

That usually means:
- write the winning operational change into standard offers, contract forms, queue rules, procurement templates, codes, or interconnection practice
- put recurring budget, staffing, tooling, and maintenance behind it
- assign a named owner and an operating clock so the gain has a steward
- reduce bespoke judgement and increase default clean routine

### 4. Ratchet the scoreboard, diagnosis priors, and tripwires to the improved baseline
Once a gain is real, the programme should not keep grading itself against the old weaker state.
The scoreboard, diagnosis priors, and tripwire thresholds should learn from the success.

That usually means:
- set the new achieved level as the ordinary expected floor where appropriate
- tighten tolerance for relapse at the repaired layer
- revise the archive's "first places to look" if the failure pattern has changed
- update verification expectations so the next correction cycle starts from the improved state rather than the pre-fix baseline

### 5. Watch for rebound, burden-shifting, and quiet regression until persistence is proven
A correction can improve the headline weak signal while quietly moving burden elsewhere or decaying after attention drops.
So stabilization requires a short persistence discipline.

That usually means:
- watch neighboring signals for burden transfer, rebound demand, gaming, or slower hidden deterioration
- keep explicit follow-up checks on retention, maintenance, uptime, affordability, and equity distribution
- demote readiness or reopen diagnosis quickly if the gain fades once exceptional attention is withdrawn
- only then mark the correction as fully banked into routine

## Minimal operator sequence

1. **Verify that the targeted weak signal really improved.**
2. **Shrink the detour and move the working element back into ordinary governed routine.**
3. **Write the gain into defaults, standards, budgets, staffing, and named ownership.**
4. **Ratchet the scoreboard, diagnosis priors, and tripwire thresholds to the stronger baseline.**
5. **Watch for relapse, rebound, burden-shifting, and quiet decay until persistence is proven.**
