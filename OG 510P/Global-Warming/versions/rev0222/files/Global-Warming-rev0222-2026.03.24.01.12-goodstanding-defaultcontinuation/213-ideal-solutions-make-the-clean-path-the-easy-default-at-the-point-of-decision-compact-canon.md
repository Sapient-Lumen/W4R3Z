# 213 — Ideal Solutions: Make the Clean Path the Easy Default at the Point of Decision Compact Canon

## Claim

The archive already has the physical-jobs note (`208`), the grow / shrink / stop note (`209`), the abundance / rationing / retirement note (`210`), the public-meaning note (`211`), the public-bargain note (`212`), and the deeper low-friction note (`185`).
That is enough doctrine.
But the front door still lacked one tiny bridge between the archive's physical answer and the ordinary decision point:

**how should ideal climate solutions behave when a household, landlord, fleet owner, builder, utility, school, hospital, or local government is actually deciding what to replace, approve, finance, connect, or install?**

This note repairs that gap.
It is a compression note, not a new branch.
Its job is to state the archive's smallest uptake rule in one place: the cleaner path should usually be the easier path to notice, qualify for, finance, approve, and carry through, while the dirtier repeat choice should lose hidden privilege and become harder to reproduce by default [S53][S69][S118][S119][S120].

## Fast rule

**Among physically serious options, prefer climate pathways that make the cleaner action the easier default at the decision point through standards, codes, procurement, one-stop delivery, embedded support, and simpler finance. Demote pathways that mainly work only if ordinary users repeatedly overcome high search, cash, paperwork, or coordination burdens while the dirty fallback remains faster, easier, or quietly subsidized** [S53][S69][S118][S119][S120].

## The compact canon

### 1. The decisive moment is often a replacement or approval moment
Climate progress does not arrive only through abstract support for clean technology.
It arrives when boilers, air conditioners, vehicles, roofs, wiring, appliances, contracts, permits, and capital plans are actually being replaced or approved.
At that moment, a serious climate program should not leave the cleaner path at a procedural disadvantage [S53][S69][S118][S119].

House rule: **treat the point of decision as a control point, not as a neutral shopping scene.**

### 2. Where direction is already known, the cleaner path should increasingly arrive by default
If government, utilities, or large institutions already know the cleaner and safer direction, they should not express that knowledge mainly as scattered optional programs.
Codes, standards, procurement rules, default offers, and standard replacement pathways should carry more of the burden so ordinary users do not need to rediscover the answer from scratch each time [S53][S118][S119][S120].

House rule: **when the public answer is already known, move more of the work into the default pathway.**

### 3. Embedded support beats rebate mazes
Many nominally good climate programs fail because the cleaner option asks the decisive actor to search more, front more cash, assemble more documents, and coordinate more parties.
The archive therefore prefers point-of-sale support, on-bill or embedded finance where appropriate, prequalified offers, assisted pathways, and one-stop delivery over pay-first-recover-later schemes and proof mazes [S69][S118][S119].

House rule: **do not make the weakest or busiest actor carry search, proof, cash, and coordination burdens at the same time.**

### 4. Dirty defaults should lose silent advantages
A climate program is weaker when the dirtier fallback still keeps the faster permit, the easier financing path, the familiar contract, the quiet subsidy, and the lower paperwork load.
Part of making the clean path easier is removing these silent asymmetries so the dirty repeat choice is no longer the path of least resistance [S53][S69][S119].

House rule: **do not only subsidize the cleaner path; also stop overprotecting the dirtier repeat choice.**

### 5. Upstream design often matters more than downstream persuasion
Some of the strongest gains come from shrinking the need for repeated expert choice in the first place: stronger envelopes, passive cooling, cleaner building codes, standard retrofit packages, procurement templates, and routine replacement pathways.
UNEP's sustainable-cooling work emphasizes passive and low-energy design plus mandatory passive cooling in codes as major levers because they reduce later strain and later decision burden at the same time [S120].

House rule: **prefer friction removed upstream to friction merely managed later.**

### 6. Easy-path design is a speed rule, a fairness rule, and a scaling rule at once
A cleaner default that is easier to traverse will usually diffuse faster, exclude fewer people, produce less administrative waste, and copy more readily across places.
That is why low-friction design belongs near the front door of the archive rather than buried as a specialist administrative note [S69][S118][S119].

House rule: **the clean path should be easier not only because people deserve simplicity, but because simplicity is one of the main engines of scale.**

- what it means to make the clean path easier than the dirty path

## Compression rule

**An ideal climate solution does not merely make the clean option available. It makes the clean option the easier default at the decisive moment through standards, codes, procurement, one-stop delivery, and embedded support, while stripping the dirty fallback of silent convenience, silent subsidy, and procedural privilege.**
