## Fast rule

**Name the roles, then split the decisions: the sponsor sets direction and ratchets, lane owners run routine delivery, the operations lead can escalate and coordinate correction, the truth function owns measurement and public reporting, and the protection backstop can force shielding when affordability, safety, or legitimacy are breaking.**

## Five decision rights

### 1. Direction and ratchet belong to the mission sponsor
The sponsor should own the big directional decisions: targets, lane priorities, trade-offs across ministries or regulators, escalation of ambition, and the decision to tighten when evidence says the current pace is inadequate.

What this usually means:
- setting whole-program direction and the few headline goals that other roles then work under
- resolving conflicts that cut across lanes or institutions when lower-level coordination has failed
- deciding when to ratchet standards, budgets, or schedules rather than letting drift quietly become the new baseline [S59][S60][S65][S147]

Why it matters:
- if the sponsor does not own direction, nobody can settle cross-system conflict
- if the sponsor also micromanages routine execution, delivery slows and accountability blurs

### 2. Routine delivery decisions belong to lane owners
Lane owners should own routine operational choices inside their lane: queue clearing, procurement, contracting, sequencing, staffing, standards implementation, and backlog reduction within the rules already set.

What this usually means:
- clear authority to move projects, programs, and fixes without asking the sponsor to reauthorize normal work
- responsibility for delivery plans, correction loops, and bottleneck release inside the lane’s actual control
- obligation to surface cross-lane conflicts early rather than hiding them until deadlines slip [S103][S117][S147][S180][S181][S182]

Why it matters:
- a lane owner without real routine authority is a messenger, not an owner
- making the center approve ordinary delivery decisions turns climate governance into a queue behind a queue

### 3. Escalation and cross-lane correction belong to the operations lead
The operations-and-escalation lead should own the decision to convene correction once a tripwire fires, assign the initial response mode, and force cross-lane coordination when the normal machinery is too slow.

What this usually means:
- authority to call an incident, require a correction plan, and set a short proof window once performance fails visibly
- authority to convene the relevant lane owners, truth function, and protection backstop rather than merely circulate observations
- responsibility for determining whether the system is in routine mode, escalation mode, or de-escalation after proof of recovery [S111][S114][S147][S181][S182][S183]

Why it matters:
- if nobody owns the escalation call, tripwires become commentary rather than governance
- if the same role owns both routine delivery and the trigger to admit failure, delay becomes structurally tempting

### 4. Measurement and public truth belong to the independent truth function
The truth function should own metric definitions, verification methods, public reporting, audit challenge, and the right to publish underperformance without delivery veto.

What this usually means:
- keeping score with physical outcomes, service reliability, bottleneck release, exposure reduction, and verified performance rather than paper claims alone
- maintaining methodological discipline and comparability across time so the programme cannot improve its numbers mainly by changing the ruler
- separating proof from delivery strongly enough that bad news can surface before failure compounds [S111][S113][S114][S129][S130][S131]

Why it matters:
- if delivery edits the truth function, the system learns too late and trusts the scoreboard too much
- correction is only as good as the independence of the signal that triggered it

### 5. Shielding and stop-harm decisions belong to the protection backstop
The protection-and-fairness backstop should own the right to require shielding, hardship relief, safety correction, or temporary procedural adjustment when the clean path is becoming unaffordable, illegible, or casually dangerous for exposed people.

What this usually means:
- authority to trigger bill protection, hardship routing, direct protection surges, or safer fallback while the clean path is repaired
- authority to say that a programme may remain clean in direction but may not proceed by casually producing avoidable harm, illegibility, or exclusion
- responsibility for making visible whether the programme is still politically and socially legible to the people asked to live inside it [S105][S108][S109][S117][S141][S143]

Why it matters:
- without a live protection decision right, fairness is demoted to rhetoric
- without a stop-harm right, delivery pressure can quietly overpower legitimacy until backlash resets the programme

## Minimal boundary rule

The smallest serious boundary map is:

- the sponsor decides **direction and ratchet**
- lane owners decide **routine delivery**
- the operations lead decides **when correction mode begins and who must coordinate**
- the truth function decides **how performance is measured and reported**
- the protection backstop decides **when shielding or stop-harm adjustment is required**

This does not mean the roles never interact.
It means each role has one decision right that cannot be vague without the whole system degrading.

The most important separations are these:
- do not let **routine delivery** absorb the right to define success
- do not let **truth telling** be subordinate to short-run delivery comfort
- do not let **protection** become purely advisory when real harm is visible
- do not force the **mission sponsor** to clear every ordinary operational move
