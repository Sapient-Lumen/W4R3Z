## The compact canon

### 1. Treat truth infrastructure as part of the solution, not reporting overhead
A climate programme is weaker than it looks when it can announce, subsidise, and permit activity faster than it can tell what is real. So the archive’s truth rule is simple:
- build the physical package,
- but also build the measurement, comparison, audit, and enforcement capacity that prevents the package from dissolving into claims fog [S11][S59][S65][S113][S123][S124].

### 2. Keep the scoreboard led by physical outcomes and service reality
The archive should not let budgets spent, strategies published, credits issued, or nameplate capacity proposed outrank:
- gross emissions actually cut,
- dirty assets actually retired or displaced,
- methane actually reduced,
- storage actually verified,
- and people actually safer, cooler, healthier, or less exposed [S10][S11][S16][S17][S40][S41][S65][S69][S70][S85][S90][S104][S106][S113][S136].

### 3. Measure the high-leverage hidden sources instead of inferring them forever
The archive’s sharpest truth lesson is methane. Where emissions are large, intermittent, and routinely undercounted, engineering estimates and paper factors are not enough. So a serious truth layer should prefer:
- measurement-based methane reporting where feasible,
- public or regulator-visible alert systems,
- required response and repair,
- and consequences for major emissions events that are ignored or repeatedly denied [S11][S66][S67].

### 4. Use interoperable definitions and public ledgers for contested claims
Claims about low-emissions materials, clean products, removals, carbon intensity, or resilience delivery become politically and commercially useless when every actor carries a private definition. So the joined truth layer should favour:
- interoperable product and emissions definitions,
- explicit accounting boundaries and chain-of-custody rules,
- public ledgers for what has been built, retired, stored, restored, or protected,
- and metrics comparable enough to support procurement, trade, and policy without creating a bespoke claims market for every jurisdiction [S68][S123][S124].

### 5. Tie proof to correction, ratchet, and remedy
Measurement is not fully valuable if it only decorates dashboards. The archive’s corrigibility rule applies here: evidence should trigger repair, enforcement, stronger standards, tighter procurement, faster tripwires, or revised allocation — not just another transparency cycle. So the truth layer should usually be wired into:
- recurring review,
- automatic correction where possible,
- remedial pathways where needed,
- and ratchets that tighten once the evidence base is good enough [S34][S59][S65][S113][S114].

### 6. Keep proof burdens proportionate enough to scale
The archive rejects two opposite errors at once: no proof, and proof systems so ornate that only large incumbents can comply. A serious truth layer should therefore prefer:
- direct measurement where it matters most,
- standard methods for routine cases,
- sampled audit where sensible,
- and verification burdens calibrated to harm, value, and reversibility rather than ceremonial maximalism [S68][S72][S73][S123][S124][S136].

### 7. Separate gross cuts, removals, storage, and protection outcomes
The joined truth layer should not let unlike things blur together. It should distinguish:
- gross emissions cuts,
- carbon removals,
- durable storage,
- adaptation / protection outcomes,
- and temporary fallback or emergency measures.
That keeps the archive from mistaking counterbalancing claims for primary decarbonisation, or temporary protection for solved vulnerability [S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S100][S101][S104][S136].

### 8. Treat unverifiable claims and repeated non-response as governance failures
The archive’s practical truth rule is not just “collect better data.” It is also “refuse to centre claims that stay unverifiable, unresponsive, or consequence-free.” So the truth layer should demote or quarantine:
- climate claims with no credible measurement path,
- private labels that cannot be compared across markets,
- removal or CCS claims without auditable storage and liability architecture,
- and alert, reporting, or review systems that repeatedly fail to change behaviour [S11][S66][S67][S123][S135][S136].

## Compression rule

**Treat truth / measurement / enforcement as part of the climate solution itself. If the package is physically attractive and socially saleable but the system cannot tell what is real, compare competing claims, or enforce consequences for false or weak claims, the solution is still incomplete.**
