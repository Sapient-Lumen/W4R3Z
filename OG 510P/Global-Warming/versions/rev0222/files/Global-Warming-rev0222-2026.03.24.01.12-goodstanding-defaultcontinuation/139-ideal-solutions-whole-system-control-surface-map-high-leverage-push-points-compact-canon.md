## The compact canon

### 1. Push hardest at replacement and turnover moments

The clean programme moves fastest when it catches the moments when assets, equipment, and contracts are already being replaced:
- heating and cooling equipment
- water heaters and appliances
- cars, buses, and fleet vehicles
- power plants and grid equipment
- industrial kit and material procurement
- public works, rebuilds, and major repairs

Those moments are high leverage because one decision locks in years of downstream emissions, bills, reliability, and user experience.
A serious programme should not let dirty defaults casually win those moments.

### 2. Push the network-and-queue surface, because blocked connection blocks everything else

Many good projects fail in practice because the system around them is slow, congested, or bespoke.
The highest-leverage delivery surface is often not another announced target.
It is the throughput layer:
- transmission and distribution upgrades
- interconnection reform
- transformer and component availability
- permitting and siting throughput
- standard designs and repeatable contracts
- workforce pipelines
- inspection, commissioning, and aftercare capacity

If the queue does not move, the rest of the portfolio becomes paper inventory rather than physical change.

### 3. Push the fast-warming and obvious-waste surface

Some controls buy time unusually fast or cheaply:
- methane leaks, venting, flaring, and super-emitter events
- refrigerant leakage and weak cooling discipline
- gross efficiency waste in buildings and industry
- avoidable fossil standby and poor operational practices

These are high-leverage surfaces because they can cut warming pressure, fuel waste, local pollution, and system cost together.
A serious programme should not neglect them simply because they are less glamorous than large flagship builds.

### 4. Push the burden-and-default surface so clean service becomes easier than dirty service

The right physical answer can still fail if households, firms, cities, or building owners face:
- high upfront cost
- search and coordination hassle
- reimbursement mazes
- poor financing terms
- high perceived risk
- weak aftercare
- visible unfairness

That makes the social-contract surface a control surface, not just a fairness add-on.
Lower burden, shorten pathways, default people into credible offers, and make the gains visible in bills, comfort, health, and reliability.

### 5. Push the truth-and-liability surface so delay, gaming, and paper substitution stop paying

High-leverage programmes harden the places where false claims, soft accounting, and consequence-free delay can metastasize:
- measured methane and auditable operations
- interoperable definitions and common metrics
- public ledgers and queue visibility
- performance-backed contracts
- sampled audit where full metering is not sensible
- automatic correction, visible remedies, and real liability
- narrow exceptions with explicit sunset and exit

A strong truth layer does not merely report.
It changes behaviour because the easiest surviving strategy becomes actual delivery.

### 6. Push the protection surface where damage is compounding fastest

Where warming is already arriving, the high-leverage move is often not another generic target.
It is protection of the specific places where repeated loss can unravel public support and human welfare:
- heat-health systems
- backup power and resilient critical facilities
- water security and drought management
- flood protection and drainage
- ecosystem buffers
- insurance realism, exposure discipline, and dignified retreat where necessary

This is a control surface because repeated unmanaged loss destroys legitimacy, fiscal room, and future delivery capacity.

### 7. Push the international capital-and-standards surface so the system scales beyond rich jurisdictions

A world-scale climate answer fails if only low-cost-capital jurisdictions can build it cleanly.
The cross-border control surface includes:
- cheaper and longer-tenor capital for poorer countries
- guarantee and blended-finance structures that genuinely reduce cost of capital
- common product, MRV, and procurement standards
- cleaner trade and supply-chain rules
- burden-sharing for fossil transition and adaptation
- sector regimes for shipping, aviation, methane, and hard-to-govern traded goods

This surface matters because a slow or expensive transition in most of the world feeds back into technology cost, politics, trade conflict, and emissions lock-in everywhere else.

## How to use this note

When bandwidth is scarce, ask:

1. **Which push point changes several scoreboard lines at once?**
2. **Which push point removes a bottleneck that is currently blocking otherwise-good projects?**
3. **Which push point prevents long lock-in at the next turnover moment?**
4. **Which push point lowers political resistance by improving affordability, fairness, and visible gains?**
5. **Which push point reduces the probability that a live detour or exception will be needed later?**

Promote moves that score well on several of those questions at once.
Demote moves that are impressive in isolation but do not move the mature system's real control surfaces.

## Bottom line

**The mature archive's answer is not only a map of layers, loops, and scoreboards. It is also a map of leverage. Push hardest where one intervention changes many downstream outcomes: turnover moments, network throughput, methane and obvious waste, low-burden defaults, truth with consequence, exposure reduction, and cheaper cross-border capital plus common standards.**
