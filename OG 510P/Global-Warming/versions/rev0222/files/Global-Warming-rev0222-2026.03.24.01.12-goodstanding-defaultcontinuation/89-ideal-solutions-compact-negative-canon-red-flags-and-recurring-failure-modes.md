# 89 — Ideal Solutions, Compact Negative Canon: Red Flags and Recurring Failure Modes

## Claim

The archive now has a strong positive canon: what ideal climate solutions should build, how they should be staged, and how they should reach people in ordinary life.
That is necessary, but not sufficient.
The archive also needs the shortest possible **negative canon**:

**if a proposal reproduces one of a small number of recurring climate failure modes, it should usually be demoted, quarantined, or rejected even when it sounds innovative, market-friendly, or politically comfortable.** [S100][S101][S102][S103][S104][S106][S118][S119][S123][S143][S146][S147]

This note does not replace `28-ideal-solutions-selection-rules-and-exclusions.md`, `59-ideal-solutions-fast-screen-promote-demote-quarantine.md`, or `88-ideal-solutions-compact-canon-core-screens-in-one-place.md`.
It is the inverse companion to them: the shortest list of things the archive should learn to distrust quickly.

## Recurring red flags

### 1. Paper substitution instead of physical substitution
If the proposal mainly changes accounting, certificates, credits, or narrated net positions while leaving the physical system largely intact, it is not close to an ideal solution.
The archive should keep preferring measured displacement, measured leak reduction, measured exposure reduction, and measured service improvement over paper substitution stories. [S11][S27][S59][S65][S66][S67][S68][S113][S123][S136]

### 2. Future cleanup used to excuse weak near-term cuts
If the proposal delays hard action now by assuming very large future removals, very cheap future clean fuels, or painless later repair, it is usually failing the archive's time discipline.
Overshoot is damage, not a harmless waiting room. [S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S100][S101][S105]

### 3. Scarce clean resources spent on easy uses
If the proposal uses scarce hydrogen, e-fuels, biomass, durable storage, skilled labour, fiscal room, or public tolerance where direct electrification, efficiency, or simpler options already work, it is probably misallocating scarcity.
The archive should keep reserving scarce tools for hard residual uses. [S26][S27][S28][S29][S30][S31][S32][S33][S34][S35][S42][S112][S165][S166][S167]

### 4. Clean-build story with no dirty-retirement story
If the proposal celebrates new clean investment but says little about fossil retirement, subsidy reform, cleanup liability, lock-in prevention, or exit from incumbent assets, it is incomplete.
Ideal solutions are not just additive; they are also subtractive where subtraction is necessary. [S9][S23][S24][S25][S102]

### 5. Household heroics and burden stacking
If the proposal depends on ordinary people doing too much searching, comparing, financing, proving, coordinating, troubleshooting, and monitoring by hand, it is probably offloading system failure onto the wrong actor.
The archive should keep distrusting solutions that require users to behave like unpaid systems integrators. [S40][S55][S56][S58][S59][S65][S66][S67][S68][S69][S76][S80][S81][S82][S83][S84][S85][S86][S87][S88][S89][S90][S118][S119][S121][S123][S126][S127][S128][S129][S130][S131][S132][S135][S136][S137][S143][S146][S147]

### 6. Fragmentation with no accountable lead
If the proposal technically contains the right pieces but no one owns the joined path from offer to delivery to correction, it is still weak.
A fragmented clean path often functions like a hidden veto system. [S40][S55][S56][S58][S65][S66][S67][S68][S76][S80][S81][S82][S83][S118][S119][S121][S123][S143][S146][S147]

### 7. Public downside with private upside
If public authority absorbs routine risk, early losses, or tail cleanup while private actors keep one-way upside and weak obligations, the proposal is usually badly designed.
The archive should keep preferring disciplined downside allocation and material public-facing value capture. [S68][S69][S91][S92][S93][S94][S95][S96][S129][S130][S131][S132][S137][S138][S139][S141][S142][S143][S144][S145][S146]

### 8. Exceptions, fallbacks, or temporary bridges with no real sunset
If the proposal relies on waivers, emergency backstops, fossil bridges, or dirty standby modes that have no clear owner, closure trigger, review discipline, or route back to the main clean path, the archive should assume drift.
Bad-day modes and loopholes often become the real policy unless designed against explicitly. [S9][S23][S24][S40][S55][S56][S58][S64][S68][S76][S80][S84][S85][S121][S123][S135][S136][S137][S143][S146][S147]

### 9. Rich-country-only or pilot-only validity
If the proposal works only where capital is cheap, institutions are unusually strong, users are affluent, and exceptional coordination is available, it should not be mistaken for an ideal general solution.
The archive should keep distrusting climate ideas that cannot travel. [S56][S59][S60][S61][S62][S63][S64][S70][S71][S91][S92][S93][S94][S95][S96][S103]

### 10. Announcement metrics instead of real outcomes
If success is scored mainly by money announced, projects launched, memoranda signed, nominal capacity promised, or tonnes claimed on paper, the archive should slow down.
The relevant proof is usually physical outcome, service gain, bottleneck release, delivery repetition, and audited correction when wrong. [S27][S59][S65][S66][S67][S68][S113][S123][S136]

## Use rule

A future proposal does not need to trigger every red flag to deserve caution.
Usually **two or three strong red flags are already enough** to demote, quarantine, or at least slow promotion into the core canon.
This is especially true when the triggered red flags are the expensive ones: paper substitution, weak near-term cuts, scarcity misuse, user burden dumping, or loophole drift.

For the archive’s shortest reusable positive screen, see `59-ideal-solutions-fast-screen-promote-demote-quarantine.md`.
For the archive’s shortest joined positive canon, see `88-ideal-solutions-compact-canon-core-screens-in-one-place.md`.

## One-paragraph compression

**The archive’s negative canon is now simple: distrust climate proposals that substitute paper for physics, later cleanup for near-term cuts, scarce tools for easy uses, additive clean stories for real fossil exit, household heroics for system design, fragmented programmes for accountable offers, public downside for one-way private upside, temporary loopholes for real transition discipline, affluent or pilot conditions for general validity, and announcement metrics for physical proof.** [S100][S101][S102][S103][S104][S106][S118][S119][S123][S143][S146][S147]
