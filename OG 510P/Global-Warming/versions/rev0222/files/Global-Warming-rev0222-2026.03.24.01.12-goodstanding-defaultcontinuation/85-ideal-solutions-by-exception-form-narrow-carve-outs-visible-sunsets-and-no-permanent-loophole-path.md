# 85 — Ideal Solutions by Exception Form: Narrow Carve-Outs, Visible Sunsets, and No Permanent Loophole Path

## Claim

The archive already says that ideal climate solutions should usually work through defaults, repeatable delivery, and visible public rules rather than through ad hoc discretion [S53][S59][S64][S68][S80][S118][S119][S123][S129][S130][S131][S143][S146][S147].
It already says that emergency fossil or security exceptions, where they exist, should be narrow, time-limited, and paired with explicit exit plans [S9][S23][S24].
And it already warns that opaque exemptions, insider discretion, and emergency-only pathways becoming the normal route are signs of capture rather than signs of a well-designed transition [S40][S129][S130][S131][S132].

One more compact rule now belongs in the canon:

**ideal climate solutions should treat exceptions as relief valves, not as a second operating system. Carve-outs should usually be narrow, explicit, visible, reviewable, and sunset-bound, with a designed route back to the main clean path rather than a quiet slide into permanent loophole politics.**

In plain terms, serious climate systems will sometimes need hardship accommodations, technical waivers, security backstops, or temporary transition flexibility.
The archive does **not** say “no exceptions ever.”
It says:

**exceptions must stay exceptional. If the carve-out becomes broad, opaque, routine, or indefinite, it is no longer an exception; it is the real policy.** [S9][S23][S24][S40][S59][S64][S68][S80][S123][S129][S130][S131][S132][S143][S146][S147]

## Why this belongs in the canon

### 1. Every large transition faces edge cases

Real systems encounter hardship, reliability risk, technical non-compatibility, supply bottlenecks, emergency response, national-security constraints, and other cases that do not fit the standard path cleanly [S9][S23][S24][S59][S80][S143][S146].
A canon that only describes the main path but says nothing about the exception path is incomplete.

But the archive’s answer is not “make the whole system discretionary.”
It is to define a strong main path and a disciplined exception path.

### 2. Bad exception design quietly rewrites the policy

A climate programme may look ambitious in law or on paper, but if its waivers are broad, unlogged, politically tradable, or repeatedly renewed, the exception layer can become the real governing layer [S40][S64][S68][S129][S130][S131][S132][S143][S146][S147].

This is one way capture happens without an obvious frontal repeal.
The public story remains “the rule stands.”
The lived story becomes “the connected or persistent actor gets a carve-out.”

### 3. Exception discipline protects both fairness and throughput

Poorly governed exceptions are not only unfair.
They also slow delivery because every borderline case becomes a negotiation, every applicant seeks bespoke treatment, administrators spend time on waiver logic instead of routine throughput, and honest entrants cannot plan around stable rules [S59][S80][S118][S119][S123][S129][S130][S131][S143].

A disciplined exception path therefore helps both justice and speed.
It protects people who genuinely need relief while preventing discretionary overload from becoming the normal operating model.

### 4. The right response to repeated exceptions is often to fix the main path

If the same waiver is requested constantly, that is often evidence that the baseline design, timeline, support package, or substitute buildout is still wrong [S53][S59][S64][S80][S118][S119][S137][S143][S146].
Repeated exceptions should therefore be treated as design feedback, not merely as a queue of special favors.

## The archive’s exception-form tests

### 1. Necessity test
Is the exception tied to a real hardship, safety, reliability, or no-substitute constraint, or is it mainly a convenience path for avoiding the main rule? [S9][S23][S24][S64][S143][S146]

### 2. Narrowness test
Is it defined as tightly as possible by case type, duration, asset class, geography, or hardship condition, or does it sprawl into a broad loophole? [S9][S23][S24][S40][S130][S132][S146]

### 3. Visibility test
Will someone outside the immediate deal know that the exception exists, why it was granted, how many such cases exist, and how long they last, or is the waiver effectively private? [S68][S123][S129][S130][S131][S143]

### 4. Sunset-and-review test
Does the exception expire automatically unless affirmatively renewed under published criteria, or does it roll forward by inertia? [S9][S23][S24][S40][S143][S146][S147]

### 5. Return-path test
Is there a designed route back to the main clean pathway — including substitute buildout, compliance support, or a closure timeline — or is the exception simply a durable alternative regime? [S9][S23][S24][S53][S64][S80][S137][S143][S146]

### 6. Main-path-repair test
If similar exceptions keep recurring, is the programme required to ask what needs fixing in the standard pathway, or does it just accumulate waivers forever? [S53][S59][S64][S80][S118][S119][S123][S143][S146]

## What this rule promotes

Promote:
- hardship, safety, reliability, and transition exceptions that are explicit, published, and bounded rather than improvised in private [S9][S23][S24][S68][S123][S129][S130][S131]
- waiver logic with automatic sunsets, recurring review, and clear return conditions rather than indefinite grandfathering [S9][S23][S24][S40][S143][S146][S147]
- temporary flexibility paired with substitute buildout, delivery support, or explicit exit plans so the exception helps people through a constraint instead of preserving the constraint forever [S9][S23][S24][S53][S64][S137][S143][S146]
- programme governance that treats repeated exception demand as a signal to repair the baseline pathway, not as evidence that permanent discretionary politics is acceptable [S53][S59][S64][S80][S118][S119][S123][S143]

## What this rule demotes

Demote:
- broad or opaque carve-outs that let politically strong actors bypass the main rule while weaker actors must comply in full [S40][S129][S130][S131][S132]
- emergency or security exceptions with no sunset logic and no credible route back to the main pathway [S9][S23][S24][S40][S143][S146]
- programme designs that depend on constant case-by-case waiver bargaining to remain politically tolerable [S59][S80][S118][S119][S129][S130][S131][S143]
- “temporary” transitional loopholes that quietly become the durable policy [S9][S23][S24][S40][S64][S146][S147]


For the matching rule on how temporary waivers, bridge assets, and emergency dirty supports should actually close rather than merely carry sunset language, see `86-ideal-solutions-by-exit-form-temporary-means-temporary-clear-off-ramps-and-decommissioning.md`.

## One-paragraph compression

**Ideal climate solutions should keep exceptions exceptional. Hardship, safety, reliability, and transition carve-outs may sometimes be necessary, but they should be narrow, explicit, visible, reviewable, and sunset-bound, with a designed route back to the main clean path. If waivers become broad, opaque, routinely renewed, or politically tradable, they stop being relief valves and become the real policy. Repeated exception demand should usually trigger repair of the baseline design rather than an endless accumulation of loopholes** [S9][S23][S24][S40][S53][S59][S64][S68][S80][S118][S119][S123][S129][S130][S131][S132][S137][S143][S146][S147].
