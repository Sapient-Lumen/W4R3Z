# 214 — Ideal Solutions: The Clean Transition Should Arrive as a Coherent Offer, Not a Scavenger Hunt Compact Canon

## Claim

The archive already says that the climate programme should:
- do three physical jobs at once (`208`),
- make better ordinary service more common (`211`),
- protect necessity while constraining waste and delay (`212`),
- and make the clean path easier at the point of decision (`213`).

That is strong.
But the front door still lacked one tiny bridge about **how the programme should actually arrive to the intended user or place**.

A serious climate programme should not reach a household, renter, landlord, school, clinic, small firm, or municipality as a puzzle of scattered rebates, loans, contractor lists, tariff exceptions, proof rules, and disconnected offices.
It should increasingly arrive as a **coherent clean offer**.

This note is a compression note, not a new branch.
Its job is to state one front-door delivery rule in the fewest useful words:

**ideal climate solutions should increasingly arrive as coherent offers that already join the route in, the payment path, the delivery path, the proof path, and the correction path, rather than asking the user to assemble those pieces by hand** [S69][S80][S118][S119][S141][S143].

## Fast rule

**Prefer climate pathways that present a complete clean offer over pathways that present a scavenger hunt across fragmented programme pieces. Demote pathways that are “technically available” only because the intended user is expected to discover, finance, coordinate, document, and defend the package for themselves** [S69][S80][S118][S119][S141][S143].

## The compact canon

### 1. Availability is not the same as a usable offer
A climate pathway is not yet strong merely because all the parts exist somewhere.
A rebate may exist, a loan may exist, a contractor directory may exist, a permitting route may exist, and an advice line may exist — but if the intended user still has to stitch those pieces together manually, the programme is not yet arriving as a real transition offer [S80][S118][S119][S141][S143].

House rule: **do not confuse component availability with offer quality.**

### 2. The programme should increasingly arrive as one joined route
For common transition tasks, the stronger design is one joined route from first contact to persistent clean service: one understandable front door, a workable payment path, a delivery path, routine proof, clear remedy, and aftercare strong enough that the cleaner state actually holds [S69][S80][S118][S119][S141][S143].

House rule: **the intended user should receive a pathway, not assemble one.**

### 3. Coherent offers are a fairness rule and a scale rule at once
Fragmented programme design quietly sorts by spare time, spare cash, credit, tenure security, bureaucratic fluency, and contractor literacy.
That makes it slower and less fair at the same time.
A coherent offer therefore matters not only because it feels better, but because it reaches more people and places without requiring heroic local improvisation [S69][S80][S118][S119][S137][S143].

House rule: **design for the cash-constrained, time-constrained, renter, and low-capacity case, not only for the easiest adopter.**

### 4. A coherent offer is often the public-facing expression of the deeper system answer
Upstream systems may still involve standards, tariffs, procurement, codes, utility programmes, contractor networks, data reuse, and targeted subsidy.
The point is not to abolish complexity in the backend.
The point is to stop exporting that complexity to the weakest actor at the front end [S53][S69][S80][S118][S119][S143].

House rule: **hide backend complexity where possible; do not dump it downhill.**

### 5. The scavenger hunt is itself a climate failure mode
A weak programme often announces many helpful pieces while leaving no actor responsible for the whole journey.
The result is drop-off, inequality, slower uptake, and political distrust.
So the archive should treat the fragmented scavenger hunt as a real failure pattern, not as a cosmetic inconvenience [S80][S118][S119][S141][S143].

House rule: **when no one owns the whole offer, the user ends up owning the failure.**

**An ideal climate solution should increasingly arrive as a coherent clean offer — one joined route into payment, delivery, proof, remedy, and persistence — rather than as a scavenger hunt across programme fragments that asks the intended user to act as financier, coordinator, document specialist, and systems integrator at once.**
