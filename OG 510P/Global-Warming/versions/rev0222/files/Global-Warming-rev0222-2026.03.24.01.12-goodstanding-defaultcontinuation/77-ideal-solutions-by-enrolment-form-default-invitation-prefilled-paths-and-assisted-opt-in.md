# 77 — Ideal Solutions by Enrolment Form: Default Invitation, Pre-Filled Paths, and Assisted Opt-In

## Claim

The archive already says ideal climate solutions should be low-friction, easy to find, easy to qualify for, affordable to start, and realistic about who carries risk and paperwork.
It says the cleaner path should usually come through one clear front door, existing records, simple contracts, strong local channels, and fewer proof mazes [S69][S80][S118][S119][S137][S140][S141][S143].

One more compact rule belongs in the canon:

**ideal climate solutions should usually not wait passively for every intended user to self-start from zero.**

In plain terms:
- invite likely-eligible users directly where existing records and relationships already make that possible [S69][S71][S80][S118][S137][S143]
- pre-fill what the system already knows instead of asking people to begin with a blank page [S118][S119]
- use default continuation, default appointment slots, or other low-burden enrolment devices where the step is routine, low-risk, and strongly in the user’s interest [S118][S119][S137]
- reserve fully bespoke self-assembly for genuinely unusual or high-consequence cases rather than making it the universal doorway [S118][S119][S140][S143]

So the archive’s shortest enrolment-form rule is:

**use direct invitation, pre-filled pathways, and assisted or default enrolment where appropriate; do not build climate uptake around the assumption that the intended user will discover, initiate, and complete the whole process alone.**

## Why this belongs in the canon

### 1. Many good climate offers underperform because they are merely available, not actively taken up

A programme can have a usable interface, reasonable eligibility rules, affordable payment, and fair contract terms, yet still miss much of its target population because it waits for the user to begin everything voluntarily and from scratch [S69][S118][S137][S143].

That is a different failure from bad interface design or bad eligibility design.
The person may be able to understand the offer and qualify for it.
They just never cross the activation threshold because everyday life is busy, the trigger moment is short, and the system asked them to do all the initiating work.

So the archive needs a rule against confusing **formal access** with **practical enrolment**.

### 2. Good public administration should reduce initiation burden, not just processing burden

OECD’s 2025 regulatory work says people and businesses should not have to become experts in government operations to complete necessary tasks, and it points toward one-stop shops, interoperability, and once-only information practices that save time and effort [S118].
IEA’s efficiency-policy toolkit likewise emphasizes one-stop shops, streamlined procedures, standardised pathways, and digitised processes that lower transaction costs [S119].

The archive’s judgement is that these principles should bite one step earlier than many programmes currently allow.
If the system already knows enough to identify a likely fit, the user should often receive a **prompted path** rather than a blank search problem.

That does not always mean mandatory enrolment.
It more often means:
- direct outreach,
- pre-populated journeys,
- quick yes / no routing,
- assisted scheduling,
- or default continuation unless something material has changed [S118][S119][S137].

### 3. Enrolment form is part of justice because initiative burden is unevenly distributed

The people most meant to benefit from cleaner homes, safer cooling, lower bills, public transport improvements, efficient appliances, clean cooking support, or resilience upgrades often have the least spare time, the weakest cash buffers, the least administrative slack, or the lowest confidence that the process will be worth the effort [S69][S71][S137][S143].

That means a self-start-heavy design quietly selects for the already organised, already informed, and already confident.
A programme can be inclusive in budget theory and still exclusive in enrolment practice.

So the archive needs a plain justice rule:

**the cleaner path is weaker when it depends on high initiative from the very users whose daily conditions make initiative hardest.**

### 4. Direct invitation and assisted enrolment often fit concentrated control points better than mass self-navigation

The archive already prefers concentrated control points and trusted intermediaries over heroic end-user problem solving [S80][S118][S141].
That has an enrolment implication.
Utilities, landlords, social-housing systems, municipalities, schools, clinics, employers, and other already-connected institutions can often do more than merely advertise the offer.
They can route, pre-qualify, schedule, bundle, and follow up [S80][S118][S137][S141].

That is usually better than posting a technically correct programme online and hoping the right people arrive in the right week with the right documents and enough patience left.

### 5. Enrolment form is not the same as interface, eligibility, or continuity form

This note is not the same question as:
- `76-ideal-solutions-by-interface-form-one-front-door-plain-language-and-service-first-choice-architecture.md`, which asks **whether the user can find and understand the clean path at first contact**,
- `72-ideal-solutions-by-eligibility-form-presumptive-inclusion-existing-data-and-no-proof-mazes.md`, which asks **how the user qualifies without repeated proof burdens**,
- `70-ideal-solutions-by-payment-form-low-upfront-burden-pay-over-asset-life-and-no-reimbursement-mazes.md`, which asks **how the cleaner option should usually be paid for**,
- or `75-ideal-solutions-by-continuity-form-no-reset-to-dirty-defaults-at-handoffs-moves-and-rebuilds.md`, which asks **how clean gains should survive handoffs once the user is already inside the system**.

This note asks a narrower and earlier question:

**once the clean path exists and the user could plausibly qualify, who does the work of activation?**

### 6. Good enrolment form improves speed because it catches short windows

Many climate opportunities appear briefly: appliance failure, lease renewal, bill distress, heat season, boiler replacement, insurance changes, municipal works, disaster recovery, fleet renewal, or school and clinic upgrade windows.
A system that waits for self-started action often misses those moments [S80][S118][S119][S137].

Direct invitation, pre-filled routing, assisted scheduling, and short decision paths are not cosmetic conveniences.
They are ways to keep the clean option alive when time, attention, and trust are scarce.

## The archive’s enrolment-form hierarchy

A future proposal is usually stronger when it fits most of this hierarchy.

### 1. Invitation layer
Use direct invitation where likely eligibility can be inferred through existing regulated or public-service relationships rather than expecting users to discover and start alone [S69][S71][S80][S118][S137][S143].

### 2. Pre-filled layer
Pre-populate facts the system already knows and route users quickly rather than opening with a blank multi-step form [S118][S119].

### 3. Assisted-start layer
Use assisted opt-in, scheduled support, trusted local intermediaries, or short guided pathways where users are unlikely to complete a cold self-serve process unaided [S80][S118][S137][S141][S143].

### 4. Default-continuation layer
Where the user is already inside a beneficial low-risk pathway and material facts have not changed, prefer default continuation or light-touch reconfirmation over forced restart from zero [S118][S119][S137].

### 5. Human-exit layer
Keep it easy to decline, pause, or ask for a person when the fit is wrong, the case is unusual, or the consequences are significant [S118][S140][S141].

## The archive’s enrolment-form tests

### 1. Activation test
Does the system wait for the intended user to initiate everything, or does it actively carry likely-fit users toward the clean path? [S69][S118][S137][S143]

### 2. Blank-page test
How much of the journey begins from an empty form even when the system already holds relevant facts? [S118][S119]

### 3. Initiative-burden test
Who must supply the scarce attention — the constrained user, or the institution that already has the data, the relationship, or the delivery mandate? [S80][S118][S137][S141]

### 4. Assisted-completion test
Can a time-poor, cash-stressed, or administratively wary user complete the path with light support, or only by acting as a persistent self-advocate? [S69][S71][S137][S143]

### 5. Drop-off test
At what stage does the intended user disappear — before application, during first form completion, before scheduling, or before signature? [S118][S140][S143]

### 6. Re-start test
Does the design force users to begin again from zero after minor changes or routine renewals when a continuation path would have been enough? [S118][S119][S137]

## What this rules in

This note rules **in**:
- direct invitations, prompted renewal paths, and targeted outreach where likely-fit users can already be identified through existing service relationships [S69][S71][S80][S118][S137][S143]
- pre-filled or short forms that ask only for what is new or genuinely uncertain [S118][S119]
- assisted-start models through utilities, municipalities, housing providers, schools, clinics, employers, and other trusted channels that can carry users into the cleaner pathway [S80][S118][S137][S141]
- default continuation for routine beneficial arrangements when material facts have not changed [S118][S119][S137]
- designs that treat early-stage drop-off as a core delivery problem rather than blaming intended users for not finishing a needlessly self-start-heavy journey [S118][S140][S143]

## What this demotes

This note demotes:
- programmes that are technically available but rely on cold discovery and full user initiative for mass uptake [S69][S118][S137][S143]
- blank-page application journeys that ignore data the system already holds [S118][S119]
- outreach designs that stop at awareness and never reduce the activation burden itself [S118][S140][S141]
- delivery systems that force routine participants to restart enrolment from zero when a continuation path would have worked [S118][S119][S137]
- climate support that quietly sorts for the time-rich and administratively confident by making self-starting persistence the hidden admission test [S69][S71][S137][S143]

## One-paragraph compression

**Ideal climate solutions should usually not wait for every intended user to self-start from scratch. Prefer direct invitation, pre-filled pathways, assisted opt-in, and default continuation where appropriate, especially when the system already knows enough to identify a likely fit. Do not build mass climate uptake around blank-page forms, cold discovery, and the hidden assumption that constrained users will provide unlimited initiative just to reach support that was supposedly designed for them [S69][S71][S80][S118][S119][S137][S140][S141][S143].**
