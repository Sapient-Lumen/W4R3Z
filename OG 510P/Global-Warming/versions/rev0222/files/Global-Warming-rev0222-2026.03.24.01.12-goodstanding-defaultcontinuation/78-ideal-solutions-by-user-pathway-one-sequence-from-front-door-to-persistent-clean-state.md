# 78 — Ideal Solutions by User Pathway: One Sequence from Front Door to Persistent Clean State

## Claim

The archive now contains compact rules for interface form, enrolment form, eligibility form, payment form, contract form, aftercare form, verification form, remedy form, and continuity form.
Those notes are useful on their own.
But the archive now also needs the smallest synthesis rule that binds them together:

**ideal climate solutions should usually work as one coherent user pathway from first contact to persistent clean state, not as a set of individually defensible steps that fail when stitched together.**

In ordered form, the pathway is usually:
1. **find the path**,
2. **enter the path**,
3. **qualify with light proof**,
4. **pay in a tolerable temporal shape**,
5. **sign a comparable and enforceable offer**,
6. **receive clean service or delivery**,
7. **settle the new state through aftercare and early tuning**,
8. **prove ordinary claims proportionately**,
9. **get fast correction when something goes wrong**,
10. **keep the clean gain through ordinary handoffs** [S69][S80][S118][S119][S137][S140][S141][S143][S144].

So the archive’s shortest pathway rule is:

**do not optimize one step while leaving the adjacent steps as hidden veto points. A climate pathway is only as usable as its worst routine transition.**

## Why this belongs in the canon

### 1. A good programme can still fail as a bad sequence

A climate offer can have generous subsidy levels, reasonable contracts, and strong technical performance on paper and still fail in practice because the steps do not join up.
A user may find the offer but fail at enrolment.
They may enrol but stall at proof.
They may qualify but not be able to float the upfront cost.
They may get the installation but never settle into stable use because nobody commissions or tunes it well, or they may later lose the gain at a move, tenancy change, account change, or rebuild [S53][S58][S69][S108][S118][S119][S120][S121][S137][S143][S144].

That means many climate failures are not failures of any single component.
They are failures of **pathway integrity**.

### 2. The archive had the parts but not yet the shortest whole-sequence rule

The archive already says:
- the clean path should be easy to find and understand (`76-ideal-solutions-by-interface-form-one-front-door-plain-language-and-service-first-choice-architecture.md`) [S118][S119][S140][S141][S143]
- likely-fit users should be invited and helped into the path rather than left to self-start from zero (`77-ideal-solutions-by-enrolment-form-default-invitation-prefilled-paths-and-assisted-opt-in.md`) [S69][S71][S80][S118][S119][S137][S140][S141][S143]
- qualification should usually use presumptive inclusion and existing records where possible (`72-ideal-solutions-by-eligibility-form-presumptive-inclusion-existing-data-and-no-proof-mazes.md`) [S69][S71][S118][S119][S137][S143]
- payment should usually be low-upfront and matched to service life (`70-ideal-solutions-by-payment-form-low-upfront-burden-pay-over-asset-life-and-no-reimbursement-mazes.md`) [S69][S91][S96][S118][S119][S137][S143]
- contracts should usually be easy to compare and hard to game (`71-ideal-solutions-by-contract-form-standard-offers-prequalified-delivery-and-performance-backed-terms.md`) [S56][S118][S119][S143]
- ordinary claims should use the lightest credible proof (`73-ideal-solutions-by-verification-form-simple-proof-metered-where-possible-and-sampled-audit-where-sensible.md`) [S113][S118][S123][S135][S136]
- ordinary failures should be easy to correct (`74-ideal-solutions-by-remedy-form-fast-recourse-automatic-correction-and-no-appeals-mazes.md`) [S118][S119][S143]
- ordinary handoffs should not reset users or assets to dirty defaults (`75-ideal-solutions-by-continuity-form-no-reset-to-dirty-defaults-at-handoffs-moves-and-rebuilds.md`) [S69][S118][S119][S137][S143]
- delivery should usually be followed by active commissioning, first-use support, early follow-up, and fast tuning so the cleaner state becomes stable in ordinary use (`82-ideal-solutions-by-aftercare-form-active-commissioning-follow-up-and-early-performance-retention.md`) [S53][S58][S69][S108][S119][S120][S121][S143][S144]

This note adds the shortest integrating rule:

**judge the whole user pathway as one chain.**

For the matching provider-side synthesis rule — that the user should receive this chain as one coherent clean offer rather than as a set of disconnected programme pieces — see `80-ideal-solutions-by-offer-quality-complete-clean-offers-not-fragmented-programme-pieces.md`.
For the matching provider-side assurance rule — that the promise itself should stay connected to delivery, proof, correction, and retention rather than floating free from them — see `83-ideal-solutions-by-assurance-stack-promise-delivery-proof-correction-and-retention.md`.

### 3. Pathway integrity is part of justice, not mere convenience

Poorly joined systems sort by time, literacy, language, confidence, liquidity, and ability to survive delay.
That means a pathway can be formally open but substantively selective.
The more stages that require extra initiative, extra cash, or repeated proof, the more the system favors actors who are already organised, liquid, and administratively experienced [S69][S71][S118][S119][S137][S140][S143].

So pathway integrity is part of the archive’s justice doctrine:

**the intended user should not have to win a sequence of small administrative endurance tests just to reach a supposedly public-interest clean outcome.**

### 4. Sequence discipline improves copyability and delivery capacity

A joined-up pathway is easier to train, easier to explain, easier to replicate across utilities, municipalities, schools, landlords, social-housing systems, and local intermediaries.
A fragmented pathway forces local actors to invent handoffs manually and absorbs scarce delivery capacity in casework rather than scale [S80][S118][S119][S141].

So this note also belongs in the archive’s copyability and routinization doctrine:

**good climate pathways should be teachable as sequences, not only defensible as policy fragments.**

## The archive’s ordered pathway stack

A future proposal is usually stronger when it can answer all of these in one sequence.

### 1. Front-door layer
Can the intended user tell quickly what the pathway is and where to start? Prefer one clear front door, service-first language, and a small number of intelligible routes [S118][S119][S140][S141][S143].

### 2. Enrolment layer
Does the system actively invite and carry likely-fit users into the path, or does it wait for cold discovery and blank-page initiative? Prefer direct invitation, pre-filled journeys, and assisted opt-in where appropriate [S69][S71][S80][S118][S119][S137][S140][S141][S143].

### 3. Eligibility layer
Can users qualify through existing records, once-only proof, and local channels instead of repeated paperwork or duplicative checks? [S69][S71][S118][S119][S137][S143]

### 4. Payment layer
Can suitable users cross the moment of decision without large upfront cash, long reimbursement waits, or financing structures that quietly assume they are already liquid? [S69][S91][S96][S118][S119][S137][S143]

### 5. Contract layer
Are the promises comparable, legible, and enforceable, especially when providers claim savings, comfort, flexibility, or reliability? [S56][S118][S119][S143]

### 6. Delivery layer
Does the user receive clean service and measurable performance rather than a pile of unmanaged integration work, split incentives, and maintenance risk? [S56][S69][S118][S137][S143].

### 7. Aftercare layer
After delivery, is there a routine commissioning, onboarding, first-bill or first-season check, and quick tuning path so early confusion or drift does not become abandonment or fallback? [S16][S17][S53][S58][S108][S119][S120][S121][S143][S144]

### 8. Verification layer
Are ordinary claims proved with the lightest credible method — direct data where possible, standard methods for routine measures, sampled audit where sensible — rather than bespoke MRV for everything? [S113][S118][S123][S135][S136]

### 9. Remedy layer
If the system fails, underdelivers, or misroutes the user, is there a fast correction path that puts the fix burden on the actor with the control or the data? [S118][S119][S143]

### 10. Continuity layer
After success, can ordinary handoffs preserve the clean state rather than resetting the user or asset to a dirtier baseline for administrative reasons? [S69][S118][S119][S137][S143]

## The archive’s pathway-integrity tests

### 1. Weakest-link test
Which routine step is most likely to cause abandonment, exclusion, or reset? That step should be treated as a primary design problem, not a footnote.

### 2. Handoff test
Are transitions between stages automatic, assisted, or at least clearly signposted, or is the user repeatedly dumped back into blank-page navigation? [S118][S119][S137]

### 3. Burden-shift test
At which stage does the system start asking the weaker actor to carry cash, proof, coordination, or troubleshooting that a stronger actor could have handled? [S69][S71][S118][S137][S143]

For the archive’s shortest cross-cutting rule on this issue, see `79-ideal-solutions-by-burden-shape-do-not-stack-search-proof-cash-coordination-and-risk-on-the-weakest-actor.md`.

### 4. Drop-off test
Where do typical users disappear: before first contact, during qualification, at the payment step, after the contract, or at a later handoff? [S118][S140][S143]

### 5. Persistence test
If the pathway works once, does the gain stay in place through normal life events and administrative change? [S69][S118][S119][S137][S143]

## What this rule promotes

Promote:
- climate pathways that are designed as complete sequences rather than disconnected programme fragments [S118][S119][S141]
- front-to-back designs where each stage reduces the burden of the next stage instead of reloading it onto the user [S69][S71][S118][S137][S143]
- common referral paths, shared records, standard offers, embedded finance, proportionate verification, fast correction, and sticky continuation across handoffs [S69][S91][S96][S118][S119][S123][S137][S143]
- routine measurement of drop-off points and handoff failures as delivery indicators, not just counts of nominal programme availability [S118][S140][S143]

## What this rule demotes

Demote:
- programmes that look strong within one administrative silo but fail when the user has to move from one stage to the next [S118][S119][S137]
- climate offers that are easy to announce but hard to traverse end to end [S118][S140][S143]
- designs that solve discoverability but not qualification, or solve payment but not verification, or solve installation but not continuity [S69][S118][S119][S137][S143]
- any system that treats abandonment, repeated restart, or loss of gains at handoffs as user weakness rather than design failure [S118][S119][S140][S143]

## One-paragraph compression

**Ideal climate solutions should usually work as one coherent pathway from first contact to persistent clean state. Make the path findable, invite likely-fit users in, qualify them with light proof, shape payment tolerably, use comparable contracts, deliver clean service, verify claims proportionately, correct ordinary failures quickly, and preserve gains through handoffs. Do not optimise one step while leaving the next step as a hidden veto point; a climate pathway is only as usable as its weakest routine transition. And from the provider side, the promise made at the front of the pathway should stay visibly connected to delivery, proof, correction, and retention rather than floating free from them [S69][S80][S91][S96][S118][S119][S123][S137][S140][S141][S143][S144].**
