# 200 — Ideal Solutions: Basic Unit of Progress — A Verified Replacement Package at a Real Control Point Compact Canon

## Claim

The archive now has a shortest answer (`195`), a non-substitutions note (`196`), a simultaneous-verdicts note (`197`), a transition-mechanism note (`198`), a replacement-proof / what-counts note (`199`), a package-logic note (`190`), a control-point-duties note (`163`), and a turnover-aware note (`169`).
What it still lacked was one tiny front-door note answering a practical design question that sits underneath many climate arguments:

**what is the smallest serious unit of progress in a climate programme?**

That gap matters because climate debate often swaps between very different units without noticing:
technology names, policy instruments, investment totals, project announcements, user purchases, or national targets.
But the archive’s house view is stricter.
The smallest serious unit is not a technology by itself, a policy by itself, or a clean asset added beside the old regime.
It is a **verified replacement package at a real control point**: one cleaner service made truly usable, one switching moment actually used, one dirty pathway actually constrained or retired, one protection problem actually handled, and one physical gain actually evidenced.
This note is therefore a compression note, not a new doctrinal branch.
Its job is to gather the archive’s package logic, control-point logic, turnover logic, transition-mechanism logic, replacement-proof logic, and physical-first logic into one atomic design rule [S100][S101][S103][S104][S106][S143][S145][S146][S147][S161][S163][S164][S180].

## Fast rule

**Do not treat a technology, target, or project announcement as the basic unit of climate success. Treat the basic unit as a verified replacement package at a real control point: cleaner service ready to carry load, a switching moment actually captured, the dirtier path actually constrained, people protected through the handoff, and the result proved in physical outcomes.**

## The five parts of the basic unit

### 1. A real control point
A serious unit of progress should sit where one decision can move many later outcomes: procurement, grid connection, fleet renewal, landlord obligation, equipment standards, public rebuilding, industrial purchasing, tariff design, finance terms, or other concentrated levers.
If the proposal only names a desirable technology without naming the control point that can spread it, the archive should treat the unit as incomplete [S103][S106][S143][S145][S163][S180].

### 2. A cleaner service that can actually carry load
A serious unit of progress should make the cleaner path usable enough to deliver real service, not merely appear as premium niche capacity beside an unchanged system.
That means the package may include grid work, interconnection, installation, maintenance, standards, finance, or operational reform as part of the same unit rather than as “later details” [S100][S103][S106][S143][S180].

### 3. A switching moment actually captured
A serious unit of progress should ride a real trigger point: sale, lease, replacement, retrofit, refinance, rebuild, public procurement cycle, post-disaster reconstruction, or plant reinvestment moment.
If the package misses the turnover moment, the archive should usually mark it down because the same system will soon have to pay to revisit the decision under harder conditions [S146][S161][S163][S164].

### 4. A dirty path actually prevented, retired, or denied another cycle
A serious unit of progress should include some visible contraction of the inferior path: no-replacement, no-rebuild, retirement, stricter standard, subsidy removal, leak repair, closure, permit restraint, or another credible form of dirty shrinkage.
Clean addition without dirty contraction is often useful, but it is not yet the full unit the archive should count as solved replacement [S101][S145][S147][S163][S164].

### 5. A physical result that can be checked
A serious unit of progress should end in evidence that gross emissions, methane, waste, exposure, or another real harm has actually moved in the right direction.
If the package cannot be checked in physical outcomes, the archive should treat it as unfinished design rather than banked progress [S101][S103][S106][S147].

## Why this compression helps

### It stops category drift
This note keeps the archive from bouncing carelessly between technologies, policies, projects, and outcomes as if they were the same thing.
They are related, but the serious unit is the joined replacement package that links them.

### It makes programmes decomposable
A climate programme becomes easier to govern when large ambitions can be broken into repeatable, comparable units:
school retrofits with clean heat and bill protection,
fleet turnover with charging and diesel retirement,
industrial procurement with common definitions and old-process contraction,
cooling upgrades with peak-load relief and minimum-performance standards,
or methane programmes with measurement, repair, and enforcement.

### It keeps scaling honest
Once the basic unit is stated clearly, the archive can ask better scaling questions:
how many of these units exist,
which control points still lack one,
which units are easiest to copy,
and which supposedly “scaled” plans are really only lists of ingredients without verified replacement packages inside them.

## Compression rule

**The archive should count progress in verified replacement packages at real control points, not in isolated technologies, targets, or announcements: make the cleaner service usable, capture the switching moment, constrain the dirty fallback, protect people through the handoff, and prove the physical gain.**
