# 87 — Ideal Solutions by Edge-Conditions Form: Fallback, Exception, and Exit as One Discipline

## Claim

The archive already says that serious climate systems need a good bad-day mode rather than a silent slide into sticky dirty fallback [S16][S17][S55][S56][S58][S76][S81][S82][S83][S84][S126][S127][S128][S143][S144].
It already says that hardship, reliability, and transition carve-outs should stay narrow, visible, reviewable, and sunset-bound rather than becoming the real policy [S9][S23][S24][S40][S59][S64][S68][S80][S123][S129][S130][S131][S132][S143][S146][S147].
And it already says that anything justified as temporary should come with a named owner, visible closure triggers, a practical off-ramp, and funded decommissioning or cleanup where relevant [S9][S23][S24][S40][S55][S56][S58][S68][S76][S80][S84][S85][S121][S123][S135][S136][S137][S143][S146][S147].

One more compact synthesis rule now belongs in the canon:

**ideal climate solutions should have good edge-conditions form. They should treat fallback, exceptions, and exits as one joined discipline: the off-normal path should do the least damage, the carve-out should stay narrow and visible, and the temporary bridge should already contain its own return and closure logic.**

In plain terms, a climate system is not fully specified when it only describes the happy path.
It also needs a compact answer to three linked questions:
- what happens when the normal clean path is stressed or interrupted,
- what departures from the main rule are allowed,
- and how those departures actually end.

So the archive’s shortest edge-conditions rule is:

**design the margins of the system as deliberately as the center: fail soft, keep carve-outs exceptional, and make temporary dirt easy to see, hard to normalize, and straightforward to close.** [S9][S23][S24][S40][S55][S56][S58][S64][S68][S76][S80][S84][S85][S121][S123][S135][S136][S137][S143][S146][S147]

## Why this belongs in the canon

### 1. Many bad climate outcomes live at the edges, not in the stated main rule

A transition can look strong on paper because its mainline clean pathway is well designed.
But real performance is often decided at the edges: outages, reliability scares, hardship claims, emergency backstops, bridge subsidies, grandfathering, post-disaster operating modes, and the political habit of renewing what was supposed to be temporary [S9][S23][S24][S40][S55][S56][S58][S76][S84][S85][S143][S146][S147].

That means the archive needs not only a theory of the normal pathway, but also a compact theory of **non-normal governance**.

### 2. Fallback, exception, and exit are different questions, but one policy discipline

`84-ideal-solutions-by-fallback-form-fail-soft-use-the-least-damaging-backup-and-return-fast.md` asks what happens when the normal clean pathway is breached.
`85-ideal-solutions-by-exception-form-narrow-carve-outs-visible-sunsets-and-no-permanent-loophole-path.md` asks how waivers and carve-outs stay exceptional.
`86-ideal-solutions-by-exit-form-temporary-means-temporary-clear-off-ramps-and-decommissioning.md` asks how temporary dirty bridges actually end.

This note adds the missing synthesis:

**those are not three unrelated side topics. They are one edge-conditions discipline for keeping the off-normal zone from quietly becoming the real operating system.**

### 3. Weak edge design is a common route to quiet lock-in

Dirty lock-in often returns through the side door, not the front door.
A system announces a clean mainline path, then tolerates a dirty fallback, broadens the exception, renews the temporary bridge, and eventually treats the off-normal regime as the durable baseline [S9][S23][S24][S40][S55][S56][S58][S64][S68][S76][S84][S85][S143][S146][S147].

So the archive needs a sharper compression:

**the edges are where relapse hides.**

### 4. Good edge conditions protect legitimacy as well as emissions goals

People can live with a clean transition that has bounded relief valves and temporary backup logic.
What corrodes trust is a system whose bad-day mode is dirty and sticky, whose exceptions are opaque or politically biased, and whose temporary measures never really close [S40][S58][S69][S76][S80][S84][S85][S118][S119][S123][S129][S130][S131][S132][S137][S143][S146][S147].

Edge-conditions quality therefore belongs inside the archive’s legitimacy and durability doctrine, not outside it.

## The archive’s edge-conditions hierarchy

A future proposal is usually stronger when it can answer this full non-happy-path stack clearly.

### 1. Fallback layer
When the clean normal pathway is interrupted, what backup appears in practice, and is it the least damaging realistic option? [S16][S17][S55][S56][S58][S76][S81][S82][S83][S84][S126][S127][S128][S143][S144]

### 2. Exception layer
What departures from the main rule are allowed, for whom, under what evidence, and how are those departures kept narrow, visible, and reviewable? [S9][S23][S24][S40][S59][S64][S68][S80][S123][S129][S130][S131][S132][S143][S146][S147]

### 3. Exit layer
What makes the temporary measure end in practice, who closes it, how do users rejoin the main clean path, and who pays any cleanup or retirement obligation? [S9][S23][S24][S40][S55][S56][S58][S68][S76][S80][S84][S85][S121][S123][S135][S136][S137][S143][S146][S147]

## The archive’s edge-conditions tests

### 1. Non-happy-path test
Can the proposal describe what happens when normal conditions do not hold, or does it become vague exactly where political and operational stress begins? [S55][S56][S58][S76][S84][S85][S143][S146][S147]

### 2. Quiet-relapse test
Do bad-day backup, hardship carve-outs, or temporary bridges create an easy route for dirty practice to become the real baseline? [S9][S23][S24][S40][S55][S56][S58][S64][S68][S76][S84][S85][S143][S146][S147]

### 3. Visibility test
Will managers, regulators, and the public be able to see when fallback is active, when exceptions are being used, and which temporary measures remain open? [S40][S65][S66][S67][S68][S80][S118][S119][S121][S123][S129][S130][S131][S132][S143]

### 4. Closure test
Are there real triggers, owners, and return paths for ending temporary dirty arrangements, or is continuation the path of least resistance? [S9][S23][S24][S40][S55][S56][S58][S68][S76][S80][S84][S85][S121][S123][S135][S136][S137][S143][S146][S147]

### 5. Baseline-repair test
If edge cases keep recurring, does the design repair the mainline system, or does it merely accumulate more exceptions and emergency patches? [S55][S56][S58][S76][S84][S85][S118][S119][S123][S137][S143][S146][S147]

## What this rule promotes

Promote:
- climate pathways whose bad-day mode, exception logic, and exit logic visibly point back toward the main clean path rather than away from it [S9][S23][S24][S55][S56][S58][S76][S84][S85][S143][S146][S147]
- bounded relief valves that reduce hardship or reliability risk without quietly replacing the main policy [S9][S23][S24][S40][S59][S64][S68][S80][S123][S129][S130][S131][S132][S143]
- temporary measures introduced together with named owners, closure triggers, return routes, and funded cleanup responsibilities where relevant [S23][S24][S68][S121][S123][S135][S136][S137][S143]
- visible monitoring of off-normal use so repeated edge-case demand becomes a repair signal for the baseline design [S65][S66][S67][S68][S80][S118][S119][S121][S123][S143]

## What this rule demotes

Demote:
- climate systems whose off-normal path is dirtier, stickier, more opaque, and more politically durable than the official clean path [S9][S23][S24][S40][S55][S56][S58][S64][S68][S76][S84][S85][S143][S146][S147]
- dirty standby arrangements, rolling exemptions, or emergency programmes that are easy to invoke and institutionally hard to close [S9][S23][S24][S40][S55][S56][S68][S76][S84][S85][S121][S123][S135][S136][S143][S146][S147]
- any design that treats fallback, exception, and exit as separate administrative trivia instead of one joined relapse-control problem [S40][S55][S56][S58][S76][S80][S84][S85][S143][S146][S147]
- proposals that interpret repeated emergency reliance as proof that more carve-outs are needed, instead of proof that the mainline system still needs repair [S55][S56][S58][S76][S84][S85][S118][S119][S123][S137][S143][S146][S147]

## One-paragraph compression

**Ideal climate solutions should have good edge-conditions form. They should treat fallback, exceptions, and exits as one joined discipline: the off-normal path should do the least damage, the carve-out should stay narrow and visible, and the temporary bridge should already contain its own return and closure logic. A climate system is less ideal if its official clean pathway is strong but its margins quietly normalize sticky dirty relapse. The edges are part of the design, not an afterthought.** [S9][S23][S24][S40][S55][S56][S58][S64][S68][S76][S80][S84][S85][S121][S123][S135][S136][S137][S143][S146][S147]
