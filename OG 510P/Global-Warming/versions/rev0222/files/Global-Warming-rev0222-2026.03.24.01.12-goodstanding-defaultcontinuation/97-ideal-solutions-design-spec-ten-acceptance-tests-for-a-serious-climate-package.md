# 97 — Ideal Solutions Design Spec: Ten Acceptance Tests for a Serious Climate Package

## Claim

The archive already has:
- a one-page answer (`58`)
- a compact package note (`57`)
- a compact canon of screens (`88`)
- an operator workflow (`91`) and operator card (`93`)

What it still lacked was the shortest bridge between **“what is the answer?”** and **“what must a serious proposal actually satisfy?”**

That gap matters because many users do not first need the whole archive.
They need the shortest acceptance test for deciding whether a policy package, technology strategy, municipal plan, or institutional design is even in the neighborhood of an ideal solution.

This note is a **compression note**, not a new doctrinal branch.
Its job is to turn the archive's answer into ten reusable acceptance tests.

## Fast rule

**A climate package counts as ideal only if it passes most or all of these tests at once. Failing one edge test may be repairable. Failing the first three usually means the proposal is not serious enough to lead the portfolio.**

## Ten acceptance tests

### 1. Fast physical effect test
Does it reduce real warming pressure or real exposure on a decision-relevant timescale?

Pass signs:
- cuts gross emissions, methane, or direct exposure in years that matter for overshoot and near-term harm
- delivers physical effects, not mainly paper claims, offsets, or remote accounting substitutions
- starts producing some visible gains before the whole long buildout is complete [S100][S101][S105][S106][S109]

Why it matters:
- ideal solutions must care about the next decade, not only the end-state story

### 2. Replacement-not-delay test
Does it build or protect the cleaner system while shrinking dependence on the dirtier or more dangerous one?

Pass signs:
- adds real clean substitute capacity
- closes, constrains, or disciplines incumbent dirty capacity rather than assuming passive displacement will be enough
- avoids locking in assets, habits, or land-use patterns that make later cleanup harder [S100][S101][S102][S109][S110][S112]

Why it matters:
- an ideal solution is not only additive; it changes the direction of the system

### 3. Service-and-justice test
Does it raise essential service floors while cutting waste, excess, and unjust burden?

Pass signs:
- protects or improves basic access to cooling, heat, mobility, food, safety, health, and resilience
- lowers bills, hassle, or exposure for constrained households and places
- puts stronger restraint first on affluent waste, avoidable luxury throughput, and other high-footprint excess [S104][S106][S108][S117][S143]

Why it matters:
- ideal climate solutions that deepen deprivation or dump burden downward are not durable solutions

### 4. Low-overhead-substitute test
Does it use the cleanest and least resource-heavy good-enough substitute first, reserving scarce options for hard residuals?

Pass signs:
- prioritizes efficiency, passive performance, direct electrification, compact form, and material thrift where workable
- treats scarce clean fuels, biomass, carbon capture, and removals as constrained tools, not default excuses
- reduces recurring claims on land, water, materials, and clean electricity where possible [S100][S103][S104][S106][S111][S112]

Why it matters:
- ideal solutions solve climate with the smallest plausible biophysical overhead

### 5. Buildability test
Can it actually be financed, permitted, connected, staffed, maintained, and supplied at scale?

Pass signs:
- faces grid, queue, permitting, workforce, procurement, and maintenance bottlenecks directly
- uses sequencing that fits available delivery capacity
- prefers what can scale through repeatable routines rather than synchronized heroic bets [S102][S103][S107][S112][S117][S121]

Why it matters:
- a theoretically elegant solution that cannot clear real bottlenecks is not an ideal solution in practice

### 6. Low-friction-user-path test
Can normal users or institutions get through the clean path without becoming expert coordinators?

Pass signs:
- one clear front door where possible
- low upfront cost, standard offers, prefilled paths, and minimal proof mazes
- clean performance is delivered as a coherent service or package rather than a scavenger hunt across fragmented programmes [S118][S119][S120][S143][S144][S146]

Why it matters:
- ideal solutions should make the cleaner choice ordinary, not heroic

### 7. Truth-and-correction test
Can the system tell whether it is working, and can it correct failure quickly?

Pass signs:
- measures real outcomes, not only commitments or spend
- uses verification that is simple where possible and strict where needed
- includes remedy, repair, and ratcheting when performance disappoints [S103][S111][S113][S114]

Why it matters:
- climate strategy needs feedback loops, not ceremony

### 8. Edge-conditions test
Does it behave well on bad days, under exceptions, and during transition bridges?

Pass signs:
- fallback options are temporary, least-damaging, and visibly subordinate to the main clean path
- hardship and reliability carve-outs stay narrow, reviewable, and sunset-bound
- temporary dirty bridges have explicit exits and decommissioning logic [S108][S109][S110]

Why it matters:
- many climate systems fail at the edges first and quietly normalize the exception path

### 9. Copyable-but-place-fit test
Can the package travel across jurisdictions without pretending every place is identical?

Pass signs:
- has a common spine that can be translated into model rules, templates, and routines
- adapts to climate zone, settlement pattern, institutional capability, and resource context
- does not depend on one unusually capable team or one-off local heroics [S112][S115][S117][S118]

Why it matters:
- ideal solutions should spread faster than bespoke solutions while still fitting local reality

### 10. Durability test
Will it survive fiscal pressure, electoral turnover, and changing political conditions?

Pass signs:
- lowers future burn rates, not only current emissions
- shares public-created gains visibly enough to keep legitimacy
- builds constituencies, institutions, standards, contracts, and assets that are hard to reverse casually [S100][S143][S145][S146][S147][S148]

Why it matters:
- a solution that vanishes at the next budget shock or election is not an ideal solution

## Screening shortcut

A proposal should usually be demoted quickly when it is:
- slow in physical effect
- only additive and not replacement-oriented
- regressive in burden
- dependent on scarce miracle inputs for routine uses
- institutionally delicate or maze-like for normal users
- weak on proof, correction, or exit discipline
