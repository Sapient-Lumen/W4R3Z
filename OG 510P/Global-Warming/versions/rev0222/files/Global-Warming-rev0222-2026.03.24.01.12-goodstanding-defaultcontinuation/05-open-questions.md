# 05 — Open Questions

These are the archive’s highest-salience unresolved questions. They are not reasons for delay; they are where more research most improves the program.

## 1. What financial architecture can actually scale clean buildout in emerging markets?
The problem is not whether projects exist. It is whether they can clear currency, debt, and policy-risk hurdles at sufficient speed [S4][S6][S13][S91][S92][S93][S95][S96]. The archive now has a dedicated finance-architecture note, but the comparative design question remains open: which mixes of concessional capital, local-currency finance, guarantees, tariff reform, debt treatment, and project-preparation support travel best across different EMDE contexts?

## 2. Which industrial pathways dominate where?
There will not be one global winner across steel, cement, chemicals, shipping, and aviation. The archive should continue mapping regional pathway pluralism rather than forcing fake uniformity.

## 3. How much high-integrity CDR is realistically scalable without ecological or energy-system distortion?
IPCC makes clear some removals are unavoidable for residuals [S1]. State of CDR sharpens the implementation gap: around 2 GtCO2/yr of CDR already occurs, but almost all of it is conventional land-based removal, while novel CDR remains around 1.3 MtCO2/yr [S42]. The unresolved issue is scale, timing, permanence, governance, MRV quality, and competition for land, energy, and water.

## 4. Which managed-decline vehicles work best in which political economies?
Buildout is more politically popular than retirement. Yet retirement is essential. The archive now has a dedicated decline doctrine note, but still needs sharper comparative work on coal-closure compacts, oil-and-gas production decline, compensation, decommissioning liability, and legal tools.

## 5. How should adaptation be integrated into the same political coalition as mitigation?
This is under-theorized. In practice, durable climate politics may depend on visible protection from current harms, not only promises of future stabilization [S12][S15]. Cooling access may be one of the strongest bridge issues because it is both life-saving and energy-system shaping [S16][S17]. The archive now adds a civil-protection note, but the coalition question remains open.

## 6. Which adaptation package travels best across countries with different state capacities?
The archive now treats adaptation more explicitly as civil protection, resilient infrastructure, and financial preparedness. The unresolved question is which bundle of early warning, land-use discipline, critical-infrastructure hardening, disaster-risk finance, social protection, and managed retreat works best under different political and fiscal constraints [S12][S15][S40][S41].

## 7. What are the most transferable grid-governance templates?
The archive now treats grid congestion as a central failure mode, but the comparative institutional question remains open: which combinations of planning, tariff design, queue reform, flexibility markets, and heat-peak planning travel best across political systems [S14][S17]?

## 8. What is the best policy package for sustainable cooling in hot, rapidly urbanizing countries?
The archive now treats cooling as a hinge variable, but the optimal mix of passive design, appliance standards, tariff design, public cooling access, district cooling, and refrigerant policy remains under-specified [S16][S17].

## 9. What is the least-regret agrifood package that cuts methane and nitrous oxide without worsening hunger or farmer precarity?
The archive now upgrades agrifood systems, but the politically durable mix of livestock methane action, fertilizer reform, rice measures, food-waste rules, and diet shifts remains highly context-dependent [S18][S19][S20][S21][S22].

## 10. What should be the minimal but sufficient global governance for SRM research?
The archive keeps SRM outside the core solution stack, but governance questions remain real if research continues.

## 11. What is the fastest politically durable package for low-embodied-carbon construction?
The archive now treats industrial materials more seriously, but the best transferable package for refurbishment-first policy, embodied-carbon rules, procurement, product definitions, and circular-material systems is still under-specified [S26][S27][S28][S29].

## 12. How should scarce sustainable fuels be allocated across aviation, shipping, long-haul freight, and industry?
The archive now adopts a stronger transport hierarchy, but the best global allocation rule for scarce sustainable fuels and e-fuels remains open. The wrong answer would waste them on easy road uses; the right answer likely depends on real physical alternatives, public value, and verified lifecycle performance [S30][S33][S34][S35].

## 13. What is the fastest high-integrity ecosystem-protection package?
The archive now treats forests, peatlands, wetlands, and blue-carbon systems as part of the core solution, but the optimal package is still under-specified. Key unresolved design questions include: which mixes of enforcement, restoration, finance, hydrological management, and land-use rules travel best across countries; how to distinguish high-integrity protection from offset theatre; and how to protect sinks that climate change is already weakening [S36][S37][S38][S39].

## 14. What is the right boundary between targeted CCS and fossil-delay theatre?
The archive now adds a dedicated removals-boundary note, but a hard governance question remains open: where is CCS genuinely high-value (for cement, some chemicals, waste, BECCS, selected legacy assets, or durable CDR infrastructure), and where does it mainly function as a political device to prolong unabated fossil dependence? The answer likely depends not only on capture rates, but on methane leakage, storage quality, energy penalty, system alternatives, and whether the project displaces faster options [S1][S43][S44][S45][S46].

## 15. What is the fastest socially legitimate mineral strategy for the transition?
The archive now treats critical minerals and batteries more explicitly, but the hardest design question remains open: how to move faster on copper, refining, processing, recycling, and battery manufacturing without normalizing environmental sacrifice zones, corruption, or broken consent. The right answer likely differs by material and geography, and may require different combinations of diversification, substitution, circularity, public stockpiles, producer responsibility, community-benefit agreements, and stronger permitting with stronger protections rather than looser ones [S47][S48][S49][S50].

## 16. What is the fastest politically durable buildings package?
The archive now treats buildings more explicitly, but the implementation bundle is still under-specified. Which mixes of building codes, renovation plans, one-stop shops, green mortgages, social-housing-first retrofits, electricity-price reform, installer training, clean-cooking support, and public-building procurement travel best across different housing systems and state capacities [S28][S51][S52][S53][S54]?

## 17. What is the right power-system mix for reliability without fossil lock-in?
The archive now adds a dedicated clean-power-integration note, but a hard comparative question remains open: in different system geographies, what is the best balance among transmission, distribution upgrades, storage, flexible demand, interconnection, hydro, nuclear, geothermal, and other dispatchable low-emissions resources? The wrong answer either overbuilds costly firm capacity out of habit or underbuilds reliability and loses political legitimacy. The right answer probably depends on load shape, climate, seasonal storage options, existing asset base, and institutional capability [S55][S56][S57][S58].

## 18. Which delivery-state models travel best across political systems?
The archive now adds a dedicated delivery-capacity note, but the comparative institutional question remains open. Which mixes of pre-zoning, one-stop permitting, statutory deadlines, local-authority support, workforce training, community-benefit rules, digital case management, and public participation produce the fastest durable buildout without weakening environmental integrity or democratic legitimacy [S59][S60][S61][S62][S63][S64]?

## 19. What is the minimum viable truth infrastructure for a real transition?
The archive now adds a dedicated truth-infrastructure note, but the implementation bundle is still under-specified. Which combinations of measurement-based methane rules, satellite-alert response protocols, interoperable product definitions, public emissions ledgers, BTR support, chain-of-custody rules, and MRV / liability architecture travel best across different state capacities and market structures [S11][S65][S66][S67][S68]?

## 20. What is the fastest politically durable climate social contract?
The archive now adds a dedicated access-and-affordability note, but the durable implementation bundle remains under-specified. Which combinations of targeted cash support, social tariffs, low-income retrofits, clean-cooking finance, public-transit support, electricity-access programs, and adaptive social protection best preserve legitimacy while still pushing the energy system toward structurally lower emissions and lower bills [S58][S69][S70][S71]?


## 21. What is the fastest durable water-security package under climate stress?
The archive now adds a dedicated water-security note, but the implementation bundle is still under-specified. Which combinations of watershed protection, groundwater governance, irrigation reform, leakage reduction, wastewater reuse, hydropower climate-proofing, floodplain rules, and drought early warning travel best across different state capacities and basin geographies [S72][S73][S74][S75][S76]?


## 22. What is the fairest workable doctrine for retreat from the unprotectable?
The archive now adds a dedicated exposure-discipline note, but the operational design problem remains open. Which combinations of open risk information, mortgage/property disclosure, repeat-loss limits, premium support, buyouts, land swaps, tenant protection, and safer relocation support travel best across different legal systems and housing markets without turning retreat into coercion, silent abandonment, or upward displacement [S81][S82][S83][S84]?

## 23. What is the fastest politically durable climate-health package?
The archive now adds a dedicated health note, but the implementation bundle is still under-specified. Which combinations of fossil-combustion reduction, clean-cooking expansion, air-quality rules, heat-health plans, occupational heat standards, cooling access, hospital resilience, and low-carbon health-system procurement travel best across different income levels and state capacities [S85][S86][S87][S88][S89][S90]?


## 24. What is the highest-leverage way to turn climate-finance headlines into usable project pipelines?
The archive now adds a dedicated EMDE finance note, but the operational conversion problem remains under-specified. Which combinations of guarantee platforms, concessional windows, local-currency instruments, standardized contracts, project-preparation facilities, and public-capacity support most reliably turn NCQG-style headline commitments into disbursing, buildable pipelines without excessive delay, subsidy waste, or debt accumulation [S91][S94][S95][S96]?

## 25. What is the fastest high-wellbeing low-demand package in affluent contexts?
The archive now adds a dedicated demand-shaping note, but the operational bundle remains under-specified. Which combinations of compact urban reform, parking and street-space reallocation, repairability and durability standards, refurbishment-first building policy, food-waste rules, public procurement, product information, and targeted pricing most reliably cut excessive energy and material demand without reading as austerity, class punishment, or lifestyle coercion [S58][S77][S79][S97][S98][S99]?
