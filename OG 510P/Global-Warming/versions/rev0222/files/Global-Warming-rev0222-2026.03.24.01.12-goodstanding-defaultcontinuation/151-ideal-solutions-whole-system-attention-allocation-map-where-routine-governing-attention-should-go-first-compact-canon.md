## The compact attention-allocation map

### 1. Keep truth and weak-signal visibility live enough that attention is pulled by reality rather than by noise
A serious climate programme should not start its ordinary governing cycle with speeches, target refreshes, or public-relations summaries.
It should start with the live scoreboard and truth function:
- which outcome signals are weakening
- which service signals are slipping
- which bottlenecks are growing
- which integrity signals suggest paper progress outrunning physical progress [S133][S138][S141].

Without that first cut, scarce attention gets captured by the loudest lobby, the freshest crisis theatre, or the most flattering metric.

### 2. Put most discretionary attention on the top live bottleneck or leverage point, not on a long balanced menu
Once the scoreboard is visible, routine governing attention should not be spread evenly across every worthy topic.
It should concentrate on the top live constraint or push point whose release makes other progress easier:
- a grid or permitting bottleneck
- a payment or finance blockage
- a default or standards gap at a control point
- a dirty path that is still being quietly subsidized or protected [S124][S132][S138][S139][S140].

Equal attention across everything usually means enough attention for nothing.
The routine rule is: **one top release, then the next**.

### 3. Keep protection and continuity visible enough that buildout success is not bought with silent loss
A serious programme should not let buildout and decarbonization headlines crowd protection off the operating table.
Routine governing attention should always keep a visible share for:
- exposed people and places
- fragile lifelines and public-health risk
- affordability stress
- continuity through heat, flood, drought, outage, and insurance failure [S123][S124][S131][S134].

This is not a side programme.
It is part of what makes the climate programme legitimate enough to keep moving.

### 4. Keep readiness and detour discipline live before stress forces improvisation
Routine attention should not wait for a reliability scare, disaster, backlash event, or administrative failure before checking whether readiness is still real.
A serious programme keeps attention on:
- readiness status
- stale or disputed readiness claims
- rehearsals and assurance
- tripwires that may be getting close
- and whether any current detours are still narrow, explicit, and on an exit clock [S102][S103][S122][S123].

The practical rule is simple:
**treat readiness as ordinary maintenance, not as an emergency specialty.**

### 5. Move any material weak signal through the full correction-and-hardening cycle
When routine attention finds a material weak signal, the programme should not leave it sitting in the dashboard.
It should move it into the correction cycle:
- diagnose the likely weak layer
- launch the smallest fitting correction
- verify
- run a second pass if needed
- bank gains if the move works
- spread the better pattern
- retire the inferior path
- and keep the inferior path from quietly returning [S138][S142][S143][S144][S145][S146][S147][S148][S149][S150].

This prevents two common failures at once:
- dashboards that report weakness without correction
- and corrections that happen without hardening into routine.
