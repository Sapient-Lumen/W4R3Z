# 115 — Ideal Solutions: Five Compensating Duties When Readiness Is Partial

## Claim

The archive already has:
- a readiness note (`111`)
- a live-discipline note (`112`)
- a rehearsal-discipline note (`113`)
- a readiness-assurance note (`114`)
- a burden-of-proof note for dirty delay, fallback, or exception (`108`)
- and a return-to-routine note (`109`)

What it still lacked was the shortest **partial-readiness note** for the question:

**If a serious climate state checks honestly and finds that readiness is incomplete, what should it do in the meantime before those gaps are closed?**

That gap matters because many systems fail exactly here.
They do the honest thing once by admitting that readiness is not yet good enough.
But then they do the dangerous thing next: they keep operating as if nothing follows from that admission.
The readiness claim is downgraded on paper while the workload, risk appetite, fallback scope, and public promise remain unchanged.
That is not honesty.
It is honesty without compensation.

A compact archive needed one short bridge between **readiness assurance** and **live repair under degraded conditions**.
This note is a **compression note**, not a new doctrinal branch.
Its job is to reduce partial readiness to five compensating duties that keep a serious climate state from pretending that known weakness is harmless until the repair path catches up [S34][S55][S83][S102][S103][S108][S109][S111][S112][S113][S114][S145][S147].

## Fast rule

**If readiness is partial, do not merely say so: de-rate the claim, narrow the mission to what can be done safely, raise protection and escalation sensitivity, concentrate repair on the highest-consequence gaps, and borrow support until readiness is restored.**

## Five compensating duties when readiness is partial

### 1. De-rate the readiness claim and the promise that rests on it
A serious climate state should not convert a downgraded readiness finding into unchanged public assurance.
If the system is only partly ready, the claim of what it can safely guarantee should shrink to the portion that evidence still supports.

What this usually means:
- readiness status is reported in bounded levels rather than as a binary badge
- public promises, internal targets, and manager attestations are narrowed to the capability that is still evidenced
- marketing language, political comfort language, and legacy baseline assumptions do not outrun the downgraded state [S55][S83][S102][S111][S114][S145]

Why it matters:
- overstated capability is itself a risk multiplier
- the first compensation for partial readiness is honest de-rating, not cheerful reinterpretation

### 2. Narrow discretionary complexity and protect the core first
When readiness is partial, the system should simplify operations and concentrate on protecting the most important services, users, and assets rather than continuing every optional or fragile activity at full stretch.

What this usually means:
- postpone nonessential complexity, bespoke pilots, fragile handoffs, or optional dependencies until readiness recovers
- preserve bandwidth for critical services, exposed users, and the clean mainline with the highest consequence of failure
- prefer the simpler safe path over the more ambitious but coordination-heavy path while weakness remains open [S39][S52][S83][S103][S111][S112][S147]

Why it matters:
- a partly ready system is most likely to break at the edges, joins, and optional complexities
- de-complexing early prevents a manageable weakness from cascading into a larger incident

### 3. Tighten protection posture and tripwire sensitivity rather than relaxing them
A serious climate state should respond to partial readiness by becoming easier to alarm and quicker to protect, not by waiting longer for proof that harm is already underway.

What this usually means:
- lower tolerance for backlog growth, service failure, unsafe fallback drift, or unprotected exposure while readiness is below standard
- increase monitoring frequency, review cadence, and management attention on the weak points
- move exposed users and critical services closer to the front of the protection queue until readiness is repaired [S55][S83][S102][S103][S104][S111][S114][S147]

Why it matters:
- weak readiness and slow escalation are a dangerous combination
- when capability is thinner, caution should become tighter, not looser

### 4. Put the highest-consequence readiness gaps on a live repair clock
A serious climate state should not treat all readiness defects equally.
It should force the most dangerous missing capability onto a visible repair path with owners, deadlines, temporary containment, and closure tests.

What this usually means:
- rank defects by consequence for protection, truth, repair speed, clean-mainline continuity, and dirty-fallback risk
- put the top gaps on a short review clock with visible blockers and escalation rights if closure stalls
- treat unresolved high-consequence weaknesses as governance items, not only technical housekeeping [S34][S55][S83][S103][S110][S113][S114][S145]

Why it matters:
- partial readiness becomes chronic when every gap is logged but no gap is forced to closure
- compensation without repair is just managed drift

### 5. Borrow support, redundancy, or mutual aid until the system can carry itself
If a serious climate state knows it is partly ready, it should import reinforcement from stronger actors, neighboring jurisdictions, reserve providers, mutual-aid arrangements, or external challenge functions rather than pretending isolated self-sufficiency.

What this usually means:
- use reserve contracts, external support teams, mutual-aid agreements, backup data paths, or third-party verification where they materially reduce exposure
- prefer temporary reinforcement that preserves the cleaner mainline over dirty improvisation that merely hides the weakness
- make the borrowed support visibly temporary and tie it to the live repair path rather than letting it become a permanent substitute for capability [S39][S73][S83][S103][S111][S112][S114][S147]

Why it matters:
- the serious question is not whether the system can look self-contained; it is whether it can protect people and keep the programme on track
- borrowed resilience is often safer than fake autonomy

## Minimal partial-readiness posture

The smallest serious operating posture when readiness is known to be partial is usually:

1. **downgrade the claim**
2. **simplify to the core mission**
3. **tighten protection and tripwire sensitivity**
4. **force the biggest gaps onto a live repair clock**
5. **borrow support until readiness recovers** [S34][S55][S83][S103][S111][S112][S113][S114][S147]

If these compensations are absent, then the system is not merely partly ready.
It is partly ready **and still pretending to operate at full trust**.
