# 62 — Ideal Solutions by Time: Three Lanes — Emergency, Replacement, Residual

## Claim

The archive already knows **what** belongs in the ideal portfolio.
It now needs the shortest rule for **when different kinds of action belong in that portfolio**.

Not every good climate measure sits in the same temporal lane.
Some actions are valuable because they cut warming pressure or exposure **fast**.
Some are valuable because they **replace the underlying stock and infrastructure** that keep emissions recurring.
A smaller set is valuable because it helps clean up the **hard residuals** that remain after the first two jobs are done.

So the archive should add a simple timing rule:

**ideal solutions should be built as a three-lane package:**
1. an **emergency lane** that cuts near-term forcing, waste, and exposure quickly,
2. a **replacement lane** that builds the new default system for the 2030s and beyond, and
3. a **residual lane** reserved for the hardest remaining emissions and cleanup tasks [S2][S10][S11][S42][S100][S101][S102].

## The timing rule

A climate portfolio becomes worse when it inverts the lanes.
The archive should therefore prefer portfolios that obey all three of the following disciplines at once:

### 1. Run the emergency lane immediately
The world is not choosing from a calm baseline.
The remaining 1.5°C carbon budget is now extremely small, methane still offers one of the fastest available warming gains, and unmanaged heat, flood, drought, and fire exposure are already damaging people now [S10][S11][S15][S16][S101].

That means ideal solutions should front-load measures whose physical effects arrive early, especially when they also lower bills, peak load, fuel use, or direct exposure.

Typical emergency-lane measures include:
- methane leak detection and repair, venting and flaring control, and fast waste / wastewater action [S8][S11][S66][S67][S133]
- coal-retirement preparation and no-new-lock-in moves where they stop long future damage before it is baked in [S9][S23][S24][S25]
- efficiency, passive cooling, strong appliance standards, and other measures that cut waste faster than new supply can be built [S16][S17][S53][S58][S104][S106]
- anti-deforestation, peatland and wetland protection, and other avoidable land-use damage prevention [S36][S37][S38][S39]
- heat plans, early warnings, public cooling access, and protection of highly exposed people while mitigation is still incomplete [S12][S15][S16][S17][S85][S104]

House rule: **do first what stops the bleeding.**

### 2. Build the replacement lane in parallel
The emergency lane matters, but it cannot by itself solve the stock problem.
The world still needs a different power system, different buildings, different transport defaults, different industrial markets, and stronger public delivery institutions.

So ideal solutions must simultaneously build the replacement layer that makes high emissions and high exposure stop recurring.
This includes:
- grids, transmission, distribution, queue reform, storage, flexibility, and selected clean firmness [S4][S5][S6][S14][S55][S56][S57]
- retrofit institutions, heat pumps, efficient new construction, and better envelopes as durable building defaults [S28][S51][S52][S53][S54][S162]
- EVs, charging, zero-emission bus and fleet procurement, rail and logistics improvements, and compact urban form where it fits [S30][S31][S32][S77][S78][S79][S80]
- low-emissions steel and cement market formation, standards, and procurement [S26][S27][S28][S29]
- EMDE finance, guarantees, local-currency capacity, standards, workforce, and state capability so deployment can actually travel [S13][S59][S60][S61][S62][S63][S64][S91][S92][S93][S94][S95][S96]

House rule: **while stopping today’s damage, build the system that makes tomorrow’s damage less likely to recur.**

### 3. Reserve the residual lane for the hard remainder
Some climate work genuinely belongs later or in smaller volumes.
That does not make it unimportant.
It means it should not be allowed to displace the first two lanes.

The residual lane includes:
- scarce low-emissions fuels for aviation, shipping, selected industrial heat, and only the hardest freight uses [S33][S34][S35][S112][S165][S166][S167]
- targeted CCS where process emissions or durable storage logic are real, not as a blanket excuse for preserving large fossil systems [S43][S44][S45][S46]
- high-integrity carbon removal for residual emissions and atmospheric cleanup that remains after strong direct abatement, with strict attention to scale, durability, land, water, and energy limits [S1][S42][S100][S101]

House rule: **cleanup is for the remainder, not a licence to delay the replacement.**

## What lane inversion looks like

The archive should demote strategies that scramble the order.
The main inversion errors are now clear.

### 1. Residual-first planning
This is the mistake of treating removals, offsets, hydrogen, e-fuels, or CCS as the main story before waste reduction, electrification, fossil decline, and clean-power buildout have done most of the work [S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S100][S101].

### 2. Replacement-without-emergency planning
This is the mistake of thinking only in terms of 2035–2050 backbone assets while underweighting methane, cooling efficiency, heat protection, ecosystem protection, and other moves whose benefits start before the backbone fully lands [S11][S15][S16][S17][S36][S37][S38][S39][S104].

### 3. Emergency-without-replacement planning
This is the mistake of doing only short-latency protection and efficiency while failing to build the grids, institutions, industrial standards, and financing layers needed for a durable low-emissions system [S4][S6][S13][S14][S59][S60][S91][S92][S96].

## What this changes in practice

This timing rule sharpens the archive’s answer to “what would ideal solutions be?”

Ideal solutions are **not** merely the measures with the biggest modeled tonnes by 2050.
They are the measures that, taken together, do three jobs at once:
- **reduce near-term harm and forcing quickly**,
- **replace the underlying infrastructure and stock that would otherwise keep producing damage**, and
- **keep the expensive cleanup tools in a disciplined residual role**.

That is the portfolio most consistent with a world where the carbon budget is tight, exposure is already rising, infrastructure takes time, and magical late repair remains a bad governing assumption [S10][S12][S42][S100][S101][S102].

In shorter form:

> **first stop the bleeding, then build the replacement, then clean up the remainder — and do not invert the order.**
