# 222 — Ideal Solutions: The Clean Transition Should Arrive Through Standing Channels People Already Use, Not Ad Hoc Discovery

## Claim

The archive already says the clean path should be easy (`213`), arrive as a coherent offer rather than a scavenger hunt (`214`), keep burden off the weaker party (`215`), hold together as one accountable path (`216`), and ask most ordinary users to choose a clean result rather than manage a technology stack (`221`).
One more compact front-door rule belongs in the canon:

**a serious climate programme should usually arrive through standing channels people already use or must already touch, not rely on ad hoc discovery, lucky timing, or expert programme hunting.**

In plain terms:
- the cleaner path should often come through the utility bill, the landlord or social-housing manager, the municipal permit or rebuild desk, the fleet or payroll system, the school / hospital / public-procurement route, the farm cooperative or extension channel, the insurer / recovery pathway after loss, or another standing channel already connected to the relevant service, asset, or life event [S71][S76][S77][S80][S81][S118][S119][S137][S141][S143]
- and weaker actors should not have to discover from scratch which office, contractor, subsidy, loan, tariff, technical standard, and proof sequence might let them reach a cleaner state [S69][S71][S118][S119][S137][S141][S143]

So the shortest rule is:

**bring the clean path through standing channels that already have cadence, records, or duty — not through ad hoc discovery.**

## The compact canon

### 1. Discovery burden is itself a selection mechanism
A programme can look generous on paper and still underserve the people or places it claims to help if access depends on who hears about it, understands it, trusts it, can compare it, and can coordinate it in time.
Administrative complexity, fragmented offers, and weak local delivery already sort uptake by time, literacy, credit, tenure, and confidence [S69][S71][S118][S119][S137][S141][S143].

House rule: **do not treat discovery as free. Discovery burden is part of the burden shape.**

### 2. Standing channels already carry cadence, records, or duty
Some channels already have recurring contact, existing data, service obligations, or legitimate reasons to touch the relevant asset or user.
That makes them stronger entry points than a fresh search from zero.
Utilities already bill and connect service; landlords and social-housing actors already manage buildings; municipalities already touch permits, codes, and recovery; insurers and recovery systems already touch post-loss repair; public buyers already touch fleets, schools, hospitals, and civic assets; payroll, benefits, or social-protection systems may already reach targeted households and workers [S71][S76][S77][S80][S81][S118][S119][S141][S143].

House rule: **prefer channels that already have a legitimate relationship to the service, asset, or life event in question.**

### 3. Standing channels are especially strong at trigger moments
The archive already values turnover-aware trigger points (`169`).
That logic becomes more usable when the clean offer arrives through the channel already present at the trigger: lease renewal, account opening, major appliance replacement, permit submission, fleet refresh, post-disaster rebuild, tariff change, or public procurement cycle [S53][S77][S80][S81][S118][S119][S137][S143].

House rule: **put the clean offer where the decision is already happening.**

### 4. Not every existing channel is a good channel
A channel is not good merely because it exists.
The archive should prefer channels that have at least some combination of legitimacy, duty to serve, low exclusion risk, usable records, recourse, and the ability to carry a standard offer without hidden predation.
Bad channels can reproduce cream-skimming, captive sales pressure, misinformation, or privacy abuse [S69][S71][S80][S81][S118][S137][S141][S143].

House rule: **standing channel does not mean unchecked intermediary. Good channels need rules, recourse, and bounded powers.**

### 5. The point is not just outreach; it is joined delivery
A good standing channel should not merely advertise a scheme.
It should be able to invite, prefill, route, or hand off into a joined clean path with less repeated proof, less re-shopping, and less user-side coordination.
That is what lets one informed yes become plausible in real life instead of remaining a slogan [S118][S119][S137][S141][S143][S144].

House rule: **use the channel to carry the user into the path, not only to tell the user that the path exists somewhere else.**

### 6. When no good standing channel exists, build assisted discovery rather than pretending the problem away
Some groups or assets will not sit inside a strong existing channel.
Informal settlements, weak-tenure cases, rural edge cases, fragmented small-enterprise sectors, and politically neglected communities often need outreach, intermediaries, or public navigators.
But the archive should still treat that as a design problem to solve, not as permission to fall back to “go find your own programme” [S69][S71][S77][S80][S118][S137][S141][S143].

House rule: **where standing channels are weak, create assisted channels rather than abandoning the user to discovery.**

## Compression rule

**Ideal climate solutions should usually arrive through standing channels that people already use or must already touch — utilities, landlords, municipal service and rebuild desks, public buyers, payroll or benefit systems, recovery pathways, and similar channels with real cadence or duty — rather than relying on ad hoc discovery. The stronger pathway is the one that turns discovery from a private burden into upstream programme design.**
