## Fast rule

**If you want to know whether a climate program is actually working, watch six front-door scorelines: gross emissions falling, methane falling, dirty defaults retiring or being denied new lock-in, clean service connecting and carrying load, household exposure and energy burden falling, and the main delivery or truth bottlenecks loosening. Treat spending, targets, announcements, and pipeline volume as supporting context rather than primary proof** [S10][S11][S14][S16][S17][S59][S65][S69][S70][S103][S104][S113][S123][S136].

## The six front-door scorelines

### 1. Gross emissions are falling
The first scoreline is still physical.
A serious program should show gross emissions moving down at meaningful speed, not only cleaner investment rising alongside stubborn combustion.
If the gross line is flat or weak, the rest of the dashboard should not be allowed to flatter the program [S10][S14][S59][S65][S103][S136].

### 2. Methane is falling fast enough to matter
Methane belongs near the top of the front-door dashboard because it is one of the fastest available ways to slow near-term warming.
A program that talks like a climate program but leaves super-emitters, venting, flaring, waste methane, and weak response discipline largely untouched is not yet working hard enough on the atmosphere [S8][S11][S65][S66][S67][S103][S133].

### 3. Dirty defaults are shrinking, retiring, or being denied another cycle
The third scoreline is whether the inferior path is actually losing ground.
Look for fossil retirements, blocked lock-in, repaired leaks, stricter dirty-default rules, and fewer opportunities for the old system to reproduce itself at replacement moments.
If clean build keeps rising while the dirty default stays institutionally safe, the transition is still shallow [S14][S24][S59][S65][S103][S136].

### 4. Clean service is connecting and carrying real load
The fourth scoreline is whether the cleaner system is not only proposed but connected, installed, used, and carrying ordinary demand.
Watch connected clean power, functioning grids, installed efficient cooling and clean heat, retrofits that actually stick, and electrified transport or industrial shifts that are visibly taking work away from combustion rather than waiting in the pipeline [S14][S16][S17][S53][S55][S56][S57][S58][S65][S103][S106].

### 5. People are safer, less exposed, and less burdened in ordinary life
A serious climate program should improve lived conditions, not only the tonnes ledger.
Watch whether bills, fuel-price exposure, dangerous heat, repeat-loss exposure, outage risk, smoke exposure, or water stress are falling for the intended population.
If households experience the program mainly as hassle, higher fragility, or moral instruction, the working scoreline is weaker than the official story [S16][S17][S40][S41][S69][S70][S71][S85][S90][S104].

### 6. The main bottlenecks and truth failures are loosening rather than compounding
The last front-door scoreline is whether the program is getting easier to deliver and easier to trust.
Look for shorter queues, faster lawful permitting, stronger workforce and maintenance capacity, cheaper clean finance where capital is scarce, faster methane-response discipline, auditable claims, and real correction when performance weakens.
If the bottlenecks harden or the truth layer softens, the next wave will stall even if the first wave looked strong on paper [S11][S13][S14][S59][S65][S66][S67][S91][S96][S103][S113][S123][S136].

## What should not lead the dashboard

Do **not** let these lead the front-door verdict by themselves:
- money announced
- money spent without outcome proof
- strategy documents
- target adoption counts
- pipeline size not yet connected
- labels, credits, or disclosure coverage without strong physical linkage

These can still matter.
But they matter mostly as supporting explanations for movement in the six scorelines above [S59][S65][S113][S123][S136].
