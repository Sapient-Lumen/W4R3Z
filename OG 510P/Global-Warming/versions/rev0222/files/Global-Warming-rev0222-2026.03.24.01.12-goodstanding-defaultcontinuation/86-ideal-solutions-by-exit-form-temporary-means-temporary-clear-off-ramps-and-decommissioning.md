# 86 — Ideal Solutions by Exit Form: Temporary Means Temporary, with Clear Off-Ramps and Decommissioning

## Claim

The archive already says that managed fossil decline requires retirement, cleanup, and legal clarity over who pays [S9][S23][S24][S121][S135][S136].
It already says that bad-day fallback should be temporary and designed to return fast to the clean path rather than hardening into sticky dirty relapse [S16][S17][S55][S56][S58][S76][S81][S82][S83][S84][S126][S127][S128][S143][S144].
And it already says that hardship, safety, reliability, and transition exceptions should be narrow, visible, reviewable, and sunset-bound rather than becoming a second operating system [S9][S23][S24][S40][S59][S64][S68][S80][S123][S129][S130][S131][S132][S143][S146][S147].

One more compact rule now belongs in the canon:

**ideal climate solutions should have a good exit form. Anything justified as temporary — an emergency fossil backstop, a transition subsidy, a hardship waiver, a dirty standby asset, an interim billing fix, or a grace-period technical exception — should have a named owner, visible trigger conditions, a closure test, and a practical off-ramp back to the main clean path. Temporary should mean temporary in operations, not just in rhetoric.**

In plain terms, many weak climate systems do not fail because they lack a mainline clean policy.
They fail because their temporary bridge, waiver, standby system, or emergency patch never really leaves.
The archive therefore needs the shortest rule for **ending** transitional dirt, not only for permitting it.

So the archive’s shortest exit-form rule is:

**when you create a temporary dirty allowance or bridge, design the end state at the same time: who closes it, what conditions end it, who pays decommissioning or cleanup, and how users or assets rejoin the normal clean path.** [S9][S23][S24][S40][S55][S56][S58][S76][S84][S85][S121][S123][S135][S136][S137][S143][S146][S147]

## Why this belongs in the canon

### 1. “Temporary” is one of the most abused words in transition politics

Climate policy often creates interim supports or exceptions for understandable reasons: reliability concerns, supply shortages, household hardship, industrial adjustment, disaster recovery, or national-security stress [S9][S23][S24][S40][S55][S56][S58][S76][S84][S85][S143][S146][S147].
But if the archive only says that such measures may be temporary, and not **how they actually terminate**, then the canon leaves a large failure mode underdescribed.

The missing question is simple:

**what makes the temporary thing end in practice?**

### 2. Exit form is different from exception form and fallback form

`85-ideal-solutions-by-exception-form-narrow-carve-outs-visible-sunsets-and-no-permanent-loophole-path.md` asks whether carve-outs stay narrow, visible, and sunset-bound.
`84-ideal-solutions-by-fallback-form-fail-soft-use-the-least-damaging-backup-and-return-fast.md` asks what happens on the bad day and how quickly the system returns to the clean path.
`09-managed-fossil-decline.md` asks for actual retirement, cleanup, and who pays.

This note covers the joining problem among them:

**when a temporary dirty bridge, waiver, standby mode, or transition support is created, does the system already contain the instructions for ending it?** [S9][S23][S24][S55][S56][S58][S76][S84][S85][S121][S135][S136][S143][S146][S147]

### 3. Good exit form protects credibility

A policy can be technically justified at the moment it is introduced and still become politically or climatically corrosive if it lingers by inertia.
That is how emergency coal returns, diesel backup, price shields, fossil subsidies, grandfathered exemptions, interim permitting shortcuts, or “temporary” dirty operating rules become the real baseline [S9][S23][S24][S40][S55][S56][S58][S64][S68][S76][S84][S85][S143][S146][S147].

So the archive needs a sharper rule:

**do not judge a temporary measure only by its entry logic; judge it by its exit logic too.**

### 4. Exit form belongs to justice as well as discipline

Bad exit design does not only raise emissions.
It often leaves weaker users trapped in interim arrangements, landlords or utilities sitting on extended waivers, and publics holding cleanup or decommissioning liabilities that private actors created [S23][S24][S68][S69][S70][S121][S123][S135][S136][S137][S143].

So exit form is not just about neat administration.
It is about whether the burdens of ending the dirty bridge are pushed onto the weaker actor, future publics, or nobody in particular.

## The archive’s exit-form tests

### 1. Named-owner test
Who is explicitly responsible for closing the temporary regime, bridge asset, or waiver rather than merely benefiting from its continuation? [S23][S24][S68][S121][S123][S135][S136]

### 2. Trigger test
What observable condition starts the exit — substitute capacity online, reliability restored, repair completed, debt stabilised, supply bottleneck resolved, season ended, or contract milestone met — rather than leaving closure to vague future discretion? [S9][S23][S24][S55][S56][S76][S84][S85][S143][S146][S147]

### 3. Sunset-and-ratchet test
Does the temporary arrangement face a real sunset, glidepath, or ratchet toward closure, or is it merely reapproved in rolling emergencies? [S9][S23][S24][S40][S45][S55][S56][S84][S85][S143][S146][S147]

### 4. Return-path test
Once the temporary measure ends, is there a practical route back to the main clean pathway for users, institutions, or assets, or does closure simply drop them into disorder? [S56][S58][S69][S76][S80][S84][S85][S118][S119][S137][S143]

### 5. Decommissioning-and-cleanup test
If the temporary measure uses dirty physical assets or creates long-tail obligations, who pays for retirement, remediation, monitoring, or safe disposition? [S23][S24][S68][S121][S123][S135][S136]

### 6. Visibility test
Can the public or the relevant regulator see which temporary measures remain active, for how long, at what cost, and under what closure conditions, or do they disappear into waiver fog? [S40][S65][S66][S67][S68][S80][S118][S119][S121][S123][S129][S130][S131][S132][S143]

## What this rule promotes

Promote:
- temporary measures that are introduced together with their exit triggers, sunset logic, and responsible owner [S9][S23][S24][S40][S55][S56][S84][S85][S143][S146][S147]
- emergency or bridge assets whose retirement, cleanup, and return-to-normal obligations are funded and assigned at the start rather than left for later publics [S23][S24][S68][S121][S123][S135][S136]
- policy designs where repeated reliance on a temporary bridge triggers repair of the mainline system rather than habitual renewal of the bridge itself [S55][S56][S58][S76][S84][S85][S143]
- visible off-ramp rules that make temporary dirt harder to quietly normalize [S40][S65][S66][S67][S68][S80][S118][S119][S123][S129][S130][S131][S132][S143]

## What this rule demotes

Demote:
- “temporary” fossil supports, waivers, or dirty backup assets with no credible closure owner, no trigger, and no funded end-of-life plan [S9][S23][S24][S68][S121][S123][S135][S136][S143][S146][S147]
- transition bridges that are politically easier to renew than to close [S40][S55][S56][S58][S64][S68][S84][S85][S143][S146][S147]
- policy designs that use sunset language symbolically while leaving actual continuation on autopilot [S9][S23][S24][S40][S45][S84][S85][S146][S147]
- any system that treats decommissioning, remediation, or post-use liability as somebody else’s problem later [S23][S24][S68][S121][S123][S135][S136]

## One-paragraph compression

**Ideal climate solutions should have good exit form. Anything justified as temporary — waivers, backstops, transition subsidies, dirty standby assets, grace periods, emergency operating modes, or interim fixes — should come with a named owner, visible closure triggers, a real sunset or ratchet, a practical route back to the main clean path, and funded decommissioning or cleanup where relevant. A climate system is less ideal if its temporary dirt is easy to start but institutionally hard to end** [S9][S23][S24][S40][S55][S56][S58][S68][S76][S80][S84][S85][S121][S123][S135][S136][S137][S143][S146][S147].
