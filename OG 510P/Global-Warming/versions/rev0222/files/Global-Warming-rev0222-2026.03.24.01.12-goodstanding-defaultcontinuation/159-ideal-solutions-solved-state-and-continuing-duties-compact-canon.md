## Fast rule

**Global warming is not solved by a pledge, a target year, or a narrow rich-country success story. It is solved only when gross emissions have been driven down close to a shrinking residual floor; dirty defaults are being retired faster than they return; methane, land-use, and ecosystem losses are under real control; exposure and repeat-loss are falling through protection and land discipline; the cleaner path is affordable and financeable across rich and poorer countries alike; and the remaining residuals are small enough to be covered only by bounded, high-integrity removals inside a permanent anti-regression regime** [S1][S11][S12][S13][S36][S37][S38][S39][S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S91][S92][S93][S95][S96][S100][S101][S141][S143].

## The compact canon

### 1. Treat the solved state as a durable system condition, not as a slogan or date label
The archive should not confuse “we adopted a target,” “we passed a package,” or even “we had several good years” with having solved the problem.
A solved state is harder than that.
It means the cleaner system has become normal enough, cheap enough, reliable enough, and politically durable enough that the dirty system is no longer the background default waiting to return under stress [S58][S69][S91][S96][S100][S101][S141][S143].

House rule: **do not call it solved while the old dirty baseline can easily reassert itself through normal capital cycles, price shocks, weak households, or routine politics.**

### 2. The mitigation side is solved only when gross emissions are low, still falling where they should, and hard residuals are clearly bounded
The archive does not treat the problem as solved merely because some net accounting identity is available.
It treats it as solved only when the physical system has changed: clean electricity is dominant and deliverable, buildings and mobility mostly run on cleaner defaults, industrial decarbonization is moving through the real hard cases, and new fossil lock-in is exceptional rather than routine [S1][S9][S11][S23][S24][S25][S55][S56][S100][S101][S103][S106].

At that point, most remaining emissions should be recognizably residual: hard, narrow, declining, and governed under proof-heavy rules rather than serving as cover for easy-sector delay [S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S100][S101].

House rule: **the solved-state test is gross physical decline first, bounded residuals second, and only then residual-balancing tools.**

### 3. Non-CO2 forcing, ecosystem loss, and exposure must also be on the right side of the curve
A climate programme has not solved global warming in the archive’s sense if it decarbonizes electricity and vehicles while leaving methane leakage, nitrous-oxide pressure, deforestation, peatland loss, and repeated household exposure to heat, flood, fire, and drought on autopilot [S11][S12][S15][S19][S36][S37][S38][S39][S81][S82][S83][S84].

The solved state therefore includes:
- methane and other fast forcing sources under measured control,
- major carbon-rich ecosystems better protected and restored rather than still being liquidated,
- and exposure trends that are bending downward because settlements, infrastructure, health systems, water systems, and civil protection have become safer in practice [S11][S12][S15][S36][S37][S38][S39][S40][S41][S81][S82][S83][S84].

House rule: **it is not solved if tonnes fall while repeat-loss, dirty air, deadly heat, or ecosystem collapse keep rising.**

### 4. The solved state must travel beyond rich, low-capital-cost jurisdictions
The archive rejects the fantasy that a handful of rich countries can solve the problem alone and the rest of the world can be treated as a later diffusion story.
If cleaner infrastructure remains structurally more expensive in much of the world because of capital costs, debt stress, weak access, or thin institutional support, then the solution is still incomplete [S13][S91][S92][S93][S94][S95][S96][S141][S142][S143].

A real solved state therefore requires that the clean path is financeable and governable across very different country conditions, with service gains visible enough that the programme remains legitimate rather than depending on permanent elite patience [S13][S58][S69][S70][S71][S91][S95][S96][S141][S142][S143].

House rule: **no global solved-state claim while the clean option remains structurally unaffordable or institutionally unreachable across large parts of the world.**

### 5. Removals belong inside the solved state only as a bounded residuals tool
The archive does not deny a role for carbon removal.
It denies that removal should define success before the physical economy has been transformed.
Inside a real solved state, removals should be doing a narrow job: balancing residual emissions that remain after aggressive gross cuts, under strong MRV, durability, and liability rules [S1][S42][S43][S44][S45][S46].

That means the solved state is incompatible with:
- large open-ended claims that future removals will compensate for slow present decline,
- weak storage or weak durability assumptions,
- or residual categories that keep expanding because easy sectors did not decarbonize when they could have [S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S100][S101].

House rule: **small, hard residuals may remain; a large lazy residual economy means the problem is not solved.**

### 6. After the solved state, climate governance becomes permanent maintenance, anti-regression, and fair replacement work
Even after the system crosses into a real solved state, the archive does not picture a final victory parade followed by neglect.
It pictures a shift in governing mode.
The work becomes more like public health, utility stewardship, land management, and infrastructure maintenance: keep systems performing, replace aging assets cleanly, watch for regression, respond to new exposure, and keep dirty fallback narrow, temporary, and costly enough that relapse does not become routine [S37][S40][S41][S81][S82][S83][S84][S108][S121][S122][S143].

So “solved” still includes continuing duties:
- operate and maintain the cleaner backbone,
- retire inferior paths as better defaults prove durable,
- prevent waiver drift, subsidy relapse, and dirty stress fallback,
- keep adaptation and civil protection live for locked-in warming,
- and keep international finance and standards architecture from backsliding [S12][S13][S37][S81][S82][S83][S84][S91][S96][S108][S121][S122][S143].

House rule: **solved means maintenance mode, not amnesia.**

## Tie-breaker rule

If there is a dispute about whether the archive should treat the problem as “solved enough,” prefer the more conservative answer unless you can say yes to most of the following at once:
- are gross emissions low and clearly headed toward a small residual floor?
- are new dirty defaults exceptional rather than normal?
- are methane, ecosystems, and repeat-loss exposure on controlled downward paths?
- is the cleaner path affordable and financeable far beyond the richest jurisdictions?
- are residuals truly hard and matched only by bounded high-integrity removals?
- and could the system survive political turnover or a stress event without snapping back to the dirty baseline? [S1][S11][S12][S13][S36][S37][S38][S39][S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S81][S82][S83][S84][S91][S96][S100][S101][S141][S143]

## Compression rule

**The archive should treat global warming as solved only when the cleaner system has become the durable default: gross emissions are near a shrinking residual floor, dirty relapse is hard, methane and ecosystem damage are under real control, exposure is falling, the transition is financeable and politically durable across rich and poorer countries alike, removals are bounded to hard residuals, and the remaining work is governed as permanent maintenance and anti-regression rather than as a deferred excuse for unfinished transition.**
