## Fast rule

**The ideal solution to global warming is not a silver bullet but a governed clean-development system with six joined layers: cut gross emissions fast; build abundant clean service systems and retire dirty defaults; protect people and places against already-arriving damage; align burdens, duties, prices, and finance so the cleaner path becomes the normal path; keep claims honest through measurement, correction, and proof; and spread the package across borders through cheaper capital, standards, technology diffusion, and practical coordination** [S100][S101][S102][S103][S104][S106][S108][S112][S113][S115][S117][S120][S133][S141][S143].

## The compact canon

### 1. The first layer is fast gross-emissions decline, not elegant late neutrality
The archive’s starting point is physical: the ideal solution cuts gross emissions early, limits overshoot, lowers near-term forcing where possible, and does not treat delayed action as acceptable just because a future net claim can be written down [S100][S101][S105][S133].
That is why methane control, efficiency, electrification, fossil decline, and avoided lock-in usually outrank slower pathways that promise later cleanup.

House rule: **the ideal solution begins by making the atmospheric problem smaller quickly in the real world.**

### 2. The second layer is abundant clean service systems, not merely cleaner gadgets
The archive’s programme shape is about delivering cooling, heat, mobility, light, shelter, industrial output, and food with much lower recurring emissions and lower system overhead.
That usually means abundant clean electricity, stronger grids, efficiency first, direct electrification where practical, better buildings, better urban and transport systems, cleaner industrial processes, methane and agrifood action, and serious ecosystem protection rather than trying to substitute scarce clean molecules into every easy use [S100][S103][S104][S106][S111][S120][S133][S168][S169][S170][S171].

House rule: **build cleaner service systems, not just a shopping list of cleaner devices.**

### 3. The third layer is protection against harms already arriving
Even perfect mitigation from now on would not cancel the warming already in the system.
So the ideal solution also includes protection: heat safety, cooling access, early warnings, resilient infrastructure, flood and fire preparation, water and ecosystem protection, and adaptation as a standing civil-protection duty rather than a side charity for later [S104][S108][S109][S110][S120][S128][S156][S164][S175].

House rule: **solve both the cause and the already-arriving consequences.**

### 4. The fourth layer is political economy, burden allocation, and institutional form
A climate package is not ideal if it works only for rich early adopters, relies on expensive capital where cheaper public or regulated capital is available, or asks weaker actors to carry search, proof, cash, coordination, and risk all at once.
Serious climate delivery therefore needs public or tightly regulated backbones where the task is a coordination and duty-to-serve problem, competitive edges where contestability actually helps, progressive burden sharing, lower cost of capital, visible gains, and real transition terms for workers, regions, and host communities [S102][S112][S117][S118][S122][S129][S130][S131][S137][S141][S143][S145][S147][S153].

House rule: **the cleaner path must become the easier, fairer, and more financeable path for the decisive actors.**

### 5. The fifth layer is truth, proof, correction, and anti-fraud discipline
The archive does not treat climate success as a story told by labels, spending totals, pipeline announcements, or weak accounting.
The ideal solution therefore includes metering where possible, plain scoreboards, strong verification, conservative treatment of paper substitutes, visible fallback and exception rules, and standing correction loops when delivery underperforms [S101][S113][S114][S123][S124][S133][S135][S136][S183].

House rule: **make reality easier to observe than rhetoric.**

### 6. The sixth layer is international diffusion, not national islands of success
The problem is global and the package must travel.
So the ideal solution includes cheaper capital for emerging and developing economies, common metrics and standards, supply-chain coordination, technology diffusion, workforce and capability buildout, and international sector rules where purely national action is too leaky or too weak [S102][S112][S115][S116][S135][S138][S141][S143][S152][S153][S176][S179].

House rule: **the ideal solution is one that many places can actually afford, operate, and copy.**

## Six screens for whether a proposal matches the programme shape

A future proposal should usually score higher when it passes most of these tests:

1. **Gross-decline test** — does it cut real emissions soon rather than mainly improving long-run narrative position?  
2. **Service-system test** — does it improve the clean delivery of heat, cooling, mobility, shelter, food, and reliability rather than merely adding a new technology component?  
3. **Protection test** — does it reduce current exposure to heat, flood, fire, outage, water stress, or other already-arriving climate harms?  
4. **Political-economy test** — does it lower burden, cost of capital, user hassle, or opposition enough to make wider adoption more durable?  
5. **Truth test** — are the gains observable, attributable, and corrigible rather than mostly inferred from nominal activity?  
6. **Travel test** — can the package spread across places with reasonable adaptation rather than bespoke reinvention each time?
