## Claim

The archive already has:

- a response-modes note (`104`)
- a burden-of-proof note (`108`)

What it still lacked was the shortest **live-discipline note** for the question:

**Once escalation, fallback, or exception has been admitted and is underway, what must a serious climate state keep doing until the off-normal period actually closes?**

That matters because many systems fail in the middle.
They prepare a playbook, detect the trigger, authorize the detour, and even design an exit test — but while the detour remains open, truth blurs, protection thins, the cleaner repair path slows, and the workaround quietly becomes the new default.
A compact archive needed one short bridge from **admission** to **safe closure**.

This note is a **compression note**, not a new doctrinal branch.
Its job is to reduce mid-incident governance to five live duties that should remain in force while escalation, fallback, or exception is open [S34][S55][S83][S84][S85][S87][S103][S104][S108][S109][S110][S111][S114][S141][S145][S147][S181][S182][S183].

## Fast rule

**While an off-normal path remains open, keep one live truth picture, protect exposed people first, keep the cleaner mainline repair ahead of the workaround, re-test necessity on a short clock, and force the detour to shrink rather than settle into normality.**

## Five live duties

### 1. Keep one live operational truth
A serious climate state should keep one current picture of the incident: what triggered the detour, who is exposed, what fallback is being used, what protections are live, what the cleaner repair path is doing, and what would justify tightening or closing the detour.

What this usually means:
- one current dashboard or operating brief rather than competing narratives across ministries, utilities, contractors, or local authorities
- visible statement of trigger status, protection status, dirty-fallback status, queue status, repair progress, and next review time
- public-facing summaries where legitimacy and trust matter, with a more detailed operator view behind them [S34][S55][S83][S103][S109][S110][S111][S114][S147]

Why it matters:
- systems lose control when they lose a shared picture of reality
- an exception without live truth quickly becomes an argument about stories instead of an operating problem to be closed

### 2. Protect exposed people and places first, every review cycle
During any off-normal period, protection should not be treated as a one-time announcement.
A serious climate state should keep checking who remains exposed and whether support is actually arriving.

What this usually means:
- protected classes, places, or services remain explicitly named throughout the incident rather than disappearing after the launch memo
- support is judged on delivered shelter, cooling, affordability relief, backup service, health access, or continuity — not only on budget allocated
- if the detour is reducing service quality somewhere, the exposed group should be identified and compensated or shielded rather than left to absorb the gap [S20][S21][S23][S24][S61][S68][S69][S101][S104][S111][S145]

Why it matters:
- climate legitimacy collapses if fallback is managed as an engineering problem while vulnerable people quietly carry the cost
- protection has to stay live for as long as the detour stays live

### 3. Keep repair of the cleaner mainline ahead of stabilization of the workaround
The off-normal path should buy time for the cleaner mainline to recover or overtake, not become the place where effort concentrates because it feels easier.

What this usually means:
- every review should compare energy, money, staffing, procurement, and executive attention going to the workaround versus to fixing the clean path
- queue reform, grid upgrades, delivery capacity, maintenance, replacement procurement, or clean-service restoration should stay ahead of the temporary patch
- if the workaround is getting more comfortable, funded, or politically entrenched than the mainline repair, that is a governance failure and should trigger stronger correction [S31][S32][S39][S41][S49][S84][S87][S101][S103][S104][S111][S181][S182]

Why it matters:
- many “temporary” climate detours become durable because the repair path is slower, less glamorous, or more institutionally difficult than the workaround
- the workaround is acceptable only insofar as it accelerates a return to the cleaner mainline

### 4. Re-test necessity and proof on a short clock
Entry proof is not enough.
If an off-normal path remains open, the actor benefiting from it should keep proving that it is still necessary, still the least damaging available option, and still contained.

What this usually means:
- each review cycle asks again whether the trigger is still active, whether a cleaner substitute is now ready, and whether the exception boundary can narrow
- self-justifying claims from the benefiting actor are not enough; independent review should remain in the loop where stakes are high
- stale fear, convenience, and incumbent comfort should not count as renewed proof [S83][S84][S85][S87][S107][S108][S109][S110][S145][S147]

Why it matters:
- off-normal paths often persist not because they remain necessary but because nobody forces the proof burden to refresh
- recurring re-justification keeps temporary measures temporary

### 5. Force the detour to shrink
A serious climate state should treat every off-normal path as something that must narrow in scope, dirt, duration, or affected population over time.

What this usually means:
- the detour should have a visible shrink plan: fewer users, less geography, lower emissions, cleaner backup, shorter duration, or tighter eligibility
- if shrinkage stalls, the system should explain why and either ratchet repair harder or reconsider whether the detour is still admissible
- “stable but still open” should not be mistaken for success if the workaround is not actually receding [S84][S85][S86][S87][S104][S108][S109][S110][S145][S147]

Why it matters:
- the most common failure of exceptions is not dramatic collapse but quiet normalization
- shrinking the detour is the clearest sign that the system is moving back toward disciplined clean operation

## Minimal live loop

While escalation, fallback, or exception is open, the smallest serious loop is usually:

1. refresh the shared operational truth
2. verify delivered protection for exposed people and places
3. compare repair progress against workaround stabilization
4. re-test necessity and containment
5. narrow the detour or escalate further if narrowing is failing [S55][S83][S103][S104][S108][S109][S110][S111][S147]

## One-paragraph compression

**The archive now has the shortest mid-incident governance note for serious climate states: once escalation, fallback, or exception is live, keep one operational truth picture, protect exposed people first, keep the cleaner mainline repair ahead of the workaround, re-test necessity on a short clock, and force the detour to shrink rather than settle into permanence. This closes the archive’s operational gap between admission and closure without adding a new sectoral branch.**
