# 79 — Ideal Solutions by Burden Shape: Do Not Stack Search, Proof, Cash, Coordination, and Risk on the Weakest Actor

## Claim

The archive now has compact rules for actor allocation, delivery form, payment form, contract form, eligibility form, verification form, remedy form, continuity form, and the joined user pathway.
Those rules explain many parts of good climate delivery.
But the archive still needs the shortest cross-cutting rule for **burden shape**:

**ideal climate solutions should not stack search burden, proof burden, upfront-cash burden, coordination burden, and performance-risk burden on the weakest actor all at once.**

A pathway can be formally open and technically sound and still fail because it asks the intended user to do too many different hard things at the same time.
So the compact burden-shape rule is:

**push burdens upward, separate them where possible, and never require the weaker actor to carry all the hardest burdens in one bundle** [S69][S71][S91][S96][S118][S119][S137][S140][S141][S143].

## Why this belongs in the canon

### 1. Many climate failures are burden-stack failures

A programme can look generous on paper and still underperform because the target user must:
- find the programme,
- understand the jargon,
- prove eligibility,
- float the upfront cost,
- coordinate vendors and documents,
- absorb delay,
- troubleshoot errors,
- and keep the downside if promised performance does not arrive.

Each burden may look tolerable in isolation.
In combination, they sort sharply by income, time, literacy, housing tenure, legal confidence, language, and organisational capacity [S69][S71][S118][S137][S140][S143].

So this note adds a sharper rule than "keep things simple":

**count how many distinct burdens are being stacked on the same actor, especially when that actor is a household, tenant, small business, school, clinic, or cash-constrained municipality.**

### 2. Weak actors usually fail on bundles, not on single hurdles

The archive already says not to rely on heroic consumers and not to force the clean path through proof mazes, reimbursement mazes, contract mazes, or appeals mazes.
This note identifies the deeper pattern:

**the real exclusion mechanism is often cumulative burden, not any one single step** [S69][S118][S119][S137][S140][S143].

A household may survive paperwork if payment is easy.
It may survive upfront payment if the service is turnkey and the contract is trustworthy.
It may survive a complex contract if a trusted intermediary manages the process.
But asking the same weak actor to carry search, proof, cash, coordination, and risk together is usually a design failure.

### 3. Good climate delivery is partly a burden-allocation problem

The actor-allocation note says harder duties should sit with concentrated control points.
The delivery-form note says users should often receive clean service rather than unmanaged technology stacks.
The payment-form note says uptake should not require large upfront liquidity.
The risk-bearing note says controllable downside should stay with managers and providers.
This note binds those ideas together:

**good climate design often works by moving whole categories of burden onto the actors that already have the data, scale, capital, leverage, or operational control to carry them better** [S69][S91][S96][S118][S119][S137][S141][S143].

That is not charity.
It is better systems engineering.

## The archive’s burden categories

A proposed solution is weaker when it stacks several of these on the same weak actor.

### 1. Search burden
Can the intended user find the right path, vendor, finance option, and programme without specialist help? [S118][S119][S140][S141][S143]

### 2. Proof burden
Must the user repeatedly prove identity, need, building condition, eligibility, or compliance when the system or a provider could often verify much of that from existing records or standard methods? [S118][S119][S137][S143]

### 3. Cash-flow burden
Must the user pay first, borrow expensively, or wait for reimbursement before receiving the cleaner outcome? [S69][S91][S96][S137][S143]

### 4. Coordination burden
Must the user assemble quotes, sequence contractors, chase permits, reconcile conflicting advice, or manage multi-party handoffs that stronger actors could have standardized or integrated? [S80][S118][S119][S137][S141]

### 5. Performance-risk burden
If savings, comfort, reliability, or resilience do not materialize, does the weaker actor quietly keep the downside anyway? [S69][S91][S96][S118][S143]

## The archive’s burden-shape rule

The best climate pathways usually do at least four things.

### 1. Unbundle burdens where possible
Do not ask the same user to handle discovery, qualification, financing, contractor management, and performance enforcement unless there is no credible alternative.
Separate the burdens and assign each to the actor best placed to carry it.

### 2. Push recurring burdens upstream
Recurring burdens — vendor qualification, contract design, measurement method choice, finance structuring, default tariff logic, insurance design, or data matching — should usually be solved once upstream rather than thousands of times downstream [S91][S96][S118][S119][S143].

### 3. Keep temporary burdens away from cash-constrained actors
Even when a burden is small in net terms, timing matters.
A low-income household, small landlord, clinic, or municipality can still be excluded by reimbursement delay, bridging-cost requirements, or payment timing that assumes spare liquidity [S69][S71][S91][S96][S137][S143].

### 4. Keep downside with control
If a provider, owner, lender, utility, or programme manager controls a key performance variable, that actor should usually keep the correction duty and much of the downside when the promised result fails [S91][S96][S118][S143].

## Quick screen

A future proposal should usually be promoted only if it can answer these clearly.

### 1. Burden-count test
How many distinct burdens fall on the intended weak actor? Fewer is usually better.

### 2. Burden-bundle test
Which burdens are being imposed simultaneously at the moment of decision? That bundle is often more important than any single burden viewed alone.

### 3. Upstream-shift test
Which of those burdens could instead be carried by a utility, landlord, city, lender, public agency, trusted intermediary, programme integrator, or provider network? [S80][S91][S96][S118][S119][S141][S143]

### 4. Failure-burden test
When the process stalls or the promised performance fails, who has to do the work of correction? [S118][S143]

### 5. Repeatability test
Can the burden-bearing arrangement be repeated at mass scale without depending on unusually capable users? [S118][S119][S137][S141][S143]

## What this rule promotes

Promote:
- designs that ask weaker actors to make one intelligible choice while stronger actors carry most of the search, coordination, paperwork, and performance-management load [S118][S119][S137][S141][S143]
- on-bill, tariff-linked, service-linked, or otherwise embedded payment structures that avoid cash-first adoption [S69][S91][S96][S137][S143]
- public or regulated intermediaries, one-stop delivery channels, and prequalified networks that solve coordination once instead of repeatedly [S80][S118][S119][S141]
- provider-backed or programme-backed performance models when providers claim savings, comfort, reliability, or flexibility value [S91][S96][S118][S143]
- social-protection-aware and people-centred climate design that treats administrative endurance as a real distributive issue rather than as user weakness [S71][S140][S141][S143]

## What this rule demotes

Demote:
- climate pathways that are formally open but practically usable only by cash-rich, high-literacy, high-time, owner-occupier users [S69][S71][S137][S140][S143]
- reimbursement-heavy, document-heavy, contractor-shopping-heavy programmes that quietly combine three or more hard burdens at once [S69][S118][S119][S137][S143]
- proposals that celebrate user choice while offloading search, integration, and downside onto people with the least slack [S118][S140][S141][S143]
- any system that treats high abandonment or selective uptake as evidence of weak demand rather than of bad burden allocation [S118][S137][S140][S143]

## One-paragraph compression

**Ideal climate solutions should not stack search, proof, cash, coordination, and performance-risk burdens on the weakest actor at the same time. The better rule is to push recurring burdens upstream, keep upfront and timing burdens away from cash-constrained users, keep downside with actors that control the outcome, and let weaker actors reach the cleaner state through a short, legible, low-burden path rather than a bundle of endurance tests** [S69][S71][S80][S91][S96][S118][S119][S137][S140][S141][S143].
