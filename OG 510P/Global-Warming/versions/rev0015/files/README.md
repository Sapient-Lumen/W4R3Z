# Global Warming — rev0015

Global Warming is a compact research archive for answering one enormous question with discipline: **what would an actually workable solution portfolio for stopping further warming look like, if we optimized for physics, deployment speed, political durability, justice, and archive legibility at once?**

This revision keeps the archive intentionally small. It adds one missing operating layer from rev0014: **workforce architecture**. The archive already had the clean-buildout portfolio, the international delivery bargain, the managed-decline rule, the outcome scoreboard, the throughput note, the observability note, the time-discipline rule, the reliability note, the adoption note, the lead-market note, the innovation-discipline note, the spatial-systems note, the material-throughput note, and the non-CO₂ completion note; rev0015 adds a compact note on skills, apprenticeships, reskilling, and service capacity so the archive says more clearly that skilled labour is explicit climate solution infrastructure rather than background implementation detail.

The archive remains citation-heavy and biased toward:

- solution portfolios rather than silver bullets;
- deployment constraints rather than vibes;
- global equity and political economy rather than technology-only stories;
- near-term emissions cuts before speculative late-century cleanup;
- clean markdown notes and machine-readable indices rather than stored PDFs or bulky scratch.

## Archive stance

The archive currently argues that the least-fantastical "ideal solution" is a **global crash portfolio** with twenty-one linked elements:

1. abundant clean electricity;
2. electrification of everything that can be electrified;
3. aggressive energy and material efficiency;
4. rapid methane abatement;
5. forest protection, ecosystem restoration, and food-system reform;
6. selective hard-sector tools for steel, cement, chemicals, shipping, aviation, and selected freight;
7. public policy, finance, and institutional capacity that make the transition durable and fair;
8. tightly bounded carbon dioxide removal for residual emissions, not as a license to delay;
9. a global delivery architecture that pairs rich-country fossil retirement and finance with development-compatible clean buildout in emerging and low-income economies;
10. an explicit managed-decline architecture for fossil fuels so buildout produces real displacement;
11. a throughput architecture that attacks queues, permitting drag, component shortages, and social-legitimacy failures before they stall deployment;
12. a climate-observability layer that uses MRV, open data, satellites, and public proof to separate real transition from paper compliance;
13. an explicit time-discipline layer that tells the archive to privilege fast first-decade cuts, start long-lead infrastructure early, and treat slower residual tools as follow-on rather than governing logic;
14. a reliability architecture that treats flexibility, storage, demand response, technical stability, and bounded clean firming as part of climate success rather than grid aftercare;
15. an adoption architecture that makes clean switching the easy default for households and small firms through better finance, standards, charging and service access, installer capacity, and renter-safe rules;
16. a lead-market architecture that uses procurement, product standards, carbon accounting, and coordinated trade rules to let clean industrial production scale without being undercut by high-emissions incumbents;
17. an innovation-discipline architecture that funds demonstrations, first-of-a-kind projects, and milestone-based learning for frontier technologies without allowing them to crowd out fast deployment of mature tools;
18. a spatial-systems architecture that treats compact urban growth, transit, safe walking and cycling, retrofit-first building policy, and whole-life carbon rules as explicit climate tools for cutting structural demand and avoiding sprawl lock-in;
19. a material-throughput architecture that treats circularity, reuse, repair, retrofit, long-lived products, and lower virgin-material demand as explicit climate tools rather than as waste-policy side issues;
20. a non-CO₂ completion architecture that treats nitrous oxide, refrigerants, and sustainable cooling access as explicit warming-governance problems rather than residual technical details;
21. a workforce architecture that treats skills, apprenticeships, reskilling, service capacity, job quality, and inclusive recruitment as explicit climate infrastructure rather than background labour-market noise.
