## Core claim of the archive

The best available evidence still does **not** point to a single master technology. It points to a coordinated portfolio: clean power, grids, storage, electrification, efficiency, methane cuts, land-use reform, hard-sector innovation, enabling governance and finance, a bounded amount of CDR, an explicit fossil-retirement architecture, a compact outcome scoreboard, a throughput architecture that solves queues, permitting, procurement, and local-legitimacy bottlenecks, an observability architecture that can verify progress in near real time, a time-discipline rule that tells the archive what must happen in the first decade, a reliability architecture that keeps clean-heavy power systems stable, affordable, and politically durable as demand rises, an adoption architecture that makes clean switching low-friction, a lead-market architecture that lets clean industrial products compete across borders, an innovation-discipline architecture that pushes frontier technologies through demonstrations and first-of-a-kind learning without letting them outrank fast deployment, a spatial-systems architecture that treats compact urban growth, transit, walking and cycling, retrofit-first practice, and whole-life carbon rules as first-order tools for cutting structural demand, a material-throughput architecture that treats circularity, reuse, repair, retrofit, and lower virgin-material demand as first-order tools for easing pressure on heavy-industry decarbonisation, and a non-CO₂ completion architecture that treats nitrous oxide, refrigerants, and sustainable cooling access as first-order tools for closing warming outside the main CO₂-and-methane frame, plus a workforce architecture that treats skills, apprenticeships, reskilling, service capacity, and inclusive recruitment as first-order tools for actually building and maintaining the transition.
