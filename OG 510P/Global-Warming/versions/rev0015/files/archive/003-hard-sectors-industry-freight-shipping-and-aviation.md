# 003 — Hard sectors: industry, freight, shipping, and aviation

Tags: industry, shipping, aviation, freight, hydrogen, ccus, circularity

## One-line thesis

The ideal solution is to electrify what is easy and reserve scarce expensive tools for hard sectors that truly need them.

## Why this matters

A lot of climate discourse lets hard sectors swallow the whole agenda. The right move is the reverse: simplify the broad stack first, then concentrate special tools where they earn their keep.

## Core claim

IPCC states that net-zero CO2 emissions from industry are challenging but possible, requiring coordinated action across value chains, demand management, energy and material efficiency, circular material flows, abatement technologies, and transformational changes in production processes.[1] It further says progress toward industrial net zero will rely on low- and zero-GHG electricity, hydrogen, fuels, and carbon management.[1]

That implies a hierarchy. The ideal portfolio should **not** spray hydrogen, biomass, or carbon capture everywhere. Instead it should:

1. use direct electrification where it is already superior or rapidly becoming so;
2. cut material throughput via durability, reuse, repair, recycling, and substitution;
3. apply hydrogen, synthetic fuels, and CCS to steel, cement, chemicals, shipping, aviation, and selected long-haul freight where direct electrification is constrained;
4. use procurement, standards, and contracts-for-difference to create early markets for clean industrial output.

IEA's 2023 Net Zero Roadmap update is useful here because it breaks the transition into sector dashboards for steel and aluminium, cement, primary chemicals, road transport, aviation and shipping, hydrogen, CCUS, and fossil-fuel supply.[2] The important archive lesson is structural: there is no one hard-sector technology. There is a portfolio of narrow tools, each justified only where the broad fast stack runs out.

## Preferred ordering

- **First:** efficiency, circularity, and electrification.
- **Second:** process redesign and clean feedstocks.
- **Third:** targeted hydrogen or e-fuels for high-temperature heat and energy-dense transport.
- **Fourth:** CCS for residual process emissions or genuinely hard combustion sources.

## Anti-theater tests

A hard-sector plan fails this archive's standards if it:

- expands hydrogen demand faster than clean power can support it;
- treats CCS as a universal excuse to prolong unabated fossil infrastructure;
- ignores material demand reduction and circularity;
- counts pilots as solved deployment pathways.

## Pattern pack

- Scarce molecules go to scarce-use cases.
- Abatement should follow a hierarchy, not a subsidy free-for-all.
- Industrial policy matters because first-of-a-kind clean plants need market formation.
