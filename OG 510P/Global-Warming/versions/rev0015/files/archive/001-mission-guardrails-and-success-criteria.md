# 001 — Mission, guardrails, and success criteria

Tags: strategy, guardrails, urgency, portfolio, justice

## One-line thesis

The ideal solution to global warming is not one heroic technology but a disciplined global portfolio that cuts gross emissions fast, protects sinks, limits overshoot, and stays politically durable.

## Why this matters

The archive needs a north star before it accumulates details. Without one, the project could drift into gadget worship, offset theater, or a pile of disconnected sector notes.

## Core claim

The evidence base already says most of the broad shape. Near-term scalable options exist; the world is instead bottlenecked by pace, state capacity, finance, coordination, and distributional conflict.[1][2] As of January 2026, the Global Carbon Budget estimated about 170 GtCO2 remained for limiting warming to 1.5°C, or roughly four years at current emissions rates.[3]

That means an archive about "solving global warming" should optimize first for **gross emissions reduction speed** and only second for elegant long-run cleanup. UNEP's 2025 Emissions Gap Report says emissions in 2035 would need to be 55% below 2019 levels for a 1.5°C-aligned pathway, and warns that higher exceedance of 1.5°C is very likely within the next decade absent stronger action.[2]

## What success would look like

An ideal solution stack would:

1. drive fossil emissions down rapidly this decade;
2. build zero- and very-low-emission energy fast enough to replace fossil demand rather than merely accompany it;
3. cut methane and halt deforestation quickly because they move near-term warming and land-system risk;
4. reserve scarce high-cost tools for genuinely hard residuals;
5. make the transition materially beneficial or at least tolerable for workers, poor households, and developing economies;
6. avoid betting the plan on large late-century carbon removal.

## Anti-goals

The archive should reject five recurring non-solutions:

- **delay by future cleanup** — using CDR as permission to postpone gross cuts;[1]
- **pilot-project consolation** — celebrating niche demonstrations while system emissions stay high;
- **accounting-only victory** — paper targets without deployment, grids, permitting, finance, and enforcement;
- **rich-world provincialism** — assuming the solution works without development-compatible pathways for the Global South;[2][4]
- **single-sector exceptionalism** — pretending any one lever can substitute for the rest.[1]

## Pattern pack

- Fast gross cuts beat later heroic removals.
- Cheap clean power plus electrification plus efficiency is the backbone.
- Methane, land, and forests are speed levers, not side quests.
- Hard sectors need selective special treatment, not universal exemptions.
- Finance, justice, and state capacity are first-order technical variables.

## Research directives for later revisions

- Quantify which levers are most supply-constrained versus politically constrained.
- Separate globally ideal sequences from regionally feasible sequences.
- Track where climate policy can be made materially popular via health, jobs, and energy-security gains.
- Keep a live watch on which residual emissions categories truly remain after strong electrification and efficiency.
