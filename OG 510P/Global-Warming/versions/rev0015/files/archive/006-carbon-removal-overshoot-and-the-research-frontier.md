# 006 — Carbon removal, overshoot, and the research frontier

Tags: cdr, overshoot, residuals, research-frontier

## One-line thesis

Carbon dioxide removal belongs in the solution only as a bounded residual tool; the more the world delays gross cuts, the riskier and larger the required removal portfolio becomes.

## Why this matters

The archive needs a hard boundary here because climate strategy is unusually vulnerable to elegant procrastination: promise deep future removals, keep emitting now, and call the resulting gap a pathway.

## Core claim

IPCC says deployment of CDR to counterbalance hard-to-abate residual emissions is unavoidable at net zero, but that scale and timing depend on how fast gross emissions fall.[1] It also says pathways that limit warming to 1.5°C with no or limited overshoot use less net-negative CO2 emissions and less CDR than pathways that return to 1.5°C after a high overshoot.[1] UNEP's 2025 Emissions Gap Report warns that larger exceedance of 1.5°C is very likely within the next decade and that stronger cuts are needed partly to avoid excessive reliance on uncertain carbon dioxide removal later.[2]

So the archive boundary is simple:

- **Yes** to CDR for residual emissions that remain after aggressive mitigation.
- **Yes** to research, demonstration, MRV, and governance for durable removal.
- **No** to using removal as the first-line substitute for clean power, electrification, methane abatement, or deforestation prevention.
- **No** to treating overshoot as harmless because temperatures might fall later.

## Preferred CDR posture

1. Reduce gross emissions first.
2. Identify genuinely residual sectors.
3. Build transparent MRV, permanence accounting, and governance.
4. Use durable removals selectively and honestly.
5. Keep land-based removals screened for tenure, biodiversity, and food-system trade-offs.

## Research frontier

The live research agenda should prioritize:

- how much residual demand remains after an aggressive fast-stack transition;
- comparative governance and permanence quality across CDR methods;
- how to finance durable removals without crowding out faster mitigation;
- how to identify and prevent offset-market substitution errors;
- how to increase support for frontier energy innovation without slowing deployment-first work, given that public energy R&D in IEA member countries remains around 0.05% of GDP.[3]

## Pattern pack

- Removal is a mop, not the fire brigade.
- Overshoot is not free even if later reversed.
- Governance quality matters as much as chemistry.
- Residuals must be earned by prior mitigation, not assumed in advance.
