# 002 — The fast stack: clean power, electrification, and efficiency

Tags: power, electrification, efficiency, buildings, transport, urban-systems, grids

## One-line thesis

The fastest broad solution stack is abundant clean electricity plus electrification plus efficiency, because it cuts across power, transport, buildings, and part of industry at once.

## Why this matters

Most climate strategy arguments collapse into tool tribalism. This note names the stack that does the most work across the largest share of emissions with the strongest current cost and deployment evidence.

## Core claim

IPCC assesses that several mitigation options already viable at scale in the near term include solar, wind, electrification of urban systems, urban green infrastructure, energy efficiency, demand-side management, improved forest and crop or grassland management, and reduced food waste and loss.[1] It also reports that from 2010 to 2019 the unit costs of solar energy fell 85%, wind 55%, and lithium-ion batteries 85%.[1]

That cost movement matters because clean electricity is not just a power-sector story. It is the enabling substrate for EVs, heat pumps, induction and resistive end uses, parts of industrial heat, and digital control of demand. IPCC also estimates demand-side measures can reduce end-use sector emissions 40-70% by 2050 relative to baseline scenarios, with the caveat that some populations still need more energy and material access for basic well-being.[1]

The current system trend is directionally helpful but still too slow. IEA's Electricity 2026 outlook says global electricity demand is forecast to grow 3.6% per year on average through 2030, while renewables and nuclear together are expected to account for around half of global electricity generation by 2030.[2] IEA's Renewables 2025 analysis says renewable power capacity is projected to increase by almost 4,600 GW between 2025 and 2030, with renewables expected to meet over 90% of global electricity demand growth during that period.[3]

But the archive should treat **grids and flexibility** as part of the fast stack rather than as secondary plumbing. IEA says grid capacity shortages are already a critical bottleneck in many regions, with more than 2,500 GW of renewable, large-load, and storage projects stalled in queues worldwide.[4]

## Operational consequence

An ideal solution archive should therefore treat the following as the default "fast stack":

- solar, wind, hydro, geothermal, and nuclear where regionally acceptable;
- transmission, distribution, interconnection, and grid-forming flexibility;
- storage, demand response, and system integration;
- EVs, public transport, and compact urban design;
- heat pumps, insulation, efficient cooling, and building retrofits;
- material efficiency and smart demand reduction so the clean-power buildout does not chase preventable waste.

## Failure modes

- Adding clean power without retiring or displacing fossil generation.
- Electrifying demand without building enough clean supply or grids.
- Treating efficiency as optional because it looks less glamorous than supply.
- Ignoring curtailment, queues, and congestion until they become political backlash.
- Building urban form that locks in car dependence and high floor-area energy demand.

## Pattern pack

- Build power and wires faster than fossil replacement requires.
- Electrify whatever is cheap and mature first.
- Use efficiency to shrink the denominator everywhere.
- Design cities so low-carbon choices are normal, not heroic.
- Treat flexibility and grids as first-order climate infrastructure.
