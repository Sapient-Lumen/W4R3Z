# 004 — Methane, land, food, and forest systems

Tags: methane, forests, food, agriculture, land-use, biodiversity

## One-line thesis

A workable climate solution must treat methane and land systems as front-line levers, not leftovers after energy policy.

## Why this matters

Energy dominates attention, but methane and land can move warming quickly and shape biodiversity, water, food security, and local political legitimacy at the same time.

## Core claim

IEA's 2025 Global Methane Tracker says energy-related methane emissions are widely underreported; its estimate is about 80% higher than the total reported by countries to the UNFCCC.[1] IEA also estimates that around 70% of methane emissions from fossil-fuel operations could be reduced with existing technology, and that in oil and gas the reduction potential is over 75%.[2] This makes methane one of the fastest high-leverage mitigation opportunities in the archive.

On land, FAO frames halting deforestation as an integrated problem involving climate finance, agrifood-system transformation, forest governance, biodiversity protection, local rights, and data systems.[3] IPCC likewise treats improved forest and crop or grassland management and reduced food waste and loss as near-term viable mitigation options, and notes that land-related options can deliver adaptation co-benefits when well implemented.[4]

The resulting ideal portfolio is not simply "plant more trees." It is:

- stop avoidable methane leaks and venting fast;
- halt deforestation and forest degradation;
- improve agricultural productivity without frontier expansion;
- reduce food loss and waste;
- support agroforestry, soil stewardship, and degraded-land restoration where locally appropriate;
- protect Indigenous and local-community rights because land governance quality affects both permanence and justice.

## Failure modes

- Counting forests as compensation for continued fossil growth.
- Promoting large monoculture or bioenergy schemes that create new land conflicts.
- Ignoring measurement and MRV quality in methane and land accounting.
- Treating agrifood transformation as politically impossible and therefore optional.

## Pattern pack

- Fast methane abatement is a speed lever.
- Forest protection beats later restoration where both are possible.
- Agrifood reform must be productivity-plus-rights, not simply restriction.
- Land measures must be screened for biodiversity, tenure, and livelihood effects.
