# 206 — Ideal Solutions: When the Verdict Is Rewrite — The Minimum Packet That Makes a Climate Proposal Formed Enough Compact Canon

## Claim

The archive now has a smallest intake interview (`204`) and a smallest verdict card (`205`).
That is enough to reject, admit, demote, quarantine, or refuse many claims.
But one small front-door gap remained:

**when the verdict is `rewrite`, what exactly should the proposal come back with?**

That gap matters because “rewrite” is the archive’s most common honest verdict for promising climate ideas.
Without a tiny return format, rewrite can become either endless abstraction or quiet archive drift.
So this note adds the smallest useful answer.
It is not a new doctrinal branch.
Its job is to turn “promising but under-specified” into a compact target shape that can re-enter the archive cleanly [S97][S100][S101][S103][S106][S127][S128][S145][S147][S148][S180].

## Fast rule

**If the verdict is `rewrite`, do not return with more rhetoric, examples, or urgency language. Return with one minimum packet: the control point, the full replacement package, the proof ledger, the replication shape, and the displacement story. If the proposal still cannot fit that packet, it is not yet formed enough for admission.**

## The minimum rewrite packet

### 1. Control point
Name the real decision point that moves.
Who must decide, approve, procure, retrofit, retire, connect, finance, or enforce what?
A rewrite should come back with one sentence that names the actor, the asset or workflow, and the moment of intervention.
If this remains vague, the proposal is still an intuition rather than a design candidate [S97][S106][S127][S128][S163][S169].

### 2. Full replacement package
Name the complete clean offer, not the headline technology alone.
A rewrite should come back with the smallest believable package: the physical change, the delivery path, the money path, the operating responsibility, and the remedy path if performance fails.
If the proposal still assumes that finance, interconnection, maintenance, user support, or verification will somehow be solved later, it is not yet formed enough [S100][S101][S103][S106][S128][S145][S185].

### 3. Proof ledger
Name how the gain will be proved in the physical world.
A rewrite should come back with a tiny ledger: what three or fewer outcomes will be checked, on what cadence, and against what baseline.
Good examples are lower gross emissions, lower methane leakage, lower peak load, lower waste, lower exposure, lower bills for the intended group, or lower dirty throughput.
If the proposal still relies mainly on adoption counts, spending totals, labels, or target language, the proof rule is still missing [S97][S101][S103][S106][S133][S145][S189].

### 4. Replication shape
Name what makes the proposal repeatable beyond one heroic case.
A rewrite should come back with the archetype it wants to spread: the standard offer, reference design, procurement form, training pattern, finance template, or operating playbook that later adopters can reuse.
If every future case still requires bespoke redesign, the archive should treat the proposal as a one-off project rather than a scaling path [S147][S168][S171][S176][S186].

### 5. Displacement story
Name what dirtier path becomes smaller, weaker, harder, or retired because of the proposal.
A rewrite should come back with a plain displacement claim: what dirty asset, workflow, fuel, leak, waste pattern, settlement pattern, or fallback loses ground, and by what mechanism.
If the proposal still describes only clean addition beside continued dirty normality, it is not yet a serious transition claim [S101][S145][S148][S149][S189].

## What not to bring back

Do not treat the following as a sufficient rewrite:
- a longer benefits list without a clearer control point
- a more exciting technology story without a fuller package
- a finance announcement without a proof ledger
- a pilot story without a replication shape
- a cleaner image without a dirtier path that actually loses ground

Those moves make the proposal louder, not more formed [S89][S90][S92][S127][S145].

## Why this compression helps

### It makes `rewrite` concrete
`205` says when the archive should give the verdict `rewrite`.
This note says what a rewritten proposal must actually contain before returning.

### It reduces archive churn
A minimum packet keeps the archive from reopening broad design argument every time a half-formed idea comes back with new rhetoric.

### It improves proposal quality without growing the archive
Many proposals do not need a new file.
They need a better shape.
This note raises that shape threshold while keeping the front door small.

## Preferred use

Open this note first when the live question is any of the following:
- what a promising but under-specified climate proposal should come back with after a `rewrite` verdict
- how to turn a vague climate idea into something formed enough for serious archive treatment
- what minimum packet the archive wants before reconsidering a proposal
- why an idea keeps sounding interesting but not yet shaped enough for admission
- what concrete work remains after the archive says “rewrite” rather than “admit”

Then reopen:
- `204` if the question becomes the intake interview itself
- `205` if the question becomes which verdict should be given
- `92` if the question becomes what archive action should follow the verdict
- `97`, `128`, `198`, `199`, `200`, `201`, or `203` if the surviving issue is mainly about acceptance tests, the design kernel, transition mechanism, replacement proof, the atomic unit, the scaling unit, or the winning loop

## Compression rule

**When the verdict is `rewrite`, require one minimum packet before reconsideration: the control point, the full replacement package, the proof ledger, the replication shape, and the displacement story.**
