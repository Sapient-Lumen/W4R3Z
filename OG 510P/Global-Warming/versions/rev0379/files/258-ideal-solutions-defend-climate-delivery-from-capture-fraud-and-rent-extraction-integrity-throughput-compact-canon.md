# 258 — Ideal Solutions: Defend Climate Delivery from Capture, Fraud, and Rent Extraction — Integrity-Throughput Compact Canon

## Claim

Rev0247’s claims-integrity note says climate claims need proof before they move trust, money, permissions, or priority.
Rev0248’s enforcement-remedy note adds that value-channel proof must also have consequences: when public value is captured, overcharged, mis-specified, falsely claimed, or poorly delivered, the channel needs cure, suspension, clawback, reprocurement, debarment, user redress, and channel redesign routes.

But claims are only one part of the integrity problem.

A mature climate programme also moves enormous value through public procurement, grid queues, subsidies, rebates, guarantees, land decisions, minerals, recovery funds, public backstops, carbon markets, rate cases, and large-load approvals.
That value will attract capture, collusion, fraud, insider routing, political patronage, consultant dependency, false eligibility, inflated prices, weak delivery, and community bypass.

The answer is not to slow the transition into procedural paralysis.
The answer is to treat integrity as a throughput system:

**public money, scarce capacity, and climate permissions should move fast through standard, transparent, contestable, auditable channels — and stop fast when capture, fraud, poor quality, or rent extraction is detected.**

## Fast rule

**Every high-value climate packet should name its money path, permission path, procurement path, ownership path, conflict risks, price benchmark, milestone proof, grievance route, clawback rule, reprocurement or debarment trigger, user-redress path, and correction owner before value is released. Integrity is not the enemy of speed; hidden capture is.**

## The compact canon

### 1. Integrity risks cluster at predictable control points

Climate programmes should pre-map the moments where value, discretion, urgency, and weak information meet.
High-risk control points include:
- eligibility for grants, rebates, tariffs, guarantees, credit lines, and public backstops
- project preparation and consultant selection
- land, rights-of-way, water access, and permits
- interconnection queues and grid-capacity allocation
- procurement specifications and prequalification
- contract change orders and emergency procurement
- public utility rate cases and cost pass-throughs
- carbon-credit generation, validation, verification, retirement, and claims
- critical-mineral licensing, traceability, and community benefit
- recovery funds after disasters
- resilience, adaptation, and relocation programmes
- large-load approvals and “additional clean power” claims

House rule: **do not wait for scandal to discover the high-risk points.**

### 2. Public procurement is climate governance

OECD’s 2026 procurement-integrity work says public procurement is a core government function, accounting for about **13% of GDP** in OECD countries, and that its many transactions, financial interests, complex procedures, and public-private interactions create risks of corruption, fraud, conflict of interest, mismanagement, and collusion [S282].
It also reports estimates that **8–25%** of global public investment may be lost to mismanagement and corruption [S282].

For climate delivery, that loss is not only fiscal.
It is fewer retrofits, slower grids, weaker protection, lower-quality cooling, more expensive buses, bad contractors, lost trust, and stronger backlash.

House rule: **procurement integrity is emissions, resilience, and affordability policy.**

### 3. Green procurement needs environmental proof, not only green objectives

OECD’s 2025 green-public-procurement data says all responding OECD countries incorporate environmental objectives into procurement-specific policy documents and 69% report quantitative GPP targets, but only 3 of 29 OECD countries with procurement KPIs report measuring environmental impact such as CO2 savings [S283].

That gap matters.
If procurement is the market-making engine of the clean transition, then it has to know whether the purchased goods and services actually reduce emissions, increase resilience, lower lifecycle cost, or improve service.

House rule: **do not count a green procurement target as climate delivery until it has product definitions, lifecycle boundaries, contract proof, and outcome measurement.**

### 4. Competition must be designed, not assumed

Clean markets can be captured by narrow vendor pools, bespoke specifications, excessive qualification barriers, hidden incumbency advantages, opaque framework agreements, manipulated change orders, or political sponsorship disguised as speed.
The archive therefore prefers procurement platforms that are standardised enough to scale but open enough to compete.

A competition-safe climate platform should include:
- public demand calendar
- standard specifications and allowed variants
- clear qualification rules
- conflict-of-interest disclosure
- beneficial-ownership disclosure for high-value awards
- open price and performance benchmarks where feasible
- change-order discipline
- complaint and bid-challenge route
- debarment or exclusion for serious misconduct
- performance data feeding the next tender

House rule: **standardization should lower transaction cost, not freeze the market around insiders.**

### 5. Speed needs risk-based controls

The transition cannot afford a control system that treats a small heat-pump rebate, a municipal bus framework, a billion-dollar transmission contract, a public-insurance backstop, a carbon-credit programme, and post-disaster emergency procurement as identical.

Use risk tiers:
- light-touch automated checks for low-value, routine, reversible cases
- sampled audit for high-volume standard offers
- stronger due diligence for high-value or politically connected awards
- milestone-based payment for construction and service contracts
- independent verification where false claims can cause large financial or climate harm
- emergency fast tracks with post-award publication, price benchmarks, and retroactive audit

House rule: **proportionate controls are faster than both naïve trust and maximal paperwork.**

### 6. Beneficial ownership and conflicts should travel with value

Climate funds, procurement platforms, recovery programmes, land decisions, and credit mechanisms can be routed through complex corporate structures.
That creates room for hidden related parties, political exposure, self-dealing, sanctions evasion, shell companies, and circular contracting.

A high-value packet should therefore include:
- beneficial-ownership declaration
- politically exposed person declaration where appropriate
- related-party and conflict-of-interest disclosure
- subcontractor chain visibility at least for high-risk tiers
- sanctions and debarment checks
- update duty when ownership changes

House rule: **if public value is moving, ownership opacity is not a neutral administrative detail.**

### 7. Pay for verified milestones, not climate adjectives

A delivery packet should release money, guarantees, queue priority, procurement eligibility, or rate recovery against verified progress.
Examples:
- grid capacity energized, not merely contracted
- building performance verified after commissioning, not merely equipment installed
- methane repaired and non-recurring, not merely inspected
- buses operating reliably, not merely purchased
- cooling centres maintaining safe indoor conditions during heat, not merely opened
- recovery housing safe and stable, not merely funded
- carbon credits retired and claim-bounded, not merely purchased

House rule: **milestone proof turns integrity from paperwork into delivery control.**

### 8. Community bypass is a capture risk too

Capture is not only bribery.
It can also mean the people carrying land, water, health, affordability, labour, cultural, or displacement burden are excluded until the decision is functionally over.
That creates litigation, protest, bad design, distrust, and sometimes genuine injustice.

Integrity-throughput therefore needs:
- participation at the control point where design can still change
- plain-language disclosure of burdens and benefits
- grievance and remedy routes
- community-benefit terms defined before private bargaining
- protections for tenants, informal residents, workers, and exposed groups
- rights-respecting processes for land, minerals, recovery, and relocation

House rule: **participation that cannot change the packet is theatre; participation that improves the packet is throughput.**

### 9. Public value should not leak into private rents without a return path

Public authority often creates value by de-risking projects, granting monopoly access, funding networks, guaranteeing payment, standardizing demand, or providing data and land.
Some private return is necessary to deliver.
But unexplained upside capture can break legitimacy.

A serious packet should define the public return:
- lower bills or tariffs
- service quality guarantees
- local employment and training
- affordability protections
- data rights and public learning
- clawbacks for non-performance
- shared savings where public risk lowered cost
- future price reductions as markets mature

House rule: **where the public takes risk, the public should see service, affordability, resilience, or learning return.**

### 10. Enforcement has to repair the system, not only punish the bad actor

When misconduct or capture is found, the response should include more than a penalty.
The programme should ask what design allowed the failure.

Possible responses:
- suspend payment or eligibility
- claw back funds
- replace contractor or verifier
- publish corrected data
- repair affected households or communities
- revise specifications
- adjust price benchmarks
- tighten verification for a category
- lower barriers for honest competitors
- strengthen whistleblower protection
- update the risk map
- share lessons with other procurement offices, utilities, funds, or regulators

House rule: **each integrity failure should make the next packet harder to capture and easier to deliver honestly.**

## Minimum integrity-throughput packet

A formed integrity-throughput packet should include:
- value at stake: money, guarantee, permit, queue position, public backstop, rate recovery, land access, or procurement preference
- owner and decision authority
- procurement or allocation route
- eligibility rule
- beneficial ownership and conflict disclosures
- competition test or reason for sole-source / restricted route
- price benchmark or cost-reasonableness method
- environmental / physical claim being made
- milestone evidence before each payment or priority release
- audit and sampling plan
- public disclosure level
- complaint, grievance, and whistleblower route
- community or user remedy route
- clawback, suspension, debarment, or correction consequence
- update clock and post-completion review

## What this note demotes

This note demotes ten mistakes:
- treating anti-corruption as an appendix after climate design is complete
- using urgency to justify opaque procurement or insider routing
- mistaking a green objective in tender documents for measured environmental impact
- writing specifications around one vendor and calling it standardisation
- paying for installed assets with no commissioning, service, or outcome proof
- letting recovery urgency recreate waste, fraud, and unsafe rebuilding
- leaving beneficial ownership, conflicts, and subcontract chains invisible in high-value packets
- treating community bypass as faster than early design-quality participation
- using public guarantees and ratepayer money without public-value return
- punishing one bad actor while leaving the capture pathway intact

### 13. Use joined-up records as anti-capture infrastructure
Capture thrives when procurement, project, finance, ownership, milestone, complaint, and performance records cannot be connected. The integrity-throughput layer should therefore use open contracting, infrastructure-data, beneficial-ownership, price-benchmark, milestone-proof, audit, and remedy records that can be joined without turning every delivery office into a forensic unit [S297][S298].

House rule: **opacity is not throughput; it is delayed failure.**

## Compression rule

**Climate integrity is throughput discipline: move public value fast through standard, transparent, contestable, auditable channels; release money and priority against verified milestones; expose ownership and conflicts where value is high; protect users and communities; claw back failure; and redesign the channel whenever capture is found.**

## Rev0268 integrity bridge — debris contracts are high-risk throughput channels

Integrity doctrine now flags debris removal, demolition, hazardous-waste hauling, temporary-site management, landfill use, mold remediation, and repair contracting as high-risk post-shock rent-extraction channels. Load tickets, manifests, facility receipts, worker-safety proof, price transparency, and complaint resolution belong in the climate integrity ledger. [S522] [S523]
