# 02 — Program 2026–2035

This is the archive’s working decade program. It is optimized for physical effect, compounding infrastructure, and political durability.
For the shortest timing-compression version of this logic, see `62-ideal-solutions-by-time-three-lanes-emergency-replacement-residual.md`.

## Phase I: 2026–2027 — Clear the blockers

### Build and regulatory moves
- fast-track transmission, distribution, interconnection, and grid modernization
- accelerate solar, wind, storage, geothermal, hydro uprates, and region-appropriate nuclear where lead times and institutions support it
- adopt **anticipatory grid planning** rather than waiting for queues and congestion crises to accumulate [S14]
- create cross-agency delivery units for major clean-energy, grid, and industrial-infrastructure projects so spatial planning, permitting, grid access, environmental review, and procurement stop operating as disconnected queues [S59][S60][S61]
- use faster-capacity tools where possible: reconductoring, dynamic line rating, voltage uprating, transparent capacity maps, hybrid connections, non-firm contracts, and distribution-grid modernization where local bottlenecks are blocking electrification [S14][S55]
- reform electricity markets so flexibility, storage, clean firm capacity, and demand response get paid for actual system value [S56][S58]
- launch explicit demand-response programs for cooling, EV charging, and flexible industry rather than assuming they will appear spontaneously [S56][S58]
- create copper, transformer, and battery supply plans for power-system expansion rather than treating these as downstream procurement accidents [S47][S48][S50]
- adopt building-renovation plans and code-enforcement programs so building demand falls as the grid cleans up rather than rising in parallel [S51][S53][S54]
- force leak detection and repair in oil and gas systems; ban routine venting and flaring

### Demand-side moves
- mass deployment of heat pumps and building retrofits
- public retrofit-delivery vehicles, one-stop shops, and social-housing-first renovation programs, plus workforce expansion for installers and auditors [S53][S54]
- targeted affordability packages: social tariffs or lifeline structures where needed, low-income retrofit support, and bill-lowering flexibility programs rather than blunt price suppression [S58][S69][S71]
- rapid hiring, training, and digital support for permit-granting authorities, grid operators, inspectors, planners, electricians, lineworkers, HVAC installers, and environmental-review staff [S60][S62][S63]
- cooling efficiency, passive cooling, urban heat reduction, and stronger AC/fan standards [S16][S17]
- stronger building energy codes for new construction, especially in fast-growing urban markets, plus explicit clean-cooking programs where dirty household fuels remain widespread [S51][S54]
- electricity-access and clean-cooking drives in high-gap regions using grids, mini-grids, stand-alone systems, and targeted affordability support rather than waiting for trickle-down connection [S69][S70]
- zero-emission bus and fleet procurement
- depot charging and targeted freight-corridor charging for trucks, plus rail and logistics upgrades where they cut diesel demand fastest [S30][S32]
- urban design favoring transit, walking, and cycling where density supports it
- zoning / land-use reform for infill, reuse, and transit-oriented development where context supports it, so new housing and service growth do not hard-wire longer trips and higher infrastructure demand [S77][S78][S80]
- city-capable climate-finance and procurement support so municipalities can move buses, retrofits, cooling, water, and resilient public works rather than waiting on fragmented national programs [S77][S79][S80]
- appliance, motor, and building-code ratchets, plus refrigerant-management and servicing rules
- extended producer responsibility, recycling targets, and collection systems for batteries and other key clean-energy materials where scale justifies them [S50]
- fertilizer-efficiency standards and incentives, manure-management programs, rice-methane measures, food-loss/waste reduction plans, and peatland no-drainage / wetland-protection rules in exposed landscapes [S19][S21][S22][S37][S38]
- basin-level drought plans, groundwater-accounting rules, leakage reduction, irrigation modernization, and watershed / aquifer protection in overdrawn regions [S72][S73][S74][S75]

### Fossil moves
- cancel new unabated coal pipelines
- publish plant-by-plant coal retirement pathways
- create coal-closure and site-remediation vehicles, with worker and regional transition funding attached [S24][S25]
- stop using public money to expand long-lived fossil dependence except narrow short-term security backstops
- phase out inefficient fossil subsidies and redirect support to targeted household protection and clean infrastructure rather than structural fossil dependence [S9][S23]

### Resilience moves
- universal heat plans for major cities
- early community-benefit and consultation frameworks for major projects so fast deployment does not collapse into predictable local backlash [S60][S64]
- flood, wildfire, drought, and heat risk mapping tied to actual planning decisions
- water-energy coordination for hydropower, cooling demand, thermal-power water stress, wastewater reuse, and reservoir operations where climate volatility is already visible [S72][S74][S76]
- multi-hazard early warning expansion, especially in vulnerable countries; treat this as core protection infrastructure, not a side program [S3][S15]
- cooling centers, hospital/school heat resilience, and public cooling access in exposed cities
- climate-resilience audits for hospitals, schools, water systems, grids, and transport chokepoints
- no-build / managed-retreat rules where repeat losses make rebuilding irrational, plus disaster-risk financing and contingent finance for rapid recovery [S12][S40][S41]
- open hazard / exposure maps, repeat-loss accounting, and mortgage / appraisal disclosure rules so insurers do not become the only institutions pricing climate reality [S81][S83][S84]

## Phase II: 2028–2030 — Force the bend in the curve

The world should use the COP28 frame as a minimum organizing heuristic: **triple renewable capacity and double energy-efficiency improvement rates by 2030** [S9].

### Core targets
- sharply lower coal generation globally
- make clean electricity additions outpace new demand by a wide margin
- cut connection queues and congestion fast enough that new clean generation actually lands on the system rather than accumulating on paper [S55][S57]
- move road transport, building heat, and much of new cooling demand toward efficient electric systems rather than direct fossil combustion
- reserve scarce low-emissions fuels primarily for aviation, shipping, and the hardest freight uses rather than spraying them across easy road modes [S33][S34][S35]
- cut methane hard enough to produce visible near-term warming benefit [S8][S11]
- lock in tropical forest protection compacts, anti-deforestation enforcement, peatland protection, wetland restoration finance, and watershed-protection rules in heavily stressed basins [S36][S37][S38][S39][S72][S73][S74][S75]
- cut agrifood emissions through lower-loss cold chains, livestock methane programs, better nitrogen use efficiency, and demand-side food measures where politically viable [S19][S20][S21][S22]
- make EMDE clean finance materially cheaper and more available, especially for grids, storage, solar, wind, efficient buildings, and resilient agrifood infrastructure [S13]
- diversify refining, processing, and battery manufacturing enough that one-country chokepoints do not become the new energy-security regime [S47][S48][S49][S50]
- push permit times, queue times, and inspection backlogs down as explicit climate metrics rather than treating them as generic administrative trivia [S59][S60][S61][S62]
- require major metropolitan regions to align housing, transport, building codes, cooling, water, and hazard maps into one urban climate plan rather than letting each lane drift separately [S78][S79][S80]
- create buyout / relocation windows and land-swap tools for repeat-loss zones so retreat, where necessary, can be planned rather than imposed through insurance withdrawal or household insolvency [S81][S83][S84]

### Industrial moves
- green public procurement for steel, cement, and public works, with embodied-carbon limits and standardized definitions for low-emissions and near-zero materials [S26][S27]
- industrial clusters for hydrogen, CCS, and CO2 transport/storage only where residual emissions justify them
- materials-efficiency and circularity mandates in construction and manufacturing, including refurbishment-first and adaptive-reuse rules where feasible [S28][S29]

### Finance moves
- concessional finance and guarantees for emerging markets
- debt, currency, and risk-reduction tools so clean projects outside China can scale much faster [S4][S6][S13]
- adaptation finance that does not simply add more debt to already-exposed countries [S12]
- social-protection upgrades and shock-responsive payment rails so climate shocks and climate-policy costs do not simply fall on exposed households [S71]

## Phase III: 2031–2035 — Consolidate the post-fossil trajectory

### What success looks like by then
- clean power dominates capacity additions and most new final-energy demand
- storage, flexible demand, and interconnection are visibly doing system work rather than remaining boutique pilots
- most new light-duty vehicle sales are zero-emission in major markets
- building heating and cooling loads are increasingly handled by efficient electric systems and passive-design upgrades
- coal is in structural decline almost everywhere
- deforestation is far lower than today
- peatland drainage and wetland loss are no longer treated as normal development defaults
- fertilizer and manure nitrous oxide are on a downward path in major agricultural regions
- food waste is measurably lower in major cities and supply chains
- major urban regions are growing through lower-carbon, lower-exposure patterns rather than continued sprawl and floodplain expansion [S77][S78][S80]
- repeated-loss districts are no longer being rebuilt on autopilot; risk disclosure, buyouts, safer relocation options, and land-use change have started to replace improvised household abandonment [S81][S82][S83][S84]
- first commercial heavy-industry decarbonization pathways are replicating rather than merely piloting
- battery and critical-mineral recycling are materially reducing pressure for new primary extraction and easing financing needs for continued buildout [S50]
- high-integrity CDR exists, but remains much smaller than gross reductions
- early warning, heat resilience, water security, disaster-risk financing, and climate-risk protection are visibly better than they were in the 2020s
- permit authorities, grid operators, and training systems are visibly less backlogged and better staffed than they were in the 2020s
- household energy burdens are lower because buildings, appliances, tariffs, and targeted support were redesigned to lower bills structurally rather than only subsidize them temporarily [S58][S69]

## Strategic note

The decade program is not “solve everything by 2030.”  
It is **lock in an irreversible decline trajectory** by 2030 and make the 2030s a decade of systems replacement rather than marginal greening.

That is the difference between a plausible stabilization path and permanent drift.

## Rev0289 2026–2035 program amendment: nuclear buildability decade

The 2026–2035 program now includes a nuclear buildability track: preserve existing reactors where safe, pursue uprates and life extension, test restarts where workforce and safety cases can be reconstituted, standardize repeat-build procurement, and create open dockets for advanced reactor demonstrations. Program evidence must be fleet-status aware [S777], delivery-risk aware [S771][S776], finance/governance aware [S778], and SMR-readiness aware across licensing, siting, fuel, engagement, supply chain and financing [S780][S784].

## Rev0297 nuclear geopolitical-resilience overlay

The nuclear-positive orientation now includes an explicit geopolitical-resilience burden of proof. Clean-firm nuclear preference is strengthened when it reduces fossil/import/coercion exposure, but maturity is capped by unresolved fuel-cycle concentration, vendor lock-in, sanctions exposure, export-finance/debt risk, safeguards capacity, export-control boundaries, and sensitive-publication controls.


## Rev0298 nuclear human-capital refactor note

The cube's pro-nuclear orientation is now explicitly conditioned by workforce depth, operator licensing, regulator capacity, safety/quality culture, knowledge retention, craft labor, and public training pathways. Nuclear remains favored for clean firm power and system benefit, but human-capital and institutional-capacity gaps are binding maturity caps rather than implementation footnotes. Sources: [S864], [S868], [S871], [S873], [S875].


## Rev0300 upstream uranium accountability note

This revision keeps the cube explicitly nuclear-positive while adding a responsible uranium supply constraint: pro-nuclear planning must not erase mining, milling, tailings, radon, groundwater, host-community, Indigenous-rights, legacy-cleanup, transport, material-provenance, or cumulative public-health burdens. Nuclear remains preferred in relevant clean-firm contexts, but upstream uranium evidence now caps maturity when missing.

## Rev0303 physical-security nuclearization note

Rev0303 keeps nuclear energy explicitly favored while adding a physical-security maturity cap: design-basis-threat protection, force-on-force or equivalent performance testing, insider mitigation, drone/perimeter posture, transport security, law-enforcement coordination, and security-safe publication must be traceable before nuclear security claims can mature.
