# 232 — Ideal Solutions: Methane Cuts Now Set a Large Part of Near-Term Warming Speed Compact Canon

## Claim

The archive already has:
- a deeper methane-and-agrifood note (`08`)
- a hard-conclusions canon (`192`)
- a first-moves canon (`193`)
- and, more recently, tempo-setting notes for electricity, finance, institutions, uptake, protection, and grid hardware (`226`–`231`)

That is already enough to say that methane matters.
What it still lacked was one smallest front-door note for a now-central practical question:

**if the world is already inside the damage era, what still changes the 2030s warming path fast enough to matter in lived time rather than only in end-state accounting?**

This note is not a new doctrinal branch.
It is a compression note.
Its job is to make one practical truth harder to miss at the front door:

**methane cuts now set a large part of near-term warming speed.**

## Fast rule

**Treat methane abatement across fossil fuels, waste, and agriculture as a parallel emergency programme. Because methane is already responsible for a large share of current warming and strong cuts can be made with measures that exist now, delay leaves avoidable 2030s warming, crop loss, and health damage on the board even if long-run CO2 strategy is sound** [S212][S213][S214][S215].

## Why this note is not redundant

- `08-agrifood-methane-and-nitrogen.md` answers **the deeper cross-sector methane-and-agrifood case**
- `192-ideal-solutions-twelve-hard-conclusions-for-actually-solving-global-warming-compact-canon.md` answers **why methane belongs in the non-optional package**

- this note answers something smaller and more front-door:

**why the archive now treats methane cuts as one of the fastest levers on near-term warming itself rather than as a side-benefit lane inside a larger CO2 story.**

## The compact canon

### 1. Methane is not a side pollutant in lived time
UNEP says methane is responsible for **nearly a third of current warming**, while WMO says atmospheric methane concentrations reached an unprecedented high of **1 942 ± 2 ppb in 2024**, around **166% above pre-industrial levels** [S214][S215].
That means methane is no longer best understood only as one term in a greenhouse-gas inventory.
It is part of what sets whether the next two decades become more survivable or more punishing.

House rule: **near-term warming speed is partly methane speed.**

### 2. The fossil-fuel methane opportunity is still scandalously underused
IEA says the fossil-fuel sector is responsible for **nearly one-third** of methane emissions from human activity today and that energy-sector methane emissions remain above **120 Mt** annually [S212][S213].
The same analysis says around **70%** of annual methane emissions from the energy sector could be avoided with existing technologies, yet only around **5%** of global oil and gas output demonstrably meets a near-zero methane standard [S213].
IEA also says total energy-related methane emissions are about **80% higher** than the total countries report to the UNFCCC [S212].

House rule: **do not wait for fossil-fuel demand decline alone to solve fossil methane; run direct methane abatement now.**

### 3. The 2030 methane gap is now clearly an implementation gap
UNEP’s 2025 status report says methane emissions are still rising, and that NDCs and national Methane Action Plans submitted by mid-2025 could translate into only about an **8% cut by 2030 below 2020 levels** if fully implemented [S214].
To reach the Global Methane Pledge goal of a **30%** cut by 2030 below 2020 levels, UNEP says full implementation of maximum technically feasible reductions globally is needed [S214].
The same source says **over 80%** of 2030 emissions-reduction potential can be achieved at low cost, with the energy sector offering **72%** of overall mitigation potential, followed by waste at **18%** and agriculture at **10%** [S214].

House rule: **methane is no longer blocked mainly by missing ideas; it is blocked by slow implementation across sectors.**

### 4. Methane cuts buy time in ways that show up outside climate ledgers
IEA says targeted methane mitigation in the fossil-fuel sector under today’s policies would avoid roughly **0.1 °C** of warming by **2050** [S213].
The same IEA analysis says methane abatement could have made around **100 bcm** of natural gas available to markets in **2024**, while UNEP says full technically feasible methane reductions could prevent over **180 000 premature deaths** and **19 million tonnes of crop losses** every year by **2030** [S213][S214].
UNEP also says all fossil-fuel methane mitigation could be deployed at just **2%** of the sector’s **2023** income [S214].

House rule: **count methane abatement as climate protection, cleaner air, crop protection, and energy-waste reduction at once.**

### 5. Better measurement matters, but imperfect data is no longer an excuse for delay
IEA says more than **25 satellites** can now provide methane insights, that very large leaks detected by satellites hit a record high in **2024**, and that better measurement is improving confidence even though major data gaps remain [S212][S213].
But the same IEA work also says tried-and-tested measures can already be implemented as data quality improves, including leak detection and repair, low-emissions equipment, and restrictions on routine venting and flaring [S212].

House rule: **measure harder, but abate now.**

### 6. Every serious climate programme now needs a methane packet
The archive already says to slash methane as part of the first decade of work.
This note adds the operating corollary: every serious climate programme should now carry an explicit **methane packet** — fossil leak detection and repair, venting and flaring discipline, abandoned-well and abandoned-mine action, landfill-gas and wastewater measures, organic-waste separation and treatment, rice-water management, manure and livestock measures where credible, and measurement systems strong enough to expose large recurrent leaks [S212][S213][S214].

House rule: **no serious climate programme without a methane packet that is specific enough to inspect and hard enough to verify.**

### 7. What this note demotes
This note demotes seven recurring mistakes:
- treating methane as a minor accounting side lane instead of a front-rank near-term warming lever [S214][S215]
- assuming long-run CO2 strategy can substitute for direct methane abatement in the 2020s and 2030s [S212][S213]
- speaking as if methane is mainly an oil-and-gas issue when waste and agriculture also require programme-grade action [S214]
- treating poor data as a reason to wait rather than a reason to measure and abate in parallel [S212][S213]
- celebrating pledges without testing whether implementation plans and verified reductions exist [S212][S214]
- ignoring abandoned wells and mines even though they are now counted as a material source [S212][S213]
- framing health, crop, and wasted-gas gains as side benefits rather than part of the main return on action [S213][S214]
