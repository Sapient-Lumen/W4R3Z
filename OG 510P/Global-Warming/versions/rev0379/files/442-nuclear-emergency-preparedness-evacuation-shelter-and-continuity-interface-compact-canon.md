# Nuclear emergency preparedness, evacuation, shelter, and continuity interface

Nuclear projects touch the same service floors as heat, smoke, outage, flood and evacuation planning. The cube should therefore connect nuclear emergency preparedness to alerting, evacuation, shelter, public health, disability access, language access, continuity of medical care and after-action correction.

NRC has specific emergency-preparedness rulemaking for SMRs and other new technologies [S795]. FEMA preparedness and continuous-improvement materials remain relevant because emergency plans are only real when exercises, corrective actions and retests close the loop [S763][S764]. Civil-rights and language-access duties remain binding in emergency communications and access pathways [S768].

## Rule

No nuclear project can claim local readiness without a tested offsite-response interface covering alerts, protective-action recommendations, transportation-constrained populations, shelter or evacuation options, emergency worker protection, medical/public-health coordination, and after-action corrective actions.

## SMR caution

A smaller emergency-planning zone or alternative rule path cannot be converted into a claim that emergency preparedness is unnecessary. It changes the evidence needed; it does not eliminate the evidence duty.
