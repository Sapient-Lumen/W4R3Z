# Nuclear portfolio sequencing and deployment triage router

The nuclear-positive orientation needs a sequence. The cube should not treat every nuclear option as equally ready, equally cheap, or equally near-term. The default sequence is:

1. keep safe existing reactors running where economics and safety cases are sound;
2. recover output through uprates, power recovery, maintenance and outage discipline;
3. restart retired capacity only when the safety case, workforce, market contract and host-community docket are independently reviewable;
4. build repeatable large-reactor or standardized fleet programs where procurement discipline and risk allocation are visible;
5. deploy SMR and advanced-reactor projects only where licensing, fuel, factory, customer, siting, supply-chain and financing evidence is stronger than promotion.

This is a pro-nuclear sequence, not an anti-SMR sequence. SMRs and advanced reactors remain favored, but they move from marketing claims into the same assurance engine as every other high-stakes service floor. IEA and DOE both frame renewed nuclear momentum alongside delivery, financing and construction risks [S771][S776]. NEA's SMR dashboard logic makes readiness observable across licensing, siting, fuel, engagement, supply chain and financing dimensions [S780][S784][S792]. IEA's nuclear tracking also treats nuclear as a low-emission dispatchable option that can complement renewables where accepted [S790].

## Rule

The cube should prefer nuclear, but prefer **sequenced nuclear** most. A jurisdiction that cannot yet build new reactors can still nuclearize its climate program by preserving existing capacity, preparing uprates, protecting nuclear workforces, pre-permitting clean-firm interconnection, and building public challenge records.

## Anti-crowding-out gate

No nuclear project should be allowed to crowd out cheaper, faster reliability or emissions reductions unless the decision record shows why that project is necessary for the service floor, how interim emissions and reliability are managed, and which delivery risks remain unresolved.
