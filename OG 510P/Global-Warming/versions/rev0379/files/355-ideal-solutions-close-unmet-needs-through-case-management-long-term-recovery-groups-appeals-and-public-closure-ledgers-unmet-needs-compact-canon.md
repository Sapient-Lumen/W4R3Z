# 355 — Ideal Solutions: Close unmet needs through case management, long-term recovery groups, appeals, and public closure ledgers

## Claim

A disaster can be declared over while households still lack safe housing, documents, repairs, transport, income, medicine, school stability, legal help, or grief support. The gap between program closeout and lived recovery is where unmet needs hide.

HUD's Disaster Impact and Unmet Needs Assessment Kit treats unmet-needs assessment as the first phase of long-term recovery program design [S633]. National VOAD's Long-Term Recovery Guide describes community long-term recovery as organizations working together to address needs after disaster [S634]. FEMA's NDRF and Community Recovery Management Toolkit frame recovery as coordinated, fair, effective, and community-managed across systems [S635][S636].

House rule: **do not close recovery because funds are spent; close only because needs are resolved, transferred with accountability, denied with reasons and appeal, or publicly acknowledged as unfunded.**

## Fast rule

Every recovery packet needs a closure ledger: open need, responsible program, referral, document status, denial reason, appeal status, funding gap, safety status, follow-up date, and whether the case can be reopened.

## The compact canon

### 1. Casework is a recovery rail

Households navigate insurance, FEMA, SBA, CDBG-DR, landlords, utilities, schools, health care, legal aid, unemployment, benefits, charities, contractors, and appeals. A case manager or trusted intermediary can turn fragments into a path. Without casework, the most excluded people are told to self-coordinate the hardest bureaucracy of their lives.

### 2. Referrals must be warm enough to count

A phone number is not a referral. A real referral shows contact made, eligibility screened, appointment scheduled, documents transferred with consent, language access provided, transport or digital help arranged, and outcome recorded.

### 3. Appeals are part of the service floor

A denial with no reason, no language access, no document-substitution path, or no help is not due process. Appeal clinics, legal aid, community navigators, and clear denial codes should be planned before deadline waves hit.

### 4. Public dashboards need honest residuals

Unmet-needs dashboards should show needs by category, geography, tenure, disability, language, age, income, race / ethnicity where appropriate and lawful, program status, funding gap, and closure reason. A dashboard that only reports dollars spent can hide failure.

### 5. Reopen paths prevent false finality

Mold appears late. Insurance denies late. Contractors fail. Landlords evict. Health effects emerge. Documentation is found. Family status changes. Recovery programs need a reopen path, not just a closeout gate.

## Minimum packet

An unmet-needs packet includes: disaster impact and unmet-needs assessment; household casework standard; consented data-sharing rules; referral ledger; denial-code and appeal ledger; gap-funding register; closure categories; reopen rule; dashboard; and after-action review of who remained unrecovered after official closeout.

## Bottom line

Recovery is not complete when the archive, government, or grantee can explain what was offered. Recovery is complete only when remaining need is visible, assigned, resolved, appealed, or honestly counted.
