# Nuclear climate, water, thermal, and site-hazard screen

The cube should favor nuclear, but it should not freeze a past-climate siting assumption into a future-lifetime asset. A nuclear site has to be screened against external hazards, climate shift, water availability, thermal discharge, derating risk, emergency access and grid deliverability.

IAEA site-evaluation and design safety standards provide baseline safety posture [S781][S782]. IAEA climate-adaptation work highlights how extreme weather, rising temperatures and changing water availability affect nuclear operation and resilience [S787][S788]. Bulk-power reliability assessments matter because nuclear projects are often justified by adequacy and high-load stress, not only emissions [S789].

## Rule

Every nuclear service floor that depends on a site must carry a site screen covering:

- climate-adjusted flood, drought, heat, storm, wildfire-smoke and seismic/external-hazard assumptions;
- cooling source, water rights, thermal discharge and heatwave derating;
- grid interconnection and deliverability;
- emergency protective-action zones and accessible evacuation/shelter pathways;
- host-community and downstream-water-user challenge rights;
- periodic re-screening as climate observations and models change.

## Cap

If a nuclear option lacks climate-water-site evidence, it remains nuclear-favored as a technology class but cannot be treated as a buildable local project.
