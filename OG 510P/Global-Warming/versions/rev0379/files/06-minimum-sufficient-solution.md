# 06 — Minimum Sufficient Solution

This note asks a stricter question than the rest of the archive:

**What is the smallest package that still plausibly counts as a real solution to global warming, rather than a gesture?**

## Minimum sufficient does not mean idealized perfection

A solution is “minimum sufficient” if it is strong enough to put the world on a durable path toward falling greenhouse-gas emissions, lower fossil dependence, and rising protection against already-locked-in harms.

It does **not** require solving every sector instantly, eliminating every uncertainty, or staying exactly within a rhetorically perfect scenario line.  
It **does** require escaping indefinite emissions drift.

## Five conditions that must all be true

### 1. Clean electricity must grow faster than electricity demand
This is the first non-negotiable condition. If electricity demand rises because of cooling, EVs, industry, and data centres, but clean supply and grids do not outrun that growth, electrification stalls or just shifts emissions around [S4][S5][S14].

So a minimum sufficient climate solution must include:
- very large clean generation buildout
- major transmission and distribution expansion
- storage, demand flexibility, and system balancing
- explicit attack on queue, distribution, and congestion bottlenecks
- credible residual-reliability planning through interconnection and dispatchable low-emissions capacity where needed rather than by default fossil fallback [S55][S57][S58]
- enough copper, transformers, batteries, and processing capacity that buildout is not stranded by avoidable material chokepoints [S47][S48][S50]
- enough permitting, inspection, planning, and workforce capacity that buildout is not stranded by administrative backlog or labour scarcity [S59][S60][S61][S62][S63][S64]
- enough national-city alignment and municipal delivery capacity that urban housing, transport, cooling, and water systems do not keep locking in new demand and new exposure [S77][S79][S80]

### 2. Final energy demand must move away from combustion — and unnecessary demand must be designed down
A world can add renewables quickly and still fail if buildings, vehicles, and industry remain organized around direct fossil combustion. It can also fail if clean supply is forced to chase wasteful service demand that better planning could have prevented.  
That makes electrification, efficiency, and demand shaping inseparable from the power buildout [S1][S4][S97][S98][S99].

The minimum package therefore includes:
- heat pumps, efficient ACs, fan access, and retrofit programs
- mass retrofit and clean-heat delivery in advanced economies, where most 2050 building stock already exists [S52][S53][S54]
- strong codes for new floor area, passive design, and clean-cooking access where service demand is still growing rapidly [S28][S51][S54]
- EVs, buses, rail, and freight-efficiency measures
- compact and connected urban growth where appropriate, so settlements do not lock in avoidable travel demand [S77][S79][S97][S98]
- appliance and building standards
- passive cooling and better urban form where they materially reduce heat stress and energy demand [S16][S17]
- repairability, reuse, and refurbishment rules where they avoid unnecessary new material demand [S26][S97][S99]
- urban growth patterns and municipal planning strong enough that new housing, transport, cooling, and water infrastructure reinforce rather than undermine decarbonization [S77][S78][S79][S80]

### 3. Methane, nitrous oxide, and ecosystem destruction must fall fast this decade
CO2 is the long-arc driver, but methane, nitrous oxide, and ecosystem destruction are too climatically important to leave in a secondary lane. Methane cuts are among the fastest ways to reduce the rate of near-term warming; nitrous oxide is an underweighted but rising agrifood threat; and protection of forests, peatlands, wetlands, and other carbon-rich systems prevents large avoidable emissions while preserving hydrological and ecological stability [S8][S10][S11][S19][S36][S37][S38][S39]. The archive now adds a harder water-security constraint: only about one-third of river basins had normal conditions in 2024, glacier losses continued worldwide, and freshwater reserves have been declining over the past two decades [S72][S73][S75].

The minimum package therefore includes:
- leak detection and repair, venting/flaring bans, and coal-methane action
- agricultural and waste methane measures where feasible
- measurement-based methane rules, response obligations for very large observed emissions events, and public reporting strong enough to distinguish real cuts from paper improvements [S11][S65][S66][S67]
- nitrogen-use-efficiency measures and manure-management reform in major agricultural systems [S19][S22]
- anti-deforestation enforcement, peatland no-drainage rules, and carbon-rich ecosystem protection [S36][S37][S38][S39]
- drought planning, watershed / aquifer protection, groundwater-accounting rules, and water-energy coordination strong enough to keep hydrological instability from cascading into food and power-system failure [S72][S73][S74][S75][S76]

### 4. Fossil decline must be explicit, not assumed
The archive treats this as the line between a real solution and a fantasy solution. If the theory is merely “clean technologies will eventually outcompete fossil fuels,” then the theory has not yet solved lock-in, subsidy, political capture, or long-lived asset expansion.

A minimum sufficient solution therefore requires:
- no large new unabated coal lock-in
- published retirement schedules for existing coal assets
- strong presumption against new long-lived oil and gas dependence
- removal of inefficient fossil subsidies and public support for avoidable expansion [S9]

### 5. Protection and finance must scale with mitigation
A solution that asks vulnerable countries to decarbonize without cheaper finance, or vulnerable communities to wait for future stabilization while present damage intensifies, is not politically or morally sufficient.

So the minimum package must include:
- cheaper clean finance in EMDE through guarantees, concessional capital, lower risk premia, and more local-currency finance [S13][S91][S95][S96]
- finance architecture that respects debt-space constraints rather than assuming vulnerable countries can borrow without limit [S92][S93]
- adaptation finance and implementation that do not intensify debt burdens [S12]
- heat protection, sustainable cooling access, flood protection, drought preparedness, and early warning systems as basic public infrastructure [S3][S15][S16][S72][S73][S74]
- risk-informed infrastructure standards, resilience audits for critical systems, and some form of disaster-risk financing / contingent recovery capacity so the same shocks do not repeatedly destroy the same assets [S40][S41]
- exposure discipline in repeat-loss zones: open risk information, stronger rebuilding conditions, no-build rules where warranted, and public buyout / relocation capacity where protection in place stops being credible [S81][S82][S83][S84]
- a health layer: fast reduction of combustion pollution, worker and household heat protection, and climate-resilient low-carbon health facilities so mitigation is experienced as cleaner air and more reliable care rather than abstraction [S85][S86][S87][S88][S89][S90]
- basic mineral and battery governance: diversified supply chains where possible, scaling recycling, and stronger social/environmental protections so the clean buildout does not stall on legitimacy or concentration [S47][S48][S49][S50]
- a delivery-state layer: faster but credible permitting, better local administrative capacity, workforce training, and visible community benefit so projects can move without collapsing into backlog or backlash [S59][S60][S61][S62][S63][S64]
- a truth-infrastructure layer: interoperable definitions for low- and near-zero-emissions materials where procurement is used, plus hard MRV / liability rules wherever CCS or CDR is claimed [S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S65][S68]
- an affordability-and-access layer: targeted support for vulnerable households, social-protection capacity for shocks and transition costs, and serious electricity / clean-cooking access programs where deprivation remains large [S58][S69][S70][S71]

### 6. Critical-service floors must be named and stress-tested

A minimum sufficient solution must now name the service floors it protects. Climate policy cannot be considered complete if it cleans energy supply but leaves ordinary services brittle under heat, smoke, flood, storm, outage, displacement, disease, price shock, or repeated recovery conditions [S40][S536][S537].

The minimum floor includes water, food, health care, housing / shelter, mobility, communications, household finance, legal access, energy, waste, public safety, emergency response, cooling refuge, family reunification, recovery records, and environmental-health closure. Each floor needs pre-shock capacity, degraded-mode rules, live-shock triage, recovery backlogs, worker protection, rights / proof access, and after-action correction [S538][S539][S540][S545].

This is a sufficiency test, not an encyclopedic expansion rule. A new service-continuity note should be admitted only when it introduces a distinct service floor, a distinct failure mode, a distinct owner / ledger problem, or a distinct shock interaction that the existing archive cannot already route.

## What is not sufficient

The archive rejects the following as insufficient on their own:

### Offsets-first climate strategy
This does not replace infrastructure change or fossil retirement. The same warning now applies to removals-heavy plans without explicit near-term deployment, durable storage, and MRV architecture [S42][S43][S44][S45][S46].

### Net-zero by distant date without strong 2030s decline
This fails the cumulative-emissions test [S1][S2].

### Generation buildout without grids and flexibility
This fails the connection bottleneck test [S14][S55].

### Methane promises without measurement
This fails the enforcement test [S11][S66][S67].

### Low-emissions claims without common definitions
This fails the comparability test [S68].

### Climate policy that preserves wasteful baseline demand as if cleaner substitution alone were enough
This fails the design test. A package that electrifies and cleans supply but keeps locking in avoidable travel, oversized material demand, short product lifetimes, and unnecessary floor-area / cooling loads is not minimum sufficient because it makes the rest of the transition harder and more expensive than it has to be [S58][S97][S98][S99].

### Climate policy that preserves or worsens household exposure
This fails the legitimacy test. A package that is clean on paper but unaffordable, inaccessible, or badly targeted is not minimum sufficient because it will not travel politically or socially far enough [S58][S69][S70][S71].

### Climate policy that leaves dirty air, deadly heat, and brittle care systems untouched
This fails the health test. A package that lowers tonnes on paper but leaves combustion pollution, dangerous heat exposure, and climate-vulnerable care systems outside the core design is still under-specified as a real-world solution [S85][S86][S87][S88][S89][S90].

### Rebuilding repeated-loss zones as if the baseline were stable
This fails the exposure-discipline test. A package that decarbonizes upstream systems but keeps recreating household, insurer, and public-budget exposure in flood, fire, erosion, or other repeatedly damaged zones is still not minimum sufficient [S81][S82][S83][S84].

### Adaptation without mitigation
This fails the stabilization test.

### CDR as the main plan
This fails the timing and scale test [S1].

## Compression

The minimum sufficient global solution is not mysterious:

**Build a lot of clean electricity and grid capacity; build the permitting, planning, and workforce capacity needed to install it; electrify, increase efficiency, and reduce unnecessary service demand faster than wasteful baseline growth; shape cities so housing, transport, cooling, and water systems stop locking in new emissions and new exposure; make buildings a real delivery lane through codes, retrofits, clean heat, and durable envelopes; secure the mineral and battery inputs that buildout requires while using repair, reuse, and refurbishment to reduce avoidable throughput; slash methane, nitrous oxide, and ecosystem destruction with real measurement and enforcement; force explicit fossil decline; make the cleaner system affordable and accessible to ordinary households; lower the cost of clean finance in EMDE through guarantees, local-currency finance, and catalytic concessional support that respects debt-space limits; cut dirty-air exposure fast, protect workers and households from dangerous heat, harden health systems for a hotter world, and make thermal survivability inspectable; stop repeated-loss zones from being rebuilt on autopilot; protect public safety, emergency response, family reunification, environmental health, coastal / ocean systems, and other critical service floors under degraded conditions; quarantine carbon-market claims that cannot pass additionality, permanence, accounting, rights, leakage, and liability tests; and protect vulnerable societies with finance, social protection, and adaptation at the same time.**

Anything materially weaker than that is not yet a solution. It is a hope. A plan that depends on large future removals without a believable residuals doctrine is also still only a hope [S42][S43][S44][S45][S46].

## Rev0270 minimum-sufficiency patch

A minimally sufficient climate solution now has to pass eight extra checks:

1. it maps critical-service interdependencies before shocks turn them into cascades;
2. it treats cryosphere, permafrost, glacier, ocean, and sea-level commitments as present planning constraints;
3. it cuts SLCPs beyond methane tunnel vision, including black carbon, tropospheric ozone precursors, and HFCs;
4. it protects breathable air during smoke, heat, outage, pollution, and recovery;
5. it governs plastics and petrochemicals as fossil-demand, waste, toxic-exposure, and worker-safety systems;
6. it applies gendered-safety, maternal-health, reproductive-health, and GBV proof lenses to every service floor;
7. it protects people in custody and closed institutions who cannot self-evacuate or self-protect;
8. it funds the local administrative, fiscal, records, and procurement capacity that makes service floors real.

These are not substitutes for clean power, electrification, methane cuts, land protection, finance, efficiency, or fossil decline. They are maturity checks that keep those pillars from failing in the lived systems they are meant to protect.


## rev0271 hidden-rails corollary

rev0271 adds the hidden rails beneath service floors: medical products and oxygen, digital infrastructure and cyber recovery, payment rails and cash access, humanitarian logistics, displacement / receiving capacity, climate-conflict integrity, and readiness assurance. The archive should not treat these as support functions. They are the difference between a service floor that exists on paper and a service floor that works under outage, heat, smoke, flood, conflict, cyber disruption, aid scarcity, or displacement [S564][S565][S567][S568][S570][S571][S573][S574][S575].

New rule: **every continuity packet must identify hidden rails, degraded modes, and assurance tests before it can be marked ready.**

## Rev0272 minimum addition — the recovery rails must work

The minimum sufficient solution now includes a recovery-rail test: emergency energy inputs, floodwater controls, safe repair capacity, claims / assistance cashflow, essential local enterprises, trusted community bridges, agrifood input continuity, and proof capacity for labs / inspection / sampling must keep working when climate shocks hit.

A program that can build clean infrastructure but cannot safely repair, verify, reopen, compensate, feed, power, or communicate after shocks is not sufficient.

## Rev0273 minimum-sufficiency patch — name the bad-day operating rules

A minimum sufficient climate solution must now name its bad-day operating rules. It is not sufficient to list service floors. The programme must also say how scarce restoration is prioritized, how mutual aid works when neighbors are also hit, where critical spares and consumables come from, whether baseline assets are maintained, how psychosocial recovery is supported, how elections and public participation survive disruption, how reentry / credentialing avoids blocking care and repair, and which compound loadcases can demote a packet from ready to conditional [S587][S590][S592][S593][S595][S598][S600][S602].

## Rev0274 minimum addition — safe access is part of sufficiency

A minimally sufficient solution now includes a final access test: **can people use the service floor without being harmed by the interface?** A shelter that exposes people to heat, smoke, violence, infection, family separation, or inaccessible toilets is not a refuge. A registry that creates surveillance risk is not care. Recovery money sent into a fraud-saturated repair market is not recovery. Evacuation without pets, service animals, transport, rural access, fatality dignity, and cultural continuity is not protection [S603][S605][S606][S607][S608][S612][S614][S617].


## Rev0275 minimum-sufficient clause

A minimally sufficient climate solution must now include a rebuild-authority floor: property and occupancy proof, managed-retreat rules, emergency procurement integrity, reimbursement and match capacity, enforceable and financed safe-rebuild codes, essential-worker sustainment, shared-housing governance, and unmet-needs closure. Without these, the service-floor system names recovery but cannot reliably turn loss into safe, equitable, audited repair.

## Rev0276 minimum-sufficient addition

A minimally sufficient climate program now needs a scored service-floor layer: every critical service must be owned, current, tested under compound loadcases, financeable under fiscal stress, visible to insurance / market-exit signals, workable across borders or basins where relevant, and accessible to informal, undocumented, unbanked, disabled, remote, and employer-controlled users.

## Rev0277 minimum addition

A minimally sufficient climate solution must include a public proof layer. It must make service floors queryable, observable, dashboarded, rumor-aware, OT-safe, toxic-site-aware, disease-surveillance-aware, and publicly contestable. Without that layer, readiness scores and recovery ledgers can become stale authority rather than live correction [S638][S653][S667].

## Rev0278 addition — minimum forecast-to-action rule

A minimally sufficient climate solution must make warnings actionable. For predictable hazards, the minimum floor is an owned trigger, an action bundle, money or procurement authority, accessible alerting, an excluded-user path, degraded-mode operations, and a false-alarm / missed-alarm review. Route to `372`–`380`.

## Rev0279 minimum floor — assessment, intake, and authority must work after impact

A minimally sufficient climate programme now includes an impact-to-intake floor: incident coordination, rapid damage / needs assessment, public restoration clocks, safe-return decisions, survivor intake, commodities, volunteer / donation routing, and rights-constrained emergency authority. Without those, anticipatory action may fire correctly but recovery will still fail at the first post-impact handoff.

## Rev0280 addition — stabilization-to-recovery pathways

rev0280 adds the rule that impact-to-intake is not recovery. A person can be registered, sheltered, assessed, or referred and still remain stranded. Climate service floors therefore need stabilization pathways: interim-to-durable housing, displaced-student stability, chronic-care continuity, nutrition-benefit continuity, utility-arrears protection, deadline tolling, and no-wrong-door benefit sequencing [S698][S699][S712].

The cube should now ask not only whether a service exists, but whether a household can move from first contact to a durable outcome without losing school, medicine, food, utilities, legal rights, or case status along the way.

## Rev0281 minimum condition — functional recovery and nonrecurrence

A minimum sufficient climate solution must not count recovery as complete at registration, shelter exit, assistance award, or case closure. It must prove household function after the shock: safe housing, renter rights, income or benefit path, debt and credit protection, school, medicine, food, utilities, recovery mobility, essential devices and property, document / account access, and a reopen path. Closeout must also transfer residual risk to mitigation, retreat, maintenance, standards, budgets, or accountable owners so the same failure is not rebuilt [S699][S714][S715][S716].

## Rev0282 minimum addition — no-new-risk and performance monitoring

A minimally sufficient climate solution must now include a post-closeout risk-reduction loop. The minimum is not only to help households recover; it is to stop the same hazard from being publicly financed again. That requires a mitigation pipeline, repetitive-loss register, future-condition maps and disclosure, resilience finance stack, equity-aware benefit-cost test, no-new-risk land-use control, nature-based-solution lifecycle governance, and post-project performance monitoring [S727][S728][S733][S736][S738].

## Rev0283 addendum — adaptation must be path-governed

The canon now treats adaptation and resilience as pathway-governed rather than project-governed. A mature programme must expose signposts, thresholds, model assumptions, option value, lock-in risks, portfolio stress tests, service-level contracts, unsafe-asset exits, and professional duties. Otherwise recovery can rebuild, mitigation can fund projects, and dashboards can look current while the system still commits to a brittle future [S741][S743][S745].

## Rev0284 addendum — minimum sufficiency includes proof

A minimally sufficient climate solution now also needs proof discipline: normalized data, reliable controls, corrective-action closure, independent challenge, open delivery ledgers, civil-rights access gates, workforce succession, and assurance cases for high-stakes service floors. Otherwise the programme may name the right services while failing to deliver them to actual people under stress [S761][S763][S770].
