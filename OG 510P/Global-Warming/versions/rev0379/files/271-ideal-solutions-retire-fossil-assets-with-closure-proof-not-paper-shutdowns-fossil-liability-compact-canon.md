# 271 — Ideal Solutions: Retire Fossil Assets with Closure Proof, Not Paper Shutdowns — Fossil-Liability Compact Canon

## Claim

Rev0253 made place and host-community legitimacy explicit.
The next missing layer is the dirty place left behind.

A fossil asset is not retired merely because it stops selling energy, loses dispatch, files a closure notice, transfers ownership, enters bankruptcy, or disappears from a company's transition plan.
Coal mines, coal plants, oil and gas wells, gas processing sites, refineries, pipelines, terminals, ash ponds, tailings, contaminated soils, produced-water systems, abandoned boreholes, and fossil transport assets can keep imposing climate, safety, water, health, land, fiscal, and community burdens after production slows or ends.

The archive therefore needs a closure-proof rule: **dirty retirement must include physical closure, methane control, environmental remediation, worker and community transition, financial assurance, and post-closure monitoring, not only emissions accounting or asset sale** [S11][S24][S25][S341][S342][S343][S344][S347][S349][S350].

## Fast rule

**Every fossil-retirement, fossil-subsidy, asset-transfer, decommissioning, transition-credit, or public-backstop packet needs a fossil-liability ledger before it is called ready.**

That ledger should name:

1. the physical asset, connected assets, owner, operator, parent, secured lenders, insurers, buyers, landholder, regulator, and affected communities;
2. the retirement event: closure, non-renewal, licence expiry, regulated phase-out, early retirement, transfer, bankruptcy, repurposing, mothballing, or emergency extension;
3. methane, CO2, local air pollution, water, soil, subsidence, fire, explosion, leakage, tailings, ash, waste, and safety hazards that persist after production falls;
4. the closure standard and who can inspect it;
5. financial assurance, bonding, escrow, insurance, levy, royalty set-aside, parent guarantee, or public-backstop rule;
6. worker, pension, contractor, supplier, local-government, and host-community obligations;
7. remediation, plugging, sealing, dismantling, waste handling, site stabilization, monitoring, and reporting duties;
8. asset-transfer restrictions, bankruptcy safeguards, beneficial-ownership disclosure, and anti-dumping rules;
9. proof that the dirty service is actually displaced rather than sold, exported, reopened, relabelled, or replaced by another fossil default;
10. what happens if the owner fails, the proof fails, emissions continue, or harms emerge after closure.

No closure ledger, no mature retirement packet.

## Compact canon

### 1. Retirement is physical, legal, financial, and social

A serious fossil-retirement packet closes four things at once:

- **physical operation** — production, combustion, transport, venting, flaring, or dirty service falls;
- **legal permission** — permits, subsidies, capacity payments, licences, and extensions do not quietly preserve the dirty path;
- **financial exposure** — closure costs, environmental liabilities, and social obligations do not migrate silently to households, municipalities, landholders, workers, or taxpayers;
- **social dependence** — workers, local governments, suppliers, and host regions get formed transition pathways before the asset's revenue floor disappears.

If only the first one happens, the programme has shut a facility, not retired a system.

### 2. Paper shutdown can hide physical leakage

Abandoned and inactive fossil infrastructure can remain climate-relevant.
IEA's 2026 methane tracker estimates energy-sector methane emissions of nearly 150 Mt in 2025, with coal production, oil operations, gas activities, inactive mines, end-use equipment, and abandoned wells all contributing [S341].
IEA also finds that a large share of fossil methane can be abated with existing technology, and that more than 35 Mt could be avoided at no net cost under 2025 energy-price assumptions [S342].

So retirement doctrine must ask not only whether the asset is economically dead, but whether it is **sealed, measured, monitored, and prevented from continuing to leak danger**.

### 3. Closure must be funded before the revenue disappears

The worst time to discover a closure bill is after the owner is insolvent and the host region has lost its tax base.

A fossil-liability ledger should therefore prefer:

- full-cost financial assurance before operation or extension;
- periodic bond updates as closure standards and risks change;
- parent-company guarantees where ownership structures are thin;
- dedicated closure escrow or trust funds that survive bankruptcy;
- sector levies for unknown, historic, or orphan liabilities;
- transfer restrictions that prevent dumping depleted assets onto weak operators;
- public backstops only after private responsibility is exhausted and recovery routes remain open.

Polluter pays does not mean the public never acts.
It means the public does not become the first and quietest payer while private actors keep the upside [S349].

### 4. Asset transfer is a climate-control point

Dirty assets often become most dangerous when they are sold late in life.
A sale can move the asset from a large operator with balance-sheet capacity to a smaller operator with thinner capital, shorter time horizons, weaker compliance systems, or a plan to extract residual value while leaving closure risk behind.

Any sale, spin-off, lease, licence transfer, restructuring, or bankruptcy filing should trigger:

- beneficial-ownership disclosure;
- updated closure-cost estimate;
- methane and environmental baseline inspection;
- financial-assurance top-up;
- worker and community notice;
- regulator power to block or condition the transfer;
- public ledger update;
- anti-avoidance review for related-party transfers.

A fossil asset should not be allowed to become orphaned by paperwork.

### 5. Mine closure is more than blocking the entrance

World Bank coal-transition work emphasizes that sustainable mine closure requires good-practice technical standards, post-closure risk management, people-and-community measures, and repurposing plans; it also distinguishes simple physical closure from closure that supports sustainable development [S343][S344][S346].

A serious mine-closure packet should include:

- hydrology and acid-drainage control;
- subsidence and geotechnical safety;
- fugitive methane assessment and mitigation;
- waste-rock, tailings, and spoil management;
- landform stabilization;
- worker and contractor transition;
- local-government revenue transition;
- community-governed reuse options;
- long-term monitoring and maintenance;
- proof that the site is safe, stable, and usable for its agreed post-closure purpose.

### 6. Oil and gas closure must find the missing wells

Documented wells are not the whole universe.
DOE notes that orphaned wells are idle wells whose operator is unknown or insolvent, and that U.S. states report between 210,000 and 746,000 undocumented orphan wells with missing location or construction information [S347].

A serious oil-and-gas closure packet should include:

- well inventory and location work;
- undocumented-well detection;
- leak screening and prioritization;
- plugging and site restoration;
- post-plug verification;
- public records that survive operator exit;
- worker redeployment into plugging, monitoring, and remediation crews;
- rules preventing new orphaning as marginal assets change hands.

### 7. Transition credits, buyouts, and early-retirement deals need closure terms

A coal-to-clean finance deal, transition credit, securitization, regulated asset write-down, or public-interest buyout is incomplete if it pays for early closure while leaving:

- methane leakage;
- mine or ash hazards;
- land and water contamination;
- pension exposure;
- tax-base collapse;
- workers without pathways;
- communities without reuse rights;
- owners free to reinvest proceeds in new fossil expansion elsewhere.

The price of early retirement should buy actual displacement and actual closure, not just a spreadsheet retirement date.

### 8. Closure proof should be public enough to inspect

The minimum public record should include:

- asset identity and map;
- closure date and legal status;
- owner and responsible parties;
- closure standard;
- financial assurance and public backstop exposure;
- methane / pollution baseline and post-closure measurements;
- worker and community measures;
- reuse status;
- inspection results;
- unresolved claims;
- long-term monitoring owner.

Sensitive security information may need protection, but “commercial confidentiality” should not hide public liabilities.

### 9. Red flags

Pause or rewrite a fossil-retirement packet when:

- closure is described only as reduced output, sale, mothballing, or accounting treatment;
- asset transfers occur without liability top-ups;
- public money pays for retirement without remediation, worker, community, and non-reinvestment conditions;
- closure funds can be raided or subordinated in bankruptcy;
- methane measurement stops when production stops;
- undocumented wells, abandoned boreholes, mine methane, ash ponds, tailings, and contaminated water are outside scope;
- communities inherit risk while prior owners retain value;
- cleanup is postponed until “after transition”;
- transition plans count emissions reductions but not residual hazards.

### 10. Completion condition

A fossil asset is retired only when the dirty service is displaced, legal permission and subsidy support are closed or sunset, physical hazards are sealed or controlled, liabilities are funded, affected workers and communities have real transition pathways, and post-closure evidence remains visible long enough for late failures to be corrected.

## Preferred use

Use this canon when the live question is about:

- coal, oil, gas, refinery, pipeline, terminal, or mine retirement;
- fossil-asset sales, spin-offs, bankruptcies, or ownership transfers;
- orphan wells, abandoned mines, methane leakage, ash, tailings, or contaminated sites;
- transition credits, coal-to-clean finance, securitization, or early-retirement deals;
- polluter-pays rules, closure bonds, financial assurance, or public backstops;
- whether fossil decline is real or merely booked.

## Compression rule

**Retirement is not paper exit. A dirty asset is not done until the service is replaced, the licence and subsidy path are closed, methane and hazards are controlled, cleanup is funded, workers and regions are protected, and proof survives ownership change, bankruptcy, and time.**
