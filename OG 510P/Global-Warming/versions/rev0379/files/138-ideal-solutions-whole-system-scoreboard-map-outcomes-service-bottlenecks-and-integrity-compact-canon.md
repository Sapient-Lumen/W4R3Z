## The compact canon

### 1. Put physical climate outcomes at the top of the scoreboard
The first question is still atmospheric and biophysical:
- are gross greenhouse-gas emissions falling fast enough
- are methane super-emitters being found and fixed
- are fossil assets retiring, shrinking, or being prevented from locking in
- are ecosystems that matter for climate stability being protected or restored
- are carbon removals, if used, actually durable and tightly bounded

If those outcomes are weak, the rest of the scoreboard should not be allowed to flatter the programme.

### 2. Watch whether the clean system is actually replacing the dirty one
The second question is substitution and connection:
- is clean power being connected, not merely proposed
- is grid capacity being expanded where it is actually needed
- are efficient cooling, clean heat, retrofits, and electrified transport actually displacing fossil service
- are lower-emissions industrial options actually being bought, connected, and used

This is the layer that distinguishes transition theatre from real system turnover.

### 3. Watch whether people experience safer, cheaper, more reliable daily life
The third question is lived service and protection:
- are households seeing lower energy burden or lower fuel-price exposure
- are heat, flood, fire, water, health, and insurance risks being reduced where danger is already real
- is cleaner service ordinary enough to be felt as comfort, safety, reliability, and access rather than as abstract virtue

A serious programme should not count as healthy if tonnes improve while bills, vulnerability, or service experience visibly degrade.

### 4. Watch whether the next bottleneck is actually loosening
The fourth question is delivery throughput:
- are grid queues shrinking where they are the true choke point
- are permitting, procurement, workforce, finance, and administrative frictions being released
- are the measures that unblock the next wave of projects arriving before the queue simply refills at a higher level

A mature programme can look busy and still be stuck. The scoreboard has to show whether the choke point is moving.

### 5. Watch integrity outcomes, not just output outcomes
The fifth question is whether the truth layer is still holding:
- are headline claims auditable
- are methane alerts answered quickly enough to matter
- are removals, storage, labels, and low-emissions product claims staying inside interoperable, enforceable rules
- are underperforming programmes corrected quickly instead of being cosmetically re-described

A programme that improves some outputs while letting comparability, auditability, or remedy collapse is storing a later legitimacy failure.

### 6. Treat inputs and announcements as secondary evidence only
Money spent, subsidies announced, strategies published, pipelines declared, and credits issued still matter.
But they matter mainly when they help explain movement in the higher layers above.
They are not the main proof that climate progress is real.

### 7. Read the scoreboard as a hierarchy, not a bucket of equal metrics
The archive's smallest scoreboard hierarchy is:
1. physical climate outcomes
2. replacement and connection outcomes
3. service and protection outcomes
4. bottleneck-release outcomes
5. integrity outcomes
6. supporting input and announcement metrics

That ordering keeps the archive physical-first while still seeing lived service, throughput constraints, and truth-system health.
