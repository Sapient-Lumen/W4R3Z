That is enough doctrine.
What the archive still lacked was one smallest front-door answer to the practical question that now matters most:

**what needs to be done next, once the high-level answer is known?**

This note is not another sector doctrine.
It is an execution note.
Its job is to turn the archive’s answer into a governed work queue: named physical outcomes, named bottlenecks, named owners, named replacement events, named protection duties, and named proof that the dirty system is actually shrinking.

Current evidence makes this sharper rather than softer. IEA’s 2026 global energy review says clean growth is now physically large — solar PV met more than a quarter of higher energy demand in 2025, low-emissions sources supplied nearly 60% of demand growth, annual renewable capacity additions reached about 800 GW, and battery-storage additions reached almost 110 GW — but energy-related CO2 emissions still rose by around 0.4%, demand for oil, gas, and coal all still grew, and electricity demand grew at well over twice the rate of total energy demand [S247]. IEA’s 2026 methane tracker adds the fast-warming version of the same problem: there is still no sign that fossil-fuel methane emissions fell in 2025, even though about 70% of fossil-fuel methane emissions can be abated with existing technology and more than 35 Mt could be avoided at no net cost [S248].

So the next work is not to admire the transition.
The next work is to make clean growth displace dirty systems, make fast-warming emissions fall, make protection arrive before losses compound, and make delivery bottlenecks loosen faster than new load, heat, politics, and incumbency can harden them.

Rev0241 adds the scarce-capacity implication. IEA's 2026 electricity outlook says more than 2 500 GW of renewable, large-load, and storage projects are stalled in grid queues worldwide and that grid buildout often takes 5–15 years while data centres and other new loads can arrive much faster [S252]. IEA's 2026 data-centre analysis says data-centre electricity demand rose 17% in 2025 and is set to double by 2030, while AI-focused data-centre power use is poised to triple [S253]. So the work queue also needs allocation rules: scarce grid headroom, clean power, hardware, finance, land, water, and staff time should go first to packets that cut danger, retire dirty service, protect people, release bottlenecks, and replicate clean defaults; see `245` and `246` [S252][S253].

Rev0242 adds the readiness implication. A work queue and an allocation rule do not by themselves create absorbable projects. IEA, CPI, World Bank, and MIGA evidence now point to a practical conversion layer: prepare project shelves and procurement platforms so finance, guarantees, grid capacity, recovery money, and public demand can move into real packets without bespoke reinvention each time; see `247` and `248` [S254][S255][S256][S257][S258].

Rev0243 adds the human and persistence implication. Prepared packets and procurement platforms still fail if they assume crews, public offices, inspectors, operators, maintainers, commissioning systems, warranties, spare parts, and user support that do not exist. So the work queue now needs a crew-and-capability lane and an aftercare / stable-service lane; see `249` and `250` [S176][S178][S259][S260].

Rev0244 adds the bad-day implication. A packet that is ready for normal operation may still fail under heat, outage, flood, fire, smoke, drought, cyber, supply-chain, staff, or restoration stress. So the work queue now needs a stress-test lane and a critical-service continuity lane; see `251` and `252` [S40][S261][S262][S263].

## Why this note is not redundant

- `193` answers **what the first serious decade of work should prioritize**.
- `207` answers **how to tell whether the programme is working**.
- `200`–`203` answer **how one verified replacement becomes many and then becomes the default**.
- `226`–`241` answer **which tempo-setting bottlenecks have become too important to leave in the background**.
- this note answers the next operator question:

**how should all of that be held as a live queue of work rather than as a pile of true sentences?**

The difference matters.
A doctrine can be right and still fail if it does not become budget lines, grid-connection orders, closure schedules, building-code updates, procurement templates, methane-response crews, retrofit offers, water and heat protection plans, insurance and retreat rules, social-protection flows, and inspection ledgers.

House rule: **every canonical conclusion should eventually cash out as a work packet or a no-go rule; otherwise it is still commentary.**

## The work queue in one view

A serious climate programme should carry at least these standing queues at the same time:

| Queue | The job | First proof that it is real |
|---|---|---|
| Clean backbone | Build and connect clean power, grids, storage, flexible demand, efficient buildings, clean heat, clean cooling, and charging fast enough to carry real load | Shorter queues, connected capacity, falling curtailment where it is wasteful, lower peak stress, faster interconnection, and clean service actually used |
| Dirty-default retirement | Make coal, oil, gas, inefficient equipment, leaky assets, high-carbon materials, and dirty fallback rules lose ground | Retirements, denied reinvestment, tighter standards, fossil-support phase-down, and measured gross emissions falling |
| Fast-warming cuts | Cut methane, nitrous oxide, refrigerants, black-carbon-heavy combustion, waste methane, and agricultural losses fast | Measured methane down, super-emitter response working, flaring and venting falling, fertilizer and manure losses falling, refrigerant leakage controlled |
| Demand relief | Reduce avoidable energy, peak, material, land, and transport demand while protecting service floors | Lower same-service load, stronger codes, passive design, retrofits, efficient equipment, proximity, modal shift, and less luxury waste |
| Protection | Keep people alive, housed, cooled, warned, insured where possible, relocated where necessary, and supplied with water and care | Early-warning coverage, heat-health systems, resilient care and water, repeat-loss rules, safer rebuilding, and dignified retreat pathways |
| Water and WASH continuity | Keep safe drinking water, sanitation, hygiene, water quality, utility continuity, groundwater, allocation, and drought / flood shock absorption formed as operating packets | Water-continuity ledgers, basin / aquifer accounts, priority-use rules, WASH service floors, quality monitoring, demand / loss controls, and after-action rule changes |
| Finance, shelf, and capacity | Lower cost of capital, expand guarantees and local-currency finance, staff the delivery state, prepare ready project shelves, and standardize procurement platforms | Projects reach financial close, public agencies have capacity, training ladders fill, contracts repeat, high-value packets are window-ready, and EMDE finance rises in usable form |
| Crews and capability | Grow the workers, public offices, inspectors, operators, maintainers, and local delivery partners needed before packets call for them | Training seats, certifications, placement, permit staffing, inspector capacity, and contractor quality improve ahead of deployment |
| Commissioning and aftercare | Make stable clean service the completion gate rather than first installation or first contract | Commissioning, O&M, warranty, spare parts, user support, callbacks, and 6/12/24-month performance are tracked and corrected |
| Minerals and hardware | Keep buildout from stalling on copper, refining, transformers, cables, conductors, batteries, and opaque supply chains | Copper and grid-hardware plans, diversified refining, recycling systems, take-back pathways, traceability where it matters, and procurement that values resilience |
| Scarce-capacity allocation | Decide which packets get constrained grid capacity, finance, hardware, staff, land, water, clean power, and attention first | Public allocation ledger, priority rules, demotion of weak claims, and bottleneck capacity going to replacement, protection, flexibility, and replication |
| Large-load entry | Make data centres, AI campuses, electrolysers, depots, mines, ports, factories, and cooling districts enter as grid citizens rather than passive claims | Efficiency, locational discipline, additional clean supply, flexibility, fair grid-cost contribution, water / heat disclosure, and narrow fossil fallback |
| Hard residuals | Reserve scarce fuels, CCS, biomass, and removals for uses without better substitutes | Allocation rules, lifecycle proof, storage integrity, no easy-use leakage, and no fossil-delay theatre |
| Truth and correction | Measure physical outcomes, enforce claims, and correct weak signals before they become failure loops | Public scoreboards, satellite and field response, product definitions, audited ledgers, and visible correction when scorelines weaken |
| Data spine and proof passports | Link records across finance, permits, procurement, assets, claims, risks, supports, remedies, and life-cycle handoffs | Persistent identifiers, data stewards, access rules, privacy / security protections, proof passports, and correction triggers that survive handoff |
| Circular design and end-of-life | Make clean assets durable, repairable, reusable, recoverable, and safe to retire before hardware scale locks in waste | Repairability, spare parts, software support, product passports, take-back finance, reverse logistics, decommissioning owners, recycling capacity, recovered-material offtake, and asset-closure proof |

This table is not a programme plan by itself.
It is the minimum skeleton that keeps the answer from dissolving into separate sector speeches.

House rule: **if a national, regional, city, utility, or institutional plan cannot place its actions into this queue, it is probably not formed enough to inspect.**

## The compact canon

### 1. Name the physical replacement, not only the policy instrument
The unit of work is not “a subsidy,” “a target,” “a pilot,” or “a strategy.”
The unit of work is a verified change at a real control point: a coal unit retired, a feeder upgraded, a heat pump installed and performing, a cooling standard enforced, a bus lane carrying trips, a methane leak stopped, a permit queue cleared without sacrificing integrity, a floodplain rule preventing repeat loss, a procurement rule creating a market for cleaner material, or a finance guarantee lowering the cost of capital for a real project [S200][S201][S202][S203][S207].

This means the programme should state every important action in the form:

**replace / retire / protect / reduce / finance / verify _what_, at _which control point_, by _whom_, by _when_, measured by _which physical outcome_?**

House rule: **rewrite vague measures until they name the physical state that changes.**

### 2. Run clean buildout and dirty retirement as one coupled queue
The archive’s central anti-confusion rule is that clean additions are not the same thing as decarbonization.
A country can build solar and still grow fossil demand.
A city can electrify new buildings and still let inefficient old equipment cycle back in at replacement moments.
A company can buy certificates and still leave high-emissions production unchanged.
IEA’s 2026 energy review makes the point globally: clean deployment is now enormous, but energy-related CO2 still rose by around 0.4% in 2025 and demand for all three fossil fuels still grew [S247].

So every clean-build queue needs a paired retirement queue:
- what dirty capacity loses dispatch, subsidy, permit, renewal, or market share when the clean service arrives?
- what reinvestment is disallowed because a cleaner default is now ready?
- what fallback is allowed only under emergency conditions, with a return-to-routine test?
- what gross-emissions line proves that the clean build is taking work away from combustion rather than merely adding another layer of supply?

House rule: **never let “clean capacity added” stand alone when the live question is whether the dirty system is shrinking.**

### 3. Treat bottleneck release as first-order climate work
The next climate gains are blocked less by a lack of ideas than by throughput limits: grid queues, permitting offices, local planning capacity, transformers, conductors, trained installers, inspection slots, project-preparation teams, public procurement skill, social license, and finance able to carry high-upfront-cost clean assets in ordinary places [S132][S170][S176][S197][S198][S226][S228][S231][S241].

That means a serious work queue should include boring capacity releases beside famous technology deployments:
- feeder and substation upgrade calendars
- queue reform and connection deadlines
- standard designs for retrofits, charging, cooling, and public procurement
- training ladders for installers, planners, maintainers, inspectors, and caseworkers
- prequalified contractor pools and performance-backed contracts
- component and spare-part strategies for transformers, cables, conductors, switchgear, heat pumps, efficient cooling, chargers, and batteries
- digital permitting and case-management tools that shorten lawful process instead of weakening the law

House rule: **do not call a plan serious if the bottleneck-release queue is smaller than the deployment promise.**

### 4. Make fast-warming gases a delivery emergency, not a symbolic side lane
Methane, nitrous oxide, and refrigerants deserve a queue with the urgency of their atmospheric leverage.
IEA’s 2026 methane tracker says there is still no sign that fossil-fuel methane emissions fell in 2025; fossil-fuel operations alone emit about 124 Mt a year, around 70% of fossil-fuel methane can be abated with existing technology, and more than 35 Mt could be avoided at no net cost [S248].
The archive’s nitrogen and cooling notes add the parallel point: fertilizer and manure discipline, refrigerant management, passive design, and high-efficiency cooling are not peripheral virtues but near-term climate, health, food, peak-load, and protection work [S216][S217][S218][S219][S232][S233][S234].

The fast-warming queue should therefore carry:
- satellite and field response to super-emitters
- bans or tight limits on routine venting and flaring
- leak detection and repair with repair deadlines
- methane intensity standards and procurement rules for fossil supply that still exists during decline
- waste-sector methane capture and organic-waste diversion
- rice, livestock, manure, and fertilizer packages where they protect food security rather than punish farmers blindly
- refrigerant recovery, leakage control, and transition rules tied to cooling access and efficiency

House rule: **run methane and other fast-warming cuts on delivery clocks measured in months and a few years, not only on 2050 pathway charts.**

### 5. Put same-service demand relief before oversized supply
The programme should not try to brute-force every avoidable load with more generation, wires, minerals, and public money.
Efficiency, passive design, retrofits, repair, better appliance standards, high-performing cooling, proximity, transit-supportive growth, logistics improvement, and sufficiency rules for luxury waste all reduce the size, cost, and fragility of the rest of the transition [S97][S98][S99][S161][S173][S237][S238][S239][S240].

This is not austerity as climate policy.
The governing distinction is service.
The programme should protect or improve warmth, cooling, mobility, food, housing, care, communication, and ordinary economic participation while reducing avoidable throughput, peak stress, and wasteful high-end demand.

House rule: **first ask whether the same or better service can be delivered with less load, material, land, and peak stress before sizing the clean-supply answer.**

### 6. Treat adaptation as civil protection inside the main queue
Protection is not an admission that mitigation failed.
It is what a serious state, city, utility, health system, school system, housing agency, or employer owes people while warming and extreme events continue to intensify [S12][S13][S40][S41][S203][S204][S205][S206][S230][S237].

The protection queue should include:
- heat-health systems, public cooling access, shade, passive cooling, and care-system surge planning
- multi-hazard early warning with last-mile reach, not just forecast capability
- water security, groundwater discipline, watershed protection, leakage reduction, reuse, drought planning, WASH service floors, water-quality surveillance, and priority-use allocation rules
- flood, fire, smoke, and storm protection where protection is credible
- land-use rules and risk disclosure before new exposure is created
- repeat-loss rules and dignified retreat where repeated in-place defense is no longer honest
- social protection and disaster recovery that build back safer rather than recreating the same exposure

House rule: **measure protection by reduced exposure and faster recovery in lived places, not by resilience language in plans.**

Rev0245 adds one more control point: recovery itself should be on the queue. The programme should pre-arrange finance, temporary safe service, safer-rebuild gates, clean-default reconstruction, residual-loss ledgers, insurance / public-backstop rules, repeat-loss triggers, and dignified relocation options before the shock. Otherwise disasters can become a fast mechanism for recreating the same exposure and shifting losses to households, renters, public budgets, banks, and future debt [S264][S265][S266][S267][S268].

House rule: **do not let recovery money buy repetition of the last loss when it can buy lower risk and cleaner service for the next one.**

Rev0246 adds the financial-transmission control point: every major packet should say who carries the risk and cost before and after implementation. The programme should name household burden, insurance and credit exposure, utility and ratepayer effects, rent and property-market pathways, public backstop exposure, and treasury contingent liabilities before the packet is approved. It should also define affordability firebreaks across bills, premiums, rents, rates, taxes, fares, fees, deductibles, debt, and time burdens [S269][S270][S271][S272][S273][S274].

House rule: **do not let the climate programme hide risk or cost on the weakest balance sheet and then call the resulting backlash a failure of public will.**

### 7. Move complexity and risk upstream
The archive’s user-path notes make a practical claim: ordinary households, tenants, small businesses, farmers, local officials, and exposed communities should not have to discover, finance, coordinate, risk-manage, and verify the transition by hand at the moment they are already facing a replacement, repair, bill shock, heat wave, disaster, lease change, or permit decision [S213][S214][S215][S216][S217][S218][S219][S220][S221][S222][S223][S224][S225].

So the queue should produce clean offers rather than scavenger hunts:
- one-stop retrofit and clean-heat offers
- prequalified installer and contractor systems
- standard finance with low upfront burden
- automatic eligibility where data already exists
- default continuation for good-standing cases when facts have not materially changed
- performance remedy when the clean option fails early
- standing channels through utilities, landlords, municipalities, benefit systems, procurement offices, recovery programmes, and account changes

House rule: **do not make the weaker party carry search, proof, cash, coordination, and performance risk that system managers can carry more efficiently.**

### 8. Build the finance architecture around usable capital, not only promised totals
The missing finance problem is not only that climate money is too small.
It is often in the wrong currency, at the wrong risk allocation, behind too much project-preparation friction, or incompatible with debt and affordability constraints [S13][S91][S92][S93][S94][S95][S96][S195][S196][S227].

The finance queue should therefore include:
- guarantees that lower real project risk and perceived risk
- local-currency lending and deeper domestic capital-market channels where feasible
- concessional capital aimed at leverage, affordability, and first-loss positions rather than scattered visibility
- project-preparation facilities able to turn good concepts into bankable work
- tariff and payment-risk structures that protect service floors and keep utilities solvent enough to invest
- debt-space-aware packages for countries already fiscally constrained
- public value capture where public action creates durable private upside

House rule: **judge finance by projects built, cost of capital lowered, service made affordable, and risk allocated to actors able to carry it — not by headline pledges alone.**

The finance queue should also maintain a ready shelf. High-value packets should be prepared before the grant, guarantee, grid-capacity release, procurement calendar, or recovery window appears. Repeated needs should move from bespoke deals into procurement platforms with common specifications, prequalified suppliers, standard finance, service warranties, and performance data [S247][S248][S254][S255][S256][S257][S258].

House rule: **money moves faster and fairer when the best work is ready before the window opens.**

### 9. Keep scarce premium tools for the hard remainder
Hydrogen, synthetic fuels, sustainable biomass, high-integrity CCS, and durable removals are not garbage.
They are scarce, expensive, infrastructure-heavy, and easy to misuse as delay stories.
The work queue should reserve them for real hard residuals: parts of steel, cement, chemicals, shipping, aviation, long-duration storage, and durable carbon removal where simpler electrification, efficiency, material substitution, or demand reduction cannot do the job [S14][S28][S29][S30][S33][S34][S35][S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S165][S166][S167].

This requires allocation rules before scarcity becomes an excuse for capture.
It also requires lifecycle proof, storage integrity, and a rule that premium tools do not justify new long-lived fossil dependence where cleaner substitutes already exist.

House rule: **spend scarce molecules and removals like strategic medicine, not like universal seasoning.**

### 10. Make truth infrastructure corrective, not ceremonial
The proof layer should not merely report what happened after the programme is politically committed.
It should change what the programme does next [S65][S66][S67][S68][S113][S123][S133][S138][S189][S207].

A serious truth queue should include:
- gross-emissions and sectoral emissions scorelines
- measured methane and super-emitter response ledgers
- asset-retirement and non-reinvestment ledgers
- connection, curtailment, queue, and peak-stress metrics
- household burden, service quality, exposure, and protection metrics
- interoperable definitions for low- and near-zero-emissions materials and fuels
- public procurement and claims audits
- correction triggers when scorelines move the wrong way

House rule: **truth infrastructure is not a museum of reports; it is a control system for deciding what gets promoted, demoted, fixed, quarantined, or stopped.**

Rev0247 adds the claim-and-channel integrity control point. The queue should not let net-zero, transition-plan, carbon-credit, green-product, clean-power, green-finance, procurement, or resilience claims move money, trust, or priority unless they are bounded, auditable, and correctable. Nor should it let public money, grid capacity, guarantees, recovery funds, permits, or procurement channels become capture and rent-extraction machines. Claims integrity and anti-capture controls now belong in the work queue itself [S275][S276][S280][S281][S282][S283][S284].

Rev0248 adds the enforcement-remedy control point. The queue should not admit high-stakes duties unless it can say what happens when they are missed: who gives notice, who cures, who supports, who escalates, who remedies harm, who protects essential users, and which forum can correct the failed condition if ordinary administration does not. Remedy ladders and accountability routes now belong in the work queue itself [S285][S286][S287][S288][S290][S291][S292].

Rev0249 adds the data-spine and proof-passport control point. The queue should not admit high-stakes packets unless it can say what record identifies the object, which systems need to link it, who stewards the data, what fields are public or protected, what proof travels with the product, building, project, site, claim, or service, and what correction route fires when records are wrong, stale, missing, or misleading [S293][S294][S295][S296][S297][S298][S299][S300].

Rev0250 adds the cyber / AI control point. The queue should not admit digitally dependent high-stakes packets unless it can say what connected systems, vendors, data classes, controls, models, and automated rules the packet depends on; what happens when they fail; what fallback mode protects minimum service; what privacy and security safeguards apply; what human review and contest path exists; and what retirement trigger fires if the tool cannot support the duty [S301][S302][S303][S304][S305][S306][S307][S308][S309][S310].

Rev0251 adds the cross-border and market-access control point. The queue should not admit high-volume or high-criticality packets unless it can say what traded materials, components, spares, routes, suppliers, customs classifications, standards, conformity-assessment paths, certificates, and mutual-recognition routes the packet depends on; what happens when a dominant supplier, route, code, standard, certificate, port, or lab fails; and what responsible-supply, circular-supply, and market-surveillance duties apply [S244][S311][S312][S313][S314][S315][S316][S317][S318][S319].

Rev0252 adds the circular-design and end-of-life control point. The queue should not admit high-volume, high-criticality, public-funded, or hazardous clean-asset packets unless it can say how the product is maintained, repaired, supported, reused, taken back, decommissioned, recycled, and safely retired; what recovered-material route exists; what waste-shipment rules apply; and what happens if the producer, owner, installer, or recycler fails [S320][S321][S322][S324][S325][S326][S327][S328].

House rule: **do not let claims outrun proof or value outrun integrity.**

## The first scheduling move

A serious programme can start by sorting work into three clocks.

### Clock 1 — emergency and short-latency work
This clock covers work whose benefits can arrive quickly enough to matter for trust, near-term warming, bills, safety, and system stress:
- methane leak repair, venting and flaring rules, and satellite-response protocols
- heat-health protection, public cooling, shade, early warning, and care-system readiness
- efficient cooling, demand response, smart charging, and peak management
- insulation, appliance standards, passive design, and high-priority retrofits
- grid-queue triage, reconductoring, transformer procurement, and fast lawful interconnection reforms
- protection for households facing bill shock or unsafe housing conditions

### Clock 2 — stock-turnover work
This clock captures moments when long-lived assets are already changing:
- equipment replacement
- building sale, lease, renovation, refinancing, and code update
- vehicle and fleet procurement
- grid expansion and industrial-site investment
- post-disaster rebuilding
- public procurement cycles
- fossil-asset reinvestment and closure decisions

The rule is to meet the live decision when the file is already open and make the cleaner or safer path the default then.

### Clock 3 — hard-residual and system-learning work
This clock covers slower or more uncertain domains where learning, standards, and market formation are needed without letting uncertainty delay ready measures:
- industrial materials
- sustainable fuels
- shipping and aviation
- durable carbon removals
- long-duration storage and firm low-emissions resources
- mineral recycling, substitution, and traceability systems
- retreat governance and long-horizon relocation finance

House rule: **do not let slow-clock work excuse failure on fast-clock work; do not let fast-clock wins distract from stock-turnover decisions that lock in decades of emissions or exposure.**

## The “what needs to be done” checklist

A reader who wants the operational answer can start here.
The work is to:

1. **Build and connect clean backbones** — grids, clean power, flexibility, storage, efficient buildings, clean heating, clean cooling, charging, transit-supportive infrastructure, and industrial-enabling infrastructure [S226][S231][S235][S239][S240].
2. **Retire dirty defaults on purpose** — coal, unabated fossil support, inefficient equipment, routine venting and flaring, new fossil lock-in, dirty fallback rules, and reinvestment into high-emissions assets [S198][S199][S238][S247].
3. **Cut fast-warming emissions now** — methane, nitrous oxide, refrigerants, waste methane, and other short-latency forcing lanes [S232][S233][S234][S248].
4. **Lower avoidable load before oversizing supply** — efficiency, passive design, retrofits, repairability, proximity, modal shift, demand response, and high-wellbeing sufficiency [S97][S98][S99][S235][S239][S240].

14. **Link proof and correct** — publish the physical scorelines, audit claims, keep interoperable operating records, use proof passports where life-cycle evidence matters, respond to weak signals, and change the queue when reality disagrees with the plan [S65][S66][S67][S68][S113][S138][S189][S207][S293][S294][S295][S296][S297][S298][S299][S300].

## What this note demotes

This note demotes eleven habits that make climate programmes look serious while leaving the hard work underformed:
- treating new clean capacity as success even when dirty systems are not retiring [S199][S207][S238][S247]
- letting targets, announcements, or spending totals outrank physical outcome scorelines [S65][S113][S138][S207]
- putting grid, permitting, workforce, component, procurement, commissioning, and aftercare bottlenecks in appendices after deployment targets are already announced [S132][S226][S228][S231]
- treating methane, nitrogen, refrigerants, and waste as side clean-up rather than short-latency climate work [S232][S233][S234][S248]
- treating adaptation as a consolation prize rather than civil protection [S230][S236][S237]
- pushing application burden, proof burden, cash burden, coordination burden, and performance risk onto weaker parties [S215][S216][S217][S221]
- treating finance as a promise of money rather than a design of risk, currency, affordability, and bankability [S91][S95][S96][S195][S196][S227]
- treating critical minerals, recycling, and grid hardware as background supply-chain trivia [S231][S241][S244][S245][S246]
- spending scarce fuels, CCS, biomass, or removals on easy uses while hard residuals wait [S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S165][S166][S167]
- accelerating permits by weakening trust rather than by adding capacity, standardization, transparent deadlines, and participation fit for purpose [S59][S140][S141][S142][S197][S198]
- failing to say what happens when a measure underperforms [S143][S144][S145][S207]
- letting green claims, transition-plan claims, credit claims, procurement claims, or resilience claims move money and trust without bounded proof, correction, or consequence [S275][S276][S280][S281]
- letting urgency become a cover for captured procurement, opaque ownership, inflated prices, weak delivery, or private rents from public risk [S282][S283][S284]

## Rev0253 addendum — place, rights, benefits, and grievance repair

Rev0253 adds a missing control to the delivery stack: **place is climate infrastructure**.
A packet can be financed, permitted, procured, staffed, commissioned, cyber-secured, standards-compliant, supply-chain mapped, circular, and recyclable, yet still fail if its land, water, seabed, corridor, biodiversity, cultural, cumulative-impact, host-community, Indigenous-rights, benefit-sharing, and grievance conditions are unformed.

The new minimum is two ledgers for footprint-bearing or place-intensive packets:

1. a **place-capacity ledger** covering alternatives, low-conflict siting, no-go screens, biodiversity and water filters, cumulative impacts, grid / access dependencies, climate exposure, restoration, decommissioning, and correction triggers; and
2. a **host-community and rights ledger** covering affected people, Indigenous Peoples and FPIC where applicable, independent support, enforceable benefits, grievance routes, remedy triggers, and promise durability across ownership changes.

The practical rule is not “slow everything down for process.”
It is “move siting, rights, benefits, and grievance design upstream so fast clean deployment is legitimate enough to survive.”
See `269` and `270` [S60][S64][S329][S330][S331][S332][S333][S334][S335][S337][S338][S339][S340].

## Rev0254 addendum — dirty retirement has an afterlife

Rev0254 adds the closure-proof control point. The work queue should not count a fossil retirement packet as mature until it carries a fossil-liability ledger: asset identity, owner and parent responsibility, methane and environmental baseline, financial assurance, worker and community terms, closure standard, anti-orphaning transfer rules, post-closure monitoring, and remedy triggers.

## Rev0255 addendum — put climate mobility in the work queue

The work queue now includes climate mobility as a formed operating lane, not only as a humanitarian outcome after failure.
