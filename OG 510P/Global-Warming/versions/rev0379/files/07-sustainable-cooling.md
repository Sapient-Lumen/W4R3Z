# 07 — Sustainable Cooling as a Core Climate Lane

## Claim

Cooling is not a side issue within buildings policy. It is a **core climate lane** because it sits at the intersection of:
- adaptation and human survival
- electricity-demand growth and peak-load risk
- urban design and public health
- appliance efficiency and refrigerant policy
- equity, because many of the people most exposed to heat still lack access to safe cooling

## Why the archive upgrades cooling

UNEP’s **Global Cooling Watch 2025** says cooling demand could **more than triple by 2050** under business as usual, driving cooling-related emissions to about **7.2 billion tonnes of CO2** and straining grids at peak load [S16]. The same source says a **Sustainable Cooling Pathway** could cut those emissions by **64%** relative to expected 2050 levels, and by **97% below business-as-usual** when paired with rapid power-sector decarbonization [S16].

IEA’s 2025 cooling commentary adds the systems view: heat waves increasingly create large, cooling-driven power peaks, so efficient equipment and better system design are not merely consumer-side improvements; they are grid policy [S17].

## House doctrine

### 1. Treat cooling access as essential infrastructure
In many places, cooling is now a life-and-health necessity. The archive treats this as a civil-protection issue, not a luxury issue.

### 2. Go passive-first, not AC-only
The cleanest unit of cooling is avoided heat gain. The first moves should be shade, trees, reflective roofs, insulation, ventilation, passive design, and urban form that reduces heat stress.

### 3. Push appliance efficiency hard and early
Weak efficiency standards lock in decades of avoidable electricity demand. Cooling policy should aim for high minimum performance standards, rapid turnover of inefficient stock, and consumer access to better equipment.

### 4. Govern for peak load, not annual averages alone
Cooling stress shows up most dangerously at peak times. That means tariffs, demand response, thermal storage, smart controls, and local grid reinforcement matter alongside appliance policy [S17].

### 5. Include refrigerants and servicing
A cooling strategy that ignores refrigerant leakage is incomplete. Servicing quality, refrigerant transition, and end-of-life handling are part of mitigation, not housekeeping.

### 6. Build cooling into public systems
Schools, hospitals, transit, public housing, food cold chains, and medicine cold chains should be explicit priorities. Climate resilience fails if only affluent households can buy their own protection.

## Minimum package for cooling

A serious climate program should include at least:
- passive-cooling urban programs and heat-resilient building codes
- stronger AC and fan efficiency standards
- large-scale deployment of efficient fans, ACs, and heat pumps where appropriate
- refrigerant management and leak reduction
- district cooling and thermal storage where density and climate justify them
- heat plans, cooling centers, and targeted support for vulnerable households
- grid planning that anticipates hotter summers rather than reacting after failures

## What the archive rejects

- treating cooling purely as a consumer purchase rather than public infrastructure
- meeting heat risk with inefficient AC expansion on weak grids
- analyzing annual electricity demand while ignoring dangerous peak demand
- mitigation plans that omit heat adaptation
- adaptation plans that omit efficiency and refrigerants

## Compression

**Cooling is a hinge variable. If the world solves cooling badly, it deepens heat risk, peak load, emissions, and inequality at once. If it solves cooling well, it reduces all four.**

## Rev0267 cooling bridge — passive survivability reduces emergency power need

Cooling is now linked to the energy-continuity canon in `297`. Cooling policy should not only add air conditioning. It should reduce emergency load through passive survivability, shading, insulation, reflective surfaces, ventilation, thermal storage, efficient equipment, hub planning, and affordability protections so heat protection does not fail when the grid is stressed. [S502] [S515]

## Rev0268 cleanup bridge — cooling and drying after floods

Sustainable cooling now links to mold-safe drying and cleanup after floods. Drying, ventilation, dehumidification, safe power, and passive moisture control can become health protection when buildings flood; oversizing fossil generator-dependent cleanup is not a durable adaptation. Pair with `299` and `300`. [S526]
