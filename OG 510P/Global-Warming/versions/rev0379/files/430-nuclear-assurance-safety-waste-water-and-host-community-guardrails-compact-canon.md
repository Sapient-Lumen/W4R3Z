# Nuclear assurance guardrails

The cube is pro-nuclear, but not pro-unchecked-nuclear. Nuclear preference must be bounded by stronger assurance than ordinary infrastructure because errors can be durable, public legitimacy is fragile, and bad delivery can damage the entire clean-firm program.

## Minimum nuclear gates

A nuclear service floor cannot be scored above documented-template maturity unless it has:

1. a regulator-visible safety case;
2. site evaluation for external hazards and climate stress;
3. water availability and thermal-discharge resilience under future climate;
4. spent-fuel and waste stewardship responsibilities;
5. emergency preparedness and protective-action pathways;
6. security and cyber-physical protection controls;
7. workforce qualification, fatigue, and succession controls;
8. independent public challenge and redress;
9. open-contracting and delivery milestone evidence;
10. ratepayer, taxpayer, and host-community burden accounting.

IAEA safety standards and site-evaluation standards supply the minimum reference posture for design and siting, while national regulators govern licensing and enforcement [S781][S782][S783]. PRIS supplies global reactor status evidence [S777]. For SMRs and advanced reactors, public delivery evidence must cover licensing, financing, siting, fuel, engagement and supply chain readiness [S784].

## Nuclear-positive does not mean risk-blind

A project that lacks waste stewardship, emergency planning, host-community challenge, or cost/schedule disclosure should remain nuclear-favored in principle but capped in maturity. The cube should say: "this is a favored technology class with unresolved project evidence," not "this project is ready."

## Civil-rights and legitimacy rule

Host-community consent and public challenge are not anti-nuclear add-ons. They are part of making nuclear buildable. Language access, tribal consultation where applicable, disability-accessible emergency planning, renter and carless-household evacuation paths, and ratepayer contestability must be treated as delivery infrastructure.


## Rev0298 nuclear human-capital refactor note

The cube's pro-nuclear orientation is now explicitly conditioned by workforce depth, operator licensing, regulator capacity, safety/quality culture, knowledge retention, craft labor, and public training pathways. Nuclear remains favored for clean firm power and system benefit, but human-capital and institutional-capacity gaps are binding maturity caps rather than implementation footnotes. Sources: [S864], [S868], [S871], [S873], [S875].

## Rev0303 physical-security nuclearization note

Rev0303 keeps nuclear energy explicitly favored while adding a physical-security maturity cap: design-basis-threat protection, force-on-force or equivalent performance testing, insider mitigation, drone/perimeter posture, transport security, law-enforcement coordination, and security-safe publication must be traceable before nuclear security claims can mature.
