# 415 — Preserve real options, modularity, and reversible choices before capital locks in bad futures

## Core claim

Adaptation capacity is often destroyed by capital discipline that looks efficient on paper. A project can be cheap because it ignores option value, fast because it skips staged learning, or politically attractive because it hides exit costs. Rev0283 adds a real-options rule: where future hazard, demand, technology, population, or finance conditions are uncertain, the archive should prefer choices that preserve safe flexibility unless a public record explains why a committed path is better.

Dynamic adaptive pathways show why sequencing and path dependency matter [S741]. UNDRR's infrastructure principles emphasize continuity of critical services and net resilience gain [S745]. Climate-resilient infrastructure guidance stresses lifecycle risk management and options appraisal across project development [S736]. Adaptive management guidance treats adaptation as learning and adjustment, not a single terminal decision [S752], while infrastructure-resilience professional materials frame adaptive design and risk management as part of resilient practice [S754].

## Real-options packet

A capital or policy decision should disclose:

- what future options it preserves;
- what options it forecloses;
- whether the design can be expanded, relocated, raised, cooled, hardened, or retired;
- the cost of waiting;
- the cost of switching;
- the cost of locking in;
- the date by which the decision must be revisited.

## Temporary-is-not-free rule

Temporary measures are valid only when they have an expiry, monitoring trigger, and transition plan. Otherwise temporary sandbags, generators, shelters, road detours, rental units, manual workarounds, and emergency waivers become the new baseline while risk compounds.

## Cube rule

The field `real_options_flexibility` tests whether the packet names modularity, reversibility, expandability, and exit. Blank means the decision may be silently consuming future choice.
