# 291 — Ideal Solutions: Protect Legal Identity, Civil Justice, Administrative Redress, and Legal Help as Climate-Critical Services — Civil Justice Continuity Compact Canon

## Claim

Files `273`–`290` made mobility, rights portability, food, water, health, disease ecology, children, care, workers, housing, household finance, payments, debt, benefits, and consumer protection visible as climate operating layers.
This file adds the layer that lets ordinary people actually use those rights and services when the system is stressed: **legal identity, civil justice, administrative redress, legal help, claim navigation, community paralegals, ombuds routes, and accessible appeal are climate-critical services, not courtroom afterthoughts.**

Climate shocks create legal problems before they create lawsuits.
A flooded tenant receives an eviction notice while waiting for repairs.
A worker is disciplined for refusing unsafe heat exposure.
A benefit recipient misses an appeal deadline because the notice went to a damaged address.
A family loses birth certificates, IDs, custody papers, disability records, immigration documents, land records, insurance proof, or medical-authority forms.
A homeowner signs a predatory repair contract.
A displaced person crosses a jurisdictional boundary and discovers that school, health, housing, work, benefit, and claim systems no longer recognize the old proof.
A survivor of family violence is pushed into a shelter or temporary accommodation system without legal help for protection, custody, housing, documents, and money.

The World Justice Project estimates that 5.1 billion people face at least one unmet justice need; this includes 1.5 billion people who cannot obtain justice for civil, administrative, or criminal justice problems, 4.5 billion who are excluded from legal opportunity such as identity documents, land or housing tenure, and formal work arrangements, and 253 million living in extreme conditions of injustice [S458].
The World Justice Project's 2025 Rule of Law Index press materials report that civil justice weakened in 68% of countries, with longer delays, less effective alternatives to court, and greater government interference [S459].
OECD's access-to-justice materials now frame people-centred justice, administrative justice, legal empowerment, plain-language services, data, prevention, and user-centred pathways as core infrastructure [S460][S461][S462][S463].
The climate conclusion is direct: **a right that cannot be understood, claimed, appealed, documented, represented, enforced, or carried across displacement is not yet an operating protection.**

## Fast rule

**Any climate packet that touches warnings, evacuation, housing, repair, benefits, wages, debt, insurance, utilities, food, water, health, care, schools, child protection, disability support, migration, land, tenancy, recovery, relocation, public backstops, digital ID, data sharing, AI scoring, permits, procurement, or consumer protection should prove that affected people can get legal identity, plain-language notice, help with claims, deadline protection, representation or advocacy, administrative review, appeal, anti-retaliation protection, and enforceable remedy before hazards cascade into rights loss.**

That proof should name:

1. **the legal relationships at risk** — tenancy, mortgage, utility, insurance, debt, employment, benefit, pension, disability, child support, custody, guardianship, immigration, land, title, informal settlement, school, health, care, repair contract, public-assistance, and recovery-funding relationships;
2. **the identity and document dependencies** — birth registration, identity cards, residency documents, school records, health records, disability proof, benefit records, bank records, land records, lease documents, wage records, tax records, insurance policies, prescriptions, device prescriptions, court orders, powers of attorney, and digital credentials;
3. **the notice and deadline map** — who must receive notice, through which channel, in which language and format, with which accessibility features, which deadlines pause during shock, and how notices are reissued after displacement or outage;
4. **the legal-help floor** — legal aid, duty counsel, community paralegals, navigators, ombuds staff, advocates, translators, disability-access support, child-friendly support, elder and adult-safeguarding support, worker advocates, tenant advice, consumer help, and migrant / displaced-person support;
5. **the administrative-justice route** — intake, eligibility review, evidence flexibility, correction of records, reconsideration, appeal, interim relief, independent review, and systemic error correction;
6. **the anti-retaliation and safety floor** — protection against landlord retaliation, employer retaliation, debt harassment, domestic and gender-based violence, coercive control, discrimination, immigration intimidation, benefit threats, or service denial for asserting rights;
7. **the portability rule** — which rights, records, claims, protective orders, benefits, IDs, appeals, case files, and remedies move with the person, household, child, worker, care recipient, tenant, or displaced community;
8. **the data and privacy rule** — how legal records, eligibility data, identity proof, risk scores, complaints, and evidence are shared, corrected, minimized, consented, protected, and made usable offline;
9. **the forum ladder** — informal resolution where safe, administrative reconsideration, ombuds, regulator, tribunal, mediation, collective redress, court, emergency injunction, and international or human-rights forum where needed;
10. **the correction dashboard** — missed notices, wrongful denials, deadline defaults, inaccessible forms, failed appeals, retaliation, unresolved disputes, discriminatory outcomes, unrepresented parties, repeated provider errors, and systemic legal need.

## Why this belongs in the climate archive

The archive already says duties need remedy ladders (`259`) and accountability channels (`260`).
That is the institutional side.
This file adds the household and community side: **the person who is harmed must have the capability, proof, assistance, safety, time, and forum to make the remedy real.**

Legal capability is not an optional service after the climate programme is designed.
It is part of whether the programme is usable.
A payment system that denies benefits without understandable review uses legal exclusion as rationing.
A housing policy that funds repairs but gives tenants no rent, habitability, anti-retaliation, or displacement remedy uses private conflict as the shock absorber.
A worker heat rule without wage-protection enforcement and retaliation protection is a poster.
A relocation programme without title, tenancy, family, school, benefits, immigration, and appeals help moves people into legal limbo.
A data spine without correction rights becomes a proof maze.

OECD's 2025 toolkit describes people-centred legal and justice services as a continuum of accessible, high-quality support that responds to people's needs, rights, and capabilities, using clear language, timely and affordable solutions, systemic attention to recurring needs, fair processes, and feedback loops [S461].
OECD's 2025 report on people-centred justice emphasizes legal empowerment, prevention, administrative justice, data, open justice, multidisciplinary services, non-lawyer legal assistance, and non-court solutions as ways to make the rule of law usable in practice [S462].
Pathfinders' Justice for All platform similarly frames closing the justice gap around people-centred justice, data, evidence, resources, and prevention of common justice problems [S464].

## The compact canon

### 1. Treat legal identity as a service-access dependency

Birth registration, official ID, digital ID, nationality proof, residency proof, disability documentation, school records, health records, and land / housing records are not administrative trivia.
They are often the keys to evacuation support, school enrolment, health care, cash transfers, repairs, insurance, remittances, benefits, employment, voting, housing, and protection orders.
UNICEF's 2024 birth-registration update reports that 150 million children under five remain unregistered and more than 50 million whose births are recorded lack birth certificates [S470].
World Bank ID4D's 2025 dataset already shows large official-ID and digital-ID gaps [S449].

House rule: **lost, missing, expired, inaccessible, or non-portable documents should trigger legal-identity recovery, not rights loss.**

### 2. Put administrative justice before courtroom escalation

Most climate legal need will not start in court.
It will start as a denied payment, wrong address, missing form, account lock, inspection dispute, utility arrears, repair invoice, school transfer, tenancy notice, insurance delay, work discipline, benefit overpayment, relocation offer, or data error.
The first justice system is therefore the administrative interface.

House rule: **every climate service should have a visible route to correct records, challenge decisions, pause deadlines, obtain interim relief, and escalate when routine correction fails.**

### 3. Make notice understandable, accessible, and survivable

A notice is not real because it was mailed, posted, emailed, texted, or uploaded.
It is real when the affected person can understand it, receive it despite outage or displacement, act on it with available support, and contest it before harm occurs.

House rule: **critical climate notices should be plain-language, multilingual where needed, disability-accessible, trauma-aware, offline survivable, and reissued when address, phone, power, connectivity, or displacement breaks delivery.**

### 4. Bundle legal help with the place where the problem appears

People should not have to diagnose legal categories while evacuating, recovering, caring, looking for work, or replacing documents.
Legal help should be embedded in shelters, recovery centres, schools, clinics, community hubs, labour offices, housing offices, cash-transfer sites, mobile units, hotlines, and trusted local organizations.

House rule: **justice should arrive where climate need appears, not require a second scavenger hunt.**

### 5. Fund legal empowerment, not only expert representation

Lawyers matter, especially where rights, liberty, status, housing, family safety, major money, or precedent are at stake.
But many climate justice problems need earlier, closer, more trusted help: community paralegals, navigators, advocates, tenant counsellors, worker centres, disability organizations, older-person organizations, youth and child-rights advocates, Indigenous and customary institutions where rights-compliant, and multilingual community groups.
Namati describes community paralegals trained in basic law, mediation, organizing, education, and advocacy as a frontline that helps people use and shape law in broken systems [S471].

House rule: **fund the full justice continuum: information, prevention, negotiation, advocacy, representation, appeal, enforcement, and systemic reform.**

### 6. Protect people from retaliation for using the climate programme

Rights are unusable when asserting them causes eviction, job loss, violence, immigration threat, benefit loss, debt harassment, school exclusion, service denial, policing risk, or community retaliation.
Climate packets should therefore include anti-retaliation rules and safe channels.

House rule: **a remedy path is not accessible if the person must choose between safety and complaint.**

### 7. Make civil justice mobile, accessible, and low-friction

Climate shocks close roads, courts, offices, schools, clinics, and networks.
They scatter people across shelters, relatives' homes, temporary housing, detention, camps, hotels, or other regions.
Justice continuity therefore needs mobile clinics, remote hearings with safeguards, offline filing, deadline tolling, simplified forms, document substitution, accessible transport, and trusted intermediaries.

House rule: **the justice system should bend around displacement and outage instead of defaulting people for absence.**

### 8. Treat legal need data as early-warning data

Repeated legal problems reveal where the climate system is failing: eviction filings, benefit denials, wage complaints, debt claims, insurance disputes, utility shutoffs, repair fraud, school transfer delays, ID replacement failures, and family-safety cases.
OECD's legal-needs survey guidance frames legal-needs data as an empirical tool for understanding people's everyday legal problems and experience with the justice system [S463].

House rule: **legal-need spikes should update the climate work queue before they become visible as homelessness, hunger, unsafe work, or displacement.**

### 9. Use mediation and informal resolution only with safeguards

Alternative dispute resolution can be faster and less costly.
It can also pressure weaker parties into unfair deals, especially where there is violence, landlord / employer power, debt pressure, language barriers, immigration fear, or unequal technical knowledge.

House rule: **informal resolution is useful when it is voluntary, informed, safe, reviewable, and backed by enforceable rights.**

### 10. Make legal records portable without turning them into surveillance

Protective orders, custody arrangements, school records, health consents, disability accommodations, benefit appeals, housing claims, work complaints, land claims, insurance files, and identity records need to travel when people move.
But portability can become surveillance if records are over-shared, used for enforcement, exposed to abusers, or made impossible to correct.

House rule: **portable legal records need consent, minimization, correction, role-based access, offline fallback, and safe redaction.**

### 11. Recognize care, family, and safeguarding legal need as climate need

Climate shocks intensify family strain, caregiving burden, elder abuse, disability-rights issues, child protection, custody disputes, domestic violence, guardianship, supported decision-making, and institutionalization risk.
Legal continuity should therefore connect care, health, housing, benefits, schools, and safety systems without stripping agency.

House rule: **protective systems should preserve autonomy and family stability where safe, not default to coercion or separation.**

### 12. Do not confuse climate justice with climate litigation alone

Strategic climate litigation matters.
So do international advisory opinions, constitutional claims, human-rights proceedings, and corporate accountability.
But ordinary climate justice is often a rent notice, denied cash transfer, missed wage, lost ID, disputed repair, insurance form, school enrolment, water shutoff, custody issue, or unappealable algorithmic score.
IDLO's climate-justice rule-of-law work emphasizes legal empowerment, people-centred laws and institutions, feminist climate action, prevention and resolution of climate-related disputes, and engagement with customary, informal, and Indigenous justice systems [S465].

House rule: **a climate programme is legally serious when ordinary people can solve ordinary climate-shaped legal problems before they ruin ordinary life.**

## Minimum civil-justice-continuity packet

A formed packet should include:

- legal-need map by hazard, service, group, place, and season;
- identity and document-recovery route for people without proof, phone, address, account, certificate, or digital access;
- plain-language notice and deadline-tolling rule for warnings, denials, arrears, evictions, claims, appeals, repairs, relocation offers, and hearings;
- embedded legal-help route through shelters, recovery centres, schools, clinics, housing offices, labour offices, cash-transfer sites, mobile units, hotlines, and community hubs;
- administrative-justice route: intake, evidence flexibility, reconsideration, interim relief, appeal, ombuds, regulator, tribunal, court, and emergency protection;
- anti-retaliation and safety rule for tenants, workers, migrants, benefit recipients, survivors, children, older adults, disabled people, and informal residents;
- legal-data dashboard tracking legal-need spikes, wrongful denials, inaccessible notices, missed deadlines, unresolved appeals, unrepresented parties, group disparities, and repeat provider failures;
- portability rule for legal records, protective orders, benefits, school, health, care, work, housing, identity, land, and insurance files;
- quality and accountability rule for legal aid, paralegals, navigators, mediators, ombuds, digital tools, and AI-assisted triage;
- after-action rule that turns recurring legal failures into policy correction, budget changes, form redesign, staffing, training, enforcement, or legislation.

## What this rules out

This file rejects:

- treating legal help as a charitable afterthought after climate harm occurs;
- using strict documentation, address, digital, deadline, or proof rules during shocks that predictably exclude exposed people;
- designing benefits, repairs, housing, labour, mobility, insurance, or recovery systems with no practical appeal route;
- counting court access as enough when the live problem is administrative denial, landlord retaliation, debt harassment, work discipline, or records loss;
- digitizing justice without plain-language, accessible, privacy-protecting, offline, and human-help fallbacks;
- calling a right portable when the file, proof, protective order, appeal, or remedy cannot move with the person.

## Compression rule

**A serious climate programme should make civil justice continuous: protect legal identity, pause harmful deadlines, make notices understandable, put legal help where climate need appears, fund paralegals and legal aid, keep administrative review usable, prevent retaliation, make records portable, and treat every missed appeal, unclaimed benefit, eviction, debt default, denied repair, lost document, unsafe work complaint, or unresolved dispute as climate operating data.**

## Rev0265 addendum — civil justice begins with notice people can receive and understand

Civil-justice continuity now includes warning and information continuity. Legal identity, claims, appeals, deadlines, administrative review, anti-retaliation, legal aid, paralegals, ombuds, and portable records all depend on accessible notice and reachable help channels.

Justice rule: **a right does not exist operationally if the notice is inaccessible, the deadline is hidden, or the help channel fails during outage or displacement.** Use `293` and `294` with `291` [S479][S481].

## Rev0266 civil-justice bridge — legal help must reach displaced and isolated people

Legal identity, claims help, administrative redress, legal aid, paralegals, ombuds, and courts require access. Civil-justice packets should include mobile legal-help teams, transport to hearings and document offices, remote options with non-digital fallback, route-status awareness, and deadline tolling when transport failures block action [S487][S494][S501].

## Rev0267 civil-justice bridge — outage creates legal need

Civil-justice continuity now tracks outage-related legal need: shutoff disputes, reconnection, benefit access failure, missed deadlines, eviction risk, debt collection, spoiled food / medicine claims, inaccessible notices, and identity / records access. Pair with `297`, `298`, and `292`. [S515] [S517]

## Rev0268 civil-justice bridge — cleanup creates legal need

Civil-justice continuity now includes debris receipts, habitability notices, mold disputes, landlord repair duties, contractor fraud, missed pickup, hazardous-site complaints, insurance / assistance evidence, worker claims, and temporary-site challenges. Legal help should be present in the cleanup system. [S523]
