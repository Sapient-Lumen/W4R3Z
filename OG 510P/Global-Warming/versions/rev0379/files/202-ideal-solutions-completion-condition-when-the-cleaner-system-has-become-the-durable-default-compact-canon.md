# 202 — Ideal Solutions: Completion Condition — When the Cleaner System Has Become the Durable Default Compact Canon

## Claim

The archive now has a shortest whole answer (`195`), a one-page answer (`58`), a seriousness threshold (`197`), a transition mechanism (`198`), a counting rule (`199`), an atomic unit of progress (`200`), a unit of scale (`201`), and a deeper solved-state note (`159`).
That is already strong.
But it still left one front-door gap: the archive did not yet have one **tiny completion-condition note** for the ordinary question that follows all of those.

**When should a serious climate programme say it has changed the world enough to count as solved enough — not because the work is over, but because the cleaner system has become the durable default and the governing mode can shift from emergency acceleration toward maintenance, anti-regression, and bounded residual management?**

This note is therefore a compression note, not a new branch.
Its job is to make the archive answer that question in one short stop, without forcing the reader straight into the longer solved-state note or the deeper whole-system tree [S37][S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S81][S91][S96][S100][S101][S149].

## Fast rule

**Call the problem solved enough only when the cleaner system has become the durable default: gross emissions are near a shrinking residual floor; dirty relapse is hard rather than easy; methane, ecosystem loss, and repeat-loss exposure are under real control; the cleaner path is financeable and governable beyond the richest jurisdictions; and the remaining residuals are small enough to be covered only by bounded, high-integrity removals inside a permanent anti-regression regime** [S1][S11][S12][S13][S37][S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S81][S91][S96][S100][S101][S149].

## The five tests inside the completion condition

### 1. The dirty default has been broken
A programme should not call the problem solved enough while the old high-emissions baseline can easily reassert itself through routine capital replacement, ordinary household stress, price shocks, planning defaults, or political turnover.
The completion condition is therefore not “many clean things exist.”
It is “the cleaner path has become normal enough, reliable enough, and easier enough that the dirtier path is no longer the background option waiting to reclaim the field” [S37][S58][S69][S96][S100][S101][S149].

House rule: **do not call it solved enough while the dirty system still returns by default whenever conditions get hard.**

### 2. Gross emissions are low enough that the remainder is recognizably residual
The archive’s completion condition is gross-first, not net-first.
The programme should not claim completion merely because it has a target year, a compensating accounting identity, or a future removal story.
It should claim completion only when the physical economy has already been changed enough that most remaining emissions are visibly narrow, hard, and declining: not a large lazy residual economy, but a small bounded lane of hard cases under proof-heavy rules [S1][S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S100][S101].

House rule: **first push gross emissions down near a shrinking residual floor; only then let bounded residual-balancing tools finish a narrow remainder.**

### 3. Fast forcing, land damage, and repeat-loss exposure are on controlled downward paths
A climate programme has not reached its completion condition if it cleans up one major lane while leaving methane leakage, ecosystem liquidation, and repeated losses from heat, fire, flood, drought, or water insecurity on autopilot.
The archive’s completion condition is therefore broader than power-sector decarbonization or annual CO2 accounting.
It includes methane under measured control, major carbon-rich ecosystems better protected than liquidated, and exposure trends bending downward because settlements, health systems, lifelines, and civil-protection systems are actually safer in practice [S11][S12][S15][S36][S37][S38][S39][S40][S41][S81][S82][S83][S84].

House rule: **it is not solved enough if tonnes fall while repeated damage, deadly heat, or ecosystem loss keep climbing.**

### 4. The cleaner path travels across the world rather than stopping at the rich edge
The archive rejects any completion claim built on a few rich-country successes plus a hand-wave about later diffusion.
A climate programme has not reached its completion condition while the cleaner option remains structurally unaffordable, unfinanceable, or institutionally unreachable across large parts of the world.
The cleaner system should be governable and financeable across different capital costs, debt conditions, state capacities, and exposure profiles, with visible service gains strong enough to sustain legitimacy rather than depending on permanent sacrifice rhetoric [S13][S91][S92][S93][S94][S95][S96][S141][S142][S143].

House rule: **no global completion claim while clean development remains a narrow club good.**

### 5. The governing mode has shifted from emergency buildout to maintenance and anti-regression without relaxing integrity
Completion does not mean amnesia.
It means the primary governing burden changes.
Earlier in the transition, the dominant tasks are acceleration, buildout, switching, retirement, and protection under shortage and bottleneck conditions.
After the completion condition is reached, the dominant tasks become maintenance, replacement of aging assets with clean successors, anti-regression, bounded residual management, exposure management for locked-in warming, and continuous truth discipline so the old regime does not creep back through waivers, neglect, or stress fallback [S37][S40][S41][S81][S82][S83][S84][S108][S121][S122][S149].

House rule: **solved enough means maintenance mode with vigilance, not victory mode with drift.**

## Tie-breaker rule

If there is a dispute about whether the archive should treat a climate programme as complete enough to shift governing mode, prefer the more conservative answer unless you can say yes to most of the following at once:
- is the dirty baseline hard to restore through ordinary stress or turnover?
- are gross emissions already down near a small and shrinking residual floor?
- are methane, ecosystem damage, and repeat-loss exposure on controlled downward paths?
- is the cleaner path financeable and governable beyond the richest places?
- are removals bounded to true hard residuals rather than carrying unfinished transition?
- and could the cleaner regime survive a bad year without snapping back to the old default? [S1][S11][S12][S13][S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S81][S91][S96][S100][S101][S149]

## Preferred use

Open this note first when the live question is any of the following:
- what the shortest honest completion condition is
- when the archive thinks the problem should count as solved enough
- what must be true before governing mode can shift from emergency acceleration toward maintenance
- what the difference is between promising progress and a durable climate default shift
- why “solved enough” still leaves permanent duties in place

Then reopen `159` if the surviving question is about the deeper solved-state doctrine, `149` if the surviving question is mainly about anti-regression, or `134` if the surviving question is mainly about continuing protection duties under locked-in warming.

## Compression rule

**The archive should treat global warming as solved enough only when the cleaner system has become the durable default: gross emissions are near a shrinking residual floor, dirty relapse is hard, methane and ecosystem loss are under real control, repeat-loss exposure is falling, the clean path works beyond the richest jurisdictions, removals are bounded to hard residuals, and the remaining work is governed as maintenance and anti-regression rather than as a delayed excuse for unfinished transition.**
