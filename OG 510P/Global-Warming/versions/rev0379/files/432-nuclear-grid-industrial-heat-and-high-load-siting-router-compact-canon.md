# Nuclear grid industrial heat and high-load siting router

The nuclear-positive archive should not confine nuclear to generic electricity supply. It should route nuclear into service floors where clean firm heat, steam, water, hydrogen, desalination, district energy, high-load reliability, and transmission-constrained decarbonization matter.

## Routing rule

Route a service floor to nuclear assessment when the floor has one or more of the following properties:

- high load factor;
- clean firm capacity need;
- heat or steam demand;
- constrained transmission or land availability;
- energy-security exposure;
- desalination or water-treatment coupling;
- data-center or AI-load growth;
- industrial decarbonization with poor electrification alternatives;
- critical public-service dependence on long-duration grid stability.

## Siting rule

Nuclear siting is not only a project-development choice. It is a climate adaptation question. Future heat, drought, wildfire smoke, flood, sea-level, seismic, workforce, emergency access, transmission, cooling-water, and host-community conditions must be evaluated before maturity is raised [S782]. Lifecycle emissions and system costs belong in the siting comparison [S775][S779].

## Load-following and complementarity

The cube should prefer nuclear as a partner to renewables, storage, transmission, demand flexibility, geothermal and hydro, not as their universal replacement. Nuclear is the clean-firm backbone where intermittency, long-duration stress, and high-load service floors require it; other resources remain valued when they reduce cost, speed, local burden and system risk.


## Rev0294 nuclear integrated-energy propagation note

Rev0294 extends the nuclear-positive preference into cogeneration, district heating, process heat, desalination, hydrogen and clean molecules, data-center/AI infrastructure, critical loads, and co-product public-value scorecards. These additions are explicitly bounded by new gates NG_53–NG_66 and by sector-coupling exception rules; preference is not treated as maturity without local evidence.


## Rev0298 nuclear human-capital refactor note

The cube's pro-nuclear orientation is now explicitly conditioned by workforce depth, operator licensing, regulator capacity, safety/quality culture, knowledge retention, craft labor, and public training pathways. Nuclear remains favored for clean firm power and system benefit, but human-capital and institutional-capacity gaps are binding maturity caps rather than implementation footnotes. Sources: [S864], [S868], [S871], [S873], [S875].
