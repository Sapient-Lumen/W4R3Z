# 83 — Ideal Solutions by Assurance Stack: Promise, Delivery, Proof, Correction, and Retention

## Claim

The archive now says that the intended user should usually receive a complete clean offer, that one accountable lead should usually own the joined pathway, and that delivery should be followed by aftercare, proportionate proof, fast correction, and continuity across ordinary handoffs [S69][S80][S118][S119][S137][S141][S143][S144].

One more compact synthesis rule now belongs in the canon:

**ideal climate solutions should usually present one intelligible assurance stack: the promise made up front should connect to delivery, proof, correction, and retention in a way that ordinary users, counterparties, and public institutions can actually trust.**

In plain terms, a climate pathway should not ask people to trust a promise that floats free from what gets installed, how performance is shown, who fixes failures, or whether the gain survives the next routine handoff.
A coherent offer is stronger when its trust chain is visible from the start [S56][S69][S80][S118][S119][S121][S137][S141][S143][S144].

So the archive’s shortest assurance-stack rule is:

**connect promise → delivery → proof → correction → retention into one legible trust path. Do not treat these as unrelated programme fragments.** [S56][S69][S80][S118][S119][S121][S137][S141][S143][S144]

## Why this belongs in the canon

### 1. A clean offer is only as trustworthy as its weakest assurance link

A provider can make a generous-sounding promise about savings, comfort, resilience, bill reduction, emissions, or service quality.
But if delivery is loose, proof is obscure, correction is hard to trigger, or gains disappear at the next move or account change, then the promise was never fully backed in practice [S56][S69][S80][S118][S119][S121][S137][S143][S144].

That means climate credibility is not just a matter of technical performance or moral intent.
It is also a matter of **assurance structure**.

### 2. The archive had the pieces but not yet the shortest trust-path synthesis

The archive already says:
- users should receive a coherent clean offer rather than a scavenger hunt across programme fragments (`80-ideal-solutions-by-offer-quality-complete-clean-offers-not-fragmented-programme-pieces.md`) [S69][S80][S118][S119][S141][S143]
- one accountable lead should usually own the joined pathway rather than forcing the user to act as systems integrator (`81-ideal-solutions-by-orchestration-form-one-accountable-lead-and-no-user-as-systems-integrator.md`) [S69][S80][S118][S119][S137][S141][S143]
- delivery should usually be followed by commissioning, onboarding, follow-up, and early tuning (`82-ideal-solutions-by-aftercare-form-active-commissioning-follow-up-and-early-performance-retention.md`) [S53][S58][S69][S108][S119][S120][S121][S143][S144]
- ordinary claims should use the lightest credible proof form (`73-ideal-solutions-by-verification-form-simple-proof-metered-where-possible-and-sampled-audit-where-sensible.md`) [S113][S118][S123][S135][S136]
- ordinary failures should be easy to correct (`74-ideal-solutions-by-remedy-form-fast-recourse-automatic-correction-and-no-appeals-mazes.md`) [S118][S119][S143]
- ordinary handoffs should preserve the clean state rather than quietly resetting it (`75-ideal-solutions-by-continuity-form-no-reset-to-dirty-defaults-at-handoffs-moves-and-rebuilds.md`) [S69][S118][S119][S137][S143]

This note adds the missing provider-side synthesis:

**a credible climate pathway should show how each of those pieces backs the others, so the promise is never separated from the proof-and-repair chain that makes it believable.**

For the archive’s shortest companion rule on what should happen when stress or interruption still pushes the system off its normal clean path, see `84-ideal-solutions-by-fallback-form-fail-soft-use-the-least-damaging-backup-and-return-fast.md`.
For the matching synthesis that compresses fallback, exception, and exit into one joined non-happy-path discipline, see `87-ideal-solutions-by-edge-conditions-form-fallback-exception-and-exit-as-one-discipline.md`.

### 3. Assurance is different from interface or pathway design

`78-ideal-solutions-by-user-pathway-one-sequence-from-front-door-to-persistent-clean-state.md` looks at the joined path from the user’s point of view.
This note looks at roughly the same terrain from the provider or institutional side.
It asks not only whether the path is traversable, but whether the promised outcome is **assured**.

A clean pathway can be easy to enter yet still weakly assured.
A user may sign quickly and receive delivery quickly, but still have no clear basis for trusting the claim because proof, correction, and persistence remain vague or disconnected.
So the archive needs both lenses:
- **pathway integrity** for usability,
- **assurance stack** for credibility [S69][S80][S118][S119][S137][S141][S143][S144].

### 4. Assurance quality shapes public legitimacy and repeat uptake

People do not only ask whether the cleaner option exists.
They also ask, often implicitly: who stands behind it, how will I know it worked, what happens if it disappoints, and will it still hold next year?
If those answers are weak, climate offers accumulate distrust even when the underlying technology is sound [S69][S80][S118][S119][S137][S140][S141][S143][S144].

So assurance belongs inside the archive’s legitimacy doctrine:

**public-interest climate delivery should not rely on trust without showing users what the promise is anchored to.**

## The archive’s assurance-stack hierarchy

A future proposal is usually stronger when it can answer most of this stack clearly.

### 1. Promise layer
Is the promised outcome specific enough to matter — lower bills, cleaner air, more reliable comfort, safer cooling, lower emissions intensity, or more resilient service — rather than vague green branding? [S56][S69][S80][S118][S119][S141][S143]

### 2. Delivery layer
Is there a credible operational path from promise to actual installation, service provision, commissioning, or routine performance rather than a merely nominal entitlement? [S53][S58][S69][S80][S108][S119][S120][S121][S143][S144]

### 3. Proof layer
Can ordinary users or counterparties see how the claim is shown — meter data, standard methods, first-bill checks, service records, or sampled audit — without requiring bespoke expert interpretation? [S113][S118][S119][S123][S135][S136][S143]

### 4. Correction layer
If the outcome falls short, is there a fast route to clarification, tuning, repair, credit, or other correction that does not force the weaker actor into an appeals maze? [S118][S119][S121][S143][S144]

### 5. Retention layer
If the pathway succeeds once, does the gain survive ordinary handoffs, account changes, tenancy changes, provider switches, or rebuild moments instead of quietly dissolving? [S69][S118][S119][S137][S143]

## The archive’s assurance-stack tests

### 1. Floating-promise test
Does the proposal make strong promises that are weakly connected to delivery, proof, or correction?
If yes, the offer is probably under-assured.

### 2. Show-your-work test
Can a non-expert tell what counts as proof of success and where that proof comes from? [S113][S118][S123][S135][S136][S143]

### 3. Failed-promise test
When performance slips, who acts first and what happens next?
If the answer is unclear, slow, or user-heavy, assurance is weak [S118][S119][S121][S143][S144].

### 4. Next-handoff test
Does the climate gain remain trustworthy after the next routine administrative or life-event transition, or does the trust chain break there? [S69][S118][S119][S137][S143]

### 5. Trust-path test
Can the intended user, a regulator, or a public-interest buyer trace the whole chain from offer to retained outcome without guessing who owns the joins? [S69][S80][S118][S119][S137][S141][S143][S144]

## What this rule promotes

Promote:
- climate offers whose promises are specific, comparable, and visibly connected to delivery, proof, and correction [S56][S69][S80][S118][S119][S143]
- provider-side designs where proof and remedy are built into the offer rather than improvised after disappointment [S80][S118][S119][S121][S141][S143][S144]
- operational models that preserve trust through the first months of use and across ordinary later handoffs [S53][S58][S69][S108][S119][S120][S121][S137][S143][S144]
- public or institutional buying rules that prefer backed promises over green assertions or consultant-heavy after-the-fact interpretation [S56][S80][S118][S123][S141][S143]

## What this rule demotes

Demote:
- climate programmes that advertise outcomes more clearly than they deliver, prove, or repair them [S56][S118][S119][S141][S143]
- offers where the proof method, correction route, or continuity logic only becomes visible after trouble starts [S118][S119][S121][S137][S143][S144]
- provider arrangements that leave trust to goodwill, repeated user initiative, or unclear institutional boundaries [S69][S80][S118][S119][S137][S141][S143]
- any design where a nominally clean success can vanish at the next routine handoff without anyone clearly owning the retention task [S69][S118][S119][S137][S143]

## One-paragraph compression

**Ideal climate solutions should usually present one intelligible assurance stack: the promise made up front should connect to delivery, proof, correction, and retention in a way that ordinary users, buyers, and public institutions can actually trust. Do not let climate promises float free from how they will be fulfilled, shown, repaired, and kept in place. A credible pathway is not just available; it is backed end to end by a legible trust path** [S56][S69][S80][S118][S119][S121][S137][S141][S143][S144].
