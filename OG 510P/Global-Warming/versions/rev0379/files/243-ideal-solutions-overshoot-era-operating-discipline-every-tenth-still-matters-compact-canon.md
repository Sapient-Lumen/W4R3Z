# 243 — Ideal Solutions: Overshoot-Era Operating Discipline — Every Tenth Still Matters Compact Canon

## Claim

The archive already has:
- the shortest whole answer (`195`)
- the three physical jobs (`208`)
- the grow / shrink / stop rule (`209`)
- the abundance / ration / retire rule (`210`)
- the governed work queue (`242`)
- the removals boundary note (`14`)
- and the working dashboard (`207`)

What it still lacked was one front-door rule for the psychological and operational condition now surrounding the problem:

**how should the climate programme behave when 1.5°C is no longer a clean distant boundary but a live overshoot-risk regime?**

Current official evidence makes this unavoidable. Copernicus reports that 2025 was the third warmest year on record, that 2023–2025 was the first three-year period averaging more than 1.5°C above the pre-industrial level, and that long-term warming is around 1.4°C above pre-industrial levels [S249]. WMO’s 2025–2029 annual-to-decadal update says there is an 86% chance that at least one of the next five years will exceed 1.5°C and a 70% chance that the five-year average for 2025–2029 will exceed 1.5°C, while long-term warming averaged over decades remains below 1.5°C [S251]. UNEP’s 2025 Emissions Gap Report says current policies point to about 2.8°C this century, full implementation of current NDCs to about 2.3–2.5°C, and that a higher exceedance of 1.5°C is very likely within the next decade unless emissions cuts accelerate sharply [S250].

That does **not** mean the problem is over.
It means the archive needs an overshoot-era discipline.

The work is now to lower the peak, shorten the overshoot, reduce cumulative harm, prevent irreversible losses where possible, protect people while the system is still hot, and build high-integrity repair capacity without letting repair become a license for delay.

## Fast rule

**In the overshoot era, every tenth of a degree still matters, every year of overshoot still matters, and every avoidable tonne still matters. Do not translate breach-risk into fatalism, adaptation-only politics, or magical future removals. Translate it into faster gross cuts, faster methane cuts, stronger protection, stricter lock-in prevention, and bounded removals for true residuals and repair** [S1][S10][S12][S14][S42][S103][S207][S242][S247][S248][S249][S250][S251].

## Why this note is not redundant

The archive already says to minimize overshoot.
This note says what that instruction means once overshoot risk becomes part of ordinary climate governance rather than a remote scenario.

Three bad translations must be blocked.

1. **Fatalist translation:** “1.5°C is effectively gone, so ambition no longer matters.”  
   Wrong. Lower peak warming, shorter overshoot, and fewer cumulative emissions still reduce losses, irreversible risk, adaptation burden, and future dependence on removals [S250][S251].

2. **Adaptation-only translation:** “Since impacts are here, just protect and rebuild.”  
   Wrong. Protection is now central, but without rapid mitigation it becomes an expanding disaster-management treadmill [S12][S15][S230][S237].

3. **Future-cleanup translation:** “Overshoot can be repaired later, so present cuts can be slower.”  
   Wrong. Removals are needed for residuals and repair, but they are small, uncertain, energy- and land-constrained, and not equivalent to avoiding damage in the first place [S1][S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S250].

House rule: **overshoot is not permission to surrender, and it is not permission to gamble on cleanup. It is a reason to govern harder.**

## The compact canon

### 1. Treat 1.5°C as a damage threshold and return constraint, not as a cliff edge
Crossing a single-year threshold is not the same as stabilizing long-term warming above it.
But neither is it harmless.
The right governing posture is to treat 1.5°C as a live risk boundary: avoid additional exceedance where still possible, minimize peak warming where exceedance occurs, and preserve the possibility of return by cutting gross emissions fast [S249][S250][S251].

House rule: **do not use threshold semantics to make either panic or complacency look scientific.**

### 2. Keep gross emissions cuts as the main throttle
The climate programme’s first lever remains gross emissions reduction.
That means clean buildout must displace dirty systems, not merely add supply; efficiency and demand relief must reduce the size of the buildout burden; and managed fossil decline must prevent incumbents from converting the overshoot period into another reinvestment cycle [S1][S10][S14][S207][S238][S242][S247].

House rule: **the overshoot throttle is gross emissions, not the elegance of the net-zero story.**

### 3. Put short-latency forcing on emergency clocks
Methane, nitrous oxide, refrigerants, black-carbon-heavy combustion, and waste emissions are not side lanes in an overshoot regime.
They are part of how much warming, crop loss, health damage, and peak stress arrives in the next decade or two [S11][S16][S17][S19][S21][S232][S233][S234][S248].

House rule: **run fast-warming cuts on months-to-years delivery clocks, not only on mid-century pathway charts.**

### 4. Protect people without pretending protection substitutes for cause reduction
Heat-health systems, cooling access, shade, resilient care facilities, water security, early warning, smoke protection, flood and fire discipline, social protection, insurance reform, and retreat from the unprotectable are no longer optional adaptation appendices [S12][S13][S15][S16][S17][S230][S236][S237].

But protection without mitigation becomes an ever-harder chase.
Mitigation without protection abandons people now.

House rule: **solve the cause and reduce the harm in the same programme.**

### 5. Do not average away the harm
Overshoot is not experienced by a global mean person.
It lands unevenly across heat-exposed workers, children, older people, tenants, informal settlements, farmers, Indigenous communities, low-lying places, water-stressed regions, fire-prone landscapes, and countries with the least fiscal space [S12][S13][S40][S41][S71][S230][S237].

House rule: **a lower global-average pathway is not sufficient if the programme leaves the most exposed people without protection, voice, finance, or relocation rights.**

### 6. Stop creating new exposure and emissions during the overshoot period
An overshoot regime makes lock-in more dangerous, not less.
New inefficient buildings, car-dependent sprawl, long-lived unabated fossil assets, unmanaged cooling load, drained peatlands, avoidable deforestation, and repeat rebuilding in indefensible locations all make later correction harder [S23][S24][S37][S38][S77][S78][S80][S237][S238][S239][S240].

House rule: **do not use a hotter world as an excuse to build more future loss.**

### 7. Build removals and carbon management, but keep them subordinate
High-integrity removals should be built because residual emissions and overshoot repair are real.
But the removals lane must remain bounded by gross-cut priority, durability, storage integrity, MRV, ecological safeguards, and long-run liability [S1][S42][S43][S44][S45][S46].

House rule: **removals help repair what remains; they do not license what could have been avoided.**

### 8. Use a peak / duration / damage / repair scoreboard
In an overshoot regime, the working dashboard needs more than a yes-or-no threshold label.
It should watch:
- gross emissions and fossil retirement
- methane and other fast-warming gases
- clean service carrying real load
- same-service demand reduction
- protected population and reduced exposure
- avoided new lock-in
- peak warming and estimated duration of overshoot
- irreversible-loss risk
- removals integrity and residual-emissions discipline
- whether corrective actions fire when scorelines weaken [S207][S242][S249][S250][S251]

House rule: **score overshoot by how much harm is avoided and how much repair is credible, not by whether the slogan survived.**

### 9. Keep public meaning simple: less danger, less waste, better service
Overshoot-era communication should not ask people to carry climate despair.
It should make clear that the programme is still about cleaner air, safer heat, lower waste, more reliable power, better homes, less fuel-price exposure, protected water, safer rebuilding, and less future damage [S211][S212][S213][S214][S217][S229][S230].

House rule: **the public meaning of every tenth is concrete harm avoided.**

### 10. Maintain a return-to-safer-conditions doctrine
The programme should state not only how it reaches net zero, but how it minimizes overshoot and prevents overshoot governance from becoming permanent emergency management.
That means gross cuts first, strict residual definitions, high-integrity removals, adaptation that reduces exposure rather than merely subsidizing repeated loss, and periodic tightening when the physical scoreboard says the path is too hot [S14][S207][S237][S242][S250].

House rule: **the aim is not to normalize the overshoot condition; the aim is to shorten it, lower it, survive it, and exit it.**

## Overshoot work queue in one view

| Queue | Overshoot-era duty | First proof that it is real |
|---|---|---|
| Gross-cut throttle | Make emissions fall fast enough to lower peak warming | gross emissions down, dirty assets retiring, clean service carrying load |
| Fast-warming throttle | Cut methane, nitrous oxide, refrigerants, and waste emissions on short clocks | measured methane down, super-emitter response working, venting/flaring falling |
| Protection | Reduce deaths, illness, displacement, outage harm, water stress, and repeat loss | heat-health coverage, cooling access, resilient lifelines, recovery faster and safer |
| Lock-in prevention | Stop new emissions and exposure that would harden the overshoot path | stricter codes, no new routine fossil lock-in, no repeat rebuilding in indefensible places |
| Residual and repair | Build removals, CCS, and storage only for hard residuals and credible repair | separate gross/removal accounting, durable storage, residual definitions, liability rules |
| Truth and correction | Update the queue when the physical path is too hot or protection is failing | public scoreboards, correction triggers, escalated action when scorelines weaken |

## What this note demotes

This note demotes:
- “1.5°C is lost, so nothing matters” fatalism
- “1.5°C has not been legally breached, so urgency can wait” complacency
- adaptation-only climate realism
- removals-first climate optimism
- net-zero accounting that hides gross-emissions failure
- plans that treat exposed communities as averages
- rebuilding systems that recreate the same loss after each disaster
- dirty reinvestment justified by near-term stress that cleaner planning could have avoided

## Compression rule

**Overshoot discipline means: every tenth still matters, every year still matters, and every avoidable tonne still matters. Lower the peak, shorten the overshoot, cut gross emissions and methane fast, protect people now, prevent new lock-in, build bounded removals for residuals and repair, and correct the programme whenever the physical scoreboard says the path is still too hot.**
