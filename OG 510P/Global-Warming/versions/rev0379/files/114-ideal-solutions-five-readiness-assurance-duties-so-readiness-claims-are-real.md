# 114 — Ideal Solutions: Five Readiness-Assurance Duties So Readiness Claims Are Real

## Claim

The archive already has:
- a readiness note (`111`)
- a live-discipline note (`112`)
- a rehearsal-discipline note (`113`)
- a burden-of-proof note for dirty delay, fallback, or exception (`108`)
- and a broader assurance-stack note for ordinary delivery claims (`83`)

What it still lacked was the shortest **readiness-assurance note** for the question:

**When is a serious climate state actually allowed to say it is ready, rather than merely having a plan, a drill calendar, and a hopeful self-description?**

That gap matters because readiness is often the first claim to become ceremonial.
A ministry says the fallback ladder exists.
A utility says the contacts are current.
A regulator says the exception path is bounded.
A protection agency says vulnerable people will be shielded.
A dashboard exists, a drill happened once, and a slide says lessons were learned.
But none of that yet proves that readiness is current, cross-checked, independently challenged enough to trust, or still valid after staff turnover, supplier failure, data drift, or institutional conflict.

A compact archive needed one short bridge between **rehearsing readiness** and **being allowed to claim readiness**.
This note is a **compression note**, not a new doctrinal branch.
Its job is to reduce readiness assurance to five duties that keep readiness claims from becoming self-certified optimism [S34][S55][S73][S83][S102][S103][S108][S111][S112][S113][S124][S145][S147][S181][S183].

## Fast rule

**Treat readiness as an evidentiary claim, not a mood: require current proof, cross-role challenge, spot checking where sensible, visible downgrade when evidence decays, and no authority to claim “ready” without a live repair path for known defects.**

- `111-ideal-solutions-five-readiness-duties-before-escalation-fallback-or-exception.md` answers **what should already be prepared before a bad day**

## Five readiness-assurance duties

### 1. Every readiness claim needs current evidence, not an old plan
A serious climate state should not call itself ready merely because a playbook exists.
It should be able to point to current evidence that the necessary people, tools, authorities, inventories, data paths, and protection routes are in working order now.

What this usually means:
- readiness status expires unless refreshed on a visible calendar
- key claims depend on dated evidence rather than undated standing assertions
- “prepared once” does not count as “prepared now” after turnover, procurement changes, system updates, or a changed risk season [S55][S102][S111][S113][S145][S181][S183]

Why it matters:
- stale readiness is one of the easiest forms of institutional self-deception
- most bad-day failures are not caused by the absence of any plan, but by decay since the plan was last believed

### 2. Readiness should be challenged across roles, not only attested inside one silo
A serious climate state should not let the same actor design the readiness claim, mark it complete, and certify it sufficient without meaningful challenge from the other roles that depend on it.

What this usually means:
- mission owners, lane operators, truth functions, and protection backstops all get to challenge readiness claims that affect them
- evidence is tested at the joins between roles, not only within one agency’s internal memo
- where sensible, some readiness checks should include external or quasi-independent review rather than pure self-attestation [S73][S83][S105][S106][S107][S111][S113][S124][S147]

Why it matters:
- readiness usually fails at boundaries: who can authorize, who can publish, who protects, who repairs, who exits
- self-certification hides the very coordination failures that matter most under stress

### 3. Some readiness checks should be live enough to catch theatre
A serious climate state should use spot checks, call-tree tests, data-path tests, inventory checks, and partial surprise drills where sensible so readiness evidence is not optimized only for the scheduled exercise.

What this usually means:
- not every check is announced long enough in advance to permit cosmetic preparation
- the system periodically tests whether contacts answer, data is reachable, equipment is actually available, and handoff routines function under ordinary operational conditions
- where surprise testing would create disproportionate risk, the state should still choose the lightest live check that can reveal false confidence [S34][S39][S73][S83][S111][S113][S124][S145]

Why it matters:
- rehearsals alone can drift toward performance for the drill rather than performance for the incident
- systems become honest when they are occasionally tested under conditions close enough to real use

### 4. Readiness status should downgrade visibly when evidence decays or defects stay open
A serious climate state should treat readiness as a status that can weaken, not as an honorific once earned.

What this usually means:
- expired evidence, failed drills, open repair items, unavailable assets, or unresolved cross-role conflicts reduce claimed readiness until repaired
- the archive’s operating logic should prefer explicit downgrade over quiet optimism
- management attention, protection posture, and escalation sensitivity should change when readiness falls below the required floor [S34][S55][S83][S103][S111][S113][S145][S147]

Why it matters:
- the purpose of assurance is not to flatter the system but to warn it in time
- if readiness can never be downgraded, it is not really being assessed

### 5. No one gets to claim “ready” without a live repair path for known weaknesses
A serious climate state may still have some defects while being more prepared than before.
But it should not claim readiness unless known weaknesses have owners, deadlines, fallback boundaries, and a real path to closure.

What this usually means:
- open defects are named rather than buried inside a generic lessons log
- each material weakness has an owner, a deadline, a temporary containment rule, and a check for closure
- if the system depends on unresolved weaknesses, the claimed readiness level should be bounded accordingly rather than advertised as full [S34][S55][S83][S103][S108][S109][S110][S113][S145][S147]

Why it matters:
- most institutions are never literally defect-free
- the serious distinction is whether weaknesses are controlled, visible, and being repaired, or merely being socially ignored

## Minimal readiness-assurance loop

The smallest serious readiness-assurance loop is usually:

1. collect current evidence for the readiness claim
2. challenge that claim across the relevant roles and joins
3. run live-enough checks to catch theatre
4. downgrade readiness when evidence or defects demand it
5. keep every material gap on a live repair path until closed [S34][S55][S73][S83][S111][S113][S124][S145][S147]

## One-paragraph compression

**The archive now has the shortest readiness-assurance note for serious climate states: treat readiness as an evidentiary claim, not a mood; require current proof, cross-role challenge, live-enough checks, visible downgrade when evidence decays, and no “ready” status without a real repair path for known weaknesses. This closes the gap between rehearsing readiness and credibly claiming readiness, without adding another sectoral branch.**
