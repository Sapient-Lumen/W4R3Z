# 273 — Ideal Solutions: Plan Climate Mobility as Adaptation Capacity, Not Displacement Afterthought — Receiving-Capacity Compact Canon

## Claim

Rev0254 made the archive ask what happens to dirty places after fossil production ends.
The next missing layer is what happens to people when places become harder, more expensive, or impossible to inhabit safely.

The archive already had pieces of this problem: exposure discipline, repeat-loss rules, recovery discipline, protection gaps, dignified retreat, host-community rights, data passports, and adaptive social protection.
But those pieces still left a gap.
They could say **leave what is becoming unprotectable** without fully forming the other side of the sentence: **arrive somewhere that is actually ready to receive people with dignity, services, work, community continuity, and protection**.

Climate mobility is not only sudden disaster displacement.
It includes staying safely in place where adaptation still works, temporary evacuation, seasonal or circular mobility, labour mobility, assisted voluntary movement, planned relocation, post-disaster displacement, cross-border movement, and host-community growth.
Treating all of that as emergency response is too late [S351][S352][S353][S354][S355].

The archive therefore needs a receiving-capacity rule: **retreat, relocation, evacuation, and migration are not mature climate policies unless the destination capacity, host-community bargain, service continuity, financing, and remedy routes are formed before movement becomes forced**.

## Fast rule

**Every exposure-discipline, buyout, no-build, relocation, post-disaster recovery, loss-and-damage, adaptation, urban-growth, or labour-mobility packet should carry a climate-mobility and receiving-capacity ledger before it is called ready.**

That ledger should name:

1. the origin risk: heat, flood, erosion, sea-level rise, drought, wildfire, water stress, crop failure, insurance exit, public-service failure, conflict interaction, or repeated loss;
2. the stay-in-place option and why it is still viable, insufficient, or exhausted;
3. the likely mobility form: evacuation, temporary displacement, circular or seasonal mobility, individual migration, household relocation, community relocation, or cross-border pathway;
4. who decides, who consents, who gets independent support, and which rights, tenure, cultural, Indigenous, livelihood, and family ties are affected;
5. the destination or receiving region, including housing, land, water, power, cooling, health care, schools, childcare, transport, jobs, language access, legal status, and community-support capacity;
6. the host-community bargain: benefits, fiscal transfers, service expansion, anti-displacement protections, grievance routes, and visible gains for existing residents as well as newcomers;
7. what records, benefits, eligibility, remedies, health information, education records, tenure claims, insurance claims, and social-protection links travel with the person, household, or community;
8. what financing is pre-arranged and which authority can release it;
9. what happens if the first destination becomes unsafe or service capacity fails;
10. what metrics prove durable integration, safe return, or dignified relocation rather than mere removal from the origin risk.

## Why this belongs in the climate archive

World Bank's Groundswell work projects that, without concrete climate and development action, climate change could force about **216 million** people across six world regions to move within their own countries by 2050, while early emissions cuts plus inclusive resilient development could reduce the scale by as much as **80%** [S352].
IDMC's 2026 GRID shows the present-tense system is already strained: by the end of 2025, more than **82.2 million** people were living in internal displacement, including nearly **13.6 million** displaced by disasters, and the year still recorded **29.9 million** disaster displacement movements even after a drop from exceptional 2024 levels [S351].
IOM's 2026 World Migration Report and climate-mobility chapter make the local point: climate mobility is shaped in communities and cities, not only in national border debates [S353].
PDD's COP30 messages put human mobility — migration, displacement, and planned relocation — inside the loss-and-damage architecture rather than outside climate policy [S354].

The point is not that all mobility is failure.
Planned movement can be an adaptation strategy.
The failure is unmanaged movement: people pushed out by heat, fire, flood, salinization, insurance exit, debt, or collapsed services, then received by places with no housing, no school capacity, no work pathway, no water plan, no cooling plan, no legal route, no community bargain, and no remedy.

## Receiving capacity is physical capacity

A receiving place is not ready merely because it has lower hazard exposure on a map.
It needs real capacity:

- safe and affordable housing, not only vacant land;
- water security, not only cooler temperatures;
- reliable clean power, cooling, and heat;
- schools, clinics, transport, childcare, language support, and legal services;
- labour-market access and credential recognition where relevant;
- climate-resilient infrastructure that can absorb growth without creating new exposure;
- fiscal transfers or public finance so host jurisdictions are not punished for receiving people;
- anti-gentrification and anti-displacement protections for existing residents;
- grievance, remedy, and participation channels for both movers and hosts.

The archive should therefore reject the phrase **climate haven** unless it is backed by receiving-capacity proof.
There are no magic havens.
There are only places with different risks and different capacities, and those capacities can be built or overwhelmed [S356][S357].

## Triage ladder

The mobility ledger should distinguish six cases:

1. **Protect in place.** Upgrade housing, water, cooling, drainage, fire buffers, early warning, social protection, and service continuity where risk can still be reduced enough.
2. **Temporary evacuation with return.** Plan transport, shelter, documents, care continuity, pets and livestock where relevant, and safe return triggers.
3. **Circular or seasonal mobility.** Protect labour rights, remittances, housing, health care, and family continuity instead of forcing all movement into permanent relocation categories.
4. **Assisted voluntary movement.** Provide safer housing, service access, job links, school continuity, and benefit portability before household depletion forces a worse move.
5. **Community-shaped planned relocation.** Use when protection in place is no longer honest, but design with community control, cultural continuity, destination capacity, and long-term support.
6. **Durable solutions after displacement.** Do not confuse camp survival or temporary shelter with a solution; the test is safe return, local integration, or dignified settlement elsewhere.

## Failure modes

Demote any policy that:

- uses retreat language without naming receiving capacity;
- offers buyouts without affordable destination options;
- relocates people to new hazard zones, service deserts, or unaffordable markets;
- treats host communities as empty containers rather than rights-bearing communities with their own needs;
- lets landlords, speculators, insurers, or local governments convert climate movement into extraction or exclusion;
- turns climate mobility into border panic while ignoring mostly internal and regional movement;
- moves people but not benefits, records, remedies, school continuity, medical continuity, or legal status;
- measures success by origin-site vacancy rather than durable safety and integration.

## Compression rule

**Do not call retreat dignified, relocation planned, migration adaptive, or recovery complete unless there is a formed receiving-capacity ledger. Climate mobility policy should help people stay safely where they can, move before collapse where they must, arrive somewhere prepared to receive them, and carry services, records, rights, benefits, and remedies across the move.**

## Rev0259 addendum — receiving capacity includes school seats, childcare, and child protection

Receiving capacity now explicitly includes school seats, teachers, early childhood and childcare slots, school meals, WASH, safe transport, language support, disability support, psychosocial support, youth pathways, family unity, child protection, and child-friendly grievance routes. A destination is not ready for climate mobility if children arrive but cannot enroll, eat, travel safely, receive services, or be protected.

Use `281` for school and childcare continuity in receiving places and `282` for child protection, caregiving burden, youth agency, and child-rights remedy [S395][S397][S398][S400][S401][S402].

## Rev0260 addendum — receiving capacity must be accessible and care-ready

Rev0260 adds the access-and-care requirement to receiving capacity. A receiving place is not ready merely because it has housing, schools, water, clinics, and jobs in aggregate. It must also have accessible housing, accessible transport, home care, long-term-care capacity, pharmacies, device repair, interpreters, social workers, adult safeguarding, disability services, older-person services, supported decision-making, and OPD / older-persons organization participation.

A relocation packet that moves medically dependent people, older adults, disabled people, or care users without assistive devices, care relationships, medication continuity, accessible housing, and supported decision routes is not a dignified move; it is a risk transfer. See `283` and `284` [S407][S408][S409][S410][S412][S413][S414][S416].

## Rev0262 addendum — receiving capacity starts with actual housing

Receiving capacity is not credible without homes people can occupy. Rev0262 adds a housing-market test to climate mobility: serviced land, affordable rentals, public / social housing, temporary accommodation, WASH, power, cooling, schools, care, health, work access, tenant protections, host-community benefits, and anti-displacement rules must be prepared before movement is treated as adaptation.

House rule: **do not call mobility planned if destination housing is left to a stressed private market.** See `287` and `288` [S439][S441].

## Rev0265 addendum — mobility needs portable, trusted information before and after movement

Mobility and receiving-capacity plans now need trusted information routes across origin, transit, shelter, temporary accommodation, and destination. People need to know what rights, services, documents, schools, care, work, housing, cash, and legal remedies travel with them, and receiving communities need timely, accurate service information.

Mobility rule: **movement without trusted information becomes rumor-driven displacement.** Use `293` and `294` with `273` [S480][S481].

## Rev0266 mobility bridge — evacuation and receiving capacity need transport operations

Climate mobility includes the short movement of evacuation and the long movement of relocation. Both require transport operations: no-car pickup, accessible movement, pets / devices / medicines / records, staging, route status, shelter intake, return, freight for receiving places, and logistics for sudden population shifts. Receiving capacity should therefore include transit, paratransit, freight, fuel / charging, and depot / warehouse capacity, not only housing and services [S493][S494][S487].

## Rev0267 mobility bridge — receiving capacity needs energy continuity

Receiving-capacity packets now include energy-continuity checks: shelter power, cooling / heating, charging, fuel, water pumping, healthcare power, payment systems, communications, elevators, refrigeration, and restoration priority. Movement plans fail if receiving places cannot keep critical services powered. [S502] [S504]

## Rev0268 mobility bridge — receiving capacity includes cleanup capacity

Receiving-capacity doctrine now includes waste collection, debris pickup, temporary debris sites, mold-safe housing, medical-waste capacity, landfill / transfer throughput, and cleanup-worker protection. Moving people without cleanup capacity can turn receiving places into exposure and waste bottlenecks. Pair with `299` and `300`. [S520] [S524]
