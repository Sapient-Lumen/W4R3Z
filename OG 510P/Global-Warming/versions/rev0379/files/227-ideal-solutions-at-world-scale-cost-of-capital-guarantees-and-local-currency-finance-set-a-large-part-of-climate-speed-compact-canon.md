# 227 — Ideal Solutions: At World Scale, Cost of Capital, Guarantees, and Local-Currency Finance Set a Large Part of Climate Speed Compact Canon

## Claim

The archive already has:
- a deeper EMDE-finance note (`25`)
- an international-layer canon (`135`)
- a funding-and-burden-allocation canon (`156`)
- a minimal world-bargain canon (`194`)
- and, from the last revision, a tempo-setting electricity canon (`226`)

That is already enough to say that finance matters.
What it still lacked was one smallest front-door note for a now-central world-scale question:

**once the needed technologies are mostly mature, what sets a large part of climate speed outside the richest countries?**

This note is not a new doctrinal branch.
It is a compression note.
Its job is to make one practical truth hard to miss at the front door:

**at world scale, the climate programme is now slowed not only by missing projects or weak politics, but by expensive money, currency mismatch, shallow local capital markets, weak guarantee architecture, and unresolved payment and off-taker risk** [S13][S91][S95][S96][S195][S196].

## Fast rule

**Treat cost of capital, local-currency finance, guarantees, off-taker reliability, and project-bankability architecture as first-order climate infrastructure. When the same clean assets are mature in engineering terms but expensive, volatile, or dollarised in financing terms, the programme slows, shrinks, or becomes geographically lopsided** [S13][S91][S95][S96][S195][S196].

## The compact canon

### 1. The missing buildout is mostly ordinary, not speculative
IEA’s cost-of-capital work says almost all required EMDE clean-energy investment this decade is in mature technologies and sectors with tried and tested policy formulas, while only about **5%** of cumulative needs to 2035 depend on more nascent areas such as hydrogen, hydrogen-based fuels, or CCUS [S91].
IEA’s private-finance work adds that the biggest spending blocks are not exotic either: by the early 2030s, roughly one-third of EMDE clean-energy investment is in low-emissions power, another one-third in efficiency and end-use sectors, and just under a quarter in grids and storage [S195].

House rule: **do not hide finance failure behind frontier-technology storytelling when most of the missing buildout is already ordinary.**

### 2. Cost of capital is now a climate variable, not background macro scenery
IEA says financing costs for utility-scale solar PV projects in EMDE can account for around **half or more** of levelised electricity cost, and that narrowing the EMDE / advanced-economy cost-of-capital gap by **1 percentage point** could reduce average clean-energy financing costs in EMDE by about **USD 150 billion per year** [S91].

House rule: **if expensive money can erase much of the advantage of mature clean technology, then cost of capital belongs inside climate strategy, not outside it.**

### 3. Public money alone will not carry the world-scale buildout
IEA estimates that around **60%** of the finance for EMDE clean-energy investment outside China will need to come from the private sector, equal to about **USD 0.9-1.1 trillion annually** by the early 2030s, up from only **USD 135 billion** today [S195].
That is why IEA also says concessional funding for EMDE energy transitions needs to **triple**: not because public money can do the whole job alone, but because it must change risk and financing terms enough to mobilise much larger private flows [S91].

House rule: **public finance should usually be used to change the risk shape of the market, not just to announce concern from the sidelines.**

### 4. Local-currency finance is part of climate realism
Many clean assets earn domestic-currency revenues but seek finance in hard currency.
The archive already notes the FX-risk problem [S95]. OECD’s 2025 local-capital-markets work sharpens the policy implication: EMDE sovereign issuers should prioritise deepening local-currency bond markets where possible to reduce reliance on external debt and mitigate exchange-rate risks, and doing so is particularly critical for unlocking private finance for capital-intensive low-emissions infrastructure such as renewable-power projects [S196].

House rule: **domestic-revenue climate infrastructure should not depend by default on hard-currency fragility.**

### 5. Guarantees, payment discipline, and off-taker quality sit inside the delivery stack
IEA says project- and sector-specific issues such as regulation, the reliability of revenues, off-takers’ ability to pay on time, and the availability of transmission infrastructure or land can account for **20-30%** of the higher cost of capital in EMDE [S91].
That means guarantee architecture, power-sector payment discipline, contract quality, and project-preparation capacity are not afterthoughts.
They are part of the machinery that determines whether the cleaner system can actually be financed and built [S91][S96].

House rule: **bankability is not a spreadsheet epilogue; it is part of whether the physical programme exists in time.**

### 6. What this note demotes
This note demotes five recurring mistakes:
- treating headline climate-finance numbers as if they were already deployable project finance [S94][S96]
- assuming sovereign borrowing can close most of the gap without debt-space stress [S92][S93]
- treating all capital as interchangeable while ignoring FX mismatch and shallow local markets [S95][S196]
- telling an EMDE story that overweights frontier technologies when the bulk of need is ordinary clean power, efficiency, end use, and grids [S91][S195]
- talking as if climate speed were only about technology cost while ignoring financing terms, guarantees, and payment risk [S13][S91][S96]

## Preferred use

Open this note first when the live question is any of the following:
- what now sets climate speed at world scale outside the richest countries
- why mature clean technology can still arrive too slowly in poorer countries
- why guarantees, concessional finance, and local-currency finance are climate issues rather than merely finance-ministry detail
- what is wrong with climate-finance rhetoric that names totals but not deployable architecture
- how `226` about grid speed connects to the world-scale finance problem

Then reopen `25`, `135`, `156`, or `194` depending on whether the surviving question is mainly about deeper doctrine, international mechanics, burden allocation, or the shortest world bargain.

## Compression rule

**At world scale, climate speed is not only technology speed. It is also finance speed: lower the cost of capital, expand guarantees, deepen local-currency finance, fix payment and off-taker risk, and stop treating mature clean infrastructure as if expensive money were somebody else’s problem.**
