# 313 — Ideal Solutions: Make gendered safety, maternal health, and reproductive continuity proof lenses for every service floor

## Claim

The archive mentions women, girls, caregiving, gender-based violence, maternal health, water burden, schools, safety, and legal access across many files.
That is useful but not enough.
Gendered risk should become a **proof lens** applied to service floors.

A climate packet can look universal while failing in gendered ways:

- shelters without safe toilets, lighting, privacy, menstrual supplies, lactation space, or GBV referral;
- heat policy that ignores pregnancy, breastfeeding, outdoor work, and unpaid water / fuel collection;
- water scarcity that increases time burden and safety risk;
- school disruption that shifts care burdens and increases child-protection risk;
- legal and payment systems that assume people control documents, phones, accounts, and mobility;
- recovery programs that put compensation, land, tenancy, or benefit rights in male household heads' names;
- displacement systems that separate families or expose people to violence.

UN Women’s climate-policy scorecard method asks whether climate policies identify gender-specific risks and barriers that are likely to be exacerbated by climate change [S558]. WHO / HRP's 2026 call summarizes the SRHR evidence frontier: climate shocks are linked to adverse maternal and perinatal outcomes, gender-based violence, and disrupted access to contraception and abortion care when health systems are overwhelmed [S560]. The 10 New Insights 2024 synthesis highlights maternal and reproductive-health risks from heat, flooding, food / water insecurity, and displacement [S559].

## Fast rule

**No service floor should be marked ready until it has passed a gendered-safety, maternal-health, reproductive-health, caregiving, participation, and remedy check.**

## The compact canon

### 1. Use gender as a failure detector, not an identity checkbox

The question is not whether a document mentions women.
The question is whether the service works for people whose exposure is shaped by gendered power, care duties, income, violence risk, pregnancy, reproductive health needs, documentation, mobility, disability, migration status, caste / race / ethnicity, or household control.

House rule: **gender mainstreaming without failure detection is decoration.**

### 2. Put maternal and reproductive health into heat, flood, smoke, food, and water planning

Service floors should test:

- pregnancy and postpartum heat protection;
- prenatal, birth, emergency obstetric, abortion, contraception, and postnatal continuity;
- maternal nutrition during food and price shocks;
- safe water and sanitation for pregnancy, menstruation, lactation, and infant care;
- transport to clinics during flood, heat, smoke, or evacuation;
- continuity of medicines, records, and referrals;
- protection from occupational heat without job loss or discrimination.

House rule: **maternal and reproductive health is not a niche health add-on; it is a continuity test for heat, water, food, transport, work, and shelter.**

### 3. Treat gender-based violence risk as climate-relevant service failure

GBV risk can rise with displacement, shelter crowding, economic stress, water / fuel journeys, school closure, family separation, legal insecurity, loss of phones, and dependence on gatekeepers.

Climate packets should include:

- safe shelter design;
- confidential reporting;
- referral pathways;
- legal aid and protective-order continuity;
- cash access without abuser control;
- child and adolescent safety;
- privacy in registration and data systems;
- anti-exploitation safeguards in aid, work, and housing programs.

House rule: **a climate plan that increases exposure to violence is not protective adaptation.**

### 4. Count unpaid care and time burden

Heat, water failure, school closure, disease, damaged homes, food insecurity, and care-facility disruption shift labour into households.
That labour is often unpaid and gendered.

The packet should ask who collects water, waits in lines, supervises children, cleans mold, cares for older adults, navigates benefits, repairs records, and replaces medicine.

House rule: **a service that survives only by moving work into unpaid care is not truly continuous.**

### 5. Make participation and grievance real

Affected groups should shape thresholds, shelter design, water points, benefit rules, warning channels, employment protections, legal remedies, and after-action correction.
Participation must include local women's organizations, disability groups, youth groups, Indigenous and customary institutions where rights-compliant, migrant groups, worker organizations, and GBV / SRHR providers.

House rule: **consultation after design is not participation; participation changes the design.**

### 6. Use sex-, age-, disability-, pregnancy-, and status-disaggregated ledgers carefully

Data is needed, but sensitive data can create risk.
Collect only what is necessary; protect confidentiality; avoid exposing survivors, undocumented people, minors, or people seeking reproductive care.

House rule: **the proof ledger must not become a surveillance or retaliation system.**

## Minimum gender / SRHR proof lens

| Question | Evidence required |
|---|---|
| Who carries new care and time burdens? | water, food, school, care, cleanup, benefits, legal navigation, medicine access |
| What safety risks increase? | shelter, transport, water points, fuel collection, displacement, registration, aid distribution, digital contact |
| What maternal / reproductive services must continue? | prenatal, birth, emergency obstetric, postpartum, contraception, abortion where legal, menstrual health, lactation, infant care |
| Who controls documents, money, phones, and mobility? | account access, ID, SIM, payment, consent, legal status, household power |
| Who participated in design? | local organizations, frontline providers, worker groups, youth, disability groups, survivors' advocates |
| What remedy exists? | confidential complaint, legal aid, protective order, benefit appeal, safe transfer, anti-retaliation |

## What this routes to

- use `313` when a prompt asks about gender, women, girls, pregnancy, maternal health, reproductive health, gender-based violence, unpaid care, safety in shelters, water burden, family safety, or whether a service floor is actually equitable;
- pair with `279` for climate health;
- pair with `281` / `282` for schools, children, and care;
- pair with `285` / `286` for workers and paid safety;
- pair with `289` / `290` for cash and payment control;
- pair with `291` / `292` for legal remedies and family safety.

## Compression rule

**Gender is not a footnote. Every service floor should test maternal and reproductive-health continuity, GBV risk, unpaid care burden, control over documents / phones / money, participation, confidentiality, and remedy before it is called ready.**
