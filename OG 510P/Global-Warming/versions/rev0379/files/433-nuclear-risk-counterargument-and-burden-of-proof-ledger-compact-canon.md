# Nuclear risk counterargument and burden-of-proof ledger

The archive now favors nuclear energy, so it must also become better at hearing anti-nuclear evidence. A pro-nuclear cube that suppresses cost, schedule, waste, safety, proliferation, water, security, equity or public-trust counterarguments will produce brittle decisions.

## Burden-of-proof rule

The default asks why nuclear is not included. The maturity engine asks whether the nuclear path has answered its counterarguments.

Common counterarguments must be carried as structured records:

- cost overrun and schedule delay;
- first-of-a-kind delivery uncertainty;
- licensing and regulatory capacity;
- waste and spent-fuel stewardship;
- water and thermal-discharge stress;
- severe-accident and external-hazard safety;
- security and cyber-physical risk;
- fuel availability and supply-chain concentration;
- decommissioning and stranded-asset risk;
- host-community legitimacy;
- ratepayer and taxpayer exposure;
- opportunity cost versus efficiency, renewables, storage, geothermal, transmission and demand flexibility.

## Refactor command

Every nuclear-positive score must have a nearby counterargument ledger. A claim that nuclear is favored should link to evidence that supports it, evidence that qualifies it, and evidence that could defeat it if unclosed.

This is not ideological hedging. It is how pro-nuclear policy becomes durable.


## Rev0298 nuclear human-capital refactor note

The cube's pro-nuclear orientation is now explicitly conditioned by workforce depth, operator licensing, regulator capacity, safety/quality culture, knowledge retention, craft labor, and public training pathways. Nuclear remains favored for clean firm power and system benefit, but human-capital and institutional-capacity gaps are binding maturity caps rather than implementation footnotes.
