# 192 — Ideal Solutions: Twelve Hard Conclusions for Actually Solving Global Warming Compact Canon

## Claim

The archive already has:
- a shortest front-door answer (`58`)
- a shortest joined scoring rule (`157`)
- a shortest joined physical-first rule (`189`)
- a shortest joined package-logic rule (`190`)
- and a shortest joined programme-shape rule (`191`)

That is already a strong front door.
What it still lacked was one even more direct note for the question many readers are really asking:

**what are the hard conclusions — not the full architecture, not the whole router, but the shortest set of propositions that any serious attempt to solve global warming must now treat as basically non-optional?**

This note is not a new doctrinal branch.
It is a compression note.
Its job is to turn the archive’s mature answer into the smallest set of hard conclusions that can still guide design, ranking, and refusal [S100][S101][S102][S103][S104][S106].

## Fast rule

**The ideal solution is not a menu of loosely substitutable climate gestures. It is a governed package with twelve hard conclusions: cut gross emissions fast; build clean electricity and grids as the backbone; electrify ordinary end uses; slash methane; stop new fossil lock-in and manage decline; protect and restore ecosystems; treat adaptation and civil protection as core climate work; keep service floors high while pushing burden up the income-and-control ladder; make the clean path the easy path; finance diffusion across poorer countries; measure real physical outcomes; and reserve removals plus scarce clean molecules for hard residuals rather than easy excuses.**

- `58-ideal-solutions-one-page-answer.md` answers **the shortest front-door question**

- this note answers **what the archive’s hard conclusions now are, in the most direct practical language it can sustain without becoming vague**

## The twelve hard conclusions

### 1. Gross emissions cuts outrank elegant net stories
The first job is still to reduce gross emissions quickly, especially in the 2026–2035 window, because overshoot matters, irreversible losses accumulate, and later cleanup cannot fully erase the damage of delayed action [S100][S101][S105][S109][S110].

House rule: **treat future removals as cleanup of the hard remainder, not permission for slower gross cuts now.**

### 2. Clean electricity, grids, and flexibility are the main backbone
A serious climate programme now rests on clean power, grid expansion, grid modernization, storage, and demand flexibility because more and more of the rest of the solution must run through electrified end use [S100][S102][S103][S107][S111].

House rule: **if the grid cannot connect, move, and balance the cleaner system, much of the rest of the portfolio becomes theatre.**

### 3. Electrify ordinary end uses before reaching for scarce molecules
For routine buildings, passenger mobility, and a large share of low- and medium-temperature heat, direct electrification is usually the cleaner, cheaper, and lower-overhead path. Scarce clean fuels should be saved for the harder residual uses that truly need them [S100][S106][S107][S111].

House rule: **do not spend premium molecules where wires and efficient equipment can already do the job.**

### 4. Methane cuts belong in the emergency lane
Methane is not a side issue. Fast measured cuts in oil and gas, waste, and agrifood systems are one of the clearest near-term warming relief valves available, and a large share of energy-sector abatement is available at low or even negative net cost [S1][S11][S100].

House rule: **treat methane like a parallel emergency programme, not as a footnote to long-run CO2 strategy.**

### 5. No serious solution keeps adding long-lived fossil lock-in by default
Clean addition by itself is not enough if governments and firms continue to add or prolong long-lived fossil assets, subsidize dirty dependence, or rely on passive displacement to do the political work of managed decline [S9][S23][S100][S101][S112].

House rule: **build the clean system and deliberately shrink the dirty default.**

### 6. Ecosystems and land systems are core infrastructure, not scenery
Forests, peatlands, wetlands, soils, water systems, and agrifood reform belong inside the main solution because they affect emissions, resilience, hydrology, heat, biodiversity, and disaster risk all at once [S18][S19][S22][S36][S37][S38][S39].

House rule: **protect major natural buffers first and treat land-use destruction as climate sabotage, not as a side environmental issue.**

### 7. Adaptation and civil protection are part of solving the problem
Even perfect mitigation from now on would not remove already-banked heat and risk. So heat-health protection, sustainable cooling, water security, early warning, resilient lifelines, and exposure reduction are part of the solution itself rather than a consolation prize for mitigation failure [S12][S15][S16][S17][S40][S41][S104][S108].

House rule: **a programme that cuts future warming on paper but leaves people unprotected in the meantime is not a complete climate solution.**

### 8. Some places need stronger defence; some need planned retreat
Climate realism means refusing the fantasy that every settlement pattern can be defended forever at tolerable cost. A serious programme distinguishes defendable places, places requiring major adaptation in place, and places where dignified managed retreat or no-build discipline is the safer path [S40][S41][S83][S84][S109][S110].

House rule: **do not let retreat happen by insurer exit, mortgage withdrawal, or household ruin while public policy pretends no decision was made.**

### 9. Justice is not decorative; it is operating logic
Serious solutions protect service floors, lower hassle and exposure for ordinary households, and place more of the cost, constraint, and obligation on affluent overconsumption, powerful control points, and actors able to change systems at scale [S12][S20][S21][S40][S41][S91][S97].

House rule: **do not stack proof, cash, coordination, and risk on the weakest actor while calling the programme fair.**

### 10. The clean path must be the easy path
If households, landlords, firms, hospitals, schools, or municipalities must become expert systems integrators to reach the cleaner state, deployment will remain too slow and too unequal. So ideal solutions use standards, defaults, one-stop pathways, prequalified offers, public or regulated backbones where necessary, and low-friction delivery [S13][S14][S24][S25][S91][S95][S96][S112].

House rule: **design for ordinary uptake, not expert heroics.**

### 11. Global diffusion needs cheaper capital, common standards, and shared buildout capacity
The solution is not serious at world scale unless it can spread through emerging and developing economies, where financing costs, debt pressure, currency risk, and thinner delivery systems can block obviously useful clean infrastructure [S4][S13][S25][S27][S91][S92][S93][S94][S95][S96].

House rule: **treat lower cost of capital, guarantees, common product language, and repeatable procurement as part of climate infrastructure.**

### 12. Measure real outcomes, correct fast, and keep scarce tools for residuals
Programmes need live proof: real emissions, real exposure reduction, real bills, real reliability, real build speed, real retirement, real performance retention. At the same time, scarce clean fuels, biomass, carbon capture, and removals should be held for the genuinely hard remainder rather than routine uses that already have simpler substitutes [S100][S101][S103][S106][S111][S113][S114].

House rule: **make physical outcomes easy to see and hard to fake, then keep the scarce cleanup tools in the residual lane.**

## Preferred use

Open this note first when the live question is any of the following:
- what the archive now treats as the hard conclusions of a serious climate solution
- what should now be considered basically non-optional
- what should be refused quickly as too weak, too slow, too paper-based, or too dependent on wishful cleanup
- how to restate the archive’s answer in direct language without reopening the whole system map

Then reopen the deeper note that matches the surviving live question.

## Compression rule

**Do not ask which single technology or single policy solves global warming. Ask whether the programme satisfies the hard conclusions: fast gross cuts, clean-electrified backbone, methane emergency action, managed fossil decline, ecosystem protection, adaptation and civil protection, justice in burden allocation, low-friction delivery, international diffusion, live proof, and strict scarcity discipline for removals and other premium tools.**
