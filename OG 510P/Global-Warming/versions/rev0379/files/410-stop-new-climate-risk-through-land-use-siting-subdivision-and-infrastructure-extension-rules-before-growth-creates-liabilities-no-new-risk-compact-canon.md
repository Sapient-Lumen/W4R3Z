# 410 — Stop new climate risk through land use, siting, subdivision, and infrastructure-extension rules before growth creates liabilities

## Core claim

Risk reduction fails if recovery removes old exposure while ordinary growth creates new exposure faster. Land use, subdivision, infrastructure extension, utility connection, and siting rules are climate instruments. They decide whether future households inherit safety or public liabilities.

FEMA floodplain-management and CRS materials support higher standards and practices that exceed minimum NFIP requirements [S728][S730]. NOAA Atlas 15 and ASCE / NIST future-hazard work show why long-lived design and siting decisions need future-condition inputs [S733][S734][S735]. FEMA flood-risk disclosure and substantial-damage materials show that risk decisions occur at sale, lease, permit, repair, and rebuild gates [S731][S740].

## No-new-risk rule

A jurisdiction should not approve new growth, infrastructure extension, or major repair in a known or foreseeable hazard area without:

- future-condition hazard screen;
- safe and affordable alternative analysis;
- evacuation / access / utility service test;
- lifecycle fiscal note;
- disclosure before commitment;
- mitigation or retreat condition;
- public exception record.

## Housing justice rule

No-new-risk governance must not become exclusionary zoning by another name. The paired duty is to legalize and finance safe infill, retrofits, social housing, manufactured-housing safety, and receiving-area capacity so people are not forced into hazard zones by price.

## Cube rule

All planning, housing, infrastructure, and rebuild packets should expose `no_new_risk_land_use`. Blank means the archive should assume adaptation can be undone by ordinary development approvals.
