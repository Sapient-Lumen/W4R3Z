# 363 — Ideal Solutions: Protect informal settlements, worker camps, dormitories, and migrant labour under heat, flood, smoke, and outage

## Claim

Climate service floors often assume formal addresses, recognized employers, lawful tenure, banked households, registered utilities, and safe complaint channels. Informal settlements, worker camps, dormitories, mobile-home parks, seasonal labour housing, and migrant workforces break those assumptions.

UN-Habitat's urban climate work emphasizes that billions of urban residents face hotter temperatures and flood exposure [S78]. IDMC's displacement reporting shows disaster displacement remains widespread and can intersect with conflict, poverty, and housing stress [S351]. NIOSH and OSHA heat materials emphasize that heat risk depends on hydration, rest, shade, acclimatization, work practices, radiant heat, and worker vulnerability, not temperature alone [S631][S649][S650].

House rule: **a service floor fails if it requires a formal address, safe legal status, standard employer, or retaliation-free complaint path that the user does not have.**

## Fast rule

Test every heat, shelter, WASH, health, payment, and evacuation floor with a person who has no formal address, no car, no bank, no secure tenure, limited language access, employer-controlled housing, and fear of retaliation.

## The compact canon

### 1. Informality is not invisibility

Informal housing and work are often known locally but absent from official registries. The cube should map service consequence without turning mapping into eviction, policing, or immigration enforcement.

### 2. Worker housing is a climate-critical site

Dormitories, camps, farms, factories, construction sites, brick kilns, fishing vessels, warehouses, and temporary barracks can become heat, smoke, flood, disease, and power-outage traps. Housing and labour protection should be inspected together.

### 3. Piece-rate and debt pressure can defeat safety rules

Water, rest, shade, and cooling are ineffective if workers lose pay, jobs, housing, visas, or debt standing when they stop. Heat protection needs wage and non-retaliation rules.

### 4. Upgrading must not trigger displacement

Drainage, cooling, WASH, fire access, and flood reduction are necessary. But climate upgrading can become eviction if tenure protection, resident participation, and relocation safeguards are missing.

### 5. Complaint systems must be safe to use

A hotline is not a safeguard if the caller risks deportation, job loss, eviction, violence, or debt retaliation. Trusted intermediaries, anonymous reporting, firewall rules, and worker organizations are part of the service floor.

## Minimum packet

An informal habitation and labour packet includes: settlement and camp risk map with privacy safeguards; WASH and cooling delivery plan; heat work-rest-wage rule; non-retaliation complaint path; mobile health outreach; language access; no-ID shelter path; temporary tenure safe harbor during upgrades; employer-housing inspection; and public ledger of unresolved hazards.

## Bottom line

The people most exposed to climate heat, flood, smoke, and outage are often those least visible to formal systems. The cube should treat invisibility as a design failure, not as absence of need.
