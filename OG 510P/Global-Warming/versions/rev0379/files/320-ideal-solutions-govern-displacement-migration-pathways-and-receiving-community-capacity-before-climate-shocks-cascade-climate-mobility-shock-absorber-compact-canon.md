# 320 — Ideal Solutions: Govern displacement, migration pathways, and receiving-community capacity before climate shocks cascade

## Claim

Files `273` and `274` made climate mobility and portability visible.
This file sharpens the shock absorber: **displacement, migration pathways, planned relocation, receiving-community capacity, host-community investment, and rights portability are climate-critical services, not residual humanitarian overflow.**

People move before, during, and after climate shocks.
Some evacuate briefly.
Some circulate seasonally.
Some are displaced repeatedly.
Some are trapped because they lack money, documents, transport, disability support, custody release, safe routes, or legal permission.
Some need planned relocation because staying is no longer safe.
Some arrive in places that have no housing, schools, clinics, jobs, water, legal aid, benefit capacity, or public trust to receive them.

IDMC's 2026 displacement reporting shows that internal displacement remains near record levels, with both disaster and conflict movements shaping risk [S351].
IOM's migration work and UNDRR's disaster-displacement guidance make the planning point: mobility must be governed before shocks, not improvised afterward [S353][S439].

## Fast rule

**Any climate packet that discusses retreat, resilience, adaptation, housing, water, food, work, schools, health, legal identity, insurance, public finance, conflict, or disaster recovery must say who may need to move, who cannot move, where people can go, what rights and services travel with them, and how receiving places are funded.**

## Minimum service floor

The floor includes:

1. voluntary, informed, rights-respecting evacuation and movement support;
2. safe routes and accessible transport;
3. documentation, identity, records, and legal status continuity;
4. portable benefits, school enrollment, health care, disability support, and legal deadlines;
5. temporary accommodation that can become durable housing where return is unsafe;
6. planned relocation with consent, livelihood, culture, land, and host-community investment;
7. receiving-community infrastructure, staffing, schools, clinics, water, sanitation, transport, and dispute resolution;
8. anti-trafficking, child protection, gendered safety, and labor-protection pathways;
9. return rules that do not send people back into mold, contamination, heat, flood, debt, or violence;
10. public data that protects privacy and does not criminalize movement.

House rule: **mobility is adaptation only when the destination has capacity and the person keeps rights.**

## Trapped-population rule

The archive should avoid a false binary between staying and moving.
Some people are trapped by poverty, disability, caregiving, documentation, debt, custody, fear of deportation, language, family separation, or lack of transport.
Others are pushed to move without viable destination.

The packet must therefore ask:

- who wants to stay but needs protection;
- who wants to leave but lacks means;
- who must leave because risk is intolerable;
- who cannot safely return;
- who is being displaced by adaptation or reconstruction itself.

House rule: **do not count immobility as consent.**

## Receiving-capacity rule

Receiving places are not empty containers.
They need:

- housing and rental-market firebreaks;
- school and childcare seats;
- clinics, pharmacies, interpreters, and mental-health support;
- water, sanitation, waste, transport, and energy capacity;
- legal aid, documentation, and benefits desks;
- jobs, credential recognition, and worker protection;
- local-government fiscal support;
- host-community participation and investment.

House rule: **a relocation plan that funds departure but not reception is a displacement plan.**

## Minimum proof ledger

| Question | Evidence required |
|---|---|
| Who moved or could not move? | privacy-safe displacement and trapped-population register |
| Were services portable? | school, health, benefit, legal, identity, and payment transfer records |
| Could receiving places absorb arrivals? | housing, clinic, school, water, transport, and budget capacity map |
| Was relocation rights-respecting? | consent, compensation, livelihood, culture, land, and grievance records |
| Were host communities supported? | host investment, dispute, service-pressure, and participation logs |
| Did return become unsafe? | habitability, contamination, heat, insurance, debt, and violence checks |
| What changed? | receiving-capacity finance, portability rule, relocation standard, or no-return rule |

## What this routes to

## Compression rule

**Climate mobility is protective only when movement is safe, rights and services are portable, receiving places are funded, trapped people are visible, and return is not forced into danger.**
