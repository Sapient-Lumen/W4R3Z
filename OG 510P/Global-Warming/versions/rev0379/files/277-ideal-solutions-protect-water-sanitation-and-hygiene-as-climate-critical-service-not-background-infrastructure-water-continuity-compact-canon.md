# 277 — Ideal Solutions: Protect Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene as Climate-Critical Service, Not Background Infrastructure — Water-Continuity Compact Canon

## Claim

Rev0256 made food security and nutrition visible as climate-critical service layers.
This file adds the paired upstream service that decides whether food, health, shelter, schools, care systems, and receiving places can function at all: **safe water, sanitation, hygiene, and water-quality continuity**.

The archive already treats water security as a climate lane through drought, groundwater, watersheds, glaciers, floodplains, reuse, hydropower, and food-system stability.
That remains necessary.
But it is not sufficient.
Water is also a household service, a public-health service, a care-system service, a school service, a food-preparation service, a cooling service, a fire-suppression service, a dignity service, and a mobility service.

A climate programme that manages river basins on paper while letting people lose safe drinking water, toilets, hygiene, wastewater treatment, handwashing, menstrual hygiene, water for clinics, water for schools, water for shelters, or minimum household water during heat, flood, drought, outage, contamination, or displacement has not protected people.

The missing rule is therefore simple: **water, sanitation, hygiene, and water quality are climate-critical services**.
They should be governed with the same seriousness as power, health care, cooling, food, shelter, communications, and lifeline mobility during climate stress [S371][S372][S373][S379][S380].

## Fast rule

**Every adaptation, recovery, mobility, food, health, school, housing, utility, agriculture, city-receiving, critical-service, and climate-finance packet should carry a water-continuity ledger before it is called ready.**

That ledger should name:

1. **the exposed users** — infants, children, pregnant and lactating people, older people, disabled people, immunocompromised people, informal-settlement residents, tenants, people without on-premises service, displaced people, host communities, schoolchildren, health-care patients, care-home residents, outdoor workers, smallholders, pastoralists, fishers, and people reliant on hauled, shared, intermittent, or unsafe water;
2. **the minimum service floor** — drinking water, basic hygiene, toilets, sanitation, safe wastewater handling, water for food preparation, water for medicine and medical devices, water for cooling or heat illness prevention, water for cleaning, menstrual hygiene, and water at schools, clinics, shelters, and reception centres;
3. **the source and system risk** — watershed stress, groundwater drawdown, reservoir condition, drought, flood, salinity, wildfire ash, algal bloom, contamination, pipe failure, power outage, pump failure, treatment failure, sewer overflow, septic failure, chemical release, road access, or tanker dependence;
4. **the water-quality and sanitation risk** — microbial risk, chemical risk, turbidity, wastewater bypass, flooded latrines, sewage backflow, unsafe storage, household treatment failure, loss of chlorine residual, hygiene interruption, vector breeding, and waterborne-disease signal;
5. **the public-service dependencies** — electricity, fuel, pumps, treatment chemicals, spare parts, operators, labs, telemetry, roads, tankers, payment systems, schools, clinics, waste systems, drainage, and emergency shelters;
6. **the continuity instrument** — utility resilience, pressure management, leak reduction, household or community storage, safe tanker routes, emergency water points, mobile treatment, backup power, solar pumping, bottled-water fallback where necessary, sanitation-safe shelter design, wastewater bypass controls, boil-water communication, or hygiene-kit distribution;
7. **the prioritization rule** — which uses are protected first when supply is short: drinking, hygiene, sanitation, clinics, schools, care homes, shelters, firefighting, food preparation, livestock survival, food production, ecosystem flows, and other essential uses;
8. **the anticipatory trigger** — drought index, reservoir threshold, groundwater level, streamflow, flood forecast, heat alert, water-quality exceedance, power-restoration forecast, pump or treatment failure, wildfire / smoke / ash risk, displacement signal, disease signal, or price / affordability signal;
9. **the owner and finance route** — utility, municipality, health department, school system, emergency manager, social-protection programme, water user association, basin authority, humanitarian actor, regulator, treasury, insurer, or climate fund able to act before service collapses;
10. **the recovery proof** — hours or days of safe service restored, water-quality compliance, sanitation functionality, household burden, school / clinic continuity, exclusion errors, grievance resolution, avoided disease, reduced time fetching water, and whether recovery reduced future vulnerability rather than repeating failure.

## Why this belongs in the climate archive

Water continuity is not only an engineering issue.
It is one of the ways climate shocks become disease, hunger, gendered time poverty, school absence, hospital failure, informal-settlement crisis, social conflict, displacement, and loss of trust.

The current evidence is enough to require front-door status.
The 2026 United Nations World Water Development Report frames unequal water and sanitation access as a health, education, livelihood, safety, gender, and rights issue, and reports that women and girls spend an estimated 250 million hours every day collecting water [S371].
The WHO/UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme reports that 2.1 billion people still lack safely managed drinking water, including people who still drink directly from untreated surface sources, while safely managed WASH targets are increasingly out of reach [S372].
WMO's global water-resource reporting shows the operating environment becoming more erratic, with too much water and too little water increasingly producing cascading impacts on society and economies [S373].
WHO's water-safety planning doctrine gives the practical service rule: protect drinking-water safety from catchment to consumer through ongoing risk assessment and management, now explicitly integrating climate resilience and equity [S379].
The World Bank's climate-and-water analysis makes the development point: water-related climate risks appear across agriculture, energy, transport, forests, fisheries, health, education, sanitation, infrastructure, and employment, so water continuity is not a narrow utility appendix [S380].

The archive's practical conclusion is that **water continuity is climate protection in operational form**.
A heat plan that does not protect drinking water and sanitation is incomplete.
A flood plan that restores roads while leaving sewage in homes and clinics is incomplete.
A drought plan that says “reduce demand” without protecting essential users is incomplete.
A relocation plan that moves people without safe water, toilets, drainage, hygiene, and wastewater capacity at the destination is incomplete.

## The compact canon

### 1. Treat WASH as a critical service

Safe drinking water, sanitation, and hygiene belong beside power, health care, food, shelter, cooling, communications, and transport in critical-service continuity planning.
The minimum question is not only whether a utility has a capital plan.
It is whether exposed users can access safe, affordable, sufficient water and sanitation **through the shock and recovery window** without disease, indignity, predatory expense, dangerous travel, exclusion, or forced movement.

### 2. Protect quality, not only quantity

Scarcity can be fatal, but unsafe water and sanitation collapse can be just as dangerous.
A serious packet therefore protects source water, treatment, distribution pressure, household storage, chlorine residual, toilets, wastewater, drainage, lab testing, and risk communication.
Water-volume metrics alone can hide contamination, sewer overflow, septic failure, or household-level unsafe storage.

### 3. Put public health in the same ledger

Water continuity should be co-owned by utilities and health authorities.
Floods, heat, drought, power outages, and displacement can increase diarrhoeal disease, wound infection, vector risks, hygiene interruption, heat illness, and clinic failure.
The packet should therefore link water-quality surveillance, health surveillance, clinic continuity, school continuity, and emergency communication before the shock.

### 4. Design for the people who carry the water burden

Water failure is not socially neutral.
Where service is absent, intermittent, expensive, distant, unsafe, or unreliable, women and girls, disabled people, children, older people, informal-settlement residents, tenants, displaced people, and low-income households often carry disproportionate burden.
A mature water-continuity packet therefore measures time burden, safety, accessibility, dignity, affordability, exclusion, and grievance outcomes, not only utility assets built [S371][S372].

### 5. Join water, food, health, power, and shelter

Water systems fail through dependencies: electricity, fuel, treatment chemicals, staff, roads, telemetry, labs, pumps, tanks, drainage, and spare parts.
They also protect other systems: food preparation, school meals, clinics, cooling, firefighting, sanitation, and shelters.
The packet should therefore be stress-tested with `251` and `252`, food-tested with `275` and `276`, and mobility-tested with `273` and `274`.

### 6. Make utilities climate-service institutions

Utilities should not be judged only by coverage expansion or tariff collection.
Under climate stress they need source diversification where appropriate, leakage reduction, pressure management, energy resilience, backup power, spare parts, emergency operating procedures, safe tanker contracts, operator training, mutual-aid agreements, transparent rationing rules, and after-action correction.
A utility that cannot describe its degraded-mode service floor is not climate-ready.

### 7. Do not let emergency water become permanent inequality

Tankers, bottled water, communal points, temporary toilets, mobile treatment, and emergency hygiene kits can save lives.
But they should not become normalized substitutes for durable service.
The ledger should say when emergency measures end, what permanent service replaces them, and how costs, access, and quality will be monitored during the transition back to routine.

### 8. Carry WASH continuity through movement

Climate mobility is not only housing, jobs, and documents.
A household that moves should not lose safe water, toilets, hygiene, school WASH, clinic WASH, water-affordability support, water-quality remedies, or claims related to damaged wells, boreholes, livestock water, irrigation, or household systems because its jurisdiction or registry changed.
Receiving capacity includes water, sanitation, drainage, wastewater, and hygiene capacity at destination [S371][S372].

### 9. Finance O&M and contingency, not only visible construction

Water continuity often fails through underfunded maintenance, operator shortages, unpaid electricity bills, poor leak control, missing chemicals, absent labs, deferred sanitation, or weak emergency contracts.
A climate-finance packet that funds only new hardware while leaving O&M, tariff fairness, affordability support, and contingency finance unformed is not complete.

### 10. Review every water failure as a correction event

Every major water outage, boil-water order, sanitation failure, tanker crisis, sewer overflow, drought restriction, flood contamination event, or clinic / school water failure should leave a public after-action record: trigger timing, communication, service continuity, quality results, exclusion errors, affordability effects, health outcomes, grievance volume, and whether the next packet changed.

## Minimum operating packet

A water-continuity packet should include:

- a source-to-user water-safety and sanitation-safety map;
- a climate hazard and season calendar for drought, flood, heat, wildfire, salinity, contamination, outage, and displacement;
- a minimum WASH service floor for households, schools, clinics, shelters, care homes, and reception centres;
- a water-quality monitoring and public-health surveillance rule;
- a power, pump, chemical, spare-parts, lab, operator, and road-dependency map;
- a priority-use and rationing rule that protects essential users first;
- an emergency water and sanitation delivery plan with accessibility and dignity safeguards;
- an affordability and grievance rule for essential water service;
- a recovery rule that repairs service and reduces future exposure;
- an after-action correction duty.

## Failure modes

Demote any policy that:

- treats WASH as a humanitarian add-on after climate failure;
- measures water continuity only by litres delivered, not safety, sanitation, dignity, accessibility, and affordability;
- restores pumps without power resilience, treatment chemicals, operators, spare parts, or labs;
- builds shelters, schools, clinics, or receiving settlements without WASH capacity;
- assumes tanker water is an acceptable long-term substitute for durable service;
- protects average service while leaving informal settlements, renters, disabled people, displaced people, or peripheral communities behind;
- separates water-quality surveillance from health surveillance;
- hides underfunded O&M behind capital-project announcements;
- lets water bills, connection fees, storage costs, or tanker prices break the service floor;
- treats contamination, sewer overflow, or boil-water events as isolated incidents rather than correction triggers.

## Compression rule

**Do not call a climate packet protective, recovery-ready, mobility-ready, health-ready, food-ready, school-ready, or city-ready unless safe water, sanitation, hygiene, and water quality can continue through the shock. WASH is climate-critical service infrastructure; the test is whether people can drink, wash, use toilets, cook, receive care, attend school, and recover with dignity before water failure becomes disease, hunger, forced movement, or public-service collapse.**

## Rev0258 addendum — WASH continuity must carry disease-surveillance and care-continuity links

Rev0258 adds the health bridge to WASH continuity. A safe-water and sanitation packet is not mature if it protects pipes and toilets but fails to connect water-quality surveillance, diarrhoeal disease signals, cholera preparedness, clinic continuity, hygiene access, vaccine or oral-rehydration routes where relevant, health-worker communication, and trusted public messaging.

Use `279` when the WASH packet needs climate-health surveillance, care floors, and health-system continuity. Use `280` when unsafe water, wastewater, flooding, drought, displacement, shelter crowding, or drainage creates waterborne or vector-borne disease risk [S382][S384][S386][S387][S389][S390][S393].

## Rev0259 addendum — school WASH is a child-continuity condition

WASH continuity now explicitly includes schools, childcare, temporary learning spaces, shelters used by children, and receiving-place education systems. Safe drinking water, toilets, handwashing, menstrual hygiene, sanitation, drainage, and cleaning are conditions for learning continuity, disease prevention, dignity, disability access, and gender equity.

A school cannot be treated as reopened if WASH has failed. Use `281` for the school-continuity ledger and `282` when WASH failure creates child-protection or caregiving risks [S395][S397][S398][S399].

## Rev0260 addendum — WASH continuity must be accessible to care users

Rev0260 adds an access test to water, sanitation, and hygiene continuity. Safe water and sanitation do not reach everyone if water points, toilets, bathing, handwashing, hygiene kits, queues, shelters, and information are inaccessible to older adults, disabled people, people using wheelchairs or walkers, people needing carers, people with incontinence supplies, people with dementia, and people with communication needs.

A WASH-continuity packet should therefore name accessible water distribution, home delivery when needed, accessible toilets and showers, hygiene supplies for care and continence, caregiver safety, infection-control support in long-term care, and clear water-quality communication in accessible formats [S407][S412][S413][S415].

## Rev0262 addendum — housing continuity depends on household WASH

Water and WASH continuity now needs a household and shelter test. A home or temporary accommodation site is not habitable if drinking water, toilets, bathing, drainage, sewage, handwashing, hygiene supplies, or safe wastewater handling fail. Floods, drought, outages, and water-quality events should trigger housing inspections and temporary accommodation where WASH failure makes shelter-in-place unsafe.

House rule: **WASH failure is housing failure when it makes the dwelling uninhabitable.** See `287` and `288` [S431][S434].

## Rev0265 addendum — water continuity needs trusted public advisories

Water-continuity packets now need communication continuity for boil-water notices, do-not-use warnings, distribution points, tanker schedules, sanitation instructions, school / clinic water status, and restoration updates.

Water rule: **a water advisory is not protective unless every affected user can understand what water is safe, where alternatives are, and when guidance changes.** Use `293` and `294` [S472][S481].

## Rev0266 WASH bridge — safe water often rides on roads

Water and WASH continuity need transport for repair crews, treatment chemicals, tanker water, bottled water where unavoidable, portable toilets, sludge removal, hygiene kits, generators, fuel, lab samples, and mobile teams. Water packets should include route clearance, fuel / charging priority, logistics staging, and shelter-in-place resupply for isolated places [S487][S495][S499].

## Rev0267 WASH bridge — powered water is a climate-critical dependency

WASH continuity now requires proof of powered pumping, pressure, treatment, wastewater conveyance, laboratory monitoring, emergency water distribution, and electric-provider coordination. Use `297` and `298` for the energy annex and `278` for basin / scarcity shock interaction. [S514]

## Rev0268 WASH bridge — solid waste is a water-quality dependency

WASH continuity now includes debris blocking drains, waste contaminating floodwater, landfill / temporary-site runoff, household hazardous waste, medical waste, contaminated sediment, vector breeding, and solid waste at shelters and clinics. Pair with `299` and `300` whenever water safety depends on cleanup. [S521] [S524]
