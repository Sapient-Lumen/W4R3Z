# 274 — Ideal Solutions: Make Rights, Services, Records, and Benefits Portable When People Move — Climate-Mobility Continuity Compact Canon

## Claim

File `273` says climate mobility needs receiving capacity.
This file says movement also needs continuity.

When people move because of climate stress, disaster, recovery, relocation, livelihood loss, insurance exit, water insecurity, heat, fire, flood, or slow-onset degradation, the climate system should not force them to restart their whole civic and service life from zero.
A move should not erase eligibility, school records, health records, disability support, social protection, housing support, energy-bill protection, cooling access, disaster claims, tenure claims, work credentials, language access, or grievance rights.

The archive already has a carrier-packet rule in `220`: status, records, obligations, support, and remedy should travel with the right carrier.
Climate mobility is the hardest version of that rule.
The carrier may be a person, household, community, claim, benefit, school record, medical record, social-protection case, insurance case, utility account, lease, tenure right, visa or work pathway, or relocation agreement [S353][S354][S359][S360].

## Fast rule

**Every climate-mobility packet should carry a mobility-continuity passport that makes essential rights, services, records, benefits, and remedies portable enough to survive the move without creating surveillance, exclusion, or proof mazes.**

That passport should be minimum, consent-aware, privacy-protected, and duty-serving.
It should not become a generalized tracking tool or a new barrier to support.

## What should travel

A minimum mobility-continuity passport should be able to carry or link:

1. identity and household information, with alternatives when documents are lost;
2. eligibility for disaster aid, social protection, food support, health coverage, disability support, cooling support, energy support, housing support, or relocation assistance;
3. school enrollment, education records, language support, and child-protection routes;
4. health records needed for care continuity, medicines, assistive devices, pregnancy care, mental-health support, elder care, and disability accommodation;
5. work authorization, labour rights, credential recognition, skills records, and placement pathways where relevant;
6. tenure, land, lease, buyout, insurance, compensation, and recovery claims;
7. cultural, community, family, and Indigenous continuity obligations where applicable;
8. grievance and remedy routes at origin, in transit, and at destination;
9. data steward, access rule, correction rule, expiry rule, and deletion or archival rule.

## The continuity test

A climate move is not complete when a household leaves the origin site.
It is complete only when one of three durable outcomes is formed:

1. **safe return** with reduced exposure and restored service;
2. **local integration** with housing, school, health, work, legal, and community continuity; or
3. **settlement elsewhere** with the same minimum service and rights floor.

Temporary shelter, camp placement, hotel vouchers, emergency cash, or bare transport may be necessary.
They are not completion conditions.

## Adaptive social protection is part of mobility infrastructure

Social protection cannot remain static if climate hazards are dynamic.
Shock-responsive and adaptive systems need usable registries, payment systems, grievance mechanisms, frontline service providers, early-warning links, and pre-arranged finance that can expand before displacement strips households of assets and documents [S359][S360].

This matters for both origin and destination:

- before movement, support can help households adapt in place or move before distress;
- during movement, support can keep documents, medicines, income, school, and care from breaking;
- after movement, support can prevent destination poverty, exploitation, homelessness, or host-community backlash.

## Privacy and anti-exclusion rules

Portability does not mean data maximalism.
A mobility passport should:

- collect only what is needed for a named duty;
- avoid making support conditional on documents people are likely to lose in disasters;
- allow human review, correction, and appeal;
- protect sensitive health, status, caste, ethnicity, religion, Indigenous, immigration, and household data;
- not be used for policing, eviction, exclusion, predatory lending, or immigration enforcement unrelated to the protective duty;
- work offline or on paper during outages and disaster conditions;
- expire or narrow when the duty ends.

The archive's cyber, AI, data-spine, and remedy rules apply here with extra force because mobile and displaced people are often least able to contest a bad record, algorithmic score, or missing document [S261][S262][S263][S264].

## Host-community continuity

Continuity is not only for movers.
Receiving communities also need continuity of service, affordability, trust, and public value.
A receiving packet should therefore fund expansion of housing, schools, clinics, water, cooling, transit, interpretation, legal services, and public spaces for hosts and arrivals together.
That is the opposite of treating newcomers as a private burden on already-stressed neighbourhoods [S356][S357].

## Failure modes

Demote any mobility or relocation policy that:

- moves people before benefits, records, schools, health care, and claims can travel;
- asks disaster-affected households to prove eligibility with documents destroyed by the disaster;
- creates separate support mazes for origin, transit, and destination agencies;
- lets a data system decide eligibility without appeal or human correction;
- leaves host jurisdictions with new service demand but no fiscal transfer;
- counts relocation as complete while people remain in temporary shelter, insecure tenure, joblessness, school interruption, or debt;
- treats displaced people as evidence problems rather than rights-bearing residents.

## Compression rule

**A climate move should not reset a person's life to zero. When climate stress forces or encourages movement, essential rights, services, records, benefits, and remedies should travel with people and receiving communities should be funded to absorb growth without losing service quality, affordability, or trust.**

## Rev0259 addendum — children need portable school, health, protection, and care records

Rev0259 sharpens portability for children. Climate movement should not break enrollment, grade placement, exam history, disability supports, school-meal eligibility, vaccination records, chronic-care needs, guardianship, custody, social-work files, benefits, or child-protection referrals.

The portability packet should support rapid enrollment and service attachment even when documents are missing, delayed, damaged, or held by another jurisdiction. Privacy matters, but privacy should not become a reason children lose essential services [S400][S402][S403].

## Rev0260 addendum — portability must include devices, care records, decision supports, and adult-safeguarding claims

Rev0260 extends portability beyond identity, benefits, school, health, and claims records. When older adults, disabled people, medically dependent people, and care users move, portability should include assistive-technology records, repair / warranty records, device prescriptions, medication lists, dialysis / oxygen / refrigeration needs, home-care hours, personal-assistance arrangements, supported decision-making documents, guardianship or alternatives, adult-safeguarding files, accessibility accommodations, utility medical-baseline status, and accessible-housing needs.

The mobility-continuity rule is that the support package should travel with the person, not dissolve at the border between jurisdictions, shelters, providers, insurers, utilities, or recovery programmes. See `283` and `284` [S407][S409][S412][S413][S414][S416][S417].

## Rev0262 addendum — housing claims and tenancy records must be portable

Portability now includes housing claims: leases, rent ledgers, utility accounts, repair records, insurance claims, grant eligibility, proof of residence, school catchment ties, public-housing applications, disability-access needs, homelessness service records, and buyout / relocation entitlements. Without portable housing documentation, climate movement can erase claims and force households into informal, overcrowded, or unsafe arrangements.

House rule: **people should not lose housing rights because the hazard moved them faster than the paperwork.** See `287` and `288`.

## Rev0263 addendum — portability includes money, accounts, debts, claims, and remittances

Mobility continuity now includes financial portability. When people evacuate, relocate, migrate, return, or move repeatedly, they need benefits, wages, pensions, disability payments, child benefits, bank or mobile-money access, remittances, insurance claims, debts, utility accounts, tax records, school and health payment records, and complaints to travel or be safely re-established.

A mobility packet is incomplete if a person can cross a boundary but their payment rail, ID, claim, remittance corridor, benefit eligibility, or debt remedy cannot. Pair `274` with `289` and `290` [S447][S449][S455][S456].

## Rev0264 addendum — portability includes legal files, deadlines, and protective orders

Mobility continuity now includes legal-file continuity. When people evacuate, relocate, migrate, return, or move repeatedly, benefits, school, health, work, housing, care, debt, insurance, immigration, custody, guardianship, protective orders, disability accommodations, identity records, and appeal files must remain live.

A mobility packet is incomplete if people move but their legal clock, evidence file, protective order, claim, or appeal does not. Use `291` and `292` with `274` [S466][S469][S470].

## Rev0265 addendum — portability must be communicated and verifiable

Rights, services, records, benefits, claims, and remedies are not portable if people do not know how to invoke them in the new place. Portability packets should include plain-language instructions, multilingual support, offline information points, case-status updates, contact routes, and feedback channels.

Portability rule: **a portable right needs a portable explanation and a reachable help channel.** Use `293` and `294` with `274` [S481][S485].

## Rev0266 portability bridge — services must travel through transport and logistics

Rights, services, records, benefits, and remedies are portable only if people can physically reach the offices, clinics, schools, shelters, cash-out points, legal-help desks, and receiving agencies that recognize them, or if mobile teams can reach them. Portability packets should include transport, route-status, mobile-service, last-mile, and return-trip plans for displaced, isolated, no-car, disabled, and digitally excluded people [S487][S494][S501].

## Rev0267 portability bridge — records and benefits need powered access

Portable services and records need outage fallback. Identity, benefits, school records, prescriptions, bank access, claims, legal notices, and communication with agencies should work when power, broadband, cell service, ATMs, and payment systems are degraded. Pair with `293`, `294`, `297`, `298`, `289`, and `291`. [S509]

## Rev0268 portability bridge — cleanup proof and habitability status must travel

Portability now includes debris receipts, mold / habitability records, hazardous-material notices, safe-return status, landlord duties, contractor disputes, cleanup benefits, insurance / assistance proof, and environmental-health complaints. These records should travel when people move or return. Pair with `299` and `300`. [S523]
