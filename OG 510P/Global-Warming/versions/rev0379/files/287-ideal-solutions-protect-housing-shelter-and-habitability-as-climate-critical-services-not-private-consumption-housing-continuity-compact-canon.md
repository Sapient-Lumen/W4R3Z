# 287 — Ideal Solutions: Protect Housing, Shelter, and Habitability as Climate-Critical Services, Not Private Consumption — Housing Continuity Compact Canon

## Claim

Files `275`–`286` made food, water, WASH, health, disease ecology, schools, childcare, child protection, ageing, disability, medical dependency, care, workers, livelihoods, and labour shock absorption visible as climate operating layers.
This file adds the daily place where those layers are either made usable or made impossible: **housing, shelter, and habitability are climate-critical services, not private consumption.**

A climate programme can harden hospitals, schools, water systems, food hubs, shelters, and work rules and still fail if people return to homes that are too hot, too cold, moldy, smoky, flooded, structurally unsafe, unaffordable, inaccessible, disconnected from power / water / sanitation, or legally insecure.
The missing premise is blunt: a household cannot follow a heat warning, recover from a flood, keep medication cold, cook safely, attend school, stay employed, or relocate with dignity if the housing floor collapses.

WHO's housing and health guidelines treat unsafe and substandard housing as a public-health problem and provide recommendations on crowding, low and high indoor temperatures, injury hazards, and accessibility for people with functional impairments [S431].
The UN Special Rapporteur on adequate housing says the climate crisis severely threatens enjoyment of the right to adequate housing and calls for rights-compliant, climate-resilient, carbon-neutral housing [S432].
UN-Habitat's 2024 adequate-housing work describes the housing gap as enormous, with billions facing housing inadequacy, more than a billion people living in informal settlements or slums, and hundreds of millions experiencing homelessness [S433][S441].
The archive's conclusion is therefore: **every serious climate programme needs a housing-continuity and habitability ledger.**

## Fast rule

**Any climate packet that touches adaptation, recovery, buildings, cooling, clean heat, electrification, insurance, retreat, mobility, health, schools, care, food, WASH, workers, social protection, disaster finance, public procurement, or local government should prove that people can remain in or reach habitable, affordable, safe, accessible, serviced, and legally secure housing or shelter before climate hazards become injury, disease, eviction, homelessness, displacement, school loss, care failure, or household debt.**

That proof should name:

1. **the households and tenures at risk** — homeowners, renters, public-housing residents, social-housing residents, cooperative residents, informal-settlement residents, people in informal rentals, mobile-home residents, people in employer-provided housing, dormitories, shelters, encampments, temporary accommodation, collective centres, hotels, care homes, prisons, student housing, refugee / IDP settlements, Indigenous housing, coastal and floodplain communities, isolated rural homes, and people doubled up with family or friends;
2. **the climate hazards** — heat, cold, wildfire smoke, outdoor air pollution, flood, storm surge, riverine and pluvial flooding, groundwater rise, mold, damp, landslide, wildfire, drought, water contamination, sewage overflow, power outage, fuel shortage, vector exposure, debris, contaminated sites, and repeated loss;
3. **the habitability floor** — safe indoor temperature, shade, ventilation, clean air, moisture control, mold prevention, safe drinking water, sanitation, hygiene, drainage, electricity, backup power where needed, safe cooking, refrigeration for food and medicine, structural safety, fire safety, accessibility, egress, communications, pest control, and proximity to critical services;
4. **the tenure and affordability floor** — rent protection, mortgage relief, utility relief, tax / fee relief, insurance and deductible support where justified, repair grants, relocation assistance, anti-eviction rules, anti-rent-gouging rules, legal aid, documentation support, and protection for informal or undocumented households;
5. **the repair and retrofit route** — pre-disaster risk reduction, roof / envelope / drainage / fire / flood / heat / smoke upgrades, clean cooling and heating, one-stop repair offices, trusted contractors, material supply, inspection capacity, permits, grants, loans, landlord compliance, and post-repair quality checks;
6. **the temporary accommodation route** — cooling shelters, clean-air shelters, flood shelters, accessible shelters, hotels, host-family support, rent vouchers, safe sites, family unity, pets, possessions, food, WASH, schools, care, transport, work access, privacy, security, and transition plans back home or to permanent housing;
7. **the informal-settlement and homelessness route** — upgrading, drainage, shade, water, sanitation, tenure-sensitive risk reduction, no forced eviction disguised as adaptation, outreach, heat and cold survival support, safe storage, mobile services, and pathways to permanent housing;
8. **the anti-displacement guardrail** — climate retrofits, repairs, insurance changes, buyouts, redevelopment, resilience districts, and receiving-place investments should not become rent hikes, evictions, exclusionary eligibility rules, or speculative land capture;
9. **the receiving-capacity link** — when people move, destination housing, water, power, cooling, schools, care, health, work, transport, and community acceptance must be prepared before relocation is treated as a solution;
10. **the proof and correction rule** — indoor heat, smoke ingress, flooding, mold, utility shutoff, sewage overflow, inaccessible shelter, eviction, rent spike, repair fraud, contractor failure, return delay, repeated loss, or homelessness should trigger inspection, payment, remedy, code correction, financing correction, and after-action redesign.

## Why this belongs in the climate archive

Housing is where many climate risks become bodily and social risks.
A heat wave becomes dangerous when the room cannot cool.
Wildfire smoke becomes a chronic exposure when the home cannot filter air.
Flooding becomes disease risk when water, sanitation, mold, and repairs fail.
A power outage becomes medical risk when elevators, oxygen, refrigeration, pumps, communications, and cooling fail.
A disaster becomes displacement when rent, insurance, repairs, debt, documentation, and temporary accommodation fail.

Buildings files already cover emissions, retrofits, clean heat, cooling, and construction.
But housing continuity is not the same problem as building decarbonization.
A high-performance building can still be unaffordable.
A resilient retrofit can still displace tenants.
A buyout can still leave renters with nothing.
A shelter can exist and still be inaccessible, unsafe, overcrowded, or disconnected from schools, care, work, food, water, and records.
A recovery grant can still arrive too late to stop homelessness.

World Bank resilient-housing work argues for building better before disasters so households are safer before losses occur [S435].
GFDRR treats resilient housing as a disaster-risk-reduction and reconstruction field that should identify opportunities to reduce housing-sector risk before and after disasters [S436].
OECD's real-estate climate-risk work links physical and transition risks to location-specific real-estate pressures, risk transfer, hazard zoning, building codes, and financial mechanisms [S437].
The practical conclusion is that **housing continuity is adaptation infrastructure.**

## The compact canon

### 1. Treat home as a critical-service platform

Homes are not only assets.
They are platforms for sleep, heat safety, clean air, food storage, medication, schooling, caregiving, disability support, remote communication, work readiness, recovery, and social stability.
A climate packet that protects services but leaves the home uninhabitable is not protecting service access.

House rule: **ask what critical services are supposed to happen inside the home, and whether the home can still support them during the hazard.**

### 2. Make habitability a measurable floor

A warning that tells people to stay indoors is weak if indoor temperatures are unsafe, smoke penetrates, toilets fail, water is contaminated, or mold follows floodwater.
Housing-continuity packets should set local measurable floors for temperature, ventilation, clean air, water, sanitation, electricity, drainage, structural safety, and accessibility.

House rule: **do not rely on shelter-in-place instructions unless the place is actually shelter.**

### 3. Govern affordability as a safety condition

A safe home that becomes unaffordable after retrofit, insurance repricing, repair debt, rent increase, tax reassessment, or disaster displacement has not delivered durable protection.
Renters, low-income owners, informal residents, mobile-home residents, and households with weak documentation often absorb climate costs through eviction, doubling-up, debt, or homelessness.

House rule: **a housing climate packet should say who pays, who cannot pay, and which firebreak prevents safety upgrades from becoming displacement.**

### 4. Build better before the loss

The cheapest recovery is often the avoided collapse.
Pre-disaster roof strengthening, drainage, elevation, cool roofs, shade, passive design, clean cooling, weatherization, clean heat, fire-safe retrofits, smoke filtration, safe wiring, and accessible modifications can prevent the post-disaster repair queue from becoming a poverty trap.

House rule: **do not wait for disaster aid to fund the housing upgrade that was predictable before the disaster.**

### 5. Put renters and split incentives at the centre

Landlords often control envelopes, HVAC, drainage, windows, appliances, repairs, and insurance, while tenants experience heat, smoke, mold, outage, rent hikes, and eviction risk.
Climate policy that subsidizes property improvements without tenant protection can convert public adaptation money into private rent extraction.

House rule: **rental retrofits need rent-stabilization, anti-eviction, habitability enforcement, benefit-sharing, and tenant complaint routes.**

### 6. Upgrade informal settlements without using risk as a pretext for removal

Informal settlements are often highly exposed to heat, flooding, landslide, poor drainage, inadequate WASH, weak tenure, and service exclusion.
But treating risk as a reason for forced eviction can worsen vulnerability.
The first answer should be in-situ upgrading, service access, drainage, shade, cooling, tenure-sensitive protection, participatory risk mapping, and negotiated relocation only when protection in place is not credible.

House rule: **adaptation should reduce exposure without converting informality into dispossession.**

### 7. Treat emergency shelter as a continuity system

Emergency shelter is not a room count.
It is a temporary service platform: WASH, food, medical support, disability access, family unity, child protection, privacy, security, pets, transport, school continuity, worker access, records, legal help, communications, and pathways to durable housing.
Sphere's shelter and settlement standards sit beside WASH, food security and nutrition, and health because shelter is part of a life-with-dignity response, not a warehouse function [S434].

House rule: **a shelter that interrupts care, school, work, records, disability access, or safety is not a complete shelter solution.**

### 8. Prepare the repair ecosystem before everyone needs it

After a hazard, households compete for contractors, inspectors, permits, materials, insurance adjusters, grants, legal help, temporary housing, and transport.
Bad markets invite price gouging, fraud, unsafe repairs, delayed returns, and debt.
A serious housing-continuity packet pre-arranges repair crews, material supply, public procurement, standards, permit surge, quality control, and accessible complaint routes.

House rule: **repair capacity is a climate-critical supply chain.**

### 9. Connect housing to mobility and receiving capacity

Retreat, relocation, migration, and displacement fail when destination housing is treated as a private search problem.
The receiving place needs actual homes, not just welcome language: serviced land, utilities, cooling, schools, care, health, work access, transport, local finance, tenant protections, and host-community benefits.

House rule: **do not route people away from climate risk into housing scarcity.**

### 10. Correct every housing failure after each shock

A housing after-action review should not stop at damaged units.
It should ask who overheated indoors, who sheltered in a car, who lost medicines, who could not evacuate because of stairs or documents, who was denied rent assistance, who was evicted, who waited too long for repairs, who entered debt, which contractors failed, which codes underperformed, and which households were missing from the data.

House rule: **housing continuity improves only if hidden household failures become public correction signals.**

## Minimum housing-continuity packet

A formed packet should include:

- exposed housing stock, tenure, household types, and legal status;
- hazard profile for heat, smoke, flood, storm, wildfire, cold, water, sanitation, outage, and repeated loss;
- habitability floor and inspection method;
- affordability and anti-displacement firebreak;
- renter, informal-resident, mobile-home, social-housing, public-housing, and homelessness routes;
- repair, retrofit, contractor, materials, permit, and quality-control plan;
- emergency and temporary accommodation plan;
- links to food, WASH, health, school, care, worker, mobility, and social-protection ledgers;
- owner agency, funding source, trigger ladder, and public reporting;
- remedy, grievance, legal-aid, fraud-control, and after-action correction process.

## What this rules out

This file rejects:

- treating housing as private background while climate plans depend on people staying safely at home;
- decarbonizing buildings without tenant protection, affordability, accessibility, and habitability proof;
- shelters that provide a bed but break care, school, work, privacy, safety, WASH, or disability access;
- disaster recovery that counts units repaired while ignoring debt, rent spikes, eviction, mold, unsafe repairs, and delayed return;
- buyouts or retreat that help owners while abandoning renters and informal residents;
- informal-settlement policies that call displacement adaptation;
- resilience investments that raise land values while pushing exposed households somewhere worse.

## Compression rule

**Housing continuity is climate adaptation infrastructure: set a measurable habitability floor, protect tenure and affordability, repair and retrofit before losses compound, make shelters true service platforms, upgrade informal settlements without dispossession, prepare temporary accommodation and repair markets before disasters, link housing to receiving capacity and portability, and correct every indoor heat, smoke, flood, mold, eviction, rent spike, shelter failure, and delayed return before the next shock.**

## Rev0263 addendum — housing continuity needs household financial continuity

Housing-continuity packets now need financial-continuity fields. A home cannot remain habitable if the household cannot pay rent, utilities, repair deposits, insurance deductibles, transport, temporary accommodation, storage, mold cleanup, cooling, clean-air equipment, or document replacement. A safe retrofit can still displace tenants if the payment and affordability design fails.

Housing packets should therefore name emergency rent and utility support, repair grants, anti-debt guardrails, insurance advance routes, landlord / tenant payment rules, cash-out points, documentation flexibility, and rapid redress. Use `289` and `290` with `287` and `288` [S444][S447][S451][S455][S456].

## Rev0264 addendum — housing continuity needs tenant, owner, and informal-resident legal help

Housing-continuity packets now need civil-justice continuity. Habitability, repair, rent support, utility continuity, temporary accommodation, insurance, mortgage forbearance, informal settlement upgrading, and homelessness prevention all run through legal relationships.

Housing packets should therefore include anti-eviction guardrails, habitability claims help, document substitution for leases and title, tenant and informal-resident legal help, landlord-retaliation protection, repair-contract support, and appeal routes for shelter or housing denials. Use `291` and `292` [S458][S461][S466].

## Rev0265 addendum — housing continuity needs habitability communication

Housing-continuity packets now need communication pathways for indoor heat, smoke, mold, water damage, repair status, rent support, utility status, temporary accommodation, legal aid, and safe return.

Housing rule: **a home is not safely habitable because the hazard passed; households need usable information about the conditions inside and the remedies available.** Use `293` and `294` [S481][S483].

## Rev0266 housing bridge — shelter depends on trips and deliveries

Housing and shelter continuity require evacuation transport, accessible shelter routes, temporary-accommodation access, repair-worker movement, inspections, materials delivery, debris removal, utility crews, cash / legal help, and return. A shelter plan should not announce capacity without transport, accessibility, route status, resupply, and return logic [S487][S493][S494][S495].

## Rev0267 housing bridge — habitability depends on energy service floors

Housing-continuity packets now include indoor thermal safety, refrigeration, elevators, lighting, communications, medical-device power, rent / utility support, reconnection, generator safety, and repair access. Housing is not habitable if outage, shutoff, or unaffordable energy makes it unsafe. [S515]

## Rev0268 housing bridge — habitability includes debris, mold, ash, and safe disposal

Housing continuity now includes debris removal, mold remediation, ash and dust cleanup, hazardous-material disposal, safe reoccupancy, tenant / landlord duties, pickup support, contractor integrity, temporary accommodation, and proof that housing is safe to return to. Pair with `299` and `300`. [S526] [S527]
