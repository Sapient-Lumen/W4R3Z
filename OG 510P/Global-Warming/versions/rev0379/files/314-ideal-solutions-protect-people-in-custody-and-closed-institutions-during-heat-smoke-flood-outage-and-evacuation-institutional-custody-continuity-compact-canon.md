# 314 — Ideal Solutions: Protect people in custody and closed institutions during heat, smoke, flood, outage, and evacuation

## Claim

Climate service continuity must include people who cannot freely leave.

That includes prisons, jails, immigration detention, police custody, juvenile custody, secure hospitals, locked wards, some residential institutions, and other settings where the state or an operator controls movement, cooling, water, medication, information, evacuation, complaint routes, and family contact.

The archive already covers care homes and medically dependent people.
This note covers **custodial and closed-institution continuity**, where the core failure is not only vulnerability but loss of agency.

Recent evidence makes the gap concrete. A Nature Sustainability study of U.S. carceral facilities found large heat exposure burdens, including tens of millions of person-days of hazardous heat exposure annually in 2016–2020 and highly concentrated exposure in some states [S562]. The National Commission on Correctional Health Care states that extreme indoor and outdoor temperatures adversely affect people living and working in carceral settings and calls for standards to minimize extreme temperature exposure [S561].

## Fast rule

**Any climate packet that discusses heat, smoke, flood, outage, disease, water, evacuation, public safety, or civil rights must ask what happens to people who are confined, institutionalized, detained, or otherwise unable to self-evacuate or self-protect.**

## The compact canon

### 1. Define custody as a duty amplifier

When the state or an operator controls movement, it also controls exposure.
A person locked in a hot cell, smoky dorm, flooded unit, inaccessible ward, or powerless facility cannot independently choose shade, cooling, transport, cleaner air, water, medication, or evacuation.

House rule: **custody converts climate exposure into an affirmative duty of protection.**

### 2. Set indoor heat and air thresholds

Closed institutions need measurable thresholds for:

- indoor temperature and humidity;
- heat index / wet-bulb conditions;
- indoor PM2.5 during smoke;
- ventilation and filtration operation;
- water access;
- medication refrigeration;
- medical-device power;
- staff heat exposure;
- relocation or emergency release triggers where lawful and necessary.

House rule: **a heat plan without indoor logs is not a custodial heat plan.**

### 3. Do not use lockdown as the default climate response

Climate shocks can trigger lockdowns, staff shortages, transport restrictions, security concerns, and information blackout.
But lockdown can increase heat, smoke, medical, mental-health, violence, family-contact, and legal-access risks.

The packet should require rights-preserving degraded modes: hydration, cooling, showers, medical checks, phone access, legal access, outdoor or cool-zone access where safe, and monitored grievance channels.

House rule: **security cannot be the name given to unmanaged exposure.**

### 4. Prepare evacuation and transfer before the hazard arrives

Custodial evacuation is difficult and often delayed.
Plans should identify:

- transport capacity;
- accessible transport;
- receiving facilities with cooling, water, health care, and records;
- medication and medical-device transfer;
- family and legal notice;
- chain-of-custody documentation;
- protection for pregnant, disabled, older, chronically ill, juvenile, and medically vulnerable people;
- criteria for release, furlough, community supervision, or non-custodial alternatives when lawful and safer.

House rule: **evacuation plans that cannot move people before roads fail are paper plans.**

### 5. Include staff without letting staff shortage erase detainee rights

Staff also face heat, smoke, flood, disease, violence, and family-care burdens.
Continuity requires worker protection, backup staffing, training, rest, PPE, cooling, safe transport, and family-support policies.
But staffing stress cannot justify abandoning confined people to unsafe conditions.

House rule: **staff safety and resident safety are coupled duties, not competing excuses.**

### 6. Keep courts, inspectors, families, and monitors connected

Closed institutions are high-risk because harm can be hidden.
Climate-continuity doctrine should require:

- public facility exposure logs;
- independent inspection;
- mortality and morbidity reporting;
- family contact;
- legal access;
- disability accommodation;
- confidential grievance;
- court access and emergency remedy;
- after-action review.

House rule: **closed doors require open ledgers.**

## Minimum custody-continuity ledger

| Question | Evidence required |
|---|---|
| Who cannot self-protect? | facility census, medical vulnerability, disability, pregnancy, age, juvenile status, language, legal status |
| What is the exposure? | indoor temperature, humidity, PM2.5, power, water, sanitation, medication, crowding, staffing |
| What threshold changes operations? | cooling access, relocation, evacuation, medical transfer, release / alternatives, court notification |
| What contact remains? | family, counsel, monitors, courts, medical providers, interpreters |
| What remedy exists? | grievance, inspection, emergency court route, ombuds, public health order, regulator action |
| What was corrected? | HVAC, filtration, water, staffing, evacuation plan, population reduction, procurement, inspection standard |

## What this routes to

- use `314` for prisons, jails, detention, custody, locked facilities, secure hospitals, juvenile custody, heat behind bars, prison smoke, evacuation of confined people, carceral climate risk, or closed-institution rights;
- pair with `306` for thermal survivability and `311` for clean air;
- pair with `279` and `283` for health, disability, chronic illness, and medically dependent people;
- pair with `291` / `292` for legal remedy;
- pair with `303` / `304` for emergency response and family contact.

## Compression rule

**People in custody cannot self-evacuate, self-cool, self-filter air, self-secure water, or self-preserve legal access. Climate continuity in closed institutions therefore requires indoor heat and air thresholds, evacuation and transfer plans, health and medication continuity, family / legal contact, independent inspection, and public correction ledgers.**
