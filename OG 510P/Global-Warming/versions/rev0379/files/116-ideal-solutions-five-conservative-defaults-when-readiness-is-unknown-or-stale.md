# 116 — Ideal Solutions: Five Conservative Defaults When Readiness Is Unknown or Stale

## Claim

The archive already has:
- a readiness note (`111`)
- a live-discipline note (`112`)
- a rehearsal-discipline note (`113`)
- a readiness-assurance note (`114`)
- a partial-readiness note (`115`)
- and a burden-of-proof note for dirty delay, fallback, or exception (`108`)

What it still lacked was the shortest **unknown-readiness note** for the question:

**If a serious climate state cannot currently verify whether readiness is still real — because evidence is stale, checks are missing, joins have not been tested recently, or ownership has drifted — what should it do until that uncertainty is closed?**

That gap matters because systems often do not fail only by being plainly unready.
They also fail by **not knowing** whether they are ready any longer.
The drill happened, but too long ago.
The contact tree exists, but has not been tested since turnover.
The inventory is recorded, but nobody has checked whether the reserve still works.
The exception path is written, but the live authority chain is uncertain.
In those moments, the most dangerous institutional move is to treat **unknown** as if it were still **ready**.

A compact archive needed one short bridge between **readiness assurance** and **partial-readiness compensation**.
This note is a **compression note**, not a new doctrinal branch.
Its job is to reduce unknown or stale readiness to five conservative defaults that keep uncertainty from being silently promoted into confidence [S34][S55][S73][S83][S102][S103][S108][S111][S112][S113][S114][S115][S145][S147].

## Fast rule

**If readiness is unknown, do not assume ready: downgrade to “not proven,” verify the highest-consequence joins first, narrow risk appetite while status is unresolved, keep dirty fallback on strict proof gates, and force uncertainty onto a short closure clock.**

## Five conservative defaults when readiness is unknown or stale

### 1. Default to “not proven ready,” not to inherited confidence
A serious climate state should treat stale, missing, or unverified readiness evidence as a loss of proof, not as permission to keep the old badge.

What this usually means:
- readiness status expires unless refreshed on a visible clock
- inherited assurance from the last exercise, season, team, or vendor does not survive automatically
- unknown status is reported as a downgrade in confidence, not hidden inside vague comfort language [S55][S83][S102][S111][S114][S145]

Why it matters:
- the easiest falsehood in governance is not an explicit lie but an outdated assumption left standing
- unknown readiness becomes dangerous when institutions keep acting as if the prior proof still exists

### 2. Verify the highest-consequence joins, tools, and protection paths first
When readiness is uncertain, the first task is not to refresh every detail equally.
It is to test the pieces whose failure would do the most harm if the bad day arrived now.

What this usually means:
- check the contact chain, authority chain, critical inventories, reserve access, vulnerable-user protection path, and key data links first
- verify the joins between mission owner, lane operator, truth function, and protection backstop before lower-consequence details
- use the lightest live check that can answer whether the critical path still works [S73][S83][S103][S105][S111][S113][S114][S147]

Why it matters:
- stale readiness is most dangerous at the points where the system must hand off, authorize, protect, or repair quickly
- a conservative state tests the critical path before refreshing the ornament

### 3. Narrow promises, complexity, and risk appetite while status is unresolved
A serious climate state should not continue full-claim operation while it is still unsure whether readiness is real.

What this usually means:
- bound public claims to what is still evidenced rather than to the old nominal standard
- postpone optional complexity, fragile coordination, or thinly staffed initiatives until verification catches up
- run the simpler, safer operating posture while unknowns remain [S39][S52][S83][S102][S111][S114][S115][S147]

Why it matters:
- uncertainty is itself a reason to simplify
- unknown readiness plus unchanged operating ambition is a quiet form of overclaim

### 4. Tighten protection and tripwire sensitivity until uncertainty is closed
When readiness status is unknown, a serious climate state should become easier to alarm and quicker to protect.

What this usually means:
- lower tolerance for backlog growth, missed contacts, unsafe fallback drift, or service deterioration while readiness evidence is stale
- increase review cadence and management attention on the uncertain parts of the system
- move exposed users and critical services closer to the front of the protection queue until confidence is restored [S55][S83][S102][S103][S104][S111][S114][S115][S147]

Why it matters:
- if the system knows less about its own readiness, it should demand less proof before protective action
- uncertainty and complacency are a dangerous pair

### 5. Keep dirty fallback, exception, or delay on strict proof gates and a short closure clock
A serious climate state should not use uncertain readiness as a back door to broad dirty improvisation.
It should force unresolved uncertainty onto a short verification-and-repair clock while preserving the archive’s stricter proof rules for dirtier detours.

What this usually means:
- unknown readiness does not by itself justify broad dirty fallback or open-ended exception
- any temporary workaround remains bounded, reviewable, and tied to rapid verification or repair
- owners, deadlines, containment rules, and closure checks are attached to the uncertainty itself until status is known again [S34][S55][S83][S103][S108][S109][S110][S114][S115][S145][S147]

Why it matters:
- otherwise, “we are not sure” quickly mutates into “let the dirtiest familiar option run”
- the disciplined move is to shorten the uncertainty window, not to widen the loophole

## Minimal unknown-readiness posture

The smallest serious posture when readiness is unknown or stale is usually:

1. **downgrade status to not proven**
2. **verify the highest-consequence path first**
3. **simplify claims and operating ambition while unknowns remain**
4. **tighten protection and tripwire sensitivity**
5. **force uncertainty onto a short closure clock without relaxing dirty-proof gates** [S34][S55][S83][S102][S103][S108][S111][S114][S115][S147]

If these defaults are absent, then the system is not only uncertain.
It is uncertain **and still behaving as if certainty were owed to it**.
