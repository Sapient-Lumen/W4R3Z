# 212 — Ideal Solutions: Public Bargain — Protect Clean Necessity, Carry People Through Turnover, and Constrain Luxury Waste and Dirty Delay Compact Canon

## Claim

The archive already has a shortest whole answer (`195`), a one-page answer (`58`), a political-economy / social-contract canon (`131`), a world-bargain canon (`194`), a lived-experience canon (`160`), a service-floors canon (`161`), a workers / regions / host-communities canon (`162`), and a public-meaning canon (`211`).
That is enough doctrine.
But the front door still lacked one tiny bridge between **what the climate program is for** and **what it should ask, promise, and protect in ordinary politics**:

**what is the shortest public bargain a serious climate program should offer inside a country?**

This note supplies that bridge as a compression note, not a new doctrinal branch [S20][S25][S69][S70][S141][S143][S145].

## Fast rule

**The shortest public bargain is: make clean necessity easier, carry people and places through turnover, and put earlier, harder pressure on luxury waste and dirty delay.**

## The three-part bargain

### 1. Make clean necessity easier

The mass offer of a serious climate program is not austerity.
It is better access to the basics people need for decent life: reliable power, safe cooling and heat, cleaner buildings, affordable mobility, resilient water, and lower exposure to volatile fossil dependence.
So the public bargain should usually try to lower recurring burden, lower hassle, and raise reliability for ordinary clean necessity rather than making the median household the main shock absorber of transition policy [S16][S17][S20][S69][S70][S143].

House rule: **protect and improve clean essentials first.**

### 2. Carry people and places through turnover

A serious climate program changes assets, jobs, places, and routines.
That turnover should not be financed by abandonment.
When plants close, systems are retrofitted, land is repurposed, or new infrastructure is sited, the bargain should attach income protection, worker pathways, regional replacement investment, host-community benefit, cleanup duties, and usable recourse early enough to be believable rather than late enough to read as apology [S25][S59][S60][S62][S63][S71][S141][S143].

House rule: **do not ask a few workers, regions, or host places to eat transition risk for everyone else.**

### 3. Constrain luxury waste and dirty delay earlier and harder

The archive does not treat all throughput as equal.
A stronger bargain protects necessity while tightening first against affluent excess, prestige combustion, needless waste, methane leakage, new fossil lock-in, and incumbent delay stories that try to preserve easy high-carbon habits behind the language of prudence or optionality.
The point is not moral theatre.
It is to put earlier pressure where the burden is most bearable and the waste is most avoidable [S11][S20][S59][S69][S145].

House rule: **start harder against wasteful excess and organized delay than against ordinary need.**

## What this note clarifies

This note makes four front-door clarifications explicit:
- the archive’s public bargain is not generic sacrifice language
- the archive does not ask households to become unpaid systems integrators
- the archive does not call concentrated abandonment an acceptable financing strategy
- the archive does not treat luxury waste and basic necessity as morally or politically equivalent

Instead, the front-door public bargain is: **easier clean essentials, real protection through turnover, and harder constraints on luxury waste and dirty delay** [S20][S25][S69][S70][S141][S143][S145].

## Preferred use

Open this note first when the live question is any of the following:
- what a serious climate program should promise ordinary people in exchange for faster transition
- what should be protected, what should be supported, and what should be constrained harder
- how fairness should look in domestic climate politics rather than only in global burden-sharing
- why the archive protects necessity but tightens earlier against luxury waste and delay
- how public meaning, social contract, and transition discipline fit together in one short note

Then reopen `211`, `160`, `161`, `162`, `131`, or `194` depending on whether the surviving question is mainly about public meaning, everyday fit, protected necessities, workforce and place transition, the wider political-economy layer, or the world-scale bargain.

## Compression rule

**The shortest serious public bargain is not “everyone sacrifice more.” It is: make clean necessity easier, carry people and places through turnover, and constrain luxury waste, dirty delay, and new lock-in earlier and harder than ordinary need.**
