# 190 — Ideal Solutions: Package Logic, Complementarity, Sequencing, and Bottleneck Release Compact Canon

## Claim

The archive already has strong notes for:
- package logic and complementarity as a deeper design rule (`32`)
- the reference portfolio / physical portfolio (`57`, `130`)
- delivery bottlenecks and bottleneck release (`132`)
- sequencing / order of attack (`140`)
- dependency-light design (`172`)
- and the physical-first rule against paper progress (`189`)

What it still lacked was one joined first-stop note for a front-door question that keeps deciding whether a climate programme is a real system strategy or just a pile of individually respectable moves:

**what should ideal climate solutions look like when the decisive issue is whether the parts actually unlock one another rather than arriving as orphan technologies, orphan policies, or orphan targets?**

This note is not a new branch.
It is a compression note.
Its job is to gather the archive’s package-logic rule into one place so the programme does not keep mistaking a list of good instruments for a buildable pathway.

## Fast rule

**Prefer climate packages whose parts unlock one another and whose enabling conditions arrive before the obligations that depend on them. Demote orphan moves: clean-power targets without grids and flexibility, electrification pushes without tariff and installer reform, pricing without protection or substitutes, and phase-outs without replacement service.** [S103][S106][S111][S112]

## The compact canon

### 1. Climate programmes fail surprisingly often by orphaning good moves
A measure can be individually sound and still underperform when the complements it depends on do not arrive with it. OECD’s 2025 sequencing paper is explicit that climate instruments interact, that some work better when preceded by enabling conditions, and that public investment in infrastructure and innovation can make later economic and regulatory instruments more effective by making cleaner alternatives available, affordable, and accessible [S112].

House rule: **do not judge a climate measure only by its isolated logic; judge whether its companion conditions are present, near-ready, or deliberately bundled.**

### 2. Package logic is physical before it is rhetorical
This is not just a policy-design nicety. It is physical system logic.
Clean-power buildout without networks, interconnection, storage, flexible demand, and queue reform becomes curtailment, waiting, and local stress instead of delivered clean service [S103][S111].
Building electrification without envelope work, installer capacity, tariff reform, and intelligible offers can make the cleaner option look expensive, risky, or confusing [S51][S52][S53][S69][S106].

House rule: **if one component only becomes real service when several others move, treat the whole bundle as the unit of judgment.**

### 3. Sequence enabling conditions before hard ratchets that rely on them
A serious programme usually works better when it first builds or clears the cleaner path, then makes that path cheaper and easier, and only then tightens obligations, standards, or phase-outs that depend on it. OECD’s 2025 packaging and sequencing work says sequencing can enhance mitigation outcomes because instruments become more effective when enabling conditions are in place [S112].

House rule: **build substitutes and delivery first, then tighten sticks that rely on those substitutes being usable.**

### 4. Social and political complements are still complements
A package is incomplete if it contains only hardware and regulation.
Pricing without redistribution, phase-out without replacement service, or standards without visible household protection can turn a physically sensible move into a legitimacy problem [S69][S97][S112].
That is why the archive treats affordability, burden shape, payment form, and visible benefit sharing as package components rather than optional aftercare.

House rule: **if the cleaner path needs protection, compensation, or service substitution to stay legitimate, those are package parts, not side benefits.**

### 5. Bottleneck release is part of package logic, not a later implementation detail
Package quality is not just about having all the right boxes on paper. It is also about identifying the actual choke points that prevent the package from landing.
The archive’s delivery canon already says queues, permits, grids, workforce, standardisation, commissioning, and proof can convert good ambition into paper inventory if they are not released [S103][S106][S111].

House rule: **a package is weak if its bottleneck layer is treated as an implementation detail outside the main design.**

### 6. Good packages are joined, but still legible
The answer is not to make every climate move ever into one giant plan.
Good package logic means identifying the smallest bundle that makes the core move real: the enabling infrastructure, delivery capacity, social protection, and rule changes without which the headline measure will keep failing or stalling [S112].
This is why the archive pairs package logic with dependency-light design rather than with maximal complexity.

House rule: **bundle what is necessary for the move to work, but do not excuse needless synchronisation burden by calling it comprehensiveness.**

### 7. Prefer bundles that create later release, not later blockage
The strongest climate packages do not just solve today’s task. They make the next moves easier: cheaper clean power makes electrification easier, better delivery pipelines make later waves faster, visible household gain makes later tightening more durable, and interoperable standards make replication simpler [S103][S106][S111][S112].

House rule: **promote bundles whose first success releases the next bottleneck instead of creating a fresh one.**

## The archive’s package-logic screen

A future proposal should usually score higher when it passes most of these tests:

1. **Complementarity test** — are the decisive physical, institutional, and social complements present, near-ready, or explicitly bundled rather than assumed away? [S103][S106][S112]  
2. **Orphan-risk test** — would the headline measure still underperform badly if the missing complements lagged? [S103][S111][S112]  
3. **Enable-before-ratchet test** — does the order of attack build substitutes, throughput, and protection before tightening obligations that depend on them? [S69][S97][S112]  
4. **Bottleneck-release test** — does the proposal name and attack the actual queue, permit, workforce, tariff, finance, or proof constraints that would stop the bundle from landing? [S103][S106][S111]  
5. **Social-complements test** — are affordability, burden shape, payment form, and visible benefit sharing treated as package parts where they materially determine uptake or legitimacy? [S69][S97][S112]  
6. **Smallest-sufficient-bundle test** — is the package the smallest joined bundle that makes the move real, rather than an overbuilt list that increases synchronisation burden without releasing the core pathway? [S106][S112]  
7. **Next-release test** — if this package works, does it make later waves easier, cheaper, faster, or more durable? [S103][S106][S111][S112]  

## Preferred use

Open this note first when the live question is any of the following:
- why ideal climate solutions must come as bundles of complements instead of isolated “best technologies” or single-policy answers
- whether a proposal is strong in isolation but weak as a real package
- how to think about enabling conditions, substitutes, protection, and bottleneck release in one front-door note
- whether standards, pricing, phase-out, or electrification measures are being pushed ahead of the conditions that make them workable
- how to distinguish a smallest sufficient bundle from a laundry list of nice-to-have additions

Then reopen the deeper note that matches the surviving sub-question.

## Compression rule

**Ideal climate solutions should come as smallest sufficient bundles of complements. Build and bundle the conditions that make the cleaner path real, release the bottlenecks that would otherwise trap the package on paper, and sequence enabling conditions before obligations that depend on them. Demote orphan moves whose success mostly assumes the rest of the system will somehow catch up later.**
