# 31 — Ideal Solutions Under Irreversibility: Option Value, Lock-In Avoidance, and One-Way Loss

## Claim

An ideal solution to global warming should not only cut fast, allocate scarcity well, and remain robust when assumptions fail. It should also **treat irreversible losses and long-lived lock-ins as emergency priorities in their own right**.

This note adds an **irreversibility rule** to the canon. `27-ideal-solution-architecture.md` states what makes a climate solution ideal in general. `28-ideal-solutions-selection-rules-and-exclusions.md` screens what belongs in the portfolio. `29-ideal-solutions-allocation-under-scarcity.md` says what scarce capacity should fund first. `30-ideal-solutions-robustness-failure-tolerance-and-no-regrets.md` asks whether the package still works when assumptions break. This note asks a different question:

**which delays create one-way losses, close future options, or lock later generations into a harder world?**

## Why irreversibility belongs inside the ideal solution

Not all climate harms are symmetric in time.

Some delays merely postpone gains. Others destroy things that are slow, partial, or impossible to restore: stable coastlines, intact peatlands, species ranges, glacier-fed water systems, development options for vulnerable places, or urban form that has not yet been locked into decades of sprawl and exposure.

WMO says some consequences of recent warming are already irreversible over hundreds if not thousands of years, and that ocean warming and sea-level rise are irreversible for hundreds of years or longer [S109]. IPCC says some irreversible impacts have already occurred, that overshoot can cause irreversible impacts even if warming is later reduced, that hard adaptation limits have already been reached in some ecosystems, and that maladaptation can lock in vulnerability, exposure, and risk in ways that are difficult and expensive to undo [S110].

In that setting, ideal climate strategy cannot evaluate options only by annual tonnes abated or even by average cost. It must also ask:

- what becomes impossible if this is delayed?
- what damage becomes inherited rather than merely incurred?
- what future options are preserved or destroyed by this choice?

That is an option-value question, not only an emissions question.

## Two kinds of irreversibility the archive cares about

### 1. Physical and ecological irreversibility

Some losses are slow-moving but deeply persistent.

Examples include:
- ocean warming and sea-level rise that continue or persist over very long timescales [S109]
- ecosystem losses that cross adaptation limits or tip into collapse, especially where peatlands, wetlands, forests, reefs, or cryosphere-linked water systems are degraded [S36][S37][S38][S39][S72][S73][S74][S75][S109][S110]
- overshoot-driven damages that cannot simply be “undone” by later cooling or later carbon removal [S100][S101][S110]

This is why the archive treats anti-deforestation, peatland / wetland protection, watershed and aquifer governance, and overshoot minimization as more than side benefits. They preserve conditions that later spending may not be able to recreate.

### 2. Infrastructural and institutional irreversibility

Other losses are human-made lock-ins.

Examples include:
- new long-lived fossil assets that create decades of emissions pressure, political constituency, and stranded-asset conflict [S9][S23][S24][S25][S102]
- auto-forcing urban form, road-dependent settlement, and building patterns that hard-wire high energy demand and exposure for generations [S51][S52][S77][S78][S79][S80][S97]
- rebuilding in repeatedly flooded, burned, eroding, or uninsurable places in ways that deepen later retreat costs [S81][S82][S83][S84][S110]
- maladaptive infrastructure that appears protective in the short run but locks in vulnerability, social inequity, or future public liabilities [S40][S41][S81][S82][S110]

These are not just bad investments. They narrow the menu of future good choices.

## What ideal solutions do differently under irreversibility

### 1. They act earliest where damage is one-way

Ideal solutions promote earlier action when delay risks crossing thresholds that later money cannot fully repair.

That strengthens the case for:
- methane cuts and overshoot minimization [S8][S11][S100][S101]
- anti-deforestation enforcement and peatland / wetland protection before degradation compounds [S36][S37][S38][S39]
- groundwater, watershed, and floodplain governance before depletion, salinization, or settlement lock-in deepen [S72][S73][S74][S75]
- early exposure discipline in high-risk places instead of repeated rebuild-as-before [S81][S82][S83][S84]

The archive therefore does not treat every tonne or every adaptation measure as interchangeable in time.

### 2. They preserve future option value

Ideal solutions prefer measures that keep many future pathways open.

Examples:
- grids, interconnection, storage, and flexible demand preserve options across many decarbonization mixes [S55][S56][S57][S103]
- efficient buildings and passive design preserve affordability and reduce dependence on whatever future power mix exists [S16][S17][S51][S52][S53][S54][S104][S106]
- compact, transit-supportive growth keeps later mobility and energy choices easier than locked-in sprawl [S77][S78][S79][S80][S97]
- open risk information, risk-aware lending, and no-build or limited-rebuild rules preserve the option of orderly retreat instead of chaotic future abandonment [S81][S82][S83][S84]
- managed fossil decline preserves capital and political space for cleaner systems instead of entrenching incumbents that later demand rescue [S9][S23][S24][S25]

The archive treats these as ideal partly because they reduce emissions, but also because they prevent future decision sets from shrinking.

### 3. They distrust “repair later” reasoning

A recurring failure mode in climate policy is to assume that later clean-up will compensate for earlier damage.

The archive rejects that reasoning where the underlying harm is only partly repairable or where the “repair” itself requires future land, energy, money, water, and political tolerance that may not exist at the required scale [S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S100][S101][S110].

This is why the archive demotes:
- overshoot-as-plan [S100][S101][S110]
- fossil-lock-in justified by hypothetical future CCS or removals [S43][S44][S45][S46]
- ecosystem destruction paired with later restoration narratives [S36][S37][S38][S39]
- repeated rebuilding in unprotectable locations justified by short political cycles [S81][S82][S83][S84]

### 4. They distinguish reversible inconvenience from irreversible harm

Ideal strategy should be willing to accept some reversible discomforts or transitional costs in order to avoid one-way losses.

Examples:
- faster code changes, permitting reform, street redesign, or appliance standards may be inconvenient, but they are reversible [S52][S53][S59][S60][S61][S79]
- extinction risk, coastal exposure lock-in, peatland collapse, glacier loss, deep aquifer depletion, or decades of new fossil dependence are far less reversible [S36][S37][S72][S73][S81][S109][S110]

This matters because political systems often overweight short-run annoyance and underweight irreversible future damage.

## The archive’s irreversibility screen

A future proposal should usually be promoted when it passes most of these tests.

### 1. Irreversibility test
If this is delayed by ten years, what losses become impossible or much more expensive to avoid?

### 2. Lock-in test
Does this create long-lived emissions, exposure, land-use, debt, or institutional commitments that later generations would struggle to unwind?

### 3. Option-value test
Does this preserve multiple future decarbonization and adaptation pathways, or does it bet heavily on one uncertain future?

### 4. Repairability test
If the plan fails partly, can the damage really be repaired later, or only narrated as repairable?

### 5. Inheritance test
Does this choice hand future people durable assets and wider choices, or durable liabilities and narrower choices?

Measures that pass four or five of these tests deserve extra priority even when their narrow annual-abatement metrics do not fully capture that value.

## What irreversibility pushes the portfolio toward

### Protect-before-restore ecology
- anti-deforestation enforcement, peatland / wetland protection, mangrove protection, watershed restoration, and aquifer discipline before systems cross into slower or partial recovery [S36][S37][S38][S39][S72][S73][S74][S75]

### Right-first-time built form
- efficient new buildings, passive design, social-housing-first retrofit, compact urban growth, and street / transit choices that avoid locking in needless demand and exposure [S51][S52][S53][S54][S77][S78][S79][S80][S106]

### Exposure discipline earlier, not later
- open risk maps, insurance realism, repeat-loss limits, no-build / limited-rebuild rules, and dignified retreat before serial disaster and asset destruction force worse outcomes [S81][S82][S83][S84][S108][S110]

### No-new-long-lived fossil dependence
- managed decline, no-new-lock-in discipline where feasible, and fast investment in the cleaner systems that make retirement politically possible [S9][S23][S24][S25][S102][S103]

## What irreversibility makes the archive distrust more strongly

The archive becomes even more skeptical of:
- new fossil infrastructure defended by future-abatement promises [S23][S24][S43][S44][S45][S46]
- adaptation projects that preserve exposure growth rather than reducing it [S81][S82][S83][S84][S110]
- overshoot narratives that treat later removals as if they can fully reverse ecological and human losses [S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S100][S101][S110]
- restoration rhetoric used to excuse preventable ecosystem damage now [S36][S37][S38][S39]
- urban and transport strategies that accept new sprawl today because electrification is expected later [S30][S31][S32][S77][S79][S97]

These are not only risky. They are **bad ancestral choices**.

## One-paragraph compression

Ideal climate solutions should not only be fast, low-overshoot, and robust. They should also be **option-preserving and lock-in-averse**. They should move earliest where delay causes one-way losses, treat ecosystem and water-system protection as protect-before-restore priorities, avoid new long-lived fossil and exposure lock-in, build cities and buildings right the first time, and distrust all “repair later” stories that depend on future removals, future restoration, or future retreat after preventable damage has already been normalized [S36][S39][S72][S81][S100][S101][S102][S109][S110].
