## Core doctrine added

- A climate operating system is incomplete until critical objects — projects, permits, contracts, assets, buildings, products, meters, claims, grants, guarantees, resilience duties, support entitlements, recovery payments, and remedies — can be identified, linked, updated, audited, and corrected across handoffs.
- Every high-stakes packet should carry a minimum interoperable operating record: identifier, object type, boundary, location, owner, duty, authority, finance source, procurement link, metric, baseline, status, evidence source, timestamp, uncertainty, access rule, privacy/security rule, correction trigger, remedy route, and closure condition.
- Product, building, project, and service passports should be treated as proof carriers, not branding QR codes. They should carry enough interoperable evidence to govern procurement, installation, commissioning, maintenance, repair, warranty, reuse, recycling, retirement, claims, and remedies.
- Record architecture is governance architecture: the schema decides what can be seen, compared, paid for, procured, audited, challenged, repaired, copied, or retired.
- Climate data systems should make powerful actors legible without overexposing vulnerable people. Public-value transparency, privacy, security, redress, and ordinary-user burden reduction have to be designed together.
