rev0280 adds the rule that impact-to-intake is not recovery. A person can be registered, sheltered, assessed, or referred and still remain stranded. Climate service floors therefore need stabilization pathways: interim-to-durable housing, displaced-student stability, chronic-care continuity, nutrition-benefit continuity, utility-arrears protection, deadline tolling, and no-wrong-door benefit sequencing [S698][S699][S712].

The cube should now ask not only whether a service exists, but whether a household can move from first contact to a durable outcome without losing school, medicine, food, utilities, legal rights, or case status along the way.

## Rev0281 routing rule — after intake, ask whether the household can function

After routing to `389`–`396`, open `397`–`404` when the question involves case closure, household outcomes, credit / debt / forbearance, renters, work loss, transport to recovery tasks, lost contents / devices, lost documents / account access, or programme closeout. The front door should treat these as recovery gates, not follow-up services.

Use `397` for functional recovery outcome, `398` for balance-sheet repair, `399` for tenancy stability, `400` for livelihood return, `401` for recovery mobility, `402` for essential personal property and devices, `403` for digital identity / document / account recovery, and `404` for closeout / nonrecurrence.

## Rev0282 routing rule — after closeout, ask whether risk was actually reduced

After `397`–`404`, route to `405`–`412` when the question involves repeated losses, hazard mitigation, buyouts or elevation queues, land-use approvals, future-condition design values, risk disclosure, adaptation finance, benefit-cost analysis, green infrastructure, project maintenance, or whether a completed project actually reduced risk.

Use `405` for the mitigation pipeline, `406` for repetitive-loss and chronic-exposure registers, `407` for hazard maps / disclosure / future-condition design values, `408` for resilience finance stacks, `409` for benefit-cost and equity selection, `410` for no-new-risk land-use governance, `411` for nature-based-solution lifecycle performance, and `412` for post-project monitoring and residual-risk transfer.

## Rev0283 routing note — pathway and portfolio tests

Route unresolved long-lived decisions through `413`–`420`: adaptive pathways, model governance, real options, maladaptation gates, asset-portfolio stress tests, service-level contracts, unsafe-asset exits, and professional duty. The key query is no longer only whether a service floor exists; it is whether the floor can change course before physical risk outruns its design [S741][S744][S747][S750].

## Rev0284 route — assurance and cube normalization

After service floors, recovery pathways, risk-reduction pipelines, and adaptive pathways are named, route to the assurance layer: `421` for cube normalization, `422` for internal controls, `423` for corrective actions, `424` for independent audit and redress, `425` for open contracting, `426` for civil-rights and language access, `427` for workforce competency, and `428` for the high-stakes assurance case [S756][S761][S763][S768].
