## Fast rule

## The compact canon

### 1. Start by making the state able to tell what is real and to protect against obvious harm

The first order-of-attack is not glamorous.
It is what keeps the rest of the programme from running blind or losing legitimacy early:
- measured methane and other high-salience waste
- visible queue and project-tracking truth
- credible readiness classification
- heat, water, health, and lifeline protection where damage is already compounding
- narrow exception rules and liability so dirty delay does not quietly become normal governance

A programme that cannot tell what is happening, or cannot protect against repeated obvious harm, will mis-sequence everything else.

### 2. Then release the system throughput layer

Once the programme can see and protect, attack the bottlenecks that stop good projects from arriving:
- grids and interconnection
- permitting and siting throughput
- transformers, equipment, and critical component availability
- workforce, inspection, commissioning, and aftercare capacity
- standard contracts, standard offers, and repeatable public or regulated delivery pathways

This is the order-of-attack hinge because blocked throughput turns even good portfolio choices into paper inventory.

### 3. Then capture turnover moments and fast-warming waste streams

After the throughput layer starts moving, catch the places where one decision locks in years of downstream emissions, bills, and reliability:
- equipment replacement cycles
- public procurement and capital stock refresh
- fossil retirement moments
- methane leaks, venting, flaring, and refrigerant loss
- large efficiency waste in buildings, cooling, industry, and public systems

These moves should not wait forever, but they land better once the throughput layer can actually carry the cleaner alternative.

### 4. Then make clean service the easier mass default

As clean supply and delivery become more real, shift the main pressure to burden-shaping and mass adoption:
- lower upfront cost
- pay over asset life
- default people and firms into credible clean offers
- simplify eligibility, proof, remedy, and aftercare
- keep bills, comfort, reliability, and visible fairness at the centre

This is where the system becomes politically durable.
If the clean path is still the hard path after the buildout begins, the programme will stall or generate backlash.

### 5. Then harden the international and scarce-resource layer

Once the domestic machine is moving, push the cross-border surfaces that determine whether the world-scale answer compounds:
- cheaper and longer-tenor capital for poorer countries
- common product, procurement, and MRV standards
- cleaner trade and supply-chain rules
- coordinated treatment of methane, shipping, aviation, and traded industrial goods
- disciplined allocation of scarce clean fuels, carbon management, and removals to hard residuals rather than easy uses

This stage matters because a climate programme that solves scale only in rich jurisdictions has not solved the climate problem.

### 6. Throughout all stages, close dirty bridges rather than letting them become the plan

Sequencing is not a one-way ladder that ignores bad-day governance.
At every stage:
- keep dirty fallback narrow and temporary
- demote exception claims that no longer meet the burden of proof
- return quickly from detours to a cleaner routine
- turn incidents into harder standards, stronger readiness, and fewer future exceptions

A serious programme does not merely sequence good things.
It also sequences the retirement of bad temporary things.

## How to use this note

When two good ideas compete for limited bandwidth, ask:

1. **Which move makes several later moves easier or cheaper?**
2. **Which move removes a binding bottleneck rather than polishing a downstream output?**
3. **Which move prevents long lock-in at the next replacement moment?**
4. **Which move reduces the probability that backlash, exception, or fossil relapse will be needed later?**
5. **Which move helps poorer or slower-capital jurisdictions join the same direction of travel?**

Promote the move that scores better on those questions, even if the competing move is more visible or rhetorically attractive.

## Bottom line

**The mature archive's answer is not only what layers exist, how they interact, what to watch, and where to push. It is also a rough order of attack: establish truth and protection, release throughput bottlenecks, catch turnover and obvious waste, make clean service the low-burden default at scale, harden the international and scarce-resource layer, and continuously shut the exit from temporary dirty bridges.**
