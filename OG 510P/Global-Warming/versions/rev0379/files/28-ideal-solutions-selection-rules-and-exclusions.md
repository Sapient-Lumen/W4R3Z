# 28 — Ideal Solutions: Selection Rules and Exclusions

## Claim

The archive now has enough sector coverage that the main risk is **not** missing yet another climate topic. It is letting weak solution logics drift back into the canon.

This note is therefore an operational screen for future research. It answers a stricter question than “is this climate-related?” It asks: **does this belong inside the ideal solution portfolio, or should it be demoted, quarantined, or excluded because it solves the wrong problem the wrong way?**

The archive’s answer is simple:

> ideal solutions are the ones that cut hard before 2035, reduce overshoot and future repair burden, lower everyday exposure and bills, work under real delivery constraints, remain valid in EMDE, and can be measured well enough to govern.

For the shortest reusable version of that rule, see `59-ideal-solutions-fast-screen-promote-demote-quarantine.md`.

## Positive screen

Future additions should generally be preferred when most of the following are true.

### 1. They cut before 2035
The archive prefers options that reduce emissions and warming pressure quickly, because overshoot is damage, not a harmless waiting room [S100][S101][S105].

### 2. They shrink the denominator, not only clean it
The archive prefers options that reduce wasted electricity, fuels, materials, land pressure, or peak load instead of only trying to green an ever-larger baseline [S58][S97][S98][S99][S103][S104][S106].

### 3. They improve ordinary life while decarbonizing
The archive prefers solutions that lower bills, improve comfort, reduce dangerous heat exposure, clean the air, or make daily systems more reliable, because these are more likely to survive politics [S69][S85][S90][S104][S106].

### 4. They are deliverable by real institutions
The archive prefers solutions that recognize queues, permitting, workforce limits, procurement capacity, grid bottlenecks, and weak local administrative capacity rather than assuming them away [S59][S60][S61][S62][S63][S64][S96][S103].

### 5. They remain valid outside rich-country conditions
The archive prefers solutions that still make sense where capital is expensive, infrastructure is incomplete, and development needs are still rising [S70][S91][S92][S93][S94][S95][S96].

### 6. They can be measured, compared, and enforced
The archive prefers solutions that can be verified in the atmosphere, at the smokestack, at the meter, or in product standards rather than only narrated [S65][S66][S67][S68].

### 7. They do not quietly increase future removal dependence
The archive prefers options that avoid pushing the problem forward into large future removals, heroic assumptions, or weakly governed compensation mechanisms [S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S100][S101].

## What the screen keeps selecting

Applied honestly, this screen keeps selecting the same core portfolio again and again:

1. **clean electricity plus grids, storage, flexibility, and selected clean firmness** [S4][S55][S56][S57][S58][S103]
2. **efficiency, passive design, retrofit, efficient appliances, and sustainable cooling** [S16][S17][S51][S52][S53][S54][S104][S106]
3. **demand shaping and high-wellbeing low-throughput system design** [S58][S77][S79][S97][S98][S99]
4. **fast methane and nitrous-oxide cuts** [S8][S11][S19][S20][S21][S22]
5. **ecosystem and water-system protection as stability infrastructure** [S36][S37][S38][S39][S72][S73][S74][S75][S76]
6. **managed fossil decline instead of passive drift** [S9][S23][S24][S25][S102]
7. **adaptation, cooling, health, exposure discipline, and retreat where necessary** [S12][S15][S40][S41][S81][S82][S83][S84][S85][S90][S104]
8. **EMDE finance, guarantees, local-currency capacity, standards, and truth infrastructure** [S27][S65][S66][S67][S68][S91][S92][S93][S94][S95][S96]

That repeat selection is useful. It means the archive is converging on a stable design rather than wandering across fashionable themes.

## Exclusions and demotions

The ideal solution should **not** be centered on the following logics.

### 1. Price-only or virtue-only climate policy
Prices and household choices matter, but they do not by themselves build transmission, retrofit housing, restructure cities, install cooling access, or create local delivery capacity. The archive therefore demotes strategies that treat carbon prices, disclosure, or consumer choice as sufficient on their own [S58][S59][S60][S61][S69][S97][S106].

### 2. Removal-first or overshoot-as-plan
The archive demotes strategies that delay near-term cuts and rely on very large future removals to clean up later. Novel CDR remains tiny, while energy-system pathways that act later raise the eventual repair burden [S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S100][S101].

### 3. Hydrogen or e-fuels for easy uses
The archive demotes strategies that use scarce low-emissions fuels to avoid direct electrification where direct electrification is practical. Those fuels are more valuable in aviation, shipping, selected industrial uses, and only the hardest transport applications [S30][S32][S33][S34][S35][S100].

### 4. Fossil-plus-CCS as the default transition story
The archive allows targeted CCS and durable storage where residual process emissions or removal needs are real. It demotes strategies that use CCS mainly as a political device to preserve large fossil systems or justify new lock-in [S43][S44][S45][S46].

### 5. Adaptation-only fatalism
The archive treats civil protection as essential, but adaptation cannot indefinitely outrun unmitigated warming, repeated loss, insurance withdrawal, and rising physiological heat limits. Adaptation without strong mitigation is a holding action, not an ideal solution [S40][S41][S81][S82][S85][S90][S101][S105].

### 6. Austerity, deprivation, or anti-development as “demand reduction”
The archive distinguishes **high-wellbeing low-demand** from poverty. Many people still need more electricity, cooling, mobility, housing quality, and clean cooking. The target is excessive throughput and bad system design, especially in affluent contexts — not deprivation for those still underserved [S70][S71][S91][S97][S98][S99].

### 7. Nature-only substitution for energy and industry action
The archive treats forests, peatlands, wetlands, mangroves, and other ecosystems as core climate infrastructure. But it demotes strategies that use nature protection as a substitute for fossil decline, industrial decarbonization, or methane control [S36][S37][S38][S39][S100][S101].

## What this means for archive growth

This note exists partly to control archive size.

Future additions to the main canon should usually do one of two things:

1. sharpen one of the repeatedly selected core lanes above, or
2. tighten one of the exclusion boundaries above where policy confusion remains high.

If a proposed addition does neither, it is a candidate for **non-core**, **quarantine**, or **not being added at all**.

That is how the archive stays small without becoming shallow.

## One-paragraph compression

The ideal solution to global warming is **not** every plausible climate action at once. It is the subset that passes a strict screen: cut fast, reduce overshoot, lower demand and exposure, improve ordinary life, work in the world as it exists, remain valid for poorer countries, and be governed honestly. That screen repeatedly selects clean electricity, grids, efficiency, passive cooling, demand shaping, methane cuts, ecosystem and water protection, managed fossil decline, adaptation and health protection, and finance-plus-state-capacity — while demoting price-only, removal-first, hydrogen-everywhere, fossil-plus-CCS-by-default, adaptation-only, austerity, and nature-as-offset-substitute strategies [S58][S69][S97][S98][S100][S101][S103][S104][S106].
