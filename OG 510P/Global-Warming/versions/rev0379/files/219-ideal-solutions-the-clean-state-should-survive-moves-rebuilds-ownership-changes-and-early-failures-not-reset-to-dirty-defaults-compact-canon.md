# 219 — Ideal Solutions: The Clean State Should Survive Moves, Rebuilds, Ownership Changes, and Early Failures — Not Reset to Dirty Defaults

## Claim

The archive already says that a serious climate programme should work as one accountable path, should not ask ordinary users to gamble on bills, comfort, or reliability, and should usually let one informed yes start the joined clean sequence rather than a maze of repeat decisions and proof loops [S53][S69][S70][S71][S72][S74][S76][S77][S78][S79][S80][S81][S82][S118][S119][S137][S141][S143][S144].

One more front-door bridge belongs beside those notes:

**the clean state should usually survive ordinary life events. It should not quietly reset to a dirtier fallback when a household moves, a tenancy changes, an owner changes, an account changes, a building is rebuilt, or an early performance problem appears.**

In plain terms, climate success is still too fragile if it depends on one uninterrupted administrative story. People move. Tenants rotate. Buildings refinance. Providers change. Equipment fails early. Storms damage assets. Schools and clinics change administrators. Landlords relett units. Municipalities retender contracts. If each ordinary handoff recreates the old fossil baseline by default, then the programme has not really made the cleaner state stick.

So the archive’s shortest persistence rule is:

**once a cleaner and safer state has been reached, ordinary handoffs should usually preserve it; do not let moves, rebuilds, ownership changes, account changes, or routine early failures become quiet reset points back to the dirty default.** [S53][S69][S70][S72][S74][S75][S76][S77][S78][S79][S80][S81][S82][S118][S119][S120][S121][S137][S141][S143][S144][S161][S162][S164]

## Why this belongs in the front door

### 1. A joined entry path is not enough if the gain evaporates at the next handoff

`216` and `218` tighten the path into the clean state.
That matters.
But the archive also needs the shortest rule for what happens after apparent success.
A climate programme can be legible, financed, installed, tuned, and even liked, then still lose the gain when the household changes, the tenant turns over, the building is sold, the service account moves, or reconstruction starts from an old template.

So the front door needs the smallest anti-fragility rule after uptake:

**do not judge the pathway only by entry. Judge whether the cleaner state survives ordinary continuity shocks.** [S75][S78][S82][S118][S119][S143][S144][S161][S162][S164]

### 2. Many real reversions are mundane, not ideological

The archive often has to fight an implicit fantasy that backsliding mainly happens because people consciously reject climate action.
Sometimes that happens.
But many reversions are quieter.
The paper trail disappears at a move.
The new tenant does not inherit the support.
The replacement contractor swaps in a familiar fossil device after a failure.
The disaster rebuild copies yesterday’s baseline.
The utility or housing file does not carry forward the relevant facts.
The user never gets fast follow-up when early underperformance appears.

Those are not mainly failures of belief.
They are failures of continuity design, data carriage, procurement rules, default templates, repair protocols, and accountable follow-through [S69][S72][S74][S75][S79][S80][S82][S118][S119][S120][S121][S143][S144][S161][S162][S164].

### 3. The durable-default goal is reached through persistence, not just first adoption

`202` says the programme is solved enough only when the cleaner system has become the durable default.
That durable-default condition is not built only by more first-time clean adoptions.
It is also built by preventing routine reset points from recreating the dirty path over and over.

So the archive needs the shortest bridge from uptake to durable default:

**the cleaner state has to stick through ordinary life, not merely win the first decision.** [S75][S78][S82][S148][S161][S162][S164]

## What serious design looks like in practice

- attach durable clean gains to the asset, meter, building record, route, service relationship, procurement rule, or reconstruction template when that is the more sensible carrier than the individual alone
- carry forward known facts across ordinary handoffs instead of forcing full rediscovery and re-proof
- make post-damage rebuilds and major replacements ratchet upward into safer and cleaner states rather than recreate yesterday’s fossil baseline by default
- treat early-failure correction and aftercare as part of persistence, not as optional extras after the “real” work is done
- prefer programme forms where handoffs, moves, retenders, and replacements preserve the cleaner state automatically unless a real reason for change appears [S69][S72][S74][S75][S79][S80][S82][S118][S119][S120][S121][S143][S144][S161][S162][S164]

## Compression rule

**A serious climate programme does not only help people enter the clean state. It designs the clean state to survive ordinary life events, so that moves, rebuilds, ownership changes, account changes, and early failures do not quietly restart the dirty default.**
