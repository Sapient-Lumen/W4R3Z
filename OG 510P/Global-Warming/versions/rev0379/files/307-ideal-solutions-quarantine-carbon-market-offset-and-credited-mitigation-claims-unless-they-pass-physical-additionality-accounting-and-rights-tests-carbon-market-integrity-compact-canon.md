# 307 — Ideal Solutions: Quarantine carbon-market, offset, and credited-mitigation claims unless they pass physical, additionality, accounting, and rights tests

## Claim

Carbon markets can finance real mitigation.
They can also launder delay.
The archive should neither reject every credited mitigation claim as useless nor allow offset language to substitute for physical decarbonization.

The right doctrine is quarantine until proof.

Article 6.4 and voluntary-market governance now provide more formal machinery: UNFCCC's Paris Agreement Crediting Mechanism has a Supervisory Body, methodologies, validation and verification, a registry, and accounting rules intended to avoid double counting; the ICVCM Core Carbon Principles and assessment framework define supply-side quality principles for voluntary credits; VCMI adds demand-side claims discipline. [S135] [S280] [S281]

But formal machinery is not the same as physical integrity.
Carbon-market watchdogs continue to warn that Article 6 and voluntary-credit rules can leave weaknesses around permanence, human rights, additionality, accounting, and claim misuse if implementation is weak. [S546]

So the archive's rule is simple: credited mitigation may support the programme only when it passes a higher burden of proof than ordinary project finance, because it can be used to justify continued emissions elsewhere.

## Fast rule

**No carbon credit, offset, internationally transferred mitigation outcome, avoided-emissions claim, nature credit, removal credit, or results-based carbon-finance claim should count as climate progress unless it passes the physical-mitigation hierarchy, additionality, conservative baseline, leakage, permanence, double-counting, rights, use-of-claim, and host-country tests.**

## Carbon-market integrity canon

### 1. Start with the mitigation hierarchy

A buyer should first reduce its own gross emissions.
A jurisdiction should first reduce emissions inside the real emitting system.
Credits can finance additional mitigation or removals, but they should not become the main plan for avoidable emissions.

House rule: **credits are not a substitute for retiring dirty assets, cleaning power, cutting methane, electrifying end uses, or reducing wasteful demand.**

### 2. Separate contribution from offsetting

A climate contribution finances mitigation beyond the buyer's value chain without claiming to neutralize the buyer's emissions.
An offset claim says the buyer's emissions are balanced or counteracted.
These are different public meanings and need different proof.

House rule: **when proof is uncertain, claim contribution, not neutralization.**

### 3. Require additionality that survives incentives

A credited activity should not be something that would likely have happened anyway because of law, economics, common practice, existing finance, or foreseeable policy.
Additionality must be tested against realistic baselines, not against a fictional dirty world preserved for credit generation.

House rule: **a credit for the inevitable is not mitigation finance; it is rent extraction.**

### 4. Make baselines conservative and temporary

Baselines are political and technical risk points.
They should be conservative, updated, transparent, independently reviewed, and expire before they become entitlements.

House rule: **the dirtier the baseline story, the stronger the proof required.**

### 5. Count leakage honestly

A forest, land, waste, industrial, or energy project can push emissions elsewhere.
The credit must account for market leakage, activity shifting, rebound, and supply-chain displacement where material.

House rule: **moving emissions outside the boundary is not reducing emissions.**

### 6. Treat permanence as a liability, not a slogan

Stored carbon can reverse through fire, drought, disease, harvest, land conflict, management failure, policy change, insolvency, or monitoring collapse.
Removal and storage claims should carry durability class, reversal-risk assessment, buffer or insurance, replacement duty, monitoring term, and responsible party.

House rule: **temporary storage cannot honestly neutralize permanent fossil CO2 without a long-term liability structure.**

### 7. Prevent double counting and double claiming

A mitigation outcome cannot credibly count toward multiple claims in incompatible ways.
For Article 6 transfers, corresponding adjustments and authorization rules matter.
For voluntary claims, registry tracking and claim wording matter.

House rule: **one tonne cannot do two incompatible climate jobs.**

### 8. Protect host-country mitigation space

Carbon finance should not extract the cheapest mitigation options from lower-income countries or communities while leaving them with harder, more expensive NDC delivery.
Host-country authorization should be transparent about whether the outcome is used for the host NDC, another country's NDC, another international purpose, or a voluntary claim.

House rule: **a credit that helps the buyer but hollows out the host's transition is not climate justice.**

### 9. Require rights, land, and grievance safeguards

Nature, land, forest, soil, blue-carbon, and community projects can affect tenure, Indigenous rights, food access, customary use, benefit sharing, labour, surveillance, and grievance.
The packet should require FPIC where applicable, community benefit, no forced displacement, accessible grievance, independent support, and remedy.

House rule: **carbon integrity without rights integrity is not integrity.**

### 10. Make MRV independent and physically meaningful

MRV should measure the relevant physical outcome with enough accuracy, frequency, transparency, and independent validation to support the claim.
World Bank MRV guidance emphasizes baselines, additionality, leakage, overcrediting risk, monitoring, reporting, and verification as credibility conditions for carbon credits. [S136]

House rule: **MRV is not paperwork; it is the bridge between claim and atmosphere.**

### 11. Require buyer-claim discipline

Even a high-integrity credit can be misused.
Buyers should disclose gross emissions, reduction plan, credit type, vintage, registry, corresponding-adjustment status where relevant, durability, project location, safeguards, and the exact claim being made.

House rule: **a good credit can still support a bad claim.**

### 12. Quarantine credit types by risk

The archive should treat credit categories differently.

- Avoided deforestation, soil carbon, and ecosystem credits need very strong baseline, leakage, permanence, and rights tests.
- Renewable-energy credits in grids where renewables are already least-cost need strong additionality proof.
- Methane-abatement credits need measured emissions and repair verification.
- Industrial reductions need conservative product and process accounting.
- Carbon-removal credits need durability, reversal liability, and storage-quality proof.
- Blue-carbon credits need tenure, ecology, permanence, hydrology, leakage, and community safeguards.

House rule: **riskier credit classes need heavier proof and narrower claims.**

### 13. Keep credits below direct public investment when the public can act directly

Where a public authority can regulate, procure, subsidize, build, retire, or enforce directly, carbon credits may be a weaker instrument than ordinary policy.
Credits should not become a way to avoid regulation.

House rule: **do not use a market instrument to simulate what a law could require.**

### 14. Build a credit-use ledger

Every credited-mitigation claim should publish:

- activity type and location;
- host authorization and corresponding-adjustment status where applicable;
- buyer, seller, registry, vintage, methodology, verifier;
- baseline and additionality rationale;
- leakage assessment;
- permanence / durability class and reversal liability;
- safeguards, FPIC / rights status where relevant, grievance route;
- credit retirement and claim wording;
- buyer gross emissions and reduction pathway;
- whether the credit is contribution, compensation, neutralization, or NDC transfer.

House rule: **if the claim cannot survive a public ledger, it should not count.**

### 15. Define the quarantine verdicts

A credit claim can receive one of five verdicts.

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `countable mitigation finance` | strong proof, no incompatible claim, supports real additional outcome |
| `contribution only` | useful finance, but not valid as neutralization / offset |
| `restricted claim` | may be used with narrow wording and disclosed risks |
| `quarantined` | material uncertainty or missing proof; cannot count yet |
| `rejected` | false baseline, double counting, rights harm, no additionality, or claim misuse |

House rule: **uncertainty should narrow claims before it inflates them.**

## Minimum carbon-market integrity ledger

| Question | Evidence required |
|---|---|
| What is being claimed? | Offset, contribution, neutralization, NDC transfer, result-based finance, removal, avoided emission, reduction. |
| What happened physically? | Measured reduction or removal, method, monitoring, baseline, uncertainty, verification. |
| Would it have happened anyway? | Legal, financial, common-practice, policy, and investment additionality tests. |
| Did emissions move? | Leakage, rebound, market displacement, boundary effects. |
| How durable is it? | Storage class, reversal risks, monitoring term, buffer, insurance, replacement liability. |
| Who else counts it? | Registry, authorization, corresponding adjustment, retirement, host NDC use, buyer claim. |
| Who bears local effects? | Land / tenure, Indigenous rights, labour, community benefit, grievance, safeguards, remedy. |
| Is the buyer still reducing? | Gross emissions, capex, transition plan, fossil-expansion status, credit share, claim wording. |

## What this rules out

It rules out offset-first strategies.
It rules out net-zero claims that hide gross emissions.
It rules out credits with no additionality, weak baselines, uncounted leakage, temporary storage pretending to neutralize permanent emissions, double counting, vague rights safeguards, undisclosed host-country implications, or buyer claims that imply more certainty than the evidence supports.
It rules out treating a registry entry as atmospheric proof.

## Compression rule

**Carbon-market claims stay quarantined until they prove physical mitigation, additionality, conservative baselines, leakage control, permanence or durability liability, no double counting, host-country integrity, rights safeguards, independent MRV, disciplined buyer claims, and a public credit-use ledger. Contribution can be useful; offsetting requires a much higher proof burden.**
