# Nuclear delivery finance and open contracting refactor

A nuclear-positive cube must be delivery-positive, not merely technology-positive. Cost and schedule failures can destroy public trust and crowd out other climate investments. Therefore, nuclear spending must be traceable from policy preference to procurement, contract milestones, construction progress, fuel readiness, grid interconnection, workforce, and delivered megawatt-hours.

## Delivery rule

Nuclear projects should be favored when they are packaged as repeatable fleet delivery or credible life-extension/uprate programs, and disfavored when they appear as one-off prestige megaprojects without open milestone evidence.

The cube should distinguish:

- existing fleet continuity;
- uprates and life extension;
- restarts;
- repeat-build large reactors;
- SMR/advanced reactor first-of-a-kind demonstration;
- nth-of-a-kind modular deployment;
- industrial heat or high-load customer colocation;
- desalination or water-security coupling.

Each path has different evidence needs. IEA and DOE nuclear outlooks emphasize policy, financing, construction, supply-chain and delivery risks, not just theoretical decarbonization value [S771][S776]. The World Bank Group/IAEA partnership signals renewed development-finance interest, but also frames nuclear development as safe, secure and responsible [S778]. SMR dashboards track progress across financing, siting, licensing, fuel, engagement and supply chain [S780][S784].

## Open-contracting corollary

Nuclear preference must increase—not relax—open contracting. The cube should track:

- public need case;
- technology selection record;
- procurement method;
- contract form;
- change orders and claims;
- milestone payments;
- fuel contract dependencies;
- waste and decommissioning obligations;
- ratepayer/taxpayer risk allocation;
- public complaint and challenge pathways.

## Anti-greenwash rule

No nuclear project should receive high maturity merely for being low-carbon. It must show delivery discipline.


## Rev0298 nuclear human-capital refactor note

The cube's pro-nuclear orientation is now explicitly conditioned by workforce depth, operator licensing, regulator capacity, safety/quality culture, knowledge retention, craft labor, and public training pathways. Nuclear remains favored for clean firm power and system benefit, but human-capital and institutional-capacity gaps are binding maturity caps rather than implementation footnotes. Sources: [S864], [S868], [S871], [S873], [S875].
