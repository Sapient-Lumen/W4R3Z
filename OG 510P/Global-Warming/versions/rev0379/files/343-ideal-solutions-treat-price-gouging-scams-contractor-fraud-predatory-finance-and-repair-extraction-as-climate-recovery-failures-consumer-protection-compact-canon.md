# 343 — Ideal Solutions: Treat price gouging, scams, contractor fraud, predatory finance, and repair extraction as climate recovery failures

## Claim

Recovery markets are climate infrastructure. **When price gouging, scams, contractor fraud, predatory finance, identity theft, and insurance-check abuse spread after a disaster, adaptation fails at the household interface.**

FTC guidance warns people to check contractors, get written contracts, verify licensing and insurance, and report fraud after weather emergencies and natural disasters [S608]. Federal consumer-protection agencies have also warned that scammers exploit disasters through fee-for-relief claims, payment-app demands, impersonation, and insurance-check schemes [S609]. The archive's rule is that these are not merely consumer tips; they are recovery-throughput controls.

## Compact rule

**Fast recovery requires a safe recovery market before the shock.**

The packet should prebuild verified contractor pools, emergency repair standards, licensing checks, price monitoring, complaint hotlines, legal-aid links, multilingual warnings, insurer / bank safeguards, and rapid enforcement. It should also prevent anti-fraud systems from excluding the very people most likely to lack documents, credit, online access, stable addresses, or formal contracts.

## Minimum packet

A consumer-protection recovery packet includes:

1. verified contractor and inspector roster;
2. emergency contract and payment guidance;
3. price-monitoring for hotels, generators, fuel, repairs, transport, food, and supplies;
4. complaint intake that works offline and in multiple languages;
5. legal-aid / ombud routes for claims, debt, eviction, benefit denial, and contractor disputes;
6. insurer, bank, and payment-provider safeguards for checks, assignments, scams, and identity theft;
7. rapid-redress and enforcement metrics;
8. public warnings through trusted channels.

## Failure modes

The bad version sends cash into a broken repair market. Households then meet unlicensed contractors, inflated prices, hotel gouging, fake charities, impersonators, debt collectors, aggressive lenders, and claims middlemen. The visible metric says assistance was delivered; the real metric says loss was converted into extraction.

The archive's rule: **cash plus scarcity without protection is a transfer opportunity for predators.**
