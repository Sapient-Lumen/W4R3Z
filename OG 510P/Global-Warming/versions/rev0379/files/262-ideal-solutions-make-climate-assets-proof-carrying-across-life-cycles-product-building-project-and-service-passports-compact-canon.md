# 262 — Ideal Solutions: Make Climate Assets Proof-Carrying Across Life Cycles — Product, Building, Project, and Service Passports Compact Canon

## Claim

`261` adds the data-spine rule: the climate operating system needs interoperable records.
This companion note answers the more concrete carrier question:

**what should travel with the asset, product, building, project, or service so proof survives the life cycle?**

Climate work increasingly depends on objects that move through many hands: batteries, transformers, heat pumps, refrigerants, grid components, buses, chargers, clean-cooking devices, building systems, low-carbon steel and cement, resilience works, public-facility retrofits, recovery projects, carbon-management assets, and reusable materials.
If their records do not travel, claims become unverifiable, warranties fail, maintenance disappears, circularity breaks, procurement cannot compare bids, and dirty or unsafe replacements return at the next decision point.

So the doctrine is:

**where a climate-relevant object carries public value, risk, duty, claim, warranty, material value, or remedy across time, it should carry a minimum proof passport: enough structured evidence to verify what it is, what was promised, what was delivered, what must be maintained, what can be reused or recycled, and what happens if performance fails.**

Rev0250 adds the passport-security corollary: a proof passport should not depend on a vendor portal, QR code, cloud account, or automated verifier that can disappear, be manipulated, expose sensitive data, or deny repair without appeal. Passport systems need cyber resilience, data portability, privacy rules, offline or exported records for essential use, and model-governance rules where AI validates claims; see `263` and `264` [S301][S302][S305][S306][S307][S310].

Rev0252 adds the circularity corollary: a proof passport should also carry enough fields to support repair, spare parts, software support, reuse, second life, decommissioning, recycling, recovered-material claims, and asset-closure proof. The Global Battery Alliance Battery Passport points in the same direction for battery supply chains, from mining to recycling [S325].

## Fast rule

**Do not treat a passport as a branding QR code. A climate passport should carry the minimum life-cycle record needed for procurement, installation, commissioning, operation, maintenance, repair, reuse, recycling, handoff, claim verification, and remedy. It should reduce rediscovery and false claims, not create a new paperwork wall that only incumbents can survive.**

## Why this note is not redundant

- `220` says the continuity packet must travel across handoffs.
- `261` defines the interoperable data spine.
- `248` governs procurement platforms and standard service contracts.
- `250` counts completion at stable clean service.
- `257` controls climate claims.
- `259` makes duties enforceable.

This note adds the proof-carrier rule:

**some climate proof should travel with the thing itself — or with the building, project, meter, site, contract, or service relationship that carries its real-world meaning.**

## The compact canon

### 1. Start by deciding what kind of passport is needed

Not all climate records belong to the same carrier.

Common passport types include:
- **product passport**: materials, components, embodied carbon, durability, repairability, hazardous content, recycled content, provenance, and end-of-life options
- **building passport**: envelope, heating, cooling, wiring, ventilation, embodied carbon, hazards, retrofit history, performance, tenancy-relevant obligations, and future-ready upgrade route
- **project passport**: sponsor, location, permits, finance, procurement, safeguards, construction status, commissioning, O&M, aftercare, and public outcome
- **service passport**: clean heat, cooling, mobility, public-facility, resilience, or recovery service obligations, performance, support, and recourse
- **site or meter passport**: interconnection, capacity, flexibility, outage, backup, criticality, and service-floor facts
- **claim passport**: boundary, baseline, metric, method, verification, uncertainty, correction route, and expiration

House rule: **choose the carrier that preserves the fact through the decision where it matters.**

### 2. Use passports where life-cycle proof affects climate value

Proof-carrying assets are most useful where the object or service is:
- expensive
- public-funded
- safety-critical
- emissions-relevant
- material-intensive
- warranty-dependent
- high-maintenance
- repeatedly transferred
- claimed as low-carbon, circular, resilient, or clean
- eligible for procurement preference, subsidy, credit, or public finance
- likely to be reused, refurbished, recycled, or retired under a duty

Priority categories include:
- batteries and battery materials
- transformers, cables, conductors, switchgear, and grid components
- heat pumps, air conditioners, refrigerants, and thermal systems
- public buses, rail components, chargers, and depots
- solar, storage, and inverters in public or subsidised systems
- clean-cooking equipment and service systems
- low-carbon steel, cement, concrete, aluminium, and chemicals
- public buildings, social housing, schools, hospitals, shelters, and water systems
- flood, heat, fire, and critical-service resilience assets
- disaster-recovery reconstruction projects
- methane repair systems and monitoring equipment
- carbon-management, storage, and removal assets

House rule: **the stronger the claim, the public value, or the future handoff, the stronger the passport case.**

### 3. Keep the minimum passport concrete

For a product, project, building, or service, the minimum useful passport often includes:

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Identity | What this object, project, building, site, or service is |
| Carrier | Product, building, meter, site, contract, project, claim, or service relationship |
| Origin / provenance | Where material, component, or service responsibility begins |
| Specification | What standard, tender, code, or claim it was supposed to meet |
| Climate attribute | Embodied carbon, operating energy, methane, refrigerant, resilience, circularity, or protection outcome |
| Boundary and method | What is counted, over what life cycle, using what method |
| Verification | Who checked it, how, when, and with what assurance level |
| Installation / delivery | Where it was placed, by whom, and when |
| Commissioning | Whether it worked at handover and what test passed |
| O&M duty | Who must maintain, service, inspect, update, or replace parts |
| Warranty / remedy | Who fixes underperformance and by what route |
| Handoff rule | What travels to a new owner, tenant, operator, contractor, or agency |
| Circularity / end of life | Repair, reuse, refurbishment, take-back, recycling, safe disposal, or retirement route |
| Update cadence | When facts refresh and when old facts expire |
| Access rule | What is public, regulator-visible, purchaser-visible, or protected |

House rule: **a passport that cannot support procurement, commissioning, maintenance, handoff, reuse, or remedy is only a label.**

### 4. Make passports serve procurement first

Procurement is where many climate attributes become real or fake.
A passport should make it easier to compare bids, enforce standards, track delivery, and verify public value.

Procurement platforms can use passport data to check:
- conformance with standard specifications
- embodied-carbon or efficiency thresholds
- refrigerant and hazardous-content rules
- recycled-content and traceability claims
- warranty and aftercare obligations
- delivery milestones and payment gates
- supplier conflicts, provenance, and eligibility
- maintenance costs over life, not only purchase price
- end-of-life value and producer responsibility

Open contracting and infrastructure data standards make this more useful when contract-level and project-level records can be linked rather than published as disconnected fragments [S297][S298].

House rule: **if public procurement rewards a climate attribute, the attribute should be visible in the proof carrier.**

### 5. Make passports serve aftercare and repair

Climate assets fail politically when they are installed but not maintained.
A useful passport should help the operator or user answer:
- what was installed
- who installed it
- what performance was promised
- what maintenance is due
- what consumables or refrigerants are allowed
- what fault codes or weak signals mean
- who is responsible for warranty repair
- what spare parts or substitutions are acceptable
- what data are needed to close the service ticket

House rule: **the passport should shorten the path from underperformance to repair.**

### 6. Make passports serve circularity and material security

The clean transition is also a materials transition.
If valuable components and minerals cannot be identified, recovered, reused, or recycled, the programme burns more primary material than necessary and worsens supply-chain pressure.

A passport can support circularity by carrying:
- material composition
- recycled content
- hazardous substances
- disassembly instructions
- repairability information
- component serials where useful
- take-back obligations
- recycling pathway
- second-life eligibility
- safety warnings

Digital product-passport policy is moving in this direction: the European Commission describes the Digital Product Passport under the 2024 Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation as a way to store and share product sustainability, durability, and environmental data for consumers, businesses, and public authorities [S295]. UNECE and ISO also frame DPP work as a digital language and trust architecture for product traceability [S296].

House rule: **do not ask circularity to scale while hiding what the object is made of and who can take it back.**

### 7. Make passports reduce ordinary-user burden

Passports should not make households, renters, small firms, or municipalities reassemble proof already known to the system.

They should support:
- prefilled rebate or support applications
- warranty repair without document scavenging
- tenant-visible performance facts where relevant
- buyer and renter disclosure without forcing expert interpretation
- service provider handoff
- insurance or recovery proof after damage
- continued eligibility when ownership or tenancy changes
- safer replacement after failure

House rule: **the passport should move proof upstream, not push paperwork downstream.**

### 8. Do not confuse transparency with consent or safety

Passports can expose commercially sensitive, security-sensitive, Indigenous, household, or personal information.
They can also create new discrimination if building or location risk data are released without protections.

Design should therefore distinguish:
- public climate claims and public-money records
- purchaser-visible product and warranty facts
- regulator-visible compliance facts
- operator-visible maintenance and safety facts
- privacy-protected household or occupant facts
- security-protected infrastructure details
- community-governed cultural, land, or Indigenous data

House rule: **make the climate claim auditable without making people or critical infrastructure needlessly exposed.**

### 9. Link passports to remedies

Proof should have consequences.
The passport should say what happens when:
- the product fails the specification
- embodied-carbon or recycled-content claims are false
- installation fails commissioning
- maintenance is missed
- dangerous substitution occurs
- warranty service is denied
- public procurement preference was wrongly granted
- recovery reconstruction violates safer-rebuild rules
- a circularity or take-back promise is not honoured

Remedies may include repair, replacement, claim correction, payment holdback, clawback, warranty enforcement, supplier suspension, consumer redress, reprocurement, or regulator referral.

House rule: **a proof carrier without a remedy route is a scrapbook.**

### 10. Keep passports interoperable with national and international reporting without pretending they are the same thing

A product or project passport is not a national inventory.
A BTR is not a maintenance record.
A corporate disclosure taxonomy is not a spare-parts ledger.
But each can strengthen the others when identifiers, boundaries, and methods are compatible enough to map.

Good design allows:
- project evidence to feed public spending and outcome ledgers
- product and material data to support procurement and industrial policy
- building facts to support retrofit sequencing and affordability support
- claim records to support disclosure and enforcement
- recovery-project data to support adaptation and loss-reduction evidence
- measured emissions data to challenge weak reporting where appropriate [S293][S294][S299]

House rule: **make records mappable, not identical.**

### 11. Do not let passports become an incumbent moat

If compliance requires expensive bespoke systems, proprietary portals, or constant consultant mediation, passports can entrench large incumbents and exclude smaller suppliers, municipalities, cooperatives, and poorer jurisdictions.

A fair passport system should use:
- open or common schemas where possible
- simple templates for routine cases
- public or shared infrastructure for smaller actors
- staged requirements by product class and risk
- technical assistance
- transitional safe harbours with clear deadlines
- low-cost verification options for low-risk claims
- anti-vendor-lock-in rules

House rule: **passport proof should raise quality, not raise entry barriers for honest actors.**

### 12. Treat the passport as living memory, not one-time compliance

The most valuable record is often not the initial declaration.
It is the updated record after installation, maintenance, repair, handoff, disaster, recall, reuse, or retirement.

So the passport should have update triggers:
- installation
- commissioning
- inspection
- major repair
- refrigerant service
- component substitution
- ownership or operator change
- public claim use
- performance failure
- warranty event
- storm, flood, fire, heat, or outage damage
- reuse or refurbishment
- recycling or disposal

House rule: **a passport that never updates becomes a historic label, not operating memory.**

## Minimum proof-passport checklist

Before creating a passport requirement, answer:

1. What decision will the passport improve?
2. What object or relationship should carry the record?
3. Which climate attribute matters physically?
4. What proof burden is proportionate?
5. Who updates the record?
6. Who can access which fields?
7. What information is privacy- or security-sensitive?
8. How does the record support procurement, commissioning, O&M, warranty, handoff, reuse, recycling, or remedy?
9. What happens if the record is wrong, stale, or misleading?
10. How does the passport map to the broader data spine?

## What this note demotes

This note demotes:
- QR-code green branding with no enforcement value
- one-time certificates that vanish at installation
- product labels that do not help repair, reuse, or recycling
- building records that disappear at sale or tenancy change
- procurement claims that cannot be checked after award
- warranties no ordinary user can activate
- circularity promises without composition, disassembly, or take-back facts
- public subsidies for climate attributes that cannot be traced
- proof systems so heavy that honest small actors are excluded

### Rev0252 bridge: the passport should close at end of life
Proof passports should now carry enough information to support repair, reuse, second life, decommissioning, recycling, waste-shipment compliance, recovered-material claims, and asset-closure proof. A passport that can sell an asset but cannot safely retire it is incomplete. Use `267` for circular-design fields and `268` for end-of-life infrastructure fields [S320][S321][S322][S324][S325][S326][S328].

## Compression rule

**Where climate value travels with a product, building, project, site, meter, contract, claim, or service relationship, give it a proof passport: enough structured life-cycle evidence to buy it honestly, install it correctly, operate it reliably, repair it quickly, hand it off without rediscovery, reuse or recycle it safely, survive vendor and cyber failure, and correct false claims or underperformance.**
