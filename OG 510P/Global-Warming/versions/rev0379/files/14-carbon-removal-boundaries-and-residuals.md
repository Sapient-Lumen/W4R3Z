# 14 — Carbon Removal Boundaries and Residuals

## Claim

The archive treats carbon dioxide removal (CDR) and carbon capture as **necessary but subordinate**.

They belong inside the solution only under strict boundaries:
- first, **gross emissions cuts must dominate**
- second, removals should be used mainly for **hard-to-abate residual emissions and overshoot repair**
- third, claims must rest on **durable storage, credible MRV, and explicit long-run liability**
- fourth, CDR and CCS must **not** be allowed to function as licenses for continued fossil expansion

This note exists because the archive had the slogan — “reserve CDR for residuals” — but not yet the doctrine.

## Why the archive upgrades this lane

IPCC is clear that some deployment of CDR is unavoidable if the world is to reach net zero and counterbalance hard-to-abate residual emissions [S1]. But the scale-and-timing picture is easy to misread.

State of CDR’s second edition says around **2 GtCO2/yr** of CDR is already taking place, yet almost all of it comes from **conventional** land-based methods, while **novel CDR contributes only about 1.3 MtCO2/yr**, less than **0.1%** of the total [S42]. The same assessment identifies a **7–9 GtCO2/yr** central range of CDR in 2050 across a subset of more-sustainable Paris-consistent scenarios [S42].

So the archive’s house view is: **CDR is real, but the currently operational part of the novel-CDR world is still tiny relative to what many narratives casually assume.**

Rev0240 strengthens the overshoot warning rather than weakening this boundary. UNEP’s 2025 Emissions Gap Report says a higher exceedance of 1.5°C is very likely within the next decade and that limiting overshoot requires faster and bigger emissions reductions, while also warning that every fraction of a degree avoided lowers losses, costs, and reliance on uncertain removals [S250]. That means CDR belongs in the repair and residual lane, but gross cuts remain the main throttle.

IEA’s 2024 tracking reinforces the implementation warning. Broad CCUS deployment is still **not on track**, with announced 2030 capture capacity around **435 MtCO2/yr** and announced storage capacity around **615 MtCO2/yr**, versus about **1 GtCO2/yr** of capture and storage needed by 2030 in the IEA’s NZE scenario [S43][S46]. DAC is also off track: only **27 plants** are operating worldwide, capturing almost **0.01 MtCO2/yr** [S44]. BECCS is likewise off track: only around **2 MtCO2/yr** of biogenic CO2 is currently captured, and the project pipeline for 2030 remains far below NZE requirements [S45].

That means two errors must be resisted at once:
1. **dismissal error** — pretending removals are unnecessary
2. **escape-hatch error** — pretending future removals solve today’s political failure to cut gross emissions

## House doctrine

### 1. Residual-first, not delay-first
CDR belongs after strong gross mitigation, not instead of it. The archive uses removals mainly for emissions that are genuinely hard to eliminate fully: parts of agriculture, some industrial process emissions, some aviation and shipping residuals, and overshoot repair [S1].

### 2. Durability matters
Not all tonnes are equal. Land-based removals matter and should be expanded where ecologically sound, but they differ from highly durable geological or mineral storage. The archive therefore rejects single-number accounting that blurs permanence, reversal risk, and storage quality [S42].

### 3. Storage is not an afterthought
CO2 transport and storage infrastructure is the backbone of durable carbon management [S46]. A CDR or CCS plan without pipelines, shipping, hubs, injection capacity, monitoring, and long-run liability allocation is not yet an implementation plan.

### 4. Target CCS where it solves a real hard problem
The archive is open to CCS where it clearly addresses difficult residual emissions or enables durable CDR: cement, selected chemicals, some waste streams, some legacy assets where retirement is not immediately feasible, BECCS, and DACCS. It is much more skeptical when CCS mainly appears as a narrative to preserve large-scale routine fossil expansion despite faster substitutes [S1][S43].

### 5. Novel CDR should be scaled, but under explicit discipline
Novel CDR deserves demonstration support, procurement, and learning-by-doing because current scale is far too low [S42][S44][S45]. But support should be tied to transparent MRV, lifecycle accounting, ecological safeguards, and a credible path to durable storage.

### 6. Do not let removals hide gross failure
A plan with weak near-term emissions cuts and very large modeled removals later is not stronger because the spreadsheet closes. It is often weaker, because it transfers burden forward into a world with more warming, more ecological stress, and more uncertainty about land, water, energy, and storage availability [S1][S42].

## Minimum package for a high-integrity removals lane

A serious climate program that includes CCS or CDR should include at least:
- a published **residual-emissions doctrine** stating what categories of emissions removals are intended to balance
- separate accounting for **gross cuts** and **removed / durably stored tonnes**
- explicit treatment of **durability** and reversal risk rather than one flat removals number [S42]
- transport-and-storage planning, permitting, monitoring, and liability rules for projects that rely on geological storage [S43][S46]
- procurement or contracts-for-difference style support for carefully chosen first-of-a-kind durable-removal projects where learning value is real [S44][S45]
- a clear rule that removals do **not** justify avoidable new unabated fossil lock-in

## What the archive rejects

- net-zero claims that rely mainly on future removals rather than near-term gross cuts
- treating all removals as equally durable
- counting short-lived biological gains as if they were storage-equivalent to durable geological removal
- CCS used rhetorically to excuse routine fossil expansion where faster substitutes exist
- carbon-management plans without transport, storage, MRV, or long-run liability architecture

## Compression

**CDR is necessary for residuals, useful for overshoot repair, and worth building carefully — but only as a bounded carbon-management lane. Gross cuts still do the main work; durable storage and honest accounting decide whether removals are real.**
