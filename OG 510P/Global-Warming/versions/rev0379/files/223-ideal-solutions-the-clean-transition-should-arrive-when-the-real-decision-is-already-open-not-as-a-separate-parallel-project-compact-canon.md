# 223 — Ideal Solutions: The Clean Transition Should Arrive When the Real Decision Is Already Open, Not as a Separate Parallel Project

## Claim

The archive already says the clean path should be easy (`213`), arrive as a coherent offer rather than a scavenger hunt (`214`), show up through standing channels people already use (`222`), and move burden off the weaker party (`215`).
One more compact front-door rule belongs in the canon:

**a serious climate programme should usually place the clean offer inside the real decision that is already open — replacement, move, lease renewal, permit, rebuild, account opening, procurement, repair, recovery, or other live trigger — not ask the weaker party to start a separate parallel project beside it.**

In plain terms:
- when the boiler fails, the lease turns over, the roof is rebuilt after storm damage, the fleet is refreshed, the permit is filed, the service account is opened, or the public buyer is already replacing equipment, the clean pathway should be there inside that live moment rather than somewhere else on a separate website, loan search, or programme maze [S53][S77][S80][S81][S118][S119][S137][S141][S143]
- and ordinary actors should not have to pause a live decision, go learn a parallel policy stack, prove themselves again, and then somehow stitch the clean path back into the original decision before time runs out [S69][S71][S72][S76][S80][S81][S118][S119][S143]

So the shortest rule is:

**bring the clean offer to the live decision, not to a separate side process.**

## The compact canon

### 1. Parallel projects lose to live decisions
When a boiler has failed, a tenant is moving in, a permit clock is running, or a damaged building is being rebuilt, the live decision already has urgency, deadlines, counterparties, and paperwork.
A separate climate project usually loses to that live decision because it asks the weaker actor to run two processes at once [S53][S71][S76][S80][S81][S118][S119][S143].

House rule: **do not require a weaker actor to manage the live decision and the clean transition as two separate tracks.**

### 2. The strongest climate offer enters through the open file
The archive already prefers standing channels with cadence, records, or duty (`222`).
This note adds the timing rule: the strongest channel is the one that can place a standard clean offer into the file that is already open — the replacement order, lease turnover, permit application, rebuild case, account opening, fleet refresh, procurement cycle, or repair ticket [S53][S77][S80][S81][S118][S119][S137][S141][S143].

House rule: **prefer channels that can place the clean offer inside an already-open file.**

### 3. Trigger moments are not only opportunities; they are deadlines
The deeper turnover note (`50`) already says the archive should not miss replacement cycles and other trigger points.
At the front door, that becomes a simple practical rule: if the clean path is not inside the trigger moment, the dirtier fallback often wins by clock speed alone [S53][S77][S80][S81][S118][S119][S137][S141][S143].

House rule: **treat live decisions as deadlines for climate delivery, not just as outreach opportunities.**

### 4. Joining the clean path late is often too late
If the ordinary actor has to decide first and only afterward go search for the clean pathway, many clean gains are already lost.
The dirtier unit has been bought, the simpler repair authorized, the weaker lease signed, the like-for-like rebuild approved, or the procurement window has closed [S53][S71][S76][S80][S81][S118][S119][S143].

House rule: **the clean path should be present before fallback hardens into a new round of lock-in.**

### 5. The real job is to splice climate logic into ordinary administrative logic
The archive should prefer designs that splice climate logic into existing administrative and commercial sequences: prequalified replacement offers at failure, code-compliant clean defaults at permit, recovery-linked resilient rebuild offers after loss, lease-turnover upgrade packages, enrollment-linked tariff or service offers, and procurement-linked clean replacement standards [S71][S76][S77][S80][S81][S118][S119][S137][S141][S143].

House rule: **embed the climate sequence in the ordinary sequence that already governs the asset or service.**

### 6. Where no open-file channel exists, build one deliberately
Some actors and sectors will not naturally have a strong open-file channel.
Then the archive should treat that as a delivery-design problem: create a standing intake, navigator, or public intermediary that can attach the clean offer to the next real decision, rather than sending the actor back into ad hoc discovery [S69][S71][S77][S80][S118][S137][S141][S143].

House rule: **if no good open-file entry exists, build one rather than accepting a separate parallel project as normal.**

## Compression rule

**Ideal climate solutions should usually place the clean offer inside the real decision that is already open — replacement, move, permit, rebuild, renewal, enrollment, repair, recovery, or procurement — not ask the weaker party to run a separate climate project in parallel. The stronger pathway is the one that meets the live trigger with a ready clean route before the dirtier fallback hardens into another round of lock-in.**
