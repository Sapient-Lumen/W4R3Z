# 347 — Ideal Solutions: Protect cultural heritage, rituals, libraries, houses of worship, community anchors, and memory as recovery infrastructure

## Claim

Recovery is not only utilities, housing, cash, and roads. **Cultural heritage, rituals, libraries, archives, houses of worship, sacred sites, community centers, and other anchors can be recovery infrastructure because they carry memory, trust, identity, records, mutual aid, grief, and public legitimacy.**

ICCROM's crisis-resilience work treats tangible and intangible heritage and associated communities as part of disaster-risk reduction and policy, not decorative assets [S618]. WHO / PAHO / ICRC fatality guidance also shows why culture matters after disasters: dignified handling of the dead, family communication, and community rites have profound consequences [S614].

## Compact rule

**Climate recovery should protect the places and practices through which communities remain communities.**

This does not mean every artifact outranks life safety. It means debris removal, reentry, temporary housing, relocation, repair funding, archive salvage, deathcare, and public decision-making should know where heritage, ritual, records, sacred sites, libraries, and community anchors are before they are destroyed by neglect or rushed cleanup.

## Minimum packet

A cultural-continuity packet includes:

- map of heritage, ritual, archive, library, worship, and community-anchor assets;
- no-go and consult-before-disturbance screens for sacred or sensitive sites;
- salvage and stabilization priorities for records, collections, and community memory;
- cultural and religious liaison roster;
- temporary ritual, gathering, and mourning spaces;
- reentry and access rules for cultural leaders where safe;
- repair funding and reopening plan for community anchors;
- privacy rules for sacred or sensitive knowledge.

## Failure modes

The bad version treats culture as sentimental and therefore optional. Recovery crews clear sacred material as debris. Mold consumes archives. A house of worship that served as a food, cooling, and grief hub receives no continuity support. Relocation severs people from graves, language, landmarks, kinship, and community decision spaces.

The archive's rule: **what holds people together after loss is part of the recovery system.**
