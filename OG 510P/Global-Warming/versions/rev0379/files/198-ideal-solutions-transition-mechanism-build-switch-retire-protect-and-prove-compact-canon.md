# 198 — Ideal Solutions: Transition Mechanism — Build, Switch, Retire, Protect, and Prove Compact Canon

## Claim

The archive already has a shortest answer (`195`), a non-substitutions note (`196`), a simultaneous-verdicts note (`197`), a package-logic note (`190`), a turnover-aware note (`50`), a three-lanes note (`62`), and a retirement map (`148`).
What it still lacked was one very small front-door note answering a practical question that recurs underneath many climate arguments:

**How does a serious climate programme actually move the world from a dirty system to a cleaner one, rather than merely adding clean things beside the old system?**

That gap matters because many climate proposals name attractive ingredients without naming the transition mechanism.
They describe technologies, targets, or funding totals, but not the joined sequence by which cleaner capacity becomes the default, dirtier dependence loses its grip, vulnerable users are protected through the handoff, and the result is verified in physical outcomes.
This note is therefore a compression note, not a new doctrinal branch.
Its job is to gather the archive’s package logic, turnover logic, retirement logic, protection logic, and truth logic into one first-stop transition rule [S100][S101][S103][S104][S106][S143][S145][S146][S147][S161][S162][S163][S164][S180].

## Fast rule

**A serious climate programme does not finish when it announces clean buildout. It has to build the cleaner system, switch people and firms onto it at real decision moments, retire the dirtier default and its supports, protect people through the turnover, and prove the gains in measured physical outcomes.**

## The five parts of the transition mechanism

### 1. Build the cleaner system until it can carry real service
A serious programme must create enough clean supply, network capacity, efficient equipment, and institutional delivery capacity that the cleaner option is not just admirable but usable at scale.
That means grids, generation, storage, building upgrades, clean heat, transit and vehicle charging, industrial process change, standards, procurement, and affordable finance are part of the mechanism rather than background conditions [S100][S103][S106][S143][S180].

### 2. Switch users at real trigger points rather than waiting for perfect voluntary drift
A serious programme should ride the moments when assets are already being bought, replaced, renovated, rented, refinanced, procured, or rebuilt.
Sales, rentals, equipment failure, fleet renewal, retrofit cycles, and post-disaster reconstruction are when defaults, disclosure, finance, and standards can move large stocks with less friction than permanent exhortation can [S146][S161][S162][S163][S164].

### 3. Retire the dirtier default instead of letting dual-running become the long-term regime
A serious programme cannot stop at adding clean capacity while leaving the older fossil, wasteful, or exposed pathway fully normal, subsidized, and easy.
It has to remove privileged support, shrink fallback space, close loopholes, and deliberately retire the inferior path once the better default is real enough to carry continuity [S101][S145][S147][S180].

### 4. Protect people through the handoff so the transition does not solve emissions by exporting pain
A serious programme should preserve or improve service floors in heat, cooling, mobility, health, and safety while concentrating extra cost, proof burdens, and disruption as little as possible on poorer households, workers in exposed sectors, and vulnerable places.
Protection is part of the mechanism because an unaffordable or brittle handoff is not a stable replacement, only a political boomerang [S104][S143][S161][S164].

### 5. Prove the result in physical outcomes and correct when the handoff is weak
A serious programme should judge success by lower gross emissions, lower methane leakage, lower waste, better service reliability, lower exposure, and visible dirty retirement — not mainly by pipeline size, labels, or spending totals.
If the measured handoff is weak, the next duty is correction, not ceremonial optimism [S101][S103][S106][S147].

## Preferred use

Open this note first when the live question is any of the following:
- how the climate solution is actually supposed to move the world from dirty to clean
- why buildout alone is not enough
- why turnover moments matter
- why retirement and protection belong inside the transition itself rather than as optional side notes
- how to explain the archive’s basic transition mechanism in one place before opening the longer design-spec, retirement, or package notes

Then reopen `50`, `62`, `148`, `190`, `193`, `196`, or `197` depending on whether the surviving question is about trigger points, timing lanes, retirement after propagation, package logic, first-moves order, non-substitutions, or seriousness tests.

## Compression rule

**Do not merely add clean supply beside the old regime. Build the replacement, switch users at real decision moments, retire the dirtier default, protect people through the handoff, and verify that the physical result is actually arriving.**
