# 58 — Ideal Solutions, One-Page Answer

## Best answer

The ideal solution to global warming is **not** one technology, one policy, or one future cleanup machine.
It is a **physical-first package** that cuts gross emissions fast, slows near-term warming, prevents new lock-in, protects people directly, and can actually be financed, permitted, staffed, commissioned, operated, maintained, stress-tested, priced honestly across balance sheets, protected against affordability pile-ups, defended against weak claims and capture, made enforceable through remedy ladders and accountability channels, linked through interoperable records and proof passports, protected against cyber-physical brittleness, governed when AI or automation is used, sited in real places with rights and benefit discipline, retired with closure proof, cleaned up without orphan liabilities, reused only when legacy places are actually repaired, prepared for climate mobility with receiving-capacity ledgers, able to carry rights / services / records / benefits / remedies across movement, measured, copied, and made durable across many places at once [S100][S101][S102][S103][S104][S106]. In richer electricity-heavy systems, grid speed increasingly sets climate speed; at hardware-constrained moments, transformer, cable, conductor, and switchgear throughput increasingly set whether grid plans become installed capacity on time; at clean-buildout scale, critical mineral diversification, recycling, and traceability increasingly set a material part of manufacturing speed and geopolitical durability; at world scale, the price and form of finance increasingly do the same outside the richest countries; at mass-buildout scale, institutional throughput in planning, permitting, procurement, and workforce systems increasingly sets whether mature solutions become installed reality on time; at rising-load scale, efficiency, retrofits, and passive demand reduction increasingly set how much grid, storage, and generation buildout is needed to deliver the same service at tolerable cost; at mass-uptake scale, affordability of clean alternatives, bill certainty, and reliability increasingly set whether the programme becomes normal life fast enough to hold public support; and because methane already drives a large share of current warming, methane cuts now set a meaningful part of whether the 2030s damage path bends soon enough to matter in lived time. In a hotter world, sustainable cooling access, passive-first design, efficient equipment, and refrigerant discipline also set a material part of heat protection, peak-load growth, and cooling emissions. As hydrological instability deepens, water security and hydrological stability also set a material part of food security, city viability, and power reliability. And as repeated losses widen protection gaps, exposure discipline, repeat-loss rules, and dignified retreat from the unprotectable also set a material part of protection cost, insurance stress, housing strain, and fiscal durability. At system-replacement scale, dirty-asset retirement, non-reinvestment, and fossil-support phase-down also set a material part of net decarbonization speed, because clean buildout alone does not guarantee that the old high-emissions system stops governing the ledger [S55][S56][S59][S61][S91][S95][S176][S195][S196][S197][S198][S199][S200][S201][S202][S207][S208][S209][S210][S211][S212][S213][S214][S215][S16][S17][S169][S219][S224][S225][S226][S227][S228][S229][S230][S231][S232][S233][S234][S235][S236][S237][S238][S239][S240][S244][S245][S246][S247][S248].

Rev0241 adds one more operating layer: because electricity demand, data-centre and AI loads, grid queues, transformers, public finance, staff, land, and water can all become binding at once, scarce capacity itself has to be governed. New large loads should enter as efficient, transparent, flexible grid citizens with real clean-system contribution and fair cost assignment, not as passive claims on the backbone needed for replacement and protection; see `245` and `246` [S252][S253].

Rev0242 adds the readiness layer: high-value work has to be prepared before finance, grid capacity, disaster recovery, procurement calendars, or political attention appears. Rev0243 adds the human and persistence layer: a shelf and procurement platform still fail unless the crews, public offices, inspectors, operators, maintainers, commissioning tests, warranty paths, and aftercare systems exist before the packet needs them; see `247`–`250` [S247][S248][S176][S178][S259][S260].

Rev0244 adds the stressed-service layer: a packet that works on a normal day can still fail the programme if it collapses during heat, outage, smoke, flood, drought, cyber, supply-chain, staff, or restoration stress. The archive now asks every serious packet to carry a stress-test path and asks every jurisdiction to protect critical services — hospitals, schools, water, shelters, telecoms, public housing, lifeline mobility, cold chains, and grid-control functions — with decarbonized continuity packages rather than private improvisation or routine diesel dependence; see `251` and `252` [S40][S261][S262][S263].

Rev0245 adds the recovery and protection-gap layer: a packet can survive the shock and still fail if recovery money restores the same exposure, if uninsured losses are silently shifted to households and public budgets, or if uninsurability becomes chaotic retreat by market abandonment. The archive now treats recovery, insurance stress, public backstops, residual-loss ledgers, and rights-respecting relocation as part of the main climate operating system; see `253` and `254` [S264][S265][S266][S267][S268].

Rev0246 adds the balance-sheet and affordability-firebreak layer: a packet can cut emissions, survive shocks, and still fail if risk and cost migrate invisibly into household debt, rent, premiums, utility bills, rate cases, public backstops, bank collateral, or fiscal emergencies. The archive now asks major packets to name the absorbing balance sheets and to protect essential users from cumulative burden across bills, premiums, rents, rates, taxes, fees, deductibles, debt, and time; see `255` and `256` [S269][S270][S271][S272][S273][S274].

Rev0247 adds the claims-integrity and anti-capture layer: a packet can be technically right, financeable, affordable, and stress-tested, yet still lose trust if its net-zero, transition-plan, carbon-credit, green-product, green-finance, clean-power, procurement, or resilience claims are vague, unverifiable, or consequence-free; it can also fail if public money, grid capacity, guarantees, recovery funds, rate recovery, permits, or procurement channels become capture and rent-extraction machines. The archive now asks major packets to carry claims-integrity and integrity-throughput controls; see `257` and `258` [S275][S276][S280][S281][S282][S283][S284].

Rev0248 adds the enforcement-remedy and accountability-router layer: a packet can be auditable and still fail if missed duties, false claims, captured value channels, affordability breaches, recovery failures, or harms have no cure path, escalation trigger, remedy owner, or accessible forum. The archive now asks major packets to carry duty-to-remedy ladders and to route failures to the channel that can actually correct the failed condition — regulator, funder, auditor, procurement challenge, ombuds route, court, Paris transparency / compliance channel, public ledger, or other accountable forum; see `259` and `260` [S285][S286][S287][S288][S290][S291][S292].

Rev0249 adds the data-spine and proof-passport layer: a packet can be enforceable and still fail if its permits, contracts, assets, claims, finance, meters, exposure records, support routes, recovery records, and remedies sit in disconnected ledgers that cannot survive handoff. The archive now asks high-stakes packets to carry interoperable operating records and proof-carrying product, building, project, and service passports, so climate evidence can move from promise to procurement to installation to commissioning to maintenance to repair to retirement without being reinvented at each step; see `261` and `262` [S293][S294][S295][S296][S297][S298][S299][S300].

Rev0250 adds the cyber-resilience, fallback, and climate-AI accountability layer: a packet can be interoperable and still fail if linked records, connected devices, remote controls, support portals, model outputs, or proof passports become brittle failure surfaces, privacy hazards, or unowned automated authorities. The archive now asks digitally dependent packets to carry cyber / fallback records and asks AI, models, digital twins, scoring tools, and automated rules to be registered, bounded, tested, monitored, contestable, overrideable, and retired when they cannot support the duty they claim to serve; see `263` and `264` [S301][S302][S303][S304][S305][S306][S307][S308][S309][S310].

Rev0251 adds the cross-border supply-chain and market-access interoperability layer: a packet can be secure and accountable in its own digital layer and still fail if its minerals, components, spares, shipping routes, customs classification, standards, conformity-assessment path, certificates, testing capacity, product-passport fields, or mutual-recognition route are missing or fragmented. The archive now asks high-volume or high-criticality packets to carry cross-border dependency records and asks clean products, materials, services, and claims to carry market-access interoperability packets; see `265` and `266` [S244][S311][S312][S313][S314][S315][S316][S317][S318][S319].

Rev0252 adds the circularity and end-of-life layer: a packet can have its supply chain, standards, and certificates in order and still fail if the assets it installs are disposable, unrepairable, software-stranded, impossible to take back, unsafe to reuse, or undocumented at end of life. The archive now asks high-volume, high-criticality, public-funded, or hazardous clean-asset packets to carry circular-design and end-of-life infrastructure records; see `267` and `268` [S320][S321][S322][S323][S324][S325][S326][S327][S328].

Rev0253 adds the place-siting and host-legitimacy layer: a packet can have its supply chain, circular design, and end-of-life route in order and still fail if land, water, seabed, corridors, biodiversity, cultural places, cumulative impacts, host communities, Indigenous consent duties, benefit-sharing, and grievance repair are handled as afterthoughts. The archive now asks footprint-bearing and place-intensive packets to carry place-capacity and host-community / rights ledgers before they are called ready; see `269` and `270` [S60][S64][S329][S330][S331][S332][S333][S334][S335][S337][S338][S339][S340].

Overshoot evidence now strengthens the same answer. Copernicus reports that 2023–2025 was the first three-year period averaging more than 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels, WMO says a five-year average above 1.5°C in 2025–2029 is now more likely than not, and UNEP says current policies still point to around 2.8°C this century [S249][S250][S251]. The conclusion is not that the project is over. It is that every tenth, every year, every tonne, every methane repair, every protected person, and every avoided lock-in now has more value, while future removals must be treated as bounded repair rather than permission for delay.

## One-page answer

### 1. Cut gross emissions fast and physically
The atmosphere responds to physical emissions, not ambition language.
So the center of the answer is still fast gross cuts in CO2, methane, and other warming pollution, with removals kept mainly for hard residuals and not used as an excuse for delay [S1][S2][S10][S11][S14][S20].

### 2. Build the clean electric and efficiency backbone first
The fastest serious package starts by expanding clean electricity, grids, storage, flexibility, efficiency, cooling performance, and electrification because those moves unlock later reductions across buildings, transport, and part of industry.
This backbone now faces more bottlenecks from permitting, interconnection, capital cost, hardware lead times, and delivery capacity than from basic technical feasibility [S4][S5][S6][S14][S17][S20]. In the Age of Electricity, the tempo-setting layer is increasingly the grid-and-flexibility layer itself: IEA says power demand is growing strongly through 2030, more than 2 500 GW of projects are stalled in connection queues, and data centres are only one part of the demand story globally, smaller than air conditioning, industrial motors, or EVs even though local grid effects can be sharper [S55][S56][S106][S158][S194]. The hardware layer is now part of that tempo too: IEA says procurement now takes two to three years for cables, up to four years for large power transformers, and more than five years for some direct-current cables, while grid-material prices have risen sharply and credible forward demand is needed to justify manufacturing expansion [S207][S208]. DOE and NREL add that distribution transformers are also under strain, with long lead times, component shortages, aging stock, and strong demand growth under electrification [S209][S210][S211]. Cooling now sharpens the same load story: UNEP says cooling demand could more than triple by 2050 under business as usual, while IEA says income-driven air-conditioning use adds around 330 GW to global peak demand by 2035 and higher temperatures add another 170 GW in the STEPS [S16][S169][S219]. But climate speed now also depends on whether institutions can clear permits, run procurement, staff inspections, and keep workforce pipelines full enough that cleaner assets are not stranded in administrative queues after capital is announced [S59][S61][S176][S197][S198].

### 3. Replace dirty service systems, not just buy cleaner parts
The real target is not a pile of cleaner assets sitting next to the old regime.
It is the replacement of dirty ways of delivering heat, cooling, mobility, power, and materials with cleaner service systems that actually carry ordinary load and retire inferior defaults over time [S14][S17][S20][S24]. Current official evidence now sharpens the retirement half of that claim. IEA says clean-energy investment is set to reach about USD 2.2 trillion in 2025, roughly double fossil-fuel investment, yet its Global Energy Review shows that demand still rose for all major fuels in 2024 and energy-sector CO2 emissions still increased, even though rapid clean-tech deployment avoided about 2.6 Gt of additional annual CO2 emissions [S233][S234]. OECD adds that fossil-fuel support still cost about USD 916.3 billion in 2024 across more than 1 700 documented measures [S235]. And IEA’s net-zero pathway work says a 1.5°C-consistent route leaves no room for routine approval of new long lead-time conventional oil and gas projects or new unabated coal plants beyond those already under construction at the start of 2023 [S236]. So the programme has to count dirty defaults exiting, not only clean assets arriving.
Current official evidence now sharpens the spatial-form half too. OECD’s 2025 intermediary-cities work says compact urban forms, strong public transport, and safe non-motorised mobility reduce emissions and improve quality of life, while uncoordinated expansion often produces car dependence, congestion, and higher emissions [S241]. IEA’s 2025 transport work adds that transport still accounts for around 30% of global energy demand, that road transport makes up nearly 90% of domestic transport energy demand, and that governments should manage travel demand, distances travelled, and car dependency rather than rely only on cleaner vehicles [S243]. OECD’s 2025 zero-carbon-buildings report widens the same point by warning that embodied emissions could account for around half of the carbon footprint of new buildings by 2050 if left unaddressed [S242]. So urban growth geometry now belongs nearer the front door: the programme has to shape access, proximity, and settlement form early enough that electrification is not forced to compensate forever for bad spatial lock-in.

### 4. Run fast-warming and waste lanes as core lanes
Methane, venting, flaring, wasteful cooling, food loss and waste, nitrogen losses, and ecosystem destruction are not side issues.
They are among the fastest available ways to reduce near-term warming, pressure on grids and supply chains, and long-run ecological damage [S8][S11][S16][S17][S18][S19]. Current official evidence now sharpens the methane part of that claim: WMO says methane concentrations hit an unprecedented high in 2024; IEA says energy-sector methane remains above 120 Mt annually, around 70% could be avoided with existing technologies, and targeted fossil-methane mitigation under today’s policies would avoid roughly 0.1 °C of warming by 2050; UNEP says over 80% of 2030 methane-reduction potential is low cost, but current national plans submitted by mid-2025 would still translate into only about an 8% cut below 2020 levels if fully implemented [S212][S213][S214][S215]. So methane belongs not only in the package, but near the front of the working queue.
Current official evidence now sharpens the nitrogen part too: UNEP says nitrous oxide is responsible for about 10% of net global warming since the industrial revolution, is the top ozone-depleting substance still being emitted, and can be cut by more than 40% with practical action; FAO adds that humans now add about 150 Tg of reactive nitrogen to land each year, more than double the pre-industrial rate, and that governments should integrate sustainable nitrogen management into mitigation plans and NDCs [S216][S217]. UNEP’s 2025 nitrogen-governance work adds that governments now have practical tools for national nitrogen budgets, impact assessment, and policy measures [S218]. So nitrogen discipline also belongs nearer the front of the agrifood working queue than climate politics usually grants it. Cooling belongs nearer the front too: UNEP says nearly two-thirds of the emissions cuts available in sustainable cooling come from passive and low-energy solutions, while only 54 countries yet have comprehensive policy coverage across passive cooling, minimum energy performance standards, and refrigerant transition despite much wider strategic recognition [S169]. So cooling can no longer be treated as merely a buildings subtopic or a private appliance lane.
A closely related implication is that flexibility now belongs nearer the front door too. IEA says global electricity demand is set to add around **1 000 TWh each year until 2035**, yet only around **100 GW** of demand response is currently utilised worldwide; it also says households can typically save **5–15%** on electricity bills under dynamic tariffs and that flexibility can provide grid capacity at up to **three times lower** cost than building new capacity [S220][S221]. IEA’s 2025 renewables work adds that curtailment and negative prices are rising in multiple countries and that smart EV chargers, heat pumps, storage, and other demand-side flexibility are increasingly needed to integrate wind and solar securely and cost-effectively [S222]. So the programme now has to treat controllable demand, storage, and smart charging as part of the minimum packet for affordable electrification, not as optional system polish.

### 4A. Keep high-value work ready before the window opens
A governed work queue still fails if the best work is not absorbable when money, grid capacity, disaster-recovery funding, guarantees, procurement calendars, or political attention becomes available. So the ideal solution maintains a ready project shelf: owner-led, permit-routed, financeable, procurable, socially governed, bottleneck-tagged, measurement-ready, and organized around repeatable archetypes. It also turns repeated needs into procurement platforms with common specifications, prequalified suppliers, standard finance, service warranties, aftercare, and performance data [S247][S248][S254][S255][S256][S257][S258].

### 4B. Build crews and count completion at stable service
A prepared packet is still not deliverable if the needed people and public offices do not exist. The ideal solution therefore attaches a crew-and-capability plan to the shelf and procurement calendar: occupations, training seats, certifications, apprenticeships, public-office staffing, inspectors, operators, maintainers, job-quality rules, and placement routes for affected workers. It also counts completion at stable clean service, not first installation: commissioning, O&M, warranty, spare parts, user support, performance data, and correction triggers must be part of the packet from the start [S176][S178][S259][S260].

### 4C. Buy clean assets for their whole life
A clean asset is not mature if it is unrepairable, firmware-stranded, impossible to take back, unsafe to reuse, or undocumented at end of life. Procurement should carry durability, spare parts, repair rights, software support, product-passport fields, take-back, recycling, and decommissioning finance; delivery packets should count repairability, recovered material, and safe retirement alongside installation [S320][S321][S322][S325][S326][S327][S328].

### 5. Protect people directly while the transition happens
Even a strong mitigation path still leaves dangerous heat, flood, drought, fire, health, and infrastructure risks arriving now.
So adaptation belongs inside the ideal solution: cooling access, early warnings, resilient water and care systems, nature-based protection where credible, and retreat where repeated defence is no longer honest [S3][S12][S15][S16][S17].
Current evidence makes the front-door implication sharper. WMO says **2015–2025** were the hottest **11 years** on record and that **2025** was still roughly **1.43 °C** above the 1850–1900 average, with extreme events causing disruption and billions in losses [S203]. WMO’s 2025 early-warning status report says **119 countries** now report multi-hazard warning systems, but that still leaves **40%** of countries without them [S204]. UNEP says developing-country adaptation-finance needs in **2035** are around **USD 310–365 billion per year** versus only **USD 26 billion** in international public adaptation finance in **2023** [S205]. OECD adds that many countries still underuse resilience outcomes, resilience appraisal, and resilience-led project prioritisation in infrastructure governance [S206]. So protection speed is now part of whether the whole climate programme stays durable in lived time.

### 6. Allocate burdens honestly
Richer countries move earlier and finance wider diffusion.
Poorer countries need a buildable clean-development path, not a permanently constrained one. And because much of the missing buildout is in mature clean infrastructure rather than frontier technology, the world-scale question is increasingly whether finance arrives in usable form: lower cost of capital, deeper local-currency finance, stronger guarantees, and less off-taker and payment risk [S13][S91][S95][S96][S195][S196].
Inside countries, the shortest public bargain is easier clean essentials and real protection through turnover, while earlier and harder pressure falls on luxury waste, methane leakage, new lock-in, and organized dirty delay.
Fossil producers and other incumbents face managed decline duties rather than a veto over the transition, and scarce premium tools are rationed to true hard residuals [S4][S9][S13][S20][S24][S25].

### 7. Make the public meaning legible
A serious climate program should cash out in everyday reality as better ordinary service, less waste, and less danger.
That means lower emissions and methane, but also lower bills or burden for ordinary users, more reliable clean service, less avoidable throughput, and lower exposure to heat, pollution, and repeated loss.
At the point of choice, it should also make the cleaner path easier to take than the dirtier one through standards, codes, one-stop delivery, embedded support, and coherent public offers rather than reimbursement mazes, policy scavenger hunts, and expert-only coordination [S3][S6][S10][S11][S14][S16][S53][S69][S80][S118][S119][S143].
And for most ordinary users, it should frame that choice as acceptance of a better clean result — warmth, cooling, reliability, lower burden, safer recovery — rather than as a demand to specify, finance, and manage a full technology stack by hand [S16][S17][S53][S69][S80][S104][S106][S118][S119][S137][S141][S143][S144].
It should also usually arrive through standing channels people already use or must already touch — utility, landlord, municipal service, payroll or benefits, public procurement, and recovery pathways — rather than depending on lucky discovery or expert scheme-hunting [S71][S76][S77][S80][S81][S118][S119][S137][S141][S143].
And whenever possible, it should arrive when the real decision is already open — replacement, move, lease renewal, permit, rebuild, account opening, procurement, repair, or recovery — rather than as a separate parallel project that asks the weaker party to start from zero beside the live decision [S53][S71][S76][S77][S80][S81][S118][S119][S137][S141][S143].
And when it arrives, it should usually arrive as a drafted clean offer — reusing known facts, narrowing to likely-fit routing, and leaving bounded choices rather than a blank intake page of self-assembly [S69][S71][S72][S77][S80][S118][S119][S137][S141][S143].
And when that drafted path is already live and nothing material has changed, it should usually continue by default with delta-only updates, reminder and grace before hard drop, and no quiet lapse back into full reapplication or dirty fallback [S72][S74][S75][S77][S79][S118][S119][S143][S144].
It should also move search, proof, cash, coordination, and performance risk upstream as far as honesty allows, rather than stacking them on the weaker party at the exact moment a clean replacement is supposed to happen [S68][S69][S70][S72][S74][S79][S80][S81][S118][S119][S143].
And it should hold together as one accountable path from first contact to persistent clean state — one front door, light proof, tolerable payment, standard offer, prequalified delivery, fast recourse, and aftercare until the cleaner state is routine rather than fragile [S53][S69][S70][S72][S74][S76][S77][S78][S80][S81][S82][S118][S119][S120][S121][S143][S144].
Ordinary users also should not have to gamble on bills, comfort, or reliability just to take the cleaner path; a serious programme protects essential service floors and puts the duty of correction mainly on the actors who control the system rather than on the weaker party [S67][S69][S70][S71][S74][S79][S80][S81][S82][S118][S119][S137][S141][S143][S144].
And once an ordinary actor says yes to a serious clean offer, the path should usually stay joined: not a chain of repeated re-decisions, re-proof, re-shopping, and re-coordination at every handoff, but one carried sequence until the cleaner state is stable in routine life [S72][S76][S77][S78][S79][S80][S81][S82][S118][S119][S143][S144].
That cleaner state should also survive ordinary life events: moves, tenancy change, account change, ownership change, major repair, post-disaster rebuild, and early performance trouble should not quietly reset the user or asset to a dirtier fallback when the relevant facts have not materially changed [S75][S78][S82][S118][S119][S120][S121][S143][S144][S161][S162][S164].
And the minimum continuity packet should travel with the right carrier — person, household, asset, building, meter, account, site, route, or service relationship — so status, records, obligations, support, and remedy do not disappear at the next handoff and force rediscovery from zero [S72][S73][S74][S75][S78][S79][S80][S81][S82][S118][S119][S120][S121][S143][S144][S161][S162][S164].
Money, targets, and announcements matter only insofar as they make those outcomes show up.

## Rev0254 addition — do not leave ghost liabilities behind

The one-page answer now has a dirty-retirement afterlife test. The one-page answer now has a civil-justice continuity test. Climate protections do not become real because a statute, plan, budget, or benefit exists. They become real when people can understand the notice, keep the document, prove eligibility, meet or pause the deadline, get help, contest the decision, receive interim relief, stop retaliation, and carry the remedy through displacement and handoff.

The practical minimum is two records: a **civil-justice-continuity ledger** for legal identity, notice, claims help, administrative review, legal aid, paralegals, ombuds, anti-retaliation, and portable legal records; and a **legal-need shock-absorber packet** for deadline tolling, evidence substitution, evictions, benefit denials, debt claims, insurance disputes, identity loss, family safety, displacement, digital contestability, and systemic correction [S458][S459][S461][S462][S466][S467][S470][S471].

## Rev0265 one-page addendum — make climate warnings actionable

The one-page answer now includes warning and communication continuity: serious climate programmes should turn forecasts into trusted, accessible, multilingual, multi-channel, supported action. Alerts should be interoperable, telecom and broadcast channels should have fallback power and repair plans, trusted intermediaries and local media should be funded, rumor should be listened to and corrected, and every missed or unactionable warning should feed the work queue.

One-page rule: **a climate instruction is not complete until the intended person can receive, understand, trust, and act on it.** See `293` and `294` [S472][S473][S481][S485].

## Rev0266 one-page update — the action-to-access spine

Add one more line to the one-page answer: **warnings, rights, cash, care, food, water, schools, health, housing, and recovery only work when people, goods, crews, fuel, charging, vehicles, depots, ports, and routes can move under degraded conditions.** The archive now treats transport continuity as the physical-action layer beneath service continuity: no-car evacuation, accessible transport, public-transit emergency service, freight and last-mile logistics, route-status data, worker safety, fuel / charging priority, and missed-access correction are part of the minimum climate answer [S487][S488][S490][S501].

## Rev0267 one-page addendum — power-to-service spine

The one-page answer now includes a power-to-service spine: decarbonize the energy system, but also prove that critical loads remain powered, affordable, safe, and accountable during outages. Protect medically dependent people, health facilities, WASH systems, communications, payments, transport, housing, food cold chains, schools, care systems, and resilience hubs before blackouts become social failure. Use `297` and `298` after clean-power, critical-service, warning, transport, housing, finance, health, care, and water files. [S502] [S503] [S505]

## Rev0268 one-page-answer bridge — cleanup must be governed

Add one more test to the one-page answer: a real climate programme keeps cleanup governed. It can clear debris, separate hazards, protect workers, make homes safe to reoccupy, keep medical waste and organics controlled, prevent dumping, monitor contaminated sites, and close temporary debris operations without shifting exposure to poorer communities. See `299` and `300`. [S519] [S521]

## Rev0269 one-page addendum — the cube version

The one-page answer now has a structural rider: every serious climate answer should be readable as a datacube row. Name the emissions lane, the service floor, the hazard, the clock, the owner, the bottleneck, the proof ledger, and the failure mode. If those fields are blank, the answer is probably a slogan, not a delivery packet [S40][S83][S536][S537].

The minimum set of floors now includes public safety, emergency response, thermal survivability, family reunification, waste / debris / environmental-health closure, and coastal / ocean continuity in addition to the earlier water, food, health, shelter, mobility, information, finance, legal, care, labour, transport, and energy floors [S538][S539][S540][S541][S543][S545].

The integrity rider is equally short: do not let carbon-market or offset claims stand in for physical decarbonization, fossil decline, or protection of people. Treat them as quarantined claims until additionality, permanence, accounting, rights, leakage, and liability are proven [S135][S136][S280][S281][S546].
