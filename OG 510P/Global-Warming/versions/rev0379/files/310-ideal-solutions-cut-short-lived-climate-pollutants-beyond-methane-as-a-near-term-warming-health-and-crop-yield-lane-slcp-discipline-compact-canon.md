# 310 — Ideal Solutions: Cut short-lived climate pollutants beyond methane as a near-term warming, health, and crop-yield lane

## Claim

The archive is already strong on methane.
It should now make the whole short-lived climate pollutant lane explicit.

Short-lived climate pollutants include methane, black carbon, tropospheric ozone, and hydrofluorocarbons. The Climate and Clean Air Coalition describes them as powerful climate forcers that remain in the atmosphere for much shorter periods than CO2, with large warming, health, ecosystem, and crop impacts [S549]. UNEP's 2025 methane status update also shows why speed matters: methane is still rising, but proven measures could deliver large cuts by 2030 if implemented across energy, agriculture, and waste [S550].

The doctrine is not “SLCPs instead of CO2.”
The doctrine is: **deep CO2 cuts set the destination; SLCP cuts slow near-term warming and reduce health and crop damage while the deep transition is built.**

## Fast rule

**Treat methane, black carbon, tropospheric ozone precursors, and HFCs as a near-term protection lane that must be cut alongside, never instead of, deep CO2 decline.**

## The compact canon

### 1. Keep CO2 first for permanence, but do not waste the near term

CO2 governs long-term warming.
But short-lived pollutants shape the next decades of heat, air quality, crop loss, and health burden.

House rule: **CO2 delay cannot be laundered through SLCP action, but SLCP neglect is also a climate mistake.**

### 2. Keep methane as the emergency lane

Methane cuts remain central because they are fast, measurable in many sectors, and often cost-effective.
Priority sources:

- oil and gas leaks, venting, flaring, and abandoned wells;
- coal-mine methane;
- landfill gas and organic waste;
- manure and livestock systems;
- rice water management;
- wastewater and sludge where relevant.

House rule: **the best methane policy measures emissions directly, fixes equipment quickly, and does not let inventories hide super-emitters.**

### 3. Add black carbon where combustion is dirty and local harm is high

Black carbon is a climate forcer and a component of fine particulate pollution.
It comes from incomplete combustion: diesel, coal, biomass, kerosene, open burning, brick kilns, some industrial processes, and household energy.

Controls can include clean cooking and heating, diesel particulate filters, non-road engine standards, open-burning bans with alternatives, clean freight, waste collection, and agricultural residue management.

House rule: **black carbon policy should be designed as climate policy, health policy, and worker / household protection at once.**

### 4. Treat tropospheric ozone as a crop, health, and heat-risk pollutant

Ground-level ozone forms from methane and other precursors interacting with sunlight.
It harms respiratory health and crops, and it is also a greenhouse gas.

Ozone reduction requires action on methane, NOx, volatile organic compounds, transport, industry, solvents, and combustion sources.

House rule: **ozone is not just an air-quality compliance issue; it is a food, labour, health, and climate-stress issue.**

### 5. Govern HFCs through cooling policy, not refrigerant paperwork alone

HFCs are tied to air conditioning, refrigeration, cold chains, heat pumps, fire suppression, foams, and service practices.
A warming world will increase cooling demand.
That means refrigerant policy must be coupled with efficient cooling, passive design, technician training, leak detection, recovery, reclamation, safe alternatives, and end-of-life controls.

House rule: **cooling expansion without refrigerant discipline is a hidden warming channel.**

### 6. Beware aerosol-transition side effects without preserving dirty air

Some air pollutants have complex cooling or warming effects.
Cleaning air can reveal warming that pollution had masked.
That is not an argument to keep people breathing poison.
It is an argument to pair air-pollution cuts with rapid greenhouse-gas cuts, heat protection, and source-specific planning.

House rule: **never protect the climate by preserving toxic exposure; protect both by cutting greenhouse gases and dirty combustion together.**

### 7. Build an SLCP ledger

Every jurisdiction should know:

- top methane sources and super-emitter monitoring routes;
- black-carbon source categories and exposed populations;
- ozone precursor sources and crop / health hotspots;
- refrigerant banks, leak rates, technician capacity, and end-of-life recovery;
- open-burning and waste-burning locations;
- clean cooking / heating access gaps;
- diesel and non-road engine exposure hotspots;
- enforcement and repair timelines.

House rule: **SLCP policy without source ledgers becomes slogans about fast action.**

## Minimum SLCP ledger

| Pollutant | Minimum proof |
|---|---|
| methane | measured sources, repair duties, inventory reconciliation, satellite / aerial / facility checks, waste and agriculture plan |
| black carbon | combustion source map, exposure map, replacement / control pathway, open-burning alternatives, enforcement |
| ozone | precursor inventory, methane link, NOx / VOC strategy, crop and health exposure, seasonal alert plan |
| HFCs | refrigerant bank, leak-rate controls, technician certification, recovery / reclamation, end-of-life rules, low-GWP alternatives |

## Compression rule

**SLCP cuts are a fast protection lane: methane, black carbon, tropospheric ozone precursors, and HFCs should be cut with measured source ledgers, health co-benefits, crop-protection logic, and refrigerant discipline — always alongside deep CO2 cuts, never as a substitute for them.**
