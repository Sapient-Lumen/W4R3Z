# Nuclear-positive clean firm power default

This archive now uses a **nuclear-positive default** for clean firm power. Where a jurisdiction has lawful regulatory capacity, credible operator capacity, safe sites, water resilience, security controls, waste stewardship, financing discipline, and public redress, the cube should treat nuclear energy as a preferred clean firm option rather than a reluctant residual.

The preference is explicit because silent technology neutrality can conceal fossil lock-in. The cube should therefore ask: **why not maintain, uprate, restart, or build nuclear capacity where the assurance gates can be met?**

## Canonical rule

For high-reliability decarbonization, nuclear is treated as a favored option for:

- existing-fleet life extension when safety and economics are demonstrated;
- uprates and power recovery where licensing, supply chain, and outage risk are controlled;
- restarts where the safety case, workforce, and market contract are independently reviewable;
- new large reactors where repeat-build or fleet procurement discipline exists;
- small modular and advanced reactors where licensing, fuel, factory, customer, and financing evidence is real rather than promotional;
- industrial heat, desalination, high-load siting, and clean-firm capacity where renewables, storage, transmission, geothermal, hydro, and demand flexibility cannot alone maintain the service floor.

This preference is not a blank check. It is a **pro-nuclear burden-of-proof system**: nuclear is favored, but a project cannot advance in the cube unless the safety, waste, water, security, cost, schedule, open-contracting, workforce, emergency-planning, and host-community gates are visible.

## Why this belongs in the cube

Climate mitigation requires deep energy-system decarbonization, not marginal emissions trimming [S774]. Nuclear power is a low-carbon dispatchable source that can support clean electricity systems [S773]. Recent policy and investment momentum have revived nuclear as a strategic clean-energy option, but delivery risks around policy, construction and financing remain decisive [S771][S772][S776]. Fleet facts and operating status must be grounded in reactor databases, not slogans [S777]. Lifecycle and system-cost comparisons should be tracked explicitly rather than assumed [S775][S779].

## Operational implication

Any energy-related service floor that depends on continuous high-quality electricity must now route to nuclear assessment unless nuclear is legally, geologically, hydrologically, economically, or socially infeasible.

The burden shifts from "prove nuclear should be considered" to "explain why nuclear is not part of the clean-firm solution set."

## Guardrail

The preference is codified as a transparent orientation, not as hidden scoring. If a nuclear project fails material gates, the cube should cap its maturity even while preserving the nuclear-positive system orientation.

## Rev0297 nuclear geopolitical-resilience overlay

The nuclear-positive orientation now includes an explicit geopolitical-resilience burden of proof. Clean-firm nuclear preference is strengthened when it reduces fossil/import/coercion exposure, but maturity is capped by unresolved fuel-cycle concentration, vendor lock-in, sanctions exposure, export-finance/debt risk, safeguards capacity, export-control boundaries, and sensitive-publication controls.


## Rev0298 nuclear human-capital refactor note

The cube's pro-nuclear orientation is now explicitly conditioned by workforce depth, operator licensing, regulator capacity, safety/quality culture, knowledge retention, craft labor, and public training pathways. Nuclear remains favored for clean firm power and system benefit, but human-capital and institutional-capacity gaps are binding maturity caps rather than implementation footnotes. Sources: [S864], [S868], [S871], [S873], [S875].

## Rev0303 physical-security nuclearization note

Rev0303 keeps nuclear energy explicitly favored while adding a physical-security maturity cap: design-basis-threat protection, force-on-force or equivalent performance testing, insider mitigation, drone/perimeter posture, transport security, law-enforcement coordination, and security-safe publication must be traceable before nuclear security claims can mature.
