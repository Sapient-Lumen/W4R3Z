# 245 — Ideal Solutions: Govern Scarce Climate Bottlenecks as an Allocation System — Constrained-Capacity Compact Canon

## Claim

Rev0239 turned the climate answer into a work queue.
Rev0240 turned queue items into owner-led delivery packets and put them inside overshoot discipline.
The next missing layer is what happens when many good packets collide with the same scarce bottleneck.

That collision is now ordinary climate governance.
Grid connection capacity, transformers, cables, switchgear, skilled workers, permitting offices, project-preparation teams, guarantees, concessional finance, land, water, social license, clean power, storage, attention, and political room are all claimed at once.
If the programme treats these constraints as neutral queues, first-come processes, or auctions for whoever can pay most, scarce transition capacity can be consumed by work that is private, weakly additional, poorly timed, or even lock-in producing.

Current electricity evidence makes the problem concrete. IEA's 2026 electricity outlook says global electricity demand grew by 3% in 2025 and is forecast to grow by an average of 3.6% per year from 2026 to 2030; annual demand growth over the next five years is expected to be 50% higher than during the previous decade; electricity use is projected to grow at least 2.5 times as fast as total energy demand; and more than 2 500 GW of renewable, large-load, and storage projects are already stalled in grid queues worldwide [S252]. The same report says annual grid investment must rise by about 50% by 2030 from around USD 400 billion today, while new grids often take 5–15 years to plan, permit, and complete, versus roughly 1–5 years for wind and solar, 1–3 years for data centres, and 1–2 years for EV charging infrastructure [S252]. IEA's 2026 data-centre analysis sharpens the load-side version: data-centre electricity demand rose 17% in 2025, data-centre consumption is set to double by 2030, AI-focused data-centre power use is poised to triple, and project pipelines are now straining transformers, gas turbines, chips, planning systems, regulatory systems, and grid approvals [S253].

So the programme needs an allocation doctrine, not only a build doctrine.
It has to decide which uses of scarce bottleneck capacity lower danger, retire dirty service, protect people, and create replicable clean defaults — and which uses should wait, pay more, redesign, become flexible, bring their own enabling infrastructure, or be rejected.

## Fast rule

**Treat scarce climate bottlenecks as public allocation problems. Allocate grid capacity, clean power, public finance, staff time, hardware, land, water, and political attention by physical danger reduction, dirty-default displacement, protection value, fairness, readiness, integrity, and replication value — not by first-come status, strongest bidder, loudest incumbent, or best press release** [S130][S132][S151][S152][S153][S176][S197][S198][S207][S226][S227][S228][S231][S235][S241][S242][S244][S252][S253].

## Why this note is not redundant

- `242` says the climate answer should become a governed work queue.
- `244` says each queue item should become an owner-led delivery packet.
- `245` says how to govern when good, bad, urgent, private, public, mature, speculative, clean, and dirty claims all compete for the same constrained capacity.

A packet can be well formed and still lose priority if the shared bottleneck should be used for something more urgent.
A private load can be economically valuable and still be a bad climate allocation if it consumes grid headroom, clean power, water, staff, or political attention without displacement, flexibility, or public value.

House rule: **scarce capacity is not neutral just because the queue is formal.**

## The compact canon

### 1. Name the constrained resource before ranking the claim
A serious allocation decision should first say which scarce thing is actually binding:
- transmission connection
- distribution hosting capacity
- transformer, conductor, cable, switchgear, or converter supply
- dispatchable clean capacity, storage, or flexibility
- interconnection-study capacity
- permitting staff
- trained installers or inspectors
- project-preparation teams
- concessional capital, guarantees, or local-currency finance
- public procurement skill
- land, water, or community acceptance
- durable storage capacity or biomass supply
- political attention and administrative bandwidth

House rule: **do not argue priority in the abstract; argue priority per constrained resource.**

### 2. Score claims by physical public value, not only private willingness to pay
The relevant question is not only who wants the capacity.
It is what happens if that scarce capacity is assigned to this claim rather than another.

Priority should rise when a packet:
- retires dirty service or blocks dirty reinvestment
- cuts methane, refrigerants, nitrous oxide, or other fast-warming emissions
- protects people from heat, flood, fire, smoke, water stress, or outage harm
- lowers peak load or releases grid capacity
- improves affordability or essential-service reliability
- creates a replicable clean archetype
- serves exposed or under-served communities
- brings finance, flexibility, or enabling infrastructure with it
- prevents new lock-in

Priority should fall when a packet:
- merely adds demand without displacement
- uses clean-power claims to hide fossil backup
- is inflexible when flexibility is technically available
- consumes public bottlenecks for luxury or speculative demand
- shifts cost or risk onto weaker actors
- cannot prove additionality or physical outcome
- uses emergency language to obtain permanent dirty exceptions

House rule: **rank claims by danger avoided and durable clean service created per unit of bottleneck consumed.**

### 3. Keep essential-service floors ahead of optional premium loads
Cooling for heat-vulnerable households, clean cooking, hospital resilience, water pumping, transit, public housing retrofit, grid reliability, methane repair, and basic clean development should not be crowded out by flexible or speculative loads that can wait, relocate, pay for upgrades, or change operating patterns [S212][S214][S217][S227][S229][S230][S234][S236][S252][S253].

This does not mean all large private loads are illegitimate.
It means they should not silently absorb public transition capacity while essential protection and replacement packets remain blocked.

House rule: **protect necessity before serving convenience.**

### 4. Do not let first-come queues become climate policy
First-come rules can look fair while locking in bad order.
They favour actors who know the process early, can hold queue positions, can finance waiting, can flood applications, or can tolerate speculative delay.

A climate queue should instead use gates:
- formed packet gate: physical outcome, owner, authority, money, bottleneck, metric, fallback
- displacement gate: what dirty default loses ground?
- protection gate: who becomes safer?
- flexibility gate: can the claim move, modulate, store, or curtail?
- finance gate: is the cost and risk honestly assigned?
- readiness gate: can it actually build?
- integrity gate: are claims measured physically?
- equity gate: does it protect or burden weaker parties?

House rule: **queue order should be earned by readiness and public value, not inherited from application timestamp alone.**

Rev0242 sharpens the readiness side: capacity should not automatically go to the actor best able to pay for consultants first, but high-value claims still need a prepared file. The answer is to build public-interest project shelves and technical-assistance channels so readiness can reflect physical value and fairness rather than private preparation budgets [S247][S254][S255][S256][S257][S258].

### 5. Make bottleneck release itself a portfolio
Some of the highest-value climate work does not look like a wind farm, heat pump, bus, electrolyser, or retrofit.
It looks like a transformer framework contract, a permitting-office hiring plan, a standard interconnection study, a local-currency guarantee platform, a building-inspector training ladder, a water-reuse rule, a methane-response crew, or a procurement template.

These should be held as delivery packets too [S132][S176][S197][S198][S226][S227][S228][S231][S244][S252].

House rule: **release the bottleneck, not just the project trapped behind it.**

### 6. Require large new loads to help solve the constraint they create
A large load that wants fast connection during a constrained period should face a load-entry test:
- efficiency first
- location discipline
- incremental clean supply
- flexibility or storage
- grid upgrade contribution
- congestion and curtailment response
- water and heat disclosure
- no routine fossil backup lock-in
- public reporting where public bottlenecks are used
- contribution to local affordability or reliability where it stresses them

This is not anti-electrification.
It is pro-electrification that does not cannibalize the clean backbone.

House rule: **a new large load should arrive as a grid citizen, not a passive claim on scarce headroom.**

### 7. Separate clean-development claims from luxury-demand claims
The same technology can be part of the solution or part of the bottleneck problem depending on use.
Electricity for clean cooking, heat pumps, buses, freight, industrial efficiency, and public cooling may replace combustion or protect people.
Electricity for speculative, wasteful, poorly located, or inflexible loads may consume the same scarce capacity without equivalent public value.

House rule: **electrification is a means, not an automatic priority label.**

### 8. Keep fairness visible in the bottleneck ledger
Scarce capacity allocation will always create winners and losers.
A serious programme should publish who receives scarce capacity, who waits, what alternative is offered, how costs are assigned, and whether exposed communities, lower-income users, small firms, public agencies, and EMDE projects are being crowded out by actors with stronger balance sheets [S156][S158][S194][S212][S227].

House rule: **a hidden bottleneck allocation is a hidden distributional policy.**

### 9. Use temporary exceptions only with expiry and return tests
Constrained periods will produce emergency claims: fossil backup, queue-jumping, looser standards, new gas infrastructure, shortened consultation, or delayed retirement.
Some exceptions may be justified.
But they should be temporary, narrow, measurable, paired with correction, and subject to return-to-routine tests [S103][S104][S108][S109][S123][S238][S243].

House rule: **scarcity can justify a detour; it cannot become a permanent license for dirtier defaults.**

### 10. Review allocation outcomes, not only allocation rules
The allocation system should be judged by what it produces:
- lower gross emissions
- lower methane and other fast-warming emissions
- more clean service carrying real load
- shorter connection queues for high-value packets
- lower peak stress
- fewer blocked protection projects
- lower burden on ordinary users
- less fossil reinvestment
- less exposure
- faster replication of proven archetypes

House rule: **a fair-looking rule that produces dirty, slow, fragile, or unequal outcomes needs correction.**

## Constrained-capacity ledger in one view

| Constrained capacity | Bad default | Better allocation rule | First proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grid connection | first-come queue hoarding | priority by displacement, flexibility, readiness, and public value | clean service connected, dirty fallback reduced, queue time down |
| Grid hardware | ad hoc procurement | portfolio contracts and priority to bottleneck-release packets | transformer / cable / switchgear lead times down |
| Public finance | headline pledges and bespoke deals | standard project classes, guarantees, local-currency channels | financial close and replication rate up |
| Skilled labour | hope and local scarcity | training ladders tied to real packet pipeline | install quality up, delay and callbacks down |
| Land and water | project-by-project conflict | spatial screening, reuse, community benefit, no-go zones | fewer reversals, less ecological and social harm |
| Clean power | certificate theatre | additionality, time matching where needed, flexibility, storage | less fossil dispatch and curtailment waste |
| Political attention | crisis drift | small number of high-value operating reviews | weak signals corrected before failure loops form |

## What this note demotes

This note demotes:
- first-come queues as a substitute for public priority
- private willingness to pay as the only allocation rule
- electrification claims with no load-shape, location, or displacement test
- clean-power claims with no additionality or operational value
- projects that consume scarce capacity while leaving essential protection blocked
- bottleneck complaints that do not fund or form bottleneck-release packets
- emergency fossil detours without expiry, correction, or return tests
- hidden distributional choices disguised as neutral administration

### Rev0251 bridge: scarce capacity includes cross-border market access

Rev0251 adds one more scarce-capacity category: the ability of clean products, materials, components, certificates, and spares to cross borders and pass trusted proof gates. A packet that wins grid capacity or finance can still be idle if transformers, batteries, heat pumps, inverters, chargers, conductors, or test certificates are blocked by supplier concentration, export controls, customs uncertainty, absent labs, duplicate testing, or incompatible standards [S311][S312][S313][S314][S315][S316][S317].

House rule: **do not allocate scarce domestic capacity to packets whose cross-border dependency and market-access path is invisible.**

## Compression rule

**When climate bottlenecks are scarce, capacity allocation is climate policy. Give scarce grid capacity, finance, hardware, staff, land, water, and attention to packets that physically cut danger, retire dirty defaults, protect people, release bottlenecks, and replicate clean service — not to the first, richest, loudest, or most speculative claimant.**
