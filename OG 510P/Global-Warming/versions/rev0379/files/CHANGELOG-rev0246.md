# CHANGELOG — rev0246

## Revision title

**rev0246 — balance-sheet and affordability-firebreak front door**

## Why this revision exists

Rev0245 made recovery, protection gaps, uninsurability, public backstops, and retreat part of climate governance rather than after-disaster cleanup.

Rev0246 adds the next operating layer: climate risk and transition cost have to be placed on named balance sheets before they become hidden household debt, rent pressure, premium shock, utility-rate shock, municipal exposure, credit stress, public-backstop exhaustion, or disorderly repricing.

The governing question becomes:

**Who carries the risk and cost before the shock, during the transition, after recovery, and after repricing — and what firebreak keeps ordinary people and essential services from absorbing the programme's hidden failures?**

## New doctrine in one paragraph

A climate programme is not durable if physical risk and transition cost are hidden until the weakest balance sheet absorbs them. Rev0246 therefore requires major packets to identify household, renter, landlord, utility, insurer, lender, municipal, public-backstop, and treasury exposure; to link disclosure and stress testing to actual decisions; to make cost-recovery channels explicit; and to build targeted affordability firebreaks across bills, premiums, rents, rates, taxes, fees, deductibles, debt, and time burdens.

## Compression rule

**Do not let climate policy hide risk or cost on the weakest balance sheet and then call the resulting backlash a failure of public will. Name the absorbing balance sheets, reduce the risk, make cost recovery explicit, protect essential users, and correct burden pile-ups before they break the programme.**
