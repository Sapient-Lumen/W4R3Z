# CHANGELOG — rev0250

## Focus

Cyber-resilience, fallback, and climate-AI accountability front-door revision.

Rev0249 made climate records and proof carriers interoperable. Rev0250 adds the safety layer beneath that interoperability: a connected climate operating system must not become a brittle cyber-physical failure surface, privacy hazard, vendor lock-in machine, or unowned automated authority. Digitally dependent packets now need fallback and fail-safe records, and AI / model / digital-twin / scoring uses now need authority, human review, contest, monitoring, and retirement rules.

## Core doctrine added

- A connected climate system is not ready until its digital dependencies are mapped, secured, privacy-preserving, recoverable, and able to degrade safely.
- Cyber resilience, offline fallback, fail-safe controls, incident response, vendor obligations, and privacy minimization are climate infrastructure whenever digital failure can change emissions, protection, money, service, or remedy.
- Climate AI should help people build, protect, verify, repair, and prioritize real work; it should not become an unowned authority over money, service, risk, capacity, claims, recovery, or rights.
- High-consequence AI, model, digital-twin, scoring, or automated-rule uses should be registered, bounded, tested, monitored, appealable, overrideable, and retired when they cannot support the duty they claim to serve.
- Digitalization should reduce ordinary-user burden, not move complexity downstream into machine-mediated denials, eligibility churn, or inaccessible proof loops.
