# 199 — Ideal Solutions: Replacement Proof — What Counts as Real Displacement Compact Canon

## Claim

The archive now has a shortest answer (`195`), a non-substitutions note (`196`), a simultaneous-verdicts note (`197`), a transition-mechanism note (`198`), a physical-first note (`189`), a package-logic note (`190`), and a retirement map (`148`).
What it still lacked was one very small front-door note answering a practical counting question that sits underneath many climate arguments:

**When should a claimed climate gain count as real progress rather than as clean addition beside an only-weakly-changed dirty baseline?**

That gap matters because climate language often treats announced clean capacity, sales counts, labelled products, signed contracts, or even operating clean assets as if those alone proved system transition.
But the archive’s house view is stricter.
A climate gain counts first by the dirty service, fuel use, asset stock, leak rate, or exposure pattern it actually shrinks, prevents, retires, or avoids repeating.
This note is therefore a compression note, not a new doctrinal branch.
Its job is to gather the archive’s physical-first rule, non-substitution rule, transition-mechanism rule, turnover rule, retirement rule, and correction rule into one counting rule for real replacement [S100][S101][S103][S106][S113][S123][S135][S136][S145][S147][S161][S163][S164][S180].

## Fast rule

**Count a claimed climate gain first by what dirty dependence it actually displaces, prevents, retires, or makes unnecessary — not mainly by the volume of clean stuff added beside the old regime.**

## The five replacement-proof questions

### 1. What dirty service, asset, or exposure pattern is actually shrinking?
A serious climate claim should be able to name the thing that is declining in the real world: fossil generation, boiler gas use, diesel freight, methane leakage, wasteful cooling load, deforestation pressure, repeat-loss exposure, or some other concrete harmful baseline.
If the proposal cannot name what is materially getting smaller, the archive should treat the claim cautiously even when the clean addition sounds attractive [S101][S103][S106][S113].

### 2. Is the cleaner substitute real enough to carry service rather than merely decorate the system?
A serious climate claim should show that the cleaner pathway is connected, usable, financeable, reliable enough, and institutionally supported enough to carry meaningful service.
Nameplate capacity, pilot status, premium niche sales, or elegant roadmap language are not the same as serviceable substitution at scale [S100][S103][S106][S145][S180].

### 3. Is the old path being constrained, retired, or denied another lock-in cycle?
A serious climate gain should not rely on passive hope that the dirty path will quietly fade by itself.
It should show some combination of retirement, closure, standards, default changes, procurement shifts, permit restraint, subsidy withdrawal, leak repair, or no-rebuild / no-replacement discipline, so clean addition is joined to dirty contraction [S101][S145][S147][S161][S163][S164].

### 4. Are dual-running, rebound, and leakage being kept inside disciplined limits?
A serious climate claim should ask whether the system is simply paying for both tracks at once, exporting the harm elsewhere, or allowing demand rebound and exception drift to eat most of the gain.
If clean buildout arrives while the old pathway remains fully available, newly subsidised, weakly measured, or quietly relocated, the archive should demote the claim from solved replacement to partial progress at best [S101][S103][S113][S123][S135][S136][S147].

### 5. Does the physical ledger show the change clearly enough to survive correction?
A serious climate programme should judge the replacement by measured gross emissions, measured methane, retired dirty assets, lower energy waste, lower exposure, and other direct outcome signals rather than by pipeline size, expenditure, or public narrative.
If the physical ledger stays weak, the next duty is not rhetorical defence but correction, tighter proof, and possibly stronger retirement discipline [S103][S106][S113][S123][S135][S136][S147].

## Preferred use

Open this note first when the live question is any of the following:
- what should count as real displacement rather than mere clean addition
- when operating clean buildout is not yet enough to call the transition real
- how to tell whether a proposal is replacing the old regime or merely accompanying it
- why dual-running, rebound, leakage, and weak retirement can make climate gains look larger than they are
- how to explain the archive’s counting rule for real replacement before reopening the longer scoreboard, transition, or retirement notes

Then reopen `189`, `196`, `197`, `198`, `148`, `149`, `150`, or `65` depending on whether the surviving question is about physical proof, non-substitutions, seriousness tests, transition mechanism, retirement, anti-regression, correction cycle, or scoreboarding.

## Compression rule

**Do not count clean addition by itself as the whole win. Count first the dirty dependence, leak, waste, or exposure pattern that actually shrinks, the cleaner service that actually carries load, and the old pathway that is actually prevented, retired, or denied another cycle.**
