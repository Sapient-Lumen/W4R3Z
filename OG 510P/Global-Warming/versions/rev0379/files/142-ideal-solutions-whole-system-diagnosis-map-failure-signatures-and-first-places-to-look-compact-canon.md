# 142 — Ideal Solutions: Whole-System Diagnosis Map — Failure Signatures and First Places to Look Compact Canon

## Claim

The archive already has a mature whole-system set:
- a static layer map (`136`)
- a feedback map (`137`)
- a scoreboard map (`138`)
- a control-surface map (`139`)
- a sequencing map (`140`)
- and a boundary map (`141`)

That is now enough architecture, causality, signals, leverage, order-of-attack, and boundary structure that the next clean gain is **not** another narrow sector note.
What it still lacked was the shortest joined note for the reverse move:

**when the scoreboard weakens, where should a serious climate programme look first, and what kind of fix is probably missing?**

This note is a compression note, not a new doctrinal branch.
Its job is to gather the archive's scoreboard rule, control-surface rule, sequencing rule, boundary rule, and joined delivery / truth / political-economy / protection / governance / readiness / detour canons into one first-stop diagnosis map [S138][S139][S140][S141][S124][S122][S123][S131][S132][S133][S134][S135].

## Fast rule

**When the whole-system scoreboard slips, diagnose before you prescribe. First ask whether the signal is real or a measurement / enforcement problem. Then ask whether the physical programme is arriving or blocked. Then ask whether burden, legitimacy, or affordability are suppressing uptake. Then ask whether protection, readiness, or detour discipline is failing under stress. Then ask whether cross-border finance, standards, or supply constraints are binding. Use the boundary map to stop dirty convenience, paper substitution, or weak exceptions from disguising the true failure.**

## The compact diagnosis map

### 1. Integrity-first diagnosis: make sure the bad signal is real
If claims, labels, inventories, baselines, or programme dashboards drift away from physical outcomes, the first failure may be in the truth layer rather than in the plant, household, or grid itself.

Failure signatures:
- reported progress without matching physical outcomes
- large unexplained gaps between administrative claims and metered reality
- target compliance that depends on exclusions, accounting shifts, or unverifiable assumptions
- noisy dashboards that cannot distinguish success, delay, and failure

### 2. Throughput diagnosis: ask whether the right portfolio is actually arriving
If truth is broadly sound, the next question is whether the physical programme is being built, connected, retrofitted, retired, and maintained fast enough.

Failure signatures:
- long queues, permits, interconnection delays, or missing crews
- physical replacement cycles being missed
- supply constraints, serial bespoke delivery, or slow commissioning
- a large announced pipeline with weak completion and retention

### 3. Burden / uptake diagnosis: ask whether the programme is too hard, too risky, or too costly for normal actors
A programme can be technically available and still stall because cash burden, hassle burden, risk burden, or legitimacy burden blocks adoption.

Failure signatures:
- low uptake despite nominal eligibility or technical feasibility
- bill anxiety, financing failure, backlash, or programme distrust
- clean options that work only for high-capacity users
- rising political resistance even where the physical case is strong

### 4. Protection / stress diagnosis: ask whether exposure is outrunning mitigation success
Even a decent mitigation programme can feel like failure if people, water, health systems, lifelines, and settlements are getting hit faster than protection capacity is improving.

Failure signatures:
- rising losses, outages, deaths, or displacement despite nominal mitigation progress
- repeated service breakdowns under heat, drought, flood, smoke, or storm stress
- protection gains concentrated in stronger places while weaker places remain exposed
- emergency demand for dirty fallback because protection and resilience were thin

### 5. Governance / readiness diagnosis: ask whether correction is too slow or too confused
A programme can know what to do and still underperform because authority, clocks, readiness, or detour discipline are too weak to correct drift quickly.

Failure signatures:
- repeated bottlenecks that nobody owns end-to-end
- slow correction after visible failure signals
- fallback and exception becoming normal rather than temporary
- readiness claims that are stale, disputed, or too flattering to trust

### 6. Cross-border diagnosis: ask whether external constraints are binding faster than domestic systems can compensate
A domestic programme can stall because capital is too expensive, standards are fragmented, trade rules are dirty, or supply chains are thin.

Failure signatures:
- high cost of capital, debt stress, or weak fiscal space blocking buildout
- standards fragmentation slowing scale, interoperability, or trade
- supply-chain choke points or import dependence turning into chronic delay
- leakage, competitiveness panic, or international-sector gaps undermining domestic resolve

## Smallest useful reading pattern from this map

If the mature programme looks weak or is getting visibly worse:
1. read this note (`142`) to identify the most likely failing layer
2. read `138` to classify which class of signal is actually degrading
3. open the most likely joined canon from the diagnostic route above
4. open `139` only after diagnosis if you need the highest-leverage push points
5. open `140` only after that if you need the rough order of attack
6. reopen `141` whenever the tempting fix depends on paper substitution, dirty fallback, or a weak exception

## Boundary

**Do not treat diagnosis as ideology first.**
Do not jump from one bad outcome straight to a favourite policy. Separate measurement failure from physical failure, throughput failure from burden failure, protection failure from governance failure, and domestic failure from cross-border binding constraints before prescribing the fix.

## Admission rule

Keep this note only if it continues to reduce navigation cost across the mature front door.
If a later revision finds a cleaner way to join scoreboard, diagnosis, leverage, sequencing, and boundaries without redundancy, merge or replace rather than letting multiple near-duplicate whole-system maps accumulate.
