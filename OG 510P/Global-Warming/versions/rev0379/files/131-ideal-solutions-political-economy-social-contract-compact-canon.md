# 131 — Ideal Solutions: Political Economy / Social Contract Compact Canon

## Claim

The archive now has joined canons for:
- design (`128`)
- the physical portfolio (`130`)
- the governing kernel (`124`)
- readiness (`122`)
- off-normal operation (`123`)
- intake (`127`)
- and the mature front door itself (`129`)

What it still lacked was one joined first-stop note for the layer that decides whether the physical programme can actually survive contact with households, voters, fiscal limits, and the international capital stack:

**the political economy / social-contract layer.**

This note is not a new doctrinal branch.
It is a compression note.
Its job is to gather the archive’s core affordability, legitimacy, fiscal-durability, political-durability, payment-form, upside-allocation, and EMDE-finance rules into one place.

## Fast rule

**A serious climate programme must not only be physically right. It must also be materially livable, visibly fair, fiscally durable, politically continuous, and financeable where capital is currently too expensive. Lower structural burden, keep upfront cost and hassle low, make public upside visible, give people real voice and recourse, avoid Treasury burn that cannot survive politics, and cut the cost-of-capital barriers that stop clean buildout in poorer countries.**

## The compact canon

### 1. Keep the programme materially easier to live with
Start from service floors and recurring burden, not virtue narratives.
The programme is stronger when households experience it as:
- lower structural bills rather than new permanent exposure
- cleaner comfort, mobility, cooling, and cooking rather than moral homework
- less hassle at the point of decision
- stronger protection against both climate shocks and transition shocks

This is the archive’s basic social-contract rule from `20` and `61`: raise essential clean service floors, lower affluent waste ceilings, and do not treat household pain as proof of seriousness.

### 2. Keep cash, hassle, and risk off the weakest actor
The clean path is weaker when it quietly selects only the liquid, the time-rich, the digitally fluent, or the already-bankable.
So the preferred political-economy shape is:
- low upfront burden
- repayment over the life of the asset or service where appropriate
- targeted support where affordability gaps are real
- embedded help at point of decision rather than reimbursement mazes
- burden-shifting upward to actors better able to carry coordination, proof, and downside

This joins the archive’s payment-form, burden-shape, and user-pathway rules from `70`, `79`, and `78`.

### 3. Make public upside visible and condition private gain
Public enabling should not disappear into private extraction.
When the state de-risks, permits, procures, regulates, or subsidises, the programme is stronger when that public action visibly returns as:
- lower bills
- better service
- broader access
- stronger local benefit
- cleaner market structure
- or future cost reduction that others can reuse

This is the archive’s joined upside-allocation and anti-capture rule from `69` and `40`: public value should not be an accidental by-product.

### 4. Treat legitimacy as operating infrastructure
Political durability does not come only from good outcomes measured later.
It also comes from whether people can see that the programme is being done with them rather than to them.
So the joined legitimacy rule is:
- give real voice early, especially where siting, land, cost recovery, or closure is contested
- keep benefit-sharing visible
- keep recourse real
- keep exceptions narrow and explainable
- and keep the clean path legible enough that ordinary people can tell what they are being asked to support and why

This joins the archive’s legitimacy, remedy, and continuity logic from `44`, `74`, and `75`.

### 5. Keep public finance durable and continuity credible
A good climate programme should not depend on an implausible indefinite Treasury burn or on a single electoral coalition never losing.
The preferred shape is therefore:
- durable assets over indefinite blanket subsidies
- targeted bridge support over open-ended dependence
- regulated or embedded recovery where the service justifies it
- commitment devices and delivery forms that survive political turnover
- and visible gains early enough that the programme develops defenders

This joins the archive’s fiscal-durability and political-durability logic from `45`, `46`, and `33`.

### 6. Lower the cost of capital where money is the bottleneck
For many EMDE, the climate problem is no longer mainly missing technology.
It is expensive money, currency mismatch, debt pressure, and thin risk-reduction architecture.
So a serious global climate programme should treat the following as part of the solution, not external charity:
- concessional and catalytic finance where private capital alone is too expensive
- guarantee and de-risking structures tied to real public benefit
- local-currency and debt-space realism where hard-currency finance would otherwise choke the project
- public and MDB balance sheets used to lower spreads where they are the main blocker
- and deployment structures that copy and standardise rather than bespoke every transaction

This is the archive’s joined EMDE-finance rule from `25`, `91`, and `153`: if capital is priced wrongly, the physical portfolio will under-arrive.

## Preferred use

Open this note first when the live question is any of the following:
- how the programme stays affordable enough to keep consent
- how the clean path should be paid for
- how public value should be retained rather than extracted
- how legitimacy and participation fit inside delivery
- how the programme survives fiscal stress or electoral turnover
- how poorer countries can finance the same transition at world scale

Then reopen the deeper note that matches the surviving sub-question.

## Compression rule

**Treat political economy as delivery infrastructure, not after-the-fact communications. If the programme is physically right but unaffordable, illegible, fiscally brittle, politically whiplashed, or blocked by expensive capital, it is not yet an ideal solution.**
