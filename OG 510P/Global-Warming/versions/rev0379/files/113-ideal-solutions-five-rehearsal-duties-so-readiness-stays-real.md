# 113 — Ideal Solutions: Five Rehearsal Duties So Readiness Stays Real

## Claim

The archive already has:
- a readiness note (`111`)
- a live-discipline note (`112`)
- a tripwires note (`103`)
- a response-modes note (`104`)
- a burden-of-proof note (`108`)
- a return-to-routine note (`109`)
- and an after-action note (`110`)

What it still lacked was the shortest **rehearsal-discipline note** for the question:

**How does a serious climate state keep readiness from decaying into paper compliance, stale contact lists, and untested playbooks?**

That matters because many systems look “ready” until the first real cross-role handoff.
They have a trigger memo, a fallback ladder, some pre-authorized tools, and perhaps even a dashboard template — but the contacts are stale, the authority boundaries are fuzzy, the data path is not live, the protection promise is not operational, and nobody has practiced closing the detour once it opens.
A compact archive needed one short bridge between **having a plan** and **knowing the plan still works**.

This note is a **compression note**, not a new doctrinal branch.
Its job is to reduce readiness maintenance to five rehearsal duties that keep the readiness note (`111`) real enough to survive contact with a bad day [S34][S39][S55][S83][S102][S103][S104][S108][S109][S110][S111][S112][S114][S145][S147][S181][S182][S183].

## Fast rule

**Run drills on a calendar, rehearse the whole handoff chain, use live data and real tools where possible, practice not just entry but shrink-and-exit, and convert every rehearsal gap into an actual repair before the next round.**

## Five rehearsal duties

### 1. Rehearse on a real calendar, not only after a scare
A serious climate state should drill off-normal pathways on a standing cadence rather than only after a disaster, outage, scandal, or seasonal warning.

What this usually means:
- rehearsal dates are attached to the governing clocks rather than left to discretionary goodwill
- drills happen often enough to catch staff turnover, procurement drift, seasonal risk changes, and stale data paths
- the minimum rehearsal cadence is known in advance for high-risk lanes and exposed places [S55][S102][S111][S145][S147]

Why it matters:
- readiness decays quietly when it is not exercised
- a playbook that has not been touched since the last crisis is not real readiness

### 2. Rehearse the whole handoff chain, not just one team’s memo
A serious climate state should drill the chain from trigger detection to authority handoff, public truth, protection delivery, clean-mainline repair, and eventual exit — not only the first escalation decision.

What this usually means:
- drills include the mission owner, lane operators, truth function, protection backstop, and any key local or regulated actors
- the exercise tests who calls whom, who can authorize what, what gets published, who protects exposed people first, and how the cleaner repair path is kept ahead of the workaround
- the weak link is often a boundary between roles, rights, or institutions rather than a missing policy idea [S81][S83][S101][S105][S106][S107][S111][S112][S147]

Why it matters:
- most failures happen at joins, not inside one silo
- if the chain cannot be rehearsed end to end, it will probably fail end to end under pressure

### 3. Use live tools, current data, and real inventories where possible
A rehearsal should touch the actual dashboard, contact tree, procurement route, backup asset list, queue status, and protection channels the state expects to rely on.

What this usually means:
- drills use the current operating picture, current named contacts, and current asset or contractor availability rather than a frozen slide deck
- data feeds, message templates, switching procedures, and protection pathways are tested in the same systems used in live operations where sensible
- where full live testing is unsafe or too costly, the rehearsal should still be close enough to reveal stale assumptions quickly [S34][S39][S83][S111][S112][S114][S145]

Why it matters:
- paper readiness hides rot in contact trees, inventories, data access, and operational tooling
- the point of rehearsal is to surface reality, not to stage a comforting presentation

### 4. Rehearse shrink-and-exit, not only admission
A serious climate state should practice not only how it enters escalation, fallback, or exception, but how it narrows the detour, re-tests necessity, restores the cleaner mainline, and returns to routine.

What this usually means:
- drills include the burden-of-proof check, the dirty-boundary containment rule, the shrink plan, and the return-to-routine tests
- the exercise asks what evidence would now justify closing or narrowing the detour, not only what evidence justified opening it
- actors benefiting from the workaround should not be allowed to dominate the rehearsal design [S84][S85][S86][S87][S108][S109][S112][S145][S147]

Why it matters:
- systems that only rehearse entry tend to normalize the detour once it starts
- temporary measures stay temporary only if exit is practiced as seriously as entry

### 5. Turn every rehearsal gap into a tracked repair
A rehearsal only counts if it leaves the system more ready than before.

What this usually means:
- every drill ends with named defects, named owners, deadlines, and a visible check on whether the repair happened
- repairs can include changed contracts, refreshed contact trees, new data access, added backup assets, stronger protection routing, narrower exception wording, or a shorter review clock
- unresolved rehearsal defects should roll into escalation risk, management review, and future drill design rather than disappearing into a lessons-learned folder [S34][S55][S83][S103][S109][S110][S111][S112][S147]

Why it matters:
- a rehearsal that does not change anything is mostly theatre
- the goal is not to prove competence but to reduce the probability and cost of failure on the real day

## Minimal rehearsal loop

The smallest serious rehearsal loop is usually:

1. run the drill on a standing calendar
2. test the handoff chain from trigger to protection to repair to exit
3. use live tools and current data wherever feasible
4. practice shrink-and-return, not only admission
5. assign and close repairs before the next rehearsal [S55][S83][S102][S108][S109][S110][S111][S112][S147]

## One-paragraph compression

**The archive now has the shortest rehearsal note for serious climate governance: keep readiness real by drilling on a standing calendar, rehearsing the whole handoff chain, using live tools and current data where possible, practicing shrink-and-exit rather than entry alone, and converting every exercise gap into an actual repair before the next round. This sits between readiness on paper and live discipline in the incident, without adding another sectoral branch.**
