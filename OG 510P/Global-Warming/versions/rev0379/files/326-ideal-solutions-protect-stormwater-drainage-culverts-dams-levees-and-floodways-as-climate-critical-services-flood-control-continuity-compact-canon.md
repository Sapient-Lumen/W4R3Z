# 326 — Ideal Solutions: Protect stormwater, drainage, culverts, dams, levees, and floodways as climate-critical services

## Claim

The archive has water, WASH, coasts, transport, housing, and debris. It now needs the connecting flood-control floor: **stormwater, drainage, culverts, dams, levees, floodways, pumps, wetlands, detention, and maintenance are climate-critical services, not background civil works.**

Flood risk is often described as a hazard. But in cities, watersheds, ports, roads, and settlements, flood harm is also an operating failure: drains are blocked, culverts are undersized, dams age, levees create false security, floodplains are built over, wetlands are removed, pumps lose power, and debris converts rain into sewage, access loss, and disease risk.

FEMA's dam-safety program exists to help state programs ensure dam safety and protect human life and property [S578]. The World Bank's lifeline framing says infrastructure resilience should be judged by whether services function and meet users' needs during and after shocks [S490]. That makes floodwater management a service floor.

## Fast rule

**Every place-based climate packet should name the floodwater path: where water goes, what keeps it moving or stored, who maintains it, who is flooded first, and what happens when the designed path fails.**

## The compact canon

### 1. Drainage is a live service

A drainage system is not ready because it exists on a map. It is ready only if inlets are clear, culverts pass expected flows, pumps have power, outfalls are not tide-locked, detention has capacity, debris is managed, and the maintenance crew can reach the asset.

### 2. Flood-control assets create moral hazard unless paired with land discipline

Levees, dams, walls, pumps, and channels can reduce risk, but they can also induce new exposure. Every protection asset should carry a no-build / managed-retreat / disclosure rule for residual risk, overtopping, breach, compound flooding, and downstream consequences.

### 3. Nature-based buffers need maintenance and legal space

Wetlands, floodplains, mangroves, dunes, urban soils, trees, and detention landscapes can protect services and health [S128]. But they need land, hydrology, maintenance, governance, and protection from being converted into future development capacity.

### 4. Model compound flood, not single hazard

Sea-level rise, storm surge, river flow, extreme rain, saturated soils, debris, wastewater overflow, power loss, and pump failure can coincide. Service-floor planning should stress test compound floods and publish where protective assumptions break.

### 5. Count blockage and access consequences

A blocked culvert can cut off dialysis, school, food delivery, fire response, waste pickup, and evacuation. Drainage priority should be based on service consequence, not only flood depth or asset class.

## Bottom line

Floodwater management is a climate-critical service because water moves through every other service floor. The cube should now require a floodwater path, maintenance ledger, residual-risk rule, and downstream-consequence map.
