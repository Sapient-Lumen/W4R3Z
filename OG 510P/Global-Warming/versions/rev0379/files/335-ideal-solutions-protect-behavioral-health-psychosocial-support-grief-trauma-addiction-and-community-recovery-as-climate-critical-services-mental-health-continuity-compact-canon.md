# 335 — Ideal Solutions: Protect behavioral health, psychosocial support, grief, trauma, addiction, and community recovery as climate-critical services

## Claim

Climate recovery is not complete when roads reopen. **Behavioral health and psychosocial support are climate-critical services because heat, smoke, flood, fire, displacement, loss, debt, uncertainty, and repeated recovery create trauma, grief, anxiety, depression, substance-use harm, family violence risk, and responder burnout.**

WHO states that climate change exacerbates social, environmental, and economic risk factors for mental-health and psychosocial problems, while service access gaps remain large [S595]. IASC emergency guidelines frame mental health and psychosocial support as minimum multi-sectoral emergency response, not a specialist luxury [S596]. SAMHSA's 2025 crisis-care guidance gives a useful operational triad: someone to contact, someone to respond, and a safe place for help [S597].

## Compact rule

**Every recovery packet should include a behavioral-health path for affected people and workers.**

The minimum path is not simply a hotline. It includes outreach, referral completion, school support, grief and bereavement support, substance-use harm reduction, family-safety referral, responder support, privacy, language access, and follow-up after the television cameras leave.

## The service floor

A behavioral-health service floor should guarantee:

- psychological first aid during response;
- crisis contact by phone, text, radio, in person, or trusted intermediary;
- mobile response where travel or stigma blocks access;
- safe places for acute distress;
- child and school reentry supports;
- grief, missing-person, and bereavement support after deaths;
- care for first responders, cleanup workers, health workers, teachers, caseworkers, and volunteers;
- referral tracking without coercive surveillance.

## What not to do

Do not convert every normal grief reaction into pathology. Do not offer a one-time counseling visit while leaving people in unsafe housing, debt, hunger, or legal limbo. Do not make telehealth the only pathway after telecoms fail. Do not make crisis response primarily a policing problem. Do not offload community healing onto unpaid local groups without funding.

## Cube routing

Route this file whenever a packet involves death, missing people, displacement, school closure, long recovery, repeated disaster, smoke, heat, financial loss, workplace trauma, family violence risk, addiction, suicide risk, or responder burnout. Pair with `304` for mass casualty and family reunification, `328` for community bridges, `313` for gendered safety, and `281` / `282` for children.
