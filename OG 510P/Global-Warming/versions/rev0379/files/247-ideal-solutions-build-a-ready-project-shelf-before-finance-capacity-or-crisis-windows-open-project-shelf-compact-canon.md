# 247 — Ideal Solutions: Build a Ready Project Shelf Before Finance, Capacity, or Crisis Windows Open — Project-Shelf Compact Canon

## Claim

Rev0239 turned the climate answer into a governed work queue.
Rev0240 made queue items owner-led delivery packets.
Rev0241 added priority rules for scarce grid, finance, hardware, land, water, staff, and attention.

The next missing layer is readiness before the window opens.
A programme can know what matters, form packets, and rank bottlenecks — and still fail because the good work is not financeable, permitted, procurable, staffed, socially screened, connected, or ready to absorb a grant, guarantee, procurement round, disaster-recovery window, grid-capacity release, or political moment when it appears.

This is not clerical housekeeping.
It is a core climate bottleneck.
If climate finance becomes available but there is no prepared shelf, money waits or goes to easier but lower-value projects.
If grid capacity opens but the high-value replacement packet has no design, land, permit pathway, offtaker, or procurement route, capacity can be taken by a weaker claimant.
If a disaster recovery window opens but safer rebuilding packages are not prewritten, reconstruction can recreate the same exposure.
If a methane alert appears but no repair authority, crew, budget, or deadline exists, measurement becomes theatre.

Current finance and investment evidence makes the readiness problem concrete. IEA's 2025 investment review says global energy investment is set to reach about USD 3.3 trillion in 2025, with roughly USD 2.2 trillion going to clean energy, grids, storage, efficiency, electrification, low-emissions fuels, and nuclear; but it also says investment remains geographically uneven, Africa receives only about 2% of clean-energy investment despite about 20% of world population, and scaling finance in developing economies requires targeted action on real and perceived project risks [S254]. CPI says global climate finance reached about USD 1.9 trillion in 2023 and likely exceeded USD 2 trillion in 2024, while EMDEs still need more catalytic capital such as guarantees, grants, and catalytic equity to scale flows across regions [S255]. CPI's EMDE spotlight adds that EMDE climate finance surpassed USD 1 trillion in 2023, with about 80% mobilized domestically, which means the project shelf has to work through domestic institutions as well as international finance [S256]. World Bank Group's 2025 climate reporting shows the institutional direction: climate finance is increasingly project-level and sub-project-level data, not only headline claims [S257]. MIGA and the WBG Guarantee Platform show the de-risking direction: the new platform issued USD 12.3 billion in guarantees in its first year, while MIGA alone issued a record USD 9.5 billion in guarantees across 44 projects in fiscal 2025, including climate finance guarantees for 25 projects [S258].

So the next discipline is simple: **keep a ready project shelf, not only a policy wish list.**

## Fast rule

**A serious climate programme should maintain a public or institutionally inspectable shelf of prepared project and policy packets: screened, scoped, permitted or permit-routed, financeable, procurable, staffed, socially governed, measurement-ready, and tagged to the bottleneck they release or the dirty default they retire. The shelf is where ambition becomes absorbable capacity.** [S91][S96][S132][S168][S176][S180][S197][S198][S227][S228][S244][S245][S254][S255][S256][S257][S258]

## Why this note is not redundant

- `242` says **what belongs in the climate work queue**.
- `244` says **how to turn queue items into owner-led delivery packets**.
- `245` says **how to allocate scarce bottlenecks among competing packets**.
- `247` says **how to keep enough high-value packets ready before money, permits, grid capacity, disaster recovery, or political attention becomes available**.

A delivery packet can be conceptually correct but not shelf-ready.
A project can be desirable but not financeable.
A reform can be urgent but not administratively executable.
A resilience idea can be morally obvious but not pre-positioned for the next recovery window.

House rule: **unprepared priority is still a bottleneck.**

Rev0243 adds a hard boundary on the word ready: a shelf item is not truly ready if it has no crew plan, permit-office capacity, inspector capacity, operating plan, maintenance route, spare-parts logic, warranty path, or aftercare standard. See `249` and `250` for the crew and stable-service gates [S176][S178][S259][S260].

## The compact canon

### 1. Distinguish a wish list, a work queue, and a project shelf
A wish list names what people would like.
A work queue names what the programme must do.
A project shelf contains work that is prepared enough to move when a window opens.

The shelf should not include only construction projects.
It should include policy packets, procurement packets, repair packets, resilience packets, methane-response packets, finance packets, workforce packets, and bottleneck-release packets.

House rule: **do not call a priority ready until somebody else can inspect how it would actually move.**

### 2. Give every shelf item a minimum preparation file
A shelf item should have, at minimum:
- the service or harm it changes
- the physical control point
- the dirty default displaced, exposure reduced, or bottleneck released
- the owner and lawful authority
- the site, class of sites, customer class, or asset class
- the standard design or policy instrument
- the permit or approval pathway
- environmental, social, and land safeguards
- community and user terms where people are affected
- cost estimate and financing route
- tariff, payment, or offtaker logic where relevant
- guarantee, insurance, or credit-enhancement need where relevant
- procurement route and responsible buyer
- workforce and supplier requirements
- crew, certification, inspector, operator, and maintainer requirements
- operations, maintenance, and aftercare plan
- commissioning and stable-service completion gate
- measurement method and correction trigger
- expiry, review, or retirement rule

House rule: **a shelf item is not a paragraph of intention; it is a file that can survive handoff.**

### 3. Track readiness status explicitly
The shelf should make status visible:

| Status | Meaning | First question |
|---|---|---|
| Concept | useful idea, not yet formed | what is the control point? |
| Formed packet | owner, outcome, metric, authority, and bottleneck are named | what keeps it from moving? |
| Shelf-ready | design, finance route, approvals route, procurement path, and safeguards are prepared | what window can it absorb? |
| Window-ready | matched to an active grant, guarantee, procurement, grid-capacity, or recovery window | who signs next? |
| In delivery | funded, contracted, built, repaired, enforced, or installed | what does the live metric show? |
| Operating | service is running or rule is binding | what needs aftercare or correction? |
| Copyable | performance and documents are good enough to replicate | where is the next site or class? |
| Retired / closed | dirty default, risk, or obsolete packet is removed | what prevents rebound? |
| Quarantined | claim is weak, dirty, speculative, unsafe, or unformed | what proof would change the verdict? |

House rule: **readiness is a state, not a mood.**

### 4. Build shelves around archetypes, not bespoke hero projects
The fastest shelf is usually not a unique masterpiece.
It is a repeatable archetype:
- school heat-protection retrofit
- clinic cooling and backup-power resilience package
- social-housing clean-heat and insulation package
- methane super-emitter response packet
- distribution-transformer procurement and replacement packet
- bus-depot electrification with smart charging
- municipal LED, cooling, and building-controls package
- wastewater methane capture and organics diversion package
- grid reconductoring corridor packet
- low-income appliance and efficient-cooling replacement packet
- clean cooking and appliance finance packet
- floodplain buyout and safer relocation packet

House rule: **the shelf should make the second copy cheaper, faster, and safer than the first.**

### 5. Treat project preparation as climate infrastructure
Feasibility studies, grid studies, land screening, community agreements, standard contracts, offtaker repair, payment discipline, engineering templates, environmental and social review, and public-procurement documents are often the invisible infrastructure that determines whether physical infrastructure gets built.

This means project-preparation facilities, municipal technical-assistance units, utility planning teams, and national delivery offices should be funded as climate machinery, not as optional administrative overhead.

House rule: **the pipeline is an asset; underfunding it is a hidden delay policy.**

### 6. Match finance to the shelf, not only the shelf to finance
Good projects should not be forced to contort themselves around whatever finance instrument happens to exist.
The shelf should state what kind of finance each packet needs:
- grant or public budget
- concessional debt
- local-currency loan
- guarantee or political-risk cover
- first-loss tranche
- tariff-backed investment
- public procurement
- performance contract
- revolving fund
- utility rate base
- disaster-recovery allocation
- climate-resilient debt clause or contingent facility

House rule: **use finance architecture to clear named risks, not to decorate unfunded wish lists.**

### 7. Put protection and adaptation on the shelf before the disaster
The shelf should include heat, water, fire, flood, health, housing, school, hospital, and retreat packets before crisis money arrives.
After a disaster, the policy window is short and the pressure to rebuild familiar exposure is intense.
A serious protection shelf makes safer recovery the drafted default rather than a moral request under time pressure.

House rule: **prewrite the safer rebuild before the unsafe rebuild becomes the only ready plan.**

### 8. Put fast-warming response on the shelf before the alert
Methane, refrigerants, nitrous oxide, organic waste, manure, and flaring work cannot wait for bespoke mobilisation after every alert or seasonal peak.
The shelf should contain authority, crews, budget, protocols, enforcement routes, farm-service support, waste contracts, refrigerant recovery channels, and reporting rules.

House rule: **measurement without a ready response shelf is only alarm.**

### 9. Keep the shelf honest about bottlenecks
Every shelf item should name its live bottleneck:
- money
- grid connection
- transformer or cable
- workforce
- permit
- land or right-of-way
- offtaker payment risk
- community agreement
- standard contract
- environmental review
- data or measurement
- supplier capacity
- political authority

That tag lets the programme distinguish a project that needs finance from one that needs a transformer, a consent process, a procurement template, a tariff reform, or a workforce ladder.

House rule: **a bottleneck not named will be misfunded.**

### 10. Publish enough to prevent shelf capture
Prepared shelves can be captured.
Powerful actors can fill the shelf with their projects, then claim readiness as merit.
So the shelf should disclose enough to show public value, displacement, exposure reduction, fairness, safeguards, and why lower-power communities, public agencies, small firms, and EMDE institutions are not being crowded out by actors who can pay consultants faster.

House rule: **readiness should not become a rich-actor privilege.**

## Project-shelf ledger in one view

| Shelf question | Bad answer | Better answer | First proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| What is ready? | a list of favoured projects | status-tagged packets with owners and bottlenecks | window-ready files exist before funding opens |
| What is the unit? | bespoke project | replicable archetype | second copy moves faster |
| What does finance solve? | generic gap | named risk or cost-of-capital barrier | financial close or affordability improves |
| What is protected? | vague resilience | prewritten protection and recovery packets | safer rebuild begins faster after shock |
| What is measured? | spending and press releases | service, emissions, exposure, retirement, burden | correction triggers fire when metrics fail |
| Who benefits? | strongest applicant | public-value ranking with equity checks | weaker actors can use the shelf too |

## What this note demotes

This note demotes:
- climate-finance rhetoric without prepared pipelines
- project lists with no permit, procurement, finance, or owner file
- bespoke hero projects where repeatable archetypes are available
- disaster recovery that starts designing safer rebuilding only after the disaster
- methane measurement without repair authority and crew response
- readiness claims that hide who was resourced to become ready
- guarantees or concessional finance allocated without a named risk problem
- project-preparation work treated as overhead rather than climate infrastructure

## Preferred use

Open this note first when the live question is any of the following:
- why available climate finance is not turning into enough physical deployment
- how to prepare high-value projects before funding windows, grid capacity, or recovery money opens
- what should sit between a work queue and financial close
- how to make adaptation, methane, retrofit, grid, and clean-power priorities absorbable
- how to stop scarce capacity from going to the easiest project rather than the best prepared public-value project

Then reopen:
- `242` for the whole work queue
- `244` for the owner-led packet ledger
- `245` for scarce-bottleneck allocation
- `248` for procurement platforms and repeatable service contracts
- `227` for cost of capital, guarantees, and local-currency finance

- `200`–`203` for the one-to-many progress loop

Rev0247 adds one more shelf test: a ready project must not only be bankable and permit-routed; it must also be claims-clean, value-channel-clean, and remedy-ready. The shelf should know which claims the project is allowed to make, what proof supports them, who owns the value channel, what conflicts or ownership risks exist, what price benchmark applies, and what milestone evidence releases payment or priority [S275][S276][S280][S281][S282][S283][S284].

Rev0248 adds one more shelf test: a ready project should not only know what it will build, claim, and cost; it should also know what happens if it fails. The shelf should include cure periods, milestone holdbacks, support routes, user-protection floors, grievance paths, clawbacks, reprocurement rights, compensation routes, and closure evidence before finance, capacity, or crisis windows open [S285][S286][S287][S288][S290][S291][S292].

House rule: **a project that is ready to absorb money but not ready to withstand claims and integrity audit is not truly ready.**

### Rev0251 bridge: shelf-ready now includes supply-chain and market-access readiness

A shelf item is not truly window-ready if it assumes its traded inputs, spares, customs code, standard, certificate, testing route, or warranty channel will solve itself after money appears. Rev0251 therefore adds two shelf fields: cross-border dependency status and market-access interoperability status. Project shelves should be able to sort not only by permit and finance readiness, but by component lead time, supplier concentration, route risk, critical spares, conformity-assessment path, and quality-infrastructure gap [S311][S312][S313][S314][S315][S316][S317].

House rule: **a shelf item that cannot travel through the supply chain is not shelf-ready; it is only locally imagined.**

## Compression rule

**Do not wait for money, grid capacity, disaster recovery, or political attention to appear before deciding what should be built, repaired, financed, procured, protected, retired, or copied. Maintain a ready project shelf: owner-led, financeable, permit-routed, procurement-ready, socially governed, bottleneck-tagged, measurement-ready, and built around repeatable archetypes.**

## Rev0289 project-shelf amendment: include a nuclear shelf

The ready-project shelf now explicitly includes nuclear options: life extension, uprates, restarts, repeat-build reactor packages, SMR demonstration sites, industrial-heat pairings, desalination pairings, and high-load clean-power campuses. A nuclear shelf is not a wish list; it must include licensing posture, siting screens, fuel assumptions, workforce depth, financing structures, host-community engagement, procurement traceability, and ratepayer/taxpayer risk allocation [S771][S776][S778][S780][S784].


## Rev0294 nuclear integrated-energy propagation note

Rev0294 extends the nuclear-positive preference into cogeneration, district heating, process heat, desalination, hydrogen and clean molecules, data-center/AI infrastructure, critical loads, and co-product public-value scorecards. These additions are explicitly bounded by new gates NG_53–NG_66 and by sector-coupling exception rules; preference is not treated as maturity without local evidence.


## Rev0298 nuclear human-capital refactor note

The cube's pro-nuclear orientation is now explicitly conditioned by workforce depth, operator licensing, regulator capacity, safety/quality culture, knowledge retention, craft labor, and public training pathways. Nuclear remains favored for clean firm power and system benefit, but human-capital and institutional-capacity gaps are binding maturity caps rather than implementation footnotes. Sources: [S864], [S868], [S871], [S873], [S875].
