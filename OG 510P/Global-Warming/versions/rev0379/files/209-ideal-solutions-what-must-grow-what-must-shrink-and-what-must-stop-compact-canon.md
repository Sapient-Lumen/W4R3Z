# 209 — Ideal Solutions: What Must Grow, What Must Shrink, and What Must Stop Compact Canon

## Claim

The archive already has a shortest whole answer (`195`), a physical-jobs note (`208`), a working-scorelines note (`207`), a physical-portfolio note (`130`), a first-moves note (`193`), and a non-substitutions note (`196`).
That is enough doctrine.
But the front door still lacked one tiny asymmetry note:

**what, in plain terms, must increase, what must decrease, and what must cease if a climate program is actually serious?**

That gap matters because climate talk often becomes mushy at exactly this point.
It says “accelerate clean energy” without naming what must contract.
Or it says “phase out fossil fuels” without naming what must be built fast enough to replace them.
This note is therefore a compression note, not a new doctrinal branch.
Its job is to state the archive’s shortest grow / shrink / stop rule in one place [S1][S3][S4][S6][S8][S11][S12][S14][S16][S17][S18][S19][S20][S24].

## Fast rule

**A serious climate program must grow clean backbones, protection capacity, and delivery capability; shrink fossil combustion, methane, waste, and exposure; and stop new long-lived lock-in plus fake substitutes that let dirty systems stay institutionally safe.**

## What must grow

### 1. Clean service backbones must grow
Grow abundant clean electricity, grids, storage, flexibility, efficient buildings, cooling access, public and shared mobility where it fits, and electrified end uses that can already run well on cleaner power.
This is the growth that lets many later cuts happen at once rather than one sector at a time [S4][S5][S14][S16][S17][S20][S24].

House rule: **grow the cleaner service system, not just a pile of cleaner components.**

### 2. Protection capacity must grow
Grow heat-health systems, cooling access, flood and drought protection, early warning, resilient water and care systems, and honest retreat capacity where repeated defence is no longer real protection.
A serious climate program is not only about future emissions; it must also enlarge the systems that keep people alive and functioning in a hotter world [S3][S12][S15][S16][S17].

### 3. Delivery and proof capacity must grow
Grow permitting speed without surrendering safeguards, grid interconnection capacity, skilled labor, standard offers, public and regulated delivery backbones, maintenance, repair, and measurement systems strong enough to prove physical gains.
Without this growth, even good technologies stay trapped in queues, pilot logic, or paper success [S6][S11][S13][S14][S20].

## What must shrink

### 4. Fossil dependence must shrink
Shrink coal, oil, and gas combustion in the services where better substitutes are already available or can be made available on realistic programme timescales.
Do not let clean addition count as the whole story while the fossil system remains politically protected and physically entrenched [S1][S4][S9][S20][S24].

### 5. Methane, waste, and overhead must shrink fast
Shrink venting, flaring, fugitive methane, wasteful cooling, food loss and waste, nitrogen losses, and other high-overhead warming lanes that can move faster than full asset turnover.
These are not side quests.
They are some of the fastest available ways to slow warming and reduce pressure on the rest of the system [S8][S11][S16][S17][S18][S19].

### 6. Human exposure must shrink
Shrink the number of people, homes, settlements, and lifelines left highly exposed to heat, flood, drought, smoke, coastal loss, and infrastructure failure.
A climate program that cuts emissions but leaves exposure rising too freely is still failing one of its core physical jobs [S3][S12][S15][S16].

## What must stop

### 7. New lock-in must stop
Stop approving, subsidizing, financing, or normalizing new long-lived dirty assets, settlement patterns, and capital cycles that make later retirement harder than it already is.
The climate problem is not only ongoing emissions.
It is also the repeated creation of tomorrow’s harder-to-remove dependence [S1][S4][S9][S20][S24].

### 8. False substitution must stop
Stop treating removals as substitutes for fast gross cuts, adaptation as a substitute for mitigation, or money, targets, and announcements as substitutes for delivery and proof.
These can matter, but they do not excuse weak displacement in the live physical system [S1][S10][S12][S20].

## Preferred use

Open this note first when the live question is any of the following:
- what must actually expand versus contract in a serious climate program
- why the climate answer is not only “build more clean stuff”
- how to say the climate package in a more directional way than the one-page answer
- what the archive thinks must be actively stopped, not merely outcompeted someday
- how growth, decline, and prohibition fit together before reopening deeper notes

Then reopen `208`, `207`, `130`, `193`, `194`, or `196` depending on whether the surviving question is mainly about the physical jobs, the working dashboard, the physical portfolio, first-move order, the world bargain, or the non-substitution rule.

## Compression rule

**Grow clean backbones, protection, and delivery capacity. Shrink fossil dependence, methane, waste, and exposure. Stop new lock-in and fake substitutes that let dirty systems survive behind cleaner rhetoric.**
