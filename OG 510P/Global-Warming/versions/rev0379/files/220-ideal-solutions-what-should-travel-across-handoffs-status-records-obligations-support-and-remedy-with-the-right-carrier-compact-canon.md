# 220 — Ideal Solutions: What Should Travel Across Handoffs — Status, Records, Obligations, Support, and Remedy with the Right Carrier Compact Canon

## Claim

`219` adds the shortest persistence rule after entry: once a cleaner and safer state has been reached, ordinary life events should not quietly reset it to a dirtier fallback [S75][S78][S82][S118][S119][S120][S121][S143][S144][S161][S162][S164].

One more front-door bridge belongs immediately beside that rule:

**to preserve the clean state, the programme has to decide what actually travels across the handoff, and with which carrier.**

In plain terms, persistence does not happen by wishing the gain to endure.
It happens because the right packet survives the change:
- the relevant **status**,
- the needed **records**,
- the continuing **obligations**,
- the continuing **support**,
- the active **remedy path** if something goes wrong,
- and the minimum **data spine / passport link** needed to find, update, audit, transfer, or correct the packet.

So the archive’s shortest carrier-packet rule is:

**when a clean gain should survive a handoff, move the minimum continuity packet with the right carrier — person, household, asset, building, meter, account, route, site, project, product, claim, or service relationship — instead of forcing rediscovery and re-proof from zero.** [S72][S73][S74][S75][S78][S79][S80][S81][S82][S118][S119][S120][S121][S143][S144][S161][S162][S164]

## Why this belongs in the front door

### 1. “Do not reset” is not yet a design rule unless the packet is named

A system can agree in principle that the clean state should survive a move, rebuild, ownership change, or early failure.
Then the handoff happens and the gain still disappears because nobody specified what had to travel.
The file does not move.
The entitlement does not persist.
The contractor cannot see the prior status.
The replacement defaults to the dirty familiar baseline.
The new user is asked to prove again what the system already knew.

So a front-door persistence rule needs its smallest implementation companion:

**if a gain should persist, name the packet that must persist.** [S72][S73][S74][S75][S78][S79][S80][S81][S82][S118][S119][S120][S121]

### 2. The right carrier is not always the individual person

Some facts belong mainly with the person or household:
- income-linked support,
- enrollment status,
- a prior verification that remains materially valid.

Some facts belong mainly with the asset or place:
- a building passport,
- envelope and wiring upgrades,
- disclosed performance,
- replacement standards,
- resilient-rebuild requirements,
- meter or interconnection status,
- a route or site condition.

And some facts belong mainly with the service relationship or programme record:
- a standard offer already accepted,
- a performance guarantee,
- a remedy clock,
- a maintenance duty,
- a follow-up obligation after early underperformance.

So the archive needs the smallest carrier-choice rule:

**keep each part of the continuity packet with whichever carrier can preserve it most reliably and fairly across ordinary change.** [S69][S70][S71][S72][S73][S74][S75][S78][S79][S80][S81][S82][S118][S119][S120][S121][S143][S144][S161][S162][S164]

### 3. Persistence failures are often packet failures, not belief failures

The archive already warns that many dirty resets are mundane rather than ideological.
This note tightens that diagnosis.
A great many regressions happen because the packet is broken apart:
- status lives in one office,
- records in another,
- obligation with no visible owner,
- support tied to a vanishing application,
- remedy available only if the user can reassemble the case.

That means the programme fails not mainly because people stopped caring, but because the continuity packet was not designed to survive the handoff [S72][S74][S75][S79][S80][S81][S82][S118][S119][S120][S121][S143][S144].

## The minimum continuity packet

When the archive says a clean gain should survive ordinary change, the front door should now assume some version of this packet must travel:

1. **status** — what cleaner or safer state has already been reached
2. **records** — the minimum evidence or operating facts needed not to rediscover the case
3. **obligations** — what standard, service duty, maintenance duty, or replacement rule still binds
4. **support** — what qualifying help, tariff, financing shape, or inclusion status should continue
5. **remedy** — what correction path remains live if the gain underperforms or breaks early

If none of these travel, the programme is often only pretending the gain is durable [S70][S71][S72][S73][S74][S75][S78][S79][S80][S81][S82][S118][S119][S120][S121][S143][S144].

## What serious design looks like in practice

- choose the right carrier for each part of the continuity packet instead of dumping everything onto the current occupant
- ask at handoff what changed, not what can be painfully rediscovered from zero
- let accepted offers, valid records, and routine eligibility persist unless material facts changed
- make replacement, rebuild, retender, and provider-switch pathways inherit the cleaner baseline and active obligations by default
- keep remedy live through the handoff so early-failure correction does not die when the account, owner, or contractor changes [S72][S74][S75][S78][S79][S80][S81][S82][S118][S119][S120][S121][S143][S144][S161][S162][S164]

## Compression rule

**A serious climate programme does not only say “do not reset to the dirty default.” It decides what must travel across the handoff — status, records, obligations, support, remedy, and the data-spine or passport link — and keeps each part with the carrier most likely to preserve the clean gain fairly and without re-proof from scratch.**
