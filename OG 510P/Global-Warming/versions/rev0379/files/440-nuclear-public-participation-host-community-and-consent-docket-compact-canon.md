# Nuclear public-participation, host-community, and consent docket

A pro-nuclear cube cannot win legitimacy by burying consent, language access and public challenge. Host communities, affected ratepayers, workers, Tribal governments where applicable, downstream water users and emergency-planning populations must be able to see and contest the basis for a nuclear decision.

NRC describes public participation as part of licensing decisionmaking [S794]. DOE's consent-based siting process for consolidated interim spent-fuel storage makes consent an operational design problem, not a slogan [S796]. The IAEA Joint Convention frames spent fuel and radioactive waste safety as an internationally recognized peer-review obligation [S797]. Title VI and LEP guidance remain relevant when federally supported emergency or public-service processes are involved [S768][S769].

## Rule

Nuclear maturity cannot rise above template status without a public-participation docket that records notice, language access, hearing/comment paths, community questions, agency answers, unresolved objections, redress routes and benefit-covenant enforceability.

## Consent test

For waste, interim storage, recycling or long-lived site obligations, consent means a documented governance process with scope, decision rights, withdrawal or renegotiation rules, benefit obligations, monitoring duties and dispute resolution.
