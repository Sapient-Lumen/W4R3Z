# 157 — Ideal Solutions: Objective Function Compact Canon

## Claim

The archive already has joined notes for:
- the one-page answer (`58`)
- the design spec (`97`)
- the design-kernel canon (`128`)
- the physical-portfolio canon (`130`)
- the political-economy / social-contract canon (`131`)
- and the funding-and-burden-allocation canon (`156`)

What it still lacked was one joined first-stop note for a question that keeps appearing whenever two plausible climate packages compete:

**what, exactly, is the archive trying to maximize and minimize when it says a solution is ideal?**

This note is not a new branch.
It is a compression note.
Its job is to gather the archive’s scoring rule into one place so later trade-offs can be judged against the same objective function rather than against whatever metric is loudest in the moment.

## Fast rule

**A serious climate programme should maximize fast verified gross-emissions decline, overshoot minimization, exposure reduction, clean-service gain, delivery speed, copyability, and political / fiscal durability; and minimize future repair burden, dirty lock-in, regressivity, user burden, material and energy overhead, dependence on scarce clean fuels for easy uses, and reliance on paper claims or later mass removals** [S98][S99][S100][S101][S103][S104][S106][S141][S143][S168][S169][S170][S171].

## The compact canon

### 1. Minimize cumulative damage and overshoot, not only hit a distant label
The archive does not treat the problem as “arrive at net zero someday and let the path take care of itself.”
It treats the path itself as damage-bearing.
So the first objective is to reduce cumulative forcing fast, cut methane and other short-lived forcing where possible, and limit overshoot rather than assuming later cleanup can cheaply erase delayed action [S100][S101][S105][S133].

House rule: **earlier physical decline beats elegant late neutrality.**

### 2. Maximize clean service, not energy or material throughput
The archive’s welfare objective is comfort, mobility, cooling safety, nutrition, shelter, reliability, and health — not maximum fuel burn, maximum floor area, maximum vehicle mass, or maximum material throughput.
Ideal solutions therefore score well when they deliver the same or better human service with less recurring energy, less extraction, and less system bulk [S98][S99][S104][S106][S168][S169][S170][S171].

House rule: **score the service delivered, not the volume pushed through the machine.**

### 3. Minimize future repair burden and dirty lock-in
A solution is better when it avoids building assets, settlement patterns, contracts, or subsidy regimes that later generations must painfully unwind.
That means ideality includes avoiding fossil lock-in, reducing the eventual need for adaptation and removal, and using present capital cycles to retire inferior paths instead of extending them [S100][S101][S103][S157][S161][S162][S164].

House rule: **do not solve today by enlarging tomorrow’s cleanup bill.**

### 4. Prefer dependency-light clean paths for easy uses and reserve scarce chains for hard uses
The archive does not optimize for technological grandeur.
It prefers solutions that solve large shares of the problem with few missing prerequisites, clear signals, and mature supply chains.
That is why direct electrification, efficiency, passive design, and simpler standards-led delivery usually outrank dependency-heavy chains in easier uses, while scarce clean fuels and more fragile infrastructures are reserved for genuinely hard cases [S100][S103][S106][S165][S166][S167][S168].

House rule: **use the simple, ready, low-overhead path first; save scarce molecules and fragile chains for the places that really need them.**

### 5. Maximize real-world deliverability, repeatability, and compounding
The archive treats speed as institutional, not only technological.
So ideal solutions score higher when they can move through real grids, permitting systems, procurement routines, workforce pipelines, standard offers, and repeatable local delivery rather than depending on one-off heroics.
They also score higher when each deployment teaches the next one, lowers cost, standardizes practice, and becomes easier to copy across jurisdictions [S103][S106][S151][S152][S153][S154][S155][S156].

House rule: **prefer solutions that get easier, cheaper, and more normal each time they are repeated.**

### 6. Maximize fairness and durability together
The archive does not treat fairness as decoration after the “real” optimization is done.
A package that cuts emissions on paper but erodes affordability, pushes burden onto cash-poor households, or forces poorer countries to finance transition through expensive capital is not ideal because it will be slower, weaker, and less durable in practice.
Ideal solutions therefore protect service floors, make gains visible, recycle broad burdens progressively, and build legitimacy strong enough to survive conflict, budget pressure, and electoral change [S13][S141][S142][S143][S145][S147][S148].

House rule: **durable climate speed comes from visible fairness, not from asking the weakest actors to carry hidden transition load.**

### 7. Minimize user burden, proof burden, and paper progress
The archive distrusts solutions that work only if millions of people become expert shoppers, claims processors, or compliance managers.
It also distrusts claimed success that exists mostly in certificates, loose accounting, or unverified promises.
So ideal solutions should be easy to take up, easy to verify, measured in gross physical outcomes first, and corrigible when delivery underperforms [S11][S68][S118][S119][S123][S133][S135][S136].

House rule: **make the clean path easier than the dirty path, and make proof harder than promise.**

### 8. Preserve option value while building useful platform assets
The archive values solutions that remain useful across several plausible decarbonized futures: stronger grids, efficient buildings, compact and safer urban form, public transit, better data and standards, ecosystem restoration, and resilient public systems.
These are better than brittle bets whose value collapses if one narrow technology story disappoints [S103][S157][S158][S159][S160][S172][S173].

House rule: **prefer platform assets and repurposable gains over brittle one-path wagers.**

## Tie-breaker rule

When two proposals both look climate-positive, the archive should usually prefer the one that:
- cuts gross emissions sooner,
- reduces overshoot and exposure more,
- needs less scarce clean energy or material overhead,
- travels through existing institutions with less friction,
- protects lower-income and higher-risk people better,
- is easier to verify,
- and leaves behind more useful public backbone for the next round [S100][S101][S103][S106][S141][S143][S168][S169][S170][S171].

## Preferred use

Open this note first when the live question is any of the following:
- what ideal solutions are really trying to optimize
- what should be maximized and minimized at the same time
- how to compare two plausible climate packages when both look partly right
- how to think about trade-offs between speed, fairness, physicality, and durability
- what the archive’s scoring rule really is beneath the file tree

Then reopen the deeper note that matches the surviving disagreement.

## Compression rule

**An ideal climate solution is one that cuts real emissions and exposure fast, limits overshoot, delivers good lives with lower throughput, avoids new lock-in, travels through real institutions, stays fair enough to endure, proves itself physically, and leaves behind infrastructure, rules, and habits that make the next round easier rather than harder.**
