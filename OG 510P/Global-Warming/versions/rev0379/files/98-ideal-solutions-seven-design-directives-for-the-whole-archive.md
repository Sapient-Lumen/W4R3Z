# 98 — Ideal Solutions: Seven Design Directives for the Whole Archive

## Claim

The archive already has:
- a one-page answer (`58`)
- a compact package note (`57`)
- a compact canon of screens (`88`)
- an acceptance-tests note (`97`)
- an operator layer (`91` and `93`)

What it still lacked was the shortest **positive design grammar** for the question:

**What should ideal climate solutions usually look like when we are designing them, not merely screening them?**

That matters because readers often need something even simpler than a package note and more generative than a pass/fail test.
They need a very short set of directions that can shape a proposal before it reaches the screening layer.

This note is a **compression note**, not a new doctrinal branch.
Its job is to reduce the whole archive to seven reusable design directives.

## Fast rule

**Ideal solutions to global warming should usually be portfolio-first, substitute-first, defaults-first, service-first, scarce-tools-last, protection-now, and built for delivery-and-truth from the beginning.**

## Seven design directives

### 1. Portfolio first, not silver-bullet first
Design the solution as a package of mutually reinforcing moves, not as one privileged technology, one elegant policy instrument, or one future cleanup story.

What this usually means:
- clean electricity and grids as a backbone
- efficiency and demand shaping as load reducers
- electrification for ordinary end uses
- methane abatement as a fast parallel lane
- ecosystem protection and agrifood reform as core climate work
- adaptation and exposure reduction as co-equal protection work [S100][S101][S102][S104][S105][S106]

Why it matters:
- the climate problem is made of several coupled physical systems, so ideal solutions should usually arrive as portfolios rather than single levers

### 2. Substitute and retire, not add and wait
Design the package to build the cleaner system **and** deliberately shrink dependence on the dirtier one.
Do not confuse clean addition with climate success if fossil lock-in, dirty capital stock, or high-exposure patterns remain intact.

What this usually means:
- build replacement capacity, infrastructure, and institutions
- retire or constrain incumbent dirty assets on purpose
- avoid new long-lived lock-in that makes later cleanup harder [S100][S101][S102][S103][S112]

Why it matters:
- ideal solutions change system direction; they do not merely decorate the old system with cleaner growth at the margins

### 3. Stop waste, electrify the ordinary, reserve scarce tools for the hard residuals
Design around the lowest-overhead good-enough substitute first.
Use efficiency, passive performance, direct electrification, compact form, repairability, and material thrift wherever they work.
Hold scarce clean fuels, biomass, carbon capture, and removals back for cases that really are hard.

What this usually means:
- direct electrification for many buildings, vehicles, and industrial heat uses
- efficient appliances, envelopes, and cooling as first moves rather than optional extras
- scarce molecules and carbon management concentrated in the small residual zone, not spread across easy uses [S100][S103][S104][S106][S111][S112][S146]

Why it matters:
- ideal solutions should solve the problem with the smallest plausible recurring claim on land, water, materials, biomass, and clean electricity

### 4. Change defaults and concentrated control points before asking for heroics
Design the clean path so it is ordinary, administratively simple, and pushed through the actors who actually control systems.
Do not build the main strategy around millions of repeated acts of highly informed voluntary restraint.

What this usually means:
- standards, codes, procurement, grid rules, product rules, and public or regulated backbones
- one clear front door where possible, low paperwork, standard offers, and coordinated delivery
- obligations concentrated on utilities, firms, landlords, manufacturers, public buyers, developers, and other control points rather than mainly on end users [S112][S118][S119][S143][S146][S147]

Why it matters:
- ideal solutions should make the cleaner path normal by design, not admirable only under unusually motivated behaviour

### 5. Raise service floors, cut affluent waste, and protect people now
Design climate action to improve human welfare while reducing throughput.
The package should expand basic access where deprivation is real, lower bills and exposure where households are constrained, and apply stronger restraint first to affluent waste and luxury emissions.

What this usually means:
- cooling, heat, mobility, food, water, and safety treated as service floors
- high-footprint excess treated as a legitimate target for demand restraint
- adaptation treated as civil protection, not as an admission of mitigation defeat [S104][S105][S106][S117][S143]

Why it matters:
- climate packages that deepen deprivation or tolerate avoidable exposure are not ideal solutions and rarely remain politically durable

### 6. Build delivery capacity, not only targets and promises
Design the package around the real bottlenecks: grids, queues, permitting, procurement, finance, workforce, maintenance, and local administrative capability.
Treat delivery capacity itself as part of the solution.

What this usually means:
- grid expansion, interconnection, and queue reform
- concessional finance, guarantees, and lower cost of capital where needed
- workforce pipelines, repeatable procurement, and maintenance discipline
- place-sensitive implementation that can scale without depending on rare institutional excellence [S102][S103][S112][S117][S118][S147]

Why it matters:
- an ideal solution must clear the buildability test in the real world, not only on paper

### 7. Design for truth, correction, and durability from the start
Design the package so it can measure real outcomes, correct underperformance, defend legitimacy, and survive money and politics.
Do not bolt accountability on at the end.

What this usually means:
- physical-outcome scoreboards rather than slogan metrics
- simple verification where possible and stricter checks where necessary
- visible sharing of gains, narrow carve-outs, clear exits for temporary dirty bridges, and institutions that survive electoral turnover [S103][S111][S112][S143][S147]

Why it matters:
- ideal solutions should still work after hype fades, budgets tighten, officials rotate, and reality starts grading performance

## What this note adds

This note does **not** replace the answer note (`58`) or the design-spec note (`97`).
It sits between them.

- `58` answers: **what is the ideal solution?**
- `97` asks: **what tests must a serious package pass?**
- `98` says: **when shaping a package, what general design directives should guide it before deeper screening?**

That is a distinct navigation and compression gain.
