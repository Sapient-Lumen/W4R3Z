# Noun promotion rubric

Purpose: keep Ward from promoting attractive vocabulary into durable archive truth before the cube has earned it.

This is intentionally smaller than SlopOS’s RFC→ADR machinery.
Ward is still embryonic, so the right question is not “can we name this?” but “has this noun survived enough pressure that future revisions should rely on it?”

## Promote a noun only when all of these hold
1. **Outside pressure exists**
   - at least one load-bearing external source clearly pressures the noun, or the noun cleanly summarizes a stable cross-source pattern.

2. **Sibling/neighbor pressure exists**
   - at least one companion cube or nearby architecture surface benefits from the distinction.

3. **Specimen pressure exists**
   - at least one concrete specimen flow gets clearer because the noun exists.

4. **The noun is smaller than consequence**
   - it does not quietly swallow local policy, spend consequence, or a whole neighboring cube.

5. **The noun still has an exit ramp**
   - if later evidence shows the noun was too broad, too implementation-shaped, or better owned elsewhere, the archive can demote or relocate it without historical amnesia.

## Anti-patterns
- promoting a transport, list shape, log shape, or counter algorithm as if it were the meaning;
- promoting absence-shaped logic without naming the closure point that makes it safe enough to use;
- promoting one support-path explanation as if it proved there are no alternate derivations;
- promoting a noun just because it sounds elegant in one revision summary.
