# Court fines, fees, filing costs, and nonpayment sanctions should not be turned into revenue traps or debtors-prison rents

## Claim

Fines, fees, court costs, filing charges, surcharges, collection add-ons, warrants, and license holds are coercive fiscal instruments. They may not fund core courts or municipalities by extracting from people who lack ability to pay, and nonpayment may not become punishment without a willfulness finding.[S515][S516][S517]

## Why it matters

A legal system that prices the right to contest, file, appeal, or comply converts remedy into revenue. The burden falls hardest on low-income defendants, litigants, families, and communities already least able to purchase review.

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/court-fines-fees-ability-to-pay-and-civil-access-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/court-fines-fees-ability-to-pay-and-civil-access-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/court-fines-fees-ability-to-pay-and-nonpayment-sanction-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/court-fines-fees-ability-to-pay-and-nonpayment-sanction-ladder.md). The compact rule is: set sanction by culpability and capacity, preserve no-pay remedy access, and uncouple justice budgets from extracted obligations.[S538]

## Anti-patterns

- **revenue trap** — fines and fees fund ordinary public operations.
- **debtors-prison rent** — poverty-based nonpayment triggers jail, warrant, or license loss.
- **access toll** — filing, transcript, or appeal costs block review.
- **surcharge cascade** — add-ons exceed the original obligation and floor capacity.
