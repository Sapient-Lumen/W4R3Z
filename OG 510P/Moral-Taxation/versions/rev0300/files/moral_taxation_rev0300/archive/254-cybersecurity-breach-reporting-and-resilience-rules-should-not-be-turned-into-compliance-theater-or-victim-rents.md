# Cybersecurity breach reporting and resilience rules should not be turned into compliance theater or victim rents

## Claim

Cybersecurity rules are fiscal rules when breach costs, ransom payments, public response, identity repair, and infrastructure disruption are shifted across society. The archive charges preventable risk and funds resilience without making victims buy their way back to safety.[S615][S616][S617][S618]

## Why it matters

Cyber incidents are not merely private IT failures when they expose tax data, shut hospitals, disrupt payment rails, or consume public response capacity. Reporting without repair can become theater; repair without accountability can become subsidy.[S615][S616][S617][S618]

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/cybersecurity-breach-resilience-and-incident-cost-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/cybersecurity-breach-resilience-and-incident-cost-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/cybersecurity-breach-resilience-and-incident-cost-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/cybersecurity-breach-resilience-and-incident-cost-ladder.md). The compact rule is: report, repair, and harden before monetizing the incident.

## Anti-patterns

- **checkbox security** — formal certifications hide unrepaired risk.
- **victim rent** — identity restoration or account recovery is sold back to breached persons.
- **silent externality** — breach costs are shifted to households, hospitals, or public agencies.
- **ransom normalization** — payments become a budgeted cost without resilience changes.
