# Migration, remittance-transfer tax, and diaspora-family support should not be turned into border tolls or remittance skims

## Claim

A remittance is often family support crossing borders, not a luxury base. Any tax or required fee on remittance rails must test total transfer cost, floor impact, cash and digital access, provider exit, and recipient-household dependence before treating visibility as taxable capacity.[S505][S506]

## Why it matters

The person visible to the provider may be a low-wage migrant worker, and the real incidence may fall on a household abroad. Provider fees, FX spread, tax, KYC friction, delay, and de-risking can combine into one survival toll.

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/migration-remittance-transfer-tax-and-diaspora-family-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/migration-remittance-transfer-tax-and-diaspora-family-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/remittance-transfer-tax-migration-fee-and-diaspora-family-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/remittance-transfer-tax-migration-fee-and-diaspora-family-ladder.md). The compact rule is: reduce private transfer costs before adding public ones; preserve access; exempt or rebate floor-sensitive transfers; prevent corridor closure.

## Anti-patterns

- **remittance skim** — public or private charges extract from household-support flows without repair.
- **border toll** — migration status or diaspora family support becomes a fiscal target merely because it is visible.
- **cash-channel exclusion** — digital-only compliance blocks unbanked senders or recipients.
- **de-risking cascade** — compliance uncertainty closes corridors and raises total cost.
