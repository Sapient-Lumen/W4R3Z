# Release integrity source bijection and regression harness rules should not be turned into citation theater or prose sprawl

## Claim

A living archive needs release controls as much as doctrine. Each revision should prove that sources are bijective, machine surfaces agree, golden cases still route, stale revision openings are caught, and new files add a genuine axis, not another version of the same no-rent sentence.[S594][S645][S646]

## Why it matters

Classification seams are where public systems often leak moral clarity: an insurance surcharge looks actuarial, a wager looks like finance, a telecom contribution looks like a small bill line, a digital-ad tax looks like rent capture, a portable benefit looks like worker protection, an environmental payment looks like mitigation, or a release checklist looks like bureaucracy. The archive should ask who controls the burden, who bears it, and what floor or public claim is at stake before accepting the label.[S594][S645][S646]

## Anti-patterns

- **citation theater** — many citations exist but are not load-bearing, current, or bijective.
- **source surface drift** — source JSON, source markdown, citations, or currentness records disagree.
- **stale release opening** — public entry points still announce an older revision as current.
- **prose sprawl** — new prose repeats old rules without a distinct route, axis, parameter, or case.
- **unchecked case regression** — expected answers change without a machine-readable golden case failing.
