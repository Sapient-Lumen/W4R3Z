# Health taxes on tobacco, alcohol, sugary drinks, and addictive products should not be turned into poverty tolls or harm-license traps

## Claim

Health taxes can be morally justified when they reduce harmful consumption, price external health costs, weaken addiction rents, and fund health repair. They fail when addicted or low-income consumers become the revenue base while producers keep designing, targeting, and profiting from harm.[S480][S481][S482]

## Why it matters

A tax on a harmful product can be both effective and regressive. The archive does not treat regressivity as an automatic veto, but it requires proceeds, design, and non-tax supports to repair the protected floor rather than exploit vulnerability.

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/health-addiction-and-harmful-consumption-tax-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/health-addiction-and-harmful-consumption-tax-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/health-tax-addiction-targeting-and-floor-protection-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/health-tax-addiction-targeting-and-floor-protection-ladder.md). The compact rule is: tax the harmful product and industry rent, not the person; pair rates with treatment, prevention, healthy substitutes, floor repair, and marketing/product controls.

## Anti-patterns

- **poverty toll** — low-income addicted users fund general revenue without repair.
- **harm license** — taxes replace product standards, marketing limits, or age rules.
- **industry-captured regressivity** — floor arguments are used to preserve harmful rents.
- **rate decay** — no indexation lets affordability recover and health effects fade.
