# Corporate tax transparency, country-by-country, and public accountability rules should not be turned into publicity theater or confidentiality shields

## Claim

Corporate tax transparency is a civic accountability instrument, not a shortcut to liability. Public reporting should make profit-location, activity, and tax-payment claims legible while preserving audit process, proportional thresholds, and bounded confidentiality for genuine risks.[S576][S577][S578]

## Why it matters

Large groups can separate legal tax residence, paper profit, employees, users, assets, and public benefits. Public CbCR and tax-governance disclosure make that separation visible. But transparency becomes theater if it is too aggregated, and becomes abuse if it replaces evidence, appeal, or confidentiality boundaries.[S576][S577][S578]

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/corporate-tax-transparency-country-by-country-and-public-accountability-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/corporate-tax-transparency-country-by-country-and-public-accountability-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/corporate-tax-transparency-public-cbcr-and-confidentiality-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/corporate-tax-transparency-public-cbcr-and-confidentiality-ladder.md). The compact rule is: do not accept fiscal power that hides authority, burden, beneficiary, proof limits, floor risk, or public repair behind a more convenient label.

## Anti-patterns

- **publicity theater** — disclosure format looks transparent while hiding the substance.
- **confidentiality shield** — secrecy protects aggressive tax planning rather than legitimate privacy or trade secrets.
- **risk-signal finality** — CbCR mismatch becomes liability without audit.
- **small-entity transparency mill** — rules written for MNEs spill onto small firms.
