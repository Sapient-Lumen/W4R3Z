# Fiscal transparency, budget, debt, and tax-expenditure rules should not be turned into fiscal illusion or off-book burden shifting

## Claim

A moral tax system must show its whole fiscal body. Revenue, spending, tax expenditures, debt, guarantees, contingent liabilities, resource funds, and off-budget vehicles should be legible together before distribution or sustainability claims are credited.[S565][S566][S567]

## Why it matters

Accounting labels change political salience. A tax cut can be a subsidy, a guarantee can be delayed spending, debt can shift burden across cohorts, and a public fund can hide asset use. The archive treats opacity as an incidence problem because hidden fiscal instruments still land somewhere.[S568][S570][S71]

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/fiscal-transparency-budget-debt-and-tax-expenditure-legibility-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/fiscal-transparency-budget-debt-and-tax-expenditure-legibility-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/fiscal-transparency-budget-risk-debt-and-tax-expenditure-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/fiscal-transparency-budget-risk-debt-and-tax-expenditure-ladder.md). The compact rule is: do not accept fiscal power that hides authority, burden, beneficiary, proof limits, floor risk, or public repair behind a more convenient label.

## Anti-patterns

- **fiscal illusion** — the burden disappears because it is off-budget, deferred, or labeled a tax preference.
- **off-book burden shift** — guarantees, PPPs, or public entities carry fiscal risk without budget visibility.
- **debt camouflage** — current groups consume benefits while future taxpayers absorb interest and rollover risk.
- **tax-expenditure exceptionalism** — a subsidy escapes scrutiny because it is written into the tax code.
