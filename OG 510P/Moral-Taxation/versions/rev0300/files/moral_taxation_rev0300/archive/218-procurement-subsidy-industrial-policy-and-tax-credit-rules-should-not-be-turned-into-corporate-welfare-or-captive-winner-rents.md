# Procurement, subsidy, industrial-policy, and tax-credit rules should not be turned into corporate welfare or captive-winner rents

## Claim

Procurement, grants, guarantees, abatements, and tax credits can build public capability. They become tax defects when public money, public risk, or public purchasing power create private asset value without transparent purpose, competitive access, performance duties, clawbacks, local repair, or public upside.[S512][S513][S514]

## Why it matters

Subsidies are often hidden tax decisions. A jurisdiction that cuts taxes, builds infrastructure, guarantees demand, or buys from one vendor has already allocated public resources. The moral tax question is the net fiscal stack, not just the statutory rate.

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/procurement-subsidy-industrial-policy-and-public-upside-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/procurement-subsidy-industrial-policy-and-public-upside-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/procurement-subsidy-clawback-and-public-upside-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/procurement-subsidy-clawback-and-public-upside-ladder.md). The compact rule is: disclose the net stack, test alternatives, condition support, recover value when promises fail, and give the public upside where public risk created private value.

## Anti-patterns

- **corporate welfare** — subsidy funds ordinary private investment without public return.
- **captive winner rent** — procurement locks the state into a vendor, platform, or standard.
- **subsidy lock-in** — temporary support becomes politically permanent.
- **net-stack concealment** — credits, grants, abatements, cheap land, and procurement are not counted together.
