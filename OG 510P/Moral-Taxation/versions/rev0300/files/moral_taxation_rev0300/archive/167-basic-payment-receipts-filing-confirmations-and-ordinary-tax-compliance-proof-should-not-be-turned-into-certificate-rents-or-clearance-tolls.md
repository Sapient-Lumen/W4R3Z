# 167 — Basic payment receipts, filing confirmations, and ordinary tax-compliance proof should not be turned into certificate rents or clearance tolls

## One-line thesis

When a person, small firm, or future rights-bearing artificial person needs **ordinary proof that it filed, paid, or stands in ordinary tax compliance**, baseline access to receipts, confirmations, and standard good-standing evidence should not depend on per-certificate fees, mandatory paid intermediaries, or owner-rented verification keys.[S19][S20][S21][S26][S27][S39][S42][S43][S44][S65][S68][S134][S135][S136][S137]

## Why this matters

The archive already blocks gateway rents on filing identity, review paywalls on correction, distress rents on time-to-pay, payout skims on refunds, record rents on account history, comprehension tolls on understanding the claim, and remittance tolls on the act of payment itself. But one ordinary-life abuse path remained open: **selling compliant subjects the proof of their own compliance back as a certificate product**.[S21][S26][S27][S39][S42][S65][S68][S133][S134][S135][S136][S137]

That matters because receipt, filing-status, and good-standing proof often functions as the civic doorway to procurement, licensing, benefits, migration formalities, finance, grantmaking, or ordinary commercial contracting. If the state or its required intermediaries price that doorway aggressively, tax administration starts operating like a franchise business rather than a public rule system.[S21][S26][S27][S39][S42][S53][S65][S68][S135][S136]

Current public materials show a cleaner baseline. The IRS says electronic payments immediately generate a confirmation number and receipt or confirmation email, IRS Online Account includes payment history and a tax compliance report, Ireland's eTC system lets users view, print, save, and share tax-clearance proof, and SARS's TCS system issues a PIN and printable result letter for online third-party verification.[S65][S68][S134][S135][S136][S137]

## Design rule

Keep at least one **ordinary, visible, no-rent proof lane** open for payment receipts, filing confirmations, and standard tax-compliance status evidence, and treat unusual certification, apostille, rush paper handling, or litigation-grade attestation as separate from the baseline proof path.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Distinguish ordinary self-proof from exceptional certification

A polity may still charge modest cost-based amounts for unusual evidentiary packaging: notarized copies, apostille work, bespoke archival retrieval, rush courier handling, or custom third-party attestation outside the ordinary channel. The archive's claim is narrower and firmer: **standard proof that you filed, paid, or presently stand in ordinary compliance should not itself be the revenue line**.[S27][S39][S42][S53][S65][S68][S134][S135][S136]

### 2. Compliance proof is part of usable administration, not a luxury add-on

If the administration already knows whether a return was filed, a payment posted, a balance remains open, or a standard compliance condition is presently met, ordinary proof should travel through the same baseline service stack as notices, balances, and payment history. Selling back that status through per-document fees or mandatory agent subscriptions recreates a filing toll one step later in the pipeline.[S27][S39][S42][S65][S68][S134][S135][S136][S137]

### 3. Third-party verification should be narrow, taxpayer-controlled, and non-extractive

Good-standing proof often has to travel outward to a bank, grantmaker, public buyer, or regulator. The archive therefore prefers **narrow taxpayer-controlled sharing** — codes, PINs, standard downloadable letters, or similarly bounded proofs — over paper bottlenecks, paid broker lanes, or full-account disclosures. The point is to verify the needed status, not to monetize or overshare the whole tax file.[S39][S42][S65][S68][S89][S135][S136]

### 4. Small firms and ordinary people are where certificate rents bite hardest

A large firm may be able to absorb repeated document charges or specialist handling. A small firm bidding for a contract, a worker proving withholding, or a low-income household showing tax good standing for a benefit or subsidy may not. Certificate rents are therefore especially suspect when they sit on routine life transitions rather than on unusual evidentiary requests.[S21][S26][S27][S31][S39][S42][S65][S68][S135][S136]

### 5. Future artificial persons should inherit person-side proof access, not owner-rented compliance certificates

If a rights-bearing artificial person ever becomes real, the same rule applies on the person side. The subject should not need a former controller's paid dashboard, attestation key, proprietary export, or owner-rented verification channel just to prove that it filed, paid, or stands in ordinary compliance in its own name. Secure authorization and bounded third-party sharing may still matter; **owner-rented compliance proof does not**.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S89][S134][S135][S136]

## Guardrails

- Do not attach routine receipt, filing-confirmation, or standard compliance-status proof to per-document revenue pricing.
- Do not make a paid intermediary or broker the only realistic way to show ordinary tax good standing.
- Do not convert narrowly needed proof into broad account disclosure merely because third-party verification is requested.
- Do not hide the no-rent proof lane behind slower handling, degraded interfaces, or forced paper friction until the paid lane becomes the practical default.
- Do not let any future rights-bearing artificial person prove ordinary compliance only through a former controller's paid certificate or export key.

## Failure modes

- **certificate rent** — the subject must pay for routine proof that it filed, paid, or stands in ordinary compliance.
- **clearance toll drift** — standard good-standing evidence is nominally available but functionally routed through a paid broker, premium portal, or heavy paper bottleneck.
- **proof overshare** — the system discloses far more tax detail than needed because a simple bounded compliance proof was not kept available.
- **life-event clipping** — small firms or ordinary people repeatedly lose time or money on standard proof requests tied to licenses, contracts, grants, or benefits.
- **owner-rented good-standing key** — a future artificial person can prove ordinary compliance only through a former controller's paid verification channel.

## Practical tests

1. Can an ordinary subject obtain a routine payment receipt, filing confirmation, or standard good-standing proof without paying a special fee?
2. Is the no-rent proof lane realistically usable for common life situations that require it?
3. Does third-party verification reveal only the bounded status needed, rather than the whole tax account?
4. Are any charges limited to unusual certification or rush handling rather than to the ordinary proof path itself?
5. If the subject were a future rights-bearing artificial person, could it prove its own compliance without renting back a former controller's export or attestation key?
