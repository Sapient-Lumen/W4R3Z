# Democratic authorization, consultation, and affected voice rules should not be turned into fiscal theater or consent washing

## Claim

Revenue power needs democratic form. A tax or tax-like charge that materially shifts burdens should disclose authority, purpose, affected parties, expected incidence, participation channel, and response-to-reasons record before consequences harden.[S562][S563][S564]

## Why it matters

Tax design fails when public choice is hidden inside formulas, vendor contracts, delegated tariff schedules, local utility filings, or emergency orders. Participation is not a veto over every fiscal rule, but it is a test of whether affected people can understand the burden and whether the state can give reasons rather than merely collect.[S563][S564][S565]

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/democratic-authorization-consultation-and-affected-voice-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/democratic-authorization-consultation-and-affected-voice-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/democratic-authorization-consultation-and-participation-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/democratic-authorization-consultation-and-participation-ladder.md). The compact rule is: do not accept fiscal power that hides authority, burden, beneficiary, proof limits, floor risk, or public repair behind a more convenient label.

## Anti-patterns

- **fiscal theater** — hearings and dashboards exist but cannot alter the fiscal choice.
- **consent washing** — affected communities are asked for comments only after the deal is locked.
- **expert-only legitimacy** — technical models substitute for democratic reasons.
- **delegation drift** — emergency or administrative authority becomes permanent fiscal law.
