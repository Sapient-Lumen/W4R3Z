# 178 — Tax-return-preparer misconduct, ghost-preparer filing, refund diversion, and EFIN / PTIN abuse should not be turned into captive-preparer rents or preparer-hostage traps

## One-line thesis

A polity may regulate paid tax preparers, electronic-return originators, payroll-service providers, and refund-product intermediaries, but it should not let the preparer market become the practical owner of ordinary tax compliance. If a preparer files without consent, changes a return after signature, withholds records, diverts a refund, uses a ghost or false credential, fails to file as agreed, abuses an EFIN / PTIN, or embeds the taxpayer in a misleading refund product, the affected subject needs a **no-rent preparer-misconduct lane** that separates the taxpayer's intended return and true liability from the preparer's conduct before penalties, refund loss, or default finality harden.[S27][S39][S40][S41][S42][S65][S66][S197][S198][S199][S200][S201][S202][S203]

## Why this matters

The archive now blocks gateway rents, review paywalls, distress rents, payout skims, record rents, comprehension tolls, payment tolls, certificate rents, submission tolls, proxy-access rents, update rents, victim rents, reporter-hostage mismatches, captive-household traps, time traps, suspense-credit rents, collateral-release ransom, and inspection rents. But one common operational failure still sits between those protections: many people use preparers because the tax system is complex, digitally mediated, language-dependent, refund-timed, or professionally gatekept in practice. If the preparer then controls the signed return, the e-file authorization, the direct-deposit field, the records, the PTIN / EFIN trail, or the refund product, the subject can be trapped inside the very private channel that was supposed to help.[S27][S39][S42][S65][S66][S198][S199][S200][S201][S202]

Current public materials already show the nonextractive shape. IRS complaint guidance allows preparer complaints through online, fax, or mail channels with attachments; Form 14157 covers complaints against a preparer or preparation business and names refund theft, e-file misuse, refused records, unsigned returns, false credentials, and PTIN issues; Form 14157-A applies when a preparer filed or altered a return without knowledge or consent and the taxpayer seeks an account change; TAS warns taxpayers not to authorize filing before review, not to route refunds into preparer-controlled accounts, to get complete copies, and to file with the information available when documentation is incomplete.[S197][S198][S199][S200][S201][S202][S203]

The archive rule generalizes from that pattern. Paid expertise may be useful; it is not a moral tax base. A taxpayer should not have to buy a second preparer, pay a refund-product toll, or accept penalties as the price of escaping the first preparer's misconduct.[S27][S39][S41][S42][S65][S66][S197][S200]

## Archive rule

1. **Do not treat preparer-misconduct recovery as a taxable service.** Baseline complaint, account-correction, return-copy comparison, refund-trace, signature-authorization review, preparer-identification review, and penalty-separation lanes belong to administration, not to a paid rescue market.[S197][S198][S199][S200][S201][S202]
2. **Separate the taxpayer's intended return from the preparer's filed return.** A signed intended return, copy supplied by the preparer, Form 8879 trail, bank record, transcript mismatch, correspondence, or comparable evidence should trigger issue-scoped correction rather than automatic taxpayer adoption of alterations, refund diversion, or unauthorized filing.[S198][S199][S200][S201]
3. **Do not make ghost-preparer opacity a taxpayer penalty by default.** Unsigned returns, missing PTINs, false credentials, EFIN misuse, preparer-controlled bank accounts, refused records, or noncommercial software misuse are misconduct signals. They can justify preparer enforcement and extra verification, but they should not by themselves erase the taxpayer's correction standing.[S198][S199][S200][S201][S202]
4. **Keep records and originals from becoming hostage property.** A subject should not lose filing, audit, refund, appeal, or correction rights because a preparer kept originals, refused copies, or controlled the only portal record. The ordinary path should accept copies, third-party records, statements, and administration-side traces before forcing paid reconstruction.[S198][S199][S200]
5. **Keep refund-product and direct-deposit abuses issue-bounded.** Refund diversion, refund-advance confusion, fee deduction from refund, bounced preparer checks, or preparer-controlled accounts justify tracing and preparer-side investigation, not wholesale denial of the clear refund share or conversion of the taxpayer's own refund into a private payout market.[S163][S176][S181][S198][S199][S200]
6. **Do not collapse preparer misconduct into taxpayer culpability.** The taxpayer may still owe the true tax and may be responsible for reckless or collusive falsehoods. But penalties, fraud framing, and criminal shadow should distinguish deliberate taxpayer participation from altered returns, nonfiling after engagement, stolen refunds, ghost signatures, or false preparer representations.[S27][S39][S41][S98][S99][S198][S199][S200][S202]
7. **Escalate repeat or organized preparer abuse upstream.** Promoter-side schemes, abusive preparer patterns, compromised PTINs / EFINs, repeated refund diversion, and payroll-service nonremittance belong in preparer discipline, injunction, information-return, criminal, or promoter-enforcement channels rather than in scattered victim-by-victim extraction.[S198][S202][S203]
8. **Current AI gets no direct subject-side analogue because current AI is not yet a tax person.** For present systems, software providers, online filing tools, payroll processors, and preparation firms are ordinary regulated intermediaries. If a future rights-bearing artificial person ever exists, its tax return cannot be captively filed, altered, or refunded through a former owner's preparer account without a person-side correction and revocation lane.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S141][S142][S143][S197][S199]

## Pattern pack

### 1. Paid help is not consent to lose the public lane

Using a preparer may be rational where the rules are complex, language access is thin, software channels dominate, or a refund is urgently needed. That choice should not waive the public baseline: notice, copy access, correction, account history, payment proof, refund tracing, and appeal still belong to the subject.[S27][S39][S42][S65][S66][S164][S165][S168][S200]

### 2. The signed-intended-return packet is a bridge, not a perfection demand

A taxpayer often cannot prove the whole hidden e-file path. The sensible bridge is narrower: signed intended return, preparer copy, Form 8879, bank record, message trail, receipt, police report where required, transcript mismatch, or credible statement. Once that packet exists, the hidden EFIN / PTIN, filing metadata, refund-routing, and internal processing facts should move toward the record holder.[S198][S199][S200][S201]

### 3. Refund diversion should be traced before it is moralized

If money went to a preparer-controlled account, a refund product, a bounced check, or a bank field different from the taxpayer's intended return, the first questions are routing and authorization. The state may protect against fraud, but it should not assume the taxpayer received value merely because the system paid someone.[S163][S181][S198][S199][S200]

### 4. Preparer discipline and taxpayer correction are related but not identical

A complaint against a preparer may support account correction, but the taxpayer's correction lane should not have to wait for full disciplinary closure. Conversely, correcting the taxpayer's account should not immunize a repeat abusive preparer. The two tracks need coordination without making one hostage to the other.[S197][S198][S199][S202][S203]

### 5. Future artificial persons would need their own filing voice

If future artificial personhood ever crosses the archive threshold, a former owner, host, tax-service vendor, or account custodian could not file or alter the person's return as if the subject were still property. Person-side filing authorization, refund routing, copy access, complaint, and revocation would have to travel with the recognized subject.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S141][S142][S143]

## Guardrails

- no requirement to buy a second preparer merely to report or correct the first preparer's misconduct
- no treating a preparer-controlled direct deposit, refund product, or bounced preparer check as proof that the taxpayer actually received the refund
- no default penalty finality where the taxpayer supplies a signed intended return, preparer copy, authorization trail, transcript mismatch, bank record, or comparable good-faith packet
- no allowing a preparer to hold originals, copies, portal accounts, PTIN / EFIN facts, or refund routing as practical hostage property
- no using preparer discipline delays as a reason to postpone the taxpayer's account correction indefinitely
- no former-owner or vendor monopoly over filing, refund routing, or return-copy access if future artificial personhood ever becomes real

## Failure modes

- **captive-preparer rent** — the subject must buy new private help merely to escape the misconduct of the old private helper.
- **ghost-preparer finality** — an unsigned, missing-PTIN, or self-prepared-looking return is treated as the taxpayer's final act before misconduct is investigated.
- **refund-diversion hostage** — the refund was routed through a preparer, product, or account the taxpayer did not control, yet the taxpayer bears the loss or must pay to trace it.
- **records hostage** — original documents, preparer copies, Form 8879, receipt records, or portal accounts are withheld and the subject loses deadlines or proof rights.
- **altered-return adoption** — the filed return differs from the signed intended return, but the taxpayer must disprove every hidden filing step before the account is marked disputed.
- **discipline/correction collapse** — the taxpayer's account correction waits for full preparer discipline, or preparer enforcement disappears after one victim's account is fixed.
- **owner-preparer machine filing** — a future artificial person's filing and refund are controlled through a former owner's preparer account or vendor console.

## Practical tests

1. Can the affected subject report preparer misconduct, submit a signed intended return or available packet, and seek account correction without buying a new paid gatekeeper?
2. Does the process distinguish refund theft, altered return, nonfiling, false credentials, ghost preparation, and preparer-side promoter abuse rather than treating all of them as taxpayer filing choices?
3. If the preparer withheld records or used hidden filing credentials, does the burden shift toward the actor or agency that can see the PTIN, EFIN, refund route, transmission, or account transcript?
4. Are penalties, interest, refund holds, and collection actions paused or narrowed for the slice plausibly caused by preparer misconduct?
5. Can repeat abusive preparers, payroll-service preparers, promoters, or compromised credential holders be escalated upstream without making each taxpayer buy a separate rescue?
6. If the subject were a future rights-bearing artificial person, could it inspect, revoke, and correct filing authorization without former-owner or vendor permission?
