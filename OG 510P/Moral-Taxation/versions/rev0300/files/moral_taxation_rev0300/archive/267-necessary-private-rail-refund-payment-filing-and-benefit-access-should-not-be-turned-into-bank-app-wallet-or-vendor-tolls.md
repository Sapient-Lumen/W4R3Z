# Necessary private rail refund, payment, filing, and benefit access should not be turned into bank, app, wallet, or vendor tolls

## Claim

Private rails can help administer taxes, but ordinary filing, payment, refund, rebate, notice, appeal, and benefit access should not depend on one bank, app, wallet, device, software vendor, processor, or platform without a timely public or no-rent fallback.[S443][S648][S444]

## Why it matters

A tax right can be lost through delay, fee, account closure, dashboard lockout, or rejected payment even when no explicit tax rate changes. That availability burden is incidence and must be routed.[S443][S648][S444]

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/necessary-private-rail-and-channel-incidence-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/necessary-private-rail-and-channel-incidence-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/mandatory-private-tax-rail-and-bankless-fallback-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/mandatory-private-tax-rail-and-bankless-fallback-ladder.md).

## Anti-patterns

- **necessary-channel toll** — a private intermediary charges for ordinary civic access.
- **fallback fiction** — paper, phone, or assisted exception exists but cannot protect the floor in time.
- **rail-controller capture** — one intermediary controls identity, payment, record, correction, and appeal.
- **availability incidence** — denial, delay, or withdrawal does the work of a tax.
- **digital-only exclusion** — taxpayers without bank, device, language, disability, or identity access receive a worse substantive right.
