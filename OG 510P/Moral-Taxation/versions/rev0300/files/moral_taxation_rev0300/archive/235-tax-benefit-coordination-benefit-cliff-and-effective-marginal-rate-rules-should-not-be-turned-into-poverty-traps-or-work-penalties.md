# Tax-benefit coordination, benefit cliff, and effective marginal rate rules should not be turned into poverty traps or work penalties

## Claim

The real tax rate on a low-income household is often the combined loss of benefits, credits, subsidies, time, and fees. Moral tax design must smooth the net schedule rather than defending each silo separately.[S81][S497][S572]

## Why it matters

Benefit cliffs are tax design by another name. When a family loses childcare, health coverage, food assistance, housing support, or filing access because of a small earnings increase, the fiscal state has created a hidden marginal tax that can defeat work, care, marriage, and formalization.[S81][S497][S572]

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/tax-benefit-coordination-benefit-cliffs-and-effective-marginal-rate-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/tax-benefit-coordination-benefit-cliffs-and-effective-marginal-rate-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/tax-benefit-coordination-cliff-smoothing-and-marginal-rate-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/tax-benefit-coordination-cliff-smoothing-and-marginal-rate-ladder.md). The compact rule is: do not accept fiscal power that hides authority, burden, beneficiary, proof limits, floor risk, or public repair behind a more convenient label.

## Anti-patterns

- **poverty trap** — increased income leaves the household worse off.
- **work penalty** — earnings, hours, or formalization trigger stacked losses.
- **administrative cliff** — paperwork timing rather than income creates the loss.
- **program silo** — each agency claims fairness while the combined schedule fails.
