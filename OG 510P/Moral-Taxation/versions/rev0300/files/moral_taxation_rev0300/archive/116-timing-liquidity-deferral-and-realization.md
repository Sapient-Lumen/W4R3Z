# 116 — Timing, liquidity, deferral, and realization

## One-line thesis

Tax justice is not only about **what** is taxed, **who** bears it, and **where** the proceeds go. It is also about **when** liability comes due and whether the taxpayer has the relevant cash flow without coercive fire-sale pressure.

## Why this matters

A tax can be well-aimed and still be morally defective if it is due at the wrong moment. Annual liability on an illiquid asset can force distressed sale. A windfall tax assessed too late may let rents escape untaxed. A risk levy collected only after catastrophe may socialise downside while privatising upside. Timing is therefore part of justice, not only administration.[S2][S5][S21][S23][S24]

## Archive rule

Match **payment timing** to **cash generation, risk creation, and asset liquidity**.

- Use **withholding or near-real-time collection** for frequent visible flows.
- Use **annual or periodic charges** for recurring rents and recurring externalities.
- Use **instalments, deferral, or liens** when the base is justified but the holder is temporarily cash-constrained.
- Use **bonds, reserves, or insurance ex ante** where the taxed activity creates catastrophic downside before profits are realised.
- Use **anti-deferral backstops** where delay would otherwise become avoidance.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Flow taxes should generally meet flow income

Wages, salary-like labour income, and routine business cash flow are usually best taxed through withholding, pay-as-you-go instalments, or other schedules close to receipt. This reduces surprise liabilities and keeps compliance legible.

### 2. Land taxation should be recurring, but timing relief may be necessary

Land and site-value taxation remain first-rank bases. But cash-poor owner-occupiers should often have access to tightly designed deferral, circuit-breakers, or instalments, commonly secured by the property itself. This is timing relief, not a moral exemption from the base.[S5][S23]

### 3. Inheritance taxes should distinguish receipt from liquidity stress

Large receipts should be taxed progressively when advantage is received, especially on a lifetime-receipts view. But where the transferred asset is an ongoing small business, farm, or similarly illiquid operating asset, staged payment can be justified if anti-avoidance and valuation rules are real.[S8][S24]

### 4. Saving and gains need timing discipline across acquisition, holding, and disposal

Taxes on savings and wealth interact across multiple stages: acquisition, holding, and disposal. Good design asks where the burden belongs across those stages rather than treating annual wealth, realised gains, transaction taxes, and inheritance as interchangeable.[S2][S24]

### 5. AI stacks need both ex ante and ex post timing

Some AI liabilities should arrive **before** harm or insolvency risk materialises — for example through bonds, reserves, insurance, or licensing fees for high-risk deployment. Others should arrive **after** rents and windfalls are actually visible, such as excess-profit capture or automation-dividend contributions.[S15][S16][S17]

### 6. Emergency taxes need clock discipline

Crisis surcharges may be justified, but only if entry, duration, review, and exit conditions are named in advance. Timing opacity is one way temporary taxes become permanent rent grabs.

## Guardrails

- Do not confuse **deferral** with forgiveness.
- Put interest, liens, or recovery rules on deferred liabilities where appropriate.
- Do not design taxes that require vulnerable households to liquidate shelter to satisfy annual liability.
- Do not let large fortunes convert justified taxation into indefinite untaxed appreciation by timing games.
- Publish payment calendars, instalment rights, and hardship procedures clearly.

## Failure modes

- **Fire-sale taxation**: liability is morally justified in principle but arrives in a way that forces distressed liquidation.
- **Indefinite deferral as avoidance**: the wealthy convert timing relief into permanent non-payment.
- **Ex-post-only risk funding**: dangerous deployers pay only after harm, when losses have already been socialised.
- **Bad withholding target**: the easiest visible cash flow is taxed even though it is survival income rather than rent or harm.

## Practical tests

1. When does the liability arise relative to when cash or rent is actually available?
2. Would a justified tax still be justified if it had to be paid in one lump sum tomorrow?
3. Is deferral secured and reviewable, or merely a loophole for the well-advised?
4. Does the timing design reduce coercive liquidation without erasing the tax base?
5. For AI risk, is enough money collected before catastrophe to make compensation and remediation real?
