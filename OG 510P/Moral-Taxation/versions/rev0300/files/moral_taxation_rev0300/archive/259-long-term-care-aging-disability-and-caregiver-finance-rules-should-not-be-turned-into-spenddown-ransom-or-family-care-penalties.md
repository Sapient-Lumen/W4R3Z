# Long term care aging disability and caregiver finance rules should not be turned into spenddown ransom or family care penalties

## Claim

Long-term care finance is legitimate when it spreads predictable dependency risk, protects dignity, and supports caregivers. It fails when support is conditioned on destitution, unpaid family substitution, or institutional default.[S628][S629][S623]

## Why it matters

Ageing and disability pressure public budgets, households, and workers. The moral tax question is not whether care costs money; it is who is silently made to pay when the fiscal system pretends dependency is exceptional.[S628][S629][S623]

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/long-term-care-aging-disability-and-caregiver-finance-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/long-term-care-aging-disability-and-caregiver-finance-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/long-term-care-aging-disability-and-caregiver-finance-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/long-term-care-aging-disability-and-caregiver-finance-ladder.md). The compact rule is: treat care need as social risk, not a family penalty.

## Anti-patterns

- **spenddown ransom** — care arrives only after near-destitution.
- **family-care extraction** — public budgets assume unpaid relatives can absorb the burden.
- **institutional bias** — financing favors facilities over home/community choice.
- **longevity blame** — older or disabled people are treated as fiscal failures rather than rights holders.
