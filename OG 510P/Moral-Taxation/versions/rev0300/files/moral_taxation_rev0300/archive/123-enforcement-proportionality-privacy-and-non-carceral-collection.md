# 123 — Enforcement proportionality, privacy, and non-carceral collection

## One-line thesis

A tax that is substantively justified can still be morally defective if collection relies on unconstrained surveillance, black-box targeting, hardship-blind debt recovery, or coercive measures that are disproportionate to the amount and conduct at issue.

## Why this matters

The fiscal state does not stop being a coercive institution once liability has been computed. Collection powers reach into wages, accounts, assets, movement, and reputation. In digital tax administration, those powers can also reach into data exhaust, predictive risk scoring, and always-on monitoring. That means enforcement design is part of tax justice, not merely an implementation detail.[S27][S39][S40][S41]

This matters especially for AI-mediated administration and AI-shaped economies. Automated debt triage, third-party data matching, and real-time reporting can make collection faster, but they can also make overreach quieter. The archive therefore treats fair collection as a constitutional layer alongside incidence, proceeds, timing, measurement, jurisdiction, anti-fragmentation, and notice.[S16][S17][S39][S41]

## Design rule

Collect the right amount, at the right time, through the least intrusive and most reviewable path that can still preserve compliance and prevent asset flight.[S39][S41]

## Pattern pack

### 1. Stage coercion

Use a ladder rather than a cliff:

- reminder and explanation,
- early engagement,
- payment plans or offsets where hardship is real,
- targeted protective measures where dissipation risk is real,
- and only then stronger recovery tools.

Do not jump immediately to account seizures, travel restraints, or public exposure when softer measures would work.[S39][S41]

### 2. Separate hardship from evasion

A morally serious tax administration distinguishes the unwilling from the temporarily unable. Short-term cash-flow stress, disaster disruption, and ordinary confusion call for assistance, instalments, and tailored service. Deliberate concealment, asset stripping, and serial evasion justify a harder edge.[S27][S39]

### 3. Keep a protected minimum

Collection should leave people with enough income, assets, and procedural access to continue ordinary life, seek review, and meet basic needs. Debt recovery that strips the protected floor turns administration into domination.

### 4. Bound surveillance by purpose

Tax administrations may need substantial information, but access cannot be unconstrained, unreasonable, or perpetual. Data collection, retention, matching, and sharing should be limited to what the tax purpose actually requires, with visible safeguards, deletion rules, and contest paths.[S16][S27][S40]

### 5. No black-box enforcement finality

Predictive models may assist prioritization, but they should not quietly determine final recovery outcomes. People must be able to learn the basis for a flag, contest major decisions, and obtain human review with authority to reverse course.[S16][S17][S27]

### 6. Use controller-side collection before diffuse extraction where possible

When the archive prefers controller-first taxation for AI, it also prefers controller-side collection when feasible: collect from labs, platforms, clouds, deployers, utilities, landlords, and large intermediaries before pushing compliance burdens onto weaker workers, small suppliers, or end users. This is partly an incidence rule, but also an enforcement rule.

### 7. Prefer rights-preserving transparency over shame tools

Public disclosure, platform de-listing, or licensing consequences can sometimes be justified for large, deliberate, and persistent non-compliance, but they should be treated as exceptional measures with tight thresholds and appeal rights, not as routine humiliation machinery.[S39][S41]

## Guardrails

- No permanent mass retention merely because storage is cheap.
- No predictive debt or audit score as unreviewable authority.
- No intrusive recovery without a proportionality reason.
- No collection design that makes it impossible to contest the case.
- No AI-enforcement stack whose vendors can hide decisive logic behind trade secrecy.

## Failure modes

- **Surveillance drift**: data gathered for a narrow tax purpose becomes a standing social-control apparatus.
- **Hardship blindness**: the system treats inability and evasion as the same moral fact.
- **Automated garnishment theater**: human review exists only on paper after the money is already gone.
- **Low-value coercion**: the state spends large coercive energy pursuing small debts from weak people while negotiated large-player arrears persist.
- **Species confusion in enforcement**: controllers hide behind software agents or shell deployers to make the real collection target disappear.

## Practical tests

1. Is the recovery path staged from least to most intrusive?
2. Are hardship, disaster, or temporary liquidity lanes real and usable?
3. Are data collection and retention narrower than what surveillance-maximizing administrators would want?
4. Can a taxpayer understand and contest any model-assisted recovery action before irreversible harm occurs?
5. Does the collection architecture hit strong controllers and strategic evaders before weak and diffuse actors?
