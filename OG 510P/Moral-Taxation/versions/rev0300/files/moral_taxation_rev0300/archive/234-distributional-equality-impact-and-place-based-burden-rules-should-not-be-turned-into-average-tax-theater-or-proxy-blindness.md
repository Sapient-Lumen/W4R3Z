# Distributional, equality-impact, and place-based burden rules should not be turned into average-tax theater or proxy blindness

## Claim

A fiscal rule that is progressive on average can still fail if it creates concentrated harm by place, household structure, liquidity, disability, race proxy, immigration status, or public-service access. Distributional analysis is a floor test, not a public-relations chart.[S569][S571][S564]

## Why it matters

Tax incidence is often measured where data are easiest. But moral incidence lands through rent pass-through, service loss, compliance costs, benefit withdrawals, local burden, and liquidity shocks. The archive therefore treats equality analysis as an evidence discipline, not as an optional appendix.[S571][S564][S154]

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/distributional-equality-impact-and-place-based-burden-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/distributional-equality-impact-and-place-based-burden-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/distributional-equality-impact-and-fiscal-incidence-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/distributional-equality-impact-and-fiscal-incidence-ladder.md). The compact rule is: do not accept fiscal power that hides authority, burden, beneficiary, proof limits, floor risk, or public repair behind a more convenient label.

## Anti-patterns

- **average-tax theater** — mean burden hides tails, cliffs, and concentrated place losses.
- **proxy blindness** — refusal to measure protected effects becomes a neutrality claim.
- **decile-only justice** — income slices hide wealth, race/place, disability, care, and household structure.
- **data absolutism** — missing data are used to ignore a plausible burden.
