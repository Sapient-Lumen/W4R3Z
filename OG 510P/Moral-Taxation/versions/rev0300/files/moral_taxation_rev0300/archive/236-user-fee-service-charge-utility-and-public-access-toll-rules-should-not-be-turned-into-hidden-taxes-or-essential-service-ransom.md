# User-fee, service-charge, utility, and public-access toll rules should not be turned into hidden taxes or essential-service ransom

## Claim

Cost recovery is not automatically just. A fee must match a special benefit or avoidable cost; otherwise it should be judged as a tax, with incidence, floor, authorization, and proceeds tests. Essential services require affordability and no-rent fallback before disconnection or exclusion.[S573][S574][S575]

## Why it matters

Fees are politically easier than taxes because they look like prices. But required public channels, basic utilities, and civic access goods are not ordinary market purchases. When nonpayment cuts off water, energy, compliance, appeal, transport, or status, the instrument has entered floor-protection territory.[S573][S574][S575]

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/user-fee-service-charge-utility-and-public-access-toll-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/user-fee-service-charge-utility-and-public-access-toll-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/user-fee-service-charge-affordability-and-essential-access-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/user-fee-service-charge-affordability-and-essential-access-ladder.md). The compact rule is: do not accept fiscal power that hides authority, burden, beneficiary, proof limits, floor risk, or public repair behind a more convenient label.

## Anti-patterns

- **hidden tax** — a broad public cost is shifted into compulsory fees.
- **essential-service ransom** — water, energy, filing, transit, or permit access is withheld from floor-risk households.
- **self-funding agency trap** — the collector depends on fee volume.
- **reconnection rent** — restoration of an essential service becomes a fee ladder.
