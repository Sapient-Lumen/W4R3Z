# 104 — Land, location, and natural-resource rent taxation

## One-line thesis

Land and natural-resource rents are among the cleanest moral targets in the tax system because they attach to scarcity, exclusion, and public order more than to productive effort.

## Why this matters

Land cannot flee and its aggregate supply is fixed. Site value is heavily produced by community, infrastructure, law, and surrounding demand rather than solely by the owner. Similar logic applies to extraction rents from oil, gas, minerals, timber, spectrum, and other scarce commons.[S5]

## Design rule

Tax site value and extraction rent more aggressively than buildings, payroll, or routine savings.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Separate land from improvements

Do not punish building, maintenance, or productive renovation merely because a location is valuable.

### 2. Capture public-created location value

Transit, zoning, safety, and neighborhood demand should not accrue entirely as private windfall.

### 3. Tax extraction as depletion plus exclusion

Resource taxation should include:

- rent capture,
- depletion logic,
- restoration obligations,
- and public inheritance logic.

### 4. Revalue on a real schedule

A land tax with decades-old assessments quietly stops being a land tax and becomes a lottery. Revaluation cadence, mass appraisal quality, and appeal rights are part of the justice case for land taxation.[S23][S25]

### 5. Use local and national layers together

Land value often suits local finance; large natural-resource rents may require national equalization and sovereign wealth treatment.


## Guardrails

- Avoid forcing cash-poor residents out without deferral, instalment, or circuit-breaker mechanisms.
- Treat deferral as timing relief secured against the asset, not as a quiet exemption for appreciating land.
- Keep cadastres updated; bad valuation corrodes legitimacy.
- Do not let local dependence on land booms create anti-housing politics.


## Failure modes

- **Improvement penalty**: taxing construction harder than passive appreciation.
- **Speculative vacancy subsidy**: low holding cost lets land banking starve communities.
- **Extraction without repair**: the public gets pollution and depletion while the private side keeps rent.


## Practical tests

1. Is the tax aimed at land value itself rather than productive improvements?
2. Would speculation become less attractive relative to use?
3. Does the public share in the scarcity rent of common resources?
