# Taxpayer service, ombuds, appeals, and representation rules should not be turned into self-help mazes or review deserts

## Claim

A tax system cannot claim moral legitimacy if ordinary taxpayers can be assessed, denied, or collected from faster than they can understand, correct, appeal, or obtain help. Service, ombuds access, appeal transparency, and representation support are part of the tax instrument.[S141][S540][S543]

## Why it matters

When agency capacity is thin, the burden falls on people least able to buy expertise. Digital service, automated notices, and independent appeals are legitimate only when they preserve plain explanation, accessible correction, and representative access rather than shifting the whole cost of administration onto taxpayers.[S541][S542]

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/taxpayer-service-ombuds-appeals-and-representation-floor-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/taxpayer-service-ombuds-appeals-and-representation-floor-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/taxpayer-service-ombuds-appeals-and-representation-floor-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/taxpayer-service-ombuds-appeals-and-representation-floor-ladder.md). The compact rule is: no hardened tax consequence without a usable service, correction, appeal, and representation path scaled to floor risk.

## Anti-patterns

- **self-help maze** — rights require expert navigation.
- **review desert** — enforcement persists after correction channels fail.
- **appeals opacity** — independent review gives no usable reason.
- **representation rent** — only paid professionals can access the practical rule.
