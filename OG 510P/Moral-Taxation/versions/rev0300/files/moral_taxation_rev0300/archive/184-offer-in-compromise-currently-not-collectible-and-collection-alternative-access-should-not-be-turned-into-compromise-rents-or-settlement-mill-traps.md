# 184 — Offer-in-compromise, currently-not-collectible, and collection-alternative access should not be turned into compromise rents or settlement-mill traps

## One-line thesis

When an assessed tax debt is real but full collection is impossible, doubtful, floor-destroying, or disproportionate, access to **offer-in-compromise, currently-not-collectible, hardship, payment-alternative, and collection-alternative review** belongs to public collection design rather than to a private settlement market. A state may ask for bounded financial proof, current compliance, and enforceable terms, but it should not make inability to pay, doubt about liability, or effective-tax-administration hardship into a paid rescue lane.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S42][S121][S162][S180][S212][S240][S241][S242][S246][S247]

## Why this matters

The archive already protects payment plans, private-collector verification, collection-release, Tax Court petition access, deadlock escalation, and baseline correction. A remaining cliff sits between those lanes: a taxpayer can lose even after the liability is known, the court path is understood, and collection pressure is visible, because the only practical way to seek a compromise, hardship delay, or collectibility review appears to be a commercial tax-resolution firm.[S39][S42][S160][S162][S176][S180][S181][S212][S215][S217][S218][S219][S222]

Public materials show the non-rent shape. IRS offer-in-compromise guidance describes public application routes, lump-sum and periodic payment options, low-income certification under which the application fee, initial payment, and monthly payments during review are not required, suspended collection activity while a processable offer is evaluated, and a 30-day appeal route for rejected offers. IRS and TAS materials also describe currently-not-collectible status as a temporary suspension of most collection activity when paying would prevent the taxpayer from meeting basic living expenses, while preserving later review, refund offset, and lien warnings.[S240][S241][S242][S244][S246][S247][S248]

That same design can fail in extractive ways. Returned offers can be unappealable even when curable; fees and initial payments can deter low-income subjects if waiver is hidden; financial statements can become a data-harvesting ritual; private advertisers can imply they sell secret access; and a subject in CNC can remain trapped by refunds, liens, penalties, and recurring bills without a clear re-review lane. The moral point is not that every debt should be compromised. It is that compromise and hardship review should not be sold as mercy through actors who profit from official opacity.[S27][S39][S41][S42][S65][S66][S180][S212][S215][S240][S241][S242][S243][S245][S246][S247]

## Archive rule

1. **Separate collectibility, liability doubt, and hardship administration.** Doubt as to collectibility, doubt as to liability, and effective-tax-administration hardship ask different questions; the system should route them visibly rather than forcing everyone into one paid settlement product.[S39][S41][S42][S160][S240][S243][S245]
2. **Do not make compromise access a private toll road.** Public forms, public instructions, free preliminary tools, mail / online / assisted routes, and low-income / language / disability cues should be visible before a taxpayer concludes that a settlement mill is the official door.[S65][S66][S129][S130][S131][S132][S212][S215][S240][S242][S243]
3. **Fee and initial-payment waivers are part of the lane.** If an offer path requires a fee or initial payment in ordinary cases, the low-income certification and any waiver of periodic payments during review must be prominent enough that lack of cash does not become settlement-rent extraction.[S31][S42][S121][S122][S162][S240][S241][S242]
4. **Financial disclosure should be bounded to the collection alternative.** Income, expenses, asset equity, and business data can be required to test ability to pay, but proof demands should stay purpose-bound, explainable, and reviewable rather than becoming open-ended dignity costs or sensitive-data dragnet logic.[S27][S39][S41][S42][S64][S65][S66][S89][S240][S242][S246][S247]
5. **Pending alternative review should hold pressure enough to make the review real.** While a processable offer, CNC request, hardship review, or comparable collection alternative is under review, levy, private-collector assignment, passport escalation, lien hardening, and broad refund capture should be bounded to what is necessary and legally allowed, with the disputed or hardship-sensitive slice stayed or narrowed where feasible.[S39][S41][S42][S162][S176][S180][S181][S217][S218][S219][S240][S246][S247]
6. **Returned, rejected, withdrawn, and defaulted outcomes need status clarity.** A returned offer is not the same as a rejection; rejection should carry reasons and appeal cues; curable defects should have a cure route; and default should not erase earlier payments, hardship facts, or liability doubts without an intelligible accounting.[S160][S175][S240][S241][S244][S245]
7. **Household and business splits should not recreate captive-unit rents.** Separate forms for spouses, ex-spouses, businesses, or trust-fund liabilities may sometimes be necessary, but duplicate fees, duplicate proof, and inaccessible entity/person separation should not punish separated households, small firms, or responsible persons beyond the real liability relationship.[S31][S32][S33][S161][S162][S173][S242]
8. **Future artificial persons would need their own collection-alternative standing.** If direct artificial personhood ever became real, an owner, host, wallet provider, or former controller could not sell the subject's only access to compromise, CNC-like hardship, financial proof submission, rejection appeal, or default cure.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S240][S244][S246]

## Pattern pack

### 1. Compromise is collection calibration, not charity

An offer in compromise is a way to reconcile public recovery with what can actually be collected, what is genuinely owed, or what collection would do to the protected floor. It should be treated as a disciplined collection alternative, not a mercy commodity sold through fear.[S21][S39][S41][S42][S121][S162][S240][S245]

### 2. Low-income certification is anti-rent infrastructure

A fee and initial payment can be a manageable screen for some cases and a complete door-closer for others. Where public rules already recognize a low-income certification, hiding that route converts an administrative screen into a settlement rent.[S31][S42][S121][S122][S162][S240][S241][S242]

### 3. Settlement mills feed on official opacity

Private representatives may be useful, but they should not be needed just to learn whether a public compromise route exists, whether a free pre-qualifier is available, whether a fee waiver applies, or whether CNC is the better route. The archive's problem is not representation; it is representation becoming the price of public legibility.[S42][S141][S160][S180][S212][S214][S215][S240][S243]

### 4. CNC is a stay lane, not debt forgiveness or silent punishment

Currently-not-collectible status may suspend most collection without forgiving the debt, stopping interest, or eliminating later review. That tradeoff should be visible. A taxpayer should not discover only after the fact that refunds can still be offset, liens may be filed, or the account can reactivate when finances change.[S162][S176][S246][S247]

### 5. Return-versus-rejection is a status trap unless made legible

If a rejected offer can be appealed but a returned offer cannot, the distinction must be visible before and after submission. Curable omissions, current-compliance defects, fee mistakes, or portal/payment confusion should not become unappealable settlement failure without a clear cure route.[S160][S174][S240][S241][S244][S245]

### 6. Future artificial persons cannot be forced through owner settlement accounts

If a future artificial person has direct liability and standing, the collection-alternative bundle must follow that subject. A former owner or infrastructure host cannot remain the practical seller of compromise access, hardship proof, rejection appeal, or collection-alternative cure.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S240][S244][S246]

## Guardrails

- no mandatory paid tax-resolution firm merely to assess or request offer-in-compromise, CNC, hardship delay, payment alternative, or rejected-offer appeal
- no hiding low-income certification, fee waiver, initial-payment waiver, or periodic-payment pause while an offer is reviewed
- no treating public pre-qualifier, online account, or private software as the only realistic way to enter the compromise lane
- no unbounded financial-data demand unrelated to the collectibility, liability-doubt, or effective-tax-administration question being decided
- no private collection assignment, levy, passport escalation, lien hardening, or refund capture that defeats a live processable collection-alternative review without a separate predicate
- no returned-offer status trap for curable defects, current-compliance confusion, or payment-channel errors without clear cure information
- no duplicate household or small-business settlement tolls where the real liability can be separately allocated
- no owner-hosted compromise or CNC-like lane for a future recognized artificial person

## Failure modes

- **compromise rent** — the subject must pay a private actor, software gate, or official access fee merely to request a lawful compromise or hardship alternative.
- **settlement-mill trap** — public opacity lets advertisers sell unrealistic or unnecessary offers as though they control a secret IRS path.
- **waiver invisibility** — a low-income taxpayer abandons an offer because the fee, initial-payment, or monthly-payment waiver is not visible.
- **CNC mirage** — collection is said to be suspended, but refund offset, liens, interest, bills, or reactivation arrive without clear front-end explanation.
- **return-status forfeiture** — a process defect causes a returned offer with no appeal and no intelligible cure lane.
- **financial-proof dragnet** — collection-alternative review demands sensitive information beyond what ability-to-pay, liability-doubt, or hardship analysis requires.
- **owner-settlement capture** — a future artificial person's only compromise or hardship access remains inside a former owner or host account.

## Practical tests

1. Can an ordinary taxpayer identify the right lane — payment plan, CNC, OIC, doubt-as-to-liability, appeal, TAS, or LITC — without buying a settlement intermediary?
2. Are fees, initial payments, and low-income certification displayed before cash scarcity pushes the taxpayer away from the public route?
3. Does financial proof match the actual question being asked, or has collectibility review become generalized surveillance?
4. Are processable offers, CNC requests, and live collection-alternative reviews protected enough that collection pressure does not make the review moot?
5. Does a returned or rejected offer explain whether the next step is cure, resubmission, appeal, payment plan, CNC, or Tax Court / refund-suit logic?
6. If the subject were a future rights-bearing artificial person, would it control the collection-alternative lane directly rather than through an owner or host?

## Relations

This note narrows, rather than reopens, the hardship and collection spine. Use it with time-to-pay, payment-plan, private-collector, collection-release, deadlock-escalation, bounded-contest, standing, household-allocation, data-minimization, and sanctions notes. It does not say every liability should be compromised or forgiven; it says the route for determining collectibility, liability doubt, and hardship cannot itself become a rent.
