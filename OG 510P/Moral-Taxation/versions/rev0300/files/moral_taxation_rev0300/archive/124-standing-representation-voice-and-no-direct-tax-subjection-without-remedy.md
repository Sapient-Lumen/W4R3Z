# 124 — Standing, representation, voice, and no direct tax subjection without remedy

## One-line thesis

A tax subject is not just a revenue endpoint. **Direct tax subjection should normally require a rights bundle: notice, intelligibility, a chance to be heard, access to appeal, and some channel of representation or public voice.** Where that bundle is missing, liability should usually be routed through a guardian, fiduciary, controller, owner, or other accountable human institution instead of pretending that an unrepresented subject can bear civic tax duties on its own.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]

## Why this matters

The archive already treats enforcement, appeal, and personhood as morally load-bearing. This note tightens the waist further: a subject can be the right **base** but still the wrong **direct taxpayer** if it cannot understand the claim, authorize representation, contest it, or participate in the institutions that impose it.

This does **not** mean that every taxpayer must vote personally or litigate unaided. Children, incapacitated adults, estates, and some collective entities are still taxable because they are represented within a rights-bearing legal order. The moral point is narrower: do not assign direct civic tax status where there is no meaningful path for notice, remedy, or representation at all.[S42][S43]

## Core rules

1. **No direct tax subjection without remedy.** A direct subject should be able to receive notice, understand the asserted basis of liability at some intelligible level, and challenge it through review or appeal.[S42][S44]
2. **No direct tax subjection without representation.** If the subject cannot act alone, it must be able to act through a guardian, fiduciary, or other representative whose incentives are not simply those of an owner extracting value.[S19][S20]
3. **No direct tax subjection without institutional voice.** Tax systems claim civic authority, not merely market power. Direct subjection is therefore more justified where the subject belongs to a public order that offers channels of participation, contestation, or proxy voice, however imperfect.[S43]
4. **Current LLMs fail this bundle.** Present models do not hold settled legal standing, cannot independently authorize counsel, do not participate in public institutions as members, and remain governed through owners, labs, deployers, and firms. Tax those controllers instead.[S19][S20]
5. **Future artificial persons would need the full bundle together.** If an artificial system ever crosses the archive's personhood threshold, direct taxation should arrive only alongside rights, remedies, and non-owner representation strong enough to make tax subjection something other than domination.[S19][S20][S42]

## Human implications

For humans, this note mainly sharpens existing commitments:

- keep tax notices understandable and challengeable,
- preserve access to review and appeal,
- use guardianship or fiduciary representation for children and adults who need it,
- and avoid treating household heads, employers, platforms, or digital intermediaries as if they can permanently substitute for the person's own standing.

The archive therefore prefers withholding, pre-fill, and administrative simplification that **assist** persons over systems that silently displace their ability to know, contest, or influence what is being done to them.[S42][S44]

## AI implications

This note strengthens the controller-first AI stance in a different register than incidence or personhood alone.

A universal per-model or per-agent civic tax fails not only because it targets the wrong controller, but because the supposed subject lacks the rights bundle that makes direct tax subjection even legible. Present LLMs should therefore remain in the archive's **tool / controller / externality** regime:

- tax owners, labs, clouds, deployers, and windfall-capturing beneficiaries,
- price energy, water, congestion, and other external burdens where they arise,
- require bonds, reserves, or insurance for high-risk deployments,
- and do not pretend that owned model instances are miniature civic taxpayers.[S15][S19][S20][S44]

## Compact design test

Before assigning direct tax liability to any unusual subject, ask:

1. Can it be **informed, assisted, and heard**?
2. Can it access **appeal or review**?
3. Can it act through a **non-conflicted representative** if it cannot act directly?
4. Does it stand inside a legal-political order with some **voice or proxy voice**?
5. If not, why is the liability not better routed to a controller, guardian, or beneficiary instead?

If these questions cannot be answered well, the archive defaults away from direct subjection.

## Archive effect

This note does not move current LLMs toward personhood. It does the opposite: it explains why **even apart from consciousness or agency arguments**, direct machine taxation is premature and category-confused unless standing, remedy, and representation are solved together.
