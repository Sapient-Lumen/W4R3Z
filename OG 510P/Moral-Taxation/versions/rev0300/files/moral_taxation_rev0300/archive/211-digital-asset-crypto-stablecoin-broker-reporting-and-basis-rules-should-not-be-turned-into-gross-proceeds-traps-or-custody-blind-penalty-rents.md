# Digital-asset, crypto, stablecoin, broker-reporting, and basis rules should not be turned into gross-proceeds traps or custody-blind penalty rents

## Claim

Digital assets are tax-relevant when they produce income, gain, payment, reserve spread, or systemic-risk value. But reporting and penalties must follow custody, record power, real control, and correction capacity, not token mystique or chain-trace suspicion.[S487][S488][S489][S490]

## Why it matters

New information rails can improve compliance, but gross-proceeds reports without basis, self-transfer confusion, wallet fragmentation, stablecoin opacity, and uncertain broker boundaries can create penalty traps for users who lack the records held by platforms or never triggered a taxable event.

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/digital-asset-crypto-stablecoin-and-broker-reporting-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/digital-asset-crypto-stablecoin-and-broker-reporting-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/digital-asset-basis-custody-stablecoin-and-reporting-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/digital-asset-basis-custody-stablecoin-and-reporting-ladder.md). The compact rule is: tax real gain and issuer/intermediary rent; assign reporting to record-capable actors; shelter taxpayers from basis mismatch and custody-blind duties.

## Anti-patterns

- **gross-proceeds trap** — proceeds are treated as gain without basis correction.
- **custody-blind penalty** — duties attach to software, users, or interfaces without record power.
- **chain analytics as adjudication** — probabilistic evidence becomes liability without contest.
- **stablecoin shadow-bank rent** — reserve spread and run risk escape ordinary financial routing.
