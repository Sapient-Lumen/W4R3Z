# 119 — Tax expenditures, abatements, and hidden negative taxation

## One-line thesis

A morally serious tax system must be judged on its **net fiscal stack**, not on headline rates alone: abatements, credits, accelerated depreciation, preferential public-input pricing, and direct subsidies can function as hidden negative taxes.

## Why this matters

Many tax debates talk as if the moral object is a single levy. In practice, the effective burden is often set by the surrounding package: a rent tax paired with a long abatement, a carbon charge paired with fossil-fuel tax expenditures, or an AI facility charge paired with discounted land, power, water, or depreciation rules. IMF guidance treats tax expenditures as alternative means by which governments deliver financial support, and notes that sectoral incentives are often poorly integrated into fiscal management.[S29] OECD’s fossil-fuel support work likewise treats tax expenditures and direct transfers together as support measures, and stresses that such support can carry large fiscal costs while undermining transition goals.[S30]

## Design rule

Treat hidden negative taxes as part of the same moral ledger as explicit taxation.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Net the stack before judging the rate

Ask what the taxpayer actually faces after:

- negotiated abatements,
- credits and deductions,
- accelerated depreciation,
- below-cost access to public land, power, water, or infrastructure,
- direct operating subsidies,
- and transfer-side rebates or child-focused credits.

The relevant object is the **net effective burden**, not the announced rate.

### 2. Distinguish protection from privilege

Not all relief is a loophole. Low-income rebates, disability support, child-focused credits, and negative-income transfers can be part of the archive’s protected floor.[S4][S31] The archive is harder on relief that mainly protects sectoral incumbents, scarce-asset owners, or dominant bottlenecks.

### 3. Prefer transparent spending to opaque tax favors when possible

If a public-interest case for support is real, a visible grant, budget appropriation, or time-limited contract is often morally cleaner than a buried tax expenditure. Opaque fiscal support weakens democratic control and makes real tax incidence harder to see.[S29]

### 4. Sunset and cost every carve-out

Abatements, tax holidays, and sector credits should carry visible fiscal-cost estimates, review dates, and expiry rules. Permanent carve-outs are rarely morally neutral; they are usually hidden distribution decisions.[S29]

### 5. Apply the rule to AI and data centres

For large AI facilities and data-centre clusters, net together:

- local property-tax abatements,
- utility discounts or special contracts,
- subsidised siting or infrastructure,
- accelerated depreciation,
- R&D and investment credits,
- and any nominal externality charge or rent tax.

A stack that looks strict in public but gentle in net may fail the archive’s own controller-first and burden-bearing tests.

## Guardrails

- No secret negotiated abatements for dominant facilities.
- No indefinite sectoral tax holidays without review.
- No calling incumbent support “competitiveness” as if that settled the moral question.
- No conflation of household floor protection with investor privilege.

## Failure modes

- **Shadow nullification**: a good headline tax is quietly canceled by nearby favors.
- **Backdoor regressivity**: household-side relief is cut while capital-side favors remain.
- **Race-to-the-bottom bargaining**: localities compete away the tax base to attract mobile projects.
- **Carbon/AI contradiction**: corrective charges are announced while fossil-fuel or frontier-facility support keeps the net signal weak.[S30]

## Practical tests

1. What is the net effective rate or burden after all tax expenditures, abatements, and direct subsidies are included?
2. Could the same public-interest objective be delivered more transparently as on-budget spending?
3. Are household protections clearly separable from incumbent privileges?
4. If the support is temporary, where is the sunset or review trigger?
