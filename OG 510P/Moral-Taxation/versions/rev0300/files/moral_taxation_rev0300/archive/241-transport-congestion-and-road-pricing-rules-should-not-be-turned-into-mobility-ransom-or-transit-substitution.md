# Transport congestion and road-pricing rules should not be turned into mobility ransom or transit substitution

## Claim

Congestion charges, tolls, fuel taxes, and road-use fees are strongest when they price scarce road space or transport harms and visibly fund mobility repair. They become suspect when they burden necessary travel, weak transit users, disabled travelers, or low-income workers while ordinary transit funding is quietly withdrawn.[S586][S587]

## Why it matters

Mobility is not only consumption. It is access to work, care, medical treatment, civic life, and emergency movement. Road pricing can correct real externalities, but only if design tests real alternatives, exemption edge cases, data minimization, collection penalties, and proceeds integrity.[S586][S587]

The compact rule is: price avoidable congestion and harm, not unavoidable access.

## Anti-patterns

- **mobility ransom** — necessary travel is charged without alternative or repair.
- **transit substitution** — toll revenue replaces baseline transit support.
- **exemption formalism** — relief fails because the vehicle or paperwork is titled wrong.
- **surveillance tolling** — trip data outlives the toll purpose.
