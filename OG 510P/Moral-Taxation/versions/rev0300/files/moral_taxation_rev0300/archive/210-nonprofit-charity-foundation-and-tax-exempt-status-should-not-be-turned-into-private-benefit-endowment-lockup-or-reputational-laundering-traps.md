# Nonprofit, charity, foundation, and tax-exempt status should not be turned into private-benefit, endowment-lockup, or reputational-laundering traps

## Claim

Tax exemption and charitable deductibility are public subsidies. They are justified by real public benefit, non-inurement, broad access, mission operation, and timely public deployment, not by nonprofit label alone.[S483][S484][S485][S486]

## Why it matters

Exempt status can hide private benefit, prestige consumption, donor control, market competition, endowment warehousing, and hospital collection practices. A tax system that polices ordinary labor but under-polices subsidized nonprofit privilege has inverted its moral order.

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/nonprofit-charity-foundation-and-exempt-status-public-benefit-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/nonprofit-charity-foundation-and-exempt-status-public-benefit-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/nonprofit-exemption-community-benefit-donor-control-and-retained-surplus-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/nonprofit-exemption-community-benefit-donor-control-and-retained-surplus-ladder.md). The compact rule is: exemption follows public benefit and deployment; private benefit, donor control, and warehousing trigger caps, payout, UBIT, excise, recapture, or revocation.

## Anti-patterns

- **private-benefit laundering** — exempt form hides self-dealing, prestige, or insider advantage.
- **endowment lockup** — tax-subsidized assets remain idle while public needs persist.
- **community-benefit fog** — vague reporting substitutes for access and charity care.
- **deduction regressivity** — the richest donor receives the largest public match without matching public control.
