# Media attention digital advertising tax and speech transparency rules should not be turned into censorship or attention rents

## Claim

Digital advertising and attention extraction can contain real rents, but a tax on speech-adjacent revenue needs precise base design, apportionment, transparency, and anti-pass-through honesty. The state may tax market power and data extraction; it should not censor invoice speech or pretend incidence disappears because disclosure is forbidden.[S461][S636][S637]

## Why it matters

Classification seams are where public systems often leak moral clarity: an insurance surcharge looks actuarial, a wager looks like finance, a telecom contribution looks like a small bill line, a digital-ad tax looks like rent capture, a portable benefit looks like worker protection, an environmental payment looks like mitigation, or a release checklist looks like bureaucracy. The archive should ask who controls the burden, who bears it, and what floor or public claim is at stake before accepting the label.[S461][S636][S637]

## Anti-patterns

- **invoice speech ban** — truthful customer explanation of tax-related price changes is suppressed to hide incidence.
- **attention rent theater** — a tax claims to price data or attention harm while leaving extraction design untouched.
- **gross receipts cascade** — multiple ad-tech layers pass the tax through until non-rent actors bear it.
- **small publisher squeeze** — local or independent publishers lose revenue while dominant gatekeepers keep rents.
- **apportionment fiction** — sourcing/user-location rules are too noisy to justify coercive liability.
