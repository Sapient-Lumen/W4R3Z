# Cumulative burden environmental justice and siting tax rules should not be turned into sacrifice zone offsets or average burden theater

## Claim

Cumulative burden is not an optional equity appendix. Where multiple chemical, physical, fiscal, policing, utility, climate, and social stressors stack in one community, average statewide incidence is morally insufficient; siting, abatements, credits, fees, and offsets need place-based veto, repair, monitoring, and non-compensation limits.[S638][S639][S640]

## Why it matters

Classification seams are where public systems often leak moral clarity: an insurance surcharge looks actuarial, a wager looks like finance, a telecom contribution looks like a small bill line, a digital-ad tax looks like rent capture, a portable benefit looks like worker protection, an environmental payment looks like mitigation, or a release checklist looks like bureaucracy. The archive should ask who controls the burden, who bears it, and what floor or public claim is at stake before accepting the label.[S638][S639][S640]

## Anti-patterns

- **sacrifice zone offset** — money is paid while exposure, illness risk, displacement, or infrastructure burden remains.
- **average burden theater** — jurisdiction-wide progressivity hides concentrated neighborhood harm.
- **abatement for pollution** — tax concessions make the project cheaper while locals pay health and utility costs.
- **tool removal blindness** — loss or change of a screening tool is treated as absence of burden.
- **community benefit hush money** — small grants mute objections without enforceable exposure reduction.
