# Labor tax wedge, platform-work, and social-insurance rules should not be turned into employment penalties or misclassification rents

## Claim

Social-insurance funding is legitimate, but payroll and classification rules should not make ordinary work the easiest revenue base while allowing platform control, contract labels, or record asymmetry to erase employer duties.[S497][S498][S499]

## Why it matters

A labour tax wedge can fund pensions, unemployment, disability, health, and wage records. It can also discourage low-paid work, hide employer-side incidence in wages, and reward firms that keep control while shifting contribution, risk, and paperwork to workers.

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/labor-tax-wedge-platform-work-and-social-insurance-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/labor-tax-wedge-platform-work-and-social-insurance-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/labor-tax-wedge-classification-social-insurance-and-worker-floor-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/labor-tax-wedge-classification-social-insurance-and-worker-floor-ladder.md). The compact rule is: fund social insurance visibly; protect the work floor; assign record and contribution duties to the actor with control and payment data; do not let status labels become private tax subsidies.

## Anti-patterns

- **employment penalty** — social-insurance finance lands hardest on entering, low-paid, or care-constrained work.
- **misclassification rent** — firms retain control while avoiding payroll, benefit, and wage-record duties.
- **wage-record erasure** — workers lose benefit credits because the record-capable actor failed to report.
- **benefitless contribution** — contributions are collected without real insurance, portability, or access.
