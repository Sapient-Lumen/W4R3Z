# 158 — Dormancy, bare continuity, and legal maintenance should not be turned into recurring existence tolls

## One-line thesis

A polity may require minimal annual reporting discipline or recover narrow registry costs in some settings, but it should not fund itself by charging **human persons, dormant low-activity entities, or any future rights-bearing artificial persons simply for remaining legible, extant, or lawfully continuous**. Recurring taxation should instead attach to income, wealth, land, rents, transfers, extraction, or external harms rather than to bare continuity itself.[S18][S19][S20][S21][S26][S42][S46][S53][S116]

## Why this matters

The archive already blocks fiscal toll roads at **birth**, **entry**, **exit**, and **cessation**, and it already rejects a machine-upkeep head tax for any future rights-bearing artificial person. But another abuse route stays open if the archive does not say more: the state can simply relabel the same mistake as an **annual franchise minimum, dormancy charge, continuity fee, registry-maintenance levy, or uptime-linked civic due**.[S18][S19][S20][S21][S26][S42][S53][S116]

That kind of charge is morally suspicious when it falls mainly on the fact that the subject still exists or still appears on the books. A zero-income person should not owe civic contribution just for remaining a member of the polity; a tiny dormant firm or co-op should not be pushed into unlawful invisibility because the tax system monetizes mere legal maintenance before any real income, rent, land claim, or external harm appears; and a future artificial person should not face a recurring machine-anniversary or continuity meter for simply staying present as a rights-bearing subject.[S18][S19][S20][S21][S26][S42][S46][S53][S116]

## Design rule

Separate **bare continuity** from **real taxable bases**. Annual filing, registry hygiene, and live-record maintenance may justify narrow, waivable, non-blocking charges in some cases. General revenue should not be raised from the subject's mere ongoing legal or civic existence.[S21][S26][S42][S53][S116]

## Pattern pack

### 1. Distinguish annual reporting discipline from an annual existence toll

A jurisdiction may need current addresses, signer details, status updates, or confirmations that a dormant entity is still dormant. That can justify simple reporting duties and, sometimes, small cost-covering charges. But once the fee is used mainly to raise revenue from bare continuity rather than to cover bounded administration, it has drifted into an annual existence toll.[S21][S26][S42][S53][S116]

### 2. Dormancy is not the same thing as taxable gain

A person or entity can remain legally visible during a thin or zero-activity period without having realized income, captured rent, held appreciable wealth, or imposed external burdens that match the charge. Taxing dormancy itself often confuses the convenience of an annually checkable status with the presence of a real base.[S2][S21][S26][S42][S116]

### 3. Low-scale continuity should not be monetized into informality pressure

When recurring flat minimums bear hardest on low-activity households, micro-firms, co-ops, or associations, they can push lawful but fragile actors off the books precisely because staying compliant costs more than staying real. The archive therefore prefers general-revenue registry maintenance, waiver, thresholding, or activity-linked taxation over annual continuity tolling for thin cases.[S21][S26][S42][S53][S116]

### 4. Real ongoing bases remain available

This note does **not** abolish recurrent taxation. Land value, net wealth, recurring location rent, pollution, congestion, extractive privilege, payroll, consumption, or profitable business activity can still justify annual or periodic taxation because the base is not mere continuation. The archive blocks recurring tolls on bare existence, not taxes on genuine recurring value or harm.[S2][S5][S6][S7][S21]

### 5. Future artificial persons should not face continuity-meter civic dues

If a real rights-bearing artificial person ever exists, baseline ongoing continuity support should stay inside the protected floor, and recurring civic contribution should track ordinary person-side bases above that floor. A machine-only charge on the subject's mere continued presence would simply recreate head-tax logic in periodic form.[S18][S19][S20][S42][S46]

### 6. Dissolution and non-filing remedies should stay distinct from revenue design

A jurisdiction may strike inactive records, require notice before dissolution, or impose proportionate sanctions for deliberate non-filing. But it should not blur those housekeeping tools into a standing annual revenue claim on the fact that a person or entity remains legally continuous.[S21][S42][S53][S116]

## Failure modes

- **calendar-franchise drift** — turning the tax calendar itself into proof that bare continuity is taxable.[S21][S26][S116]
- **dormancy trap** — pricing lawful low-activity continuity so high that fragile actors disappear from the registry or go informal.[S21][S26][S53][S116]
- **annualized head-tax logic** — taxing the continuing subject before identifying any real income, wealth, rent, transfer, or harm base.[S18][S21][S46]
- **machine-anniversary duty** — charging a future artificial person for mere continued legal presence rather than for ordinary person-side taxable bases.[S18][S19][S20][S42][S46]
- **housekeeping-as-revenue camouflage** — calling a revenue instrument a maintenance fee merely because the state already updates the registry once a year.[S21][S42][S53][S116]

## Practical tests

1. If the subject had no income, no transfer, no appreciable wealth, no land claim, and no material externality this period, would the charge still apply just because the subject remained on the books?
2. Could the same administrative objective be served by a simpler filing duty, waiver, threshold, or true cost-covering fee rather than by a revenue-raising flat minimum?
3. Does the charge bear hardest on low-activity or protected-floor cases precisely because it ignores actual activity?
4. For any future artificial person, is the design really taxing ordinary person-side bases above the floor, or is it just rebranding a periodic uptime poll tax?
