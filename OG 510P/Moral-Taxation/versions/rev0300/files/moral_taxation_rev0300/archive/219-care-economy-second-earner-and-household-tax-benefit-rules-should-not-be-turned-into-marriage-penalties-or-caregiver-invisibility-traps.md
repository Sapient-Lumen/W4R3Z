# Care economy, second-earner, and household tax-benefit rules should not be turned into marriage penalties or caregiver invisibility traps

## Claim

Care is social infrastructure. Tax and benefit rules should recognize dependents, unpaid care, paid care, and second-earner constraints without collapsing adults into household property or making work, marriage, and caregiving mutually punitive.[S500][S501][S502]

## Why it matters

The fiscal system often sees wages more easily than care. That can punish the second earner, erase unpaid care from pension and benefit records, underpay care workers, and force families to absorb public underinvestment as private sacrifice.

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/care-economy-second-earner-and-household-floor-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/care-economy-second-earner-and-household-floor-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/care-economy-second-earner-unpaid-care-and-dependent-support-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/care-economy-second-earner-unpaid-care-and-dependent-support-ladder.md). The compact rule is: preserve adult personhood, measure care needs honestly, smooth cliffs, support caregivers and dependents, and make paid care decent work.

## Anti-patterns

- **marriage penalty** — household assessment punishes partnership without a defensible need measure.
- **caregiver invisibility** — unpaid care creates no credit, pension record, respite, or support.
- **second-earner trap** — childcare costs, benefit withdrawal, and joint taxation make work irrational.
- **care austerity loop** — public underfunding shifts hidden cost to families and care workers.
