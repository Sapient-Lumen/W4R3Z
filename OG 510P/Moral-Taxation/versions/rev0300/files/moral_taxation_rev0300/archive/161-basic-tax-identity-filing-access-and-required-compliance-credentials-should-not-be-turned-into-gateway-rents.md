# 161 — Basic tax identity, filing access, and required compliance credentials should not be turned into gateway rents

## One-line thesis

A polity may require bounded proofing, fraud controls, and secure account hygiene, but it should not fund itself by charging people, small firms, or future rights-bearing artificial persons for the **baseline doorway to lawful compliance**. Basic tax identity issuance, ordinary filing-account access, required certificates, or standard machine-readable credentials needed merely to declare, pay, or receive what the system already owes are part of legitimate administration rather than a separate taxable service.[S19][S20][S27][S42][S44][S65][S66][S89][S119][S120]

## Why this matters

The archive already blocks toll roads around **beginning**, **entry**, **continuity**, **exit**, **ending**, **restoration**, and **appeal**. One administrative abuse path remained open: a state can honor those rules in form while still charging subjects for the mandatory account, identifier, key, or filing doorway needed to comply at all.[S27][S42][S65][S66][S89][S119][S120]

That mistake is morally close to the others. When lawful compliance depends on buying the identifier, certificate, or account needed to be seen by the system, the tax order drifts toward charging for **visibility itself** rather than for income, wealth, transfer, rent, or harm. The pressure falls hardest on low-margin firms, low-cash households, migrants, newly formalizing workers, and any subject whose lawful presence is already administratively thin.[S21][S26][S27][S31][S38][S42][S64][S65][S66][S89][S119][S120]

The same logic matters prospectively for any future artificial persons. If a real regime switch ever occurs, a recognized artificial person should not have to rent back the tax identity, filing key, or owner-mediated account credentials needed to comply or contest liability in its own name. Person-side tax visibility must travel with standing, not sit behind a machine-only gateway fee.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S89]

## Archive rule

1. **Do not treat baseline tax identity or filing access as the tax base.** The ordinary identifier, account, or secure access credential needed to comply, pay, or receive refunds is part of tax administration, not a new revenue object.[S27][S42][S65][S66][S89][S119][S120]
2. **Allow bounded proofing and security without gateway monetization.** Identity checks, anti-fraud screening, credential rotation, or secure-device requirements may be justified, but the baseline path should not depend on revenue-raising issuance or renewal charges.[S42][S44][S65][S89][S119][S120]
3. **Optional premium service is not the baseline lane.** Rush handling, extraordinary paper reconstruction, premium support, or bespoke enterprise integrations may justify bounded cost recovery, but baseline filing access and standard compliance credentials cannot depend on buying the premium lane.[S27][S42][S65][S66][S89][S119][S120]
4. **Do not force subjects into paid intermediaries merely to obtain the doorway.** If the only realistic way to get an identifier, filing account, or required certificate is through a paid gatekeeper, the state has recreated a compliance poll tax through the side door.[S27][S38][S42][S65][S66][S119][S120]
5. **Current AI gets no direct subject-side analogue because current AI is not yet a tax person.** For present systems, access credentials and filing interfaces remain controller-side, remitter-side, or regulator-side administrative matters.[S19][S20][S21][S44][S89]
6. **Future artificial persons should not buy their own compliance doorway.** If a real rights-bearing switch ever occurs, baseline tax identity, filing access, and required person-side credentials should arrive as part of standing and ordinary administration rather than through machine-only setup fees, owner-rented keys, or species-specific account tolls.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S89]

## Pattern pack

### 1. Distinguish security proofing from a gateway rent

The archive does not require anonymous tax administration or zero proofing. It requires a different distinction: a **bounded security or identity screen** is not the same thing as a **revenue-raising price on entering the compliance channel itself**.[S42][S44][S65][S89][S119][S120]

### 2. The compliance doorway should not become a head-tax surrogate

Annual minimum fees are not the only way to tax bare legal presence. A required paid identifier, account-renewal charge, or certificate toll can achieve the same effect by making lawful visibility itself costly before any real tax base has been measured.[S21][S26][S27][S38][S42][S65][S66][S89][S116][S119][S120]

### 3. Baseline filing access should be easier to provide than to justify charging for

Modern administrations already build digital identities, online filing, and machine-readable service layers because they lower error and compliance costs when they work well. That is a reason to keep the baseline doorway broadly reachable, not to carve out new micro-rents on basic access.[S65][S66][S68][S77][S89][S119][S120]

### 4. Future artificial persons should not depend on owner-rented credentials

A regime switch would already be unstable if owners kept commercial control while a new subject carried civic duties. It would fail again if the recognized subject could only comply or receive notices through an owner's paid account, token, or certificate. Once personhood is real, baseline tax visibility must attach to the subject's own standing.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S89]

## Guardrails

- no revenue target attached to baseline tax identity issuance, ordinary filing access, or standard compliance credentials as such
- no ordinary renewal fee on the mandatory doorway to lawful filing, payment, or refund receipt
- no practical requirement to buy a private intermediary merely to obtain the baseline account or identifier
- no confusion between optional premium integration and baseline compliance access
- no machine-only setup fee or owner-rented filing key if future artificial personhood ever becomes real

## Failure modes

- **credential rent** — the subject must pay to obtain or renew the basic identifier, key, or filing doorway needed to comply
- **gateway privatization** — the only practical route to basic access runs through paid private intermediaries or proprietary lock-in
- **premium-lane bleed** — optional enterprise or expedited service becomes the only usable ordinary channel
- **visibility surcharge** — the system quietly prices lawful administrative visibility before measuring any substantive tax base
- **owner-rented machine key** — a future artificial person can comply only through a former controller's paid credential or account

## Practical tests

1. Could an ordinary low-cash subject obtain and maintain the basic identifier or filing account without paying a revenue-raising fee?
2. Are security checks screening for fraud, or functioning as a toll on access to the compliance channel itself?
3. Could the subject file, pay, or receive a refund without buying proprietary software or a paid intermediary merely to get in the door?
4. Is the charged service truly optional and premium, or is it the only practically usable route to baseline access?
5. If the subject were a future rights-bearing artificial person, would the rule look like ordinary civic visibility or like an owner-rented compliance leash?
