# Publicly funded research, patent, and open-access rules should not be turned into taxpayer-funded exclusion rents

## Claim

Public grants can coexist with patents, licenses, and publication markets, but the public claim does not vanish at commercialization. The rule fails when taxpayers fund discovery and then face paywalls, inaccessible products, unused patents, or excessive dissemination fees.[S607][S608][S609]

## Why it matters

Research finance is a fiscal instrument even when it is not called tax. Grants, facilities, procurement, and public data create a public return claim that must be balanced with commercialization incentives, research freedom, privacy, and follow-on innovation.[S607][S608][S609]

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/publicly-funded-research-patents-open-access-and-march-in-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/publicly-funded-research-patents-open-access-and-march-in-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/publicly-funded-research-patents-open-access-and-march-in-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/publicly-funded-research-patents-open-access-and-march-in-ladder.md). The compact rule is: make publicly funded knowledge publicly reachable and publicly funded inventions commercially useful without creating exclusion rents.

## Anti-patterns

- **taxpayer-funded paywall** — the public funds research and buys access back.
- **exclusive-license rent** — exclusivity exceeds commercialization need.
- **publication-fee rent** — open access becomes a grant-draining platform charge.
- **unused-patent blockade** — public invention is neither used nor licensable.
