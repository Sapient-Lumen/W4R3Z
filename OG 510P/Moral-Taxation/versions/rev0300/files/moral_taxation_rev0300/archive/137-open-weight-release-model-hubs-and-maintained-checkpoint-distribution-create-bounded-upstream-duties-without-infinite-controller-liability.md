# 137 — Open-weight release, model hubs, and maintained checkpoint distribution create bounded upstream duties without infinite controller liability

## One-line thesis

Publishing weights or checkpoints does **not** make the publisher the controller of every downstream use forever. But maintained release channels, hosted hubs, update control, access gating, ranking, telemetry, revenue-sharing, or safety governance can create **bounded upstream tax and reserve duties** even when deployment-specific liability still belongs mainly to downstream deployers and beneficiaries.[S10][S15][S16][S17][S19][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S53][S54][S83]

## Why this matters

Without a clean boundary rule, open release can be misread in two opposite ways:

1. **infinite creator liability** — the first publisher is treated as the permanent controller of every later use,
2. **open-washing** — a commercial actor sheds all meaningful duty simply by pushing weights, checkpoints, or fine-tune recipes into a public or quasi-public channel.

Both are mistakes. Responsibility should follow the remaining levers of control, benefit, gatekeeping, and risk governance rather than either romanticizing publication or treating publication as magical discharge.[S19][S20][S21][S34][S35][S83]

## Design rule

Distinguish **four positions**.

### 1. Static publication or small research release

A one-off paper, checkpoint drop, or mirror without continuing operational control is usually **not** enough for special controller-side AI tax liability. Tax the publisher under ordinary organizational rules and any direct harms or rents it actually creates, but do not assign endless downstream controller status merely because the artifact remains available.[S19][S20][S21]

### 2. Maintained distribution channel or model hub

A hub, registry, marketplace, or monetized checkpoint distributor that curates discovery, ranks models, collects fees, controls account access, or provides hosted downloads is not automatically the primary deployer. But it can justify **bounded upstream duties** tied to distribution rents, recordkeeping, remittance, incident contribution, or reserve participation because it is an ongoing gatekeeper rather than a dead publisher.[S21][S27][S34][S35][S83]

### 3. Continuing maintainer with update or governance power

Where an upstream actor can push updates, withdraw access, set safety defaults, impose license or usage conditions, approve fine-tunes, or otherwise shape whether risky deployment continues, controller-side obligations remain partly upstream. The degree of liability should track the degree of continuing governance power rather than the symbolic fact of authorship alone.[S15][S16][S17][S19][S20][S53][S54]

### 4. Downstream deployer or concentrated beneficiary

Deployment-specific taxes still belong mainly with the actor that chooses scale, audience, local siting, labor substitution, incident posture, monetization, or extractive business model. Open release does not cancel downstream liability for automation windfalls, local burdens, or high-risk deployment; it only prevents those liabilities from being pushed backward indiscriminately onto every upstream contributor.[S10][S11][S15][S17][S21][S22][S67][S83]

## Practical consequences

- **No permanent universal creator tax.** Static open-weight release does not create endless controller liability for all later uses.
- **No responsibility laundering through release theatre.** Releasing or mirroring checkpoints does not erase duties where the releaser still curates access, updates models, monetizes distribution, or governs risk.
- **Hub duties can be real but bounded.** A maintained hub may be a disclosure, remittance, reserve, or incident-fund anchor without becoming the sole bearer of downstream deployment taxes.
- **Public-interest differentiation still matters.** Small research releases and public-interest science should not be taxed as if they were oligopoly utilities, but frontier-scale commercial distribution channels should not hide behind open rhetoric either.[S10][S15][S21][S27]

## Anti-patterns

- **open-washing** — calling a still-governed commercial release “open” to shed controller duties,
- **author fetish** — treating first authorship as permanent moral control after real operational control has ended,
- **hub blindness** — ignoring continuing distribution rents, access gates, or safety levers because the hub does not run every deployment itself,
- **double counting by role confusion** — charging both the hub and every deployer as if each were the sole primary controller,
- **anti-commons overcorrection** — taxing small public-interest release like a concentrated infrastructure bottleneck.[S10][S15][S19][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S83]
