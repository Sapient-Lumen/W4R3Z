# Municipal bond tax exemption and infrastructure-finance rules should not be turned into private-activity rent or fiscal opacity

## Claim

Tax-exempt municipal finance is not free public money. It is a federal tax preference that lowers borrowing cost through investor tax exclusion, and it must be judged by public purpose, private-use limits, arbitrage controls, post-issuance compliance, debt transparency, and real service access.[S590][S591]

## Why it matters

Municipal bonds can finance essential infrastructure and intergenerational public assets. They can also obscure subsidies to private users, shift costs to ratepayers, and hide long-run debt risk behind lower coupons. The archive needs a route that treats the tax exemption as both infrastructure tool and tax expenditure.[S590][S591]

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/municipal-bond-tax-exemption-and-public-infrastructure-finance-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/municipal-bond-tax-exemption-and-public-infrastructure-finance-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/municipal-bond-tax-exemption-public-purpose-and-post-issuance-compliance-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/municipal-bond-tax-exemption-public-purpose-and-post-issuance-compliance-ladder.md). The compact rule is: public debt privilege requires public purpose, public accounts, and post-issuance proof.

## Anti-patterns

- **private-activity rent** — exemption subsidizes private upside.
- **conduit opacity** — public issuer hides private control or risk.
- **arbitrage bond** — tax benefit funds spread instead of project.
- **debt illusion** — lower coupon hides durable burden.
