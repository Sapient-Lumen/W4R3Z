# Digital gatekeeper, cloud, app-store, and payment-rail routing

## Question in one sentence

When a private digital platform, app store, cloud, wallet, processor, or data rail is the practical path to markets, payments, or civic compliance, how should tax and regulatory design distinguish legitimate cost recovery from access rent?[S460][S614]

## Core rule

A dominant private rail may be a useful collection, reporting, or interoperability anchor, but it cannot become the only purchasable gate to legal compliance, market entry, payment receipt, data portability, or basic exit. Charge rents at the bottleneck only after preserving bypass, portability, privacy, and public fallback.[S460][S614]

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| dominant app store or wallet controls market access | gatekeeper rail gate | require steering, interoperability, nondiscrimination, or rent charge before accepting private tolls. |
| cloud or platform credential becomes the only compliance interface | public fallback gate | standardize public schemas and accept equivalent channels. |
| open-banking or data-portability rule is proposed | data-rights gate | separate consumer control from data-broker extraction and fee shifting. |
| payment rail fee is passed to taxpayers, workers, or small merchants | incidence gate | cost-base the fee and protect basic access. |
| private rail self-preferences its own downstream service | self-preference gate | separate infrastructure function from affiliate rent capture. |

## Anti-patterns

- **private toll constitution** — a dominant rail sells access to what the public system should provide directly.
- **portability theater** — data access exists only through costly or privacy-invasive intermediaries.
- **compliance console lock-in** — filing, remittance, or proof works only through one vendor dashboard.
- **self-preference rent** — gatekeeper fees finance exclusion of rivals or public alternatives.
