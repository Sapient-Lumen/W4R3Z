# Municipal bond tax exemption and public-infrastructure finance routing

## Question in one sentence

When tax-exempt bonds, private activity bonds, conduit finance, arbitrage limits, or municipal disclosure are used, how do we distinguish public infrastructure finance from private rent and fiscal opacity?[S590][S591]

## Core rule

Tax-exempt municipal finance is a tax expenditure tied to public borrowing capacity. It is legitimate when lower borrowing cost funds durable public infrastructure or qualified public-benefit projects with post-issuance compliance, disclosure, and debt-risk transparency. It fails when private activity, arbitrage, conduit opacity, or investor preference does the real work.[S590][S591]

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| public-purpose bond funds essential infrastructure | public-purpose gate | preserve exemption with debt, disclosure, and affordability review. |
| private activity bond funds private operator | conduit gate | require qualified use, public benefit, private-use limits, and subsidy disclosure. |
| bond proceeds are invested before use | arbitrage gate | require yield, rebate, and timely spend review. |
| tax exemption is defended as free money | tax-expenditure gate | disclose federal revenue cost and distribution of investor benefit. |
| project burdens local taxpayers or users | incidence gate | combine debt service, user fees, and service access. |

## Anti-patterns

- **private-activity rent** — tax exemption subsidizes private upside without public benefit.
- **conduit opacity** — public issuer masks private control, risk, or fees.
- **arbitrage bond** — tax benefit funds investment spread rather than project.
- **debt illusion** — lower coupon hides long-run taxpayer/user burden.
