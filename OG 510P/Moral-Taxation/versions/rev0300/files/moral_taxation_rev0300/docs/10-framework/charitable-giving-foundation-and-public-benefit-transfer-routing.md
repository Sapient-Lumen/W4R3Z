# Charitable-giving, corporate-philanthropy, and foundation-pass-through routing

This note answers a question left too implicit between the archive's **beneficiary-side windfall** lane, its **public / nonprofit** lane, and its **proceeds / anti-supplantation** lane: **if a downstream AI winner says the gain is already being given away through charity, corporate philanthropy, foundation grants, donor-advised transfers, or other public-benefit gifting, should the archive still treat that gain as concentrated retained surplus?** The archive's stable answer is **usually yes, but not for the truly irrevocable outside-public-benefit slice**. Voluntary philanthropy is not the same thing as public-service operation, democratic proceeds routing, or broad diffusion. But a transfer that is genuinely outside the group, irrevocable, additive rather than substitutive, and no longer under the donor's private option value can narrow the retained-surplus story for that slice.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S34][S35][S67][S69][S71][S83]

## Core rule

Route these cases in order:

1. **Ask whether the claimed giving is really outside the group.** Do not treat captive foundations, donor-advised shells, affiliate charities, controlled scholarship vehicles, naming-rights conduits, or reversible pledges as proof that the gain has already left private control.[S21][S29][S34][S35][S71][S83]
2. **Ask whether the transfer is genuinely irrevocable and public-benefiting rather than reputational spend.** Binding grants to independent outside charities, public-interest funds, or legally segregated compensation / repair vehicles can matter; branding spend, marketing spend, and board-reversible "community" budgets usually do not.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S69][S71][S83]
3. **Ask whether the giving is additive rather than a substitute for pre-existing duties.** Charity language does not erase worker transition, local repair, controller-side duties, tax liability, or obligations that should have been prefunded or directly paid in the first place.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S69][S71][S83]
4. **Net the story against retained extraction and private recapture.** Claimed giving matters only after testing whether the same group still keeps exceptional margins through buybacks, executive extraction, affiliate fees, donor control, earmarked recapture, or tax-preferred self-dealing.[S18][S21][S29][S34][S35][S67][S71][S83]
5. **Separate outside giving from public-service routing and democratic proceeds routing.** Corporate philanthropy can narrow a retained-surplus story for the truly transferred slice, but it does not turn a private firm into a public body and it does not outrank tax-side proceeds design or anti-supplantation rules.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S69][S71][S83]

## Routing table

| Case | Strongest first answer | Best fiscal shape | Must not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outside, irrevocable grants to independent charities, public-interest funds, or community trusts with no donor recapture and no ongoing exceptional residual on the same slice | ordinary tax plus charitable-transfer review | ordinary profit tax first; beneficiary review only on any retained exceptional residual after the truly transferred outside slice is netted | do not relabel a genuinely outside, irrevocable public-benefit transfer as retained windfall merely because AI is involved.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S69][S71][S83] |
| Firm invokes CSR, community giving, or philanthropy while still posting persistent exceptional margins, buybacks, executive extraction, or affiliate payouts | beneficiary-side screening still applies for the retained residual | ordinary tax first; add bounded beneficiary-side contribution if concentrated gain remains materially undertaxed | do not let donations talk launder a real windfall.[S18][S21][S29][S34][S35][S67][S71][S83] |
| Claimed public-benefit giving runs through captive foundations, donor-advised funds under donor control, affiliate charities, or branded vehicles with soft recapture | anti-fragmentation and substance-over-form routing first | group screening, ordinary profit taxation, and beneficiary review on the real coordinated actor | do not treat private philanthropic plumbing as broad social diffusion.[S21][S29][S34][S35][S71][S83] |
| Transfer is legally required as settlement, restitution, host-community compensation, or other binding repair vehicle | local-repair, cleanup, or proceeds-integrity routing first | direct repair fund, settlement, or segregated trust first; beneficiary charge only on any retained exceptional residual | do not tax amounts already locked to satisfy the very outside duty that the archive says must be paid.[S18][S21][S29][S47][S48][S49][S69][S71][S83] |
| Mixed case with some real outside irrevocable giving and some retained downstream exceptional gain | split the story rather than force one label | ordinary tax plus narrower beneficiary-side charge only on the retained exceptional surplus after real outside giving is netted | do not force all-or-nothing classification when both outside transfer and retained concentration are materially real.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S67][S69][S71][S83] |

## Four routing rules that matter most

### 1. Voluntary philanthropy is not the same thing as democratic taxation or public-service operation

A private firm does not become a public body merely because it gives money away, and private giving does not by itself settle who should decide social priorities. The archive therefore treats philanthropy as morally relevant but narrower than public-service routing and narrower than democratic proceeds routing. What matters is whether a real slice of gain has already been irreversibly transferred outside private control, not whether the donor can describe its spending in benevolent language.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S69][S71][S83]

### 2. The outside, irrevocable slice can matter; the reputational or recapturable slice usually does not

Where an AI-linked windfall is genuinely being sent to independent outside public-benefit institutions or funds, and the donor cannot easily redirect, recapture, or privately monetize the transfer, the archive need not pretend that the entire gross amount remains retained surplus. But marketing budgets, naming-rights trades, captive foundations, donor-controlled vehicles, or grant structures that continue to buy private advantage are still closer to retained use of surplus than to broad diffusion.[S18][S21][S29][S34][S35][S67][S71][S83]

### 3. Charity does not outrank worker, local-repair, controller, or prefunding duties

A downstream winner cannot skip worker transition, local host repair, cleanup, settlement, controller-side reserve duties, or ordinary tax liability by pointing to charitable gifts somewhere else. The archive treats those duties as prior to or separate from discretionary giving. Real philanthropy can narrow the retained-surplus story only after those more directly owed lanes are honored or truly locked in.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S47][S48][S49][S69][S71][S83]

### 4. Mixed cases should net real outside giving, then tax the retained exceptional residual

The archive does not need an all-or-nothing answer. Some AI-linked gain can already be outside private control through real grants, settlements, or independent trusts while another slice remains concentrated private windfall. The right move is often to net only the genuinely outside and irreversible slice, then apply ordinary tax and any beneficiary-side exceptional contribution only to what remains.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S67][S69][S71][S83]

## Anti-patterns

1. **charity-halo laundering** — pointing to modest or strategic giving as if it automatically erased concentrated residual gain,
2. **foundation-loop theater** — routing the story through donor-controlled foundations, affiliate charities, or donor-advised shells and calling it outside diffusion,
3. **substitution cosplay** — using philanthropy to replace worker repair, local repair, cleanup, or ordinary tax duties that should still be paid directly,
4. **reputation arbitrage** — counting naming rights, market access, political goodwill, or procurement advantage as if they were pure public gift,
5. **all-or-nothing forcing** — refusing to admit mixed cases where some slice is truly outside and some remains concentrated residual surplus.[S18][S21][S29][S34][S35][S67][S69][S71][S83]
