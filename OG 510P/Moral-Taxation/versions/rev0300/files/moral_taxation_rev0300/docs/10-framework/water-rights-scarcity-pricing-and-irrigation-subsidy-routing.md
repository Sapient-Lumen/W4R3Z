# Water rights, scarcity pricing, and irrigation-subsidy routing

## Question in one sentence

When water scarcity, water rights, tariffs, tradeable permits, infrastructure finance, or irrigation subsidies enter fiscal design, how do we price scarcity without selling household access or entrenching senior-right rents?[S584][S585]

## Core rule

Water pricing may reflect scarcity, pollution, infrastructure cost, and opportunity cost, but basic household access, ecological minimum flows, and subsistence use must be protected before tradable rights or scarcity charges become revenue tools.[S584][S585]

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| drought or basin stress drives price reform | scarcity gate | set ecological and household floors before market allocation. |
| irrigation receives subsidized water | net-stack gate | disclose subsidy, crop choice, drainage, and opportunity cost. |
| senior rights block new users | anti-hoarding gate | require beneficial use, transfer review, and public-interest limits. |
| utility tariff funds broad infrastructure | affordability gate | lifeline block, hardship rule, and shutoff limits. |
| water-intensive facility receives local tax break | local-burden gate | combine water, power, land, and ratepayer analysis. |

## Anti-patterns

- **scarcity-rent privatization** — public scarcity value becomes private tradable windfall.
- **household exclusion** — pricing reform funds systems by pricing out basic access.
- **irrigation subsidy lock-in** — public water supports low-value or harmful crop choices.
- **paper conservation** — claimed savings lack basin-level verification.
