# Democratic authorization, consultation, and affected voice routing

## Question in one sentence

When a tax, fee, charge, subsidy, or enforcement architecture materially shifts burdens, what democratic authorization and affected-party voice is required before the result can claim public legitimacy?[S562][S563][S564][S565]

## Core rule

A fiscal rule is not legitimate merely because it is technically enacted or administratively convenient. The archive requires visible authority, intelligible reasons, affected-party notice, meaningful participation where design is still open, and a response-to-reasons record before large or floor-sensitive burdens harden.

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| new broad tax or major fee | authorization gate | identify legal authority, delegation limits, public purpose, and who can amend or repeal. |
| burden falls on a concentrated affected group | affected-voice gate | consult before the design is locked; publish what changed and what did not. |
| participation is invited after outcome is predetermined | consent-washing gate | treat consultation as defective and require redesign, narrower phase-in, or review. |
| digital or technical rule obscures political choice | legibility gate | plain-language explanation, distributional note, and accountable sign-off. |
| emergency or delegated fiscal power is used | time-limit gate | sunset, legislative ratification, public report, and post-crisis recalibration. |

## Anti-patterns

- **fiscal theater** — hearings and dashboards exist but cannot alter the fiscal choice.
- **consent washing** — affected communities are asked for comments only after the deal is locked.
- **expert-only legitimacy** — technical models substitute for democratic reasons.
- **delegation drift** — emergency or administrative authority becomes permanent fiscal law.
