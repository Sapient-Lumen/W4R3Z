# Labor tax wedge, platform work, and social-insurance routing

## Question in one sentence

When payroll taxes, social-security contributions, worker classification, platform work, and wage-record duties are at issue, how should the archive fund social insurance without turning ordinary work into the easiest tax base or letting status games erase workers?[S497][S498][S499]

Route: preserve social-insurance funding and wage records, but review marginal labour wedges, worker-status boundaries, platform control, and contribution incidence so ordinary labour is not taxed ahead of rents and so classification arbitrage does not become a private subsidy.[S497][S498]

## Core rule

Payroll and social-insurance contributions are legitimate when they finance real benefits, stabilize worker records, and follow employer/control capacity. They become suspect when they create floor-level work penalties, hide employer-side incidence in lower wages, punish second jobs or self-employment without benefit parity, or let firms buy misclassification rents by shifting records, risks, and contributions onto workers.

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| high marginal tax wedge on low wages | wedge/floor gate | rebate, credit, threshold taper, or proceeds visible as insurance. |
| app or platform controls price, access, rating, discipline, or task allocation | classification/control gate | worker-status review; do not let label override economic reality. |
| worker receives gross platform payments but no wage record | record gate | platform/employer reporting correction and social-insurance record repair. |
| independent professional with real market power | business-capacity gate | ordinary self-employment treatment with benefit and deduction parity. |
| employer shifts contribution through contract terms | non-waiver gate | anti-indemnification and private-ordering limits. |

## Anti-patterns

- **employment penalty** — social-insurance funding lands hardest on entering or low-paid work while rents stay sheltered.
- **misclassification rent** — a firm converts employment duties into contractor labels while retaining practical control.
- **wage-record erasure** — a worker loses benefit credits because the record-capable actor failed to report.
- **benefitless contribution** — contributions are collected without visible insurance, portability, or access.
