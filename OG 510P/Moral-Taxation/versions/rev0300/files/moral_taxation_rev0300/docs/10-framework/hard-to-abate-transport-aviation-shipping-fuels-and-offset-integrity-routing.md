# Hard-to-abate transport, aviation, shipping fuels, and offset-integrity routing

## Question in one sentence

When aviation or shipping carbon rules use fuel standards, levies, offsets, or transition funds, how do we distinguish real decarbonization from offset theater, bunker-fuel loopholes, or mobility-floor harm?[S602][S603][S604]

## Core rule

Hard-to-abate transport rules should price lifecycle emissions, accelerate verified fuel transition, and repair port or climate burdens. They fail when offsets substitute for cuts, fuel labels lack traceability, or island, rural, disabled, or low-income mobility is treated as discretionary luxury.[S602][S603][S604]

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| offset compliance substitutes for operational cuts | integrity gate | require eligibility, additionality, permanence, and no duplicate credit. |
| fuel standard or SAF credit proposed | lifecycle gate | verify full fuel-cycle emissions and feedstock effects. |
| shipping levy or maritime fund proposed | proceeds gate | route revenue to transition, climate repair, and port-community burdens. |
| passenger or freight costs rise | incidence gate | test essential mobility, island dependence, and household pass-through. |
| port air burden appears | local repair gate | pair global climate tools with port-adjacent health repair. |

## Anti-patterns

- **offset theater** — paper credits replace real emissions reduction.
- **bunker-fuel loophole** — mobile fuel choices avoid ordinary jurisdictional responsibility.
- **green-premium rent** — transition label hides incumbent fuel or carrier rents.
- **island-mobility penalty** — climate rule burdens isolated communities without replacement access.
