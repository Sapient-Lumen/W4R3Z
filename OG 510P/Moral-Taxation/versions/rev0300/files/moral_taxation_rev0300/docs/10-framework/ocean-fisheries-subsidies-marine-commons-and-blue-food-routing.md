# Ocean fisheries, subsidies, marine commons, and blue-food routing

## Question in one sentence

When fishing subsidies, access fees, quota systems, or blue-food policies are proposed, how do we separate marine repair and coastal floors from overcapacity, quota capture, and overfishing rent?[S595][S596][S597][S598]

## Core rule

Marine fiscal rules are justified when they rebuild stocks, protect small-scale and coastal food floors, internalize monitoring costs, or repair marine commons. They fail when they finance capacity that chases depleted stocks or convert access to a common resource into incumbent quota rent.[S595][S596][S597][S598]

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| subsidy tied to fuel, vessels, gear, or expansion | overcapacity gate | treat as suspect unless stock status, capacity, and small-scale floor are separately shown. |
| IUU or overfished-stock signal appears | legality gate | withdraw subsidy and route penalties to monitoring and stock repair before revenue extraction. |
| quota or license fees are proposed | rent gate | separate scarcity rent from livelihood protection and prevent paper quota capture. |
| aquaculture is framed as food security | externality gate | test feed, water, disease, habitat, and worker impacts before subsidy. |
| coastal community claims a floor | protected-side gate | use transition support, not incumbent vessel windfalls. |

## Anti-patterns

- **overfishing subsidy** — public money expands effort against stock reality.
- **coastal-livelihood theater** — small-fisher imagery hides large-vessel or quota-holder rent.
- **blue-washing** — marine sustainability language masks habitat, feed, or stock harm.
- **quota capture** — access rights harden into private asset windfalls without public return.
