# Withholding tax, treaty relief, MAP, and double-tax protection routing

## Question in one sentence

When tax is withheld across borders or two jurisdictions tax the same income, what relief route prevents treaty rights and double-tax protection from becoming reclaim rents or indefinite competent-authority queues?[S548][S549][S550][S551]

Route: withholding is legitimate as a collection anchor only if relief-at-source, refund, credit, MAP, and competent-authority channels are real enough to prevent overcollection and durable double taxation.[S548][S550]

## Core rule

The archive distinguishes source-state collection from source-state overholding. Treaty or domestic-law relief that exists only through slow paper forms, intermediary fees, language barriers, or multi-year competent-authority delay becomes a hidden tax on cross-border participation.

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| reduced treaty rate is available but not applied | relief-at-source gate | standardized documentation, intermediary accountability, and refund fallback.[S548] |
| refund/reclaim is slow or expensive | reclaim-rent gate | deadline preservation, interest, fee limits, and small-claim simplification. |
| same income is taxed twice | MAP gate | timely competent-authority access and domestic collection coordination.[S550][S551] |
| transfer-pricing adjustment creates double tax | correlative-relief gate | MAP/APA coordination and no settlement trap. |
| inventory or resolution time worsens | queue trigger | resource, triage, transparency, or arbitration review.[S549] |

## Anti-patterns

- **reclaim rent** — relief exists only if the taxpayer pays intermediaries or waits years.
- **double-tax trap** — two sovereign claims persist without workable coordination.
- **treaty-relief paywall** — treaty entitlement depends on expensive paperwork or custodian cooperation.
- **settlement hostage** — domestic settlement forecloses foreign correlative relief without clear warning.
