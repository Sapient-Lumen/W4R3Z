# Agriculture, food subsidy, nutrition, and rural-floor routing

## Question in one sentence

When agricultural policy uses taxes, exemptions, supports, price controls, insurance, or input subsidies, how do we distinguish food-security repair from producer rent, environmental lock-in, or nutrition-floor harm?[S582][S583]

## Core rule

Food-system tax rules are justified first by nutrition-floor access, resilient production, ecological repair, and fair rural transition. They fail when they hide producer rent, input-subsidy lock-in, land-price capitalization, or consumer food-price harm behind the language of food security.[S582][S583]

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| support raises producer prices | incidence gate | separate farmer income support, land capitalization, and consumer food burden. |
| input subsidy lowers fertilizer, fuel, water, or feed cost | net-stack gate | count environmental harm, import dependence, and lock-in before calling it affordability. |
| food tax exemption or rebate is proposed | nutrition-floor gate | prefer visible low-income nutrition repair when broad zero-rating is leaky. |
| climate or biodiversity conditions attach to farm support | ecological gate | pay for public goods, not baseline compliance or paper practices. |
| rural transition is the claimed purpose | place gate | tie support to small-farmer viability, labor standards, and local resilience. |

## Anti-patterns

- **producer-rent camouflage** — support capitalizes into land or quota value while food access stays weak.
- **nutrition-floor harm** — revenue or price policy raises healthy-food costs for low-income households.
- **input-subsidy lock-in** — cheap inputs preserve harmful production choices and block transition.
- **small-farmer theater** — large producers capture benefits designed around rural hardship imagery.
