# Transport congestion, road pricing, and mobility-access routing

## Question in one sentence

When road pricing, congestion charges, tolls, fuel taxes, or vehicle fees are used, how do we target congestion, emissions, and infrastructure cost without turning basic mobility into ransom?[S586][S587]

## Core rule

Road pricing is strongest when it prices scarce road space, congestion, emissions, crash risk, curb use, or infrastructure wear and visibly funds mobility repair. It fails when it traps low-income, disabled, shift, rural, or transit-poor travelers while replacing ordinary transit funding.[S586][S587]

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| congestion zone or cordon toll | congestion gate | publish baseline, target, exemptions, discounts, and evaluation. |
| fuel tax or carbon rate funds roads | emissions gate | separate carbon, congestion, and pavement wear. |
| toll substitutes for transit funding | proceeds gate | require maintenance-of-effort and visible transit repair. |
| exemptions are narrow or ownership-based | access gate | test disability, emergency, shift-work, and low-income edge cases. |
| cameras and license-plate enforcement expand | data gate | minimize retention and preserve correction path. |

## Anti-patterns

- **mobility ransom** — essential trips are priced without viable alternative or repair.
- **transit substitution** — new tolls replace rather than add public mobility funding.
- **exemption formalism** — emergency, disability, or low-income relief fails on paperwork shape.
- **surveillance tolling** — road pricing becomes unnecessary vehicle-location retention.
