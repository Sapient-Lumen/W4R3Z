# Community supervision, private probation, and monitoring-fee routing

## Question in one sentence

When probation, parole, electronic monitoring, drug testing, treatment programs, jail costs, or private-probation companies charge people under supervision, how should the archive prevent reentry from becoming a fee-funded compliance market?[S516][S517][S518][S537]

Route: supervision fees are presumptively suspect when they finance the apparatus supervising the person, lengthen supervision because of poverty, or sell mandatory compliance services through captive private vendors.[S537]

## Core rule

Community supervision is a public safety and reintegration function, not a customer relationship. Fees for mandatory monitoring, testing, treatment, or supervision require ability-to-pay review, conflict screening, and a no-revocation rule for poverty-based nonpayment.

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| supervision agency keeps fee revenue | conflict gate | replace with appropriations or independent fund. |
| private probation collects from supervisee | captive-vendor gate | ban, cap, or public alternative with fee waiver. |
| electronic monitoring or testing is mandatory | access gate | no markup, ability review, and nonpayment shelter. |
| probation extended for money owed | poverty-revocation gate | cancel or convert; no extension for inability. |
| reentry needs compete with fees | floor gate | prioritize housing, work, treatment, and family support.[S518] |

## Anti-patterns

- **reentry rent** — the path back to civic life is priced as a recurring service.
- **supervision debt trap** — poverty extends supervision or triggers reincarceration.
- **private-probation toll** — a private vendor monetizes court coercion.
- **mandatory-service markup** — required monitoring or testing is sold at captive prices.
