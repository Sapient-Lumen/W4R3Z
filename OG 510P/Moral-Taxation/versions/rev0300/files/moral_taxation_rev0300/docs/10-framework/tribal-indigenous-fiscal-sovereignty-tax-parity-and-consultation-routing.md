# Tribal and Indigenous fiscal sovereignty, tax parity, and consultation routing

## Question in one sentence

When fiscal design touches Tribal or Indigenous governments, members, enterprises, benefits, consultation, or federal-state-local parity, how do we respect sovereignty without creating parity gaps, consultation theater, or paternalistic tax classification?[S592][S593]

## Core rule

Tribal and Indigenous fiscal claims must start from sovereignty, treaty/trust obligations, membership, territorial relations, and self-government rather than treating Tribes as ordinary nonprofits, private businesses, or subnational afterthoughts. Parity with state and local governments is a floor, not a ceiling.[S592][S593]

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| Tribal government or wholly owned entity is involved | sovereignty gate | classify from government status and self-governance before entity form. |
| benefit to members is questioned | general-welfare gate | defer to Tribal determination unless lavish, compensatory, or non-programmatic. |
| federal tax rule gives states/localities a tool but not Tribes | parity gate | treat gap as a design defect needing justification. |
| consultation is claimed | process gate | require early notice, resources, response-to-reasons, and confidentiality protections. |
| project uses Tribal land, resources, or data | proceeds gate | require consent, local repair, revenue share, and non-substitution. |

## Anti-patterns

- **parity gap** — state/local governments get fiscal tools denied to Tribes without reason.
- **consultation theater** — notice occurs after design is fixed or without response.
- **sovereignty bypass** — entity form, vendor contract, or benefit label erases self-government.
- **paternalistic welfare reclassification** — member benefits are taxed by outsider distrust of Tribal need determinations.
