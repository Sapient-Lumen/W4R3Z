# Taxpayer-side AI agent, preparer, reliance, and liability routing

This note covers AI tools used by taxpayers, preparers, software vendors, or agents to prepare returns, answer tax questions, classify facts, route refunds, or communicate with the tax authority. The archive's rule is not that AI advice makes the model a tax person. The rule is that **accountability must stay with the actor who sells, controls, signs, submits, or relies on the tool while taxpayers keep review, correction, and victim-relief paths**.[S197][S198][S201][S654][S655][S656][S657][S658][S659]

## Core rule

Rev0286 current-source note: ghost-preparer guidance treats unsigned/PTIN-free paid preparation as a red flag and keeps the taxpayer legally responsible for the filed return, so the archive treats AI-assisted preparation as an accountability-preservation problem rather than a model-personhood problem.[S659]

A taxpayer-side AI system may assist filing, but it must not become a ghost preparer, refund skimmer, black-box eligibility screen, or liability shield. The design must identify who selected the tool, who controlled prompts/data, who reviewed output, who signed or submitted the return, where refund directions came from, and how errors are corrected.

## Routing sequence

1. **identity** — name the taxpayer, paid preparer, software vendor, firm, and any AI agent involved.
2. **role** — distinguish advice, draft preparation, review, signature, submission, refund routing, and representation.
3. **source traceability** — preserve rule version, forms, facts, assumptions, and citations used by the system.
4. **human review** — require taxpayer or accountable professional review before signature/submission.
5. **refund control** — block model/vendor/preparer control of refund rails unless explicitly authorized and contestable.
6. **reliance shelter** — protect good-faith taxpayers from penalties where they reasonably relied on official/public tools or accountable professionals, while preserving tax correction.
7. **misconduct relief** — use preparer-misconduct, victim-assistance, complaint, and injunction routes when returns are altered, unsigned, or refunds diverted.

## Anti-patterns

- **AI ghost preparer** — a paid system prepares a return but no accountable signer, PTIN, vendor, or professional responsibility trail exists.
- **prompt opacity** — the taxpayer cannot reconstruct the facts, assumptions, or authority behind the filing position.
- **refund rail capture** — the tool or preparer directs refunds to accounts or wallets the taxpayer does not control.
- **liability mirror trick** — every error is pushed to the taxpayer even when the vendor or preparer controlled the erroneous automation.
- **official-looking hallucination** — synthetic citations, fake forms, or stale guidance are presented as authority.
