# Spectrum, orbital commons, auction, and interference routing

## Question in one sentence

When radio spectrum, satellite orbits, filings, auctions, license fees, interference rules, or debris obligations are at issue, how do we charge for scarce commons without creating paper scarcity or incumbent enclosure?[S588][S589]

## Core rule

Spectrum and orbital capacity are scarce public and international resources. Fiscal design may recover scarcity value, coordination cost, interference risk, and sustainability burden, but must preserve public-interest uses, sharing, safety, and equitable access before auction revenue or filing priority becomes the whole answer.[S588][S589]

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| auction assigns exclusive license | scarcity gate | charge rent but retain buildout, sharing, public-safety, and competition duties. |
| satellite filing reserves orbital/spectrum resource | paper-scarcity gate | require use-it, milestone, coordination, and release rules. |
| harmful interference risk rises | interference gate | assign mitigation, monitoring, and compensation to avoidable risk creators. |
| public/unlicensed/community use is displaced | access gate | compare auction yield to digital-inclusion and innovation loss. |
| debris or collision risk is material | sustainability gate | require bond, insurance, deorbit, and tracking duties. |

## Anti-patterns

- **paper satellite** — filings reserve capacity without credible deployment.
- **spectrum hoarding** — license value rises through exclusion rather than service.
- **interference privilege** — powerful users externalize coordination costs.
- **auction myopia** — fiscal revenue defeats public-safety, competition, or access goals.
