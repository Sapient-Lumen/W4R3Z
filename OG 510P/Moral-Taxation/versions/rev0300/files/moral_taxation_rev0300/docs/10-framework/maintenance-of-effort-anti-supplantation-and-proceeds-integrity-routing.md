# Maintenance of effort, anti-supplantation, and proceeds-integrity routing

This note answers the question that comes **after** proceeds look morally well-routed: **are the promised repair, floor protection, local share, or public-capacity gains actually additive, or is the new revenue mostly replacing obligations the state was already supposed to meet?** The archive's stable answer is that visible routing without proceeds integrity is only half a solution. A tax sold as repair, reciprocity, or floor protection should not mainly finance disguised withdrawal elsewhere.[S22][S29][S69][S71][S78][S79]

## Core rule

Route the question in this order:

1. **name the baseline obligation or spending path**,
2. **separate genuinely new repair or sharing from ordinary civic duties**,
3. **require maintenance of effort where the tax is morally defended by visible return**,
4. **allow bounded substitution only when it is disclosed and morally neutral to the defense**,
5. and **review, true up, or claw back if the promised additivity evaporates**.[S22][S29][S69][S71][S78][S79]

A regime does not satisfy reciprocity merely by painting old obligations with new-tax colors.

## Routing table

| Context | What proceeds integrity usually requires | What may still change legitimately | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Broad taxes defended by floor protection | rebates, transfers, or service offsets should be additional enough that protected households are actually better protected after the tax lands | ordinary budget mix may still move if the floor promise remains real and visible | do not fund a rebate with one hand while quietly cutting the same households' support with the other.[S4][S18][S69][S71] |
| Corrective taxes defended by harm repair or local cushioning | exposed households and burdened places should see real net repair, adaptation, or relief | exact delivery channel can shift between cash, service, and infrastructure | do not call the price just because the revenue is nominally tagged while harm-side budgets shrink elsewhere.[S6][S7][S69][S78][S79] |
| Local-share or siting-burden regimes | host communities should see a material local gain that is not immediately neutralized by grant clawback or service withdrawal | equalisation and pooling can still coexist with a local share | do not turn local shares into accounting theater that disappears through offsetting cuts.[S7][S15][S69][S79] |
| AI, automation, or public-input reciprocity taxes | worker transition, oversight, and public-interest capacity should widen real public capability | program mix may evolve as the sector changes | do not relabel ordinary incumbent industrial support as AI reciprocity spending.[S11][S13][S22][S71] |
| Risk-funded bonds, fees, or reserves | cleanup, compensation, and reserve integrity should stay ahead of general budgeting | excess balances may eventually be released by rule | do not raid dedicated risk funding for unrelated pressure once the money is collected.[S16][S17][S53][S54][S71] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Do not sell old obligations twice

If a tax is justified by repair, household cushioning, local reciprocity, or public-capacity building, the state should identify what baseline support already existed and what the new tax actually adds. Otherwise the same dollar is being counted once as ordinary duty and again as moral recycling.[S29][S71][S78]

### 2. The tighter the moral promise, the stronger the maintenance-of-effort rule

Where the tax is sold simply as general revenue, strict anti-supplantation may be unnecessary. Where the tax is sold as visible household protection, local burden-sharing, or remediation for a named harm, stronger maintenance-of-effort discipline is usually part of the moral case itself.[S22][S69][S79]

### 3. Equalisation is compatible with local shares, but not with fake local shares

A locality can receive a genuine host-burden share while wider systems still equalise across places. What is not acceptable is to advertise local reciprocity and then neutralize it through offsetting grant cuts, service withdrawals, or bargaining-side concessions.[S7][S15][S69][S79]

### 4. Public-capacity building must not become incumbent displacement laundering

When revenue is routed toward transition, public compute, public-interest models, or oversight capacity, the archive asks whether public capability really widens. If the money mostly substitutes for incumbent obligations or quietly repays dominant actors for costs they were already meant to bear, the routing defense weakens sharply.[S11][S15][S22][S29]

### 5. Use proportionate anti-supplantation, not rigid silo worship

The archive does not require every proceeds rule to become an inflexible constitutional lockbox. The narrower demand is visible integrity: enough maintenance of effort, baseline disclosure, and review to show that the promised destination is real rather than ceremonial.[S29][S71][S78]

## Anti-patterns

1. **relabelled baseline** — old spending is rebranded as new reciprocity.
2. **grant-offset laundering** — a local share is clawed back through another fiscal channel.
3. **repair theater** — corrective-tax revenue is tagged for harm repair while exposure budgets are cut elsewhere.
4. **capacity mirage** — public-interest proceeds mostly underwrite incumbent costs or procurement favors.
5. **reserve raid** — money collected for cleanup or victim protection is absorbed into unrelated budgeting before the risk is truly closed.[S29][S69][S71][S78][S79]
