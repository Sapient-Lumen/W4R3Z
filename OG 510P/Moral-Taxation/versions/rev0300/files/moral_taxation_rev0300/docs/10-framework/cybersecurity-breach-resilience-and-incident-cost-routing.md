# Cybersecurity, breach resilience, and incident-cost routing

## Question in one sentence

When firms, agencies, or critical-infrastructure operators face cybersecurity duties, breach reporting, or cyber-related levies, how should the archive allocate costs without creating compliance theater or victim-paid recovery?[S615][S616][S617][S618]

## Core rule

Cyber fiscal design should charge preventable negligence, finance shared resilience, and repair victims before rewarding box-checking. Incident reporting is evidence and public defense, not a substitute for backups, supplier oversight, identity recovery, and post-breach audit.[S615][S616][S617][S618]

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| critical infrastructure or tax-data system is exposed | criticality gate | require resilience, reporting, and continuity funding. |
| company reports a material incident | disclosure gate | pair notice with victim repair and control remediation. |
| ransom payment or extortion occurs | ransom gate | route to reporting, law-enforcement, and anti-repeat controls. |
| breach harms customers or taxpayers | victim-repair gate | no paid recovery or credit-freeze tolls for harmed people. |
| security certification is offered as defense | evidence gate | accept it only with live controls and incident performance. |

## Anti-patterns

- **checkbox security** — formal certifications hide unrepaired risk.
- **victim rent** — identity restoration or account recovery is sold back to breached persons.
- **silent externality** — breach costs are shifted to households, hospitals, or public agencies.
- **ransom normalization** — payments become a budgeted cost without resilience changes.
