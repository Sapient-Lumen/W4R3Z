# Public health emergency stockpiles, procurement, and allocation routing

## Question in one sentence

When governments fund stockpiles, standby capacity, emergency procurement, or crisis allocation, how do we prevent panic rents, expired-inventory theater, and unequal access to life-saving supplies?[S605][S606]

## Core rule

Emergency stockpiles are public insurance. They require transparent roles, rotation, surge contracts, allocation priorities, tribal and local access, anti-price-gouging rules, and post-event review; they fail when readiness money becomes vendor lock-in or allocation becomes a lottery.[S605][S606]

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| standby contract or reserve purchase proposed | readiness gate | define inventory, rotation, surge trigger, and public option before paying premium. |
| emergency allocation begins | floor gate | prioritize exposure, medical need, disability, geography, and tribal/local access. |
| supplier raises prices during crisis | panic-rent gate | use price-gouging, open-book, or clawback tools. |
| stockpile expires unused | rotation gate | treat expiration as evidence failure, not inevitable loss. |
| tribal/rural jurisdiction faces access gap | equity gate | predefine routing, logistics, and communication before emergency. |

## Anti-patterns

- **panic rent** — crisis urgency becomes a seller windfall.
- **access lottery** — unclear allocation lets power, geography, or speed decide survival.
- **expired-stockpile theater** — inventory exists on paper but is unusable when needed.
- **vendor lock-in** — readiness contracts block competition or public capacity.
