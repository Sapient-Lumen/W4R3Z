# Child, family-benefit, childcare, and fertility-support routing

## Question in one sentence

When child credits, dependent-care credits, childcare subsidies, family benefits, or fertility supports are proposed, how should the archive protect children and caregivers without claim friction, cliffs, or provider rent?[S277][S625][S626][S627]

## Core rule

Family fiscal policy should start from the child floor, caregiver capacity, work-care compatibility, provider supply, and autonomy. Credits and subsidies fail when nonrefundability, filing barriers, SSN/identity traps, co-payments, cliffs, or provider bottlenecks prevent the intended child benefit from arriving.[S277][S625][S626][S627]

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| family has little tax liability | refundability gate | cash or refundable design before nonrefundable relief. |
| childcare cost blocks work | work-care gate | combine household subsidy with provider capacity and wage standards. |
| co-payment or waitlist rises | access gate | measure actual net childcare cost and provider participation. |
| credit depends on complex filing | automaticity gate | prefill, outreach, and no-rent correction. |
| fertility or marriage bonus is proposed | autonomy gate | center child welfare and caregiver equality, not demographic extraction. |

## Anti-patterns

- **claim-friction child policy** — a benefit exists legally but not practically.
- **provider rent** — subsidies inflate price or finance low-quality scarcity.
- **care cliff** — work or marriage triggers loss larger than earnings.
- **pronatalist extraction** — tax rules pressure births while underfunding children and caregivers.
