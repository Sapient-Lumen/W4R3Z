# Beneficiary-side jurisdiction and claim routing

This note answers a question left open once the archive accepted bounded beneficiary-side windfall contributions: **if a large downstream winner captures concentrated AI-enabled upside without being the controller, which jurisdiction gets the claim?** The archive's stable answer is to follow the **beneficiary's own fiscal home and markets**, keep **local burden repair** visible where local dislocation is real, and reject the idea that upstream server geography, rack location, or model-controller domicile automatically settles the situs of downstream gain.[S3][S9][S10][S11][S13][S14][S21][S22][S28][S67][S69][S70][S83]

## Core rule

Route these cases in order:

1. **Name the object first.** The object here is downstream concentrated gain, not upstream controller rent, facility burden, or model personhood.[S10][S11][S21][S22][S67][S83]
2. **Start with the beneficiary's ordinary fiscal home.** Broad firm- or person-level liability belongs first where the beneficiary is ordinarily taxed as a real resident, member, or grouped business actor, not where the vendor's model happens to sit.[S3][S9][S18][S21][S28]
3. **Add market-jurisdiction sharing when the gain is materially market-created across borders.** If a large downstream winner captures extraordinary surplus through many customer or user markets, a coordinated market-side share can be morally real even when the model controller sits elsewhere.[S3][S9][S21][S22][S28]
4. **Keep local burden claims narrow but visible.** Where layoffs, service strain, housing pressure, or other adaptation burdens land in particular places, those places deserve repair or revenue shares, but that does not by itself turn one locality into the sole owner of the whole beneficiary windfall.[S4][S11][S14][S23][S64][S67][S69][S70]
5. **Group related affiliates before splitting claims.** A coordinated group should not place command in one country and beneficiary gain in another merely to make each jurisdiction's story look partial or uncertain.[S9][S10][S20][S21][S28][S34][S35][S83]
6. **Credit genuine overlap and separate distinct objects.** Controller-side scarce-capacity or facility-burden claims may coexist with beneficiary-side gain claims, but the same surplus slice should not be claimed twice under two labels.[S1][S9][S10][S21][S22][S28][S83]

## Routing table

| Case | Strongest claim | Best tax shape | Must not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary household, worker, or small firm using vendor-hosted AI | ordinary residence / destination rules only | stay inside ordinary income, payroll, and consumption taxation | do not treat common AI use as a cross-border windfall base.[S3][S4][S21] |
| Large domestic beneficiary with concentrated AI-enabled gains | ordinary beneficiary-side home jurisdiction | ordinary profit or income tax first, then bounded beneficiary-side contribution if ordinary taxation still misses the concentrated gain | do not borrow the vendor's server geography as a fake situs for downstream gain.[S10][S11][S21][S22][S67] |
| Large multinational downstream beneficiary selling across many markets | beneficiary home plus coordinated market-jurisdiction sharing for the extraordinary downstream gain | apportion extraordinary beneficiary-side surplus by real market exposure rather than by upstream model location | do not let residence monopoly or server fetish erase market-created claims.[S3][S9][S21][S22][S28] |
| Locality absorbing visible layoffs, retraining load, or service strain from concentrated deployment | burdened locality gets a narrower repair claim or proceeds share | local repair, transition funding, or burden-linked charge alongside broader beneficiary-side taxation elsewhere | do not pretend local harm either owns the whole windfall or counts for nothing.[S4][S11][S14][S23][S64][S67][S69][S70] |
| Coordinated group splitting controller command and beneficiary gain across borders | grouped picture first, then distinct-object allocation across controller-side and beneficiary-side lanes | group before lane choice; then allocate claims with credits or apportionment | do not let one group manufacture stateless surplus by cross-border paper separation.[S9][S10][S20][S21][S28][S34][S35][S83] |

## Three routing rules that matter most

### 1. Upstream model geography does not settle downstream gain

A downstream bank, retailer, insurer, logistics firm, or platform can capture extraordinary automation-linked surplus because of how it reorganizes labor, pricing, or bargaining power around purchased AI. That gain is not automatically taxable where the model vendor's racks sit or where the upstream controller is incorporated. The first broad claim belongs with the downstream beneficiary's own fiscal home and any coordinated market allocation that honestly reflects where the downstream gain is realized.[S3][S9][S10][S21][S22][S28][S67][S83]

### 2. Local burden repair is real but narrower than monopoly over the whole base

Where a plant town, metro area, school system, care network, or labor market absorbs adaptation strain from concentrated AI-enabled reorganization, some local claim is morally real. But local repair is usually a narrower burden-linked share, earmark, or proceeds route, not proof that the entire multinational beneficiary-side surplus belongs to one affected locality.[S4][S11][S14][S23][S64][S67][S69][S70]

### 3. Cross-border beneficiary claims should look like ordinary tax justice, not AI mysticism

The archive rejects special beneficiary-side situs rules based on prompt geography, parameter geography, cloud region, or conversational visibility. Use the same moral grammar already used elsewhere: real residence or grouped business home for broad person- or firm-level claims, destination or market logic where markets co-create the gain, burden-linked shares where places absorb visible costs, and coordinated credits where several claims are simultaneously real.[S3][S9][S10][S21][S22][S28][S69][S70]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **server-fetish spillover** — assigning downstream windfall claims by the location of upstream model hardware or controller domicile alone,
2. **residence monopoly redux** — letting one home state capture the whole beneficiary-side claim even when many markets materially co-create the surplus,
3. **local all-or-nothing rhetoric** — acting as if burdened places either own the entire gain or have no fiscal claim at all,
4. **stateless-surplus choreography** — spreading command, sales, and beneficiary gain across borders until every claim looks incomplete,
5. **ordinary-user contagion** — widening a concentrated beneficiary rule until ordinary users or weak firms are dragged into it.[S3][S4][S9][S10][S20][S21][S22][S28][S34][S35][S67][S69][S70][S83]
