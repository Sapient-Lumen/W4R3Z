# Real-value and indexation routing

This note answers the question that comes **after** a tax base, liability rule, and burden shape look morally plausible but **before** the proposal can count as honest over time: **is the design taxing real gain, real ability-to-pay, and real protective relief, or is inflation quietly doing the work?** The archive's stable answer is that nominal values are not moral values. Where inflation or nominal drift would otherwise change the burden, the system should index, approximate honestly, or openly defend a real tightening rather than letting money illusion decide distributive outcomes in the dark.[S50][S55][S56][S57][S58]

## Core rule

Route taxes, credits, thresholds, losses, basis rules, bonds, and AI or infrastructure triggers through this order:

1. **full real-value treatment where it is administratively workable**,  
2. **public indexation or scheduled re-set rules where full real-value treatment is too costly**,  
3. **blunt but disclosed approximations where a perfect adjustment system would be disproportionate**,  
4. **deliberate non-indexation only where lawmakers openly defend the real tightening or loosening they are choosing**,  
5. and **review whenever inflation, asset-price movement, or nominal cost shifts have made a once-plausible threshold or relief rule morally stale**.[S50][S55][S56][S57][S58]

A proposal therefore fails if it depends on bracket creep, phantom gains, silently decaying loss relief, shrinking credits, or nominally rotting bonds and reserve minima to do work that the law is unwilling to name directly.

## Default routing by base or context

| Base or context | Best real-value rule | Why this rule usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wage and salary taxation | index brackets, allowances, filing thresholds, and low-income credits or re-set them on a public cadence | ordinary earners should not face hidden tightening merely because money prices rose | nominal freezes can become stealth redistribution against households whose real position has not improved.[S55][S57] |
| Broad consumption taxes with rebates | keep floor protection and child / disability support in real value even if the remittance side stays transaction-based | the protective side is part of the same tax constitution | a nominally unchanged rebate can become a real cut that turns a once-just VAT into a floor squeeze.[S3][S4][S21][S55] |
| Capital gains, inventories, depreciation, and other long-horizon capital rules | distinguish real from inflationary gain where feasible; otherwise use explicit approximations or offsets | capital taxation is especially vulnerable to phantom gain and depreciation erosion | partial adjustment that protects one asset class while exposing others can create hidden favoritism.[S50][S56] |
| Loss carry-forwards, refundable thresholds, and downside relief | preserve enough real value that downside recognition still means something after time passes | downside symmetry is defeated if relief decays while the statute remains nominally available | nominal carry-forward without value preservation can privilege incumbents that can use relief quickly.[S50][S51][S56] |
| Land, property, and site-linked charges | keep valuation cadence real and keep nominal caps, thresholds, and equalisation rules publicly updated | immovable bases can often be re-set openly instead of drifting by neglect | stale caps and frozen values can silently create privilege or arbitrary overcharge.[S5][S23][S25][S55] |
| Bonds, cleanup reserves, insurance minima, and decommissioning duties | index or re-set required amounts on a published formula | public protection fails if nominal security decays while real cleanup costs rise | nominal bond rot is a quiet transfer of catastrophic downside back onto the public.[S47][S48][S49][S58] |
| Current AI and data-centre policy | index registry fees, compute thresholds, local-burden triggers, grid-stress charges, and safe harbors, or re-set them by transparent rule | fast-moving infrastructure costs and prices make nominal thresholds unusually unstable | frozen thresholds can become either trivial theater or sudden overreach.[S15][S16][S17][S58] |

## Routing rules

### 1. If the state wants a real tightening, it should usually say so

Leaving nominal thresholds or relief frozen during inflation is often a covert way to raise burdens. The archive prefers explicit legislative tightening or loosening over stealth drift, especially for ordinary workers, caregivers, low-income households, and any protective credit or rebate that is supposed to preserve a floor.[S55][S57]

### 2. Distinguish phantom gain from real enrichment

For assets, inventories, and long-lived capital rules, ask whether the design is really taxing newly created value or mostly inflation. Full inflation adjustment is not always worth the administrative cost, but the archive still prefers honest approximation to pretending that all nominal appreciation is real gain.[S50][S56]

### 3. Preserve the real value of relief, not just liability

Indexation justice is not only about tax brackets. Credits, exemptions, carry-forwards, care support, reserve minima, cleanup bonds, and floor-protecting rebates can all decay in real value. Relief that exists only nominally is a partial repeal by erosion rather than an honest policy choice.[S50][S55][S56][S57]

### 4. Use transparent approximation when full indexation is too much

The archive does not demand perfect inflation adjustment everywhere. It does demand candor. A workable design can use published bands, periodic rather than continuous reset, standardized basis uplift, or approximate indexing formulas when these are cheaper and still morally legible. What fails is silent drift with no named method at all.[S55][S56][S57][S58]

### 5. Review real-value rules where volatility or fast-moving costs make drift dangerous

AI thresholds, facility bonds, registry fees, grid-stress triggers, safe harbors, and local-burden tests can become stale quickly. These should have review cadence, reset triggers, or formula updates rather than remaining frozen until politics happens to notice.[S15][S16][S17][S58]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Bracket-creep governance** — using inflation to raise burdens without admitting a real tax increase.  
2. **Phantom-gain taxation** — treating inflationary appreciation as if it were real enrichment.  
3. **Nominal-relief decay** — leaving credits, loss relief, or rebates nominally unchanged while their protective value melts away.  
4. **Bond and reserve rot** — letting required cleanup or failure-state security become trivial in real terms.  
5. **Threshold theater** — freezing AI, infrastructure, or small-firm thresholds until they become either meaningless or arbitrarily harsh.[S50][S55][S56][S57][S58]

## Order of preference

When lawmakers need a real-value rule, the archive usually prefers the design that is:

1. clearest about what is being held constant in real terms,
2. strongest on preserving the real floor and the real value of downside recognition,
3. most candid about where approximation rather than perfect indexation is being used,
4. hardest to game through selective asset-class favoritism or nominal freeze,
5. and easiest to review once inflation, asset prices, or infrastructure costs have materially moved.

This is why the archive prefers explicit indexation, scheduled re-set, or honestly defended real tightening over nominal drift that quietly changes the tax constitution.
