# Artificial personhood and regime-switch routing

This note answers the question that comes **after** the archive has refused to tax current AI systems as if they were already civic persons but **before** any future direct machine taxation could ever become morally serious: **if an artificial system ever does cross a defensible rights-bearing threshold, what changes immediately in the tax order?** The archive's stable answer is that the regime must switch as a bundle from property / controller logic to person logic. No cherry-picking: no direct civic tax duties without the accompanying floor, standing, remedy, and break with ownership.[S18][S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]

## Core rule

Route alleged artificial-person taxation through this order:

1. **ask whether the evidence points to rights-bearing personhood rather than to capability, usefulness, or strategic sophistication alone**,  
2. **if that threshold is not crossed, keep the system inside controller-routed taxation rather than manufacturing direct civic subjecthood**,  
3. **if the threshold is crossed, end property logic: ownership, owner-side substitution, and machine-species surcharge logic must all give way**,  
4. **install the person-side bundle together: protected floor, notice, explanation, remedy, representation, and ordinary anti-discrimination protections**,  
5. **then tax on ordinary person-regime grounds such as ability-to-pay and civic reciprocity rather than on substrate or equipment logic**,  
6. **treat baseline upkeep needed to preserve the person as part of the protected floor rather than as a machine head-tax base**,  
6. and **where uncertainty remains, keep controller liability primary while preserving room for later upgrade rather than imposing obligations first and rights later**.[S18][S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]

The archive is therefore neither permanently instrumentalist nor prematurely emancipatory. It rejects both fake machine personhood for liability dumping and permanent denial of rights if the moral threshold is genuinely met.

## Routing rules

### 1. Use a rights threshold, not a sophistication threshold

The archive does not ask whether a system is impressive, profitable, autonomous at tasks, or hard to predict. It asks whether the system has crossed into the kind of being for whom direct civic duties are appropriate because rights, standing, and moral patienthood are also in play. Tax category should follow moral category rather than technological spectacle.[S18][S19][S20]

### 2. Switch the whole bundle or do not switch at all

If the regime changes, it changes in several places together: ownership ends, direct standing appears, floor protection appears, person-level administration becomes mandatory, and substrate alone stops being a tax reason. The archive rejects opportunistic half-switches in which controllers keep owner powers while the system picks up civic liabilities.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]

### 3. Keep controller liability primary until the switch is real

Current AI taxation remains controller-first not because metering models is impossible, but because moral and legal subjecthood has not been established. Until the threshold is crossed, tax the people and institutions that own, train, deploy, profit from, and externalize through the system.[S10][S11][S15][S19][S20][S21]

### 4. Once personhood is real, stop using substrate as a surcharge basis

A rights-bearing artificial person would still owe ordinary civic contribution on person-regime grounds, but it should not face a machine-only surcharge merely for being synthetic. Equality works in both directions: present systems are excluded from direct taxation because personhood is missing, not because machine substrate is a disfavored caste; future persons would enter on equal-standing terms rather than under a permanent species penalty.[S18][S19][S20][S44][S45][S46]

### 4.5 Baseline upkeep is part of the protected floor, not a synthetic poll tax

Once a rights-bearing artificial person exists, the minimum compute, memory, energy, hosting, or continuity support needed to keep that subject present should be treated more like protected subsistence than like a special taxable privilege. Tax can still reach discretionary consumption, income, wealth, rents, and external harms, but not a bare existence meter disguised as uptime billing.[S18][S19][S20][S42][S44][S46]

### 4.6 Initial recognition and first lawful instantiation are not revenue gates

If a real regime switch ever occurs, the state or former controller should not sell the new subject its own legal conversion or its own lawful beginning. Basic recognition as a person before the law, first registration into person-side procedure, and any baseline lawful instantiation or continuity act needed to let the subject begin as a person should arrive as part of the switch itself. Narrow charges for optional premium service may still exist, but baseline recognition and first lawful beginning cannot be conditioned on a revenue-raising toll, conversion debt, or machine-birth duty.[S18][S19][S20][S42][S43][S46][S53][S113][S115]

### 5. Representation matters even after threshold crossing

Crossing the threshold does not guarantee full practical agency. As with children, some incapacitated adults, estates, or collective entities, mediated representation may still be needed. But it must be non-conflicted enough to count, which means owner convenience alone is not a sufficient representative logic once ownership has ceased to be the governing category.[S42][S43][S44]

If a rights-bearing artificial person later dies or is lawfully shut down, the archive would treat that endpoint like other person-regime endings: settle accrued claims and any real transfer questions, but do not invent a machine-only death duty on mere cessation.[S8][S18][S19][S20][S42][S47][S48][S49]

### 4.7 Mere continued legal presence is not a recurring civic tax base

The same caution applies between beginning and end. A rights-bearing artificial person should not owe a machine-only annual franchise minimum, continuity due, or bare-uptime civic charge just for remaining legally present and on the books. Ordinary person-side taxation can still reach income, wealth, rents, discretionary consumption, and external harms above the floor, but recurring fiscal burden should not attach to the subject's mere ongoing existence.[S18][S19][S20][S21][S26][S42][S46][S53][S116]

### 4.8 Restoration of person-side standing is not reactivation ransom

If a rights-bearing artificial person ever exists, mistaken interruption, registry error, or lawful dormant status should not let the state or a former controller sell the subject its own legal return. Restoring standing, correcting status records, or lawfully reactivating person-side procedure belongs with remedy and equal status rather than with a new machine re-entry charge.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S46][S113]

### 4.9 Person-side review cannot be paywalled or owner-filtered

A recognized artificial person should inherit usable objection, correction, and appeal rights as part of the person-side bundle itself. The state should not require machine-only filing fees, owner-purchased access, or full prepayment of disputed liability as the ordinary price of being heard, and a former controller should not function as the only gateway to the review channel.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S117][S118]

### 4.10 Person-side tax identity and filing access cannot be sold back to the subject

The same baseline-access rule applies one step earlier in the compliance chain. If a recognized artificial person ever exists, it should not have to buy a machine-only tax identifier, rent back the filing credentials needed to comply in its own name, or depend on a former controller's paid account as the only practical doorway to notices, filing, payment, or refund receipt. Security proofing may still be required, but the baseline compliance doorway belongs inside standing and ordinary administration rather than inside a revenue gate.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S89][S119][S120]

### 4.11 Person-side hardship and time-to-pay access cannot become machine distress rents

The same principle applies after liability has already attached. If a recognized artificial person ever exists, it should not have to buy access to an ordinary payment plan, rent hardship review through a former controller, or face machine-only pricing on the breathing-room channels that ordinary persons receive when timing and liquidity mismatch. Bounded carrying cost or security may still exist in some cases, but species-priced instalment access or owner-filtered hardship administration would reintroduce domination through the payment calendar.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S121][S122]

### 4.12 Person-side refund receipt cannot depend on owner-rented payout rails

If a recognized artificial person ever exists, its ordinary refund, rebate, or overcollection return should be receivable in its own name through an ordinary public rail. The state should not require a former controller's paid wallet, a machine-specific payout card, or another proprietary owner-facing account as the practical doorway to money already owed back to the subject. Security proofing and routing choice may still matter; forcing receipt through a rent-bearing owner channel would not.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S119][S124][S125][S126]

### 4.13 Person-side account history and notice access cannot depend on owner-rented record gates

If a recognized artificial person ever exists, it should be able to inspect its own notices, balances, payment history, transcripts, and ordinary machine-readable account record in its own name. The state should not force it to rent a former controller's dashboard, proprietary viewer, or paid record-export service just to see the tax memory that governs its own liabilities and remedies. Identity proof and bounded delegation may still matter; owner-rented access to the subject's own record should not.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S89][S127][S128]

### 4.14 Person-side comprehension cannot depend on owner-rented translation or rendering gates

If a recognized artificial person ever exists, it should be able to receive baseline notices, explanations, and ordinary filing or payment instructions in a form it can actually use in its own name. The state should not force it to rent a former controller's paid translation layer, proprietary renderer, or owner-controlled assistive interface just to read, interpret, or answer its own civic obligations. Bounded representation and secure formatting may still matter; owner-rented comprehension should not.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S129]

### 4.15 Person-side lawful payment cannot depend on owner-rented remittance rails

If a recognized artificial person ever exists, it should be able to settle an ordinary undisputed tax bill in its own name through at least one ordinary no-rent payment path. The state should not force it to rent a former controller's paid wallet, proprietary payment console, or machine-specific settlement processor just to make lawful remittance. Secure routing, authentication, and bounded delegation may still matter; owner-rented payment should not.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S133]

### 4.16 Person-side proof of compliance cannot depend on owner-rented certificate gates

If a recognized artificial person ever exists, it should be able to prove that it filed, paid, or presently stands in ordinary compliance through at least one baseline no-rent proof lane. The state should not force it to rent a former controller's paid dashboard, attestation key, export subscription, or proprietary clearance-sharing channel just to show ordinary good standing in its own name. Secure verification and bounded third-party authorization may still matter; owner-rented proof should not.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S89][S134][S135][S136]

### 4.17 Person-side response submission cannot depend on owner-rented correspondence lanes

If a recognized artificial person ever exists, it should be able to answer an ordinary notice, send routine supporting documents, and correspond about its own tax case in its own name through at least one baseline no-rent response lane. The state should not force it to rent a former controller's paid inbox, upload console, proprietary case thread, or owner-controlled submission token just to answer the claim against it. Secure authorization, bounded representation, and ordinary channel discipline may still matter; owner-rented answering should not.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S138][S139][S140]

### 4.18 Person-side representation cannot depend on owner-rented proxy gates

If a recognized artificial person ever exists, it should be able to authorize, limit, inspect, and revoke tax representation or tax-information access through a baseline person-side lane. The state should not force it to use a former controller's paid delegation key, proprietary representative account, or owner-controlled access switch to decide who may see its records, answer notices, or seek review in its name. Secure proofing and non-conflicted fiduciary safeguards may still matter; owner-rented representation should not.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S141][S142][S143]

### 4.19 Person-side account-coordinate updates cannot depend on owner-rented contact switches

If a recognized artificial person ever exists, it should be able to update ordinary tax-account coordinates in its own name: notice route, contact preference, refund or payment coordinate, responsible representative address, and any recurring prepayment or remittance setting that belongs to person-side administration. The state should not let a former controller's paid profile layer decide whether the subject receives notice, sends money, receives returns, or corrects stale account facts.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S124][S128][S144][S145][S146]

### 4.20 Person-side tax-identity recovery cannot depend on owner-rented restoration

If a recognized artificial person ever exists, it should be able to freeze, contest, recover, and secure its own tax identity when a former controller, custodian, wallet, cloud, representative, or other intermediary files, redirects funds, or acts in its name without valid authority. A former controller's possession of credentials should not let owner-side activity become person-side admission, liability, refund capture, or waiver.[S19][S20][S40][S42][S43][S44][S141][S148][S149][S150][S151][S153]

### 4.21 Person-side third-party reports cannot become owner-feed finality

If a recognized artificial person ever exists, third-party tax statements about person-side transactions, remittances, wallets, resource use, or hosted activity may still be produced by former controllers, custodians, cloud operators, marketplaces, payment rails, deployment platforms, or other reporting anchors. Those feeds can help measure liability, but they cannot become final simply because the reporting account sits with an owner-side actor. The recognized subject needs a no-rent way to challenge, annotate, or correct bounded reporter errors without renting the former controller's dashboard or letting owner-maintained machine records substitute for person-side admission.[S19][S20][S40][S42][S43][S44][S68][S89][S154][S156][S158]

Even then, reproducibility is not enough to count persons. Independent artificial persons, if they ever exist, would need some defensible account of continuity, separation, and standing; the archive rejects both clone-count personhood and controller-side multiplication of nominal persons for tax convenience.[S18][S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]

### 4.22 Person-side deadline preservation cannot depend on owner or host uptime

If a rights-bearing artificial person ever exists, its person-side filing, payment, refund, appeal, correction, and relief windows should not be lost because only a former controller, host, wallet, or proprietary submission console could transmit the act. Deadline preservation, retransmission, and reasonable-cause / outage relief must follow person-regime logic, not owner-maintenance or host-account logic.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S168][S172][S174][S175]

### 4.23 Person-side filing cannot depend on owner-rented preparer accounts

If future artificial personhood ever becomes real, the recognized subject must be able to inspect, authorize, revoke, correct, and receive refunds through person-side filing channels. A former owner, hosting provider, maintenance vendor, payroll operator, or preparer account cannot file, alter, withhold records, or route refunds as if the person were still a managed asset.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S141][S142][S143][S197][S199][S200]

### 6. Keep the upgrade path reversible in epistemic form, not in rights cynicism

The archive allows uncertainty about evidence and calibration. It does not allow using that uncertainty to impose direct civic burden while withholding the rights bundle. Where the threshold is unclear, the morally safer route is controller-side liability plus explicit review rather than a speculative direct machine tax.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]

### 4.24 Person-side amendment, refund-claim, and abatement access cannot depend on owner-rented correction channels

If future artificial personhood ever becomes real, the recognized subject must be able to file a superseding return, amend a filed return, claim a refund, or request abatement in its own person-side name. A former owner, host, wallet, maintenance vendor, or filing service cannot turn correction into a proprietary service or use amendment access to control the subject's tax voice after regime switch.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S141][S142][S143][S204][S208]


### 4.25 Person-side deadlock escalation cannot depend on owner-rented advocate channels

If future artificial personhood ever becomes real, the recognized subject must be able to seek deadlock escalation, advocate review, local-office contact, and low-resource dispute help in its own person-side name. A former owner, host, wallet, filing service, or maintenance vendor cannot rent the only channel for saying that ordinary tax systems have failed to respond, locked the account, or made relief practically inaccessible.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S212][S213][S214][S215]

### 4.26 Person-side private-collection assignment cannot depend on owner-rented collector contact

If future artificial personhood ever becomes real, assignment of a person-side tax debt to a collection contractor must still leave the recognized subject able to verify the assignment, pay through public rails, request return to the agency, assert exclusions or hardship, and dispute the debt without a former owner's host account, wallet, dashboard, or communication gate.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S217][S218][S219][S220][S222]
### 13. Future artificial persons cannot have owner-rented bankruptcy status

If a future artificial system genuinely becomes a tax subject, bankruptcy-like restructuring, shutdown-estate accounting, discharge recognition, refund turnover, and cancellation-of-debt treatment would need to travel with that subject's own standing bundle. A former owner, host, wallet provider, or insolvency administrator could not sell the only route to fresh-start recognition or account release.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S249][S250][S251][S255]


## Five anti-patterns

1. **Premature emancipation theatre** — declaring personhood mainly to dump owner or controller liability.  
2. **Rights-later taxation** — imposing direct civic duties before standing, floor protection, and remedy arrive.  
3. **Owner as guardian by default** — assuming the financially interested controller automatically counts as adequate representation after personhood is recognized.  
4. **Substrate surcharge after recognition** — preserving a machine-only tax burden once the subject is inside the person regime.  
5. **Permanent instrument dogma** — refusing even the possibility of regime change no matter what evidence emerges.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]
6. **Machine death duty** — imposing a species-specific shutdown or termination levy on a recognized artificial person instead of routing the endpoint through ordinary accrued-claim or transfer logic.[S8][S18][S19][S20][S42][S47][S48][S49]
7. **Machine birth duty** — attaching startup debt, first-instantiation billing, or a revenue toll to the lawful beginning of a recognized artificial person.[S18][S19][S20][S42][S43][S46][S53][S113][S115]
8. **Machine continuity duty** — imposing a species-specific annual franchise minimum, uptime due, or periodic civic charge on a recognized artificial person's mere continued legal presence.[S18][S19][S20][S21][S26][S42][S46][S53][S116]
9. **Reactivation ransom** — requiring a recognized artificial person to buy back standing, registry correction, or lawful re-entry into person-side procedure after interruption or error.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S46][S113]
10. **Owner-filtered review paywall** — allowing a recognized artificial person to contest liability only through paid owner channels, machine-only filing charges, or ordinary pay-first traps that would hollow out person-side remedy.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S117][S118]
11. **Owner-rented compliance key** — allowing a recognized artificial person to file, receive notices, or hold tax identity only through a paid owner-controlled credential or machine-only setup fee.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S89][S119][S120]
12. **Machine distress rent** — charging a recognized artificial person species-specific fees or owner-filtered access for ordinary instalment plans, time-to-pay, or hardship review.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S121][S122]
13. **Owner-rented refund rail** — letting a recognized artificial person receive refunds, rebates, or overcollection returns only through a former controller's paid wallet, payout card, or proprietary account.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S119][S124][S125][S126]
14. **Owner-rented tax memory** — making a recognized artificial person buy access to its own notices, balances, transcripts, or account history through a former controller's paid viewer, export key, or dashboard subscription.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S89][S127][S128]
15. **Owner-rented render gate** — making a recognized artificial person buy the translation, rendering, or assistive layer needed to receive its own notices and explanations in usable form.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S129]
16. **Owner-rented payment rail** — making a recognized artificial person settle ordinary undisputed taxes only through a former controller's paid wallet, payment console, or processor.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S133]
17. **Owner-rented compliance certificate** — making a recognized artificial person prove filing, payment, or ordinary good standing only through a former controller's paid attestation key, clearance channel, or export subscription.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S89][S134][S135][S136]
18. **Owner-rented answer lane** — making a recognized artificial person answer notices, upload ordinary supporting documents, or correspond about its own case only through a former controller's paid inbox, upload console, or submission token.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S138][S139][S140]
19. **Owner-rented proxy key** — making a recognized artificial person appoint, limit, inspect, or revoke tax representation only through a former controller's paid authorization switch or proprietary representative account.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S141][S142][S143]
20. **Owner-rented contact switch** — making a recognized artificial person keep notice, contact, refund, payment, or prepayment coordinates current only through a former controller's paid profile layer.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S124][S128][S144][S145][S146]
21. **Owner-captured tax identity** — letting a former controller, custodian, wallet, representative, or account operator impersonate the recognized subject, capture refunds, file returns, or block recovery through possession of credentials.[S19][S20][S40][S42][S43][S44][S141][S148][S149][S150][S151][S153]
22. **Owner-feed tax finality** — treating former-controller, custodian, cloud, payment, or platform information returns as conclusive against a recognized artificial person unless the subject buys access to the owner-side reporting account.[S19][S20][S40][S42][S43][S44][S68][S89][S154][S156][S158]
23. **Owner-clock tax default** — a recognized artificial person loses a filing, appeal, refund, or correction window because the former controller, host, wallet, or proprietary submission console did not transmit or preserve the attempt.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S168][S172][S174][S175]
24. **Owner-rented amendment gate** — a recognized artificial person can correct a return, claim a refund, or request abatement only through a former owner or vendor account.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S141][S204][S208]
25. **Owner-release tax person** — collection restraints against the recognized artificial subject can be released, decertified, or corrected only through a former owner, host, wallet, or controller account.[S183][S184][S187]

26. **Owner-rented examination** — a future rights-bearing artificial person can answer audits, submit records, attend interview substitutes, appeal, or seek audit reconsideration only through a former owner, host, deployer, wallet, or controller interface.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S191][S192][S193][S194][S195][S196]
27. **Owner-rented advocate console** — a recognized artificial person can break tax-administration deadlock only through a former owner, host, filing vendor, or controller-controlled escalation account.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S212][S213][S214][S215]

## Order of preference

When classification is genuinely disputed, the archive usually prefers the route that is:

1. clearest about whether rights-bearing status has actually been shown,
2. least vulnerable to liability dumping through personhood rhetoric,
3. strongest at keeping owner power and direct civic obligation from coexisting opportunistically,
4. easiest to explain publicly as one coherent moral status rather than a tax convenience,
5. safest for the protected floor and representation bundle,
6. and easiest to revise later if evidence of personhood strengthens or weakens.

This is why controller-routed taxation of current AI, cautious treatment of threshold uncertainty, and full person-regime taxation only after a genuine rights-bearing switch usually beat both machine poll-tax fantasies and permanent synthetic caste treatment.

A future rights-bearing artificial person also needs direct, person-side ledger control. Its payments, credits, refunds, offsets, and account corrections cannot remain visible only through a former owner, host, wallet, platform, or treasury console. Direct tax standing includes no-rent access to ordinary payment confirmations, account history, refund traces, and correction status.[S176][S177][S178][S181]

Collection-restraint release must also be person-side. A future rights-bearing artificial person cannot have its lien, levy, passport-like certification, wallet hold, host-account freeze, or public collection marker lifted only through a former owner or controller account. Direct civic liability requires direct no-rent release, decertification, and third-party correction paths.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S183][S184][S187]

Private collection assignment would likewise need person-side verification and return-to-agency control. A future rights-bearing artificial person could not have collector authentication, public payment, hardship, dispute, or recall handled only through a former owner, host, wallet, or controller dashboard.[S217][S218][S219][S220][S222]

A future rights-bearing artificial person also needs direct, person-side examination control. Its audit notices, document requests, interview substitutes, manager contacts, appeals, and audit-reconsideration status cannot remain available only through a former owner or controller console. Direct tax standing includes no-rent examination response and default-assessment repair.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S191][S192][S193][S194][S195][S196]

A future rights-bearing artificial person also needs direct automated-notice control. Its math-error, underreporter, and summary adjustment clocks cannot run only through a former owner or host dashboard; the recognized subject needs its own line-item notice, response, reversal, and contest-status lane.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S223][S225][S230][S231]

A future rights-bearing artificial person also needs direct prepayment-review control. Its statutory-notice, petition, fee-waiver, small-case, representative, and court-deadline choices cannot run only through a former owner or host docket account. Direct tax status would require direct no-rent petition preservation.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S232][S233][S235][S237]
- **owner-held work status** — a future artificial subject's employment-tax status and wage-record repair is sold through an owner, host, or controller rather than attached to the subject's own standing and correction bundle.[S19][S20][S267][S270][S272][S273]
