# International coordination and apportionment routing

This note answers the question that comes **after** identifying several morally real jurisdictional claims and **before** arguing over treaty text or exact formulas: **when should jurisdictions coordinate, and how should they split claims without turning coordination into rich-state monopoly or local abandonment?** The archive's stable answer is to use club coordination as a **floor and sharing device** for mobile rents, borderless profit, and race-prone bases, while preserving visible claims for burdened places, market jurisdictions, and poorer states that would otherwise be priced into dependency.[S1][S2][S3][S5][S9][S15][S23][S28][S69][S70]

## Core rule

Use international or club coordination when all five of these conditions are substantially true:

1. **the base is mobile enough that one jurisdiction cannot hold it alone without large escape or distortion,**
2. **several jurisdictions have morally real partial claims,**
3. **booking, residence, or physical-presence formalities can be gamed cheaply,**
4. **a coordinated floor or apportionment rule would reduce both no-tax space and destructive overlap,**
5. **and weaker jurisdictions would otherwise be forced into underpricing, dependency bargains, or tax competition they cannot survive.**

A coordination regime is good when it limits race-to-the-bottom pressure, keeps local-burden claims visible, gives markets a real share where they co-create profit, prevents residence or booking monopolies, and avoids building a club that merely redistributes taxing power upward into already strong states.[S1][S2][S3][S9][S15][S28][S69][S70]

## Default coordination map by problem

| Cross-border problem | Default coordinated instrument | Why this instrument usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Broad cross-border consumption | destination-based VAT / GST rules with mutual administrative co-ordination | final use is easier to justify morally than origin booking for consumption | do not let origin logic or seller location distort who really bears the tax.[S3] |
| Large multinational residual profit and mobile rents | minimum-tax floor plus coordinated market-jurisdiction allocation | mobile profit can outrun any single state, while markets still co-create value | do not let residence or booking jurisdictions keep the whole claim just because they host paper entities.[S1][S9][S28] |
| Frontier AI controller groups spanning clouds, markets, and facilities | national controller-side taxation layered with club reporting floors, anti-shifting rules, and bounded market / host sharing | present AI rents are highly mobile, but burdens and market-created value remain split across places | do not build AI co-ordination around fake model personhood, server fetish, or pure host-state monopoly.[S10][S15][S16][S17][S28][S70] |
| Carbon leakage, extractive arbitrage, and other race-prone transition bases | club floors with visible household protection and poorer-state accommodation | unilateral action alone can leak activity, but pure delay leaves harms untreated | do not turn climate or transition clubs into tariff shells that ignore justice for poorer jurisdictions.[S1][S2][S69] |
| Weaker host or poorer jurisdictions facing dominant investors or infrastructure operators | revenue-sharing, equalisation, model agreements, and anti-underpricing floors inside the co-ordination regime | bargaining asymmetry can make formally voluntary tax competition coercive | do not use "co-ordination" to trap weaker states as permanent dependents on siting deals or rich-state transfers.[S2][S5][S15][S23][S69][S70] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Use club floors as floors, not monopolies

The archive treats coordinated minima as ways to stop erosion and undercutting, not as reasons to erase local, market, or poorer-jurisdiction claims. A good club floor stops the race to the bottom **without** making all taxing rights flow upward to the most powerful residence states.[S1][S2][S9][S28]

### 2. Apportion by the hardest-to-fake morally relevant factors

When exact tracing becomes legal theatre, use disclosed apportionment by destination sales, users, payroll, tangible footprint, extractive situs, or similarly harder-to-fake factors. Prefer imperfect honesty to fake precision built on intangible booking stories.[S2][S3][S9][S15][S28]

### 3. Preserve burdened-place claims before sharing the remainder

If a locality or host region bears grid strain, water stress, land pressure, congestion, cleanup risk, or transition cost, it should not wait passively for distant treaty formulas. Coordination should sit **on top of** direct host claims for place-bound burden, not erase them.[S5][S15][S23][S69][S70]

### 4. Give markets a real claim where customers or users co-create value

For borderless or highly digital business models, market jurisdictions deserve a coordinated claim over part of residual profit even when old physical-presence tests fail. The archive treats customer and user markets as morally relevant, not as optional afterthoughts.[S3][S9][S28]

### 5. Protect poorer jurisdictions from dependency bargains

A co-ordination regime fails if poorer or weaker jurisdictions are asked to surrender present taxing rights in exchange for vague future transfers, precarious siting promises, or rich-state discretion. Floors, revenue-sharing, technical assistance, and equalisation should reduce coercion rather than reorganize it.[S2][S5][S15][S69][S70]

### 6. Keep AI coordination controller-first and choke-point based

For present AI systems, coordinated regimes should run through clouds, utilities, controller groups, deployers, registries, and major beneficiaries rather than through direct model personhood. International coordination is justified where AI rents and avoidance are mobile, but that does not change the archive's basic subjecthood rule.[S10][S15][S16][S17][S19][S20][S28]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Residence monopoly** — powerful home states keep the whole mobile-rent claim.
2. **Booking theatre** — apportionment is replaced by intangible accounting stories and shell geography.
3. **Local abandonment** — host communities lose visible claims once a treaty exists.
4. **Dependency club** — poorer jurisdictions are asked to trade real taxing rights for conditional promises.
5. **AI server fetish** — taxing rights are assigned mainly by data-centre or server location where control, market access, and burden plainly point elsewhere.[S1][S2][S9][S15][S28][S69][S70]

## Order of preference

When several cross-border designs are plausible, the archive usually prefers the one that:

1. preserves direct host claims for place-bound burden,
2. gives market jurisdictions a disclosed share where markets co-create value,
3. sets a floor under mobile-rent competition rather than a ceiling over all claims,
4. uses harder-to-fake apportionment factors before intangible source stories,
5. and leaves poorer jurisdictions with durable fiscal agency rather than permanent bargaining dependence.

This is why the archive prefers destination logic for broad consumption, market-jurisdiction shares plus minimum-tax floors for mobile profit, and controller-side AI co-ordination that sits alongside local burden charging and national controller taxation rather than replacing them.
