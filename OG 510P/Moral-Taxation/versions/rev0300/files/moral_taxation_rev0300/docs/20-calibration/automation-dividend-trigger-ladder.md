# Automation-dividend trigger ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that special AI levies need an exceptional trigger, **when should automation-linked windfall sharing activate rather than leaving the gain inside ordinary taxation alone, and when should it attach controller-side versus concentrated-beneficiary-side?**[S11][S13][S14][S22][S67][S83]

The constitutional question is already settled: **ordinary taxation remains the default, and a special automation-dividend contribution is justified only when concentrated automation-linked gain is real enough to name and meter honestly. Most such contributions will still attach controller-side, but some should attach beneficiary-side when downstream winners capture the gain without controlling the stack.**[S11][S13][S14][S21][S22][S67][S83]

## Why calibration is still needed

The closed rule now names two distinct but neighboring possibilities:

1. a **controller-side** automation dividend where the same actor governs the stack and captures the gain, and  
2. a **beneficiary-side** automation dividend where concentrated downstream winners capture labor-displacing or bargaining-power gains through outsourced stacks.

A usable ladder has to avoid three failures at once:

- **false positives** where every visible AI deployment is treated as windfall-rich even when margins are thin or downside remains genuine,
- **lane confusion** where concentrated beneficiary gain is mislabeled as controller duty or controller-side burden is dumped onto beneficiaries merely because they are richer-looking,
- and **false negatives** where unusually large productivity gains are kept while payroll, bargaining power, entry-level opportunity, or local resilience weaken and ordinary taxation does not catch up quickly enough.[S11][S13][S14][S20][S21][S22][S50][S51][S67][S83]

## Small option set

### Option A — ordinary-tax default only

Use ordinary profit, labor, consumption, land, and corrective taxes only. Do **not** activate a dedicated automation-dividend contribution unless the evidence for concentrated automation-linked gain is weak, mixed, too early, or too noisy to separate from ordinary productivity variation.[S1][S9][S11][S21]

### Option B — disclosure-and-review watchlist

Do not levy yet, but require a covered controller group, concentrated beneficiary class, or sector to publish a compact review packet when at least **two** signals move together over a review window:

- output, revenue, or deployment scale rises materially,
- payroll share, covered headcount, or entry-level hiring weakens,
- operating margins or residual profits rise,
- concentration or bargaining asymmetry visibly strengthens.[S11][S13][S14][S16][S17][S67]

This option is appropriate where displacement signals are visible but still too uncertain for immediate fiscal activation or where the right lane is not yet clear.

### Option C — provisional controller-side band

Activate a **bounded controller-side contribution** when at least **three** conditions hold over a named review window, with ordinary downturn and accounting noise screened out:

1. controller-side output, revenue, or deployment scale rises,  
2. labor share, headcount mix, or entry-level opportunity falls in a way plausibly linked to automation,  
3. margins, residual profits, or strategic bargaining power rise rather than merely staying flat,  
4. and the gain is not already being broadly recycled through worker-sharing, stronger wage floors, or comparable public-return channels.[S11][S13][S14][S22][S50][S51][S67]

Use this lane when the morally central object is still **controller-side command over the productive stack, scarce capacity, deployment rent, or facility-linked burden**. Attach the contribution to an auditable controller-side aggregate such as excess margin band, excess profit band, or a similarly reviewable gain measure, not to tokens, prompts, or raw inference throughput.[S15][S16][S17][S21][S22]

### Option D — provisional beneficiary-side band

Activate a **bounded beneficiary-side contribution** when at least **three** conditions hold over a named review window and the gain is visibly downstream rather than primarily at the model controller:

1. a large downstream firm, platform, or utility captures unusual margin expansion, labor-displacing surplus, or bargaining-power compression,  
2. the actor relies materially on AI or automation supplied from elsewhere but does **not** thereby become the real controller,  
3. ordinary profit, minimum-tax, inheritance, land-rent, or competition tools are not yet reaching the concentrated gain well enough,  
4. and a controller-side levy by itself would miss the main downstream surplus.

Before settling on this lane, group related affiliates and sister entities enough to make sure the apparent downstream winner is not just one slice of a coordinated controller-beneficiary group that has split command and surplus across paper boundaries.[S9][S10][S11][S13][S14][S20][S21][S22][S28][S34][S35][S67][S83]

Use auditable beneficiary-side aggregates such as margin bands, payroll-compression indicators, beneficiary revenue bands, or similarly reviewable gain measures. Reject seat counts, prompt counts, and generic model-use frequency as stand-ins for windfall.[S10][S15][S16][S17][S21][S22][S67]

For cross-border settings after this lane is chosen, use [`beneficiary-home-market-and-local-burden-claim-split-ladder.md`](beneficiary-home-market-and-local-burden-claim-split-ladder.md) rather than trying to smuggle server geography back in as the situs of downstream gain.

### Option E — strong windfall / concentrated-rent band

Use a stronger band only when Option C or Option D is already satisfied **and** one or more intensifiers are present:

- the gain is concentrated in bottleneck firms or controller groups,
- local labor-market or service burdens are visible,
- public-input reciprocity concerns are unusually strong,
- or ordinary tax and competition tools are visibly lagging the concentration event.[S10][S11][S15][S22][S67][S70]

This is not a universal AI surcharge. It is a temporary or review-bound rent-sharing band for concentrated automation windfalls.

## Lane-selection rule

Choose the narrowest lane that matches the visible object:

1. **Group related affiliates first** when the apparent controller-side and beneficiary-side stories sit inside one coordinated ownership or command structure; lane choice should follow the real grouped picture, not paper separation alone.[S9][S10][S20][S21][S28][S34][S35][S83]  
2. **Controller-side first** when the relevant gain still travels mainly with stack command, scarce capacity, deployment rent, or facility burden.  
3. **Beneficiary-side instead** when a downstream winner captures the extraordinary surplus while outsourcing the stack and without becoming the real controller.  
4. **Allow both only for genuinely different objects** — for example, controller-side scarce-capacity draw and downstream labor-displacing surplus capture — and run an explicit duplication check before stacking.[S1][S9][S10][S21][S22][S28][S83]  
5. **If the lane is unclear, stop at the watchlist first** rather than pretending confidence or inventing a universal user tax.[S15][S16][S17][S21][S67]

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt a **four-stage ladder**:

1. **baseline ordinary taxation** by default,  
2. **mandatory disclosure / watchlist** when two trigger families move together over the review window,  
3. **bounded automation-dividend contribution in the correct lane** — controller-side or beneficiary-side — only when three or more trigger families cohere and ordinary tax / governance has not already captured the gain adequately,  
4. **stronger but still review-bound rent-sharing band** only when concentration, local burden, or public-input intensifiers are also present.[S11][S13][S14][S15][S16][S17][S21][S22][S67][S83]

That recommendation is the archive's narrowest workable setting because it does five things at once:

- preserves the archive's ordinary-tax default,
- requires windfall evidence rather than AI branding,
- chooses between controller-side and beneficiary-side lanes rather than collapsing them,
- uses reviewable aggregates rather than fake precision,
- and leaves room to intensify only when concentration and public burden are both clearer than in the baseline case.

## Default trigger-and-review matrix

| Stage | Minimum showing | Fiscal consequence | Required companion rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. ordinary-tax default | no coherent windfall evidence yet | no special levy | keep ordinary taxes, competition scrutiny, and labor-floor review active |
| 1. watchlist | two signal families moving together over the review window | disclosure, measurement, and earlier review cadence | publish rule version, covered entities, proxies, and error bounds.[S16][S17][S44] |
| 2a. controller-side contribution | three signal families plus evidence that stack command or controller-side rent is where the gain mainly sits | bounded controller-side automation-dividend band | route proceeds to worker transition, local repair, public capacity, and floor protection rather than general incumbent subsidy.[S15][S22][S67][S68] |
| 2b. beneficiary-side contribution | three signal families plus evidence that concentrated downstream beneficiaries capture the gain and controller-side taxation would materially miss it | bounded beneficiary-side automation-dividend band | keep ordinary users and small firms out, group related affiliates before lane choice, run duplication checks, and publish why the controller lane is not enough.[S9][S10][S11][S20][S21][S22][S28][S34][S35][S67][S83] |
| 3. strong band | stage 2a or 2b plus concentrated rent / local burden / public-input intensifier | stronger but still review-bound contribution | add explicit sunset or conversion path and stronger incidence checks.[S59][S60][S61][S62][S63] |

## Anti-patterns

- **Gross-activity theater** — taxing visible AI throughput as if it were windfall even when margins are absent.[S15][S50][S51]
- **Payroll drop alone** — treating labor contraction by itself as enough without checking where gains and losses actually land.[S11][S13][S14]
- **Controller tunnel vision** — assuming every automation-dividend case must be controller-side even when the extraordinary surplus is mostly downstream.[S20][S21][S22][S67][S83]
- **Beneficiary drift** — sliding from concentrated-beneficiary design into broad taxes on ordinary use.[S10][S11][S21][S22][S67]
- **affiliate split-screening failure** — choosing the lane from paper entity boundaries before checking whether one coordinated group is splitting command and surplus across affiliates.[S9][S10][S20][S21][S28][S34][S35][S83]
- **Permanent emergency mode** — activating a temporary contribution without review cadence, conversion rule, or exit path.[S59][S60][S61][S62][S63]
- **Deficit-fill drift** — routing proceeds into general incumbent subsidy loops instead of transition, floor protection, local repair, or public-interest AI capacity.[S15][S22][S67][S68]
- **Worker-side pass-through blindness** — imposing the levy while ignoring incidence on wages, prices, weak suppliers, or tenants.[S4][S21]

## What would change the recommendation

This ladder should be tightened, loosened, or replaced if any of the following become clearer:

- a better public measure of automation-linked gain than the current margin / labor-share / concentration family,
- reliable evidence that ordinary corporate, rent, inheritance, land, and competition tools are already capturing most of the gain,
- strong evidence that controller-side and beneficiary-side lanes can be split more cleanly or should in practice merge,
- strong evidence that the levy is predictably passing through to protected workers or low-income households,
- or a shift from ordinary transition stress toward catastrophic or non-compensable risk, in which case governance and gating should dominate taxation.[S15][S17][S21][S53][S54][S67]
