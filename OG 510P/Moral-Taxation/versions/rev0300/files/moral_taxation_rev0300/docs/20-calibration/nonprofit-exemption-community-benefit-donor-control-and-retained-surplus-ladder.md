# Nonprofit exemption, community benefit, donor control, and retained-surplus ladder

## Question in one sentence

What ladder should decide whether nonprofit tax exemption or charitable deduction is a legitimate public-benefit transfer, a retained-surplus subsidy, or private-benefit laundering?[S483][S484][S485][S486]

## Option scan

| Option | Shape | Archive verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A — full exemption/deduction | ordinary charity treatment. | Accept when public benefit and non-private-benefit operation are clear. |
| B — capped deduction or credit | reduce distributional skew. | Use when giving incentive is valid but upside is concentrated. |
| C — payout or deployment rule | require timely public use of subsidized assets. | Default for warehousing risk. |
| D — excise/UBIT/recapture | tax private benefit, unrelated business, or failed public use. | Adopt where exemption perimeter is breached. |
| E — denial/revocation | remove exempt status. | Required for substantial private benefit, inurement, or mission failure. |

## Ten-gate ladder

1. **organizational gate** — verify exempt purpose, governing documents, non-inurement, and limits on political/private activity.[S483]
2. **operational gate** — test actual activities, beneficiaries, prices, access, related-party flows, compensation, and mission fit.
3. **public-benefit gate** — identify broad community benefit, direct beneficiary access, and measurable public value rather than prestige or donor preference.[S483]
4. **hospital gate** — for hospitals, test CHNA, financial assistance policy, emergency-care policy, charge limits, billing/collection practices, and Schedule H transparency.[S484][S485]
5. **donor-control gate** — test donor-advised funds, naming rights, advisory control, family employment, related parties, and circular grants.
6. **warehouse gate** — compare assets, endowment, DAF balances, payout, spending rate, crisis needs, and reason for retention.
7. **distributional gate** — assess whether deduction value flows mostly to high-income donors and whether a credit, cap, floor, or nonitemizer rule better fits.[S486]
8. **market-competition gate** — apply UBIT, taxable subsidiary, or subsidy netting where nonprofit form competes as an ordinary firm.
9. **instrument gate** — choose exemption, capped deduction, credit, payout rule, excise tax, UBIT, recapture, denial, or revocation.
10. **review gate** — trigger review after persistent low payout, charity-care shortfall, collection abuses, related-party transactions, or mission drift.

## Default settings

| Posture | Instrument | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| active public charity | exemption/deduction | public benefit and transparency. |
| donor-controlled warehousing | payout, cap, excise, or delayed deduction | no subsidy without public deployment. |
| nonprofit hospital | exemption conditional on community benefit and 501(r) compliance | charity care and billing protections. |
| market-like activity | UBIT or taxable subsidiary | prevent unfair private competition. |
| private benefit/inurement | recapture, excise, denial, or revocation | no reputational laundering. |

## Rev0299 accountability map

Rev0299 separates genuine public benefit from mission-form capture, donor recapture, and endowment lockup.

| Question | Default accountability answer |
|---|---|
| Who controls the exemption? | The exempt entity board, donor controller, foundation or DAF sponsor, related-party contractor, and exemption authority. |
| Who benefits? | Donors, insiders, related parties, endowment managers, or exempt entities that keep tax-expenditure value while community benefit remains thin. |
| Who should not be blamed by default? | Patients, students, charity beneficiaries, ordinary taxpayers, and local residents who finance or need the public-benefit floor. |
| What evidence is decisive? | Community-benefit outputs, charity care or access records, donor-control files, related-party transactions, payout ledgers, and substitution against public services. |
| Default move | Condition exemption, require payout or disclosure, and claw back private benefit where public-benefit evidence fails. |
