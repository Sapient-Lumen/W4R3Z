# Channel pluralism, access independence, and fallback ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that **ordinary tax and relief should not depend on one bank, one app, one device, one vendor, or one private platform relationship**, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when digital-primary design is acceptable, when public fallback must mirror the private rail, when protected payments require materially different receipt paths, and when temporary exclusivity can be tolerated without turning a convenience channel into the real tax constitution?**[S21][S27][S31][S39][S41][S44][S45][S46][S64][S65][S66][S68]

## Closed rules invoked

This memo does **not** reopen the archive's constitutional waist. It relies on:

The constitutional question is already settled: **private rails may help collect, report, verify, or deliver, but ordinary civic tax life should stay reachable through plural, portable, publicly accountable channels rather than a single captive intermediary.**[S21][S27][S39][S41][S44][S45][S46][S64][S65][S66][S68]

## Why calibration is still needed

The closed rule settles direction, but not yet the smallest usable operating ladder. A workable calibration still has to avoid six failures at once:

1. **fallback theater** where a paper, phone, or assisted path exists in theory but carries slower clocks, weaker correction rights, or higher proof friction than the digital-first rail,[S27][S39][S41][S65][S66][S68]
2. **vendor-made constitution** where paid software, one payroll vendor, one marketplace dashboard, or one identity stack becomes the real legal interface for ordinary tax life,[S21][S27][S39][S41][S44][S68]
3. **exit equals erasure** where job exit, account closure, device loss, landlord conflict, or platform suspension quietly wipes out notice, appeal, or refund access,[S21][S31][S39][S45][S46][S64][S65][S66]
4. **relief lag behind extraction** where taxes can be collected automatically from many rails but refunds, rebates, or corrections can flow back through only one brittle channel,[S21][S31][S39][S64][S65][S66][S68]
5. **proprietary re-proof loops** where the same fact must be reproved because each channel or vendor insists on its own evidence format,[S27][S39][S41][S44][S64][S65][S68]
6. and **temporary exception hardening** where a narrow security or transition exception quietly becomes the permanent administrative constitution.[S27][S39][S41][S59][S60][S61][S62][S63]

## Small option set

### Option A — single exclusive rail

Require one bank, one vendor suite, one portal, one payroll app, or one identity provider for the ordinary path. This is the archive's worst default because it externalizes civic access risk onto the weaker side and turns private market exit into quasi-legal exclusion.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S44][S45][S46][S64][S65][S66][S68]

### Option B — digital-first with nominal hardship exceptions

Keep one dominant digital route and offer paper, phone, or in-person paths only as special hardship exceptions. This is better than Option A, but it is still too weak as a general rule because an exception lane is often slower, more suspicious, and more burdensome than the ordinary path.[S27][S39][S41][S65][S66][S68]

### Option C — plural-channel ladder with portability and public fallback

Let administrations or remitters use efficient digital and private rails, but require a real public fallback, portable proofs, outside-the-anchor correction, and more than one receipt path where protected payments or ordinary exits make single-channel dependence too fragile.[S21][S27][S31][S39][S41][S44][S45][S46][S64][S65][S66][S68]

This is the archive's cleanest default because it preserves operational efficiency without letting convenience channels become exclusive civic gatekeepers.

### Option D — universal maximal duplication

Mirror every channel in every format at all times, regardless of scale, stakes, or user profile. This is too strong as a universal rule because some limited channel concentration is harmless when a real public fallback, portability, and fast correction already exist.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S44][S68]

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt a **five-rung channel ladder**:

| Rung | When it should apply | Channel answer | Main guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. ordinary public-open path | the obligation is routine and can be handled with simple public forms or a public portal | **public digital route plus assisted nonproprietary fallback** | fallback must keep the same substantive rights, not become a penalty lane |
| 1. digital-primary ordinary administration | a portal is the easiest ordinary path, but the subject is an ordinary filer or claimant rather than a large specialized actor | **digital-first is acceptable only if phone, paper, or assisted correction and notice remain genuinely usable** | slower, costlier, or suspicion-heavy fallback is not real fallback |
| 2. private-anchor mirror | employer, platform, bank, utility, or vendor is the strongest collection or reporting anchor | **use the anchor, but guarantee outside-the-anchor statements, correction, and portable exit** | private remittance anchor is not an exclusive civic gatekeeper |
| 3. protected-flow multi-rail | refunds, rebates, floor-protecting transfers, or hardship relief are being delivered | **offer materially different receipt paths, including at least one that does not depend on continuous app / bank / job / platform access** | extraction must not have more reliable rails than repair |
| 4. temporary exclusivity exception | a brief security transition, migration event, or unusual large-actor interface justifies tighter channel control | **allow bounded temporary exclusivity only with sunset, hardship lane, portability plan, and explicit return to ordinary pluralism** | do not let exception labels harden into permanent lock-in |

The archive's provisional setting is therefore **C as the general rule, with Rung 1 for ordinary digital-first administration, Rung 2 for remittance anchors, Rung 3 for protected payments, and Rung 4 only for narrow temporary exceptions**.[S21][S27][S31][S39][S41][S44][S45][S46][S59][S60][S61][S62][S63][S64][S65][S66][S68]

## Typical placements

| Context | Default rung | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ordinary household filing and notice | Rung 1 | portal-first design can work if paper, phone, and assisted correction stay equally real.[S27][S39][S41][S65][S66][S68] |
| payroll withholding and wage-side true-up | Rung 2 | employer or payroll software may anchor remittance, but the worker needs outside-the-employer access after job exit or retaliation risk.[S21][S31][S39][S45][S46][S65][S66] |
| platform withholding or marketplace remittance | Rung 2 | the platform can remit, but suspension from the dashboard should not erase tax standing or appeal access.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S68] |
| refunds, rebates, and negative-tax or repair payments | Rung 3 | protected-side delivery fails if a missing bank account or app login becomes the hidden eligibility rule.[S31][S64][S65][S66][S68] |
| controller-side AI reporting | Rung 2, sometimes Rung 4 | structured vendor tools can help, but open schemas and multiple compliant submission routes should usually outrank one mandated suite; any temporary exclusivity needs sunset.[S16][S17][S27][S39][S41][S44][S68] |
| emergency migration away from a compromised system | Rung 4 | brief tighter control may be justified, but only with explicit return to plural ordinary access.[S27][S39][S41][S59][S60][S61][S62][S63] |

## Anti-patterns

- **hardship-only fallback** — the non-digital route exists only as an exceptional plea path.[S27][S39][S41][S65][S66]
- **statement captivity** — the subject cannot get a durable record outside the employer, platform, or vendor dashboard.[S21][S31][S39][S41][S45][S46]
- **single-rail repair** — refunds or rebate corrections can move only through one brittle channel even though collection used several.[S21][S31][S39][S64][S65][S66][S68]
- **vendor-specific proofing** — the same entitlement or liability fact must be reproved because each submission tool demands its own format.[S27][S39][S41][S44][S64][S65][S68]
- **exceptional forever** — a temporary exclusive rail never gets sunset, substitute access, or public justification.[S27][S39][S41][S59][S60][S61][S62][S63]

## What would change the recommendation

Tighten, loosen, or partly replace this ladder if:

- strong evidence shows some categories of digital-first administration preserve equal substantive access even with lighter fallback than this memo assumes,
- interoperable public credentials and open schemas make portable correction much easier across banks, platforms, and vendors than current practice,
- or secure public disbursement rails become strong enough that protected payments can reliably avoid bank, payroll, or app dependence without creating new exclusion elsewhere.[S27][S39][S41][S44][S45][S46][S64][S65][S66][S68]
