# Frontier AI host / market / controller jurisdiction ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rules that **place-bound burdens deserve place-bound claims, destination or market access can justify a real fiscal claim even without old-style physical presence, and controller-side or club layers should close escape rather than monopolise every tax base**, **what is the smallest workable ladder for splitting claims among data-centre host localities, national controller states, and customer-market jurisdictions in frontier AI without opening new no-tax space or pretending that one jurisdiction alone morally owns the whole base?**[S3][S9][S10][S15][S28][S69][S70]

The constitutional part is already settled: **keep local burdens visible, keep market access visible, keep controller-side cleanup real, and use club co-ordination as a floor-and-sharing device rather than as a residence-state monopoly or a reason to erase host and market claims.**[S3][S9][S10][S15][S28][S69][S70]

## Why calibration is still needed

The closed rules settle the direction, but not yet the smallest operating ladder. A usable calibration still has to avoid six failures at once:

1. **server-location fetish** where the whole claim is assigned to the host facility merely because chips and racks sit there, even when customers, controllers, and booked profits sit elsewhere,[S10][S15][S28]
2. **market erasure** where customer or user jurisdictions are treated as morally irrelevant unless they can prove old-style physical presence,[S3][S9][S28]
3. **residence monopoly** where the controller state treats minimum-tax or corporate-tax administration as a reason to keep the whole mobile-rent claim,[S9][S28]
4. **local abandonment** where data-centre host communities bear land, housing, grid, water, congestion, or cleanup burdens while being told to wait for national or treaty generosity later,[S10][S15][S69][S70]
5. **overlap panic** where fear of double or triple counting is used to deny all but one claim rather than to split different bases honestly,[S3][S9][S28]
6. and **dependency bargains** where weaker host or poorer jurisdictions are pushed into underpricing, abatement races, or one-off siting deals because no durable claim ladder has been made explicit.[S9][S15][S69][S70]

## Small option set

### Option A — controller / residence primacy

Let the main controller or residence state take most meaningful tax claims, leaving hosts to property taxes and markets to ordinary VAT alone. This is administratively tempting, but it understates place-bound burden and lets powerful home states monopolise rents that clearly depend on distant customers and burdened localities.[S3][S9][S15][S28][S70]

### Option B — host primacy

Let the facility host take the lead claim over most AI-related revenue because the compute sits there. This corrects local abandonment, but it quickly becomes a **server-fetish** answer when cross-border profits and user markets are much wider than the facility footprint.[S10][S15][S28][S70]

### Option C — market primacy

Let customer or user jurisdictions take the dominant claim over AI profits wherever the service is sold or used, with hosts and controller states mostly limited to ordinary domestic taxes. This defeats the old physical-presence fiction, but it can erase genuine host burdens and leave controller states with too little incentive or capacity to close escape.[S3][S9][S28]

### Option D — base-specific stacked claims with a club floor

Split the answer by **what is actually being taxed**: keep local situs and burden charges local; keep destination-style consumption claims market-side; assign a coordinated market share of very large residual profit where markets materially co-create value; let controller or residence systems take the cleanup claim over the remaining mobile rent subject to credits and floors; and use club coordination to stop no-tax gaps and destructive bargaining, not to wipe out the earlier layers.[S3][S9][S10][S15][S28][S69][S70]

This is the archive's cleanest option because it refuses to answer a multi-base problem with a one-jurisdiction slogan.

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt a **five-lane practical ladder**:

| Lane | When it should apply | First claim | Second claim / overlay | Main guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. ordinary separated-base lane | the taxable base is already clearly tied to one fact pattern | **keep each ordinary base where it already belongs**: local property / land at situs, payroll where workers are taxed, destination consumption in the market | only add another layer if a different morally real base is being taxed | do not answer cross-border AI by pretending every claim is the same claim |
| 1. host-burden lane | the facility imposes visible land, housing, grid, water, congestion, resilience, or cleanup burdens on a locality | **burdened host locality gets the first direct claim** through land-value tax, property tax, utility / congestion charging, bonding, or explicit host-burden levies | national minima, anti-abatement rules, and equalisation can sit above it | host revenue should track real burden, not become a bargaining chip that operators can waive away |
| 2. market-access lane | the system sells into, intermediates, or monetises a customer / user market at scale without old-style physical presence | **market jurisdictions get the consumption claim and, for very large groups, a coordinated residual-profit share** | use destination rules and coordinated market allocation rather than gross local turn-over layering everywhere | market claims should focus on final use and large residual rents, not become a pile of overlapping local sales taxes |
| 3. controller-cleanup lane | residual profit remains highly mobile, booked away from host and market, or easier to collect through the parent / controller group | **controller or residence state takes the cleanup claim** through corporate-income tax, minimum-tax rules, controller-side levies, registry duties, or bonded obligations | credit or carve around earlier host and market claims so the cleanup layer closes gaps rather than crowds them out | controller-side administration is a backstop and coordination point, not proof that hosts and markets never had a claim |
| 4. club-floor and equalisation lane | competition, bargaining asymmetry, or cross-border escape would otherwise hollow the earlier lanes out | **club floors, information sharing, anti-shifting rules, model agreements, and revenue-sharing sit on top** | stronger states should also support weaker hosts and poorer jurisdictions through sharing and equalisation rather than dependency bargains | the club must act as a floor-and-sharing device, not a residence-state monopoly or host-erasure machine |

The archive's provisional setting is therefore: **host first for place-bound burden, market first for destination consumption and coordinated market-created residual profit, controller-side states next for cleanup over the remaining mobile rent, and club coordination last as the anti-escape floor and sharing overlay.**[S3][S9][S10][S15][S28][S69][S70]

That is the narrowest workable recommendation because it separates bases that are often rhetorically collapsed into one. It also means that frontier AI does **not** call for a single magical situs test. A data-centre host may deserve land-rent, grid, water, congestion, and bonding claims without owning all cross-border profit; markets may deserve destination and residual-profit shares without taking the whole corporate base; and controller states may need to collect the cleanup layer without using administrative convenience to erase the others.[S3][S9][S10][S15][S28][S70]

## Anti-patterns

- **Server fetish** — assigning most profit claims to the place where racks sit simply because the hardware is visible.[S10][S15][S28]
- **Residence monopoly** — treating controller-state administration as a moral reason to keep the whole mobile-rent claim.[S9][S28]
- **Market erasure** — acting as if users and customers create no taxable claim unless there is bricks-and-mortar presence.[S3][S9][S28]
- **Host abandonment** — pushing all local burden into discretionary subsidies, side payments, or hopeful future transfers.[S15][S69][S70]
- **One-base rhetoric** — pretending host burden, final consumption, and residual profit are one thing so only one jurisdiction can matter.[S3][S9][S15][S28]

## What would change the recommendation

This ladder should be tightened, loosened, or partly replaced if any of the following become clearer:

- reliable market-jurisdiction allocation becomes far easier to administer for very large AI groups, making lane 2 stronger relative to lane 3,[S9][S28]
- AI facilities become much less place-burdening than current hyperscale expectations suggest, shrinking the moral range of lane 1,[S10][S15][S70]
- host-jurisdiction bargaining asymmetry worsens, strengthening the case for national anti-abatement floors, mandatory host-compensation formulas, or stronger club sharing,[S15][S69][S70]
- or club minimum-tax and co-ordination regimes start to crowd out visible host and market claims rather than protecting them, in which case the archive should tighten guardrails against residence monopoly and dependency bargains.[S9][S28][S69][S70]
