# Review-trigger, conversion, and sunset ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that uncertain, emergency, negotiated, or fast-moving taxes need visible review architecture rather than inertia, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when ordinary periodic review is enough, when named trigger review is required, when reauthorisation or conversion burdens should apply, and when a tax or tax-side support package should sunset, decommission, or be replaced rather than drift on?**[S15][S54][S59][S60][S61][S62][S63]

The constitutional question is already settled: **a morally serious tax should publish a falsifiable purpose, separate routine review from trigger review, allow outsiders to contest the official success story, and name in advance how the instrument can tighten, loosen, convert, sunset, or be replaced.**[S15][S54][S59][S60][S61][S62][S63]

## Why calibration is still needed

The closed rule settles direction, but a practical regime still has to decide **how strong the exit discipline should be for different kinds of taxes and tax-side supports**. That remains live because review design still fails in five recurring ways:

1. **calendar complacency** where annual or multi-year review exists on paper but no event ever forces reconsideration when incidence, concentration, inflation, or local burden shifts quickly,[S15][S59][S60][S61]
2. **sunset theater** where a formal expiry exists but renewal is automatic, evidence-light, or negotiated by the same incumbents who benefited from the rule,[S59][S60][S61][S63]
3. **temporary-to-permanent drift** where emergency taxes, experimental AI levies, or bespoke support packages survive because no conversion burden was named at launch,[S15][S54][S59][S60][S63]
4. **review without real predicates** where policymakers name outputs like receipts but not the factual conditions that justified the policy in the first place, such as concentrated rent, utility burden, worker transition, or risk tolerance,[S15][S54][S61][S62]
5. and **decommission delay** where systems or supports that now exceed tolerated risk, fail their distributive purpose, or no longer fit the market structure remain live because repeal or shutdown was treated as politically exceptional rather than as one ordinary review outcome.[S54][S61][S62][S63]

## Small option set

### Option A — calendar-only review

Require periodic reporting or a statutory review every few years, but do not name hard trigger events, conversion burdens, or exit defaults. This is administratively familiar and politically easy, but it is the structure most vulnerable to inertia because the hardest cases are precisely the ones that move faster than the calendar.[S59][S60][S61]

### Option B — universal hard sunsets

Force almost every tax or support instrument to expire unless affirmatively renewed. This can block drift, but it also destabilises mature broad-base taxes and routine floor protection that should instead be kept current by indexation, ordinary monitoring, and occasional deeper review rather than by recurring extinction threats.[S59][S60][S61][S63]

### Option C — dual-clock review with staged exit burdens

Use ordinary monitoring plus deeper periodic review for all instruments, add named trigger review where novelty, concentration, or distributive sensitivity is material, and escalate toward reauthorisation burdens, conversion tests, or hard sunsets only for emergency, negotiated, experimental, or rapidly changing instruments.[S15][S54][S59][S60][S61][S62][S63]

This is the cleanest general default because it keeps stable mature taxes out of needless expiry theater while still treating high-novelty or bargaining-heavy instruments as presumptively temporary unless they earn a more durable home.

### Option D — strong expiry presumption for exceptional and negotiated instruments

Start from Option C but add a rebuttable presumption that emergency taxes, negotiated tax expenditures, bespoke AI-facility packages, and other factual-predicate-sensitive measures should expire on schedule unless an evidence packet justifies conversion into an ordinary standing rule.[S15][S54][S59][S60][S61][S63]

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt a **five-stage practical ladder**:

| Stage | When it should apply | Review answer | Main guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. mature ordinary lane | broad, long-settled taxes and transfers with observable bases and no exceptional factual predicate | **routine monitoring plus deeper periodic review** | use indexation and ordinary legislative revision, not recurring sunset threats |
| 1. trigger-disciplined lane | policies with meaningful incidence drift, proxy drift, inflation sensitivity, or local-burden sensitivity | **routine review plus named early triggers** | publish the few events that force reopening before the calendar says so |
| 2. conversion-test lane | targeted supports, negotiated abatements, pilot credits, simplified regimes, or actor-specific concessions | **time-bounded review with a conversion burden** | the beneficiary or sponsor must show why the instrument belongs as standing policy rather than as a fading exception |
| 3. exceptional-expiry lane | emergency taxes, experimental AI levies, novel corrective packages, or fast-moving capability-sensitive rules | **hard sunset or reauthorisation default** | renewal requires an evidence packet and a stated path to tighten, loosen, convert, or replace |
| 4. decommission / replace lane | the factual predicate has failed, hidden offsets now cancel the burden, risk tolerance is exceeded, or the instrument persistently misses its distributive target | **sunset, repeal, shutdown, or move out of tax into another governance tool** | treat exit as an ordinary success of review architecture, not as a last-resort embarrassment |

The archive's provisional setting is therefore **stage 0 for mature broad-base taxes and durable household floor protection; stage 1 where burden drift, proxy drift, or local burden can move faster than ordinary revision; stage 2 for targeted or negotiated tax-side support; stage 3 for emergency, experimental, and AI-linked rules whose factual predicate may move rapidly; and stage 4 whenever the core justification has materially weakened or the instrument now fails by its own stated criteria**.[S15][S54][S59][S60][S61][S62][S63]

This means the archive should **not** demand hard sunsets for ordinary wage withholding, broad VAT with working rebates, or mature land taxes merely to look serious. Instead it should reserve the strongest expiry discipline for rules that are novel, bargained, concentrated, or justified by a temporary predicate. Conversely, it should stop pretending that a bespoke data-centre package, emergency levy, or experimental compute charge can live indefinitely on the same footing as a mature base simply because some reporting table exists.[S15][S59][S60][S61][S63]

For present AI taxation, the operational consequence is straightforward: **controller-side levies, registry charges, or support packages tied to frontier capability, grid stress, incident rates, concentration, or public-risk tolerances should usually begin in stages 2 to 3 rather than in stage 0, and they should ship with explicit monitorable triggers plus a named conversion or shutdown path.** NIST's AI RMF materials are especially relevant here because they treat ongoing monitoring, periodic review, risk-tolerance recalibration, and safe decommissioning as normal governance duties rather than as extraordinary admissions of failure.[S54][S62]

## Anti-patterns

- **Review with no burden of proof** — everyone can say the instrument was “reviewed,” but no one must show why it still deserves to exist.[S59][S60]
- **Permanent pilot** — a temporary or experimental rule is repeatedly extended without conversion into an ordinary regime or honest repeal.[S59][S60][S61]
- **Expiry panic for mature bases** — lawmakers destabilise settled broad taxes and automatic floor protection with recurring sunsets that create noise rather than accountability.[S59][S63]
- **Trigger inflation** — so many triggers are named that none has force and every review becomes discretionary.[S60][S61]
- **No-off-ramp AI governance** — model or facility rules track deployment and collection but not drift, incidents, external feedback, or decommissioning.[S54][S62]

## What would change the recommendation

This ladder should be tightened, loosened, or partly replaced if any of the following become clearer:

- administrations become substantially better at real-time public monitoring of incidence, support offsets, and local burdens, making stage-1 trigger review more reliable and lowering the need for blunt sunsets,[S59][S60][S61]
- evidence shows that a supposedly temporary or negotiated instrument has become a genuinely general, rule-like part of the fiscal constitution rather than an incumbent bargain, in which case stage 2 or 3 should convert toward stage 0 or 1,[S59][S60][S63]
- the underlying technology, market concentration, or public-risk profile changes faster than expected, in which case stage 3 or 4 treatment should become more common for AI-linked instruments,[S15][S54][S61][S62]
- or repeated renewal practice reveals that nominal sunsets are functioning as empty ritual, in which case the archive should strengthen reauthorisation burdens and independent challenge rights rather than merely shortening the clock.[S59][S60][S61][S63]
