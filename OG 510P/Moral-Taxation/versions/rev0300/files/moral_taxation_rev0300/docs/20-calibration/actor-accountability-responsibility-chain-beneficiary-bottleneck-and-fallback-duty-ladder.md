# Actor-accountability ladder: responsibility chain, beneficiary, bottleneck, and fallback duty

Use this ladder when a proposal identifies a tax, fee, mandate, penalty, subsidy, private rail, AI stack, platform, backstop, or reporting duty but does not yet say **which actor is accountable for which moral job**.

## Ladder

| Level | Condition | Default move | Blocked shortcut |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 — no accountability issue | The statutory taxpayer, burden bearer, beneficiary, and collecting channel are the same actor, and no protected floor is implicated. | Ordinary route review. | None. |
| 1 — remitter/conduit split | One actor remits or reports while another bears the cost or receives the value. | Preserve remitter duties but trace incidence and remedy to the real burden bearer or beneficiary. | Compensation to remitter; conduit punishment. |
| 2 — bottleneck channel | A private platform, bank, payroll vendor, insurer, app store, telecom carrier, or tax-software rail controls access to compliance, refund, work, service, or benefit. | Add fallback channel, fee cap, portability, no-forfeiture, notice/cure, and public-duty owner. | Treating private-channel failure as taxpayer default. |
| 3 — controller/beneficiary separation | The actor with governance or residual upside differs from the formal taxpayer or operator. | Route rent capture, duty, reserve, or clawback to controller/beneficiary while preserving contest rights. | Paper owner finality; title-based guilt. |
| 4 — willful responsible-person failure | A person with duty and power to collect/account/pay trust-like amounts consciously prefers other creditors or disables remittance. | Escalate to personal/entity liability with evidence of responsibility, willfulness, notice, and appeal. | Automatic officer/vendor liability without control evidence. |
| 5 — public fallback breach | The state mandates, relies on, or licenses a private channel but no workable public fallback exists for protected users. | Treat the public body as a fallback-duty holder; require public option, reissue, waiver, direct service, or channel repair. | Outsourced floor with no public responsibility. |

## Minimum evidence packet

1. Legal duty or contract duty.
2. Practical power over collection, payment, access, or release.
3. Beneficial upside or rent receipt.
4. Channel bottleneck and exit options.
5. Burden bearer and protected-floor exposure.
6. Notice, cure, appeal, and refund/reissue path.
7. Public fallback owner.

## Calibration notes

Trust-fund recovery doctrine is useful as an analogy, not a universal template. It shows why responsibility should require duty, power, and willfulness rather than title, but the cube applies the same moral distinction to platforms, private rails, backstops, AI preparers, and administrative channels.[S322][S324][S667]
