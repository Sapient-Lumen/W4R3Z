# Incidence evidence and protected-burden ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that **statutory remittance never settles moral burden-bearing**, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when expected pass-through is tolerable, when visible protection must be built in, when stronger incidence evidence is required, and when a proposal should be redesigned or blocked because protected humans are the predictable final bearers?**[S4][S15][S21][S31][S67][S69][S83]

Route: judge who becomes poorer rather than who files. Prefer burdens that stick to rents, bottlenecks, concentrated beneficiaries, and priced harms; require visible protection when downstream household, worker, tenant, or weak-supplier burden is foreseeable; and do not wait for impossible full-equilibrium certainty before making a burden judgment.[S4][S15][S21][S31][S67][S69][S83]

## Option scan

| Option | Shape | Archive verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A — remitter presumption | treat the filing entity or legally liable remitter as close enough to the real payer for design work.[S21][S83] | Reject: launders incidence through labels and stops the moral analysis too early. |
| B — empirical perfectionism | refuse strong design judgments until jurisdiction-specific pass-through estimates are complete across wages, prices, rents, supplier margins, and local spillovers.[S21][S83] | Reject: turns a real design duty into strategic delay. |
| C — protected-burden screening ladder | sort proposals into rent-sticky, visible-offset, contested-incidence, weak-counterparty shield, or redesign/block lanes.[S4][S15][S21][S31][S67][S69][S83] | Adopt. |
| D — broad tax plus discretionary hardship relief | leave the tax broad and sharp, then rely on waivers, annual claims, or later politics to repair unjust burden-bearing.[S4][S69] | Reject: converts an incidence defect into an access and take-up defect. |

## Five-rung ladder

1. **rent-sticky lane** — use only where burden is genuinely likely to stay on land, monopoly rent, concession rent, dynastic transfer, or similarly inelastic surplus,[S21][S69][S83]
2. **visible-offset lane** — use when some household or user pass-through is expected but is morally acceptable only if automatic, timely, salient protection is built in from the start,[S4][S21][S69]
3. **contested-incidence lane** — use when the base may partly stick to rents but could also move through wages, supplier squeeze, or consumer prices, so the proposal needs a disclosed evidence packet and named review trigger,[S15][S21][S67][S83]
4. **weak-counterparty shield lane** — use when tenants, low-wage workers, dependent suppliers, captive users, or weak municipalities are plausible absorbers, so the tax must be narrowed, paired with stronger protection, or moved upstream,[S15][S31][S67][S69][S83]
5. **redesign-or-block lane** — use when protected humans are the predictable final bearers and the supposed compensation is speculative, delayed, discretionary, or politically fragile.[S4][S15][S21][S31][S67][S69][S83]

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt **Option C — the protected-burden screening ladder** as the archive's default calibration for live incidence disputes.[S4][S15][S21][S31][S67][S69][S83]

Presumption:

- stay in the **rent-sticky lane** only where burden is genuinely likely to remain on concentrated surplus,
- move to the **visible-offset lane** whenever broad consumers or users will foreseeably pay part of the price and the tax remains morally attractive only with automatic floor protection,
- use the **contested-incidence lane** for corporate, platform, and AI-controller levies whose burden could move through more than one chain,
- escalate to the **weak-counterparty shield lane** whenever bargaining weakness, tenant captivity, low wages, or dependence makes pass-through onto protected humans plausible,
- and move to **redesign-or-block** whenever protected humans are the predictable final bearers and the proposed protection is too slow, too discretionary, or too fragile to count.[S4][S15][S21][S31][S67][S69][S83]

That is the narrowest workable setting because it rejects both remitter worship and impossible-proof paralysis while keeping the burden of justification on the designer whenever weak parties are likely to absorb the cost.

For frontier AI, the implication is practical: **controller-side taxation is necessary but not sufficient. A levy on labs, model providers, cloud affiliates, or major deployers still belongs in stage 3 or stage 4 by default unless the archive can show that the burden will stay mainly on rents or unless automatic worker, household, or host-local protection has already been built in.**[S15][S67][S83]

## Default burden map

| Base or context | Default lane | Why this lane usually fits | Archive warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| land value, concession rent, and other clearly inelastic rent bases | rent-sticky lane | these bases are comparatively immobile and better candidates for burden sticking than routine labor or thin-margin trade.[S21][S83] | still check local housing-market spillovers where legal owners may try to pass pressure into rents or service charges. |
| broad VAT or sales-tax reform | visible-offset lane | consumption taxes are administratively strong, but household burden is foreseeable and needs explicit correction if low-income households are exposed.[S4][S21] | do not defend broad-base consumption taxes with delayed annual claims or low-take-up relief. |
| carbon, utility, or household-energy pricing | visible-offset lane moving quickly to weak-counterparty shield where affordability pressure is high | climate pricing can be efficient, but low-income households in many high-income settings face larger relative energy burdens and need visible protection.[S15][S69] | include both direct and indirect price channels; do not count invisible future spending as present protection. |
| ordinary payroll or routine wage-side taxation below or near the floor | weak-counterparty shield lane | the tax wedge bears directly on take-home pay and basic livelihood, especially for lower-wage and single-parent households.[S31] | routine labor should not become the residual absorber for revenue needs that could have stayed on rents, harms, or concentrated beneficiaries. |
| narrow excess-profit or dominant-platform rent taxes | contested-incidence lane, sometimes rent-sticky lane if genuinely narrow | some burden may stay on above-normal rents, but firms with market power can also shift through prices or squeeze suppliers and workers.[S21][S83] | require a clear excess-rent definition and review whether downstream users or weak suppliers are being used as the real payers. |
| housing-owner or property-side reforms in tight rental markets | contested-incidence lane | immovable bases are attractive, but real incidence can still interact with rent pressure, tenure insecurity, and vulnerable households.[S23] | separate site-value logic from blanket assumptions that tenants never feel any transition burden. |
| frontier-AI controller levies, compute charges, or deployment contributions | contested-incidence lane moving to weak-counterparty shield where displacement or local strain is visible | controller-side design is right in form, but worker, supplier, user, and host-local pass-through remain live risks.[S15][S67][S83] | do not treat “the lab pays” as the end of the moral analysis. |
| public-interest research, weak municipal utilities, and low-margin essential-service providers | redesign-or-block lane as hidden absorbers | these actors are visible collection points but poor moral burden-bearers.[S15][S21][S69][S83] | easy billing is not a license to make weak civic institutions carry the burden. |

## Anti-patterns

- **remitter worship** — the archive stops at the filing entity and never asks who becomes poorer.[S21][S83]
- **paper-only compensation** — relief exists formally but not at the speed, certainty, or visibility needed to stop real hardship.[S4][S69]
- **weak-counterparty squeeze** — suppliers, tenants, workers, or municipalities absorb the burden because they cannot negotiate or exit.[S15][S31][S67]
- **AI incidence exceptionalism** — a controller-side AI levy is assumed just without reviewing downstream pass-through or local burden.[S15][S67]
- **proof-to-paralysis** — policymakers demand impossible causal certainty instead of using a disclosed, reviewable burden-screening ladder.[S21][S83]

## What would change the recommendation

Tighten, loosen, or replace this ladder if:

- better jurisdiction-specific evidence shows that certain narrow corporate or platform taxes do in fact stay largely on rents,
- automatic delivery systems become reliable enough that visible-offset designs really do neutralise predictable household burden in real time,
- housing, utility, or local-burden evidence shows that tenant or user pass-through is consistently weaker or stronger than the archive currently expects,
- or frontier-AI evidence clarifies whether major controller-side levies mostly compress rents, wages, supplier margins, or consumer prices in practice.[S4][S15][S21][S67][S69][S83]

## Rev0295 accountability map

The accountable actor is whichever policy owner, incidence modeler, statutory remitter, upstream firm, or regulated intermediary controls the burden evidence. The statutory remitter is not the final answer. A remitter or upstream actor becomes accountable when it controls price, wage, rent, rate, or service pass-through evidence and uses opacity to hide burden on a protected group.

The route now asks for a statutory/economic incidence model, actual pass-through record, protected-floor exposure table, and repair-channel delivery record. If the record cannot show that relief reaches the real bearer, the remedy must be redesigned before the burden is treated as morally assigned.
