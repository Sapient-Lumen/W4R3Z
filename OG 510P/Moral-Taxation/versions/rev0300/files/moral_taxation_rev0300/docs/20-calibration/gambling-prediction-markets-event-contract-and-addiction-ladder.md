# Gambling, prediction-market, event-contract, and addiction ladder

## Question in one sentence

When an event contract is labeled a derivative or prediction market, when should the archive tax it, regulate it as gambling, prohibit it, or treat it as a bona fide hedge?[S634][S635][S644]

## Option scan

| Option | Shape | Archive verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A — commercial hedge | contract offsets a real exposure such as weather, supply, rate, or commodity risk | Permit under finance logic when exposure is documented and retail speculation is not the real market. |
| B — information market | limited-stake market produces public information without heavy addictive or integrity risk | Possible for research or civic knowledge only with caps, transparency, and no insider category. |
| C — retail gambling product | binary event contracts marketed for entertainment or high-frequency speculation | Use gambling/addiction safeguards, loss limits, advertising controls, and treatment funding. |
| D — integrity-sensitive event | sports, elections, enforcement, war, personnel, or agency action where betting can corrupt conduct | Require insider bans, data-sharing, surveillance, and often prohibition rather than taxation. |
| E — jurisdiction-laundering derivative | federal market form is used mainly to bypass state gambling, consumer, or tribal rules | Treat the label as evidence only; route to conflict and floor review before accepting preemption claims. |

## Parameter ladder

1. **contract taxonomy** — Classify each event by hedge relation, entertainment/speculation, public-interest sensitivity, and corruption potential.
2. **retail access limits** — Set age, identity, suitability, stake, loss, velocity, credit, and leverage limits before launch.
3. **self-exclusion and cooling-off** — Require portable self-exclusion, timeout, friction after loss spikes, and no inducements to chase losses.
4. **inside-information firewall** — Ban or segregate participants with material nonpublic influence or access: athletes, officials, staff, campaigns, agencies, and contractors.
5. **market-maker and data privileges** — Disclose privileged feeds, rebates, market-maker roles, and surveillance duties.
6. **state/tribal law conflict screen** — Do not let a derivative label erase local gambling, consumer, or sovereign interests without explicit legal resolution.
7. **addiction incidence test** — Track losses by income, age, geography, repeat play, and credit use; route proceeds to treatment and prevention.
8. **sports and civic integrity funding** — Charge the market for monitoring, information-sharing, and enforcement costs it creates.
9. **prohibited event categories** — Set no-go rules for events whose trading can incentivize violence, unlawful conduct, corruption, or rights violations.
10. **review trigger** — Reopen on manipulation event, insider scandal, loss concentration, new state ban/litigation, or product shift to micro-betting.

## Default settings

| Parameter | Default | Redesign trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Retail stakes | Low caps, no credit, strong loss limits | High-velocity products, leverage, or credit-funded play. |
| Eligible events | Hedging or low-integrity-risk public information | Sports micro-events, elections, enforcement, personnel, violence, or unlawful conduct. |
| Proceeds | Treatment, integrity monitoring, consumer redress | General revenue with no addiction or integrity repair. |
| Records | Participant-level harm and insider surveillance | Opaque aggregate volume only. |
| Review cadence | Quarterly while law and products move quickly | Static approval despite litigation or market redesign. |

## Anti-pattern definitions

- **derivative label laundering** — a finance label is used to avoid gambling, consumer, tribal, or public-integrity safeguards.
- **addiction rent** — revenue depends on repeat loss, chasing behavior, inducements, or vulnerable retail users.
- **inside information bet** — participants trade on influence or privileged access to the event being priced.
- **state law bypass** — market structure is chosen mainly to defeat local gambling policy rather than to hedge real risk.
- **sports integrity theater** — a league or platform announces integrity controls without enforceable data-sharing, insider bans, or funding.
