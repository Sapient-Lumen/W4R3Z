# Health-tax, addiction-targeting, and floor-protection ladder

## Question in one sentence

What ladder should decide whether a harmful-consumption tax is a legitimate health instrument, a revenue extraction from vulnerability, or a disguised permission to sell harm?[S480][S481][S482]

## Option scan

| Option | Shape | Archive verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A — no health tax | ordinary tax only. | Reject when product creates large external or addiction harms. |
| B — ad valorem excise | percentage of price. | Useful but can reward cheaper harmful products. |
| C — specific/content-based excise | tax units, alcohol, tobacco, sugar, or harmful content. | Preferred where product content tracks harm. |
| D — tax plus non-tax controls | combine excise with marketing, age, product, and disclosure rules. | Default for addiction and youth targeting. |
| E — prohibition/cap | ban or limit highest-risk product or design. | Required where harm is non-compensable or manipulative. |

## Ten-gate ladder

1. **harm gate** — specify disease burden, addiction mechanism, external costs, internality, youth vulnerability, or targeted marketing.[S480]
2. **base gate** — choose product volume, alcohol content, sugar content, nicotine/tobacco unit, potency, or other harm-linked base.[S481]
3. **elasticity gate** — estimate price response and substitution to more dangerous products, illicit markets, or untaxed substitutes.
4. **addiction gate** — distinguish casual choice from dependence; include treatment, cessation, and non-punitive support.
5. **youth/protected-status gate** — test youth targeting, poverty targeting, racialized marketing, disability/mental-health interactions, and geographic concentration.
6. **floor gate** — recycle proceeds to low-income rebates, healthy substitutes, treatment, prevention, health care, and social protection before general revenue.
7. **industry-capture gate** — record lobbying, exemptions, investment agreements, earmark capture, and front-group regressivity claims.[S480]
8. **salience gate** — make the tax visible enough to change behavior but not so administratively burdensome that small retailers become the real target.
9. **indexation gate** — index rates to inflation, income growth, or affordability so the tax does not silently decay.
10. **review gate** — monitor consumption, health outcomes, regressivity, illicit trade, and industry reformulation.

## Default settings

| Posture | Instrument | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| sugary drink | specific or sugar-content excise | fund healthy substitutes and child health. |
| tobacco/nicotine | high excise plus cessation support | index to affordability; block youth targeting. |
| alcohol | content-based excise plus harm controls | protect treatment and road-safety proceeds. |
| addictive design beyond ordinary product | restriction or prohibition plus levy | tax cannot sell manipulative design. |
| serious floor burden | rebate/treatment before general fund | no poverty toll. |

## Rev0299 accountability map

Rev0299 makes the health-tax route accountable to harm control and repair capacity rather than to the visible consumer who pays the shelf price.

| Question | Default accountability answer |
|---|---|
| Who controls the harm? | Product designers, manufacturers, marketers, retailers, and tax-base setters that control potency, targeting, price, age gates, and rate design. |
| Who benefits? | The firm, retailer, or general fund that captures addiction-linked revenue or keeps proceeds away from treatment, prevention, rebates, and healthy substitutes. |
| Who should not be blamed by default? | Addicted users, youth, patients, and floor households are protected burden bearers, not the accountable revenue source. |
| What evidence is decisive? | Harm-content records, youth/addiction targeting, price pass-through, substitution risk, proceeds ledgers, and industry-capture records. |
| Default move | Tie the charge to harm and use proceeds for floor repair before general revenue; escalate manipulative or non-compensable design to restriction rather than a permission-to-harm tax. |
