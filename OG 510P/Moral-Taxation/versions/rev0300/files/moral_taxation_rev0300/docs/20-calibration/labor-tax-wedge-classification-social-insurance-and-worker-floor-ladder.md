# Labor tax wedge, classification, social-insurance, and worker-floor ladder

## Question in one sentence

What ladder should decide whether a labour contribution, payroll levy, classification rule, or platform-reporting duty is a fair social-insurance instrument or an ordinary-work penalty?[S497][S498][S499]

## Option scan

| Option | Shape | Archive verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A — broad payroll funding | employer/worker contributions fund benefits. | Legitimate with floor relief, visible benefits, and incidence review. |
| B — shift to consumption or flat fees | reduce payroll burden by taxing spending or accounts. | Risky if it burdens the same protected floor more bluntly. |
| C — classify by contract label | accept worker-status labels chosen by the parties. | Reject where control, dependence, and integration point the other way. |
| D — platform remittance/records | require record-capable platforms to report and contribute. | Default when control and payment data are real. |
| E — portable benefit accounts | attach benefits to worker across firms/platforms. | Useful supplement; cannot excuse misclassification. |

## Ten-gate ladder

1. **floor gate** — identify whether the burden falls on low wages, entry-level work, caregivers returning to work, disability-limited work, migrants, or platform-dependent workers.
2. **benefit gate** — ask whether contributions buy real social insurance, wage credits, health, pension, unemployment, disability, or portable protection rather than disappearing into opaque revenue.[S497]
3. **incidence gate** — estimate wage pass-through, price pass-through, reduced hours, self-employment burden, and whether rents could be taxed first.
4. **control gate** — test who controls price, access, rating, discipline, equipment standards, customer relation, task allocation, and termination power.[S498][S499]
5. **dependence gate** — distinguish genuine business independence from nominal contractor status under one practical controller.
6. **record gate** — assign reporting and wage-credit correction to the actor with payment, identity, hours, task, and withholding records.
7. **private-ordering gate** — block indemnities, waivers, contract labels, or chargebacks that shift employer duties onto workers.
8. **parity gate** — compare employee and independent-worker access to deductions, benefits, credits, and contribution records.
9. **transition gate** — use safe harbors only for good-faith uncertainty, not for deliberate status arbitrage.
10. **review gate** — revisit after classification-rule shifts, platform model changes, rising effective marginal tax rates, or benefit-credit gaps.

## Default settings

| Posture | Instrument | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| low-wage payroll burden | credit/rebate/taper | protect work floor before raising rates. |
| platform control plus payment records | reporting/contribution duty | no label-only escape. |
| genuine independent business | self-employment regime | benefit and deduction parity. |
| disputed status | bounded contest | preserve records and benefits while status is reviewed. |
| missing wage credits | correction-first | no worker penalty for controller record failure. |

## Rev0297 accountability map

- **Primary actor:** the wage-record controller, platform employer, or employer with practical control over classification, hours, pay, dispatch, discipline, and payroll/social-insurance records.
- **Beneficiary to trace:** the firm or platform avoiding payroll, benefit, workers' compensation, unemployment, or social-insurance contribution duties; do not treat the worker's contract label as the rent recipient.
- **Required packet:** classification-control facts, wage/hour/task/deactivation records, payroll and contribution ledgers, benefit-credit correction logs, and worker notice/cure/appeal records.
- **Default move:** correct the wage record and social-insurance credit first, then assign contribution or classification liability to the work controller; no worker forfeiture for controller record failure.
