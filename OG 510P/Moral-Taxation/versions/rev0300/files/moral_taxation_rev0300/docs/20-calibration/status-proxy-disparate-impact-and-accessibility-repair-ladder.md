# Status proxies, disparate impact, and accessibility-repair ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that **tax design may not do protected-status work through apparently neutral proxies when cleaner measures of income, need, harm, control, or benefit exist**, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when a neutral-looking rule can stand, when it needs accessibility and channel repair, when a proxy must be replaced or constrained, and when the design should simply be barred because the proxy is doing hierarchy work?**[S32][S33][S44][S45][S46][S65][S74]

## Closed rules invoked

This memo does **not** reopen the archive's constitutional waist. It relies on:

- [`../10-framework/automaticity-and-claim-friction-routing.md`](../10-framework/automaticity-and-claim-friction-routing.md)
- [`../10-framework/administration-explanation-and-appeal-routing.md`](../10-framework/administration-explanation-and-appeal-routing.md)
- [`../10-framework/assessment-units-care-and-dependency-routing.md`](../10-framework/assessment-units-care-and-dependency-routing.md)
- [`../10-framework/standing-representation-and-remedy-routing.md`](../10-framework/standing-representation-and-remedy-routing.md)

The settled rule is already clear: **do not set liability, access to relief, or enforcement intensity by protected status or by close substitutes such as household defaults, documentation chokepoints, geography, interface design, language assumptions, banking access, or opaque scores when more direct measures are available; where accommodation or repair is the real goal, target that goal directly.**[S32][S33][S44][S45][S46][S65][S74]

## Why calibration is still needed

The closed rule fixes direction, but administrations still need an operating ladder because four different failures can hide inside "neutral" tax design:

1. **proxy laundering** where co-residence, marital status, postcode, sector labels, digital identity rules, or risk scores quietly reproduce protected-status ranking,[S32][S33][S44][S45][S46]
2. **channel inequality** where online-only registration, filing, refund, or appeal design excludes disabled people, low-connectivity households, migrants, or people without banking and documentation access,[S44][S46][S65][S74]
3. **data invisibility** where the administration refuses disaggregated review and therefore cannot tell whether a rule that looks even-handed is systematically unequal in effect,[S32][S45][S74]
4. and **over-correction theater** where a real accommodation goal is pursued through crude status categories instead of direct support, accessibility, or service design.[S32][S33][S46]

## Small option set

### Option A — clean rule plus ordinary monitoring

Keep the rule if it is already tied directly to income, benefit, harm, control, use of scarce capacity, or observable need, and if the filing and appeal path remains usable through ordinary service channels. This is the right lane for most broad taxes with person-level liability and multiple access channels.[S32][S44][S65]

### Option B — keep the rule, but add accessibility and channel repair

Keep the substantive rule, but require offline or assisted channels, accessible notice, language support, alternative authentication, and practical correction paths because the burden comes mainly from service design rather than from the tax base itself. This lane is often right for credits, refunds, withholding corrections, and routine filing obligations.[S44][S46][S65][S74]

### Option C — constrained proxy lane with mandatory replacement test

Allow the proxy only temporarily and only with disaggregated monitoring, public explanation, and a standing search for a cleaner measure. This is the lane for geography, household-resource tests, simplified indicators, or fraud-risk features that may be administratively helpful but are suspiciously close to protected-status sorting.[S32][S33][S44][S45][S74]

### Option D — replace or bar the design

Do not keep the rule. Replace it with a direct metric, direct support instrument, or narrower anti-abuse test because the proxy is doing too much hierarchy work to be morally salvageable. This is the archive's preferred answer when household defaulting, digital-only claiming, documentation gating, opaque scoring, or postcode targeting does the real distributive sorting.[S32][S33][S44][S45][S46][S74]

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt a **five-stage practical ladder**:

| Stage | When it should apply | Equality answer | Main guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. direct-metric lane | rule already tracks income, value, harm, control, use, or need with ordinary multi-channel access | **keep the rule** | continue ordinary monitoring and challenge rights |
| 1. channel-repair lane | the base is basically sound but access or usability is uneven | **keep the base, repair the service path** | offline, assisted, language, disability, and alternative-authentication routes must exist before scaling |
| 2. flagged-proxy lane | the rule uses a proxy that might be defensible but is close to status sorting | **allow only with disaggregated review, explanation, and expiration pressure** | proxy convenience never counts as a permanent justification |
| 3. direct-support substitution lane | the real goal is accommodation, caregiving support, accessibility, or repair rather than hierarchy or exclusion | **replace the burdening proxy with direct support or direct accommodation** | do not smuggle support through penalties, exclusions, or household dependence |
| 4. barred-proxy lane | the rule predictably reproduces protected-status ranking and no strong necessity case survives | **do not use the rule** | geography, household form, documentation, or black-box scoring cannot be kept just because administration finds them cheap |

The archive's provisional setting is therefore **stage 0 for genuinely direct metrics, stage 1 where the design problem is mainly service access, stage 2 only for tightly monitored temporary proxies, stage 3 when the real moral target is accommodation or repair, and stage 4 whenever a neutral-looking rule is mainly sorting by protected status in practice**.[S32][S33][S44][S45][S46][S65][S74]

That recommendation keeps the archive's anti-discrimination rule tight instead of symbolic. It says the state does **not** satisfy equality merely by deleting explicit slurs from statute text. It must also ask whether the administrative path, proxy choice, and correction channels reproduce rank indirectly.

For AI-linked tax administration the implication is practical: **risk scores, language models, interface defaults, digital identity requirements, and geography-linked compliance models belong in stage 2 or stage 4 unless the administration can show both necessity and usable correction paths.** For current LLM taxation itself, the memo changes nothing about direct subjecthood: the archive still taxes controllers and beneficiaries rather than present models, but now has a compact equality ladder for the human-facing channels around those taxes.[S19][S20][S44][S65][S74]

## Anti-patterns

- **Neutrality by interface fiction** — pretending a digital-only or document-heavy channel is neutral because everyone sees the same portal.[S46][S65][S74]
- **Postcode laundering** — using place or neighborhood characteristics when place is not the moral object of the tax.[S45][S46]
- **Jointness by convenience** — using household form as a shortcut when person-level or directly measured resource tests are available.[S32][S33]
- **Black-box equality theater** — relying on opaque risk scores while promising ex post appeal that ordinary taxpayers cannot realistically use.[S44][S45]
- **Accommodation by exclusion** — responding to disability, caregiving, or migration-related barriers with special restrictions instead of accessibility, support, or simpler proof paths.[S32][S46][S74]

## What would change the recommendation

This ladder should be tightened, loosened, or partly replaced if any of the following become clearer:

- administrations gain strong evidence that a currently suspicious proxy has unusually high explanatory value and unusually low disparate effects relative to available direct measures, which could justify keeping some stage-2 cases narrower and longer,[S44][S45]
- accessible digital identity, language, disability, and assisted-service systems become robust enough that some current stage-1 repairs can be treated as ordinary baseline administration rather than special accommodation,[S65][S74]
- or disaggregated evidence shows that specific taxes, channels, or risk models generate persistent exclusion or audit skew despite repair efforts, which should push those designs from stage 2 to stage 4 rather than preserving them through inertia.[S32][S44][S45][S74]
