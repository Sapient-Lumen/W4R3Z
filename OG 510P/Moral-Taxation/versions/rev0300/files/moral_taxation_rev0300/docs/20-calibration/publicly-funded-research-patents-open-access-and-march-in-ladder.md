# Publicly funded research, patents, open access, and march-in ladder

## Question in one sentence

How should public-research fiscal claims be calibrated so grants produce public knowledge and usable inventions without destroying commercialization incentives?[S607][S608][S609]

## Option scan

| Option | Shape | Archive verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A — public access condition | publication and metadata availability rule. | Default for grant-funded research. |
| B — nonexclusive license | multiple users can commercialize or apply results. | Prefer where exclusivity is not needed. |
| C — bounded exclusive license | limited exclusivity with diligence and access terms. | Use when commercialization requires it. |
| D — march-in / compulsory licensing review | public authority evaluates access failure or nonuse. | Reserve for serious access or utilization failures. |
| E — publication-cost cap or rule | limits grant-paid open-access charges. | Use to prevent dissemination rents. |

## Ten-gate ladder

1. **funding-trace gate** — identify grant, facility, data, or procurement support.
2. **publication gate** — require public access without unnecessary embargo or paywall.
3. **data gate** — separate privacy/security limits from shareable research outputs.
4. **patent gate** — confirm disclosure, title, government rights, and utilization plan.
5. **license gate** — test whether exclusivity is necessary and bounded.
6. **price/access gate** — test affordability, reasonable availability, and public-program burden.
7. **cost gate** — prevent excessive publication or platform fees from consuming grant purpose.
8. **competition gate** — preserve follow-on research and small-firm access where possible.
9. **remedy gate** — use reporting, licensing conditions, government-use, or march-in review before blunt tax.
10. **review gate** — reopen on access failure, unreasonable price signal, publication breach, or unused patent.

## Default settings

| Posture | Instrument | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| grant article | public access condition | no needless embargo. |
| public invention | bounded license | diligence and access terms. |
| access failure | march-in or licensing review | evidence and due process. |
| high publication charges | allowable-cost cap | do not create paywall by another name. |
