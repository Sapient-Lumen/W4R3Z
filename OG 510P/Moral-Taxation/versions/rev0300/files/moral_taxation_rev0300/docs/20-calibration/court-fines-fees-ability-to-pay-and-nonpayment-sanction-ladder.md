# Court fines, fees, ability-to-pay, and nonpayment-sanction ladder

## Question in one sentence

What ladder should decide whether a fine, fee, court cost, civil filing charge, surcharge, or nonpayment sanction is legitimate, excessive, waivable, or revenue-seeking?[S515][S516][S517][S538]

## Option scan

| Option | Shape | Archive verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A — culpability fine | proportionate penalty for proven conduct. | Allowed with ability review and floor protection. |
| B — court-cost fee | user charge for adjudicative service. | Suspect where it blocks access or funds core justice. |
| C — sliding scale / day fine | amount tied to income and harm. | Preferred for monetary sanction. |
| D — waiver / in forma pauperis | no-pay access path. | Required for remedy access. |
| E — nonpayment sanction | warrant, jail, license hold, collection. | Only after willfulness finding. |

## Ten-gate ladder

1. **purpose gate** — name sanction, restitution, access cost, administrative cost, or revenue.
2. **budget-conflict gate** — test whether the collecting body depends on the proceeds.[S515]
3. **ability gate** — determine income, assets, dependents, disability, incarceration, and cash needs.[S516]
4. **access gate** — preserve court filing, appeal, transcript, and challenge routes.[S538]
5. **proportionality gate** — compare amount to offense, harm, culpability, and income.
6. **waiver gate** — provide waiver, reduction, payment pause, or community alternative.
7. **add-on gate** — cap costs, surcharges, interest, and collection fees.
8. **willfulness gate** — no jail, warrant, license hold, or revocation without on-record willfulness.[S517]
9. **data gate** — publish assessed, collected, waived, uncollected, and used proceeds.
10. **review gate** — recalibrate when collections cluster by race, poverty, geography, or protected status.
