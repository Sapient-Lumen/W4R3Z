# Mandatory private tax rail, refund/payment access, and bankless fallback ladder

## Question in one sentence

When filing, payment, refund, rebate, benefit, or appeal access moves through banks, wallets, tax software, payment processors, or other private rails, what fallback and pass-through controls keep the public claim public?[S119][S441][S443][S444][S648]

## Option scan

| Option | Shape | Archive verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A — preferred electronic rail | direct deposit, EFT, card, wallet, or portal is fastest for most users | Accept only with alternatives and no penalty for predictable exclusion. |
| B — assisted public fallback | phone, in-person, mail, paper check, prepaid card, voucher, or public account support | Required for bankless, disabled, elderly, rural, identity-compromised, language-access, and offline users. |
| C — private vendor rail | software, bank, wallet, app store, processor, or tax-prep partner handles access | Use only with fee caps, portability, nondiscrimination, public verification, and exit. |
| D — temporary freeze or hold | payment is paused after missing/rejected bank info or fraud risk | Permitted only with prompt notice, no-rent correction, deadline preservation, and hardship escalation. |
| E — exclusive private rail | the user must buy or maintain a private relationship to receive or pay | Reject for ordinary civic tax rights unless a narrow emergency is documented and temporary. |

## Parameter ladder

1. **rail identification** — Name every required private actor and whether it controls identity, eligibility, payment, records, or appeal.
2. **exit realism** — Test whether users can switch channel without losing priority, filing date, refund timing, benefit amount, or review rights.
3. **bankless and rejected-payment protocol** — Provide no-rent reissue, alternate electronic methods, paper/check exception, and assisted correction after direct-deposit failure.
4. **fee and spread audit** — Count processor fees, prepaid-card fees, wallet fees, currency spreads, software upsells, and preparer charges as incidence.
5. **notice and cure** — Give plain notice of rail failure, required action, deadline, and human assistance before forfeiture or long delay.
6. **hardship acceleration** — Fast-track refunds, rebates, and benefits where delay threatens housing, food, medical, or utility floors.
7. **data minimization** — Do not require broader bank, device, biometric, or platform data than the payment/correction job needs.
8. **public verification** — Let users verify private collection/payment/preparer status through a public channel and pay/receive directly where possible.
9. **attempt preservation** — Preserve timestamps when a portal, bank, processor, identity vendor, or software channel fails.
10. **review trigger** — Reopen when freezes rise, rejection rates concentrate, fees appear, complaints spike, paper exceptions backlog, or vendor exit occurs.

## Default settings

| Parameter | Default | Redesign trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Fallback | Timely, assisted, no-rent, and not discretionary | Paper/electronic exception exists but takes too long to protect the floor. |
| Fees | No private fee for ordinary receipt or payment duty | Processor, wallet, app, or preparer captures a toll. |
| Freeze protocol | Prompt notice, cure, hardship path | Silent freeze or repeated rejected-deposit loop. |
| Data | Narrow payment facts only | Bank/device/biometric data becomes general surveillance. |
| Review cadence | Operational dashboard during filing season | No review until after harm occurs. |

## Anti-pattern definitions

- **one-bank refund** — taxpayer receipt depends on a bank account the taxpayer lacks or loses.
- **payout skim** — refund or rebate is reduced by processor, wallet, card, or preparer fees.
- **freeze by default** — payment is held after rail failure while the taxpayer bears delay without assistance.
- **vendor-only correction** — the public agency cannot fix a public claim unless the taxpayer navigates a private dashboard.
- **digital-only exclusion** — offline, disabled, elderly, rural, language-access, or identity-compromised users face a slower substantive right.

## Rev0293 accountability map

A private tax rail is accountable at three levels, not one:

| Level | Accountable actor | What cannot be outsourced |
|---|---|---|
| Public claim owner | revenue agency or benefit/payment program owner | public verification, no-rent correction, deadline preservation, hardship escalation, and final reissue/release. |
| Rail controller | bank, wallet, payment processor, filing vendor, identity vendor, or preparer with disbursement, freeze, fee, data, or dashboard control | rejection reason, fee/spread, refund destination, account freeze, correction path, and timestamp evidence. |
| Protected bearer | bankless, elderly, rural, disabled, language-access, identity-compromised, low-income, or offline taxpayer | no forfeiture or slower substantive right merely because the preferred rail failed.[S443][S444] |

Default answer: keep the electronic preference where it works, but treat rejected deposit, frozen account, failed identity workflow, wallet lock-in, or vendor-only correction as a public repair event. The remedy is reissue/acceptance through an assisted public channel plus fee removal, timestamp preservation, and hardship acceleration.
