# Real-value, indexation, and reset-cadence ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that nominal values are not moral values, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when tax design should use direct real-value adjustment, automatic formula indexation, scheduled public reset, disclosed approximation, or openly defended non-indexation for brackets, allowances, benefits, capital-gains basis, loss relief, depreciation, bonds, and AI or infrastructure thresholds?**[S15][S47][S48][S49][S50][S55][S56][S57][S58]

The constitutional question is already settled: **where inflation or nominal drift would otherwise change the real burden, the real floor, the real value of downside recognition, or the real adequacy of public protection, tax design should index, reset, approximate honestly, or openly defend the real tightening rather than letting money illusion govern by stealth.**[S47][S48][S49][S50][S55][S56][S57][S58]

## Why calibration is still needed

The closed rule settles the direction but not yet the smallest usable operating ladder. A workable calibration still has to avoid five distinct failures at once:

1. **stealth tightening by nominal freeze** where wages, thresholds, allowances, or protected-floor supports drift just because prices rose,[S55][S57][S58]
2. **phantom-gain taxation** where nominal appreciation or understated deductions are treated as real enrichment,[S50][S56]
3. **paper relief decay** where losses, credits, reserve minima, or bond amounts survive in statute but not in real value,[S47][S48][S49][S50][S55][S56]
4. **over-precision theater** where full inflation adjustment is demanded even for categories that are lumpy, heterogeneous, or better handled by periodic reset,[S55][S57][S58]
5. and **fast-moving threshold staleness** where AI, data-centre, or infrastructure triggers become trivial or overbroad because nominal costs and scale move quickly.[S15][S58]

## Small option set

### Option A — nominal freeze with ad hoc political correction

Leave most thresholds, relief amounts, bonds, and reserve minima untouched until periodic politics happens to notice. This looks simple, but it quietly delegates distributive choice to inflation and makes protection strongest only for items that happen to receive bespoke updates.[S55][S57][S58]

### Option B — formula index everything possible

Use a general formula for most thresholds and relief. This is better than drift for ordinary wage-side parameters and many cash floors, but it can overfit categories that need asset-specific treatment, periodic re-benchmarking, or direct market measurement rather than one common inflation factor.[S55][S56][S57]

### Option C — split-lane real-value ladder

Use **direct real-value adjustment** where money illusion is especially distortive and the base is long-horizon or capital-like; use **automatic formula indexation** for routine household-side thresholds and protected floors; use **scheduled public reset** for heterogeneous or fast-moving instruments like bonds, reserve minima, land-value bands, and AI or infrastructure thresholds; and use **disclosed approximation** where full adjustment would cost too much relative to the stakes.[S15][S47][S48][S49][S50][S55][S56][S57][S58]

This is the cleanest default because it preserves real burdens and real protection without pretending one mechanical formula fits every category.

### Option D — deliberate non-indexation only by open real-policy choice

Allow lawmakers to leave a rule unindexed **only** when they openly defend the real tightening or loosening they are choosing and name a review date or trigger. This keeps democratic choice intact without permitting silent redistribution through neglect.[S55][S57][S58]

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt a **five-step practical ladder**:

| Stage | When it should apply | Real-value answer | Main guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. direct real-value lane | capital gains basis, depreciation, inventories, long-dated loss relief, and similar long-horizon capital rules | **use direct inflation adjustment or the nearest honest real-value approximation** | do not pretend large nominal gains are real enrichment merely because a perfect system is harder |
| 1. automatic indexation lane | wage brackets, allowances, filing thresholds, refundable floors, child-linked amounts, and other routine household-side parameters | **index automatically on a published formula** | protect the floor and low-visibility households from stealth tightening |
| 2. scheduled reset lane | bonds, cleanup reserves, insurance minima, land bands, valuation safe harbors, and AI or infrastructure thresholds | **reset on a named public cadence or trigger** | use re-benchmarking where one static formula would miss real cost movement or lumpy market shifts |
| 3. disclosed approximation lane | categories where full adjustment is too costly but drift would still be morally material | **use published bands, standard uplifts, corridors, or periodic batch updates** | say what is being approximated and how much error is tolerated |
| 4. intentional non-indexation lane | rare cases where lawmakers truly want a real tightening or loosening | **permit only if the real policy choice is explicit and review-linked** | no silent nominal freeze; force an honest political claim |

The archive's provisional setting is therefore **stage 1 for ordinary household-side tax-and-transfer parameters, stage 0 for the most inflation-sensitive long-horizon capital items, stage 2 for public-protection amounts and fast-moving AI or infrastructure thresholds, stage 3 for lower-stakes categories where full adjustment is disproportionate, and stage 4 only when legislators openly defend the real burden change they are choosing**.[S15][S47][S48][S49][S50][S55][S56][S57][S58]

That is the narrowest workable recommendation because it preserves the real floor, distinguishes phantom gain from real enrichment, keeps downside recognition meaningful across time, and prevents cleanup bonds, reserve minima, or AI-trigger thresholds from rotting into theater. It also avoids the opposite mistake of demanding perfect inflation engineering where a scheduled reset or candid approximation is the more honest administrative choice.[S15][S47][S48][S49][S50][S55][S56][S57][S58]

For present AI stacks, the practical consequence is simple: **registry fees, compute or facility thresholds, grid-stress triggers, safe harbors, and security-posting amounts should rarely sit in nominal freeze; they usually belong in stage 2, with formula indexation only where the underlying cost driver is stable enough and direct market-linked re-benchmarking is not needed.**[S15][S47][S48][S58]

## Anti-patterns

- **Bracket-creep by neglect** — nominal freeze is used as an unspoken tax increase.[S55][S57]
- **Phantom enrichment accounting** — inflationary appreciation is taxed as if it were all real gain.[S50][S56]
- **Protection rot** — bonds, reserves, rebates, or loss relief decay while the statute remains nominally unchanged.[S47][S48][S49][S50][S55][S56]
- **One-index-fits-all theater** — a single formula is imposed on categories that really need re-benchmarking or direct valuation.[S55][S57][S58]
- **Threshold fossilization in fast-moving sectors** — AI or infrastructure triggers stay frozen until they become meaningless or overbroad.[S15][S58]

## What would change the recommendation

This ladder should be tightened, loosened, or partly replaced if any of the following become clearer:

- administrations can perform broader real-value adjustment at low cost for long-horizon capital items, reducing the need for rough approximations,[S50][S56]
- evidence shows that some categories now indexed by formula are drifting away from their real policy target because the relevant cost driver is not ordinary price inflation, which would push them toward stage 2 reset instead,[S55][S57][S58]
- public-protection amounts such as bonds, reserves, or insurance minima prove too volatile for cadence-based reset, requiring more dynamic formulas or market-linked floors,[S47][S48][S49]
- or AI-related infrastructure costs, capability markers, or local-burden metrics stabilize enough that some current stage-2 items can move toward simpler automatic indexation without hidden distortion.[S15][S58]
