# Water scarcity, pricing, rights, and household-irrigation ladder

## Question in one sentence

How should water taxes, fees, rights, subsidies, and permits be calibrated under scarcity without sacrificing human floors or ecological minimums?[S584][S585]

## Option scan

| Option | Shape | Archive verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A — flat tariff | same price per unit. | Simple but weak under scarcity and poverty. |
| B — lifeline plus increasing block | protected basic block, higher marginal price. | Strong default for household water. |
| C — scarcity charge | basin stress or season raises price. | Use only after floor and ecological gates. |
| D — tradeable right or permit | allocation can move across users. | Allowed with anti-hoarding and public-interest tests. |
| E — irrigation subsidy reform | price or support changes crop/water choices. | Use with transition and subsistence safeguards. |

## Ten-gate ladder

1. **floor gate** — define basic household, health, sanitation, and subsistence needs.
2. **ecology gate** — set minimum flows and pollution limits before allocation.
3. **basin gate** — measure scarcity at watershed scale, not only utility accounting scale.
4. **rights gate** — list senior, riparian, permit, treaty, and customary claims.
5. **tariff gate** — separate operating cost, capital cost, scarcity price, and penalty.
6. **irrigation gate** — disclose subsidy, crop pattern, energy link, drainage, and return flows.
7. **transfer gate** — permit trades only after anti-hoarding and community-impact review.
8. **affordability gate** — use lifeline blocks, arrears repair, and shutoff limits.
9. **local-burden gate** — net tax abatements, industrial discounts, and ratepayer upgrades.
10. **review gate** — reopen on drought, disconnection spikes, ecological failure, or hoarding signals.

## Default settings

| Posture | Instrument | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| household access | lifeline block | no shutoff for basic health need. |
| basin scarcity | scarcity charge | ecological floor first. |
| agricultural overuse | subsidy reform | transition for small/subsistence farmers. |
| industrial facility | local-burden charge | ratepayer and watershed repair. |

## Rev0298 accountability map

- **Primary actor:** the basin authority, water utility, irrigation district, or rights holder controlling allocation, tariff design, scarcity charge, transfer approval, or disconnection.
- **Beneficiary to trace:** the senior rights holder, irrigator, industrial user, or utility capturing scarcity rent, subsidized water, hoarded allocation, or paper-conservation value.
- **Required packet:** basin-stress and return-flow records, tariff block and disconnection files, water-right registry, irrigation subsidy and crop-choice records, lifeline affordability, and ecological-minimum ledgers.
- **Default move:** protect household lifeline and ecological minimums first, then price nonessential or high-volume scarcity use with anti-hoarding and watershed repair.
