# AI regulatory-role controller-evidence crosswalk ladder

## Question in one sentence

When an AI regulatory role exists, how much weight should it carry in tax routing, controller ranking, beneficiary routing, local-burden assignment, or model-assisted tax-administration safeguards?[S17][S44][S446][S447][S448]

## Six-level evidentiary ladder

| Level | Regulatory fact | Tax weight |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | mere mention in AI law or guidance | no tax weight except vocabulary alignment. |
| 1 | duty to keep documentation or cooperate | record-access weight; may support information return or audit trail. |
| 2 | release, update, or distribution governance | bounded upstream-duty evidence. |
| 3 | deployment purpose, user population, tool permissioning, or local action control | strong controller or co-controller evidence for that layer. |
| 4 | systemic-risk, critical-infrastructure, or high-impact public deployment role | precaution, assurance, and review-intensity weight.[S447][S448] |
| 5 | residual upside plus durable governance plus continuation power | primary-controller or beneficiary/rent-routing weight, subject to ordinary incidence review. |

## Required separation

Never collapse these five questions:

1. Who is an AI-law regulated actor?
2. Who controls the taxable activity?
3. Who benefits from the rent or windfall?
4. Who can remit, report, or preserve records cheaply?
5. Who bears the local or protected-side burden?

The same party may answer several questions, but each answer needs its own evidence.

## Tax administration AI setting

For model-assisted tax administration, use this minimum:

- model score cannot be the only predicate for an irreversible adjustment;
- notice should identify the issue category and correction route;
- affected taxpayers need an accessible human review path;
- training, testing, red-team, drift, bias, and override logs should be retained in a reviewable form;
- black-box limits are a reason for narrower use or stronger appeal, not for weaker taxpayer rights.[S44][S449]

## Accountability map

This crosswalk now has a narrow actor-accountability rule: an AI regulatory role is **evidence**, not a tax-liability shortcut. The role may identify the actor who holds documentation, release governance, deployment authority, or high-risk system duties, but the tax route still has to separately name the controller, beneficiary, remitter, bottleneck, burden bearer, and public fallback owner.[S17][S44][S446][S447][S448]

Default assignment:

| Question | Default accountable actor | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| role vocabulary | actor claiming tax weight from provider, deployer, importer, distributor, GPAI, high-risk, or public-use status | legal role classification and actual obligation record |
| controller weight | actor with release, deployment, update, mission, tool-permission, or continuation power | governance-lever and model/system documentation record |
| beneficiary weight | actor using the role to obtain market access, safe-harbor value, public procurement access, or burden shift | residual-upside, market-access, or compliance-rent record |
| burden protection | taxpayer, user, worker, small firm, or public body exposed to adverse action or compliance cost | notice, review, correction, and fallback-channel record |

A regulatory title alone therefore cannot answer the tax question. It must be translated into the specific evidence lane it actually supports.
