# Reliance, phase-in, and anti-grandfathering ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that major tax change should be **prospective by default** and that certainty must not fossilize privilege, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when reform needs only ordinary prospectivity, when it should add finite phase-in or person-focused liquidity relief, when short anti-forestalling is justified, and when claimed reliance is really incumbent privilege that should convert or expire rather than be grandfathered?**[S23][S27][S36][S37][S38][S59][S60][S63][S69]

## Closed rules invoked

This memo does **not** reopen the archive's constitutional waist. It relies on:

- [`../10-framework/timing-and-liquidity-routing.md`](../10-framework/timing-and-liquidity-routing.md)
- [`../10-framework/review-triggers-and-policy-reversibility.md`](../10-framework/review-triggers-and-policy-reversibility.md)
- [`../10-framework/net-fiscal-stack-and-hidden-negative-tax-routing.md`](../10-framework/net-fiscal-stack-and-hidden-negative-tax-routing.md)
- [`../10-framework/annual-wealth-tax-backstop-routing.md`](../10-framework/annual-wealth-tax-backstop-routing.md)

The constitutional question is already settled: **protect legitimate reliance against arbitrary shock, especially where households or genuinely sunk productive activity cannot cheaply unwind; but do not let tax certainty, liquidity rhetoric, or transition language become a permanent shelter for monopoly rents, speculative appreciation, fossil or extractive privilege, negotiated tax holidays, or AI-incumbent advantage.**[S23][S36][S37][S38][S69]

## Why calibration is still needed

The closed rule settles the direction but not yet the smallest usable operating ladder. A workable calibration still has to avoid six distinct failures at once:

1. **lock-in blindness** where ordinary households, caregivers, disabled people, small producers, or place-bound firms are treated as if every commitment were instantly reversible,[S23][S27][S36]
2. **privilege laundering** where rentier advantage is redescribed as reliance simply because it is old, politically organized, or wrapped in legal paperwork,[S36][S38][S63]
3. **liquidity confusion** where a real cash-flow problem that calls for instalments, deferral, or temporary smoothing is instead answered with a permanent base exemption,[S23][S27][S36]
4. **announcement gaming** where wealthy or mobile actors accelerate gifts, realizations, siting choices, or paper restructurings once reform is announced,[S36][S37]
5. **split-speed transition** where collection or price effects arrive immediately but compensating floor protection, local repair, or worker-transition support arrives much later,[S23][S69]
6. and **evergreen grandfathering** where a supposedly temporary transition becomes a hidden ongoing subsidy with no real end-state, conversion test, or sunset burden.[S59][S60][S63]

## Small option set

### Option A — one-date switch with minimal transition

Use a published future start date and little else. This can be appropriate for many broad rate or base changes, but it is too blunt when reforms hit illiquid housing, care-burdened households, or small actors already locked into durable commitments.[S23][S36][S37]

### Option B — broad grandfathering of pre-existing positions

Preserve prior treatment for assets, sectors, or incumbents that entered before the new rule. This looks certainty-friendly, but it is usually the archive's worst default because it protects the most entrenched and best-advised actors most strongly, and it lets unjust legacy treatment survive precisely where reform pressure is strongest.[S36][S38][S63]

### Option C — reliance-and-direction ladder

Use **ordinary prospectivity** where that is enough; add **finite phase-in** where adjustment time is morally doing work; use **person-focused liquidity or hardship relief** where the base is justified but cash flow is the problem; and deploy **short anti-forestalling windows** where announcement would otherwise create obvious gaming opportunities.[S23][S27][S36][S37][S69]

This is the archive's cleanest default because it protects vulnerable adjustment without reversing the reform's distributive direction.

### Option D — privilege conversion or expiry instead of grandfathering

Where the old regime mostly reflects negotiated tax holidays, fossil or extractive favoritism, monopoly bottlenecks, subsidized land capture, or AI-incumbent advantage, do **not** grandfather. Instead require the privilege either to **expire on schedule**, **convert into a visible declining support instrument**, or be **replaced by a narrower contractually justified unwind** if the state truly created compensable expectations.[S59][S60][S63][S69]

This keeps transition honest: if the public is really buying out or unwinding a state-created bargain, that cost should be visible rather than hidden inside indefinite tax favoritism.

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt a **five-stage practical ladder**:

| Stage | When it should apply | Transition answer | Main guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. ordinary prospectivity | most broad rate, threshold, or base reforms where actors can adjust before liability begins | **published future effective date only** | do not confuse ordinary political discomfort with protected reliance |
| 1. finite phase-in | broad household or small-firm reforms with real but manageable adjustment strain | **clear ramp, taper, or staged valuation update** | publish the end-state from day one |
| 2. person-focused liquidity relief | the base is still justified but particular households or place-bound small actors face cash-flow strain | **instalments, capped deferral, circuit-breaker style relief, or temporary top-up support** | target vulnerability or illiquidity directly rather than exempting the whole base |
| 3. narrow anti-forestalling | announced reform would otherwise trigger obvious avoidance windows in transfers, realizations, bookings, or siting choices | **short named cutoff or anti-abuse backdating** | explain why pure prospectivity would fail and keep the window narrow |
| 4. privilege conversion / expiry | legacy tax holidays, rent-preserving carve-outs, fossil or extractive preference, subsidized bottleneck advantage, AI-incumbent bargains | **no grandfathering; use expiry, declining conversion support, or explicit contract unwind if truly owed** | make the subsidy visible and time-bounded rather than burying it inside tax law |

The archive's provisional setting is therefore **C by default, with D replacing broad grandfathering wherever the old treatment mostly protects privilege rather than vulnerable reliance**.[S23][S27][S36][S37][S38][S59][S60][S63][S69]

That is the narrowest workable recommendation because it distinguishes **shock avoidance** from **rent preservation**, treats liquidity as a timing problem rather than a moral exemption, keeps anti-avoidance short and named, and forces legacy privilege to justify itself either as a visible temporary public buyout or not at all.

## Anti-patterns

- **Asset-size as vulnerability proxy** — treating large balance sheets or politically organized sectors as presumptively more deserving of transition protection.[S23][S36][S38]
- **Forever phase-in** — a transition path with no real destination, review burden, or sunset.[S59][S60][S63]
- **Grandfathered rentier map** — preserving old land, fossil, monopoly, or AI-incumbent advantage while calling it certainty.[S36][S38][S69]
- **Relief asymmetry** — speeding collection or price effects while delaying rebates, worker support, or local repair.[S23][S69]
- **Contract-language camouflage** — hiding an ongoing subsidy inside the rhetoric of reliance instead of stating openly whether the public is compensating a real state-created bargain.[S59][S60][S63]

## What would change the recommendation

This ladder should be tightened, loosened, or partly replaced if any of the following become clearer:

- administrative data become good enough to target liquidity relief and vulnerable reliance more directly, reducing the need for blunt phase-ins,[S23][S27]
- credible systemic-risk evidence shows that abrupt withdrawal of a legacy rule would cause broader macro or local-service disruption that finite person-focused relief cannot absorb, in which case stage 1 or 2 tools may need temporary strengthening,[S23][S69]
- a state-created bargain really does generate compensable legal expectations, in which case stage 4 should use an explicit, declining, reviewable unwind rather than pretending no transition issue exists,[S59][S60][S63]
- or repeated renewal practice shows that conversion instruments are functioning as hidden permanent support, in which case the archive should shift harder from stage 4 toward outright expiry.[S59][S60][S63]
