# 115 — Proceeds, recycling, and fiscal reciprocity

## One-line thesis

A tax is judged not only by its base and its incidence, but also by **where the proceeds go**. Ideal proceeds routing repairs harms, protects the floor, socialises common rents, and builds shared civic capacity at the scale where the burden arose.

## Why this matters

The same tax can be morally better or worse depending on its destination. A broad consumption tax with a visible rebate is different from the same tax funding opaque privilege. A carbon charge paired with household dividends and adaptation is different from one used to excuse regressive austerity. An AI windfall levy routed into worker transition, public compute, and public-interest capability is different from one that disappears into incumbent subsidy loops.[S1][S4][S7][S13][S22]

## Archive rule

Route proceeds in the order **repair -> protect -> share -> build**.

- **repair** harms and losses caused by the taxed activity,
- **protect** the human floor and other burdened groups,
- **share** common rents and inherited advantages more broadly,
- **build** durable public capacity where private concentration would otherwise dominate.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Corrective taxes should first repair the harm they price

Carbon, congestion, water-stress, and similar corrective taxes are strongest when proceeds are used for some mix of:

- household rebates or dividends,
- adaptation and resilience,
- remediation where local burdens are concentrated,
- and infrastructure that lowers the taxed harm over time.[S6][S7]

### 2. Broad consumption taxes need explicit floor recycling

A VAT or GST can be administratively strong, but its legitimacy depends heavily on what happens next. Default proceeds routing should include:

- visible low-income rebates,
- negative-income support,
- child and disability protection,
- or equivalent public-service offsets that leave protected households no worse off.[S4][S18]

### 3. Land, resource, and inheritance taxes should socialise common advantage

These taxes are attractive not only because of their base quality, but because their proceeds can justly fund:

- local public realm and equalisation,
- universal civic goods,
- child opportunity floors,
- and social dividends from common inheritance.[S5][S8][S18]

### 4. AI and automation rents should fund transition and shared capability

Where AI generates concentrated gains or accelerates displacement, the best default uses of proceeds are:

- wage insurance, unemployment protection, and portability,
- training, apprenticeships, and reskilling,
- public-interest compute and public AI capacity,
- and competition or oversight capacity aimed at bottlenecks.[S13][S14][S15][S22]

### 5. High-risk deployment fees and bonds should not disappear into the general fog

Where firms create outsized incident risk, a material share of proceeds should sit in:

- compensation funds,
- remediation reserves,
- or supervisory capacity tied to the risk class itself.

This is not because every tax must be perfectly earmarked, but because risk-funded systems should not socialise downside while privatising upside.[S16][S17]

### 6. Scale still governs destination

- **Local burdens** justify a strong local share, especially for land, congestion, and siting impacts.
- **National redistribution** should shoulder most floor protection and equalisation.
- **Transnational rents and spillovers** justify treaty or club routing for minimum taxes, carbon coordination, and cross-border AI bottlenecks.[S1][S7][S9][S15]

## Guardrails

- Do not use progressive branding to disguise regressive budget backfilling.
- Do not earmark so rigidly that democratic budgeting becomes impossible.
- Do not let AI-tax proceeds become a subsidy loop for the same concentrated incumbents that generated the rents.
- Do not ignore communities bearing local grid, water, land-use, or congestion burdens from large compute facilities.

## Failure modes

- **Sinkhole budgeting**: the tax is defensible, but proceeds vanish into unrelated fiscal holes while harmed groups see nothing.
- **Moral laundering**: corrective taxes are advertised as justice instruments but finance unrelated patronage.
- **Incumbent capture**: AI or carbon revenues are routed back into subsidies for dominant firms.
- **Distant extraction**: local burdens are real but all revenues flow upward with no local repair or equalisation.

## Practical tests

1. If this tax succeeded, who would see protection, compensation, or shared gain first?
2. Are proceeds routed toward the people or places most exposed to the tax or the taxed harm?
3. Would the same revenue routing still look just if the tax base grew dramatically?
4. Is earmarking doing real moral work here, or only rhetorical work?
