# 107 — Carbon, congestion, and other corrective taxes

## One-line thesis

When an activity imposes public costs, the tax system should price the harm directly instead of pretending those costs are free.

## Why this matters

Corrective taxes are among the clearest moral uses of public pricing: they ask actors to internalize social costs they would otherwise impose on others. Carbon is the central case, but the logic extends to congestion, extraction damage, landfill, toxic release, and certain systemic AI incident risks.[S6][S7]

## Design rule

Price harms directly, then recycle some or all revenue in ways that preserve legitimacy and protect low-income households.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Put the harm unit in view

- ton of CO2e
- congestion zone entry
- extraction unit
- toxic discharge unit
- risky deployment category

### 2. Recycle revenue visibly

Use climate or congestion dividends, transit, adaptation, or public service offsets.

### 3. Keep corrective taxes separate from moral scolding

The aim is not expressive punishment alone; it is institutional cost alignment.

### 4. Extend the logic to AI externalities carefully

Where frontier training or deployment creates measurable public burdens — energy spikes, water stress, incident probability, critical-service risk — pricing tools may be justified. But if the risk is catastrophic, irreversible, or not realistically repairable after the fact, the archive prefers ex ante governance plus sidecar charges over pretending a tax alone has morally solved the problem.[S15][S17][S53][S54]


## Guardrails

- Protect essentials for low-income households through dividends or public alternatives.
- Avoid calling a tax corrective when the harm is not being measured seriously.
- Keep emergency scarcity pricing under democratic review.


## Failure modes

- **Flat burden greenwashing**: climate pricing exists but compensation is too weak.
- **Label inflation**: every disliked activity is called an externality.
- **Revenue dependence trap**: the state becomes dependent on maintaining the harm.


## Practical tests

1. Is the tax unit tied to a real public cost?
2. Are vulnerable households compensated visibly?
3. Is there a complementary transition path, not only a price signal?
