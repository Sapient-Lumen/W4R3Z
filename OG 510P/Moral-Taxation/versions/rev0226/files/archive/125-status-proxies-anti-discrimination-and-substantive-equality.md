# 125 — Status proxies, anti-discrimination, and substantive equality

## One-line thesis

A morally serious tax should not single people out by protected status, nor should it smuggle status hierarchy in through apparently neutral proxies. **Direct discrimination is presumptively unjust; indirect discrimination demands strong public justification, disaggregated evidence, and redesign where a cleaner base is available.** Taxes should track income, rent, harm, control, benefit, need, or repair more directly than they track sex, disability, nationality, family form, neighborhood sorting, documentation status, or other markers of social rank.[S32][S33][S44][S45][S46]

## Why this belongs in a tax archive

1. **Formal neutrality is not enough.** A rule can apply to everyone on paper and still burden one group more heavily in practice through filing design, documentation requirements, geography, interface design, or automated risk scoring.[S45][S46]
2. **Tax systems can either relieve or reproduce hierarchy.** OECD and IMF work on gendered taxation shows that even tax systems without explicit bias can create strong implicit bias through second-earner penalties, household defaults, and the interaction of taxes with unequal labor markets and care burdens.[S32][S33]
3. **AI-era administration makes proxy drift easier.** If tax analytics, withholding platforms, or enforcement models use opaque proxies, discrimination can be laundered through code, vendor scoring, or convenience metrics while still appearing neutral.[S44][S45]
4. **Species is not itself a surcharge basis.** Current LLMs are not exempted from direct-person taxation because machines are a protected class; they are excluded because they are not yet rights-bearing tax persons. If future artificial persons ever exist, they should not face a substrate surcharge merely for being machine-based.[S19][S20]

## Design rule

- Do **not** use protected status itself as a tax base, rate trigger, filing condition, or compliance burden.
- Do **not** rely on apparently neutral proxies when a cleaner measure of income, rent, harm, control, or need is available.
- Where unequal effects are plausible, require **disaggregated impact review** before scaling the rule.
- When support or repair is the goal, target the support directly rather than encoding hierarchy through exclusions or status penalties.

## Common failure modes

- **Household-form bias**: filing or credit rules that assume marriage, co-residence, or full income pooling and thereby penalize second earners, single parents, or informal care networks.[S32][S33]
- **Documentation bias**: rules that tie rebates, appeals, or basic filing access to identification, banking, or digital access patterns that systematically exclude migrants, poor households, or people with disabilities.[S45][S46]
- **Geographic proxy bias**: using neighborhood, postcode, school district, or housing form as a shortcut for service use, fraud risk, or value in ways that track protected characteristics more than the moral target.[S45][S46]
- **Automation bias**: risk models or audit triage that use protected traits or close proxies without intelligible justification, monitoring, or challenge rights.[S44][S45]
- **Species stigma**: proposing a machine-only surcharge on future artificial persons merely because of substrate, rather than because of control, benefit, externality, or ability-to-pay.[S19][S20]

## What a good proposal looks like

A good proposal:

1. names the real moral target,
2. uses the least status-loaded observable base available,
3. checks disaggregated effects before and after rollout,
4. builds accessible filing, notice, and appeal paths,
5. and explains any unequal effect by reference to need, caregiving, repair, or harm rather than to inherited rank or administrative convenience alone.[S32][S33][S44][S45][S46]

## Practical tests

Ask:

1. Would this rule still make sense if every label tied to sex, disability, nationality, ethnicity, family status, or neighborhood sorting were hidden from view?
2. Is the apparent proxy doing real moral work, or is it just an easier substitute for measuring income, care load, harm, or rent directly?
3. Are accessible non-digital, translated, and disability-aware channels available where needed?
4. Has anyone checked whether automated scoring, geographic coding, or documentation filters are producing disparate burdens?
5. If a future machine person existed, would the rule still be justified on ordinary person-tax grounds rather than substrate stigma?

## Bottom line

The archive therefore adds a simple equality discipline: **be status-blind where justice requires equal standing, and status-conscious where repair, accessibility, dependency recognition, or anti-subordination requires active correction**. Do not confuse those two tasks.
