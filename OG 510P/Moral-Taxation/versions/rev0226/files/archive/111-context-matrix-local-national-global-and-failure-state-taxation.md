# 111 — Context matrix: local, national, global, and failure-state taxation

## One-line thesis

There is no single ideal tax instrument for every scale; justice depends partly on matching each base to the level of government that can see, administer, and legitimate it.

## Why this matters

Land taxes fit localities differently than multinational profit floors do. Carbon and compute may require club or treaty coordination. In fragile or failure-state conditions, a simpler and more enforceable tax mix may be morally superior to a theoretically perfect but unusable one.

## Design rule

Choose the tax level by administrative fit, spillover scale, democratic accountability, and jurisdictional claim together. For a sharper nexus rule, pair this note with [`118-nexus-situs-apportionment-and-benefit-jurisdiction.md`](118-nexus-situs-apportionment-and-benefit-jurisdiction.md).

## Pattern pack

### 1. Local-heavy bases

- land and site value
- vacancy and blight
- congestion and curb access
- tourist load or local externality fees

### 2. National-heavy bases

- personal income and transfers
- inheritance
- broad consumption tax
- corporate residual profits
- national carbon and resource regimes

### 3. International-heavy bases

- minimum tax floors
- profit shifting rules
- carbon clubs or border adjustment architecture
- AI compute and cloud bottleneck coordination
- transnational digital platform rent capture
- market-jurisdiction allocation for very large cross-border residual profits

### 4. Failure-state simplification

Where state capacity is weak, prefer enforceable instruments:

- withholding,
- customs and border collection,
- simple excises,
- land cadastre repair,
- resource rents,
- and transparent transfer rules.


## Guardrails

- Avoid pushing local governments into dependence on regressive fees because broader revenue is politically blocked.
- Do not leave siting jurisdictions holding place-bound burdens while profits are booked elsewhere.
- Do not let local competition for facilities become a race to the bottom through long abatements, cheap utility promises, or off-budget concessions that nullify the claimed tax base.[S29][S30]
- Avoid impossible global schemes that never leave the whiteboard.
- In crises, precommit to sunset rules.


## Failure modes

- **Scale mismatch**: local authorities are asked to tax borderless profits or national governments micromanage curb access.
- **Fragile-state perfectionism**: the ideal code collapses because it cannot be administered.
- **Emergency permanence**: crisis levies never leave.
- **Jurisdiction laundering**: booking entities and server geography are used to defeat destination, market, or local-harm claims.


## Practical tests

1. Does the taxing authority actually observe and enforce this base?
2. Are spillovers larger than the jurisdiction trying to tax them?
3. Is the crisis measure trigger-bound and sunset-bound?
4. Which jurisdiction bears the clearest immovable fact, final use, or local burden that justifies first claim?
