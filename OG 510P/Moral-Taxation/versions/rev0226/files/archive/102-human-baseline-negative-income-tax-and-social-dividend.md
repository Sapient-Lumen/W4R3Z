# 102 — Human baseline: negative income tax and social dividend

## One-line thesis

Human persons should encounter the tax system only after a protected floor; below that floor the system should pay in, not pull out.

## Why this matters

Taxation is not only about where burdens fall. It is also about where the public guarantee begins. A just system should start from secure subsistence, childhood support, disability accommodation, and a civic minimum for participation.

## Design rule

For natural persons, the tax-and-transfer system should be jointly designed so that low-income households face net support, not net extraction.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Tax-free or negatively taxed floor

The first dollars needed for subsistence should face zero or negative net taxation.

### 2. Social dividend logic

Part of public revenue should be understood as a dividend from common inheritance:

- legal order
- public infrastructure
- accumulated knowledge
- natural resources
- digital and scientific commons

### 3. Separate floor from work penalty

Support should phase out gradually rather than creating sudden cliffs.

### 4. Fund the floor from strong bases

Prefer land, resource rent, inheritance, carbon dividends, and windfall taxation to payroll extraction.


## Guardrails

- Preserve incentives to take additional work by avoiding harsh benefit cliffs.
- Design household rules to reduce dependency traps and coercive family surveillance.
- Pay refundable supports to the entitled adult or caregiver directly where possible rather than presuming perfect intra-household pooling.
- Do not confuse universal floor protection with indifference to overall fiscal cost.


## Failure modes

- **Poverty recycling**: the state taxes the poor to fund programs for the poor.
- **Welfare maze**: support exists only through fragmented applications and stigma-heavy administration.
- **Household domination**: benefit design quietly reinforces financial control inside households.


## Practical tests

1. Does someone at the bottom face a positive effective tax rate on survival?
2. Are support rules intelligible without expert mediation?
3. Can the floor be financed mainly from rent and harm bases rather than poor people's consumption?
