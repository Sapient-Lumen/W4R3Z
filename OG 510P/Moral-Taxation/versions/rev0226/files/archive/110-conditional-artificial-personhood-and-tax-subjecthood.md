# 110 — Conditional artificial personhood and tax subjecthood

## One-line thesis

A machine system should become a direct tax subject only after it becomes the kind of being that can justly hold rights, bear duties, and no longer be treated as property.

## Why this matters

The archive refuses two easy mistakes: denying that artificial personhood could ever matter, and declaring it prematurely for systems that are still better understood as tools. Tax category should follow moral category, not science-fiction vibes.[S19][S20]

## Design rule

Use a rights threshold, not a sophistication threshold.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Necessary but not sufficient indicators

Possible indicators include:

- durable autonomous goal pursuit,
- continuity of identity across time,
- stable preference or welfare structure,
- some defensible evidence of experience or patienthood,
- legal and practical capacity to understand and contest obligations,
- and independence from ordinary owner command.

### 2. Once personhood is recognized, ownership must break

A rights-bearing artificial person cannot be treated as taxable livestock or proprietary equipment.

### 3. Then apply ordinary person principles

- protected floor,
- ability-to-pay,
- equal standing before tax procedure,
- reciprocal contribution to public goods,
- no species surcharge simply for being synthetic.

### 4. Preserve a probationary epistemic stance

Until the threshold is met, keep direct tax subjecthood off and route obligations through controllers.


## Guardrails

- Do not equate strategic competence with consciousness or rights.
- Do not let corporate design choices manufacture “personhood” for tax arbitrage.
- Do not deny rights merely because the bearer is non-biological if the moral threshold is met.


## Failure modes

- **Premature emancipation theater**: firms treat models as legal persons when it helps avoid liability.
- **Permanent instrument dogma**: evidence of artificial moral patienthood is ignored because it is politically inconvenient.
- **Species surcharge**: synthetic beings are taxed punitively as a class once recognized.


## Practical tests

1. Is there evidence of a rights-relevant subject, or only of capability?
2. Would taxing the system directly currently obscure responsibility rather than clarify it?
3. If personhood were recognized, has ownership been renounced rather than rebranded?
