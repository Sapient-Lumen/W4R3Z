# Residence, membership, presence, and benefit routing

This note answers the question that comes **after** the archive knows a person-, controller-, or place-linked tax claim is possible and **before** it declares which polity may claim the subject at all: **what makes a person-level or deployment-level jurisdictional claim morally real?** The archive's stable answer is to ground broad person-level taxation mainly in durable residence and reciprocal membership, to ground narrower claims in source, destination, and place-bound burden, and to refuse thin substitutes such as bare citizenship, accidental transit, IP-geography, or model vividness.[S2][S3][S4][S9][S18][S19][S20][S21][S23][S28][S31][S42][S43][S64][S69][S70]

## One-line thesis

Tax people where they **really live, belong, receive protected institutions, and can contest civic claims**; tax nonresidents only on narrower source-, destination-, or burden-linked bases; tax current LLM systems through controllers and places rather than through invented machine membership; and if a future artificial person ever exists, assign its tax home by ordinary residence-and-membership logic rather than by owner nationality, rack location, or substrate.[S2][S3][S4][S18][S19][S20][S21][S28][S42][S43][S64]

## Core rule

Ask these questions in order:

1. **Is the supposed direct subject a rights-bearing person with standing, notice, and some channel of representation?** If not, do not build a broad civic tax claim on it; route to a controller, guardian, owner, remitter, or beneficiary instead.[S19][S20][S42][S43]
2. **Where is the subject's durable home or centre of life?** Broad person-level taxation belongs first where life, ordinary protection, and social membership are actually organised, not where a passport was issued or where a short trip happened to occur.[S18][S21][S31][S64]
3. **Which narrower local facts are real even without full residence?** Earnings sourced there, final consumption there, and place-bound burdens imposed there can justify partial claims without converting the subject into a full civic member of that polity.[S3][S21][S23][S28][S70]
4. **Which jurisdiction is actually carrying the protected-floor or adaptation burden?** Where one place is visibly financing shelter, care, disability support, transition, grid reinforcement, housing, water, or cleanup for the subject or the subject's activity, that place has a stronger claim than a paper residence or booking shell alone.[S4][S23][S64][S69][S70]
5. **Would the claim create duplication, statelessness, or membership arbitrage?** Use credits, priority rules, destination logic, or coordinated apportionment before pretending every plausible claimant has a full undivided right.[S3][S9][S21][S28]

## Routing table

| Case | Strongest claim | Best tax shape | Must not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Human person with durable residence and ordinary civic participation | primary residence / membership jurisdiction | ordinary person-level income and floor-protected taxation, with local service taxes where place-bound | do not let bare source claims or old formal ties stack into full duplicate citizenship-style taxation.[S18][S21][S31][S64] |
| Nonresident worker, student, contractor, or commuter with limited but real in-place activity | source or service jurisdiction for the narrow in-place base; residence jurisdiction for the broader person-level base | source withholding, local fees tied to service use, and coordinated credits against residence-side liability | do not upgrade temporary presence into a complete fiscal-membership claim without the reciprocal institutions that would justify it.[S21][S31][S42][S43] |
| Household consuming or buying in a place without deeper membership | destination jurisdiction | broad consumption taxation with the archive's ordinary rebate / floor-protection discipline | do not confuse destination claims over spending with unlimited claims over the whole person.[S3][S4] |
| Migrant, refugee, displaced person, or administratively fragile resident | the jurisdiction actually carrying current shelter, care, schooling, safety, and minimum-income duties, coordinated with any enduring home claim that remains real | protect the floor first, simplify proof, and avoid double-residence traps while status is unstable | do not make paperwork instability, camp status, missing banking, or interrupted records a reason to deny floor protection while still collecting tax.[S21][S31][S42][S43][S64][S69] |
| Host locality bearing visible burden from a workplace, facility, data centre, extractive site, or migration inflow | burdened locality or situs-linked jurisdiction | land value capture, congestion, utility-burden, adaptation, housing, water, or cleanup charges | do not tell the host community that broad national or global taxation elsewhere fully substitutes for its direct burden claim.[S5][S23][S69][S70] |
| Current LLM, model instance, swarm, or checkpoint as such | none directly as a civic member | no direct person-level taxation; route to controllers, deployers, beneficiaries, facilities, destination markets, and harms | do not infer membership from server location, user geography, copy count, or conversational salience.[S19][S20][S28] |
| Controller-routed AI stack crossing borders | local claim for siting burdens, national claim for controller-side profits and labor transition, coordinated market claim for mobile rents | split claims by burden, control, market access, and mobile-rent coordination | do not let one booking jurisdiction, cloud region, or host locality monopolise the whole fiscal story.[S9][S21][S28][S69][S70] |
| Large downstream beneficiary capturing extraordinary AI-enabled gain across borders | beneficiary's ordinary fiscal home plus coordinated market-side sharing where customer markets materially co-create the surplus | broad beneficiary-side claims follow the real beneficiary and its markets, while local burdened places keep narrower repair claims | do not infer the claim from server geography, prompt geography, or the upstream controller's domicile.[S3][S4][S9][S10][S11][S14][S21][S22][S28][S67][S69][S70][S83] |
| Future rights-bearing artificial person, if a real regime switch happens | ordinary residence / membership jurisdiction once non-owned personhood and representation are real | person-level taxation on ordinary ability-to-pay grounds after floor protection, plus normal local place-linked taxes | do not keep owner-side substitution, equipment-site logic, or a machine-species surcharge once personhood is recognised.[S18][S19][S20][S42][S43] |

## Three routing rules that matter most

### 1. Broad civic taxation follows lived membership more than formal label

The archive does not treat citizenship, registration residue, or nominal incorporation alone as enough for the strongest annual claim over a person's whole fiscal life. The stronger claim usually sits where daily life, protected-floor institutions, schooling, care, labor protection, and ordinary public obligations actually meet the person.[S18][S21][S31][S64]

### 2. Narrow in-place claims can be real without swallowing the whole person

Work performed in a place, goods and services consumed there, and local burdens imposed there can justify source, destination, or burden-linked taxation even when the subject's deeper civic home lies elsewhere. This is how the archive avoids both residence monopoly and every-place taxation.[S3][S21][S23][S28][S70]

### 3. Machine systems do not get civic membership by metaphor

Current LLMs remain routed through human and firm responsibility chains. The fact that a model speaks in first person, serves local users, or runs on local hardware does not by itself create a civic membership relation strong enough for direct tax personhood. Only a real non-owned, rights-bearing person-regime switch would change that, and then ordinary residence-and-membership logic would govern.[S19][S20][S42][S43]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Passport taxation without lived reciprocity** — asserting the broadest fiscal claim mainly from formal nationality or historic registry when ordinary protection and social membership lie elsewhere.
2. **Transit-as-membership** — treating tourism, temporary presence, airport layover logic, or brief remote work as if it justifies full person-level liability.
3. **Benefit rhetoric without visible burden** — invoking vague "benefit" while the jurisdiction cannot point to any real floor provision, infrastructure burden, market protection, or civic service.
4. **Server-location personhood** — treating a model's hardware region as the model's civic home.
5. **Double-full-claim design** — allowing several jurisdictions to assert total undivided claims over the same person or mobile rent without credits, priority, or apportionment.[S3][S9][S19][S20][S21][S28][S42][S43]
