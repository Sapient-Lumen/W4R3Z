# Bounded contest, issue-scoped correction, and period-finality routing

Use this card when the live dispute is no longer only **what the right tax answer is**, but **how long the answer should stay open, how narrow a correction can be, and what facts justify reopening a settled period**. The archive's stable answer is to open a **short and visible contest lane**, accept a **pointed objection before demanding a full rival dossier**, correct at the **smallest issue-set that can honestly change liability**, let the result harden into **operative period-bounded finality** after proportionate review, and reopen only for **material new facts, material error, integrity failure, or successor / step-in change** rather than ordinary regret.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S38][S39][S40][S41][S44][S83]

This is a general routing rule, not only a controller-map rule. It applies wherever tax justice would otherwise fail through **endless provisionality, first-filer privilege, whole-case reset for a narrow mistake, or permanent retention justified by hypothetical future reopening**: payroll and household reconciliation, property-value disputes, beneficial-ownership grouping, cross-border allocation fights, AI controller mapping, and model-assisted high-volume assessments.[S16][S17][S21][S27][S38][S39][S40][S41][S44][S83][S89]

## Default lanes

| Situation | Default finality lane | Why this lane usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Routine wage, transfer, or household-year reconciliation | short correction window plus annual true-up, then period finality | ordinary people need closure after a usable correction path, not rolling uncertainty | do not let small code or withholding errors reopen whole years after the relevant proof has gone stale.[S21][S27][S38][S39][S41][S65][S66] |
| Property, land, or simplified-regime classification dispute | issue-scoped contest on valuation, situs, or regime fit, with forward-looking correction where possible | narrow errors should usually stay narrow | do not relitigate the whole account because one appraisal field or threshold decision was wrong.[S23][S25][S27][S38][S39][S41] |
| Beneficial-ownership, grouping, controller, or cross-border allocation dispute | pointed objection or short counter-map, provisional filing while review runs, then operative finality for the period | filing, crediting, and enforcement need a settled working answer even where the structure is complex | do not make early packet quality or strategic delay decide the whole case by default.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S39][S40][S41][S83] |
| High-volume automated or model-assisted assessments | visible correction code, narrow evidence request, reversible review, then closure | machine-scaled administration needs bounded error lanes more than perpetual auditability theater | do not use black-box scoring to reopen settled periods without a material reason.[S16][S17][S27][S39][S40][S41][S44][S68][S89] |
| Future rights-bearing artificial person, if threshold is crossed | represented contest path, clear issue scope, and period finality after review | direct civic liability without an intelligible endpoint would be domination, not administration | no direct finality without standing, remedy, and non-conflicted representation.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Contest windows should be clear, short, and visible

A period cannot harden fairly if affected parties never knew when or how to object. The archive prefers visible contest windows that name the period, the kinds of issues fit for challenge, the channel for objection, and the consequences of silence.[S16][S17][S21][S27][S38][S39][S41][S44]

### 2. Pointed objection usually comes before full rival reconstruction

Ordinary challengers should usually be able to identify the disputed field, omitted actor, stale fact, wrong threshold, or contradictory record without reproducing the whole liability file. Full rival packets belong mainly where the challenger is well placed to know the competing facts.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S39][S41][S83]

### 3. Correct at the smallest honest issue-set

The archive prefers **issue-scoped correction** over whole-case reset. If the live error concerns one valuation field, one affiliate link, one period event, or one proxy input, correct that field and any tightly coupled consequences rather than reopening every settled element of the account.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S39][S40][S41][S83]

### 4. Finality should be period-bounded, not eternal immunity

Once the notice, contest, and proportionate review ladder has run, the result should harden for the relevant period so filing, refunds, credits, escrow release, and enforcement do not remain permanently provisional. Finality is a working closure rule, not a declaration that the actor may never be questioned again on later periods.[S17][S20][S21][S27][S38][S39][S41][S83]

### 5. Reopen only for material change, material error, or integrity failure

Later reopening is justified when new facts, corrected identity or chain errors, successor continuity, step-in command, sham redaction, spoliation, or similar integrity failures could realistically change liability. Reopening is not justified merely because a party dislikes the settled answer or finds a new lawyer willing to reframe the same facts.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S39][S40][S41][S83]

### 6. Retention should match contest and reopening needs, not infinite hypothetical review

Records, logs, and machine artifacts should be preserved long enough to support contest, correction, and material-change reopening, but not kept forever merely because perpetual provisionality would be convenient for the state. Finality and minimization work together.[S16][S17][S27][S39][S40][S41][S89][S91][S92]

### 7. Keep provisional filing and escrow separate from endless non-finality

Where review takes time, provisional filing, escrow, or temporary allocation can keep the system moving. But those bridges should end in a settled period answer after the bounded contest lane runs; provisional machinery is not an excuse to keep everyone permanently unsure who owed what.[S17][S20][S21][S27][S39][S41][S83]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Perpetual provisionality** — some theoretical objection always prevents closure.  
2. **First-filer privilege** — the earliest packet or easiest claimant wins practical finality because others lacked time or access.  
3. **Whole-case reset** — a narrow error reopens every settled field.  
4. **Reopening by regret** — unchanged facts are relitigated because the outcome is unpopular.  
5. **Infinite-retention by non-finality** — the archive or the state keeps everything forever because it never chooses a closure rule.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S38][S39][S40][S41][S83]

## Compression rule

If a dispute mainly turns on **how long a claim stays open, whether a narrow error should reopen the whole period, or what facts justify a later reopening**, use this card plus the archive's compact general finality ladder and any relevant administration, proof, sanctions, controller-map, or retention ladder rather than adding a new sector-specific finality doctrine.
