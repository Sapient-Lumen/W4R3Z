# Anti-discrimination and status-proxy routing

This note answers the question that comes **after** a tax base, assessment unit, and collection path look administratively workable but **before** the state can claim the design is morally neutral: **is the rule actually targeting income, rent, harm, control, benefit, or need, or is it leaning on protected status and status proxies instead?** The archive's stable answer is to use the least status-loaded measure that can still do the moral job, require disaggregated review where unequal effects are plausible, and refuse both explicit hierarchy and proxy laundering through household form, documentation, geography, or automated scoring.[S19][S20][S32][S33][S44][S45][S46]

## Core rule

Route equality review through this order:

1. **ask whether the rule explicitly differentiates by protected status, family form, nationality, disability, ethnicity, or substrate rather than by income, harm, control, benefit, or need**,  
2. **ask whether an apparently neutral proxy such as household default, documentation requirement, neighborhood code, interface channel, or audit score is doing the same hierarchy work indirectly**,  
3. **where unequal effects are plausible, require disaggregated review, accessible notice and filing paths, and a real correction channel before scaling the rule**,  
4. **if support, repair, or accessibility is the goal, target that goal directly instead of encoding rank through penalties, exclusions, or burdensome conditions**,  
5. and **if a cleaner measure exists, prefer it even when the proxy is administratively tempting**.[S32][S33][S44][S45][S46]

A good equality rule is not blind to human difference in every context. It is blind where equal standing requires blindness, and consciously corrective where dependency, disability, exclusion, or repair require real accommodation rather than formal sameness.[S32][S33][S44][S45][S46]

## Default routing by context

| Context | Best first equality answer | Why this answer usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary earnings taxation | tax the individual person on earnings and use direct income measures for progression | person-based liability avoids household-status shortcuts and second-earner traps | do not let marriage, co-residence, or default jointness quietly decide who counts fiscally.[S32][S33][S45][S46] |
| Child, disability, care, or dependency support | use direct support, services, accessibility rules, or refundable relief | the goal is accommodation or repair, not hidden hierarchy | do not confuse targeted support with status penalty or paternalistic exclusion.[S32][S33][S44][S46] |
| Means-tested credits, rebates, or floor protection | use the least exclusionary verification path compatible with integrity | documentation and banking chokepoints often screen out the very people the policy claims to protect | proof burdens can recreate caste-like access even when rates look progressive on paper.[S45][S46] |
| Place-based taxes, exemptions, or enforcement targeting | use geography only where place itself is morally central, such as land situs or localized harm | location sometimes matters genuinely, but it is also a common proxy for protected status | do not smuggle ethnicity, poverty, migration status, or household form through postcode or neighborhood coding.[S45][S46] |
| Model-assisted audit or triage | allow bounded assistance only with monitoring, explainability, and challenge rights | automation can scale both error and bias | a black-box score is not morally neutral just because no human slur appears in the rule text.[S44][S45] |
| Current LLMs and AI systems | tax controllers, deployers, grouped beneficiaries, rents, and harms rather than substrate | present systems are not direct civic persons, so equality analysis attaches to humans and institutions around them | do not treat machine substrate as either a stigma basis or a shortcut around standing analysis.[S19][S20][S44] |
| Future rights-bearing artificial person, if threshold is crossed | move to ordinary person-regime taxation with equal-standing safeguards | once personhood is real, substrate should not itself trigger a surcharge | do not preserve machine-only burdens after rights-bearing status is recognized.[S19][S20][S42][S44] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Be status-blind where equal standing is the point

Protected status should not itself set the tax base, rate, filing burden, or audit intensity. That is true for sex, ethnicity, nationality, disability, family form, and — if future artificial persons ever exist — substrate alone.[S19][S20][S44][S45][S46]

### 2. Be status-conscious where accessibility, dependency, or repair is the point

Equality does not always mean identical treatment. Disability access, caregiving support, language access, offline channels, and dependency recognition may require affirmative accommodation. The archive rejects both crude hierarchy and fake neutrality that ignores real barriers.[S32][S33][S44][S46]

### 3. Prefer direct moral measures over social-rank proxies

If the real target is income, care load, harm, control, or benefit, measure that more directly instead of using household composition, neighborhood sorting, migration paperwork, or platform risk labels as rough substitutes. Proxy convenience is not enough when coercive fiscal power is at stake.[S32][S33][S45][S46]

### 4. Treat automation as a multiplier, not a cleansing agent

Risk models, triage systems, and interface defaults can launder discrimination through scale. The archive therefore prefers explainable use, monitored disparate-impact testing, bounded feature sets, and meaningful challenge rights before automated classifications shape liability, audit exposure, or access to relief.[S44][S45]

### 5. Watch the recurring proxy traps

The archive repeatedly flags household defaults, identification requirements, banking access, neighborhood coding, language assumptions, disability-blind interface design, and online-only claiming as common places where a formally neutral tax begins tracking social hierarchy instead.[S32][S33][S45][S46]

### 6. Keep personhood analysis separate from substrate prejudice

Current LLMs are excluded from direct civic tax subjecthood because they are not yet rights-bearing persons with standing, not because machines are a disfavored caste. If future artificial persons ever exist, they should be taxed on ordinary person-regime grounds rather than through a machine-species surcharge.[S19][S20][S42][S44]

### 7. Review both design and operation

A rule that looks clean in statute can still fail in administration. Check disaggregated take-up, audit selection, denial rates, refund delays, appeal success, and channel access after rollout, especially when automated scoring or place-based targeting is involved.[S44][S45][S46]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Household neutrality theatre** — pretending joint defaults or second-earner penalties are natural rather than status-laden.  
2. **Paperwork caste systems** — making relief or ordinary compliance depend on IDs, banking, or digital access patterns that predictably exclude protected groups.  
3. **Postcode laundering** — using geography or housing form as a stand-in for social rank when place is not itself the moral object.  
4. **Black-box disparate impact** — letting opaque models shape audit, withholding, or refund treatment without intelligible oversight.  
5. **Substrate stigma** — treating machine embodiment itself as a surcharge basis if future artificial persons ever qualify as persons.[S19][S20][S32][S33][S44][S45][S46]

## Order of preference

When more than one workable rule is available, the archive usually prefers the rule that is:

1. least dependent on protected status or close status proxies,
2. most directly tied to income, rent, harm, control, benefit, or need,
3. easiest to monitor for disparate effects,
4. most compatible with accessible filing, notice, and appeal,
5. least likely to let automation or geography launder hierarchy,
6. and easiest to explain publicly without appealing to inherited rank.

This is why direct income and care measures, accessible support design, person-based ordinary liability, and explainable review usually beat household defaults, documentation chokepoints, postcode targeting, or black-box fraud scores.
