# Supplier, creator, and contractor pass-through routing

This note answers a question left too implicit between the archive's **beneficiary-side windfall** lane, its **worker-residual** lane, and its ordinary **incidence** rules: **if a downstream AI winner says the gain is already being bid away into higher payments to suppliers, creators, licensors, contractors, franchisees, or other complementors, should the archive still treat that gain as concentrated private windfall?** The archive's stable answer is **sometimes no, but only when the pass-through is real in net terms**. Durable broad improvement in what independent counterparties are paid is not the same moral object as retained concentrated surplus. But affiliate routing, captive dependence, offsetting fee recapture, pace intensification, exclusivity, or headline royalty increases that coexist with persistent exceptional margins do not dissolve the beneficiary lane.[S10][S11][S14][S18][S21][S22][S26][S27][S67][S83][S104]

## Core rule

Route these cases in order:

1. **Ask whether the alleged counterparties are real outside claimants.** Do not treat affiliates, shell licensors, captive vendors, or paper marketplaces as proof that the gain has already diffused outward.[S10][S18][S21][S27][S34][S35][S83]
2. **Ask whether the improvement is broad, durable, and net-positive.** Higher royalties, better rev-share, stronger minimum guarantees, better supplier pricing, or lower contractor extraction can matter only if they survive netting for new fees, exclusivity, tighter pacing, shifted risk, or weaker bargaining terms elsewhere.[S11][S14][S21][S22][S26][S27][S67][S83][S104]
3. **Separate independent supplier benefit from worker or public-service benefit.** Better terms for contractors, creators, and complementors can morally narrow a downstream windfall claim without turning the case into public-service routing or broad worker reciprocity.[S11][S18][S21][S22][S26][S27][S67][S83]
4. **Compare pass-through against retained margin.** Where counterparties are genuinely being paid more and residual margins stay ordinary, the windfall story weakens. Where pass-through appears but retained exceptional surplus remains large, the beneficiary lane stays live for that retained slice.[S10][S11][S21][S22][S28][S67][S83]
5. **Keep dependence, incidence, and local burden review alive.** Apparent upstream benefit does not erase worker displacement, supplier dependence, local repair claims, or controller-side duties if those are otherwise real.[S10][S15][S21][S22][S28][S67][S69][S83][S104]

## Routing table

| Case | Strongest first answer | Best fiscal shape | Must not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Broad independent supplier or creator ecosystem is visibly receiving better durable terms while downstream margins stay ordinary | ordinary tax plus incidence / supplier-benefit review | ordinary profit tax, ordinary person-side taxation on received income, and protected-burden review where needed | do not relabel a genuinely diffused gain as concentrated windfall merely because AI is involved.[S10][S11][S14][S21][S22][S26][S27][S67][S83] |
| Large downstream firm claims creator or supplier benefit while keeping persistent supra-normal margins or bargaining power | beneficiary-side screening still applies for the retained surplus | ordinary tax first; add bounded beneficiary-side contribution if concentrated gain remains materially undertaxed | do not let selective rev-share or supplier bonuses launder a real windfall.[S10][S11][S21][S22][S28][S67][S83] |
| Claimed pass-through runs through affiliates, captive marketplaces, house labels, or related-party licensors | anti-fragmentation and substance-over-form routing first | group screening, ordinary profit taxation, and beneficiary review on the real coordinated actor | do not treat intragroup transfers as social diffusion.[S10][S18][S21][S27][S34][S35][S83] |
| Supplier or creator "benefit" is offset by exclusivity, higher fees, tighter control, faster pace, or more shifted risk | skepticism first | incidence review, private-ordering scrutiny, and ordinary or beneficiary-side taxation of retained surplus | do not confuse gross payment with net counterpart-benefit.[S21][S22][S26][S27][S67][S83][S104] |
| Mixed case with some real upstream pass-through and some retained downstream exceptional gain | split the story rather than force one label | ordinary tax plus narrower beneficiary-side charge only on the retained exceptional surplus; keep person-side or supplier-side income tax for the received share | do not force all-or-nothing classification when both diffusion and concentration are materially real.[S10][S11][S14][S21][S22][S67][S83] |

## Four routing rules that matter most

### 1. Real upstream pass-through can narrow the windfall story

The archive does not assume that every downstream AI winner is keeping the upside. In some sectors, competition, procurement pressure, creator bargaining, collective contracting, or complementor scarcity may force part of the gain outward into better payments or terms for outside counterparties. Where that happens durably enough that residual margins are no longer the main story, the beneficiary-side windfall case narrows or disappears.[S10][S11][S14][S21][S22][S26][S27][S67][S83]

### 2. Gross payment is not the same thing as net counterpart benefit

A firm does not escape by pointing to some larger royalty, bonus pool, or contractor payment. The archive asks whether outside counterparties are actually better off after fees, dependency, pace, exclusivity, shifted risk, and hidden recapture are counted. A bigger check paired with tighter lock-in or weaker bargaining is not necessarily real diffusion.[S11][S14][S21][S22][S26][S27][S67][S83][S104]

### 3. Supplier benefit is different from worker, saver, customer, or public-service routing

Better terms for creators, licensors, contractors, franchisees, or complementors can morally matter without converting the institution into a public body, mutual, worker-benefit organization, or customer-diffusion case. The right answer is often simply that some gain has already diffused into upstream counterparties enough that ordinary taxation and incidence review are doing more of the work.[S11][S18][S21][S22][S26][S27][S67][S83]

### 4. Mixed cases should tax the retained exceptional residual, not the gross activity

The cleanest design in mixed cases is to leave ordinary activity on the ordinary tax base, identify the part of the gain that truly remains exceptionally concentrated after real upstream pass-through, and tax only that retained slice if ordinary taxation is missing it. The archive still rejects seat taxes, per-prompt taxes, or gross-usage levies as stand-ins for moral surplus.[S10][S11][S15][S16][S21][S22][S67][S83]

## Anti-patterns

1. **supplier-halo laundering** — pointing to some creator, contractor, or supplier payments as if they automatically erased retained concentrated gain,
2. **affiliate-pass-through theater** — routing the claim through related-party licensors, house labels, or captive vendors and calling it outside diffusion,
3. **gross-not-net defense** — highlighting higher payouts while ignoring exclusivity, risk shifting, fee recapture, or harder operating terms,
4. **all-or-nothing lane forcing** — refusing to admit mixed cases where some gain diffuses upstream and some remains concentrated,
5. **dependency-blind reciprocity** — praising counterpart payments while dependent suppliers, creators, or contractors remain structurally weak and easy to squeeze.[S10][S11][S14][S18][S21][S22][S26][S27][S67][S83][S104]
