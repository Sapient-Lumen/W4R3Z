# Public, mutual, and nonprofit beneficiary routing

This note answers a question left open once the archive accepted bounded beneficiary-side windfall contributions: **if a public body, charity, mutual, cooperative, university, hospital, or regulated utility uses AI or automation to generate large savings or service gains without a real private residual claimant, should the archive treat that gain like an ordinary private downstream windfall?** The archive's stable answer is usually no. First separate **service-side capacity gain, member or ratepayer relief, and public-benefit savings** from **distributable private rent**. Where no meaningful private residual claimant is present, route first through service quality, rate or member relief, worker protection, local repair, and anti-supplantation rather than through a generic beneficiary-side windfall charge. But public, mutual, or nonprofit form does **not** erase controller duties, ordinary taxation, or hidden private capture.[S10][S11][S18][S21][S22][S29][S64][S67][S69][S70][S83]

## Core rule

Route these cases in order. For settings on how much should appear as service quality, member or rate relief, worker transition, local repair, or disclosed retained capacity, use [`../20-calibration/public-service-member-relief-and-local-repair-ladder.md`](../20-calibration/public-service-member-relief-and-local-repair-ladder.md).


1. **Ask whether there is a real private residual claimant.** If executives, contractors, financiers, affiliates, or quasi-equity holders are extracting the gain, do not let public or nonprofit form do the moral work by itself.[S18][S21][S29][S83]
2. **Separate service or member gain from private windfall.** Budget relief, shorter queues, better care, lower rates, lower member charges, or stronger public capacity are not the same object as distributable private surplus.[S11][S22][S64][S67][S69]
3. **If the institution is rationing rights, public access, or regulated prices, governance comes before windfall language.** Public-law, anti-discrimination, rate-review, and due-process questions often dominate the tax question here.[S18][S21][S44][S69][S74]
4. **Keep gains visible and reciprocal.** If AI-enabled savings are morally defended as public or member benefit, they should show up in service quality, user or member relief, worker transition, local repair, or plainly disclosed fiscal capacity — not disappear into a dark treasury or managerial slack.[S22][S29][S64][S69][S70]
5. **Use ordinary tax and contractor-side tools where private extraction is the real target.** Salary, procurement rents, management fees, affiliate profits, land rents, vendor profits, and executive upside stay taxable on their own bases.[S10][S21][S29][S83]
6. **Controller-side, facility-side, and risk-side duties still travel separately.** A public body or nonprofit can still be a real controller, co-controller, local-burden payer, or risk creator even if it is not the right target for a private-style beneficiary windfall claim.[S10][S16][S17][S20][S21][S70][S83]

## Routing table

| Case | Strongest first answer | Best fiscal shape | Must not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Municipal or national agency using AI to cut back-office cost or speed ordinary administration | public-capacity, service-quality, and rights-protection routing first | visible service gain, worker-transition planning, maintenance of effort, and ordinary taxation of any private vendors or contractors | do not call internal budget relief a private windfall just because the savings are large.[S21][S22][S29][S64][S69] |
| Public hospital, school, university, or care system using AI to reallocate staffing or triage work | service-access, care-quality, labor-protection, and anti-discrimination routing first | keep gains in quality, access, staffing resilience, member or user relief, or transparent public-capacity expansion | do not tax service savings away while claiming the institution should somehow improve care with less.[S11][S14][S22][S32][S33][S64][S69][S74] |
| Mutual, cooperative, or regulated utility using automation to reduce costs | rate or member-benefit routing first | rate relief, member dividends, resilience investment, local-burden repair, and ordinary tax on any genuine private rent | do not treat ratepayer or member benefit as if it were shareholder windfall.[S18][S21][S22][S69][S70][S83] |
| Nonprofit or public shell with executive capture, contractor rents, monopoly margins, or related-party extraction | substance-over-form and ordinary/private-rent taxation first | tax the real private salaries, fees, profits, land rents, or concentrated beneficiary gains on the actors actually taking them | do not let the halo of charity, mission, or public branding shield private capture.[S10][S18][S21][S29][S34][S35][S83] |
| Public or nonprofit principal commissioning a vendor-hosted AI stack with real mission control | controller or co-controller routing for the governed layer, plus this note for any service-gain question | keep controller duties where real command sits; then decide whether any remaining gain is public-benefit, member-benefit, or private extraction | do not use nonprofit status to deny controller duties once live command is real.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S41][S83] |

## Four routing rules that matter most

### 1. No private residual claimant, no ordinary private-windfall logic by default

The archive accepted beneficiary-side windfall claims because some downstream actors can capture concentrated automation-linked upside while remaining outside primary controller status. That logic weakens sharply when the gain is not a distributable private residual but a service budget, member surplus, or regulated-rate cushion. In those cases the better moral question is how the gain should be shared, constrained, or made visible — not how to tax it again as if it were shareholder surplus.[S11][S18][S21][S22][S67][S83]

### 2. Public and member savings should become visible public and member benefit before they become treasury dark matter

Where AI-enabled savings are real, the archive prefers service quality, access, rate relief, worker-transition support, local repair, or openly disclosed fiscal capacity over opaque clawback. This is the service-side analogue of proceeds integrity: if savings are defended as public benefit, they should not vanish into undisclosed substitution or managerial discretion.[S22][S29][S64][S69][S70]

### 3. Mutual and utility cases are usually rate-review or member-benefit problems, not beneficiary-windfall problems

If a regulated utility, mutual insurer, credit union, or cooperative automates successfully, the morally central question is usually whether members, ratepayers, workers, and burdened localities share the gain fairly. The archive therefore routes first to rate design, member benefit, local repair, and ordinary taxation of any genuine private side-payments rather than to a free-floating windfall story.[S18][S21][S22][S69][S70][S83]

### 4. Public or nonprofit form is not a moral invisibility cloak

A public authority can still ration rights unjustly. A nonprofit can still outsource extraction. A mutual can still entrench managerial slack. A university or hospital can still impose labor or data burdens. The archive therefore treats form as informative but never conclusive: where the real gain is private, tax the private gain; where the real issue is governance, rights, or service integrity, route there first.[S10][S18][S20][S21][S29][S44][S74][S83]

## Anti-patterns

1. **service-savings cosplay** — treating public or member savings as if they were ordinary private windfall merely because the numbers are large,
2. **treasury-dark-matter clawback** — claiming public benefit while letting the gain vanish into undisclosed substitution,
3. **charitable halo shielding** — using nonprofit or public branding to protect executive, contractor, or affiliate extraction,
4. **ratepayer amnesia** — leaving automation gains inside utility or mutual managerial slack instead of visible member or user benefit,
5. **public-body innocence theater** — denying controller, due-process, anti-discrimination, or local-burden duties because the institution is public or mission-branded.[S10][S18][S20][S21][S22][S29][S44][S69][S70][S74][S83]
