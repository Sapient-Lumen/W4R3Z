# Private ordering, non-waiver, and anti-indemnification routing

Use this card when the live dispute is not the tax base alone but **whether contracts, clickwrap terms, invoices, leases, procurement language, indemnities, or reimbursement clauses are being used to rewrite the moral allocation of burden, duty, or remedy**. The archive's stable answer is that **public-law tax routing outranks private drafting where the two conflict**: strong parties may settle reimbursement among themselves after payment, but they should not be able to waive protected-floor relief, erase public claims, dump controller duties onto weak actors, or make complaint and correction rights disappear by form terms.[S20][S21][S27][S31][S34][S35][S64][S65][S66][S75][S83][S104][S105][S106]

Contracts still matter as evidence. They can show who expected to govern, who priced the deal, who held records, who kept residual upside, and who promised to reimburse whom. But **contract text is evidence of control, not a magic solvent for tax justice**. Where bargaining is adhesive, dependent, or structurally unequal, scrutiny should rise rather than fall.[S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S75][S83][S104][S105][S106]

## Default lanes

| Situation | Default public-law answer | Why this path usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Worker, dependent contractor, temp, or migrant worker is told to absorb payroll-like tax, recruitment charges, unexplained deductions, or benefit-side clawback through contract terms | keep statutory withholding, wage protection, and protected-floor rules above the contract; unauthorized recapture from weak workers is presumptively defective | bargaining power is weak, wage visibility is upstream, and ordinary earning floors should not be waived in exchange for access to work | do not let job access, visa dependence, or contractor labeling turn employer-side or principal-side obligations into worker-side liabilities.[S31][S64][S75][S104] |
| Consumer, subscriber, or household faces hidden tax-like fees, waiver of refunds or rebates, or terms restricting complaints and charge disputes | merchant / platform collection may stand, but mandatory disclosure, refund, complaint, and correction rights stay non-waivable | standard-form consumer contracts are a poor place to bury public burdens or relief conditions | do not use checkout theater, fee labels, or fine print to shift a public burden onto households that cannot realistically negotiate.[S21][S64][S65][S66][S105][S106] |
| Tenant, host locality, or utility customer is asked to swallow facility-side tax, cleanup, congestion, or host-burden duties through lease clauses or side agreements | local public claims, cleanup duties, and host-burden repair outrank private side deals; private reimbursement may occur only after public obligations are satisfied | the affected place and weaker residents cannot negotiate on equal terms with concentrated facility operators | do not let abatements, lease pass-throughs, or siting side letters null the repair path for real local burdens.[S15][S23][S29][S69][S70][S79][S83] |
| Small supplier, franchisee, app developer, or dependent business counterparty is made to indemnify a dominant buyer or platform for the buyer's own controller-side or withholding failures | keep remittance and reporting with the strongest record-holder and real controlling party first; reimbursement disputes come second | concentrated actors usually see the facts, price the deal, and can spread compliance cost more fairly | do not let a dominant principal use standard terms to dump opacity, penalty risk, or filing work downstream.[S21][S27][S34][S35][S65][S68][S83] |
| AI lab, cloud, deployer, managed-service vendor, agency, or public body uses contract language to place primary controller-side liability on a user, nominal shell, reseller, or outsourced operator that lacks real governance power | follow real command, continuation power, treasury, release control, and residual upside; treat indemnities and service descriptions as secondary evidence only | controller duties should track the actor that can materially govern the system, not the actor easiest to paper over | do not let clickwrap, white-label, or procurement drafting launder real controller duties into weaker hands.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S41][S83] |
| A future artificial person is claimed to owe civic liability while an owner, vendor, or sponsor still keeps property-style command or can waive the system's rights by contract | reject the split: either the system is still owner-routed property, or personhood arrives as a bundle with non-ownership, standing, and non-waivable basic protections | personhood without real independence is liability dumping, not moral recognition | do not keep upside and command on the owner side while externalizing civic duties onto a nominal machine person.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S101] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Public-law allocation beats private labels

The right public taxpayer, remitter, controller, or protected claimant is determined by the archive's routing rules, not by whichever party drafted the form contract. Contract text can help identify control or reimbursement expectations, but it should not override the stronger rule about who bears public duties and who stays protected.[S20][S21][S34][S35][S83][S105][S106]

### 2. Protected floors, complaint rights, and basic remedies should be hard to waive

A system that lets weaker parties sign away refunds, complaint channels, wage protection, or basic floor repair is not morally neutral just because a box was checked. Where the public scheme promises minimum protection, the default should be non-waiver or very narrow waiver under conditions of real understanding and roughly equal power.[S31][S64][S65][S66][S104][S105][S106]

### 3. Indemnity can reallocate reimbursement among strong actors, not erase the public claim

Two well-capitalized firms may agree about who reimburses whom after tax, penalty, cleanup, or reserve payment. That may matter commercially, but it does **not** decide who the state may collect from first, who had to disclose, or whether a protected third party was unlawfully used as the buffer.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S83]

### 4. Adhesion and dependence raise the suspicion level

The less negotiable the term and the more dependent the counterparty, the less confidence the archive places in contractual allocation. Employment dependence, platform dependence, migration dependence, tenancy dependence, and consumer clickwrap all weaken the moral force of paper allocation when it conflicts with tax justice or public protection.[S31][S64][S75][S104][S105][S106]

### 5. Hidden pass-through by contract is still incidence

If a controller-side, merchant-side, or landlord-side obligation is pushed downstream by non-salient terms, the archive still counts that as part of the real burden map. The answer is not merely better drafting; it may require redesign, rebate repair, prohibition, or a different collection anchor.[S4][S21][S27][S64][S65][S66][S83][S105]

### 6. For AI, contracts prove governance only when matched by real operational facts

Managed-service labels, API terms, reseller agreements, procurement documents, and white-label contracts can all contain useful clues. But the archive asks the harder question: who could continue, pause, reconfigure, fund, authorize, or materially redirect the governed system? That is the actor or real controller group the tax system should generally face first.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S41][S83]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Indemnity laundering** — a dominant actor keeps the upside and control but writes broad reimbursement clauses so weaker parties absorb the downside.  
2. **Clickwrap controller dumping** — API, platform, or managed-service terms pretend the ordinary user is the real governor of a stack they cannot actually control.  
3. **Relief waiver by fine print** — rebates, refunds, complaint rights, or floor protections disappear inside form terms that ordinary people cannot realistically negotiate.  
4. **Lease-side burden erasure** — host burdens, cleanup duties, or local repair are nullified by private side letters even though the public burden remains real.  
5. **Fake regime split** — a future artificial person is given liabilities while ownership, command, and waiver power remain elsewhere.[S20][S21][S31][S41][S64][S75][S83][S104][S105][S106]

## Compression rule

If a case mainly turns on **whether private drafting is trying to undo the archive's public routing answer**, use this card plus the relevant subjecthood, incidence, filing-parity, standing, or classification note rather than adding a new sector-specific doctrine file.
