# Compliance burden and filing parity routing

This note answers the question that comes **after** a tax looks morally targeted and technically administrable but **before** it can claim legitimacy in ordinary life: **who must do the paperwork, buy the expertise, expose the data, or navigate the interface, and is that burden distributed justly?** The archive's stable answer is to keep compliance work with the **strongest record-holder and highest-capacity actor**, make **relief at least as easy to receive as liability is to impose**, and treat paid-preparer dependence, proprietary-software dependence, or controller-side opacity dumped onto weak users as design failures rather than mere inconveniences.[S26][S27][S38][S64][S65][S66][S68][S77][S89][S91][S92]

The tax state should not make ordinary people, weak firms, or downstream users perform forensic reconstruction of facts that a large employer, platform, lab, cloud, landlord, or tax authority already sees better. Complexity is sometimes unavoidable, but **where it lands** is part of justice.[S27][S38][S65][S66][S68][S77][S89]

## Default lanes

| Situation | Default filing / proof path | Why this path usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary wage earner, pensioner, or benefit-recipient | withholding, prefill, return-free settlement, or one-step confirmation where the state already knows enough | routine income is usually already visible upstream, so repeated manual filing mainly adds friction and error | do not turn ordinary compliance into an annual scavenger hunt for facts the system already holds.[S27][S38][S65][S66][S68][S77] |
| Low-income household, disabled claimant, heavy caregiver, or person facing banking / identity friction | automatic enrolment, assisted filing, offline and accessible channels, and correction-first proofing | floor protection fails when the most burdened people must clear the highest procedural hurdles | do not let relief depend on fresh paperwork, perfect digital access, or paid help the target group cannot reliably obtain.[S31][S32][S33][S64][S65][S66][S91][S92] |
| Small firm, co-op, or thin-margin producer | simple filing, stable safe harbors, and bounded presumptions with graduation | simplicity can preserve productive activity and reduce arbitrary error when records are modest | do not let simplification harden into sheltering, threshold camping, or a preparer-dependent maze that only looks simple on paper.[S26][S27][S38][S65][S66] |
| Large firm, dominant platform, multinational group, or real controller group | rich digital reporting, beneficial-ownership disclosure, machine-readable ledgers, and versioned evidence packets | concentrated actors can bear higher reporting burdens and usually control the facts most relevant to rent, control, and cross-border claims | do not excuse opacity by calling it innovation, proprietary process, or affiliate complexity.[S1][S9][S27][S34][S35][S65][S68][S77][S89] |
| Broad consumption, excise, or platform-mediated charges | collect at merchant, platform, or other transaction-side remitter and repair regressivity through automatic rebates or transfers | upstream collection is usually cleaner than making final consumers self-report routine transactions | do not make poor households keep receipts and file later for protection that could have been delivered automatically.[S3][S4][S21][S64][S65][S66] |
| AI controller-side taxes, facility burdens, or model-governance duties | controller maps, compute / energy / location disclosures, machine-readable event logs, and registry-quality updates from labs, clouds, deployers, and real controller groups | the actors governing the stack already see the relevant operational facts better than end users, workers, or affected households do | do not push controller opacity onto prompt authors, downstream users, or local victims through user-side reporting demands.[S10][S15][S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S68][S83][S89] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Treat compliance burden as part of incidence

A tax can look progressive on its face and still be unjust if filing, proof, delay, or error risk fall mainly on weaker actors. Hours lost, paid-preparer dependence, inaccessible interfaces, repeated document submission, and delayed relief are part of the burden map, not side issues.[S27][S38][S64][S65][S66]

### 2. Make relief at least as low-friction as extraction

If tax can be withheld automatically but rebate, credit, or floor protection requires a fresh application, extra documents, or specialist help, the design is distributively suspect. The archive therefore prefers automatic enrolment, prefill, or one-step confirmation whenever the state or remitter already holds enough reliable information.[S31][S64][S65][S66][S68][S77]

### 3. Put record work on the actor who already sees the facts

The default reporter is usually the employer for payroll, the merchant or platform for transaction taxes, the land registry or assessor for immovable bases, and the controller group for frontier-AI governance facts. Do not force downstream people to reconstruct upstream control, affiliate structure, or facility-side behavior that concentrated actors already monitor more directly.[S21][S27][S34][S35][S65][S68][S83][S89]

### 4. Reserve heavy complexity for concentrated actors, not ordinary life

Strong firms, controller groups, and cross-border networks can justly be asked for richer reporting, data governance, and audit trails because they capture more upside and can spread fixed compliance cost. Ordinary workers, beneficiaries, and small firms should not need lawyer-grade interpretation or closed software just to remain compliant.[S26][S27][S38][S65][S66][S68]

### 5. Accessibility, privacy, and interoperability are not optional extras

Where proof is needed, prefer reusable credentials, bounded attestations, and accessible channels over repeated raw-document submission. A system that is digitally elegant for the administrator but exclusionary, over-collecting, or surveillance-hungry for the subject is not administratively neutral.[S27][S65][S68][S89][S91][S92]

### 6. Machine-scale governance should produce machine-legible reporting

Where a lab, cloud, or deployer operates at machine speed, the answer is not to intensify downstream paperwork. The answer is controller-side logs, stable schemas, event-bounded retention, and versioned packets that let authorities and affected parties review the claim without reverse-engineering the system from scraps.[S16][S17][S27][S68][S77][S83][S89]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Relief by scavenger hunt** — liability is automatic, but protection requires applications, re-documentation, or perfect timing.  
2. **Paid-preparer taxation by stealth** — ordinary compliance effectively requires buying expert help or proprietary software.  
3. **Controller opacity dumped downstream** — user, worker, or locality paperwork substitutes for controller-side disclosure.  
4. **Small-firm "simplicity" that only shifts discretion** — the regime is nominally simple but actually unstable, cliff-heavy, or negotiation-dependent.  
5. **Data gluttony in the name of integrity** — repeated raw-document collection where bounded attestations, reusable credentials, or upstream verification would do.[S26][S27][S38][S65][S66][S68][S89][S91][S92]

## Compression rule

If a new proposal mainly fails because **the paperwork is landing on the wrong actor** or **relief is harder than liability**, route through this card plus the relevant collection, automaticity, or administration note rather than adding a new special-case doctrine.
