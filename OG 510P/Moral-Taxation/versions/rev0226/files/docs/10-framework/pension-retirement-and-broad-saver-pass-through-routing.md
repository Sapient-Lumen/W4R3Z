# Pension, retirement, and broad-saver pass-through routing

This note answers a question left open between the archive's **beneficiary-side windfall** lane and its newer **public / mutual / nonprofit** and **worker-residual** exceptions: **if a downstream AI winner says its upside really belongs to pensioners, retirees, index-fund holders, sovereign funds, life-insurance beneficiaries, college endowments, or other broad savers through passive portfolio chains, should the archive treat that gain as already socially diffused rather than as concentrated private gain?** The archive's stable answer is usually no. Diffuse saver exposure matters, but it is **mediated pass-through ownership**, not the same thing as direct service benefit, direct member relief, or broad worker reciprocity. Tax the real concentrated gain where it sits first; then handle saver protection, retirement adequacy, and person-side fairness through ordinary pension, benefit, distribution, or progressivity rules rather than by immunizing the operating firm.[S10][S11][S18][S21][S22][S29][S34][S35][S67][S83]

## Core rule

Route these cases in order:

1. **Ask whether the apparent broad claim is direct or mediated.** Public-service users, mutual members, ratepayers, and broad worker residual claimants are direct claimants; passive portfolio beneficiaries usually are not.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S67][S83]
2. **Do not let diffuse cap-table stories erase concentrated firm-side gain.** A large downstream firm can still be the right target of ordinary or exceptional beneficiary-side taxation even if many households indirectly own small slices through funds, pensions, or wrappers.[S10][S11][S21][S22][S34][S35][S67][S83]
3. **Treat saver protection mainly as a second-order integration question.** If ordinary households, retirees, or pension beneficiaries would bear some final burden, address that through person-side progressivity, pension taxation, transfer design, or proceeds recycling rather than by pretending the firm has no concentrated gain.[S3][S4][S18][S21][S22][S64][S83]
4. **Elite or narrow wrappers do not count as broad diffusion.** Family offices, narrow endowments, concentrated private trusts, and thin retirement shells remain ordinary private residual claimants in moral substance.[S18][S21][S34][S35][S83]
5. **Real public, mutual, or worker lanes still exist when the institution itself is the claimant.** If the gain is really becoming service benefit, rate relief, member relief, or broad worker reciprocity, route there; passive exposure through a securities portfolio is not enough by itself.[S11][S18][S21][S22][S29][S67][S69][S83]

## Routing table

| Case | Strongest first answer | Best fiscal shape | Must not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large downstream beneficiary with ordinary public-market ownership and retirement-account exposure | ordinary beneficiary-side screening still applies | ordinary tax first; add bounded beneficiary-side windfall contribution if concentrated AI gain remains materially undertaxed | do not treat index-fund or retirement-account diffusion as if it turned the operating firm into a public-benefit body.[S10][S11][S21][S22][S67][S83] |
| Public pension fund, sovereign fund, or insurer passively holding a diversified stake in a private AI winner | still screen the operating firm on its own lane first | tax the real operating-firm gain where it sits; handle downstream saver incidence through ordinary pension, fund, or person-side rules | do not collapse portfolio beneficiaries into direct claimants on the firm's surplus.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S34][S35][S83] |
| Closed elite endowment, family office, narrow trust, or retiree-owner shell | ordinary private residual-claimant logic | ordinary progressive, rent, inheritance, and beneficiary-side tools as appropriate | do not let the words "retirement" or "charitable" do the moral work if control and benefit stay narrow.[S18][S21][S34][S35][S83] |
| Mutual, credit union, utility, pension trust, or other member institution where the institution itself owes visible member relief or service benefit | public / mutual / member-benefit routing first | rate relief, member benefit, service integrity, worker transition, local repair, and ordinary tax on any real private extraction | do not confuse direct member governance with generic passive portfolio exposure.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S69][S83] |
| Worker pension or retirement account holding small passive slices of many firms | person-side retirement fairness, proceeds recycling, and incidence analysis | keep firm-level concentrated-gain analysis separate from household-retirement protection | do not use tiny diffuse household ownership to erase group-level windfall screening.[S3][S4][S18][S21][S22][S64][S83] |

## Four routing rules that matter most

### 1. Passive breadth is not the same thing as direct reciprocity

The archive already recognizes cases where the gain should first become visible public benefit or broad worker benefit. Those lanes depend on a **direct institutional or labor claim on the gain**, not just on the fact that securities are widely held in the background. A firm whose shareholders include many pension funds or index funds may still be capturing concentrated private surplus at the operating level.[S10][S11][S18][S21][S22][S29][S67][S83]

### 2. Tax where concentration is visible; repair saver fairness where incidence lands

A concentrated downstream beneficiary should not escape because some ultimate claimants are retirees or households. The archive's cleaner move is two-step: first identify whether the firm or grouped beneficiary is really capturing exceptional AI-linked surplus; then ask how ordinary households, pensioners, or public beneficiaries should be protected through progressivity, pension rules, incidence repair, or visible proceeds recycling.[S3][S4][S11][S18][S21][S22][S64][S67][S83]

### 3. Wrapper labels do not settle moral class

"Pension," "endowment," "retirement," and "sovereign" are not automatic moral conclusions. Some wrappers are genuinely broad and public-facing; others are narrow, elite, dynastic, or financially intermediated in ways that leave the operating gain just as concentrated as before. The archive therefore asks who really governs, who really benefits, and whether the claim is direct or only several steps removed.[S18][S21][S29][S34][S35][S83]

### 4. Broad-saver pass-through matters more for proceeds and integration than for lane selection

Where passive portfolio exposure is broad, the main design consequences usually appear **after** the lane is chosen: how progressive the person-side system is, how pension income is taxed, whether proceeds fund floor protection or retirement adequacy, and whether incidence screens show ordinary households bearing too much. Those are real questions, but they are not reasons to deny concentrated firm-side gain where it exists. For settings after lane selection, use [`../20-calibration/pension-pass-through-incidence-and-proceeds-ladder.md`](../20-calibration/pension-pass-through-incidence-and-proceeds-ladder.md).[S3][S4][S18][S21][S22][S64][S67][S83]

## Anti-patterns

1. **retiree-halo laundering** — using pension or retirement vocabulary to blur a concentrated private gain,
2. **index-fund innocence** — arguing that public-market diffusion alone converts a windfall into ordinary socialized benefit,
3. **wrapper moralism** — letting the name of a fund settle what only governance and benefit facts can settle,
4. **incidence amnesia** — taxing the concentrated gain while refusing to check whether ordinary households need person-side repair,
5. **fake mutuality** — pointing to distant beneficiaries when the live claimant is still an operating firm or elite asset pool.[S10][S11][S18][S21][S22][S29][S34][S35][S67][S83]
