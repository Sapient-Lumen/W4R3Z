# Capacity, fragility, and enforceability routing

Use this card when the moral answer is being distorted mainly by **weak administrative capacity, crisis conditions, fragile institutions, or low-trust collection reality**. The archive's stable answer is that **administrative fit is a justice constraint, not a mere implementation footnote**: where the ideal high-information tax cannot be seen, billed, reviewed, and repaired honestly, move toward the simplest observable base and the strongest upstream anchor **without** giving up the rent-first, harm-first, floor-protecting logic.[S5][S15][S21][S23][S26][S27][S39][S41][S64][S65][S66][S68]

## Core rule

Ask these questions in order:

1. **Can the state already observe this base through an ordinary record, meter, registry, payroll, customs point, or billing rail?**
2. **Can collection move upstream to a stronger intermediary rather than downstream to weak households, tiny firms, or low-information users?**
3. **Can relief travel through the same or an easier channel than collection?**
4. **Does the simpler proxy still point at the right moral object, or has convenience changed the tax into something else?**
5. **Is the simplification temporary and review-bound, or is it quietly becoming a permanent shelter, dependency bargain, or emergency regime?**

A low-capacity tax design is good when it can be enforced with ordinary records, keeps the human floor visible, reaches rents and harms before routine labor, and leaves a clear path for later graduation as institutions improve.[S21][S23][S26][S27][S39][S41][S64][S65][S66][S69]

## Default route map

| Capacity-constrained setting | Default first move | Why this usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak general administration with limited audit and filing capacity | withholding, customs, excises, utility billing, cadastre repair, and simple resource-rent collection | these bases are already visible at chokepoints and do not require mass bespoke self-reporting | do not let convenience push the whole system onto regressive consumption or nuisance fees while wealth, land, and extraction stay undertaxed.[S5][S21][S23][S25][S26][S27][S39] |
| Fragile municipality hosting extraction, logistics, or data-centre burdens | simple site-value, service-burden, congestion, utility-stress, and host-charge tools, ideally with national floor or anti-abatement backing | the locality sees the burden first even when it cannot bargain from strength | do not trade away future claims through long abatements, cheap public inputs, or opaque side deals.[S15][S23][S25][S29][S30][S69][S70] |
| Weak transfer delivery but real payroll or billing records | universal or prefilled rebates, negative bills, and simple service-side support through channels that already pay or bill people | floor protection that depends on separate maze-like claims will fail in practice | do not automate collection while making relief manual, discretionary, or app-dependent.[S4][S21][S27][S64][S65][S66][S68] |
| Cross-border digital or AI-heavy activity in a low-capacity setting | controller-group reporting, cloud or utility chokepoints, merchants of record, payment rails, customs-like import / access points, and national or club backstops | upstream anchors see more than end users do and are harder to fragment than household filing | do not respond to weak capacity by inventing machine head taxes or user-side paperwork that misses real controllers.[S9][S10][S15][S21][S27][S39][S41][S68] |
| Emergency, fiscal collapse, or conflict-adjacent governance | short-form temporary collection at strongest visible anchors plus explicit trigger, review, and sunset rules | in crisis, a small workable code can be more just than a comprehensive code that never operates | emergency simplification must not become permanent rule by inertia.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S69] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Treat administrative fit as part of moral fit

A tax is not morally vindicated just because its base looks elegant on paper. If the state cannot identify the base, explain the bill, correct mistakes, and protect the floor with tolerable friction, the design is not yet ready for coercive use.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S64][S65][S66]

### 2. Simplify by moving upstream, not by dumping burden downward

Low-capacity justice usually means more reliance on withholding, registries, utilities, customs, large platforms, controller groups, and other chokepoints — not more self-reporting by workers, informal earners, small merchants, or ordinary model users.[S21][S26][S27][S39][S41][S68]

### 3. Keep the rent-first order even when the code gets simpler

Fragility is not a license to spare land, extraction, monopoly, bottleneck, or pollution rents while squeezing payroll, staples, or petty trade because they are easier to see. Simpler systems should still target the most visible concentrated bases first.[S5][S6][S7][S15][S23][S26][S69][S70]

### 4. Prefer repairable proxies over fake precision

If full valuation or individualized measurement is not yet credible, use bounded proxies, presumptive regimes, cadence limits, and safe harbors that remain legible and reviewable. A rough but honest proxy is better than a pseudo-precise system no one can contest.[S21][S25][S26][S27][S39][S41][S66]

### 5. Protect the floor through channels that already work

Where payroll, benefits, pensions, or utility accounts already reach people, floor protection should ride those channels whenever possible. A fragile state should not rely on narrow application rituals to make a regressive tax tolerable.[S4][S21][S64][S65][S66][S68]

### 6. In low-capacity AI governance, stay controller-first and choke-point aware

When the state cannot reliably see every downstream deployment, keep AI-related taxation at the strongest upstream anchors: major controller groups, compute facilities, utilities, payment or marketplace rails, reserve custodians, and other high-visibility bottlenecks. Fragility strengthens the case for controller-side routing; it does not weaken it.[S10][S15][S16][S17][S21][S27][S39][S41][S68]

### 7. Precommit to graduation

Simplification should carry review dates and visible graduation triggers: cadastre repair, better payroll coverage, stronger identity and credentialing, controller-map maturity, or improved appeals capacity. Otherwise the archive's temporary low-capacity answer hardens into a permanent lower-standard state.[S21][S26][S27][S39][S41][S65][S66]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Fragile-state perfectionism** — designing a beautiful code that cannot actually run.
2. **Convenience regressivity** — taxing what is easy to squeeze rather than what is morally first.
3. **Dependency bargaining** — surrendering durable tax claims for discretionary transfers, siting promises, or cheap public inputs.
4. **Emergency permanence** — keeping crisis shortcuts after the trigger is gone.
5. **Digital theater** — assuming software, dashboards, or vendor systems substitute for authority, appeals, and record integrity.[S21][S26][S27][S29][S30][S39][S41][S68][S69]

## Order of preference

When capacity is the live constraint, the archive usually prefers this sequence:

1. existing upstream records and withholding,
2. visible immovable bases and service-burden charges,
3. customs, utility, and payment-rail anchors,
4. bounded presumptive proxies with review,
5. only then diffuse self-reporting by weak downstream actors.

That order is about enforceability, not moral downgrade: it keeps the archive's substantive priorities intact while matching them to institutions that can really carry them.
