# Sanctions, culpability, and disclosure routing

This note answers the question that comes **after** liability, notice, and collection are morally live but **before** the state decides how blame, deterrence, and disclosure should change the response: **is the case best treated as correction, time-value recovery, ordinary civil sanction, upstream-enabler sanction, bounded disclosure-safe-harbor treatment, or rare criminal tax crime?** The archive's stable answer is to separate inability from bad faith, separate interest from punishment, keep ordinary cure easier than coercive escalation, move the harshest sanctions upstream toward strategic enablers and controller-side opacity, and reserve criminal treatment for serious intentional tax crime rather than using it as the routine face of collection.[S20][S21][S27][S39][S41][S98][S99][S100]

## Core rule

Route sanction choice through this order:

1. **correct and explain first where the main problem is mismatch, confusion, or usable self-cure**,
2. **use interest when the state's real claim is delay in payment rather than moral blame**,
3. **use automatic civil additions narrowly and keep hardship, waiver, and cure lanes real**,
4. **scale stronger penalties to culpability, repetition, concealment, obstruction, or strategic entity games**,
5. **aim industrial or upstream misconduct at preparers, platforms, controller groups, payroll agents, and other enablers before stopping with the weakest end filer**,
6. and **treat criminal referral as a last lane for serious intentional tax crime with full rights protection**.[S20][S21][S27][S39][S41][S98][S99][S100]

A tax system becomes morally distorted when it treats late-but-honest debt like fraud, lets upstream enablers profit while only weak end filers are punished, or turns repeated amnesty theater into an expected part of compliance.

## Default routing by context

| Context | Best first sanction answer | Why this answer usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary worker or pensioner mismatch promptly corrected after notice | correction-only or interest-only lane | routine reconciliation errors usually do not show strategic concealment | do not turn small explainable mismatches into fee cascades or fraud theater.[S27][S39][S98] |
| Small-firm late payment with visible short-term liquidity strain | interest-first or low automatic civil-addition lane plus payment plan | the main problem is delayed cash, not necessarily culpable evasion | do not punish temporary liquidity like organized concealment.[S39][S98] |
| Repeated careless filing, ignored reminders, or negligent recordkeeping after usable notice | culpability-scaled civil-penalty lane | repeated non-co-operation can justify deterrence once cure paths were real | keep escalation tied to blameworthy repetition, not administrative annoyance.[S27][S39][S98] |
| Preparer, payroll, platform, shell-service, or controller-side schemes that mass-produce false reporting | upstream-enabler lane first | the highest-leverage sanction usually sits with the actor multiplying the misconduct | do not stop with the weakest downstream filer when the pattern was industrially designed.[S21][S39][S99] |
| Falsified books, fake invoices, concealment chains, repeated obstruction, or organized high-value evasion | criminal-referral lane only after civil thresholds and rights protections are met | these facts describe tax crime more than ordinary debt management | criminal tools should track seriousness and proof, not substitute for weak civil administration.[S39][S99] |
| Voluntary disclosure after a genuine detection shock where cleanup matters more than spectacle | bounded disclosure-safe-harbor treatment inside the civil ladder | reduced penalties can sometimes surface hidden liability faster | do not teach strategic waiting by making disclosure holidays routine.[S27][S39][S100] |
| Current AI system involved in model-generated filing or controller-map error | sanction the deployer, controller, preparer, or responsible organization, not the model itself | present AI systems are still tools inside human and firm liability chains | do not let automation blur who had the standing and control to comply.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S39][S99] |
| Future rights-bearing artificial person, if threshold is crossed | same ladder only with ordinary person-regime notice, counsel, and review protections | punitive escalation requires the same standing bundle expected for any direct subject | no direct sanctions without real representation, remedy, and non-owner due process.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S99] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Separate time-value recovery from punishment

Interest exists to restore the value of delayed payment, not to do all the work of blame. Civil penalties should therefore answer culpability, obstruction, or deterrence needs that interest alone cannot meet.[S39][S98]

### 2. Distinguish inability, confusion, and self-correction from bad faith

The archive treats hardship, administration-caused error, prompt self-correction, and de minimis mismatch differently from concealment, repeated disregard, or strategic non-co-operation. A sanction regime that erases those differences usually becomes floor-stripping and distrust-building.[S27][S39][S98]

### 3. Sanction the highest-leverage enabler when misconduct is industrialized

Where non-compliance is designed, packaged, or multiplied by preparers, payroll intermediaries, platforms, controller groups, shell-service providers, or similar upstream actors, the morally serious response moves there first. This is especially important in AI-heavy filing and reporting systems, where weak end users may be visible but not architecturally central.[S20][S21][S39][S41][S99]

### 4. Keep disclosure-safe harbors narrow, credible, and non-routine

Voluntary disclosure or self-correction lanes can help when the state needs cleanup, truth-telling, and fast normalization more than spectacle. But repeated amnesty culture teaches actors to wait for the next holiday. The archive therefore permits bounded disclosure relief without turning it into the ordinary expectation of evaders.[S27][S39][S100]

### 5. Criminal referral is a tax-crime lane, not a collection style

Criminal tools fit serious intentional evasion, falsification, concealment, organized fraud, or repeated high-value offending. They do not fit routine late payment, confusion, or ordinary reconciliation. Using criminal shadow as the standard face of tax administration is both morally excessive and administratively distorting.[S39][S99]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Interest as punishment** — using time-value recovery as if it settled blame or deterrence.
2. **Late-but-honest as fraud** — treating distress, confusion, and organized evasion as one category.
3. **Penalty farming** — designing automatic additions and technical fees as a revenue stream.[S27][S39]
4. **Upstream immunity** — punishing weak end filers while enablers, platforms, preparers, or controller groups stay untouched.[S21][S39][S99]
5. **Amnesty drift** — teaching strategic waiting through repeated disclosure holidays.[S100]

## Order of preference

When more than one sanction path is available, the archive usually prefers the path that is:

1. easiest to distinguish from ordinary correction,
2. least likely to strip the protected floor for non-culpable debtors,
3. strongest against repeated or industrialized evasion,
4. clearest about who actually caused the misconduct,
5. most compatible with notice, explanation, and review,
6. and least likely to make criminal shadow the routine instrument of collection.

This is why correction-first treatment, interest for delay, narrow civil additions, culpability-scaled penalties, upstream-enabler sanctions, and rare criminal referral usually beat flat automatic punishment or periodic amnesty theater.
