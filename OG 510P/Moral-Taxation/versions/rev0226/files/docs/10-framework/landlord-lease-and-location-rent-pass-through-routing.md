# Landlord, lease, and location-rent pass-through routing

This note answers a question left too implicit between the archive's **beneficiary-side windfall** lane, its **land / site-rent** lane, and its ordinary **incidence** rules: **if a downstream AI winner says the gain is already being capitalized into higher commercial rents, lease payments, scarce-location premia, or other landlord-side claims, should the archive still treat that gain as concentrated private windfall?** The archive's stable answer is **sometimes not, but only when the location-rent story is real, outside, and mostly about scarce site value rather than ordinary improvements or group theater**. Genuine outside land or location rent is not the same moral object as retained downstream surplus. But related-party landlords, sale-leaseback choreography, improvement-heavy lease stories, or rent claims that coexist with persistent exceptional margins do not dissolve the beneficiary lane.[S5][S15][S18][S21][S23][S25][S28][S34][S35][S67][S69][S70][S83]

## Core rule

Route these cases in order:

1. **Ask whether the alleged landlord gain is really land or scarce-location rent.** Do not treat ordinary building services, fit-out recovery, maintenance, financing cost, or routine operating expense as proof that the AI gain has already diffused into an immovable-rent claimant.[S5][S21][S23][S25][S83]
2. **Ask whether the landlord or lease counterparty is really outside the beneficiary group.** Related-party REITs, treasury landlords, sale-leaseback shells, revenue-share leases, or controlled master leases do not by themselves prove outside absorption.[S21][S34][S35][S83]
3. **Route real site-rent capture to the land and local-burden lanes first.** Where scarce location, grid access, logistics position, zoning privilege, or campus siting is doing the work, the archive prefers land-value, site-rent, host-burden, and equalization tools rather than pretending the downstream beneficiary no longer exists.[S5][S15][S23][S25][S28][S69][S70][S83]
4. **Net lease stories against retained margins and side payments.** Claimed rent absorption matters only after checking whether the beneficiary still keeps persistent exceptional margins or recaptures the value through abatements, tenant-improvement subsidies, side letters, affiliated services, or under-taxed landholding structures.[S18][S21][S23][S34][S35][S67][S83]
5. **Mixed cases may split.** A firm can face real outside location-rent pressure and still retain enough exceptional surplus to justify a narrower beneficiary-side contribution on the remainder.[S5][S18][S21][S23][S25][S67][S83]

## Four routing rules that matter most

### 1. Real site-rent absorption can narrow the windfall story

The archive does not assume that every downstream AI winner keeps the full upside. In some cases, scarce site value, exclusive campus access, zoning privilege, logistics position, or other immovable advantage can absorb part of the incremental gain through higher outside rents or land values. Where that is true and residual margins are not still the main story, the beneficiary-side windfall case narrows.[S5][S18][S21][S23][S25][S67][S83]

### 2. Lease expense is not a moral halo

A firm does not escape merely by pointing to rent or occupancy cost. Most firms lease something. The archive asks whether the claim is really about outside land or location rent, or whether it is ordinary operating cost, related-party structure, or improvement recovery through which the same coordinated group still keeps concentrated gain.[S18][S21][S23][S25][S34][S35][S67][S83]

### 3. Location-rent routing is different from customer, supplier, creditor, worker, or public-benefit routing

Outside landlords can matter morally without turning the case into customer diffusion, supplier pass-through, worker reciprocity, or public-service gain. The right answer is often that some apparent downstream windfall should instead be reached through land-value, site-rent, or host-burden tools, with any remaining concentrated residual left on the beneficiary lane.[S5][S15][S18][S21][S23][S25][S69][S70][S83]

### 4. Local repair and equalization still matter

Where AI-driven location demand also strains housing, utilities, transport, grid capacity, or other host-local systems, the archive still prefers visible local repair and equalization tools. Real outside location rent can narrow a beneficiary charge; it does not excuse local burden, anti-abatement discipline, or richer-jurisdiction hoarding of site rents.[S5][S15][S23][S25][S28][S69][S70][S83]

## Anti-patterns

1. **lease-halo laundering** — pointing to ordinary occupancy cost as if it automatically erased concentrated downstream gain,
2. **related-landlord theater** — routing the claim through affiliated landlords, sale-leasebacks, or bundled property-service structures and calling it outside absorption,
3. **improvement masquerade** — treating fit-out, maintenance, financing, or service bundles as if they were scarce location rent,
4. **host-burden erasure** — acting as if landlord capture defeats local repair, equalization, or site-rent claims where place-bound strain is still visible,
5. **all-or-nothing lane forcing** — refusing to admit mixed cases where some gain is capitalized into outside site rent and some remains concentrated downstream surplus.[S5][S15][S18][S21][S23][S25][S34][S35][S67][S69][S70][S83]
