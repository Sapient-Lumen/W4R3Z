# International coordination claim-split ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that cross-border coordination should act as a **floor-and-sharing device**, **what is the narrowest workable way to split claims among host-burden, market, residence/controller, and poorer-jurisdiction interests without reopening no-tax space or turning clubs into rich-state monopolies?**[S1][S2][S3][S9][S15][S28][S69][S70]

The constitutional part is already settled: **co-ordination should stop race-to-the-bottom erosion, preserve burdened-place and market claims, and protect poorer jurisdictions from dependency bargains rather than simply transferring taxing power upward.**[S1][S2][S3][S9][S15][S28][S69][S70]

## Why calibration is still needed

The closed rule tells the archive **what counts morally** in cross-border tax design, but not yet the smallest workable operating ladder for splitting claims when more than one claim is real at the same time. A usable ladder has to avoid four failures at once:

1. **residence monopoly** where powerful home states keep the whole mobile-rent claim,
2. **host abandonment** where data-centre, grid, water, land, or cleanup burdens disappear into a distant club formula,
3. **market erasure** where customer or user markets are treated as morally irrelevant because the business is digital or borderless,
4. and **dependency bargains** where poorer jurisdictions are induced to surrender durable taxing rights for siting promises, fragile transfers, or treaty discipline they did not really shape.[S1][S2][S3][S15][S28][S69][S70]

## Small option set

### Option A — residence-heavy club floor

Use a coordinated minimum or top-up rule, but leave most residual claim with the residence or parent-controller jurisdiction unless a treaty expressly carves out a share for others.[S1][S9][S28]

This is administratively easy, but it under-protects host burdens, market co-creation, and poorer-jurisdiction agency.

### Option B — host-dominant allocation

Give the largest ongoing fiscal claim to the siting jurisdiction whenever major infrastructure, extraction, or data-centre footprint exists there.[S5][S15][S23][S70]

This works better for visibly place-bound burdens, but it overstates host claims where residual profit is really being co-created by distant markets or controller groups and risks reviving infrastructure-location theater.

### Option C — market-heavy allocation

Give markets a standing coordinated share over most residual profit whenever customers, users, or access rights are dispersed across jurisdictions.[S3][S9][S28]

This cures some digital-booking fictions, but it can understate place-bound burdens and can leave weaker host jurisdictions bargaining from the wrong end of the table.

### Option D — layered floor plus direct burden carve-out plus shared remainder

Use a three-layer design:

1. **direct host-burden carve-out first** for place-bound stresses such as land, grid, water, congestion, emergency capacity, cleanup risk, or comparable local burden,[S15][S23][S69][S70]
2. **club floor next** to stop mobile-rent escape and destructive undercutting,[S1][S2][S9][S28]
3. **shared residual split after that** across market and controller/residence jurisdictions using harder-to-fake factors such as destination sales, users, payroll, or tangible footprint rather than pure booking stories.[S2][S3][S9][S28]

This option is the archive's cleanest default because it treats co-ordination as a floor-and-sharing device while keeping burdened places visible.

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt **Option D** as the default cross-border claim-splitting ladder:

1. **identify and charge direct place-bound burden first** at the host or burdened-locality layer,
2. **apply a coordinated floor** to mobile residual profit or race-prone bases so paper shifting does not erase the base,
3. **split the coordinated remainder** between market and controller/residence claims using disclosed apportionment factors,
4. **add poorer-jurisdiction protection inside the club layer** through minimum shares, equalisation, model-agreement constraints, or anti-underpricing floors where bargaining asymmetry is obvious.[S1][S2][S3][S9][S15][S28][S69][S70]

That is the narrowest workable setting because it avoids both overlap and erasure. It neither leaves every jurisdiction to fend for itself nor treats a club as a permission structure for residence-state capture.

## Default claim-split ladder

| Layer | When it activates | Default claim | Why it comes first or later |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. ordinary domestic default | no major cross-border mobility or overlap problem | ordinary domestic rules | no extra club layer needed yet |
| 1. direct host-burden carve-out | place-bound burden is visible: land, water, grid, congestion, cleanup, emergency service strain | host / burdened locality claim | place-bound burdens should not wait for distant treaty sharing.[S15][S23][S69][S70] |
| 2. coordinated floor | mobile-rent escape or tax competition is real | club minimum / anti-shifting floor | stops no-tax space and destructive undercutting.[S1][S2][S9][S28] |
| 3. shared residual split | more than one jurisdiction still has a morally real claim after the burden carve-out and floor | disclosed market + controller/residence apportionment | residual profit in digital and AI-linked activity is co-created across places.[S2][S3][S9][S28] |
| 4. poorer-jurisdiction protection overlay | bargaining asymmetry or dependency risk is high | equalisation, minimum shares, anti-underpricing guardrails, or model-agreement discipline | co-ordination fails if weaker jurisdictions are forced into permanent surrender bargains.[S2][S5][S69][S70] |

## Anti-patterns

- **Residence-state capture** — the club floor becomes a pretext for powerful home states to keep the entire mobile-rent base.[S1][S2][S28]
- **Server or facility fetish** — all claim is assigned to physical siting even when market and controller-side value creation are plainly elsewhere.[S15][S28][S70]
- **Pure market reductionism** — customer markets get everything while host burdens disappear from view.[S3][S15][S69]
- **Dependency by transfer promise** — poorer jurisdictions surrender present claims in exchange for discretionary or fragile future transfers.[S2][S69][S70]
- **Club ceiling drift** — a minimum rule is treated as the whole moral answer rather than a floor under additional justified claims.[S1][S2][S9]

## What would change the recommendation

This ladder should be tightened, loosened, or replaced if any of the following become clearer:

- a small set of apportionment factors outperforms the current host + market + controller split with less gaming,
- host-burden charging turns out to be too noisy or too easy to launder through subsidy packages and hidden negative taxation,
- club floors prove unable to protect poorer jurisdictions without a larger equalisation or minimum-share mechanism,
- or a specific base becomes measurable enough that direct situs or destination logic can replace a shared residual formula altogether.[S2][S3][S9][S15][S28][S69][S70]
