# Beneficiary home / market / local-burden claim-split ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that **beneficiary-side windfall claims follow the beneficiary's own fiscal home and markets while burdened places keep narrower repair claims**, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when the answer is home-only, home-plus-market sharing, home-plus-local-repair, or all three together with credits?**[S3][S4][S9][S10][S11][S14][S21][S22][S23][S28][S64][S67][S69][S70][S83]

The constitutional part is already settled: **upstream model geography does not settle downstream beneficiary gain; broad firm- or person-level claims start from the beneficiary's own home, markets can deserve a coordinated share where they materially co-create the extraordinary gain, and burdened localities keep narrower repair claims rather than monopoly over the whole windfall.**[S3][S4][S9][S10][S11][S14][S21][S22][S23][S28][S64][S67][S69][S70][S83]

## Why calibration is still needed

Once the beneficiary-side lane is real, four practical failures remain easy:

1. **home-state monopoly** where the beneficiary's residence or parent state keeps the whole extraordinary claim even when many markets co-create it,
2. **market erasure** where downstream customer or user markets are treated as irrelevant because the AI stack is supplied from elsewhere,
3. **local monopoly rhetoric** where visible layoffs or service strain in one place are treated as proof that the whole multinational windfall belongs there,
4. **same-slice duplication** where home, market, and local layers all relabel the same surplus instead of separating broad liability from narrower repair overlays.[S3][S4][S9][S10][S11][S14][S21][S22][S23][S28][S64][S67][S69][S70][S83]

## Small option set

### Option A — home-only

Leave the extraordinary beneficiary-side claim almost entirely with the beneficiary's ordinary fiscal home, using markets only for ordinary destination taxes and localities only for ordinary local taxes or spending claims.[S3][S9][S18][S21][S28]

This is simple, but it is too weak where a multinational downstream winner's extraordinary gain is plainly co-created across many markets or where concentrated local adaptation burdens are real.

### Option B — market-heavy

Split most extraordinary beneficiary-side gain across customer or user markets whenever the beneficiary sells or operates at scale across borders.[S3][S9][S21][S22][S28]

This cures some residence-state overreach, but it can underweight the beneficiary's own home relation and can still leave burdened localities arguing from outside the formula.

### Option C — local-repair-heavy

Let places absorbing layoffs, retraining load, service strain, housing pressure, or comparable adaptation burdens take the lead claim over the beneficiary-side windfall.[S4][S11][S14][S23][S64][S67][S69][S70]

This keeps local pain visible, but it overstates local ownership of a broad downstream surplus and risks relabeling a repair problem as though it were the whole extraordinary gain.

### Option D — layered home + market + local-repair split

Use a three-part answer:

1. **home first** for the beneficiary's broad ordinary firm- or person-level liability,
2. **market sharing next** where many markets materially co-create the extraordinary downstream gain,
3. **local-repair overlay last** for places absorbing visible adaptation burdens, usually through a narrower share, earmark, or burden-linked companion claim rather than a second full windfall assessment on the same slice.[S3][S4][S9][S10][S11][S14][S21][S22][S23][S28][S64][S67][S69][S70][S83]

This is the archive's cleanest default because it preserves the beneficiary-side lane without letting either home states or burdened localities claim the whole thing by slogan.

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt **Option D** as the default settings ladder once grouped facts show the case remains genuinely beneficiary-side rather than controller-side.

That means:

1. **group related affiliates before splitting** so one coordinated group cannot manufacture stateless surplus by separating command, sales, and beneficiary gain across paper entities,[S9][S10][S20][S21][S28][S34][S35][S83]
2. **keep the beneficiary's home as the anchor** for broad ordinary liability and any residual beneficiary-side cleanup share,[S3][S9][S18][S21][S28]
3. **give markets a coordinated share** only where the extraordinary downstream gain is materially co-created across many markets and ordinary destination taxes alone do not already do the work,[S3][S9][S21][S22][S28]
4. **keep local claims narrower** and visibly tied to repair, transition, or burden-linked overlays instead of pretending one burdened place owns the full multinational windfall,[S4][S11][S14][S23][S64][S67][S69][S70]
5. **run a duplication screen** so the same beneficiary-side surplus slice is not assessed three times under three labels.[S1][S9][S10][S21][S22][S28][S83]

## Default claim-split ladder

| Lane | When it should apply | Default claim | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. ordinary domestic lane | the beneficiary and the extraordinary gain are not materially cross-border | beneficiary home only, inside ordinary tax plus any bounded domestic beneficiary-side contribution | no extra market or local layer unless a distinct claim is actually visible |
| 1. home-only beneficiary lane | the gain is cross-border in form but still mostly tied to one beneficiary home and markets do not materially co-create the extraordinary surplus | beneficiary home takes the broad claim | do not import upstream model geography as fake situs.[S3][S9][S10][S21][S28] |
| 2. home + local-repair lane | the broad gain still belongs mainly at home, but specific places absorb visible layoffs, retraining load, service strain, or similar adaptation burdens | beneficiary home keeps the broad claim; burdened locality gets a narrower repair share or earmark | local repair is real, but it is not monopoly over the whole windfall.[S4][S11][S14][S23][S64][S67][S69][S70] |
| 3. home + market-share lane | many markets materially co-create the extraordinary downstream gain and ordinary destination taxes do not already capture enough of it | coordinated split between beneficiary home and markets | do not let residence monopoly or gross-turnover piling replace a real claim split.[S3][S9][S21][S22][S28] |
| 4. home + market-share + local-repair lane | both multinational market co-creation and burdened-place adaptation costs are real | home and markets split the broad extraordinary gain; burdened places get narrower repair overlays or proceeds shares | keep the local layer narrow and run an explicit same-slice duplication check.[S1][S4][S9][S10][S11][S14][S21][S22][S23][S28][S64][S67][S69][S70][S83] |
| 5. club-floor / equalisation overlay | bargaining asymmetry, tax competition, or cross-border escape would otherwise hollow the earlier lanes out | treaty floor, equalisation, or anti-underpricing overlay on top of lanes 1-4 | the club is a floor-and-sharing device, not a new monopoly claimant.[S2][S3][S9][S28][S69][S70] |

## Three calibration rules that matter most

### 1. Home anchors the broad relation; markets share only where they really co-create the extraordinary gain

The beneficiary's own home remains the first broad claim because the actor is already taxed there as a real firm or person. Markets deserve more than ordinary destination taxes only when the extraordinary downstream surplus is materially built through many cross-border markets and not merely booked through the home state.[S3][S9][S18][S21][S22][S28]

### 2. Local repair should usually travel as a narrower overlay, not as a second copy of the whole windfall

When a school system, labor market, municipality, or care network absorbs visible adaptation burdens, the archive treats that as a real fiscal claim. But the claim is usually narrower: a repair share, earmark, burden-linked companion charge, or explicit proceeds route, not a second attempt to seize the full beneficiary-side surplus.[S4][S11][S14][S23][S64][S67][S69][S70]

### 3. Upstream model geography stays out unless a distinct upstream claim is being taxed

The note applies only after the archive has already decided the live object is **downstream concentrated beneficiary gain**. If the real issue instead is controller-side scarce capacity, facility burden, or upstream rent, use the controller or host ladders. Do not let upstream cloud region, rack location, or model-controller domicile silently re-enter as the supposed situs of downstream gain.[S3][S9][S10][S15][S21][S22][S28][S67][S70][S83]

## Anti-patterns

- **server-fetish return** — upstream hardware location sneaks back in as the claimant over downstream gain.[S10][S15][S21][S22][S28][S67][S70]
- **residence monopoly** — one home state claims the whole extraordinary beneficiary-side base by default.[S3][S9][S21][S22][S28]
- **local all-or-nothing** — burdened places are told they either own the whole windfall or nothing.[S4][S11][S14][S23][S64][S67][S69][S70]
- **market gross-up theater** — markets pile gross turnover rules on top of home liability without checking whether they are really sharing the same extraordinary slice.[S3][S9][S21][S22][S28]
- **split-first group-later** — jurisdictions divide paper entities before checking whether one coordinated group is hiding the real beneficiary picture.[S9][S10][S20][S21][S28][S34][S35][S83]

## What would change the recommendation

This ladder should be tightened, loosened, or replaced if any of the following become clearer:

- a better market-allocation formula for downstream extraordinary gain than the current home-plus-market split,
- strong evidence that local repair is better delivered through spending-side equalisation rather than tax-side overlays,
- strong evidence that beneficiary-side cases and controller-side cases should be merged more often after grouped screening,
- or stronger club designs that protect poorer jurisdictions and burdened localities without reopening no-tax space.[S2][S3][S4][S9][S10][S14][S21][S22][S23][S28][S64][S67][S69][S70][S83]
