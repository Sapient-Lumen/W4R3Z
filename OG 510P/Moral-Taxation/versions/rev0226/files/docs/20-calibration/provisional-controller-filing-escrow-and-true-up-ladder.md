# Provisional controller filing, escrow, and later true-up ladder

## Question in one sentence

Once the archive already knows how to **rank controllers**, assign **proof burdens**, and demand a **short controller map**, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding who files or remits now, when disputed shares should sit in escrow or reserve, and how later reallocation should happen so controller disputes do not suspend collection or permanently entrench convenience-based misassignment?**[S10][S15][S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S39][S41][S83]

Route: do not confuse interim collection with final moral incidence. The archive should keep filing and remittance moving through the strongest justified anchor while preserving credits, refunds, and later reallocation once the controller facts are clearer.[S20][S21][S27][S39][S41][S83]

## Option scan

| Option | Shape | Archive verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A — wait-for-final-adjudication lane | suspend filing, reserve funding, or remittance until the whole controller dispute is finally resolved.[S21][S27][S39][S41] | Reject: rewards delay and leaves public claims, victims, and floor protection hanging on fact-finding speed. |
| B — nearest-visible-actor lane | permanently tax whichever actor is easiest to see while the dispute lasts, with little later correction.[S10][S21][S27][S41][S83] | Reject: mistakes convenience for settled liability and invites strategic visibility theater. |
| C — provisional filing, bounded escrow, and later true-up | require interim filing at the best-supported controller or controller-group anchor, place genuinely disputed shares into bounded escrow or reserve where needed, and reallocate later through credits, refunds, or assessed balances.[S15][S17][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S39][S41][S83] | Adopt. |
| D — universal joint-and-several-on-all-visible-actors lane | treat every visible participant as fully liable until one proves itself out.[S21][S27][S34][S35][S41][S83] | Reject: overreaches against ordinary outsiders and turns messy facts into coercive overcollection. |

## Five-rung ladder

1. **mapped-controller filing lane** — where the controller map is current and only minor factual edges remain open, keep ordinary filing and remittance with the mapped primary controller or controller group.[S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S41]
2. **provisional lead-controller lane** — if the map is incomplete but the ranking ladder points strongly toward one actor or coalition, require interim filing at that lead-controller anchor while keeping the dispute reviewable.[S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S39][S41][S83]
3. **split-share provisional lane** — where two or more actors each plausibly govern distinct layers or claim distinct residual upside, use provisional apportionment or parallel filing by lane rather than forcing a false single-controller answer too early.[S15][S20][S21][S34][S35][S41][S83]
4. **bounded escrow / reserve lane** — place genuinely disputed shares, incident-finance components, or successor-sequence overlaps into bounded escrow, reserve, or supervised holdback when immediate remittance to one claimant would create obvious overcollection or undersecured downside.[S15][S17][S21][S27][S39][S41]
5. **true-up and reallocation lane** — once the facts stabilize, correct the interim answer through credits, refunds, debits, interest rules, or revised group allocation, but do not let later correction erase timely floor protection, reserve funding, or ordinary filing discipline.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S83]

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt **Option C — provisional filing, bounded escrow, and later true-up** as the archive's default interim rule for controller disputes.[S15][S17][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S39][S41][S83]

Presumption:

- keep ordinary filing with the **mapped controller** when the remaining dispute is genuinely marginal,
- move to a **provisional lead-controller** when one actor or coalition is already the best-supported ranking answer,
- use **split-share provisional filing** when the dispute is really about layered or shared governance rather than about a single winner,
- use **bounded escrow or reserve holdbacks** only for genuinely disputed shares or downside components that should not be irreversibly paid out yet,
- and finish with a visible **true-up** path rather than treating the interim remitter as the final moral loser by default.

This is the archive's narrowest workable setting because it keeps collection, repair, and precaution moving without collapsing ordinary outside actors into universal liability or letting unresolved facts become a veto on timely public claims.

## Default interim-action table

| Live posture | Interim action | Why it usually fits | Archive warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| current controller map, small factual dispute only | mapped-controller filing | the archive already has enough governance detail for ordinary filing.[S20][S21][S27][S41] | do not manufacture escrow just because some paper detail remains contested. |
| strong ranking answer, incomplete map | provisional lead-controller filing | one actor or coalition already looks like the best-supported controller anchor.[S20][S21][S34][S35][S39][S41][S83] | keep later correction open; provisional filing is not final moral incidence. |
| real layer split or plausible co-controller structure | split-share provisional filing | the dispute is about division, not total ignorance.[S15][S20][S21][S34][S35][S41] | avoid forcing a fake single winner where layered governance is the real fact pattern. |
| disputed shares, successor overlap, or incident-finance component | escrow / reserve holdback | prevents irreversible overpayment or undersecured downside while facts settle.[S15][S17][S21][S39][S41] | keep holdbacks bounded, reviewable, and tied to concrete disputed shares. |
| stale or corrected controller facts after review | true-up, refund, credit, or assessed balance | final liability should follow stabilized governance facts rather than interim convenience.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S83] | do not let true-up become a pretext for late floor repair or endless provisionality. |

## Anti-patterns

- **dispute-as-veto** — no one files, remits, or prefunds because the controller story is still contested.[S21][S27][S39][S41]
- **convenience hardening into substance** — the interim remitter quietly becomes the permanent moral loser because correction is too weak or too late.[S10][S21][S27][S39][S41]
- **escrow maximalism** — holding back everything when only a bounded share is truly disputed.[S17][S21][S39][S41]
- **outside-actor panic spreading** — using interim uncertainty to sweep ordinary hosts, channels, reviewers, or users into universal joint liability.[S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S41][S83]
- **true-up without repair symmetry** — the state corrects undercollection quickly but leaves overcollection, reserve release, or wrongly assigned burden lagging behind.[S21][S27][S39][S41]

## What would change the recommendation

Tighten, loosen, or replace this ladder if:

- controller maps become so reliable and timely that provisional lead-controller filing rarely needs escrow or split-share treatment,
- real-world disputes show systematic overcollection from interim remitters who lack real governance power,
- privacy-preserving attestations make provisional allocation much easier to verify than the archive now assumes,
- or bounded escrow proves too slow or too administratively costly relative to direct assessed balances and later correction.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S39][S40][S41][S83]
