# Loss-recognition symmetry ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that morally serious taxation must not take upside on one logic and dismiss genuine downside on another, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when tax design should rely on current-year netting, value-preserving carry-forwards, bounded carry-backs, screened refundability, or ex ante reserves instead of pretending that nominal or delayed loss relief is already enough?**[S21][S23][S50][S51][S52][S55][S56]

## Closed rules invoked

This memo does **not** reopen the archive's constitutional waist. It relies on:

- [`../10-framework/downside-symmetry-and-loss-routing.md`](../10-framework/downside-symmetry-and-loss-routing.md)
- [`../10-framework/timing-and-liquidity-routing.md`](../10-framework/timing-and-liquidity-routing.md)
- [`../10-framework/real-value-and-indexation-routing.md`](../10-framework/real-value-and-indexation-routing.md)
- [`../10-framework/failure-ordering-and-limited-liability-routing.md`](../10-framework/failure-ordering-and-limited-liability-routing.md)
- [`../10-framework/precaution-and-catastrophic-risk-routing.md`](../10-framework/precaution-and-catastrophic-risk-routing.md)

The constitutional question is already settled: **genuine downside should usually be recognized through current-year netting, real-value-preserving carry-forwards, bounded carry-backs or refundability where timing or explicit public purpose makes delay distortive, while catastrophic harm should be funded ex ante rather than misclassified as an ordinary tax-loss problem.**[S21][S23][S50][S51][S52][S55][S56]

## Why calibration is still needed

The closed rule settles the direction but not yet the smallest usable operating ladder. A workable calibration still has to avoid five different failures at once:

1. **one-way upside taxation** where gains are taxed promptly but losses count only weakly or late,[S21][S50]
2. **paper symmetry** where carry-forwards exist in law but lose practical value through inflation, expiry, or long delay,[S50][S55][S56]
3. **incumbent-only usability** where only already-profitable actors can actually use the relief, leaving new or pre-profit activity outside the supposed symmetry,[S21][S50][S51]
4. **abuse panic overreach** where real anti-avoidance problems lead to rules so blunt that genuine downside hardly counts,[S50][S52]
5. and **catastrophe misclassification** where ruinous public harms are left to ex post tax treatment instead of reserves, insurance, bonding, or controller guarantees up front.[S16][S17][S47][S48][S49]

## Small option set

### Option A — no meaningful intertemporal symmetry

Allow current-year netting only and otherwise let losses expire or decay. This is the simplest rule on paper, but it overtaxes risk, favors incumbents with steady income, and turns downside recognition into a largely symbolic promise.[S21][S50][S55]

### Option B — nominal carry-forward only

Allow unused losses to carry forward, but without real-value protection and often with strict offset limits. This is better than outright denial, but it still lets inflation, delay, and weak profitability do distributive work silently.[S50][S55][S56]

### Option C — value-preserving carry-forward plus bounded carry-back

Use current-year netting first, then a carry-forward that preserves enough value to matter, and add a **bounded carry-back or instalment refund** where cyclical shocks, timing asymmetry, or viable small-firm liquidity make waiting morally distortive.[S21][S23][S50][S56]

This is the cleanest default for most ordinary business, entrepreneurial, and capital-risk cases because it treats gains and losses as one fiscal relationship without pretending the state is a universal insurer.

### Option D — screened refundability or transferability for explicit public-purpose credits

Permit refundability or closely bounded transferability only where the policy aim is explicitly to support activity that often precedes profit — for example tightly designed innovation or similar public-purpose credits. Otherwise nominal non-refundability mainly helps firms that are already profitable enough to wait.[S17][S22][S51]

### Option E — catastrophe lane funded ex ante

Where the real downside is mass injury, cleanup, model-enabled catastrophe, or other harms that may outrun the taxpayer, do **not** rely mainly on ex post loss recognition. Use bonds, reserves, insurance, decommissioning authority, or controller guarantees instead.[S16][S17][S47][S48][S49][S53][S54]

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt a **five-step practical ladder**:

| Stage | When it should apply | Downside answer | Main guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. current-year symmetry | ordinary same-base gains and losses in the same period | **current-year netting first** | do not fragment one real base into separate favorable and unfavorable buckets |
| 1. ordinary intertemporal default | viable firms, entrepreneurs, risky investment, cyclical sectors | **value-preserving carry-forward** | preserve enough real value to matter; do not let inflation erase relief |
| 2. liquidity / cycle correction | sharp but ordinary downturns, viable small firms, timing-sensitive sectors | **bounded carry-back or instalment refund** | cap scope and duration so the state is not a blanket shock absorber |
| 3. explicit public-purpose support | credits aimed at activity that often arrives before profit | **screened refundability or tightly bounded transferability** | require a real public-purpose case and anti-abuse filters |
| 4. catastrophic public-risk lane | cleanup, victim repair, tail-risk AI, non-compensable harms | **bond, reserve, insure, decommission, or guarantee ex ante** | do not treat catastrophe as just another tax-loss problem |

The archive's provisional setting is therefore **C as the default, D only where the public-purpose case is explicit, and E wherever the real issue is catastrophic or estate-outstripping harm rather than ordinary business downside**.[S16][S17][S21][S23][S47][S48][S49][S50][S51][S52][S55][S56]

That is the narrowest workable recommendation because it gives genuine downside real fiscal recognition, preserves enough value across time to matter, avoids making profitability a precondition for being helped by the rule, and keeps catastrophic-risk funding in its morally prior lane.

For present AI stacks, the practical consequence is simple: **ordinary pre-profit or cyclical losses at labs and deployers can belong in stages 1 to 3, but tail-risk, cleanup, and public-harm exposure stay in stage 4 rather than being socialized through post-collapse tax accommodation.**[S15][S16][S17][S47][S48][S49][S50]

## Anti-patterns

- **Nominal rot disguised as symmetry** — loss relief exists but decays through inflation or strict delay.[S50][S55][S56]
- **Profit-first eligibility** — the rule supposedly supports risk-taking but works mainly for already-profitable incumbents.[S21][S50][S51]
- **Anti-abuse flattening** — genuine loss recognition is sacrificed because artificial-loss rules are drafted too broadly.[S50][S52]
- **Refundability without purpose** — current cash support is granted without a clear public-purpose case or screening standard.[S17][S22][S51]
- **Catastrophe-by-carryforward** — cleanup or tail-risk exposure is left to ordinary loss rules instead of being pre-funded.[S16][S17][S47][S48][S49][S53][S54]

## What would change the recommendation

This ladder should be tightened, loosened, or partly replaced if any of the following become clearer:

- administrations can preserve real value across time through robust indexation or equivalent mechanisms without excessive complexity,[S50][S55][S56]
- evidence shows that bounded carry-backs materially stabilize viable firms or household-linked self-employment without mainly subsidizing incumbent tax planning,[S21][S23][S50]
- a proposed refundable credit can show an explicit public-purpose case, strong anti-abuse screens, and better access for genuine early-stage activity than deferred relief can provide,[S17][S22][S51][S52]
- or a sector's downside turns out to be systematically estate-outstripping, non-compensable, or socially catastrophic, in which case the recommendation should move further toward ex ante bonding, reserves, insurance, and controller guarantees rather than more generous tax-loss treatment.[S16][S17][S47][S48][S49][S53][S54]
