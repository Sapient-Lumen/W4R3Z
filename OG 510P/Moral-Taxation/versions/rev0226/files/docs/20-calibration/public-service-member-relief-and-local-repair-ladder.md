# Public service / member relief / local repair ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that **public, mutual, nonprofit, and regulated-service AI savings usually route first through service benefit, member or ratepayer relief, worker transition, local repair, and anti-supplantation rather than private-windfall language**, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when the gain should stay in service capacity, become user or member relief, fund worker and local repair, or remain as tightly disclosed fiscal slack?**[S11][S18][S21][S22][S29][S64][S67][S69][S70][S74][S83]

The constitutional answer is already closed: **where there is no meaningful private residual claimant, the first fiscal question is how AI-enabled gains become visible public or member benefit rather than dark treasury residue, while real private extraction, real controller duties, and real burdened-place claims still travel on their own lanes.**[S18][S21][S22][S29][S64][S67][S69][S70][S74][S83]

## Why calibration is still needed

Once the archive routes these cases away from ordinary private-windfall logic, four failures remain easy:

1. **service-hoarding** where institutions keep all savings internally even though users, members, workers, or burdened places were promised visible benefit,
2. **rate-cut tunnel vision** where every gain is forced into bill relief even when queues, coverage, care quality, resilience, or rights protection are the more urgent moral use,
3. **worker-and-place erasure** where automation-linked savings are celebrated while layoffs, retraining load, or local service strain are left unfunded,
4. **mission-branded dark treasury** where management claims public benefit but the gain disappears into opaque substitution, slack, or private side payments.[S11][S14][S18][S21][S22][S29][S64][S67][S69][S70][S74][S83]

## Small option set

### Option A — service-capacity first

Keep most AI-enabled gain inside the institution as service quality, access, resilience, staffing support, or rights-protection capacity.[S11][S22][S64][S69][S74]

This is strongest where the institution is serving urgent needs, rationing public rights, or operating with visible queues and brittle capacity. It is too weak when users or members are overcharged, or when worker and local burdens are being externalized.

### Option B — member or rate relief first

Push most gain outward as lower rates, member dividends, lower fees, or comparable direct user relief.[S18][S21][S22][S69][S70]

This is strongest for mutuals, cooperatives, regulated utilities, and similar organizations where members or ratepayers are the cleanest visible beneficiaries. It is too thin where quality, reliability, staffing resilience, or rights protection are deteriorating.

### Option C — worker and local-repair first

Route most gain first to transition support, severance buffering, retraining, staffing redesign, and local repair where the automation gain is visibly riding on concentrated displacement or service strain.[S11][S14][S22][S64][S67][S69]

This keeps burdens visible, but it overstates the case when there is little actual displacement or when users are still waiting on basic service access.

### Option D — layered visible-benefit split

Use a layered answer:

1. **service integrity first** where access, quality, safety, rights, or resilience remain fragile,
2. **member or user relief next** where charges are the morally salient bearer-side problem,
3. **worker and local repair alongside** where displacement or burden is real,
4. **tightly disclosed retained capacity last** for reserves, modernization, or future resilience only inside an anti-supplantation corridor.[S11][S14][S18][S21][S22][S29][S64][S67][S69][S70][S74][S83]

This is the archive's cleanest default because it preserves visible reciprocity without pretending every institution has the same moral output channel.

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt **Option D** as the default calibration ladder once the archive has already decided the case belongs in the public / mutual / nonprofit / regulated-service lane.

That means:

1. **screen private extraction first** so executive capture, contractor rents, affiliate profit, or quasi-equity upside are taxed on their real private lanes rather than hidden inside the mission form,[S10][S18][S21][S29][S34][S35][S83]
2. **repair service fragility before treasury comfort** where wait times, coverage gaps, care quality, legal access, safety, or reliability are visibly under stress,[S11][S22][S64][S69][S74]
3. **give member or user relief priority** where rates, fees, or charges are the clearest channel through which the gain should appear,[S18][S21][S22][S69][S70]
4. **attach worker-transition and local-repair shares** where concentrated displacement, retraining load, or service-strain externalization is real,[S11][S14][S22][S64][S67][S69]
5. **allow retained fiscal capacity only inside a disclosed corridor** with maintenance of effort, named use, and review triggers so the gain does not vanish into substitution or managerial fog.[S22][S29][S64][S69][S70]

## Default visible-benefit ladder

| Lane | When it should apply | Default use of gain | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. ordinary small-gain lane | the gain is modest or uncertain and no major service, rate, worker, or local issue is yet visible | keep ordinary budgeting, disclosure, and ordinary taxation only | do not invent a ceremonial AI-gain split where the amount is trivial or the facts are weak |
| 1. service-integrity lane | access, quality, safety, reliability, legal access, or rights protection remain visibly fragile | service capacity, staffing resilience, quality improvement, and rights-protection infrastructure first | do not market the gain as user relief while queues, coverage gaps, or rights failures remain obvious.[S11][S22][S64][S69][S74] |
| 2. member- or rate-relief lane | the institution is basically stable on service integrity and the clearest injustice is user, member, or ratepayer overcharge | lower rates, lower fees, member dividends, or equivalent direct bearer relief | do not leave member-owned or ratepayer-funded gains sitting as managerial slack.[S18][S21][S22][S69][S70] |
| 3. service + worker/local lane | gains are real but displacement, retraining load, or burdened-place strain is also visible | service integrity plus worker transition and local repair shares | local and worker repair are real, but they do not erase service obligations or take the whole gain.[S11][S14][S22][S64][S67][S69] |
| 4. layered visible-benefit lane | several claimant channels are simultaneously real | blended split across service capacity, member or user relief, worker transition, local repair, and disclosed reserve | publish the split, the baseline, and the review cadence; no dark treasury residue.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S64][S69][S70][S74][S83] |
| 5. extraction override | mission form is present but the gain is actually being siphoned to executives, vendors, financiers, or affiliates | ordinary/private extraction taxes and controls on the real private takers | do not let public or nonprofit branding shield private capture.[S10][S18][S21][S29][S34][S35][S83] |

## Four calibration rules that matter most

### 1. Service fragility beats abstract treasury optimization

Where hospitals, schools, care systems, public agencies, or other rights-adjacent institutions remain visibly brittle, the first use of AI-enabled savings should usually be service integrity, access, safety, and resilience rather than generic budget relief or celebratory member payout.[S11][S22][S64][S69][S74]

### 2. Member or rate relief matters most when users are the live residual bearers

For cooperatives, mutuals, utilities, and similar bodies, the morally central question is often whether the people financing the system see the gain back through rates, fees, or member returns. If they do not, "not-for-profit" language can mask a quiet transfer into managerial slack or hidden substitution.[S18][S21][S22][S69][S70][S83]

### 3. Worker and local repair should scale with visible displacement, not slogan intensity

The archive treats worker transition and burdened-place repair as real claims when automation-linked gains ride on concentrated job loss, retraining needs, or local service strain. But these are usually companion claims or shares, not proof that the whole gain ceases to be a service or member benefit question.[S11][S14][S22][S64][S67][S69]

### 4. Retained fiscal slack is allowed only if it is named, bounded, and reviewable

Sometimes the right answer is not immediate rate cuts or complete spend-out. A public body may need reserves, modernization, cyber hardening, or future resilience. But the archive accepts retained capacity only where the use is disclosed, maintenance of effort is visible, and later review can tell whether the institution actually turned AI savings into public or member benefit.[S22][S29][S64][S69][S70]

## Anti-patterns

- **queue-blind rebates** — celebrating lower charges while service quality or rights access remains obviously broken.[S11][S22][S64][S74]
- **mission-form hoarding** — claiming public benefit while keeping gains as opaque managerial slack.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S69][S70][S83]
- **worker-erasure savings** — booking automation gains without transition support, staffing redesign, or repair for concentrated displacement.[S11][S14][S22][S64][S67]
- **all-rates, no-resilience** — forcing every gain into immediate fee relief even where reliability, safety, or future capacity is failing.[S22][S64][S69][S70][S74]
- **charity-halo laundering** — using public or nonprofit branding to hide executive, vendor, affiliate, or financier extraction.[S10][S18][S21][S29][S34][S35][S83]

## What would change the recommendation

This ladder should be tightened, loosened, or replaced if any of the following become clearer:

- better public metrics for service fragility, care quality, reliability, or rights access,
- strong evidence that rate or member relief is predictably the morally dominant benefit channel in more sectors than the archive now assumes,
- strong evidence that worker or local burdens are consistently better handled through spending-side equalization rather than institution-linked shares,
- or strong evidence that retained-capacity corridors are routinely becoming dark-treasury substitution rather than genuine resilience investment.[S11][S14][S22][S29][S64][S67][S69][S70][S74]
