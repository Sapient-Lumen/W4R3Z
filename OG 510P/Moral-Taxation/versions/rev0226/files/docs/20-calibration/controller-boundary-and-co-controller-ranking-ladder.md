# Controller-boundary and co-controller ranking ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that present AI taxation should follow **real governance, continuation power, and residual upside** rather than mere visibility, branding, remittance convenience, or paper titles, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when an actor stays outside the controller role, when it becomes a bounded co-controller for one deployment layer, when it becomes the primary controller, and when several actors must be treated as a real controller group or as predecessor / successor / step-in controllers across time?**[S10][S15][S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S41][S83]

The constitutional question is already settled: **current AI is taxed through real controllers, controller groups, beneficiaries, bottlenecks, rents, and harms; ordinary use, external oversight, passive infrastructure, paper branding, remittance visibility, or similar outside-interface facts do not by themselves relocate controller status, while real inside-the-loop command, commissioning, customization, step-in control, or group governance can.**[S10][S15][S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S41][S83]

## Why calibration is still needed

The archive now has a strong controller-boundary doctrine family, but practical disputes still need a **small ranking ladder** rather than a fresh case memo every time. A usable calibration has to avoid six opposing errors at once:

1. **visibility anchoring** — treating the actor with the invoice, logo, app store listing, meter, or billing relationship as the controller merely because it is easiest to see,[S21][S27][S83]
2. **infrastructure magnetism** — assuming the deepest host, tool rail, or capital layer must be the real controller because it sits closest to capacity,[S10][S15][S16][S17]
3. **controller vacuum theater** — letting distributed governance, reseller chains, or paper transfers dissolve liability into nobody,[S20][S21][S34][S35][S41]
4. **universal co-controller drift** — overreacting by calling every visible participant a controller and thereby blurring the difference between real governance and ordinary support,[S21][S27][S83]
5. **time-slice laundering** — using version resets, emergency handoffs, or affiliate transfers to pretend the controller changed without any real governance break,[S20][S21][S34][S35][S41]
6. and **collection-subject confusion** — mistaking the best remittance anchor, registry contact, or permit holder for the morally primary controller.[S21][S27][S41][S83]

## Small option set

### Option A — visible-anchor default

Pick the actor with the public brand, invoice, permit, or customer-facing service contract and treat it as the controller unless strong evidence says otherwise.

This is administratively tempting, but it overweights visibility and underweights real governance. It fails in white-label stacks, managed-service structures, reseller chains, and controller groups.[S21][S27][S83]

### Option B — deepest-stack default

Treat the most upstream lab, host, cloud, tool rail, or capital layer as the controller unless it can prove that someone else really governs the deployment.

This catches some hidden power, but it misfires when the real mission-setting, retrieval-governing, approval-gating, or step-in authority sits downstream or in a buyer / controller group.[S10][S15][S16][S17][S20]

### Option C — rebuttable controller ranking ladder

Start with the archive's ranking variables — durable governance, continuation power, residual upside, and bounded exceptions for real inside-the-loop command — then classify actors as outside interfaces, co-controllers, primary controllers, or controller groups only as far as the evidence requires.[S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S41][S83]

This is the archive's best default because it preserves accountability without flattening every participant into the same role.

### Option D — joint-controller blob by default

Wherever more than one actor matters, treat all materially involved firms or institutions as joint primary controllers.

This defeats some evasion but is too blunt. It collapses remitters, infrastructure, ordinary tool provision, and real governance into one undifferentiated mass and makes review and exit harder.[S21][S27][S41][S83]

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt a **five-stage practical ladder**:

| Stage | When it should apply | Classification answer | Main guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. outside-interface default | the actor only prompts, distributes, audits, insures, hosts passively, processes payments, supplies inputs, or otherwise interfaces from outside the live governance loop | **not a controller** | do not confuse visibility, billing, oversight, or support with governance |
| 1. disclosure and collection anchor | controller status is disputed but the state still needs a filer, remitter, registry contact, or permit holder now | **name a temporary collection anchor and require a controller map** | remittance convenience does not settle moral status |
| 2. bounded deployment-layer control | the actor persistently governs retrieval, tool permissions, system scaffolds, mission approval, safety posture, release gates, or other ordinary deployment choices for one layer | **treat as a real co-controller for that governed layer** | keep liability layer-specific rather than collapsing the whole stack |
| 3. primary controller ranking | one actor or aligned group can ordinarily continue, halt, redesign, monetize, or strategically benefit from the stack more than the others | **treat that actor or aligned group as the primary controller** | rank by durable governance, continuation power, and residual upside, not by logo, invoice, or physical depth |
| 4. temporal or shared-command override | governance is genuinely shared, or controller authority moves across time through a real coalition, successor chain, or step-in command period | **treat as a controller group or timed predecessor / successor / step-in controller sequence** | do not allow shared or temporal governance to dissolve duties into nobody-liability |

The archive's provisional setting is therefore **0 by default, 1 whenever collection must proceed before classification is final, 2 when a layer is materially governed downstream, 3 whenever one actor or aligned group clearly outranks the rest, and 4 for genuine coalitions or time-sliced command**.[S10][S15][S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S41][S83]

## Priority evidence order

When a live dispute is hard, rank evidence in roughly this order:

1. who can **continue, halt, or materially redesign** the deployment in ordinary conditions,[S16][S17][S20]
2. who can **set or veto** mission scope, retrieval corpus, tool permissions, approval gates, or release / rollback decisions,[S10][S16][S17][S20]
3. who captures the **residual upside** or bears the strategic downside if the system succeeds or fails,[S20][S21][S83]
4. whether authority is **durable and routine** rather than rare, advisory, or emergency-only,[S20][S21][S41]
5. and whether the apparent controller story survives anti-fragmentation, successor-continuity, and collection-anchor checks.[S21][S27][S34][S35][S41][S83]

## Anti-patterns

- **brand-shell answers** — using labels, storefronts, or OEM packaging as though they settle governance.[S21][S27][S83]
- **invoice-chain answers** — equating the payer, merchant-of-record, or platform remitter with the controller.[S21][S27][S83]
- **depth fetish** — assuming the deepest compute or API layer must be the real controller.[S10][S15][S16]
- **committee vapor** — saying the stack is governed by a protocol, foundation, or committee and therefore by no one.[S20][S21][S34][S35]
- **jointness maximalism** — treating every participant as a co-controller because several actors matter in different ways.[S21][S27][S83]

## What would change the recommendation

This ladder should be tightened, loosened, or partly replaced if any of the following become clearer:

- controller-map disclosure becomes reliable enough that stage 1 resolves most disputes with little extra adjudication,[S27][S34][S35]
- boundary gaming through reseller chains, managed service wrappers, white-label stacks, or controller groups proves more systematic than the archive now assumes,[S10][S20][S21][S34][S35]
- a recurring class of disputes shows that one evidence type — for example mission-setting procurement or real step-in command — should outrank the current ordering more decisively,[S16][S17][S20][S41]
- or future artificial persons ever become real tax subjects, in which case owner-side controller ranking must stop where person-regime standing actually begins.[S20]
