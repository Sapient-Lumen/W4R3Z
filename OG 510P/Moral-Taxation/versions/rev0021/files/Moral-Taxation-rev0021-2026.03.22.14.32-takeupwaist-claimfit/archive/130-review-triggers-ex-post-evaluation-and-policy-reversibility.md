# 130 — Review triggers, ex post evaluation, and policy reversibility

## One-line thesis

A morally serious tax should not be launched as if the first draft were the last word. It should say **what evidence would count as success or failure, who will review it, when review happens, what triggers automatic adjustment or sunset, and how the policy can be narrowed, converted, or repealed if it misfires**. Otherwise inertia, capture, and administrative convenience quietly replace justification.

## Why this matters

The OECD's Recommendation on Public Policy Evaluation says governments should establish robust institutions and practices that promote the use of public policy evaluations.[S59] OECD's 2025 implementation toolkit says evaluation systems should build a culture of continuous learning and evidence-informed policymaking, potentially improving policy impact and trust in government action.[S60] OECD's 2025 regulatory work also says governments need adapt-and-learn processes, feedback loops, and ex post review with practical recommendations for amendment, replacement, or removal.[S61] In tax-specific settings, OECD work on incentives and related policy tools notes that clear benchmarks and sunset clauses can limit fiscal costs and reduce policy capture, though they can also increase uncertainty if they are vague or constantly renegotiated.[S63]

## Design rule

When uncertainty, emergency, novelty, or concentrated power is material, pair the tax with a **review architecture** rather than pretending one statute can settle the question forever.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Name the falsifiable claim

A good tax should say what it is trying to change in observable terms:

- final burden-bearing,
- revenue adequacy,
- emissions or congestion reduction,
- rent capture,
- market-entry conditions,
- worker transition outcomes,
- local repair,
- or public-capacity gains.

If no measurable claim is named, later review becomes theater.

### 2. Separate routine review from trigger review

Use two clocks:

- a **routine cadence** for ordinary evaluation, such as annual monitoring and a deeper review every few years,
- and **trigger review** when something moves sharply: inflation, concentration, grid stress, pass-through, avoidance, insolvency, rights impacts, or capability jumps.

A policy that waits only for calendar review may miss rapid drift; a policy that reopens on every headline becomes noise.

### 3. Review incidence, not just receipts

The archive's incidence rule means review cannot stop at collections. Ask whether the burden actually stayed with the intended target or leaked onto workers, tenants, weak suppliers, dependent caregivers, or low-income users. Review should therefore include distributional and bargaining-path evidence, not just treasury totals.[S60][S61]

### 4. Build a credible exit, reset, or conversion path

The review architecture should say what happens if the tax fails:

- **recalibrate** rates, thresholds, or indexation,
- **convert** a temporary levy into an ordinary standing instrument if the emergency becomes structural,
- **sunset** the rule if the factual predicate disappears,
- or **repeal and replace** if the instrument was the wrong one.

Without an exit path, “temporary” taxes become permanent by fatigue, while failed incentives survive by lobbying rather than merit.[S61][S63]

### 5. Match review intensity to uncertainty and power

The harder the policy is to measure, the more novel the base, and the greater the concentration of power, the stronger the review architecture should be. This matters especially for:

- emergency taxes,
- experimental AI levies,
- negotiated local abatements,
- tax expenditures sold as transition tools,
- and frontier-compute or deployment charges in a rapidly changing technical environment.[S15][S60][S61][S62]

### 6. Tie AI-side taxes to post-deployment monitoring and decommissioning logic

For AI-related levies, routine evaluation should watch not only receipts and compute volumes but also:

- incident rates,
- near-misses,
- performance drift,
- market concentration,
- local utility burdens,
- worker-transition outcomes,
- and whether high-risk systems now exceed the tolerances that justified conditional operation.

NIST's AI RMF playbook says organizations should regularly review risk tolerances, monitor deployed systems for drift and impacts, capture external feedback, and decommission systems that exceed established risk tolerances.[S54][S62]

## Guardrails

- Do not use “review later” as an excuse for launching a tax with no floor protection now.
- Do not make sunset clauses so easily renewable that they become pseudo-permanent without real reconsideration.
- Do not write review triggers so vague that no failure state is ever acknowledged.
- Do not let politically powerful incumbents be the only parties with standing to define success.

## Failure modes

- **Temporary-by-inertia**: an emergency or experimental tax never expires because no real review path exists.
- **Metrics theater**: treasury collections are reviewed while incidence, exclusion, or capture are ignored.
- **Lobby-reset drift**: sunset or review becomes a recurring bargaining occasion for stronger incumbents rather than a disciplined reconsideration of the rule.
- **Frozen mismatch**: AI or platform taxes stay fixed while the underlying technology, market structure, or local burden changes rapidly.
- **Evidence-proof taxation**: policymakers refuse to name what evidence would count against the instrument, so the tax becomes insulated from correction.

## Practical tests

1. What evidence would make this tax look better, worse, or obsolete within three years?
2. Who performs the review, and are affected outsiders able to contest the official story of success?
3. What trigger automatically forces reconsideration: pass-through, inflation, concentration, local burden, capability drift, or failure to meet the stated purpose?
4. If the tax is emergency, experimental, or tightly context-bound, what is the sunset, conversion, or repeal path?

## Archive judgment

The archive therefore adds a simple warning: **a tax without a review architecture is too tempted to become policy by inertia**. Morally serious taxation should name its evidence, cadence, triggers, and exit path in advance when uncertainty, novelty, or concentrated power is material.
