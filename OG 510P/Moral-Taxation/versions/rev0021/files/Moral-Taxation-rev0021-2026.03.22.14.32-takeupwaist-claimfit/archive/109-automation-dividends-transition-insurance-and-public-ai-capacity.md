# 109 — Automation dividends, transition insurance, and public AI capacity

## One-line thesis

When AI sharply raises output or margins by displacing labor or compressing bargaining power, part of the gain should be socialized as an automation dividend.

## Why this matters

AI can increase productivity while also concentrating gains, worsening wage inequality, or eroding entry-level work.[S11][S13][S14] A moral tax system should not let owners of AI capture the whole upside while workers, young entrants, and public institutions absorb the transition cost.

## Design rule

Use AI windfall revenues to finance adjustment, training, public compute, and equal access to beneficial AI capacity.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Windfall-sharing trigger

Possible triggers include:

- sustained margin expansion attributable to automation,
- task displacement above a threshold,
- payroll contraction with output growth,
- or sectoral concentration tied to AI deployment.

### 1A. Do not confuse windfall sharing with gross-activity taxation

Automation-dividend logic is strongest when extraordinary rents or margin expansion are visible. Where firms are genuinely loss-making, early-stage, or only externally costly rather than rent-rich, controller-side taxes should shift toward externality pricing, reserves, or ordinary loss-recognizing tax rules rather than pretending every AI activity is already a surplus to be shared.[S15][S50][S51]

### 2. Use proceeds for transition, not only deficit fill

Fund:

- wage insurance,
- retraining,
- portable benefits,
- youth labor-market entry support,
- public-interest AI infrastructure,
- and local transition compacts.

### 3. Build public AI capacity

A share of revenues should support public compute, civic models, and open evaluation capacity so AI does not become a pure oligopoly utility.

### 4. Pair with competition policy

Dividend finance without anti-monopoly action can normalize concentration.


## Guardrails

- Do not create a permanent subsidy for firms to automate recklessly and let the public pick up the pieces.
- Keep the trigger measurable enough to administer.
- Avoid using “innovation” rhetoric to exempt dominant firms from reciprocity.


## Failure modes

- **Owner-takes-all automation**: productivity rises, bargaining power collapses, public gets nothing.
- **Training theater**: revenues are announced for reskilling but mostly vanish into general budgets.
- **Public-capacity starvation**: every actor depends on a few private clouds and model vendors.


## Practical tests

1. If AI doubles margins in a sector, does the public share any of that gain?
2. Are displaced or newly excluded workers better insured than before?
3. Does the regime build non-oligarchic public AI capacity?
