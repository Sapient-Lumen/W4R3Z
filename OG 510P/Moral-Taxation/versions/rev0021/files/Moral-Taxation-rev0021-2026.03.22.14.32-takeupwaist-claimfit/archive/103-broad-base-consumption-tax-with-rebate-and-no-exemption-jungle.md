# 103 — Broad-base consumption tax with rebate and no exemption jungle

## One-line thesis

If a consumption tax is used, its best form is broad and legible, with regressivity corrected openly through rebates or transfers rather than by cluttering the base with countless special rates.

## Why this matters

Consumption taxes can be productive, relatively hard to evade, and compatible with growth, but exemption jungles often create lobbying, complexity, and hidden favoritism. The better answer is usually a broad base plus an explicit distributive correction.[S3][S4]

## Design rule

Use broad consumption taxation only alongside a visible floor correction.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Uniform or near-uniform base

Final household consumption should generally be taxed on a broad base.[S3]

### 2. Rebate before relief politics metastasizes

Correct regressivity with:

- cash rebates,
- real-time refunds where feasible,
- child supplements,
- or negative-income offsets.[S4]

### 3. Reserve reduced rates for narrow constitutional reasons

Examples may include public-health or administratively unavoidable cases, not every sympathetic industry.

### 4. Pair with rent taxation

A broad VAT is more morally acceptable when the system also taxes land, inheritance, monopoly, and carbon seriously.


## Guardrails

- Keep filing and refund procedures short.
- Avoid hidden favoritism through industry-specific carveouts.
- Do not use a broad VAT as an excuse to weaken progressive direct taxation.


## Failure modes

- **Exemption jungle**: a tax code turned into an industrial-policy scrapbook.
- **Regressive opacity**: poor households pay more because rebates are underbuilt or delayed.
- **One-sided modernity**: the state adopts sophisticated consumption taxes while leaving wealth lightly taxed.


## Practical tests

1. Would a low-income family be made whole more effectively by a rebate than by today's reduced-rate clutter?
2. Can the tax be explained in one page?
3. Are politically connected sectors receiving complexity subsidies?
