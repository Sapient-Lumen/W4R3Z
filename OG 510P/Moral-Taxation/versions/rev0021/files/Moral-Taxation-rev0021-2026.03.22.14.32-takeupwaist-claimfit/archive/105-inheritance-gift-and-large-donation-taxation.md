# 105 — Inheritance, gift, and large-donation taxation

## One-line thesis

Large intergenerational transfers should be taxed more seriously than they usually are because they reproduce hierarchy without requiring corresponding contribution from the recipient.

## Why this matters

Inheritance is one of the clearest channels through which inequality hardens into durable class position. It can therefore be a justified target for revenue, anti-oligarchy, and equality-of-opportunity reasons.[S8]

## Design rule

Tax very large transfers to recipients progressively, while protecting ordinary family continuity and small bequests.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Prefer recipient-side progressivity

The moral question is how much unearned advantage the recipient receives, not only the size of the donor's estate.

### 2. Aggregate lifetime receipts

A lifetime accessions approach is often cleaner than pretending each gift exists in isolation.

### 3. Carve out small inheritances, not dynastic scale

Protect ordinary household transfers while taxing concentrations that create lasting domination.

### 4. Close trust and entity workarounds

The point is to tax real economic transfer, not theatrical form.


## Guardrails

- Avoid forced liquidation of genuinely small family businesses where staged payment or rollover is possible.
- Treat staged payment as a liquidity tool, not as a dynasty loophole; interest, valuation discipline, and anti-abuse rules matter.
- Keep anti-avoidance rules strong enough that the tax survives contact with sophisticated planning.
- Pair inheritance taxation with good capital-gains and valuation rules.


## Failure modes

- **Dynasty loophole state**: nominal inheritance tax with trust architecture that defeats it.
- **Symbolic tax**: high headline rates, tiny effective base.
- **Family-farm shield theater**: narrow hard cases are used to immunize very large fortunes.


## Practical tests

1. Does the regime distinguish modest household continuity from dynasty-scale transfers?
2. Are lifetime receipts tracked across forms?
3. Can the wealthy simply route around the tax through entities and timing games?
