# 101 — Tax rents and bads before ordinary labor

## One-line thesis

A just tax system should begin with what people can seize, exclude, inherit, monopolize, or externalize before it begins with what they merely do for a living.

## Why this matters

Modern tax systems often over-tax visible payroll and under-tax quiet rent extraction. That is backward. Labor is usually the most personally burdened and politically tractable base; land, monopoly, inheritance, and pollution are often the least defended but most normatively attractive bases.

## Design rule

When choosing between taxing routine effort and taxing socially created advantage, choose socially created advantage first.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Name the rent

- site value
- natural scarcity
- regulatory privilege
- platform lock-in
- crisis windfall
- inherited concentration

### 2. Name the bad

- carbon
- congestion
- extraction damage
- public-health burden
- systemic incident risk

### 3. Leave wages for later

Only after the rent and harm map is reasonably taxed should the state lean hard on ordinary labor income.

### 4. Prefer durable bases

Land, residual profit, inheritances, and emissions are often better constitutional anchors than ad hoc nuisance taxes.


## Guardrails

- Do not call every profit a rent.
- Do not confuse correction of external harms with general revenue extraction.
- Keep a protected floor for low-income households even when a tax is efficient in the abstract.


## Failure modes

- **Payroll state**: most revenue comes from taxing work while wealth and location rents slide free.
- **Moral camouflage**: a wage surtax is sold as fairness while oligopoly and inheritance remain lightly touched.
- **Tax tourism theater**: easy-to-announce taxes substitute for durable base repair.


## Practical tests

1. If this tax vanished, would inherited or monopoly advantage remain undertaxed?
2. Is the system taxing bad conduct or just taxing administratively weak people?
3. Could part of the burden be moved from wages to rents without losing revenue adequacy?
