# Proposal scorecard

This is the archive's smallest reusable rubric for judging tax proposals across humans, firms, places, and machine systems.

## One-line thesis

A morally serious tax proposal should score well on the same small set of tests every time: **protect the floor, keep the human assessment unit from silently becoming a domination device, follow real control across beneficial owners and related-party groups rather than paper fragmentation, target rents or harms before ordinary labor, attach liability where control and benefit are real, verify that final incidence does not quietly land on protected people through pass-through or bargaining asymmetry, route proceeds toward morally relevant protection, repair, or shared capacity, and make sure payment timing fits real cash flow rather than coercive liquidation or permanent deferral, while gains and losses are treated on sufficiently symmetric terms that upside is not taxed now while downside is recognized only weakly or too late, while nominal drift is not quietly treated as real gain, real ability-to-pay, or an acceptable way to erode relief, while notice, transition, and retroactivity rules protect legitimate reliance without fossilizing privilege, while measurement and valuation remain honest enough to justify coercion at all, while collection powers remain privacy-bounded, staged, and proportionate enough to preserve equal standing, while any direct subject has a real path to notice, remedy, and representation rather than paper subjection without voice, and while abatements, credits, deductions, and subsidies do not quietly reverse the claimed burden through a hidden negative-tax stack, while formally neutral rules are checked for direct or indirect discrimination and status-proxy effects rather than assumed innocent by default, and while insolvency ranking plus limited-liability boundaries do not let the state or controllers leapfrog wages, victims, or public cleanup through a thin shell at the point of failure, and while catastrophic or non-compensable risks are not casually converted into ordinary priced permission slips when ex ante gating, conditional approval, or disapproval should have come first, and while experimental, emergency, or fast-moving regimes publish what evidence would count as success or failure, what cadence and triggers force reconsideration, and how the rule can tighten, loosen, convert, or expire rather than hardening by inertia, and while floor-protecting rebates, refunds, and credits are delivered with low enough claim friction that the intended people can actually receive them without heroic procedural effort**.

## Score scale

- **+2** — strongly satisfies the criterion
- **+1** — mostly satisfies it
- **0** — mixed or unclear
- **-1** — significant defect
- **-2** — strong failure

## Criteria

| ID | Criterion | What a good proposal looks like |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Floor protection | Does not push people below subsistence; uses rebates, transfers, or exclusions where needed.[S4][S18] |
| C2 | Subject/base clarity | Cleanly distinguishes taxpayer, tax base, and liable controller.[S20] |
| C15 | Assessment-unit and care fit | Defaults to the individual person for direct human earnings liability unless a household unit is strongly justified; recognizes dependents and caregiving without hiding intra-household inequality or creating avoidable second-earner traps.[S31][S32][S33] |
| C16 | Beneficial-ownership and anti-fragmentation fit | Follows real control and benefit across trusts, shells, subsidiaries, and related parties; uses aggregation narrowly to defeat threshold gaming and paper splitting without erasing genuinely independent actors.[S34][S35] |
| C20 | Anti-discrimination and status-proxy fit | Avoids direct discrimination and indirect status-proxy burdens; where unequal effects are plausible, uses disaggregated review, accessible channels, and cleaner measures of need, harm, control, or benefit.[S32][S33][S44][S45][S46] |
| C3 | Rent/harm priority | Taxes land, inheritance, monopoly, pollution, or windfall before routine labor where possible.[S2][S5][S8] |
| C4 | Control-and-benefit fit | Hits actors who can redesign, insure, slow, or stop the relevant conduct.[S16][S17][S20] |
| C23 | Precaution and non-compensable-risk fit | Distinguishes ordinary priceable harms from catastrophic, irreversible, or non-compensable risks; uses ex ante approval, caps, bonding, decommissioning, or disapproval where taxation alone would amount to selling permission.[S15][S17][S53][S54] |
| C5 | Incidence discipline | The likely real burden does not mainly fall on protected workers, low-income consumers, tenants, or weak suppliers; where pass-through is likely, compensation is explicit.[S4][S21] |
| C6 | Timing and liquidity fit | Payment timing matches cash generation, asset liquidity, or risk creation; uses withholding, instalments, deferral, liens, or reserves where needed without turning relief into avoidance.[S5][S15][S23][S24] |
| C17 | Notice, transition, and reliance fit | Major liability is prospective by default, gives meaningful notice, uses finite transition where reliance is real, and keeps any retroactivity narrow enough to justify clearly without permanently grandfathering privilege.[S23][S27][S36][S37][S38] |
| C22 | Downside symmetry and loss-recognition fit | Genuine losses, negative years, and downside are recognized on terms sufficiently symmetric with the taxation of gains, using carry-forwards, value preservation, carry-backs, or refundability where justified, while artificial losses and duplicate deductions are blocked.[S50][S51][S52] |
| C24 | Real-value and indexation fit | The design does not quietly tax inflation or nominal drift as if they were real gains or real ability-to-pay; where inflation would otherwise do hidden work, brackets, allowances, loss relief, depreciation, bonds, thresholds, and safe harbors are indexed, honestly approximated, or openly left unindexed as a deliberate real policy choice.[S50][S55][S56][S57][S58] |
| C7 | Measurement and valuation fit | The base can be observed, valued, or proxied without fake precision; revaluation cadence, proxy disclosure, and graduation rules are explicit.[S16][S23][S25][S26][S27] |
| C8 | Scale and jurisdiction fit | Matches the base to the level of government and jurisdiction with the strongest moral and administrative claim: situs, burdened locality, destination, market access, residence, or club coordination as appropriate.[S1][S3][S9][S28] |
| C9 | Anti-arbitrage integrity | Does not reward relabeling human labor as capital service, profit as fee flow, or owned AI systems as fake independent persons.[S9][S19][S20] |
| C10 | Legibility and appeal | Liability can be explained, contested, and reviewed without black-box dependence.[S1][S16][S17][S27] |
| C18 | Enforcement, privacy, and proportionality fit | Collection powers are staged, hardship-aware, and bounded by purpose; predictive tools are reviewable; enforcement does not rely on unconstrained surveillance or disproportionate coercion.[S16][S17][S27][S39][S40][S41] |
| C19 | Standing, remedy, and representation fit | Any direct subject can be informed, heard, and represented, with access to appeal or review; where that bundle is absent, liability is routed to a controller, guardian, or beneficiary instead of a rightless stand-in.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44] |
| C11 | Competition and public-interest fit | Harder on bottlenecks and rent concentration; lighter on small, public-interest, or open research uses where appropriate.[S10][S11] |
| C12 | Net fiscal-stack integrity | The claimed burden survives netting against abatements, accelerated depreciation, preferential input pricing, tax expenditures, and direct subsidies; floor-protecting relief is kept distinct from rentier carve-outs.[S29][S30][S31] |
| C13 | Proceeds and reciprocity | Revenue is routed toward floor protection, harm repair, local equalisation, transition, or shared public capability rather than opaque sinkholes or incumbent capture.[S1][S4][S7][S22] |
| C25 | Automaticity and take-up fit | Floor-protecting rebates, refunds, credits, or repair payments are delivered with low claim friction through automaticity, prefill, one-step confirmation, or similarly accessible channels where the state already has enough information; non-take-up, digital chokepoints, and inaccessible correction paths are treated as defects.[S64][S65][S66] |
| C14 | Review, trigger, and reversibility discipline | Names the measurable purpose, review cadence, trigger conditions, and credible path to recalibration, conversion, sunset, or repeal when the tax is emergency, experimental, negotiated, or otherwise uncertain.[S15][S54][S59][S60][S61][S62][S63] |
| C21 | Failure ordering and limited-liability fit | In insolvency or abandonment, the design does not rely on broad tax superpriority over wages, estate administration, tort / cleanup claims, or public repair; high-risk activities use bonds, reserves, insurance, or controller guarantees where needed.[S47][S48][S49] |

## Reading the score

- **+42 to +50** — archive-aligned proposal; usually adopt or prototype
- **+20 to +41** — conditionally acceptable; redesign weak points
- **0 to +19** — thin justification; likely missing a better base or correction path
- **below 0** — reject unless the surrounding context is extraordinary

## Fast examples

| Proposal | Likely score pattern | Archive judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Land value tax with deferral option for cash-poor households and a published phase-in | high on C1, C3, C5, C6, C7, C8, C10, C12, C13, C16, C17, and usually C15 | strong default candidate.[S5][S21][S23] |
| Broad VAT with visible low-income rebate | high on C1, C5, C8, C10, C12, and C13; moderate on C3, C6, C7, and C15 | acceptable as part of a larger package, not as the sole justice instrument.[S3][S4] |
| Universal per-token LLM tax | weak on C2, C4, C5, C7, C9, C10, C11, and usually C12 | usually reject; visible throughput is not the moral center of the problem.[S10][S15][S20] |
| AI windfall levy tied to payroll compression and margin expansion, with worker-side rebate and training fund | stronger on C3, C4, C5, C6, C7, C11, C12, C13, C14, C24, and often C22 | promising when trigger design, timing, burden protection, downside treatment, real-value indexing, review cadence, and sunset / conversion logic are explicit.[S11][S13][S14][S50][S55][S56][S59][S60][S61] |
| Data-centre utility charge paired with a long property-tax abatement and discounted public-power contract | decent on C4 and C8 but weak on C12 | redesign; the net fiscal stack cancels too much of the claimed burden.[S15][S29][S30] |
| Automated debt collection regime that cross-matches broad consumer data, freezes small accounts quickly, and offers appeal only after recovery | weak on C1, C6, C10, and especially C18 | redesign; the collection architecture has become more coercive than the tax base justifies.[S27][S39][S40][S41] |
| Mandatory joint filing with steep second-earner penalty and weak caregiver support | weak on C1, C5, C10, C13, and especially C15 | redesign; the household unit is doing too much coercive work.[S31][S32][S33] |
| Direct tax on an owned model as if it were already a civic person | fails C2, C4, C9, C16, C19, and usually C10 | reject unless moral personhood threshold has actually been crossed and the subject has standing plus non-owner representation.[S19][S20][S42][S43] |
| Ordinary corporate tax claim that outranks unpaid wages and site-cleanup liabilities in insolvency | weak on C1, C4, C13, and especially C21 | redesign; require narrower trust-tax priority, controller-side reserves, or ex ante bonds instead of broad treasury leapfrogging.[S47][S48][S49] |
| Special AI minimum tax on gross revenue with no loss carry-forward and no public-interest exception | weak on C4, C6, C11, and especially C22 | redesign; the state is taxing gross activity while pretending genuine downside years do not count.[S15][S50][S51] |
| Frontier-deployment levy sold as permission to operate despite unresolved catastrophic or irreversible risk | weak on C4, C10, and especially C23 | reject or redesign; if the risk cannot be reasonably mitigated or repaired after the fact, taxation is the wrong first instrument.[S15][S17][S53][S54] |
| Nominally frozen income-tax brackets and unindexed loss carry-forwards during sustained inflation | weak on C1, C6, C22, and especially C24 | redesign; nominal drift is doing hidden distributive work that should be indexed, honestly approximated, or defended openly as a real tightening.[S50][S55][S56][S57] |
| Emergency AI facility levy with no published metrics, no review body, and auto-renewal by administrative discretion | weak on C10, C13, and especially C14 | redesign; the rule may be necessary, but without evidence thresholds, review cadence, and an exit path it is too vulnerable to inertia and capture.[S15][S54][S59][S60][S61][S62] |
| Broad VAT rebate for low-income households claimable only through a separate annual online application with mandatory bank account and weak correction support | weak on C1, C5, C10, and especially C25 | redesign; the floor exists on paper, but claim friction and digital chokepoints mean too much of the intended protection will never arrive.[S64][S65][S66] |
