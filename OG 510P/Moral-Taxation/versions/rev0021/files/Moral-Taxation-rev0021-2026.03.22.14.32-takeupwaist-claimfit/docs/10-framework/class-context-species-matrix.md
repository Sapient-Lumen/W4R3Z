
# Class / context / species matrix

This matrix is the archive's tightest single answer to the user's framing. For the more operational schedule, see [`ideal-taxation-by-class-context-and-species.md`](ideal-taxation-by-class-context-and-species.md).

| Subject or class | Moral status now | Best tax anchor | Default treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Child, dependent, or person below subsistence | rights-bearing person | protected floor, not extraction | negative income support, public provision, and where possible automatic or low-friction delivery |
| Single parent or heavy unpaid caregiver | rights-bearing person with dependent load | protected floor plus care and dependency recognition | low or negative net burden, refundable support, childcare/care provision, and individual-based liability rather than coerced jointness |
| Low-wage or median human worker | rights-bearing person | ability-to-pay after subsistence | light direct burden, rebate-protected consumption taxation |
| High-income human person | rights-bearing person | ability-to-pay plus luxury and inheritance | progressive personal taxes and capital-rent capture |
| Rentier or dynastic household | rights-bearing persons holding concentrated advantage | land, inheritance, anti-deferral capital income, luxury | steep treatment of unearned concentration |
| Household consuming in market | rights-bearing unit but not sole moral unit | final consumption | broad consumption tax with rebate or transfer offset, ideally prefilled or automatically delivered where eligibility is already visible |
| Landowner | person or firm holding scarce site value | land/location rent | land value tax or equivalent site-rent capture, with deferral or instalments where justified by liquidity |
| Extractive resource holder | person or firm | resource rent | severance / rent tax, depletion, restoration bonds, and cleanup obligations |
| Small firm or cooperative | legal instrument serving production | simple cash-flow or presumptive base with threshold and graduation | low-complexity compliance, modest burden, and genuine loss recognition rather than incumbent-only tax relief |
| Large corporation | legal instrument, not natural person | residual profit, rent, anti-shifting, beneficial ownership | minimum tax, residual profit taxation, anti-base erosion, and related-party anti-fragmentation |
| Dominant digital or AI platform | legal instrument controlling bottleneck | monopoly rent, destination, interoperability bottleneck, real group control | excess-profit, competition-linked, destination-based taxation, and group-level anti-fragmentation |
| Open or public-interest model project | tool or legal instrument | ordinary organisational taxes and real externalities | differentiated, lighter treatment than frontier commercial bottlenecks |
| Commercial frontier model lab | tool plus concentrated firm control | compute externality, profit, bottleneck rent, real controller map, collection leverage, downside symmetry, and explicit review architecture | tax controllers, owners, windfalls, and affiliated stack fragments as one economic group where appropriate; collect through strong intermediaries before weak downstream actors, avoid blind gross-throughput minima that ignore long pre-profit loss periods, and publish review triggers for capability drift, local burden, and pass-through surprises |
| High-risk AI deployer | human or firm actor | risk creation and externalized downside | licensing fees, ex ante bonding, insurance, incident reserve requirements, and no reliance on post-collapse tax grabs for repair |
| Catastrophic or non-compensable risk activity | human or firm actor creating irreparable danger | ex ante gating before price | approval / conditional approval / disapproval, caps, safety cases, decommissioning powers, and sidecar charges rather than pure tax-as-permit logic |
| Current LLM/model as such | not yet established as person and lacks standing bundle | none directly as a person | do not tax as a civic subject; tax accountable controllers instead |
| Autonomous artificial person (conditional, future) | would be rights-bearing if threshold is met and represented inside law | ability-to-pay and reciprocity after floor | tax like other persons only with standing, remedy, and non-owner representation; cannot be owned |

## Assessment-unit override

For human earnings and ordinary civic liability, the archive defaults to the **individual person** rather than a compulsory joint household. Household or family information may still matter for means-tested support, child recognition, caregiving, or anti-avoidance aggregation around wealth and beneficial ownership, but those are modifiers, not a license to disappear a weaker adult into another adult's tax identity. See note [`120`](../../archive/120-assessment-units-household-filing-care-and-dependency.md).

## Ownership-aggregation override

When paper boundaries are used to split one real controller into many taxpayers, the archive follows **beneficial ownership, related-party control, and coordinated benefit**, not just the invoice path. This matters for family wealth structures, shell chains, holding companies, threshold gaming in small-firm regimes, and especially AI stacks split across captive landlords, utilities, clouds, labs, and deployers. Use aggregation narrowly to stop evasion and fragmentation without erasing genuinely independent actors. See note [`121`](../../archive/121-beneficial-ownership-related-party-groups-and-anti-fragmentation.md).

## Notice-and-reliance override

For major reforms, the archive asks whether liability is **prospective by default**, whether ordinary households and small producers received meaningful notice, and whether any transition path protects real reliance without fossilizing incumbent privilege. This matters especially for land revaluation, inheritance reform, fossil or carbon-charge phase-ins, and new AI-facility or compute-linked charges. See note [`122`](../../archive/122-notice-retroactivity-transition-and-reliance.md).

## Enforcement-and-privacy override

Even a well-targeted tax can fail if it depends on unconstrained data access, black-box debt ranking, or disproportionate recovery. Prefer staged collection, hardship lanes, purpose-bounded data use, and reviewable controller-side collection before diffuse extraction through weak intermediaries. See note [`123`](../../archive/123-enforcement-proportionality-privacy-and-non-carceral-collection.md).

## Standing-and-voice override

A direct taxpayer should not be a pure black box or a rightless placeholder. The archive now asks whether the supposed subject can receive intelligible notice, access appeal, and act through a guardian, fiduciary, or other representative where needed, while also standing inside some channel of civic voice or proxy voice. If not, route liability to a controller, guardian, owner, or beneficiary instead. This is one more reason present LLMs remain outside the direct-person tax regime. See note [`124`](../../archive/124-standing-representation-voice-and-no-direct-tax-subjection-without-remedy.md).

## Anti-discrimination and status-proxy override

Apparently neutral tax rules can still fail if they disproportionately burden protected groups through filing units, documentation rules, geography, interface design, or automated targeting. Prefer direct measures of income, care load, harm, control, or benefit over sex, disability, nationality, ethnicity, family form, or neighborhood proxies, and require disaggregated review where unequal effects are plausible. If future artificial persons ever count as persons, they should not face a machine-species surcharge merely for substrate. See note [`125`](../../archive/125-status-proxies-anti-discrimination-and-substantive-equality.md).

## Failure-ordering and limited-liability override

When a tax base sits on a failure-prone or high-externality activity, the archive asks what happens at insolvency. Ordinary tax debts should not casually leap ahead of unpaid wages, estate administration, or serious cleanup claims, and high-risk sectors should usually carry bonds, reserve accounts, insurance, or controller guarantees **before** failure rather than asking the treasury to recover first from a ruined shell. See note [`126`](../../archive/126-insolvency-priority-workers-public-cleanup-and-limited-liability-boundaries.md).[S47][S48][S49]

## Downside-symmetry and loss-recognition override

When a proposal taxes volatile gains, risky investment, or long pre-profit activity, the archive asks how genuine downside is treated. Prefer carry-forwards with meaningful value preservation where relevant, bounded carry-backs or refundability where liquidity and neutrality matter, and anti-abuse rules aimed at artificial losses rather than at real downside itself. This matters for entrepreneurial income, capital gains and losses, cyclical industries, and frontier AI labs whose harmful externalities may be taxable even while rent has not yet appeared. See note [`127`](../../archive/127-downside-symmetry-loss-recognition-carrybacks-and-refundability.md).[S50][S51][S52]

## Precaution and non-compensable-risk override

When a proposal aims at catastrophic, irreversible, or otherwise non-compensable harms, the archive asks whether taxation is being asked to do the wrong job. Prefer ex ante approval, tiering, caps, decommissioning powers, bonding, or disapproval where ex post repair would be morally too late. Use taxes and fees as sidecars to risk governance, not as blanket permission slips for severe danger. See note [`128`](../../archive/128-precaution-catastrophic-risk-and-non-compensable-harm.md).[S15][S17][S53][S54]

## Real-value and indexation override

A proposal can still fail if it taxes nominal drift rather than real gain or real ability-to-pay. Prefer explicit indexation or transparent re-set rules for brackets, allowances, credits, loss relief, reserve minima, and AI or infrastructure thresholds where inflation would otherwise do hidden distributive work. If a design intentionally leaves values unindexed, that should be defended as an openly chosen real tightening rather than left to happen by stealth. See note [`129`](../../archive/129-real-value-inflation-indexation-and-nominal-drift.md).[S50][S55][S56][S57][S58]

## Review-trigger and reversibility override

A tax can be well targeted at enactment and still become unjust by drift, capture, or technological change. Where uncertainty, emergency conditions, local burdens, negotiated abatements, or rapidly changing AI capabilities are material, the archive asks what evidence would count as success or failure, who reviews it, what routine cadence applies, what trigger events force reconsideration, and how the rule can recalibrate, convert, sunset, or be repealed. Review is not a decorative appendix; it is part of how the tax stays morally answerable over time. See note [`130`](../../archive/130-review-triggers-ex-post-evaluation-and-policy-reversibility.md).[S15][S54][S59][S60][S61][S62][S63]

## Automaticity and take-up override

A floor-protecting tax-and-transfer design can still fail if the intended rebate, refund, or repair payment exists mainly on paper. Where the state already has enough information to know relief is probably due, the archive prefers automatic payment, prefilling, one-step confirmation, or similarly low-friction claiming with human, offline, translation, accessibility, and correction channels kept open. High-friction claiming is more tolerable for negotiated privileges than for protected minima. See note [`131`](../../archive/131-automaticity-take-up-claim-friction-and-prefilled-justice.md).[S64][S65][S66]

## Incidence override

Statutory assignment is not enough. A tax aimed at firms, landlords, or AI operators fails the archive's moral test if its predictable final burden lands mainly on people whose floor is meant to be protected. Always ask who actually pays after pass-through, wage adjustment, price increase, rent increase, or supplier squeeze. Then ask **when** the burden lands and whether payment timing matches real cash flow; a tax can be justified in base yet still be coercive if it arrives as a badly timed lump sum. Then ask **how the base is measured or valued**; a tax can be justified in theory yet degraded by stale assessments, fake precision, or opaque proxies. Then ask **how downside is recognized**; a tax can be well-aimed yet still morally degraded if gains are taxed now while genuine losses are only weakly or belatedly recognized. Then ask whether the proposal is taxing **nominal rather than real values**; a tax can be well-aimed yet still morally degraded if inflation erodes thresholds, relief, or bond amounts or turns phantom appreciation into taxable gain. Then ask where proceeds go: a tax can be well-aimed yet still morally degraded if revenues are routed away from floor protection, repair, or shared civic capacity. Then ask which jurisdiction has the strongest claim: a tax can be well-designed in the abstract yet assigned to the wrong place if it ignores immovable situs, destination use, market access, or local burden. Then ask what hidden abatements, credits, accelerated depreciation, cheap public inputs, or direct subsidies sit beside it: the moral object is the net fiscal stack, not the headline rate. Then ask whether the intended rebate, refund, or relief will actually arrive without maze-like claim friction or digital choke points: the moral object is take-up, not paper eligibility alone. Then ask what happens if the taxpayer collapses: the moral object is also the failure ordering, not just the going-concern assessment. Then ask whether any of these burdens or relief channels are using protected-status proxies or automated shortcuts that predictably exclude or disadvantage protected groups.[S3][S4][S9][S21][S23][S25][S27][S28][S29][S30][S31][S32][S33][S44][S45][S46][S47][S48][S49][S64][S65][S66]

## Context matrix

| Context | Preferred instruments | Cautions |
|---|---|---|
| Stable welfare state | land, inheritance, corporate rent, broad consumption with rebates, progressive income | avoid payroll-heavy states that spare wealth and rents |
| High-inequality / rent-heavy economy | land, inheritance, anti-monopoly, excess profits, luxury consumption | do not rely on symbolic millionaire surtaxes alone |
| Automation shock | automation dividend, windfall profit capture, transition insurance, training finance | avoid taxing displaced workers to fund adjustment |
| Climate emergency or water stress | carbon, methane, congestion, scarcity, resource depletion, adaptation finance, and where needed ex ante caps or permits | protect low-income households with dividends or public service offsets, and do not rely on taxes alone where harms are irreversible or highly concentrated |
| High inflation or rapid nominal drift | indexed or openly re-set brackets, allowances, credits, carry-forwards, reserve minima, and threshold rules | do not let bracket creep, phantom gains, or nominally decaying bonds substitute for visible policy choice |
| Weak-capacity state | simple withholding, land cadastre, excises, customs, resource rents, narrow presumptive regimes | complexity and fake precision can destroy compliance legitimacy |
| Highly digital transnational economy | destination-based profit rules, market-jurisdiction allocation, minimum tax, cloud/compute coordination, platform rent capture | unilateral digital taxes alone are brittle |
| Local or metropolitan government | land value, vacancy, curb access, congestion, data-centre siting and utility-burden charges | do not assign borderless profit bases to municipalities |

## Species rule

For now the archive recognises three analytically distinct regimes:

1. **Human persons** — direct tax subjects with rights, notice, appeal, and representative channels.
2. **Current machine systems** — not direct persons and not yet standing-capable subjects; route liability through controllers and effects.
3. **Possible future artificial persons** — only if rights-bearing agency is shown **and** standing, remedy, and representation can be supplied without owner domination.
