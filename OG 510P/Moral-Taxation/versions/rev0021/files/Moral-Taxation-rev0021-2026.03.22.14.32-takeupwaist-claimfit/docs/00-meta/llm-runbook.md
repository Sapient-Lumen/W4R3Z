## Editing posture

- Keep the central distinction stable: **current AI changes the tax base map more than the tax-subject map**.
- Add new taxes only after naming the moral target: rent, harm, reciprocity, congestion, inheritance, or emergency finance.
- Ask whether a proposed tax is really a disguised attempt to tax labor or compliance simplicity.
- Prefer operational additions that sharpen class, context, and species treatment over archive-bloating commentary.
- Check whether a proposal invites classification arbitrage across household, firm, contractor, and machine labels.
- Check final incidence, not just the remitting entity.
- Ask where proceeds go; taxes that route revenue badly can still fail constitutional fit.
- Ask when liability arrives relative to cash flow, risk creation, and asset liquidity; timing relief should not become permanent avoidance.
- Ask how the base is measured or valued; prefer observable bases, revaluation cadence, and disclosed proxies over fake precision.
- Ask whether the proposal is prospective by default, whether reliance is genuinely protected, and whether any grandfathering is finite rather than a fossilized privilege.
- Ask which jurisdiction has the strongest claim: situs, burdened locality, destination, market access, residence, or club coordination.
- Ask what abatements, credits, accelerated depreciation, preferential public-input pricing, or direct subsidies sit beside the headline tax; the moral object is the net fiscal stack.
- Ask whether the proposal silently changes the human assessment unit from person to household in ways that create second-earner traps, household domination, or care blindness.
- Ask whether any direct taxpayer actually has the standing bundle that makes direct subjection legitimate: notice, review, representation, and some channel of voice.
- Ask whether an apparently neutral rule is actually leaning on protected-status proxies through geography, documentation, household defaults, interface design, or automated scoring.
- Ask what happens when the taxpayer fails: ordinary tax priority should not quietly outrank wages, cleanup, or victim-repair claims where ex ante bonding, reserves, insurance, or controller guarantees were the morally earlier answer.
- Ask whether the proposal is pricing an ordinary harm or trying to tax a catastrophic / non-compensable risk that should first face approval, caps, bonding, decommissioning, or disapproval.
- Ask what evidence would count as success, failure, drift, or capture; what cadence and triggers reopen the rule; and how experimental or emergency taxes convert, tighten, loosen, or expire rather than fossilizing by inertia.
- Ask whether floor-protecting rebates, refunds, and credits will actually be received without heroic claiming effort; where the state already knows enough, prefer automaticity, prefill, or similarly low-friction delivery with real correction paths.
- Keep machine-readable surfaces tiny.
