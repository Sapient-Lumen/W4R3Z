# 152 — Public-input reciprocity is a bounded controller-side commons-repair claim, not a universal open-knowledge toll

## One-line thesis

When large controller groups build durable private rents on top of publicly financed research, open scientific infrastructure, public compute, public education pipelines, or other shared knowledge systems, a **bounded controller-side reciprocity contribution** can be morally justified. But that contribution is about **commons maintenance and fair return**, not state ownership of every downstream output and not a toll on ordinary open-knowledge use.[S10][S11][S22][S107][S108]

## Why this matters

The archive already allowed public-input reciprocity as one possible AI-trigger lane, but the trigger is easy to deform in two opposite directions: either everything learned from society is treated as privately ownable with no visible return, or every public contribution is treated as a license for universal micro-billing. This note closes that gap. Public inputs can justify reciprocity without turning open science into metered enclosure.[S10][S11][S22][S107][S108]

## Design rule

Keep reciprocity **controller-side, aggregate, and review-bound**. Use it only when large-scale private enclosure of publicly built inputs is visible and ordinary taxation plus existing public-return channels are not already doing enough.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Large commercial enclosure on top of public inputs

If a frontier lab, cloud gatekeeper, or dominant deployment platform commercializes capabilities that materially depend on publicly financed research, public-interest compute or datasets, or shared education and scientific infrastructure, a bounded reciprocity contribution can be justified — especially when the actor also controls access, captures concentrated rents, or benefits from other public support.[S10][S11][S22][S107][S108]

### 2. Ordinary learning and ordinary use stay out

Students, researchers, small firms, ordinary workers, ordinary users, and open or nonprofit projects should not face a special toll merely because they learn from public knowledge or use general-purpose tools shaped by it. The archive taxes concentrated enclosure and rent, not learning as such.[S11][S22][S107]

### 3. No per-output tracing fantasy

Do not pretend each output can be morally priced by tracing it back through atomized training fragments, public papers, or diffuse social contribution. The workable unit is the **controller-side beneficiary class or qualifying input bundle**, not impossible line-by-line ownership claims over thought itself.[S10][S16][S17][S107]

### 4. Credit real direct return

If a covered controller group already provides visible, auditable direct support for open research maintenance, public-interest compute, shared education, or comparable commons-repair institutions, that should count. Reciprocity is about underreturned extraction, not about collecting twice for the same public contribution.[S22][S71][S107][S108]

## Guardrails

- Do not convert open science into a universal toll road.
- Do not treat public contribution as proof that all downstream value is state-owned.
- Do not bill weak users, small researchers, or ordinary educational use like dominant commercial enclosure.
- Do not let reciprocity proceeds recycle back into hidden incumbent subsidy loops.

## Practical tests

1. Is the actor a large controller-side or bottleneck beneficiary rather than an ordinary user?
2. Can the relevant public-input bundle be named at the class level without fake micro-accounting?
3. Are ordinary taxes, subsidy discipline, and direct public-return channels visibly insufficient?
4. Would the proceeds rebuild real shared capacity rather than merely punish a salient sector?
