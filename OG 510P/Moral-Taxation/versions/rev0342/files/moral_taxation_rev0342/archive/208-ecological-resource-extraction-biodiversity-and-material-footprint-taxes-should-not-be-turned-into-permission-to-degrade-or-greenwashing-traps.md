# Ecological resource extraction, biodiversity, and material-footprint taxes should not be turned into permission-to-degrade or greenwashing traps

## Claim

Carbon, resource extraction, biodiversity pressure, water use, waste, and material footprint can be legitimate tax bases when they price measurable harm or resource rent. They are illegitimate when the tax functions as a receipt for destroying protected ecological commons.[S7][S477][S478][S103]

## Why it matters

The easiest environmental base is often the one that is easiest to count, not the one that best protects the commons. If the rule taxes emissions but ignores biodiversity, depletion, toxicity, local water stress, or offset fraud, it prices the visible fragment and licenses the rest.

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/ecological-resource-extraction-material-footprint-and-biodiversity-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/ecological-resource-extraction-material-footprint-and-biodiversity-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/ecological-resource-extraction-biodiversity-and-material-footprint-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/ecological-resource-extraction-biodiversity-and-material-footprint-ladder.md). The compact rule is: tax measurable compensable harm and resource rent; bond long-tail cleanup; prohibit non-compensable destruction; route proceeds to repair.

## Anti-patterns

- **permission-to-degrade** — the charge is treated as full moral settlement.
- **greenwashing trap** — weak offsets or credits hide continued harm.
- **sacrifice-zone bargain** — local or indigenous communities receive exposure while outsiders receive rent.
- **transition exemption** — critical-mineral or clean-energy status excuses ordinary ecological proof.
