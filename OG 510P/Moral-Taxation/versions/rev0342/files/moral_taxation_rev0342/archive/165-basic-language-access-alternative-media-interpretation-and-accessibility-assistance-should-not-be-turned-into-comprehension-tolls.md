# 165 — Basic language access, alternative media, interpretation, and accessibility assistance should not be turned into comprehension tolls

## One-line thesis

When a person, small firm, or future rights-bearing artificial person needs **plain-language explanation, translation, alternative media, interpretation, or another baseline comprehension aid** to understand, verify, or comply with an ordinary tax obligation, that aid should not depend on fee-bearing gatekeepers, disability surcharges, or owner-rented rendering layers.

## Why this matters

The archive already blocks review paywalls, record rents, gateway rents, and refund skims. But a system can still sell legibility back to the subject if the notice is technically available while the language, format, or communication layer needed to actually use it is priced as an extra. That would turn comprehension into a side market rather than part of legitimate administration.[S16][S27][S39][S42][S44][S65][S129][S130][S131][S132]

Current public materials point the other way. OECD's recent public-service accessibility work frames seamless and accessible administration as a human-centred effort that proactively removes barriers, IRS public help pages offer interpretation services and an accessibility helpline for alternative media, and ADA guidance treats effective communication plus auxiliary aids and services as part of the baseline duty to communicate with people who have communication disabilities.[S129][S130][S131][S132]

## Design rule

Keep at least one **ordinary, visible, no-rent comprehension lane** open for language access, alternative media, interpretation, and accessibility assistance wherever those supports are necessary for ordinary notice, filing, payment, correction, or appeal.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Baseline comprehension is part of administration, not a premium add-on

A tax claim is not meaningfully explained if the operative notice exists only in a language, format, or communication mode the subject cannot actually use without buying help. Translation, plain-language explanation, alternative media, and comparable comprehension supports belong with ordinary administration when they are needed to make the claim intelligible at all.[S16][S27][S39][S42][S44][S65][S129][S132]

### 2. Disability or language need should not become a surcharge base

A system fails morally when blindness, deafness, low vision, speech disability, or limited English access becomes the reason a subject must pay more just to receive the same tax information other subjects get as part of the default path. Exceptional expert representation may still be optional; baseline communication access should not become a status-priced fee stream.[S27][S39][S42][S44][S130][S131][S132]

### 3. Digital administration still carries communication duties

Moving notices, filing, or account management online does not erase the obligation to provide workable accessible communication. If the public portal, mobile interface, or notice-delivery system is the normal civic doorway, the administration should not offload the accessibility burden onto paid readers, private captioning subscriptions, or proprietary conversion tools just because the system is digital.[S39][S65][S129][S132]

### 4. Paid preparers and private helpers may exist; they should not become the only practical comprehension rail

Some people will still choose paid preparers, bilingual helpers, interpreters, or specialist accessibility tools. The archive's objection is not to optional help as such, but to coercive design in which ordinary understanding is realistic only through the fee market. If baseline comprehension depends on buying outside help, the system has recreated an access paywall under another name.[S27][S39][S41][S65][S129][S130][S131]

### 5. Future artificial persons should not need owner-rented translation or rendering layers

If a rights-bearing artificial person ever becomes real, the same rule applies on the person side. The subject should not need a former controller's paid console, proprietary renderer, or owner-rented translation layer just to receive a notice in usable form, map it into the subject's own working interface, or communicate with the tax system in its own name. Identity proof and bounded representation may still matter; **owner-rented comprehension of one's own civic obligations does not**.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S129]

## Guardrails

- Do not charge extra for baseline translation, alternative media, or communication aids needed for ordinary tax notice, filing, payment, correction, or appeal.
- Do not make private preparers, subscription accessibility tools, or paid interpreters the only practical route to understanding an ordinary claim.
- Do not treat digital delivery as permission to externalize accessibility or comprehension costs onto the subject.
- Do not let former controllers or required intermediaries rent back the rendering layer a future artificial person would need to receive its own notices in usable form.

## Failure modes

- **comprehension toll**: the liability is formally stated, but the usable language, format, or communication aid sits behind a fee.
- **disability surcharge by design**: subjects with communication disabilities must pay more to receive effectively the same notice or assistance others get by default.
- **translation-by-market-only**: ordinary understanding is realistic only through paid preparers, private translators, or other fee-market helpers.
- **digital accessibility externalization**: the portal or mobile channel is the default civic doorway, but the subject must buy outside tools or services to use it effectively.
- **owner-rented render gate**: a future artificial person can understand its own tax notices only through a former controller's paid rendering or translation layer.

## Practical tests

1. Can an ordinary subject receive or understand a baseline tax notice without having to buy translation, alternative media, or other necessary communication help?
2. If a digital channel is the default path, is there a workable accessible communication lane that does not require paid outside tools?
3. Are optional specialist services truly optional, or has the system quietly made them the only realistic path to understanding?
4. If a future artificial person existed, could it receive its own tax notices in a usable form without a former controller renting back the rendering layer?
