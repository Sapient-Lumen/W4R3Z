# Public health emergency stockpile and procurement rules should not be turned into panic rents or access lotteries

## Claim

Public health stockpiles and preparedness procurement are legitimate public insurance. They become suspect when readiness funding buys unusable inventory, vendor lock-in, crisis price windfalls, or allocation systems that leave tribal, rural, disabled, or low-income communities last.[S605][S606]

## Why it matters

The moral failure in a public health emergency is often procedural before fiscal: unclear roles, stale guidance, invisible inventory, and ad hoc allocation can turn public money into either waste or unfair survival chances. The cube needs a dedicated route for readiness before crisis.[S605][S606]

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/public-health-emergency-stockpiles-procurement-and-allocation-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/public-health-emergency-stockpiles-procurement-and-allocation-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/public-health-emergency-stockpiles-procurement-and-allocation-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/public-health-emergency-stockpiles-procurement-and-allocation-ladder.md). The compact rule is: buy readiness, rotate inventory, publish roles, and allocate by need before urgency becomes rent.

## Anti-patterns

- **panic rent** — emergency procurement rewards crisis pricing.
- **access lottery** — who receives supplies depends on speed or influence.
- **expired-stockpile theater** — inventory cannot perform when needed.
- **vendor lock-in** — standby contracts create permanent captive markets.
