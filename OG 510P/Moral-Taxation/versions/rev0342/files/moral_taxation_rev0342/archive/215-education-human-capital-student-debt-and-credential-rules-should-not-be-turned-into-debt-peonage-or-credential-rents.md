# Education, human-capital, student-debt, and credential rules should not be turned into debt peonage or credential rents

## Claim

Education finance should expand capability and public capacity. It becomes morally defective when grants, loans, credits, and tax preferences capitalize into high prices, low-value credentials, debt distress, or institutional surplus unrelated to student access and learning.[S503][S504]

## Why it matters

Students often bear the risk before the value is known. Schools, lenders, servicers, employers, and licensing systems may have better information or stronger power. A fair design routes support toward access and capability while testing price, completion, employment, repayment capacity, and institutional accountability.

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/education-human-capital-student-debt-and-credential-rent-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/education-human-capital-student-debt-and-credential-rent-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/education-finance-student-debt-and-credential-rent-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/education-finance-student-debt-and-credential-rent-ladder.md). The compact rule is: fund capability first; make repayment follow realized capacity; condition institutional subsidy on value; block credential rents and servicer traps.

## Anti-patterns

- **debt peonage** — human-capital hopes are financed by distress that cannot realistically be repaid.
- **credential rent** — tax or aid support raises price without capability, completion, or labor-market value.
- **aid-cut substitution** — public underfunding is replaced by private household debt.
- **servicer trap** — relief depends on paperwork channels that borrowers cannot reliably navigate.
