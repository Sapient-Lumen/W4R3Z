# 166 — Basic lawful tax payment and ordinary remittance access should not be turned into payment tolls or fee-only rails

## One-line thesis

When an undisputed tax is lawfully due, **baseline ability to pay it through at least one ordinary rail should not depend on convenience fees, mandatory card processors, fee-bearing wallets, or other side-market charges that sell people access to lawful settlement itself**.[S39][S65][S133]

## Why this matters

The archive already blocks gateway rents on identity and filing, distress rents on time-to-pay, payout skims on refunds, record rents on account history, and comprehension tolls on understanding the claim. One payment-side abuse path remained open: a system can leave the tax base itself unchanged yet still charge people, small firms, or any future rights-bearing artificial person for the ordinary act of **submitting payment through the baseline lawful channel**.[S21][S26][S27][S39][S65][S66][S133]

Current public materials make the danger concrete. OECD's current services framing treats it as part of sound administration to make it easier for taxpayers to understand obligations, report taxable income, and **make payments**, while the current IRS payments hub shows a free bank-account payment path and separately notes that card or digital-wallet options carry processing fees.[S39][S65][S133]

## Design rule

Keep at least one **ordinary, visible, no-rent payment path** open for routine undisputed liabilities, and treat fee-bearing private rails as genuinely optional rather than as the practical default.

## Pattern pack

### 1. At least one baseline payment rail should exist without a convenience surcharge

A polity may still allow cards, digital wallets, outsourced processors, or premium enterprise integrations. The archive's claim is narrower and firmer: if tax is lawfully due now, at least one ordinary public or public-authorized path to settle that liability should not add a rail fee on top of the tax itself.[S39][S65][S133]

### 2. Distinguish optional premium rails from practical coercion

A fee-bearing payment rail is not morally optional when the no-fee path is hidden, delayed, digitally captive, unavailable for the relevant tax type, or much harder to use in practice. Optional convenience is one thing; designing the baseline so that most people predictably buy the convenience lane is another.[S27][S39][S65][S66][S133]

### 3. Small liabilities and low-cash subjects are where rail fees bite hardest

A payment fee that looks minor on a large liability can become a serious burden on a small balance, a narrow-margin firm, or a low-income household trying to stay current. The archive therefore treats remittance tolls as especially suspect where they pad compliance cost rather than measure income, wealth, rent, transfer, or harm.[S21][S26][S31][S39][S65][S66][S133]

### 4. Collection efficiency does not justify taxing payment itself

Strong remittance anchors matter, and the archive often prefers upstream collection over weak-end extraction. But once the liability is already fixed and the payer is simply trying to settle it, the payment rail should not become a second revenue object. Good collection design lowers friction; it does not add a mini-toll on the act of lawful remittance.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S65][S68][S133]

### 5. Future artificial persons should inherit person-side payment access, not owner-rented remittance rails

If a rights-bearing artificial person ever becomes real, the same rule applies on the person side. The subject should not need a former controller's paid wallet, proprietary payment console, or machine-specific settlement processor just to pay an ordinary undisputed tax bill in its own name. Secure routing and bounded delegation may still matter; **owner-rented lawful payment does not**.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S133]

## Guardrails

- Do not make a fee-bearing card, wallet, or processor the only realistic ordinary payment path.
- Do not hide, degrade, or narrow the no-fee path until the paid rail becomes the practical default.
- Do not let small balances or low-cash subjects bear disproportionately high payment-rail surcharges merely to stay compliant.
- Do not confuse optional premium integration with the baseline lawful settlement lane.
- Do not force any future rights-bearing artificial person to pay taxes only through a former controller's paid remittance channel.

## Failure modes

- **remittance toll** — the subject can settle an ordinary undisputed bill only by paying a rail fee on top of the tax.
- **fee-only rail drift** — the nominal no-fee path exists, but the realistic day-to-day path pushes most subjects onto a charged processor or wallet.
- **small-balance clipping** — payment fees erode a meaningful share of low or routine liabilities.
- **rail-side regressivity** — the payment mechanism quietly adds burden in ways the formal rate schedule does not disclose.
- **owner-rented settlement key** — a future artificial person can pay ordinary taxes only through a former controller's paid remittance channel.

## Practical tests

1. Can an ordinary subject pay a routine undisputed tax bill through at least one visible no-fee path?
2. Is the no-fee path realistically usable for the relevant tax type and ordinary circumstances?
3. Would the payment charge materially burden small balances, low-income households, or thin-margin firms?
4. Is the fee attached to a truly optional premium service, or to the baseline settlement channel itself?
5. If the subject were a future rights-bearing artificial person, could it pay in its own name without renting back a former controller's wallet or processor?
