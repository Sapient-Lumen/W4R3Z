# Water rights, pricing, and irrigation-subsidy rules should not be turned into scarcity rents or household exclusion

## Claim

Water fiscal instruments must price scarcity, infrastructure, and pollution only after household access, ecological minimums, treaty/customary claims, and subsistence use are protected. Tradable rights and scarcity charges are not legitimate if they merely monetize exclusion.[S584][S585]

## Why it matters

Water is at once a human floor, an ecological commons, an agricultural input, a utility service, and an industrial location factor. A tax system that treats it only as infrastructure revenue can create shutoff coercion; a system that treats it only as private property can convert public scarcity into hoarded rents.[S584][S585]

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/water-rights-scarcity-pricing-and-irrigation-subsidy-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/water-rights-scarcity-pricing-and-irrigation-subsidy-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/water-scarcity-pricing-rights-and-household-irrigation-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/water-scarcity-pricing-rights-and-household-irrigation-ladder.md). The compact rule is: floors and flows before price signals; basin reality before paper rights; repair before revenue.

## Anti-patterns

- **scarcity-rent privatization** — scarcity value becomes a private windfall.
- **household exclusion** — water tariffs become an essential-access toll.
- **irrigation subsidy lock-in** — public subsidy preserves wasteful or harmful use.
- **paper conservation** — claimed savings do not improve basin conditions.
