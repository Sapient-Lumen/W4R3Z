# 193 — Transferee, nominee, alter-ego, successor, wrongful-levy, and third-party property collection should not be turned into dragnet rents or innocent-owner traps

## One-line thesis

Tax collection may follow property that really belongs to the taxpayer, a transfer that really defeated a public claim, or a third party that really became liable under a specific transferee, nominee, alter-ego, successor, fiduciary, or wrongful-transfer theory. It should not turn title proximity, family relation, vendor status, payroll contact, bank possession, purchaser confusion, successor branding, or solvency into a substitute tax base against the nearest outsider.[S21][S27][S34][S35][S39][S41][S42][S176][S182][S183][S184][S185][S330][S331][S332][S333][S334][S335][S336][S338]

## Why this matters

The previous trust-fund note closed one person-shifting path: payroll collapse cannot automatically make every officer, signer, bookkeeper, or payroll-console user a responsible person. A broader collection-shift problem remains. When the named taxpayer is insolvent, missing, reorganized, family-connected, asset-light, or hidden behind a shell, enforcement pressure can migrate to people and entities that are visible or solvent but not morally liable: spouses, relatives, buyers, landlords, banks, employers, vendors, customers, payroll processors, affiliates, successor businesses, nonprofit fiduciaries, or future hosts of a rights-bearing artificial person.[S21][S27][S31][S34][S35][S39][S41][S42][S176][S330][S331][S332][S336]

IRS materials already reveal the needed distinctions. Special-condition NFTLs distinguish nominee, alter-ego, transferee, and successor-in-interest theories, require theory-specific handling, and involve Counsel / Advisory review rather than a free-form label sweep. Transferee liability by suit can produce a judgment but does not automatically create a federal tax assessment or statutory tax lien against the transferee. Wrongful-levy procedures recognize that third-party property can be taken by mistake and must be returnable or appealable. The moral archive generalizes from those anchors: third-party collection needs a theory, a property or value limit, a notice-and-remedy path, and a burden allocation that does not make the outsider disprove the government's hidden collection theory from scratch.[S330][S331][S332][S333][S334][S335][S338]

## Archive rule

1. **Third-party proximity is not liability.** Being related to, employed by, paid by, banking for, buying from, selling to, or contracting with the taxpayer is a trigger for record development at most. It is not a tax base.[S21][S27][S31][S39][S41][S42][S336]
2. **The collection theory must be named before pressure hardens.** Nominee, alter ego, transferee, successor-in-interest, fiduciary, fraudulent-transfer, wrongful-levy, and ordinary third-party levy are different theories with different facts and remedies. A notice should not stack them as a blur.[S330][S331][S332][S334]
3. **Property scope and value caps matter.** A nominee or transferee theory usually follows identified property, transferred value, or a specific judgment / assessment route; it should not become whole-person collection unless the actual theory justifies that reach.[S330][S331][S332][S333]
4. **Counsel, Advisory, and appeal routing are substantive safeguards.** Special-condition liens and levies, successor-in-interest notices, and rejected wrongful-levy claims should preserve the review channel that matches the theory rather than forcing the outsider into paid rescue.[S185][S186][S331][S332][S334][S335][S338]
5. **Wrongful-levy return is not a favor.** If the state reaches property that belongs to someone other than the taxpayer, return of property or proceeds, interest where available, and CAP / suit routing are part of collection accuracy, not discretionary grace.[S184][S333][S335][S338]
6. **Hardship and protected floors still bind third-party collection.** A levy against wages, accounts, benefits, receivables, or business cash flow may be formally directed to a third party, but floor-stripping and immediate economic hardship cannot be laundered through that third party.[S27][S39][S41][S65][S66][S184][S337]
7. **Anti-fragmentation is not a dragnet license.** Shell tracing, beneficial-ownership grouping, and successor continuity defeat deliberate splitting; they do not authorize collection from every affiliate, purchaser, household member, service provider, or branded successor without theory-specific proof.[S34][S35][S41][S42][S330][S331][S332]
8. **Future artificial persons need person-side property boundaries.** If direct artificial personhood ever exists, a former owner, host, wallet provider, or controller cannot remain the default owner of the person-side account, property-return lane, or wrongful-levy remedy merely because it once held infrastructure access.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S176][S333]

## Pattern pack

### A. Nominee title is not enough

A nominee theory may be appropriate where another person holds legal title while the taxpayer retains the beneficial interest. But the state should identify the property, the taxpayer's retained interest, and the facts making title non-substantive before filing or levying. Title-holding should not become a penalty for relatives, trustees, agents, escrow holders, or purchasers who did not hold for the taxpayer.[S331][S332][S334]

### B. Alter ego is identity collapse, not mere relatedness

Alter-ego treatment is broader because it treats the taxpayer and third party as legally the same identity. That makes it dangerous. Shared address, common ownership, family control, overlapping managers, or brand continuity may justify investigation, but full alter-ego reach should require a record showing identity-level collapse, not just practical convenience.[S34][S35][S41][S42][S331][S332][S334]

### C. Transferee and successor routes need value, timing, and remedy limits

A transfer for less than adequate consideration, fraudulent conveyance, statutory transferee assessment, successor-in-interest theory, or suit to establish transferee liability can protect the public claim. The remedy should still track transferred value, limitations periods, judgment / assessment posture, and the difference between a lien on property and a personal judgment.[S330][S331]

### D. Third-party levy holders are not automatically debtors

Employers, banks, platforms, customers, vendors, retirement payers, and benefit payers may receive levies because they hold wages, accounts, receivables, or other property of the taxpayer. That service role should not morph into a moral conclusion that the holder owes the tax, especially where property ownership, priority, exemption, or hardship is disputed.[S184][S333][S336][S337]

### E. Wrongful levy is the outsider's correction lane

When a non-taxpayer says the seized property was not the taxpayer's, the system should treat the claim as a first-order correction route. The outsider should not have to pay a tax-resolution market merely to learn where to file, what proof is needed, whether proceeds can be returned, or how to appeal a denial.[S333][S335][S338]

### F. Collection-shift records belong with the party that made the shift

The government, lien unit, revenue officer, counsel reviewer, bank, employer, payroll provider, purchaser, title company, or other stronger record-holder usually knows the notice, transfer, name line, levy service, property description, payment path, and internal approval status. A concrete third-party counterstatement should shift the hidden-record burden toward those holders before adverse inference hardens.[S39][S41][S42][S331][S334][S335][S336][S338]

## Guardrails

- no third-party collection without a named theory and a theory-matching proof packet;
- no multiple-theory name-line blur that says nominee / alter ego / transferee / successor as a pressure tactic rather than a decision;
- no whole-person collection when the justified reach is only specific property, transferred value, or a bounded judgment;
- no treating a levy recipient, payroll contact, bank, employer, vendor, customer, family member, purchaser, title holder, successor brand, or host as liable merely because they are easier to reach than the taxpayer;
- no refusal to return wrongfully levied property or proceeds through an accessible administrative and appeal path;
- no floor-stripping or hardship laundering through a third-party levy;
- no owner-side veto over wrongful-levy, property-return, or release paths for a future rights-bearing artificial person.

## Failure modes

- **third-party dragnet rent** — collection migrates to solvent outsiders because the original taxpayer is hard to collect from.
- **innocent-owner trap** — property titled to or possessed by a non-taxpayer is treated as the taxpayer's without a property-specific theory and remedy.
- **label-stack lien** — a notice uses nominee, alter ego, transferee, and successor language as interchangeable pressure rather than one supported condition.
- **successor-brand hostage** — a buyer, reorganized entity, or new operator is collected from because the name, assets, or customers look continuous, without value / continuity proof.
- **wrongful-levy toll** — the only practical way to recover seized third-party property is paid help, premium records, or litigation before administrative correction can matter.
- **host-property capture** — a future artificial person's property or account remains reachable only through a former owner / controller host, making release or return owner-rented.

## Practical tests

1. What exact theory authorizes collection from this third party or property: nominee, alter ego, transferee, successor, fiduciary, fraudulent transfer, ordinary levy holder, or wrongful-levy return?
2. Is the reach limited to the specific property, transferred value, judgment, assessment, or period that the theory supports?
3. Who holds the decisive records: tax administration, counsel / advisory file, lien office, levy recipient, title holder, bank, employer, platform, purchaser, or the outsider?
4. Does the affected third party have a visible no-rent route to contest, appeal, recover property or proceeds, and correct public records?
5. Would the same reach still be justified if the third party were poor, hard to find, or politically weak rather than solvent and convenient?
6. If the property belonged to a future rights-bearing artificial person, could that person obtain return, release, or wrongful-levy review without a former owner or host?
