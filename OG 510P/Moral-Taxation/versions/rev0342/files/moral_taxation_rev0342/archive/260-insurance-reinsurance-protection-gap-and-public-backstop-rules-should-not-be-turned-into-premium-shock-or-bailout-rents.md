# Insurance reinsurance protection gap and public backstop rules should not be turned into premium shock or bailout rents

## Claim

Insurance is legitimate risk pooling, not a license to privatize underwriting gains and socialize correlated loss after the fact. Public backstops may protect floors where private markets fail, but only with risk reduction, transparent assessments, policyholder affordability checks, and recovery from preventable retained upside.[S493][S630][S631]

## Why it matters

Classification seams are where public systems often leak moral clarity: an insurance surcharge looks actuarial, a wager looks like finance, a telecom contribution looks like a small bill line, a digital-ad tax looks like rent capture, a portable benefit looks like worker protection, an environmental payment looks like mitigation, or a release checklist looks like bureaucracy. The archive should ask who controls the burden, who bears it, and what floor or public claim is at stake before accepting the label.[S493][S630][S631]

## Anti-patterns

- **bailout reinsurer** — public guarantee absorbs private underwriting losses without public upside, mitigation duties, or consumer protection.
- **premium shock floor breach** — actuarially plausible pricing pushes protected households, renters, or small firms below housing or continuity floors.
- **residual-market hidden tax** — pool deficits are recovered through opaque assessments on policyholders rather than visible floor-tested finance.
- **risk-blind subsidy** — aid rewards new avoidable exposure rather than legacy hardship, risk reduction, or relocation.
- **retreat ransom** — coverage withdrawal or premium shock forces exit without buyout, appeal, or transition support.
