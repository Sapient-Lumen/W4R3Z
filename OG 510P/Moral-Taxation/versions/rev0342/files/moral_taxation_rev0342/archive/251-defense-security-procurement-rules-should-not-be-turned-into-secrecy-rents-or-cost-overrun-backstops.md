# Defense security procurement rules should not be turned into secrecy rents or cost-overrun backstops

## Claim

Defense procurement can justify speed, secrecy, financing, and industrial-base support. It becomes morally unstable when those tools shield cost growth, weak testing, supply-chain opacity, incumbent lock-in, or private upside from public-risk finance.[S610][S611][S612][S613]

## Why it matters

Security is among the strongest public purposes, which is exactly why it needs stricter fiscal gates. The public cannot evaluate every classified detail, but the archive can still require cleared oversight, cost discipline, recoverable financing, supply-chain visibility, and public-upside terms.[S610][S611][S612][S613]

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/defense-security-procurement-secrecy-and-industrial-base-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/defense-security-procurement-secrecy-and-industrial-base-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/defense-security-procurement-secrecy-and-industrial-base-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/defense-security-procurement-secrecy-and-industrial-base-ladder.md). The compact rule is: fund real security capacity while denying secrecy rents and automatic public downside absorption.

## Anti-patterns

- **secrecy rent** — classification masks ordinary subsidy or weak performance.
- **cost-overrun backstop** — taxpayers absorb open-ended development failure.
- **incumbent lock-in** — urgency becomes permanent sole-source dependence.
- **security theater** — threat language hides capture or fiscal opacity.
