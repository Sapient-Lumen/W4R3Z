# Critical mineral tax and subsidy rules should not be turned into extraction rents or community sacrifice zones

## Claim

Critical-mineral policy can fund resilience, processing, stockpiles, recycling, and public-risk repair. It fails when criticality becomes a slogan for private extraction rent, environmental shortfall, or local sacrifice.[S599][S600][S601]

## Why it matters

Mineral security is real, but it is not self-validating. Supply concentration and transition demand can justify public action only after the archive tests whether the same goal can be met through recycling, substitution, stockpiles, processing competition, reclamation, and community repair.[S599][S600][S601]

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/critical-minerals-extraction-processing-stockpiles-and-recycling-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/critical-minerals-extraction-processing-stockpiles-and-recycling-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/critical-minerals-extraction-processing-stockpile-and-recycling-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/critical-minerals-extraction-processing-stockpile-and-recycling-ladder.md). The compact rule is: treat criticality as a routing signal, not a moral blank check for extraction.

## Anti-patterns

- **sacrifice-zone subsidy** — security need erases local water, labor, or Indigenous floors.
- **stockpile bailout** — public procurement supports price rather than resilience.
- **processing bottleneck rent** — midstream firms capture capacity policy.
- **frontier extraction overreach** — irreversible uncertainty is priced as if it were ordinary harm.
