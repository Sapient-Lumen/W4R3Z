# Model updates, checkpoint migrations, renames, affiliate transfers, and asset sales do not by themselves reset controller-side tax duties

When an AI deployment or model family is updated, renamed, migrated, transferred between affiliates, or sold with substantial continuity of governance and economic benefit, do **not** treat the event as a fresh moral subject with a clean fiscal slate. The archive's stable rule is to follow controller continuity, continuation power, beneficiary continuity, and inherited deployment reality rather than version labels, model names, or transaction theater.[S10][S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S41][S83]

## Why this note exists

Copies and forks were already covered, and the archive already had a tie-break for stacks with several partly controller-like actors. What remained too implicit was continuity across time. Version updates, checkpoint migrations, rebrands, affiliate transfers, or asset sales can otherwise be used to wipe threshold history, reserve duties, review triggers, or incident-linked obligations without any real break in who governs or benefits.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S41][S83]

## Continuity tests

Treat the new label, release, or entity as successor-continuous when most of these facts still hold:

1. **deployment continuity** — the same standing product, workflow, or service objective remains live,
2. **governance continuity** — the same actor or aligned group can still decide updates, rollback, access, safety posture, or continuation,
3. **economic continuity** — the same beneficiary group, revenue stream, or residual upside survives the paper move,
4. **operational continuity** — the same weights, checkpoints, update channel, model hub, customer base, or materially continuous replacement remains in service,
5. **duty continuity** — unresolved incidents, reserve duties, bonding triggers, review clocks, or reporting lanes remain morally connected to the continuing stack.[S10][S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S41][S83]

If most of those facts persist, controller-side tax duties should persist as well unless a statute deliberately and visibly chooses otherwise.

## Hard defaults

1. **A version number is not tax amnesty.** A renamed checkpoint or major model release can change measured activity without wiping controller continuity.[S16][S17][S20][S21]
2. **An affiliate transfer is not independence.** Moving a model, service, or deployment rail across shells inside the same real control group does not create a fresh taxpayer by itself.[S21][S34][S35][S83]
3. **A genuine sale can reroute future liability without erasing accrued duties.** If governance truly changes hands, future controller-side duties can re-route, but already attached reserves, reporting duties, and incident-linked obligations should be settled visibly or follow the successor chain rather than disappearing into paper closure.[S20][S21][S27][S41][S83]
4. **Collection may move before subjecthood does.** The best remittance anchor after a transfer may be the surviving registry, controller group, or billing interface, but remittance convenience still does not settle who the primary controller is.[S21][S27][S41][S83]

## Failure modes

- **checkpoint laundering** — a new version label is used as though it erased continuity of users, revenue, and governance.
- **threshold reset theater** — a controller rebrands or migrates a stack so review clocks, reserve minima, or levy thresholds start over from zero.
- **affiliate hop avoidance** — rights, liabilities, or reporting duties are shuffled across controlled entities so no node appears continuous enough to owe anything.
- **orphaned risk** — known incidents, outstanding claims, or reserve duties are left behind in an emptied vehicle while the economically continuous deployment keeps operating elsewhere.[S16][S17][S21][S34][S35][S41][S83]

## Practical tests

1. Who can still update, continue, halt, or materially redesign the deployment?
2. Are substantially the same users, customers, or beneficiaries still being served?
3. Does the same aligned group still capture the residual upside or absorb the strategic downside?
4. Did the transfer actually break governance, or did it mostly change branding, invoicing, or entity location?
5. Are unresolved reserve, reporting, or incident duties being settled visibly, or simply left behind?

If the answers mostly point to continuity, keep the controller-side fiscal chain continuous too.
