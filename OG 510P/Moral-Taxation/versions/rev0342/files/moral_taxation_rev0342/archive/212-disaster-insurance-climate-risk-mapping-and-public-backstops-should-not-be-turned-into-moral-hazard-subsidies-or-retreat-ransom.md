# Disaster insurance, climate-risk mapping, and public backstops should not be turned into moral-hazard subsidies or retreat ransom

## Claim

Disaster insurance and public backstops should make climate and catastrophe risk honest without abandoning legacy households. Underpricing hides risk and builds public debt; pure actuarial pricing can expel people from shelter without repair.[S491][S492][S493]

## Why it matters

Flood, fire, wind, and climate-related losses create a fiscal triangle among households, insurers, lenders, local tax bases, and the state. If public insurance simply suppresses premiums, it encourages repeated loss. If it withdraws support overnight, it turns adaptation into ransom.

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/disaster-insurance-climate-risk-and-public-backstop-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/disaster-insurance-climate-risk-and-public-backstop-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/disaster-insurance-climate-risk-public-backstop-and-relocation-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/disaster-insurance-climate-risk-public-backstop-and-relocation-ladder.md). The compact rule is: disclose risk, protect legacy floors, condition subsidy on mitigation, and move repeated-loss cases toward buyout, relocation, or no-build rules.

## Anti-patterns

- **moral-hazard subsidy** — risk is underpriced and rebuilding repeats.
- **retreat ransom** — households are priced out without relocation or adaptation support.
- **map shock** — new risk data creates sudden liability without transition.
- **insurer-exit dumping** — private withdrawal leaves taxpayers as unpriced reinsurer.
