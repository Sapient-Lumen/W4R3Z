# Ocean fisheries subsidies and blue-food rules should not be turned into overfishing rents or coastal-floor harm

## Claim

Fishery and blue-food fiscal policy is legitimate when it rebuilds stocks, funds monitoring, repairs marine commons, and protects genuine coastal food or livelihood floors. It becomes suspect when it finances capacity, quota capture, or incumbent rents against stock reality.[S595][S596][S597][S598]

## Why it matters

Marine resources are both food systems and commons. A tax, subsidy, quota, or access fee that ignores stock status, IUU risk, coastal floors, and quota concentration can look like food security while worsening depletion or distributing scarcity rents upward.[S595][S596][S597][S598]

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/ocean-fisheries-subsidies-marine-commons-and-blue-food-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/ocean-fisheries-subsidies-marine-commons-and-blue-food-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/ocean-fisheries-subsidies-marine-commons-and-blue-food-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/ocean-fisheries-subsidies-marine-commons-and-blue-food-ladder.md). The compact rule is: rebuild the stock, protect the coastal floor, and charge scarce access before subsidizing capacity.

## Anti-patterns

- **overfishing rent** — public support expands effort against depleted stocks.
- **quota capture** — scarce access becomes private wealth without public repair.
- **coastal-floor harm** — rules raise food or livelihood insecurity in the name of conservation.
- **blue-washing** — aquatic-food language hides habitat, feed, or disease externalities.
