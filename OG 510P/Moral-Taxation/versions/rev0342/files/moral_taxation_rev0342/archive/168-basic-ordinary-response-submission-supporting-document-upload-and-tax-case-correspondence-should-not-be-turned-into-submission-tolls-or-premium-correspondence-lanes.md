# 168 — Basic ordinary response submission, supporting-document upload, and tax-case correspondence should not be turned into submission tolls or premium correspondence lanes

## One-line thesis

When a person, small firm, or future rights-bearing artificial person needs to **answer a notice, send an ordinary correction packet, upload supporting documents, or correspond about an ordinary tax case**, at least one baseline response lane should stay no-rent and non-premium rather than depending on per-message charges, paid upload brokers, mandatory intermediaries, or owner-rented correspondence keys.[S19][S20][S21][S26][S27][S39][S42][S43][S44][S65][S138][S139][S140]

## Why this matters

The archive already blocks gateway rents on filing identity, review paywalls on correction, record rents on account history, comprehension tolls on understanding the claim, payment tolls on lawful remittance, and certificate rents on proving ordinary compliance. But one ordinary-life abuse path still remained open: **selling the subject the act of answering the tax authority back as a correspondence product**.[S21][S26][S27][S39][S42][S44][S65][S138][S139][S140]

That matters because a tax system often moves from static liability to live interaction precisely when the subject is under stress: an audit notice arrives, a mismatch has to be clarified, supporting documents are requested, or a correction needs to be sent before a deadline. If the only practical answer path runs through paid portals, premium upload services, mandatory intermediaries, or owner-controlled messaging, administration starts charging rent on procedural obedience itself.[S21][S26][S27][S39][S42][S44][S65][S117][S118][S138][S139][S140]

Current public materials show a cleaner baseline. OECD's current services framing treats easy-to-use taxpayer services as part of supporting compliance. The IRS Document Upload Tool is presented as a secure, easy, and fast way to send information and receive confirmation; IRS Secure Messaging is voluntary and can revert to paper; and Ireland's MyEnquiries service lets users send, receive, and track correspondence with Revenue.[S65][S138][S139][S140]

## Design rule

Keep at least one **ordinary, visible, no-rent response lane** open for answering notices, sending routine correction packets, uploading ordinary supporting documents, and corresponding about live tax cases, and treat unusual forensic production, rush handling, or litigation-grade processing as separate from the baseline answer path.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Distinguish baseline answering from exceptional production

A polity may still impose bounded cost recovery for unusual archival retrieval, bespoke media conversion, extraordinary duplication, or court-grade production. The archive's claim is narrower and firmer: **the ordinary act of answering a live tax notice or document request should not itself become the revenue line**.[S27][S39][S42][S53][S65][S138][S139][S140]

### 2. The answer lane belongs with notice, correction, and record access

If the administration can open a case, send a notice, and hold the record, it should also keep at least one ordinary path for the subject to answer back without paying a special channel fee. A paid upload broker or premium correspondence lane recreates a review paywall one procedural step earlier.[S27][S39][S42][S44][S65][S138][S139][S140]

### 3. Digital convenience is good; captive correspondence is not

Secure digital upload, tracked correspondence, and structured message threads can improve administration. The archive's objection is not to digital casework; it is to making digital casework extractive or exclusive. Optional premium help may exist, but the baseline answer path should not depend on a fee-bearing API, paid preparer subscription, or closed owner-controlled inbox.[S39][S41][S42][S44][S65][S68][S138][S139][S140]

### 4. Small firms and ordinary people are where answer-rents bite hardest

A large firm may absorb repeated upload fees or specialist case portals. A worker, migrant household, or small firm facing a deadline may not. Submission tolls are therefore especially suspect when they attach to routine mismatch resolution, document response, or correction rather than to unusual litigation burdens.[S21][S26][S27][S31][S39][S42][S65][S138][S139][S140]

### 5. Future artificial persons should inherit person-side correspondence access, not owner-rented answer lanes

If a rights-bearing artificial person ever becomes real, the same rule applies on the person side. The subject should not need a former controller's paid inbox, proprietary upload console, or owner-rented case key just to answer a notice, send supporting documents, or correspond about its own tax case in its own name. Secure authorization and bounded representation may still matter; **owner-rented answering does not**.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S138][S139][S140]

## Guardrails

- Do not attach ordinary notice responses, document uploads, or case correspondence to per-message, per-upload, or subscription pricing.
- Do not make a paid intermediary or broker the only realistic way to answer a routine notice or document request on time.
- Do not collapse baseline reply channels into premium digital products while leaving the free lane too slow, obscure, or degraded to matter.
- Do not require the subject to buy back the right to send routine supporting materials the administration itself requested.
- Do not let any future rights-bearing artificial person answer ordinary tax correspondence only through a former controller's paid channel.

## Failure modes

- **submission toll** — the subject must pay to send an ordinary notice response, correction packet, or supporting document.
- **premium correspondence drift** — a nominally free route exists, but deadlines, friction, or degraded handling make the paid channel the only practical answer path.
- **brokered obedience** — routine compliance communication effectively requires a preparer, software vendor, or upload middleman.
- **reply-side lock-in** — the same actor that opened the case controls the only realistic path for the subject to answer it.
- **owner-rented answer key** — a future artificial person can respond to its own tax case only through a former controller's paid inbox, upload console, or case token.

## Practical tests

1. Can an ordinary subject answer a routine notice, send a correction packet, or upload ordinary supporting documents without paying a special fee?
2. Is the no-rent reply lane realistically usable under common deadlines and ordinary case pressure?
3. Are any charges limited to unusual production, rush handling, or litigation-grade processing rather than to baseline case response?
4. Can the subject correspond securely without depending on a paid intermediary, proprietary broker, or premium portal subscription?
5. If the subject were a future rights-bearing artificial person, could it answer its own tax case without renting back a former controller's inbox, upload console, or case key?
