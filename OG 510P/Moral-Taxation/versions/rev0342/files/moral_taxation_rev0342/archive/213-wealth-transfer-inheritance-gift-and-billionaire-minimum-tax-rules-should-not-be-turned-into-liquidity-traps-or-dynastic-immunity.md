# Wealth-transfer, inheritance, gift, and billionaire-minimum-tax rules should not be turned into liquidity traps or dynastic immunity

## Claim

Large inheritances, gifts, unrealized gains at death, trust transfers, and ultra-high-net-worth low effective tax rates are priority bases because they reflect unearned transfer and concentrated power. But modest inheritances and illiquid family security require thresholds and liquidity relief.[S8][S73][S496]

## Why it matters

A wealth-transfer system can fail in two opposite ways: it can force sale of a family home, farm, or small business, or it can let dynastic wealth disappear through trusts, valuation discounts, charitable wrappers, offshore structures, and basis resets.

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/wealth-transfer-dynastic-concentration-and-liquidity-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/wealth-transfer-dynastic-concentration-and-liquidity-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/wealth-transfer-dynastic-concentration-and-liquidity-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/wealth-transfer-dynastic-concentration-and-liquidity-ladder.md). The compact rule is: protect modest and vulnerable transfers; aggregate large transfers; tax accrued gains or carry basis; use top-end backstops; provide liquidity relief without dynastic immunity.

## Anti-patterns

- **liquidity trap** — genuine illiquidity becomes forced-sale design.
- **dynastic immunity** — fragmentation, trusts, and unrealized gains erase the claim.
- **charity laundering** — donor-controlled vehicles replace public transfer.
- **exit escape** — mobility defeats accrued public claims without coordination.
