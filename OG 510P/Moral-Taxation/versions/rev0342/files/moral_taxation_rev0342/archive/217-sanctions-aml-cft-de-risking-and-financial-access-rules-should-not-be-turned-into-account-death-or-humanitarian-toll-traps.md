# Sanctions, AML/CFT, de-risking, and financial-access rules should not be turned into account death or humanitarian toll traps

## Claim

Sanctions and AML/CFT duties should target specific risk and culpable conduct. They should not create whole-category exclusion, name-match punishment, blocked humanitarian corridors, or account death without notice, correction, and proportionate alternatives.[S507][S508][S510][S511]

## Why it matters

Financial access is part of tax capacity: people need accounts, payment rails, remittance channels, refunds, payroll, and records to comply and survive. Overbroad risk avoidance shifts public enforcement cost onto migrants, charities, low-balance customers, and humanitarian beneficiaries.

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/sanctions-aml-cft-de-risking-and-financial-access-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/sanctions-aml-cft-de-risking-and-financial-access-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/sanctions-de-risking-financial-access-and-humanitarian-channel-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/sanctions-de-risking-financial-access-and-humanitarian-channel-ladder.md). The compact rule is: keep sanctions targeted, AML risk-based, and access protected; use correction, tiered KYC, licenses, and safe harbors before exclusion hardens.

## Anti-patterns

- **account death** — ordinary financial life disappears without actionable reason or review.
- **humanitarian toll** — aid corridors pay avoidable compliance rents to reach protected beneficiaries.
- **category de-risking** — whole groups or corridors are exited instead of risk-managed.
- **name-match liability** — weak identity signals become tax or payment consequences without correction.
