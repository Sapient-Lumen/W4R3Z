# Telecom universal service broadband affordability and surcharge rules should not be turned into line item rents or low income cross subsidies

## Claim

Universal connectivity is a floor claim, but a shrinking or badly chosen contribution base can turn a public-service program into a high, opaque, line-item burden on the same households it should protect. Funding should be broad, visible, non-marked-up, and audited for beneficiary, provider, and consumer incidence.[S632][S633][S645]

## Why it matters

Classification seams are where public systems often leak moral clarity: an insurance surcharge looks actuarial, a wager looks like finance, a telecom contribution looks like a small bill line, a digital-ad tax looks like rent capture, a portable benefit looks like worker protection, an environmental payment looks like mitigation, or a release checklist looks like bureaucracy. The archive should ask who controls the burden, who bears it, and what floor or public claim is at stake before accepting the label.[S632][S633][S645]

## Anti-patterns

- **line item rent** — a carrier turns a contribution obligation into a marked-up or misleading surcharge.
- **low income cross subsidy** — households needing connectivity support fund the support through unavoidable bills.
- **legacy base spiral** — a shrinking base raises the factor, which encourages more base erosion and bill shock.
- **provider support without service** — support flows without enforceable price, buildout, quality, or clawback conditions.
- **hidden connectivity tax** — a compulsory bill charge functions like tax without budget visibility or floor review.
