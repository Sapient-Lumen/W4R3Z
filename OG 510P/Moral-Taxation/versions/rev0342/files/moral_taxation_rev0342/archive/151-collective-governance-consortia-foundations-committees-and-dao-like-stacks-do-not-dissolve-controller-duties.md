# Collective governance: consortia, foundations, committees, and DAO-like stacks do not dissolve controller duties

## One-line thesis

When a live AI stack is materially governed by a consortium, foundation, committee, protocol council, or DAO-like coalition, the absence of a single dominant owner does **not** create a controller vacuum: tax law should follow the real controller group, governing coalition, or jointly accountable command structure rather than dropping into nobody-liability or fake machine personhood.[S20][S21][S34][S35][S41][S42][S43][S44][S83]

## Why this matters

After the archive blocks easy controller laundering through users, resellers, infrastructure, channels, and branding, the next evasion move is to say that the stack is now governed by a loose foundation, standards body, multisig, steering committee, or consortium and therefore has no real controller at all. That would let governance become morally decisive when convenient and magically disappear when liability arrives.[S20][S21][S34][S35][S41][S83]

## Design rule

**Where real governance is shared, tax the real controller group or governing coalition; do not let split command dissolve controller-side duties into nobody.** Use joint-and-several remittance, designated filing, or rebuttable lead-controller rules where needed for collection, but keep the substantive burden attached to the coalition that can actually continue, pause, update, authorize, or economically benefit from the stack.[S21][S34][S35][S41][S42][S83]

## Markers of a real controller group

Treat a collective arrangement as a real controller group when several actors together can do most of the following:

- set or amend deployment purpose,
- approve releases, rollbacks, or checkpoints,
- control treasury, reserve, or bond posting,
- gate model access, scaling, or update channels,
- appoint operators or remove them,
- define safety posture, incident escalation, or stop / continue rules,
- or capture residual upside through treasury, governance rights, fees, or affiliated beneficiary positions.[S20][S21][S34][S35][S41][S83]

No single seat must dominate if the coalition as a whole governs the live stack.

## What this does **not** mean

- Mere community discussion, standards advice, or issue-commenting is not enough.
- A passive foundation shell is not enough if real command still sits with one vendor or buyer.
- Decentralized branding is not enough if one actor can still unilaterally update, pause, or monetize the stack.
- Shared governance does not multiply civic taxpayers the way copy count does not; it identifies the accountable controller **group** for the governed layer.[S20][S21][S34][S35][S41][S83]

## Collection and standing

For administration, the archive prefers the lightest rule that preserves accountability: a designated filer, treasury remitter, permit holder, registry contact, or lead controller may collect first, but notice, review, and anti-fragmentation rules should still reach the governing coalition when the lead remitter is only one face of a jointly controlled system.[S21][S41][S42][S43][S44][S83]

## Failure mode to avoid

The anti-pattern is **distributed-governance theater**: using committees, multisigs, nonprofit wrappers, or cross-licenses to enjoy the advantages of control while claiming that no one is enough of a controller to owe controller-side tax, reserve, or reporting duties.[S20][S21][S34][S35][S41][S83]
