# 189 — Basis record, broker, digital-asset, transfer, and gross-proceeds reporting should not be turned into basis rents or gross-proceeds traps

## One-line thesis

Proceeds reporting is not gain measurement. Broker, custodian, transfer-agent, real-estate, barter, platform, wallet, and digital-asset reports may trigger reconciliation, but missing, noncovered, transferred, stale, or wrong basis may not turn sale proceeds into presumed taxable gain or force the taxpayer into paid basis reconstruction markets. Basis proof should remain no-rent, correction-capable, document-plural, and burden-shifted toward the actor that actually held the account, transfer statement, corporate-action record, wallet ledger, closing file, or reporting feed.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S42][S65][S66][S68][S154][S155][S158][S160][S172][S291][S292][S293][S294][S295][S296][S297][S298][S299][S300][S301][S302]

## Why this matters

The archive already blocks reporter-hostage traps, substitute-return default bases, automated adjustment finality, audit inspection rents, self-correction tolls, preparer captivity, and low-income credit proof markets. A still-distinct problem appears whenever the public record sees the sale price but not the taxpayer's investment in the property. If administration treats the visible gross proceeds as the base, the tax silently changes from a tax on gain into a tax on turnover, liquidation, migration, or record loss.[S21][S27][S41][S42][S154][S155][S158][S172][S182][S186][S291][S292][S294][S295]

IRS materials make the distinction operational. Basis is the taxpayer's investment measure and is used to compute gain or loss; Form 8949 exists to reconcile amounts reported to the taxpayer and the IRS on proceeds forms with the return; Form 1099-B and Form 1099-DA distinguish transactions where basis is reported from those where basis is absent, noncovered, or adjustable; and Form 1099-S can require reporting a home or real-estate sale even where gain may be partly or wholly excluded or reduced by basis.[S291][S292][S293][S294][S295][S296][S297][S298][S299][S301][S302]

The moral point is not that every asserted basis must be accepted. Basis can be inflated, wash-sale adjusted, inherited, gifted, split, lost, or entangled with corporate actions. The rule is narrower: where the state or a third-party reporter can see proceeds more easily than basis, that asymmetry should trigger a bounded proof lane, not zero-basis default, whole-file suspicion, or paid reconstruction as the price of not being overtaxed.[S27][S39][S41][S42][S65][S66][S68][S154][S155][S158][S160][S291][S292][S295][S297][S299]

## Archive rule

1. **Gross proceeds are an alert, not gain.** A proceeds form, wallet sale, closing statement, exchange report, or platform feed may identify a taxable event, but gain still requires basis, holding period, adjustment, exclusion, and character analysis. Treating proceeds as final gain is a measurement failure.[S21][S27][S291][S292][S293][S294][S295][S296][S301][S302]
2. **Wrong or missing reported basis gets a public adjustment lane.** Where a 1099-B, 1099-DA, substitute statement, or 1099-S-adjacent record is missing basis or reports the wrong basis, the taxpayer should have a clear no-rent way to report the reported amount, the corrected amount, the adjustment code or explanation, and supporting records without buying software or a private basis service.[S65][S66][S154][S155][S294][S295][S296][S299][S300]
3. **Covered and noncovered status is not moral finality.** Covered-security rules, transfer statements, and digital-asset broker rules can improve reporting, but noncovered status, transferred property, wallet migration, inherited or gifted property, or pre-reporting acquisition should not collapse to zero basis merely because the current broker does not have the record.[S27][S68][S291][S292][S293][S295][S297][S298][S299][S300]
4. **Upstream record burden follows custody and reporting control.** Brokers, custodians, transfer agents, issuers, exchanges, closing agents, and platforms that hold acquisition, transfer, corporate-action, split, reinvestment, fee, lot-selection, or disposition records should carry the first substantial record-correction duty once the taxpayer identifies the disputed transaction and period.[S27][S39][S41][S42][S154][S155][S158][S160][S295][S296][S297][S299][S302]
5. **Digital-asset transition needs anti-gross-up protection.** New broker reporting can improve visibility, but wallet self-custody, exchange migration, stablecoin / NFT optional methods, noncovered assets, and incomplete transfer data can separate proceeds from basis. The reporting transition should not create a class of taxpayers taxed on gross digital-asset outflows because no one reporter sees the full chain.[S27][S68][S294][S295][S298][S299][S300]
6. **Real-estate proceeds need basis and exclusion context.** A reported home or real-estate sale can require filing and reconciliation, but the sale price is not the taxable gain; basis, improvements, selling costs, exclusion rules, and ownership / use facts remain part of the measured base.[S291][S292][S294][S295][S301][S302]
7. **Approximation beats zero-basis punishment where records are genuinely unavailable.** If primary records are lost, old, held by a failed broker, destroyed, inherited, or controlled by a stronger actor, the lane should accept credible substitutes, account statements, trade confirmations, transfer histories, closing files, issuer records, affidavits, or conservative estimates before treating the basis as zero. Strategic opacity can still be sanctioned separately.[S27][S39][S41][S42][S154][S155][S160][S172][S291][S292][S293]
8. **Future artificial persons would need person-side basis memory.** If a rights-bearing artificial subject ever held capital assets or digital property, a former owner, host, wallet, exchange, or controller could not sell the only route to its basis, lot history, transfer statement, or disposition reconciliation.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S68][S298][S299]

## Pattern pack

### A. Covered securities improve reporting without ending contest

Basis reported to the IRS can simplify returns, but it can also be wrong after splits, mergers, wash-sale adjustments, lot-selection errors, or transfer breaks. The public lane should let the taxpayer show the reported basis and the correction rather than forcing binary acceptance or gross-proceeds treatment.[S292][S293][S294][S295][S296][S297]

### B. Noncovered securities and old assets need a reconstruction ladder

Assets acquired before reporting coverage, transferred between brokers, inherited, gifted, or held through older custodial systems often lack current broker basis. A reconstruction ladder should start with the most direct records available and then move to credible substitutes or conservative estimates, not to automatic zero.[S27][S291][S292][S293][S295][S297]

### C. Digital-asset reporting should not create a wallet-memory toll

Form 1099-DA and broker reporting can reduce underreporting, but self-custody and transfer histories may sit outside the reporting broker's view. A taxpayer should not have to rent an exchange dashboard, chain-analytics product, or proprietary wallet export merely to avoid being taxed on gross proceeds where basis can be reconstructed by other fit records.[S68][S294][S295][S298][S299][S300]

### D. Real-estate proceeds are not home-sale gain

A closing statement or Form 1099-S-type record can show the transaction happened. It does not settle basis, improvements, selling costs, principal-residence exclusion, ownership / use facts, or allocation among owners. A proceeds-only match should therefore stay a reconciliation prompt, not a final deficiency.[S291][S292][S294][S295][S301][S302]

### E. Bulk and summary reporting must preserve line-item visibility

Large broker statements, exchange exports, and digital-asset transaction files can be summarized, but a taxpayer must be able to identify which lots, disposals, basis fields, holding periods, and adjustments drive the proposed amount. Summary-only adverse action is especially dangerous when proceeds and basis come from different record systems.[S27][S65][S66][S68][S294][S295][S296][S299]

### F. Basis correction should propagate after acceptance

Once a basis, holding period, lot selection, exclusion, or transfer correction is accepted for a transaction, adjacent audit, underreporter, collection, refund, penalty, and future-year carryover systems should reuse the corrected delta rather than demanding a full reproof of the same sale.[S39][S42][S154][S155][S175][S294][S295]

## Guardrails

- no treating Form 1099-B, 1099-DA, 1099-S, exchange export, wallet sale, or closing proceeds as taxable gain without a basis / exclusion / adjustment lane;
- no zero-basis default solely because the current broker, exchange, closing agent, or platform did not report basis;
- no paid software, subscription broker archive, private chain-analytics tool, or captive exchange dashboard as the ordinary gateway to basis reconstruction;
- no adverse inference from using Form 8949-style corrections, substitute records, or conservative estimates before the taxpayer has had an intelligible basis-reconciliation path;
- no whole-return or whole-refund freeze where only a bounded sale, lot, basis, holding-period, or exclusion field is disputed;
- no information-return penalty logic that punishes the recipient for reporter-side basis gaps while leaving the reporter, custodian, or platform correction duty invisible;
- no owner-rented lot history, wallet export, or basis ledger for a future rights-bearing artificial subject.

## Failure modes

- **basis rent** — the taxpayer must buy access, software, professional reconstruction, broker archives, or wallet analytics to avoid being taxed on a basis record the public system could accept through fit alternatives.
- **gross-proceeds trap** — an information return or exchange feed causes tax to be computed on sale proceeds rather than gain because basis is missing, wrong, noncovered, or trapped upstream.
- **transfer-statement break** — basis fails to travel across brokers, custodians, exchanges, inheritances, gifts, reorganizations, or account migrations, and the weaker holder bears the entire repair cost.
- **digital-asset memory toll** — wallet migration or new broker reporting makes the taxpayer rent proprietary transaction history to prove what the broker cannot see.
- **home-sale proceeds finality** — Form 1099-S or a closing record becomes a deficiency shortcut without basis, improvement, cost, exclusion, and ownership / use facts.
- **summary statement opacity** — the taxpayer receives a large consolidated proceeds number but cannot see or correct the particular lots, holding periods, or basis fields driving the adjustment.
- **owner-held basis ledger** — a future artificial person can realize or contest capital gains only through a former controller's export key or custodian dashboard.

## Practical tests

1. Does the notice or match distinguish gross proceeds from gain?
2. Does it say whether basis was reported to the IRS, missing, noncovered, or disputed?
3. Can the taxpayer identify the affected lots, assets, dates, holding periods, proceeds, reported basis, claimed corrected basis, and adjustment reason?
4. Are credible substitutes accepted when primary broker, exchange, wallet, inheritance, gift, or home-improvement records are unavailable through no fault of the taxpayer?
5. Did the system seek correction from the actor that held the account, transfer statement, closing file, corporate-action record, or wallet / broker feed?
6. Are undisputed portions of the return, refund, or collection case separated from the bounded basis dispute?
7. Once corrected, does the basis / holding-period / exclusion delta propagate to adjacent audit, refund, collection, penalty, and future-year surfaces?
8. If the asset holder were a future rights-bearing artificial person, would lot history and basis correction belong to the subject rather than to a former owner or host?

## Relations

This note links measurement, third-party reporting, compliance burden, record asymmetry, same-facts reuse, automated notices, audit, nonfiler reconstruction, sanctions, refund symmetry, and artificial-personhood routing. Use it when the live issue is not whether a sale, exchange, or disposition happened, but whether the system is measuring gain rather than taxing visible proceeds because basis memory is missing, wrong, fragmented, or privately held.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S42][S65][S66][S68][S154][S155][S158][S160][S172][S182][S186][S291][S292][S294][S295][S298][S299]
