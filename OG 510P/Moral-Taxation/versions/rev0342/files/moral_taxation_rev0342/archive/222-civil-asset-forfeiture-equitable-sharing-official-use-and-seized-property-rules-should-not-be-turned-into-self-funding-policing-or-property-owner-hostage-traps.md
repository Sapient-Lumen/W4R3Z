# Civil asset forfeiture, equitable sharing, official use, and seized-property rules should not be turned into self-funding policing or property-owner hostage traps

## Claim

Forfeiture may recover crime proceeds and repair victims or public harm, but it may not impose suspicion-based property loss, make owners buy back access to their own property, or let the seizing agency finance itself from seizures.[S519][S520][S521][S522]

## Why it matters

Forfeiture becomes a tax when proceeds flow to public budgets, especially to the agency that chose to seize. The strongest moral warning appears where owners are uncharged, innocent, low-income, or unable to contest because process costs more than the property.

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/civil-asset-forfeiture-equitable-sharing-and-property-owner-protection-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/civil-asset-forfeiture-equitable-sharing-and-property-owner-protection-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/civil-asset-forfeiture-equitable-sharing-and-owner-remedy-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/civil-asset-forfeiture-equitable-sharing-and-owner-remedy-ladder.md). The compact rule is: heightened proof, prompt remedy, innocent-owner shelter, proportionality, and neutral proceeds.

## Anti-patterns

- **self-funding policing** — seizure decisions create agency revenue.
- **property-owner hostage trap** — contest cost exceeds property value.
- **suspicion tax** — property loss occurs without strong culpable connection.
- **equitable-sharing end run** — federal process bypasses stronger local protections.
