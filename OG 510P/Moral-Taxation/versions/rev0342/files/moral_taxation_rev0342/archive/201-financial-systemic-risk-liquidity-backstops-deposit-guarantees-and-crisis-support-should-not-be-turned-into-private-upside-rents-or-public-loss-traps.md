# Financial systemic risk, liquidity backstops, deposit guarantees, and crisis support should not be turned into private-upside rents or public-loss traps

## Claim

When a financial actor receives value from deposit insurance, central-bank liquidity, resolution credibility, implicit rescue expectations, or actual crisis support, that public risk-bearing is a tax-relevant benefit. Ordinary finance should not be punished, but public loss insurance should not become private upside rent.[S453][S454][S455]

## Why it matters

A public guarantee can lower funding costs, stabilize liabilities, preserve franchise value, and protect creditors before any explicit bailout occurs. If the guarantee is unpriced, the tax system subsidizes leverage and runnable claims while asking ordinary taxpayers to insure the tail.

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/financial-systemic-risk-guarantee-and-liquidity-backstop-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/financial-systemic-risk-guarantee-and-liquidity-backstop-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/systemic-finance-guarantee-and-liquidity-backstop-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/systemic-finance-guarantee-and-liquidity-backstop-ladder.md). The compact rule is: charge or regulate public backstop value, systemic amplification, and crisis support before taxing useful intermediation by blunt transaction theater.

## Anti-patterns

- **bailout amnesia** — support is treated as a temporary emergency rather than a public claim on later surplus.
- **bonus-before-repair** — dividends, buybacks, compensation, or creditor gains outrun backstop repair.
- **shadow-liability migration** — runnable bank-like promises move outside the charged perimeter.
- **FTT substitution** — a visible transaction tax is used to avoid pricing guarantees, leverage, liquidity mismatch, or resolution costs.
