# Local resource, data-center, land, and extraction revenue sharing should not be turned into fiscal hoarding or unfunded-mandate traps

## Claim

Local burden deserves local repair, but local windfalls can also owe equalization. Land, resource, port, data-center, infrastructure, platform, and extraction rents should be split by burden, benefit, capacity, need, autonomy, and spillover rather than captured wholly by either the host locality or the center.[S469][S470][S471]

## Why it matters

A host community can be exploited if national revenue sweeps local repair. A poor region can also be abandoned if rich localities hoard geography, inherited infrastructure, or public-law-created rents. The moral split is not localism versus centralism; it is repair plus equalization.

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/intergovernmental-equalization-local-share-and-fiscal-federalism-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/intergovernmental-equalization-local-share-and-fiscal-federalism-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/intergovernmental-equalization-local-share-and-subnational-capacity-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/intergovernmental-equalization-local-share-and-subnational-capacity-ladder.md). The compact rule is: host repair first for real local burden; equalization next for location and capacity windfalls.

## Anti-patterns

- **local-repair bypass** — burdened communities pay the cost while proceeds leave.
- **fiscal hoarding** — rich localities keep accidental or state-created rents while equal duties fall elsewhere.
- **unfunded mandate** — a higher government orders services without revenue authority or transfer.
- **subsidy-masked windfall** — abatements and public infrastructure are ignored when measuring local benefit.
