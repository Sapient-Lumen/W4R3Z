# 127 — Downside symmetry, loss recognition, carrybacks, and refundability

## One-line thesis

A morally serious tax system should not treat gains and losses as separate universes: when it taxes upside quickly but recognizes downside only weakly, too late, or only for actors rich enough to wait, it becomes a one-way state claim rather than a reciprocal fiscal rule.

## Why this matters

Loss rules shape risk-taking, liquidity, neutrality, and the effective burden on volatile or early-stage activity. OECD work says perfect intertemporal loss offsets are a necessary condition for neutrality across investment projects with different risk profiles, yet many systems fall short because they lack inflation indexation, carry-backs, or full offsetability.[S50] OECD also says refunds and carry-over provisions are common ways to support R&D by firms that cannot yet use tax benefits because they have insufficient tax liability or are still loss-making, and that refundability can align treatment across profitable and loss-making firms while the absence of such provisions can wipe out the intended benefit for loss-making firms.[S51] At the same time, OECD warns that loss rules can be abused through aggressive planning, artificial losses, and multiple deductions of the same economic loss.[S52]

## Design rule

Recognize **real downside** on terms that are sufficiently symmetric with the taxation of gains, while using narrow anti-abuse rules against artificial, duplicated, or trafficked losses.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Prefer bounded symmetry over one-way taxation

Where gains are taxed currently, the default should usually include one or more of:

- carry-forwards that do not silently erode through inflation where that distortion is material,
- bounded carry-backs where liquidity and cyclical stabilisation matter,
- refundable or payable credits where the policy is meant to support risky activity even in loss years,
- or equivalent time-value recognition that stops the state from taxing success now but ignoring failure until much later.[S50][S51]

### 2. Distinguish genuine downside from artificial losses

Do not confuse real failed investment, genuine operating loss, or cyclical contraction with:

- duplicate use of one economic loss,
- hybrid-instrument engineering,
- loss trafficking after ownership changes,
- wash-sale style timing games,
- or related-party arrangements that manufacture deductions without corresponding economic downside.[S52]

### 3. Human capital-income design needs real-loss recognition too

If the archive argues for greater parity between labor and capital income, it should not silently preserve special asymmetry on the downside. Real capital losses, failed entrepreneurial investment, and comparable downside events should be recognized on terms that are not purely symbolic. That does **not** imply unlimited immediate refundability for every asset loss, but it does imply that pure upside taxation with near-meaningless downside relief is morally suspect.

### 4. Small firms, start-ups, and public-interest innovation cannot wait forever

Where a tax preference or public-purpose incentive is meant to support genuinely risky activity, the benefit should not depend entirely on already being profitable. Carry-over may be enough for mature firms with stable future taxable income, but refundability, transferability, or bounded carry-back may be more just for younger firms, small producers, and public-interest projects facing real cash constraints.[S51]

### 5. Controller-first AI taxes should not become gross-throughput traps

The archive still rejects direct machine taxation, but controller-side AI taxes can also fail if they ignore downside. Special AI charges, minimum taxes, or automation levies should not be designed as blind gross-receipts or throughput claims that punish long pre-profit, high-uncertainty, or public-interest phases exactly when no rent has yet been captured. Where the moral target is rent, windfall, or externality, the design should preserve loss recognition for genuine downside while still charging observable harms such as carbon, water stress, grid congestion, or required ex ante reserves.[S15][S20][S50][S51]

## Guardrails

- Do not turn downside symmetry into a free public insurance policy for every speculative bet.
- Do not let refundability or carry-back become a subsidy for artificial losses or related-party engineering.
- Do not let inflation quietly destroy loss relief over long waiting periods where the system simultaneously taxes gains in nominal terms.
- Do not let only large incumbents with steady taxable income actually realise benefits that were advertised as general.

## Failure modes

- **One-way state call option**: gains are taxed immediately, but losses are only weakly usable years later.
- **Incumbent-only symmetry**: large profitable firms can use deductions and credits; smaller loss-making entrants effectively cannot.
- **Artificial-loss factory**: nominal downside is manufactured through affiliates, hybrids, or duplicate deductions.

## Practical tests

1. If the state taxes the upside this year, what happens to a genuine downside year of comparable scale?
2. Does the loss rule preserve real value over time, or does waiting quietly erase relief?
3. Are anti-abuse rules aimed at artificial losses rather than written so broadly that genuine downside becomes unrecognisable?
4. For AI and other frontier activity, is the proposal really taxing rents and harms, or is it taxing gross activity before profit, while pretending loss years do not count?
