# Controller-boundary synthesis: rank real governance first, then apply the outside-interface and inside-command exceptions

This note compresses the archive's controller-boundary family into one reusable rule. Use it when many actors each touch the same AI stack and the dispute is not about **whether** the archive is controller-first, but about **which actor is controller-like enough** to carry primary or co-controller tax duties.[S10][S16][S17][S20][S21][S34][S35][S41][S83]

## One-line thesis

Primary AI controller status follows durable governance, continuation power, and residual upside; ordinary use, visibility, billing position, branding frontage, oversight, finance, channel access, tool provision, or input supply does not by itself outrank the actor that can actually choose the mission, continue or stop the stack, set ordinary permissions, or inherit the strategic upside and downside.[S10][S16][S17][S20][S21][S34][S35][S41][S83]

## Fast ranking order

1. **Rank real governance first.** Ask who sets deployment purpose, routine permissions, release / rollback, safety posture, escalation, and ordinary continuation.[S10][S16][S17][S20][S21][S83]
2. **Keep primary liability separate from collection convenience.** The strongest remittance anchor may be a cloud, platform, processor, registry, or vendor account even when the primary controller sits elsewhere.[S21][S27][S41][S68][S83]
3. **Carry continuity across labels.** Renames, checkpoint migrations, affiliate transfers, and asset sales do not reset controller-side duties when governance and beneficiary continuity remain.[S20][S21][S34][S35][S41][S83]
4. **Add narrow co-controller duties only where governance is genuinely shared.** Do not multiply civic taxpayers just because many firms touch the same stack; where governance is genuinely collective, follow the real controller group rather than pretending no controller exists.[S19][S20][S21][S34][S35][S42][S43][S44][S83]

## Outside-interface presumptions

The following positions are presumptively **outside** primary controller status unless they also take on real operational governance: ordinary users and prompt authors; thin resellers; employees, contractors, nominees, and outsourced operators; passive clouds, utilities, landlords, lenders, and contract hosts; regulators, licensors, auditors, and certifiers; insurers, bond sureties, and reserve custodians; payment processors, merchants-of-record, app stores, and ad networks; third-party tool and API providers; training-input suppliers, dataset curators, benchmark operators, and label vendors; and white-label or OEM brand fronts.[S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S41][S83]

These actors can still matter as ordinary taxpayers, rent targets, remittance anchors, local-burden payers, risk-finance layers, reporting points, or bounded-duty intermediaries. The rule is not immunity; it is that **visibility is not governance**.[S21][S27][S41][S68][S83]

## Inside-command exceptions

Downstream or adjacent actors can become real controllers or co-controllers when they materially govern the live stack: durable fine-tuning or system scaffolding; retrieval governance; tool permissioning; inside-the-loop action governance; managed update or release gates; mission-specific commissioning; public-body deployment command; temporary step-in control during failure or emergency; or other durable authority over stop / continue, approval, rollback, or ordinary risk posture.[S10][S16][S17][S20][S21][S41][S83]

The same logic applies upstream. Maintained hubs, gated distribution, and successor chains can carry duties not because they are nearby, but because they retain ordinary control over continuation, access, or inherited benefit.[S17][S20][S21][S34][S35][S41][S83]

## Four anti-patterns

1. **Visibility worship** — mistaking the easiest visible actor for the real controller.[S21][S41][S83]
2. **Paper-title worship** — letting branding, contracts, or resale labels defeat the actual governance map.[S20][S21][S34][S35][S83]
3. **Collection-subject collapse** — assuming the remitter is therefore the primary bearer or primary controller.[S21][S27][S41][S68]
4. **Version-reset theater** — treating migrations, renames, or affiliate choreography as moral amnesia.[S20][S21][S34][S35][S41][S83]
