# Spectrum, orbital, and satellite-resource rules should not be turned into paper scarcity or exclusive rents

## Claim

Radio spectrum and usable orbital capacity are public and international scarcity resources. Auctions and fees can recover value, but filing priority, exclusive licenses, and coordination rules must not become a market in paper scarcity or a shield for incumbent enclosure.[S588][S589]

## Why it matters

The same narrow waist applies beyond land and carbon. Some resources are scarce because simultaneous use causes interference, collision risk, or crowding. The moral claim is not that every use should be free, but that revenue extraction must be secondary to public-safety, equitable access, competition, and sustainability duties.[S588][S589]

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/spectrum-orbital-commons-auction-and-interference-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/spectrum-orbital-commons-auction-and-interference-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/spectrum-orbital-commons-auction-interference-and-sustainability-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/spectrum-orbital-commons-auction-interference-and-sustainability-ladder.md). The compact rule is: charge scarce use, not speculative paperwork; preserve access, safety, and sustainability before auction yield.

## Anti-patterns

- **paper satellite** — resource is reserved without credible deployment.
- **spectrum hoarding** — exclusion creates value while service lags.
- **interference privilege** — powerful users externalize coordination harm.
- **auction myopia** — fiscal revenue crowds out access, safety, or competition.
