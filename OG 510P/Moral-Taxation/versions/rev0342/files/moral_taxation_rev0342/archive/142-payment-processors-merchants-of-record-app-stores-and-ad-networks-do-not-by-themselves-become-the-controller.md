# 142 — Payment processors, merchants of record, app stores, ad networks, and channel intermediaries do not by themselves become the controller

## One-line thesis

Billing, ranking, distribution, and monetization channels can collect money, gate access, or standardize terms without thereby becoming the morally primary AI controller. Channel intermediation can be a useful remittance anchor, but it is not ordinary deployment governance by itself.[S10][S20][S21][S27][S41][S68][S83]

## Why this matters

Once the archive distinguishes users, passive infrastructure, open publication, downstream co-control, delegated operators, outside overseers, and risk-finance layers, another avoidance path appears: the real controller says that because an app store bills customers, a merchant of record handles compliance, an ad network allocates traffic, or a payment processor can suspend settlement, the channel intermediary has become the true decision-maker and should bear the special AI-side fiscal burden. That would push controller liability onto whichever commercial intermediary is most visible while inviting firms to route deployment through marketplace, billing, or acquisition layers mainly to blur where control really sits.[S10][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S41][S68][S83]

## Design rule

Treat **channel power** and **controller power** as distinct unless they materially merge.

Ask separately:

- who chose the model, deployment scope, and operating budget,
- who can change persistent instructions, retrieval scope, tool permissions, safety posture, or rollout design,
- who captures the concentrated rent or strategic gain,
- whether the intermediary mainly settles payments, ranks listings, routes demand, or enforces standardized marketplace terms from outside,
- and whether the intermediary has crossed into actually governing live deployment choices rather than merely billing, distributing, or acquiring customers.[S10][S20][S21][S27][S41][S68][S83]

Where the strategic levers and gains remain with the lab, deployer, platform, or grouped beneficiary, special controller-side AI liability should remain there even if a channel can delist, pause settlement, or rank traffic.

## Boundary ladder

### 1. Ordinary payment processor or merchant of record

A processor or merchant of record that settles payments, handles chargebacks, performs routine compliance screening, or remits marketplace proceeds does **not** thereby become the primary controller. Payment visibility is not the same as choosing the model, risk posture, or ordinary deployment strategy.[S20][S21][S27][S41][S68][S83]

### 2. App store, plugin marketplace, or software channel

A store or marketplace that reviews listings, enforces standard terms, collects commissions, or can delist an app is still usually governing market access from outside rather than directing the underlying deployment from inside. Channel gatekeeping can justify reporting or remittance duties without relocating primary controller liability by itself.[S10][S20][S21][S27][S41][S68][S83]

### 3. Ad network, traffic broker, or lead-generation channel

An ad network or acquisition broker that prices reach, ranks ads, or allocates impressions does **not** by itself become the controller of the promoted AI deployment. It monetizes attention and distribution, not necessarily model choice or live operational posture.[S10][S20][S21][S27][S68][S83]

### 4. Channel with live deployment governance

The answer changes when the intermediary gains durable authority over live deployment choices: which model can serve users, which tools may execute, which standing safety settings apply, whether risky use cases may continue, or how the system behaves in ordinary operation. At that point the actor may become a real co-controller for that layer, not because billing or discovery is magical control, but because the intermediary has moved inside the deployment loop.[S10][S15][S16][S20][S21][S41][S68]

### 5. Captive marketplace, affiliated merchant of record, or paper channel loop

Do not let a controller group wash liability through an affiliated app store, reseller shell, merchant-of-record affiliate, or ad-buying vehicle that remains under the same beneficial ownership or strategic command. Where the supposed channel layer is functionally internal, follow the group rather than the paper boundary.[S21][S27][S34][S35][S83]

## Hard defaults

1. **Do not mistake billing or ranking visibility for controller status.** Seeing the money, customer, or traffic first does not decide who governs the deployment.[S20][S21][S83]
2. **Do not confuse standard marketplace terms with ordinary deployment governance.** Delisting rights, payout holds, or baseline store review are usually outside guardrails unless they become standing operational commands.[S20][S21][S27][S41][S68]
3. **Do use channel firms as remittance or disclosure anchors where that is the cleanest fit.** Payment or marketplace intermediaries may be excellent collection points for deliberately assigned duties without absorbing the whole controller-side moral burden.[S21][S27][S41][S68][S83]
4. **Do not ignore real co-control either.** If the intermediary can materially choose the live model, customer segmentation, tool permissions, or ordinary continuation of the system, it may owe co-controller duties for that governed layer.[S10][S15][S16][S20][S21]

## Failure modes

- **channel scapegoating** — the visible billing or marketplace intermediary is treated as the controller because it is easiest to see or charge.
- **anchor drift** — a good remittance point is mistaken for the morally primary taxpayer.
- **distribution laundering** — private controllers route activity through marketplaces, merchants of record, or affiliate channels mainly to blur who really governs the system.
- **inside-loop blindness** — a channel that actually chooses the live operating envelope hides behind the language of neutral intermediation.[S10][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S41][S68][S83]

## Practical tests

1. Who chose the model, system behavior, rollout scope, and ordinary risk posture?
2. Who can change retrieval, tools, standing instructions, or continuation decisions without asking permission elsewhere?
3. Is the intermediary mainly billing, ranking, distributing, or acquiring customers from outside, or governing live operation from inside?
4. Does the actor capture mainly channel fees and payment spreads, or the concentrated rents from the deployment itself?
5. Would the same AI system still be privately directed if the channel intermediary were swapped out for another processor, store, or advertising route?

If the answers still point to the lab, deployer, platform, or grouped beneficiary, keep controller-side AI taxation there. Channel intermediaries can be taxed ordinarily and used as remittance anchors without becoming the primary moral home of the deployment.
