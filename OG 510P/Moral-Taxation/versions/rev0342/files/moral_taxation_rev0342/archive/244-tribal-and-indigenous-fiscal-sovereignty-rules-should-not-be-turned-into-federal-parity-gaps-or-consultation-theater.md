# Tribal and Indigenous fiscal-sovereignty rules should not be turned into federal parity gaps or consultation theater

## Claim

Tax rules touching Tribal and Indigenous governments must begin with sovereignty, treaty/trust obligations, membership, land, and self-government. Treating Tribes as ordinary private entities, nonprofits, or afterthought subnational units creates category mistakes and often fiscal exclusion.[S592][S593]

## Why it matters

A moral tax archive cannot say “local burden” and ignore Tribal sovereignty. Tribal governments may need parity with state and local governments, but also rules fitted to treaty duties, community benefit, member support, cultural confidentiality, and land/resource self-determination. Consultation is substantive only when it can still change the answer.[S592][S593]

The compact rule is: sovereignty first, parity at least, consultation before lock-in, and Tribal public benefit before outsider classification.

## Anti-patterns

- **parity gap** — states receive fiscal tools denied to Tribes.
- **consultation theater** — process begins after the decision is effectively made.
- **sovereignty bypass** — entity form erases government status.
- **paternalistic welfare reclassification** — member support is taxed because outsiders distrust Tribal need determinations.
