# Agricultural support and food-system tax rules should not be turned into producer rent or nutrition-floor harm

## Claim

Agricultural tax preferences and subsidies are legitimate when they repair food access, rural floors, resilience, or ecological public goods. They become suspect when they capitalize into land, quota, or incumbent producer rents while healthy food remains unaffordable.[S582][S583]

## Why it matters

Food systems sit on both sides of the moral floor. Farmers and rural communities can need real stabilization, while low-income consumers can be harmed by price support, broad consumption taxes, or poorly designed exemptions. The archive must therefore route food policy through incidence, nutrition access, environmental additionality, and net-fiscal-stack disclosure before treating agricultural support as public benefit.[S582][S583]

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/agriculture-food-subsidy-nutrition-and-rural-floor-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/agriculture-food-subsidy-nutrition-and-rural-floor-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/agriculture-food-support-nutrition-and-rural-floor-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/agriculture-food-support-nutrition-and-rural-floor-ladder.md). The compact rule is: feed people, stabilize genuine rural floors, and buy verified public goods before subsidizing producer rents.

## Anti-patterns

- **producer-rent camouflage** — support capitalizes into land or quota value.
- **nutrition-floor harm** — price or tax design raises healthy-diet costs for protected households.
- **input-subsidy lock-in** — cheap harmful inputs defeat transition.
- **small-farmer theater** — hardship imagery justifies large-producer capture.
