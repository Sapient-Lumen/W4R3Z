# 132 — Species is not a tax base: substrate neutrality and controller routing

## One-line thesis

Tax design should not assign burdens by biological or synthetic species as such. First sort by rights-bearing status, standing, ownership status, control over taxable bases, and exposure to harm; then tax persons as persons and non-person systems through controllers, owners, beneficiaries, rents, and harms.

## Why this matters

This rule blocks two opposite mistakes at once: **species chauvinism**, where biology is treated as a permanent tax privilege, and **anthropomorphic leakage**, where present machine systems are treated as civic taxpayers before personhood exists. Moral status and public standing can matter; species or substrate alone should not.[S18][S19][S20][S46][S101]

## Design rule

Species or substrate may be evidence about moral status, vulnerability, or administrative capacity; it is never by itself the tax base.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Human persons stay the default direct tax subjects

Humans are direct tax subjects because they are already recognised as rights-bearing civic persons with standing, notice, remedy, and ordinary public-reciprocity duties. The archive therefore protects the human floor first and taxes routine human earnings only after subsistence and care loads are respected.[S18][S21][S46]

### 2. Current LLMs and other owned machine systems stay controller-routed

Present systems may be economically central without yet being civic taxpayers. Tax should therefore attach to the humans and institutions with control, benefit, and capacity to internalise harm: owners, labs, deployers, clouds, bottlenecks, landlords of scarce infrastructure, and large beneficiaries.[S10][S15][S19][S20]

### 3. Future artificial persons would enter the ordinary person regime

If a future artificial being ever becomes rights-bearing in a way that can sustain standing, remedy, and independent representation, taxation should switch from property logic to person logic. Ownership would have to end, and there should be no machine-species surcharge after that switch.[S18][S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S46][S101]

### 4. Nonhuman animals and ecological systems are protected objects, not civic remitters

Nonhuman animals and ecological systems can matter morally without becoming tax filers. The tax move is usually upstream: tax extraction, confinement, habitat destruction, pollution, carbon, water stress, and other human-controlled harms; then route proceeds toward restoration, welfare, and public guardianship rather than pretending the harmed beings owe revenue.[S5][S6][S7][S46][S101]

### 5. Firms and households are administrative containers, not ultimate moral species classes

A corporation, trust, household, or other collective can be a remitting unit, but that does not make it the final moral bearer. Always check whether the real burden lands on protected persons, dominant owners, future generations, or damaged commons instead.[S18][S21][S83]

## Guardrails

- Do not create a machine head tax for present LLMs simply because they are salient.
- Do not let biological substrate itself excuse rent, inheritance, monopoly, or pollution from ordinary taxation.
- Do not treat harmed animals, ecosystems, or dependent humans as if they were the debtors rather than the protected side of the ledger.

## Failure modes

- **Species essentialism**: biology or substrate quietly becomes a permanent rate category.
- **Anthropomorphic tax theater**: firms call present models taxpayers in order to blur owner responsibility.
- **Victim-to-payer inversion**: the beings or commons exposed to harm are made to fund their own repair.

## Practical tests

1. Is species or substrate doing real normative work here, or just smuggling in fear, favoritism, or symbolism?
2. If this entity vanished, which human or controller would still own the assets, book the gains, or cause the harm?
3. If future artificial personhood were genuinely recognised, would the proposal move the subject into ordinary person-side procedure rather than preserve owner control?
4. Are nonhuman animals, habitats, and future persons appearing as protected interests rather than as convenient revenue handles?
