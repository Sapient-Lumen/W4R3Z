# Beneficial ownership, company, trust, and public-registry rules should not be turned into privacy dragnets or shell-immunity gaps

## Claim

Beneficial-ownership transparency is legitimate when it reveals real control for tax, AML, sanctions, and anti-corruption purposes. It is illegitimate when it broadly exposes ordinary owners without purpose or safety controls, or when exemptions recreate anonymous shell immunity.[S526][S527][S528][S529]

## Why it matters

Tax systems need real-control information, but transparency can become either too weak to pierce shells or too broad to protect households, small entities, minors, survivors, and vulnerable owners.

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/beneficial-ownership-public-registry-and-privacy-burden-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/beneficial-ownership-public-registry-and-privacy-burden-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/beneficial-ownership-registry-access-privacy-and-small-entity-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/beneficial-ownership-registry-access-privacy-and-small-entity-ladder.md). The compact rule is: adequate, accurate, up-to-date control data; tiered access; correction before penalty; and no high-risk gap.[S530]

## Anti-patterns

- **privacy dragnet** — ownership data is overexposed without purpose or safety screen.
- **shell immunity** — exemptions preserve anonymous control.
- **paper-owner finality** — registry names are mistaken for real control.
- **small-entity penalty mill** — minor reporting errors become revenue events.
