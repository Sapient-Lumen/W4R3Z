# Customs, tariffs, carbon border adjustments, and de minimis rules should not be turned into hidden consumer taxes or trade-rent traps

## Claim

Border taxes can protect a real base, price carbon leakage, or improve import-tax collection, but they must state who bears the burden, why the border is the right collection point, how proof works, and when the rule sunsets or converts. They should not hide consumer taxes or incumbent rents behind foreigner-pays rhetoric.[S462][S463][S464][S465][S466]

## Why it matters

Tariffs and border adjustments are administratively tempting because the border is visible. But border visibility can conceal domestic pass-through, retaliation costs, small-importer burden, exporter capacity gaps, and protection rents.

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/customs-tariff-carbon-border-and-trade-retaliation-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/customs-tariff-carbon-border-and-trade-retaliation-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/customs-tariff-carbon-border-and-retaliation-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/customs-tariff-carbon-border-and-retaliation-ladder.md). The compact rule is: border taxes need honest base, incidence, proof, retaliation, and proceeds review.

## Anti-patterns

- **foreigner-pays fiction** — domestic consumers or input users bear the cost but are absent from the story.
- **carbon camouflage** — climate labels cover sectoral protection without domestic equivalence.
- **de minimis shock** — small parcels and consumers become the sudden compliance perimeter.
- **retaliation rent** — politically useful punishment protects domestic bottlenecks without public-benefit proof.
