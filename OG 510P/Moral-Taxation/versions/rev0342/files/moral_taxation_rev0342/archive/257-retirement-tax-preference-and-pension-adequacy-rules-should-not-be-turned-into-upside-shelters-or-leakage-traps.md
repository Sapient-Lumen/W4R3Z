# Retirement tax preference and pension adequacy rules should not be turned into upside shelters or leakage traps

## Claim

Retirement tax preferences are legitimate when they buy broad old-age security, care-gap repair, and longevity protection. They become suspect when they operate mainly as high-end deferral, fee extraction, or dynastic shelter.[S623][S624]

## Why it matters

The archive already rejects using pension ownership to launder firm-level windfalls. This note adds the mirror rule: person-side retirement support must serve adequacy rather than merely expanding tax-favored asset pools.[S623][S624]

## Route

Use [`../docs/10-framework/retirement-tax-preference-pension-adequacy-and-leakage-routing.md`](../docs/10-framework/retirement-tax-preference-pension-adequacy-and-leakage-routing.md) and [`../docs/20-calibration/retirement-tax-preference-pension-adequacy-and-leakage-ladder.md`](../docs/20-calibration/retirement-tax-preference-pension-adequacy-and-leakage-ladder.md). The compact rule is: subsidize adequacy, not account size.

## Anti-patterns

- **retirement halo shelter** — old-age language hides high-end tax deferral.
- **fee leakage** — tax support is captured by asset managers or plan administrators.
- **care gap denial** — pension design ignores unpaid care and interrupted work.
- **liquidity trap** — emergency withdrawals destroy adequacy through penalties and fees.
