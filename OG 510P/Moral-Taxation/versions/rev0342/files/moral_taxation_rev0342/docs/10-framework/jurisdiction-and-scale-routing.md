# Jurisdiction and scale routing

This note answers the question that comes **after** choosing a moral base and **before** arguing over administration details: **which level of government should tax first, and when should taxing claims be shared rather than monopolised?** The archive's stable answer is to assign each base to the smallest level that sees the immovable burden or benefit **without** inviting race-to-the-bottom competition, booking theatre, or fiscal hoarding, and then to layer equalisation or club coordination where mobility or uneven capacity would otherwise break justice.[S3][S5][S9][S15][S23][S25][S28][S69][S70]

## Core rule

Route taxing rights by five questions in this order:

1. **What facts are immovable or place-bound?**
2. **Where do final use, users, or customers sit?**
3. **Which level can protect the human floor and equalise capacity without destructive competition?**
4. **Which level has the administrative capacity to assess, collect, and review the base honestly?**
5. **Does the base outrun one jurisdiction unless states coordinate?**

A scale assignment is good when burdened places are compensated, person-level floor protection remains portable, mobile rents cannot escape by relabeling or relocation theatre, and no level gets a claim merely because another level is politically weaker.[S3][S5][S9][S21][S23][S25][S28][S31][S69][S70]

## Default scale map by base or context

| Base or context | Strongest first scale | Why this scale usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Land value, site rent, vacancy, and ordinary immovable property | municipal / metropolitan / other situs-linked subnational level | the base is visible, immovable, and tightly linked to local public order and infrastructure | rich localities should not be allowed to hoard all site rents where wider equalisation is needed.[S5][S23][S25][S70] |
| Local congestion, water stress, grid strain, and data-centre siting burdens | burdened locality, usually with national minima or backstops | the harm lands locally even when profits are booked elsewhere | local bargaining weakness should not let powerful operators buy underpricing or compensation waivers.[S15][S23][S69][S70] |
| Resource extraction and severance | situs-linked state / province / locality plus a national share where sovereign stewardship or equalisation requires it | extraction is place-bound, but windfalls and cleanup obligations can outrun one locality | neither total central confiscation nor full local windfall hoarding is a stable justice answer.[S5][S15][S23] |
| Ordinary earnings, person-level reciprocity, and protected-floor transfers | mainly national | mobility, portability, and equal treatment usually require a common person-level floor and broad pooling capacity | do not force municipalities to fund the protected floor mainly from weak resident wage bases.[S2][S4][S31][S64] |
| Broad consumption taxes | national or harmonised subnational destination system | destination logic is simpler and fairer than origin fragmentation for broad consumption | origin-style fragmentation can turn consumption taxation into trade distortion and jurisdiction gaming.[S3][S28] |
| Large corporate profit and residual rents | national administration plus market-jurisdiction sharing and club floors for mobile profit | the base is too mobile for purely local claims yet too market-created for booking jurisdictions alone | no server-location fetish, booking-haven triumph, or pure residence monopoly for borderless rents.[S1][S9][S28] |
| Large downstream beneficiary capturing extraordinary AI-enabled gains without controlling the stack | beneficiary home jurisdiction first, with coordinated market sharing for cross-border downstream surplus and local repair for burdened places | downstream gain belongs where the beneficiary is really taxed and where markets co-create the surplus, not where the vendor's model hardware sits | do not let upstream server geography, residence monopoly, or local all-or-nothing rhetoric settle the whole claim.[S3][S4][S9][S10][S11][S14][S21][S22][S28][S67][S69][S70][S83] |
| Current frontier AI stack | hybrid: local for siting burdens, national for controller-side taxes / bonds / major profit rules, club coordination for mobile rents | AI burdens and benefits split across levels: facilities are local, controllers are usually national or multi-state, and mobile rents outrun any one host | do not ask host communities alone to finance AI transition, grid adaptation, or downside repair while distant controllers keep the fiscal upside.[S15][S16][S17][S67][S69][S70] |
| Future rights-bearing artificial person | ordinary person-level regime in its legal home, with normal subnational service taxes where place-bound | once rights-bearing status exists, scale assignment should track ordinary personhood and local service use rather than equipment logic | no substrate surcharge or owner-side substitution just because the subject is artificial.[S18][S19][S20] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Put place-bound bases where the place is real

Land, sites, extractive deposits, utility burdens, congestion, and local environmental stress should create a real fiscal claim for the jurisdiction that hosts the burden. The archive does not treat local compensation as an optional side payment when the base itself is place-bound.[S5][S15][S23][S25][S70]

### 2. Put person-level floors and broad equalisation above the local level

A morally serious tax system should not make the protected floor depend mainly on municipal wealth, bargaining power, or local tax luck. Local revenue can help, but person-level minima and broad equalisation normally require national or strongly coordinated pooling.[S2][S4][S31][S64][S69]

### 3. Use destination and market logic where origin can be faked cheaply

Broad consumption usually belongs at destination, and a share of large cross-border residual profit belongs partly where customers and users materially generate the market. Exclusive origin or residence logic becomes morally weak when physical-presence rules, server location, or booking entities are easy to rearrange.[S3][S9][S28]

### 4. Share claims when several scales are morally real

Some bases should not be monopolised by one level. Resource rents may justify both situs-linked and national claims; AI facilities may justify local burden charges plus national controller-side obligations; mobile profit may justify national administration plus coordinated market-jurisdiction allocation. Use revenue sharing, credits, equalisation, or priority rules rather than pretending only one level matters.[S5][S9][S15][S28][S69][S70]

### 5. Do not let local bargaining undercut national minima

When a base is locally salient but politically asymmetric — large extractive projects, hyperscale data centres, dominant platforms — localities may face pressure to undercharge in exchange for siting promises. National floors, model agreements, disclosure, and anti-abatement rules are justified where one locality cannot bargain on equal terms.[S15][S21][S29][S30][S70]

### 6. Match AI taxation to the geography of burden, control, beneficiary gain, and escape

The archive's AI scale rule is not a single-level answer. Local jurisdictions deserve direct tools for land, housing, water, grid, and congestion burdens from facilities; national systems should tax controllers, large beneficiaries, and major profits; downstream beneficiary-side windfall claims should follow the beneficiary's own fiscal home and coordinated market exposure rather than upstream model geography; and clubs or treaties should address mobile rents and cross-border avoidance. Scale mismatch is one of the main ways AI taxation becomes symbolic. See [`beneficiary-jurisdiction-and-claim-routing.md`](beneficiary-jurisdiction-and-claim-routing.md). [S3][S9][S10][S15][S16][S17][S21][S22][S28][S67][S69][S70][S83]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Local burden without local revenue** — host communities absorb siting costs while fiscal upside leaves.
2. **Rich-place hoarding** — valuable local bases fund only already-advantaged jurisdictions while equalisation evaporates.
3. **Origin fiction** — borderless profits are assigned by booking, paper residence, or server geography alone.
4. **Coordination gap** — each level waits for another, and the base escapes all of them.
5. **Scale dumping** — every hard tax question is pushed either downward to weak localities or upward to distant treaties to avoid present responsibility.[S5][S9][S15][S23][S28][S69][S70]

## Order of preference

When several levels can plausibly tax the same base, the archive usually prefers the arrangement that:

1. compensates the most burdened place,
2. protects the human floor through a broad enough pool,
3. uses destination or market logic where origin is fakeable,
4. denies mobile rents an escape hatch,
5. and limits capture by any single locality, ministry, or booking jurisdiction.

This is why site-value taxes, congestion charges, and data-centre burden charges often start local; ordinary person-level floors and income taxes usually need national pooling; and large cross-border corporate or AI rents need club coordination layered on top of national administration rather than replacing it.
