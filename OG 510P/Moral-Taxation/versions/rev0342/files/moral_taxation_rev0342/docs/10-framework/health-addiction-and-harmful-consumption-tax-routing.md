# Health, addiction, and harmful-consumption tax routing

## Question in one sentence

When taxes target tobacco, alcohol, sugar-sweetened beverages, gambling-like mechanics, ultra-processed products, or other addictive and health-harmful goods, how should the archive reduce harm without turning vulnerability into a poverty toll?[S480][S481][S482]

Route: health taxes can be justified by external harm, internality, addictive design, public health costs, and industry rent; but they require floor repair, targeted proceeds, anti-industry-capture rules, and non-tax supports.[S480][S482]

## Core rule

The moral target is not the addicted consumer. It is the product design, marketing, external harm, health-system burden, and rent from dependence. Tax can raise price and reduce use, but it must not make poor or addicted people fund the repair without receiving treatment, prevention, healthier substitutes, and income protection.

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| price-sensitive harmful product | excise gate | specific or sugar/alcohol/content-based excise with indexation. |
| addiction or youth targeting | vulnerability gate | marketing restriction, age rule, product standard, and tax. |
| low-income consumption burden | floor gate | rebate, treatment, healthy substitute subsidy, or proceeds repair. |
| industry claims regressivity | capture gate | separate real floor burden from industry-protective talking points. |
| illicit market risk | enforcement gate | rate pace, track-and-trace, and public health enforcement. |
| earmarking proposed | proceeds gate | health, treatment, prevention, and social protection first. |

## Anti-patterns

- **poverty toll** — the tax extracts from addicted or low-income consumers without repair.
- **harm license** — payment substitutes for marketing, product, or access restrictions.
- **industry shield** — regressivity rhetoric blocks health taxes while industry rents continue.
- **stale-rate erosion** — inflation makes the health tax weaker each year.
