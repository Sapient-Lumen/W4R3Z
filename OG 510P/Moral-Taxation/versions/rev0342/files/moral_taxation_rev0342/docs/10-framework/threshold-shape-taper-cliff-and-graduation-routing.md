# Threshold shape, taper, cliff, and graduation routing

This note answers a narrow but recurring design question: **once the archive knows the right base and subject, how should a boundary bite?** The archive's routing answer is that thresholds may protect dignity, simplify administration, or trigger genuinely categorical duties, but ordinary justice usually prefers **zero bands, tapers, marginal step-ups, and graduation windows over hard cliffs**.[S26][S31][S80][S81][S82]

## Core rule

1. **use de minimis lines to keep trivial liability or filing out of the system**,[S31][S80]
2. **use smooth tapers or ordinary marginal bands where a cliff would create pay-rise penalties, bunching, or threshold gaming**,[S26][S31][S80][S81][S82]
3. **use graduation windows when simplified or small-actor regimes need an on-ramp into fuller compliance**,[S26][S80]
4. **reserve hard cutoffs for genuinely categorical obligations such as permit classes, registry duties, or higher-governance triggers**,[S16][S17][S80]
5. **and keep any hard jump small, buffered, or explicitly transitional rather than retroactively punitive**.[S80][S81]

## Default routing table

| Live problem | Default shape | Why this usually fits | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| tiny liability, trivial filing, or dignity floor | zero band / de minimis screen | screens out burdens not worth imposing or proving.[S31][S80] | do not let a floor line become a sudden loss of net income. |
| ordinary labor tax, rebates, and means-tested support | smooth taper or ordinary marginal band | protects work incentives and the floor better than a hard cutoff.[S31][S81][S82] | stacked withdrawal plus tax onset can still create a disguised cliff. |
| simplified small-firm, small-landlord, or presumptive regime | graduation window | crossing the line should migrate the actor, not punish the next dollar of scale.[S26][S80] | do not let the on-ramp freeze into permanent privilege. |
| stronger schedule above a threshold | marginal step-up on the slice above the line | preserves progression without retroactive bite.[S80][S81] | do not expose the whole base to a new burden at once. |
| permit, registry, or high-risk governance trigger | narrow hard cutoff, often with a short buffer | some duties are genuinely categorical and cannot be tapered forever.[S16][S17][S80] | do not smuggle ordinary extraction into a faux permit threshold. |

## Four design failures

- **cliff-edge poverty or pay-rise traps** — one more dollar, hour, or customer leaves the actor worse off.[S31][S81][S82]
- **threshold camping and splitting** — a line becomes a magnet for bunching, fragmentation, or timing games.[S26][S80]
- **retroactive step taxation** — crossing a line re-rates the whole base rather than the slice above it.[S80][S81]
- **hardship-by-appeal design** — the system keeps a known cliff and tells people to beg for repair later.[S31][S80][S82]
