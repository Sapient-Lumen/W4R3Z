# Immigration status, asylum, and benefit-fee floor routing

## Question in one sentence

When immigration agencies charge filing, asylum, parole, work-authorization, biometrics, appeal, or status-maintenance fees, how should the archive distinguish cost recovery from status ransom and humanitarian-access tolls?[S523][S524][S525]

Route: lawful status, protection claims, work authorization, and family unity are not luxury services. Fee design may recover administrative cost only after humanitarian exemptions, fee waivers, language/access support, channel pluralism, and non-refundability risks are tested.[S524][S525]

## Core rule

Immigration fees become tax-like when they condition legal personhood, labor access, safety, or family unity on cash. The base is not migration itself; the relevant questions are cost recovery, applicant floor, public benefit, employer/family beneficiary, and procedural access.

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| asylum or humanitarian filing fee | protection gate | exemption, waiver, or no-denial rule for inability. |
| online-only or card-only payment | channel gate | cash/check/assisted digital fallback. |
| employer-sponsored petition | beneficiary gate | employer-side charge; no worker pass-through. |
| annual or recurring fee while case pending | delay gate | no penalty for agency backlog; waiver and notice. |
| incorrect fee causes rejection | correction gate | clear fee schedule, grace, cure, and resubmission protection.[S523] |

## Anti-patterns

- **status ransom** — legal recognition or work access is priced beyond capacity.
- **humanitarian-access toll** — protection claims must pay to remain heard.
- **agency-delay rent** — recurring charges rise because the state has not adjudicated.
- **wrong-fee forfeiture** — opaque fees cause rejection or loss of priority.
