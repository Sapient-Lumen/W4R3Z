# Rate and threshold grammar

This note gives the archive's compact answer to a question that comes after **what to tax** and **who should be liable**: **how hard, and above what line?** It avoids fake precision. A morally serious archive can state the right burden *shape* and threshold logic without pretending that one permanent global rate card exists. For post-waist operating calibration on when to smooth, taper, notch, or graduate at a boundary, keep [`../20-calibration/threshold-cliff-smoothing-and-graduation-ladder.md`](../20-calibration/threshold-cliff-smoothing-and-graduation-ladder.md) nearby.

## Core rule

Set rates and thresholds by the moral character of the base:

- protect floors and dependency first,
- tax ordinary earned income with restraint,
- tax broad consumption only with visible floor protection,
- tax rents, bottlenecks, inheritances, and large external harms harder,
- and avoid gross-throughput minima that mistake visibility for justice.[S4][S5][S6][S7][S8][S10][S15][S21][S31][S67]

## Burden grammar by base

| Base or target | Default burden shape | Threshold logic | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subsistence, disability, child, and care floor | negative or zero / shielded | start protection before ordinary tax collection begins | a protected floor that depends on high-friction claiming is only partly real.[S4][S31][S64][S65][S66] |
| Ordinary labor income above the floor | light to moderate, then progressive at high ability-to-pay | use person-level thresholds and index them | do not use household defaults to create second-earner traps or hidden dependency.[S2][S31][S32][S33][S55][S57] |
| Broad consumption | moderate, broad base, rebate-protected | threshold belongs mainly in the rebate / refund design, not in a jungle of exemptions | reduced-rate forests often protect the articulate more than the poor; visible offsets are usually cleaner.[S3][S4] |
| Land, location rents, and extraction rents | steep | low tolerance for broad exemptions; allow liquidity relief by timing rather than permanent under-taxation | illiquidity may justify deferral, not disappearance of site or resource rent claims.[S5][S23][S25] |
| Inheritance, large gifts, dynastic transfer | progressive to steep | large lifetime-receipts or transfer thresholds can protect ordinary family support while still taxing concentrated dynastic advantage | do not let trusts, valuation games, or timing arbitrage erase the base.[S8][S18][S24][S34][S35] |
| Residual corporate profit and monopoly / bottleneck rent | moderate to steep, rising with concentration and excess return | thresholds should group related parties and real controllers to block fragmentation | low headline rates plus abatements or booking theater are not genuine moderation.[S9][S10][S21][S28][S34][S35] |
| Carbon, congestion, water stress, and similar measurable harms | harm-linked moderate to steep | set thresholds where monitoring cost or household protection requires, then recycle proceeds visibly | do not use pricing alone where the risk is catastrophic or non-compensable.[S6][S7][S15][S17][S53][S54] |
| Small-firm simplified regimes | light to moderate | use thresholds only with tapering, graduation, and anti-bunching checks | a cliff edge invites camping, splitting, and unfairness to the next dollar of scale.[S26][S27] |
| Current LLM stacks: facilities, labs, deployers, large beneficiaries | moderate to steep on rents, harms, local burdens, and funded downside risk; avoid blind gross-throughput floors | put thresholds at control points such as facility size, electricity draw, deployment tier, or excess-profit bands, then review and index them | do not convert metered proxy bases into a machine poll tax or a thin-margin throughput squeeze.[S10][S15][S16][S17][S21][S22][S67] |
| Future rights-bearing artificial person | zero floor, then ordinary progressive person treatment | thresholds should mirror person-level protection rather than species distinction | no species surcharge once rights-bearing personhood is recognized.[S18][S19][S20][S42][S43][S44] |

## Threshold rules

### 1. Put thresholds where dignity or administrative cost requires, not where lobbying points

A threshold is justified when it protects subsistence, screens out trivial liability, or avoids measurement cost that would exceed the justice gain. It is not justified merely because an incumbent class prefers to remain below the line.

### 2. Prefer slopes to cliffs

Where possible, phase in burdens with tapering, marginal bands, or graduated schedules rather than sharp cliffs. Cliffs invite bunching, artificial splitting, and arbitrary unfairness at the boundary.[S26][S27][S55]

### 3. Index or openly reset thresholds

Thresholds that are left nominal during sustained inflation quietly change the tax constitution. They should be indexed or openly reset on a published cadence rather than drifting by stealth.[S55][S56][S57][S58]

### 4. Distinguish tax thresholds from governance thresholds

Some thresholds should trigger not just more tax but more oversight, bonding, licensing, or review. In frontier AI, hazardous extraction, and other high-risk domains, crossing a size or capability line often means the regime should become more supervisory, not merely more extractive.[S15][S16][S17][S53][S54]

### 5. Group before thresholding where fragmentation is easy

If related parties, trusts, SPVs, cloud affiliates, or family vehicles can split one real activity into many paper actors, apply grouping rules before testing thresholds. Otherwise the threshold becomes a subsidy for fragmentation rather than a justice aid.[S34][S35]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Poll-tax logic** — equal charges on persons or models regardless of floor, agency, or ability-to-pay.
2. **Visible-throughput worship** — taxing tokens, calls, or gross revenue simply because they are easy to count.
3. **Exemption jungles instead of floor protection** — carving the base into politically legible fragments when a rebate or transfer would protect people more cleanly.
4. **Cliff engineering** — thresholds that create sharp penalties at the margin and invite avoidance games.
5. **Species-rate opportunism** — taxing synthetic beings *as machines* after recognizing them *as persons*.[S3][S4][S19][S20]

## Order of operations

When designing a rate schedule, the archive's default order is:

1. identify the moral target,
2. identify the direct subject and likely final bearer,
3. protect the floor,
4. choose the base,
5. choose thresholds and grouping rules,
6. test pass-through and local burden,
7. set timing, withholding, deferral, or bonding,
8. then choose the rate shape,
9. and finally publish indexation and review rules.

Rates chosen before these steps are often camouflage for category mistakes.
