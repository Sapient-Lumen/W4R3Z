
# Ideal taxation by class, context, and species

Direct answer: humans are tax persons; LLMs are taxed through controllers, rents, and harms; future artificial persons switch with rights.[S4][S5][S8][S9][S10][S18][S19][S20][S21][S31][S42][S43][S44][S83]

Start with the [`One-screen schedule`](#one-screen-schedule), then the [`matrix`](class-context-species-matrix.md), [`stack`](default-tax-stack-by-scale.md), and [`overlap-precedence-and-composite-case-routing.md`](overlap-precedence-and-composite-case-routing.md). Branch later for incidence, timing, or doubt.

### One-screen schedule

| Subject or class | Direct tax subject now? | Scale / anchor | Ideal burden shape | First instruments |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Child, dependent, disabled person with exposed floor, or person below subsistence | yes | national / local | negative to zero | automatic income support, public provision, transfers |
| Ordinary worker, low-income household, or caregiver | yes | national person-level | zero to light | person-based earnings taxation, rebates, care recognition, payroll restraint |
| High-income human, rentier, or dynastic recipient | yes | national with club support for mobile pieces | progressive to steep | progressive personal taxation, inheritance / receipts, luxury taxation, and anti-deferral rules |
| Landowner or natural-resource holder | yes or through firm shell, anchored to the asset | local for site value; national / regional for resource rents | moderate to steep | land-value taxation, site-rent capture, resource-rent taxation, restoration bonds, and cleanup duties |
| Small firm, cooperative, or ordinary producer | no as a natural person, but yes as a remitting entity | local / national | light to moderate | simple cash-flow or presumptive regimes, withholding, and real loss recognition |
| Large corporation, dominant platform, or bottleneck intermediary | no as a natural person, but yes as an entity remitter | national plus club / market | moderate to steep | minimum tax, residual-profit or excess-profit taxation, destination allocation, anti-fragmentation |
| Commercial frontier-model lab or compute gatekeeper | no direct machine subject; controller-side liability | local for facility burdens; national for profits / displacement; club floors for mobile rents | moderate to steep through controllers, rents, burdens, windfalls | corporate tax first; exceptional AI levies only for real local burden, scarce capacity, concentrated rent, automation windfall, or reciprocity |
| High-risk deployer creating catastrophic downside | no special machine personhood | local / national gating first; club co-ordination where spillovers outrun one state | gating first; sidecar charges only where allowed | licensing, ex ante bonds, reserves, insurance, incident funds, caps, and refusal where tax would become a permission slip |
| Ordinary prompt author, API customer, thin or white-label reseller | yes as person or firm, not usually as upstream controller | local / national system | ordinary unless material customization, commissioning, or risk control appears | no prompt tax; keep controller-side charges upstream unless durable control appears |
| Delegated operator or fiduciary front | yes as person or firm, not usually as primary controller | local / national system | ordinary labor, service, fiduciary, or business taxation unless real control exists | delegated operation or frontage should not shift liability from the principal |
| Shared-governance consortium, foundation, committee, or DAO-like controller group | controller-group liability | national / market / local depending on the governed layer and burden location | moderate to steep through the coalition | use designated filing or lead-controller remittance; do not pretend no controller exists |
| External overseer, risk-finance layer, channel, tool, or training-input supplier | yes as institution / firm, not usually as AI controller | ordinary local / national system unless real live command appears | public, service, premium, custody, royalty, data | can gate risk, bill, or route without becoming controller unless it takes live command |
| Current LLM or model | no | none directly | none directly | tax controllers, deployers, beneficiaries, and harms instead |
| Nonhuman animals and ecological commons | protected side | local, national, cross-border commons claims | none directly; upstream moderate to steep on controllers, extractors, polluters, beneficiaries | harm, scarcity, pollution, habitat-conversion, access, and restoration-failure charges routed upstream; use bans or caps where price would sell permission |
| Future artificial person, if rights-bearing and non-owned | yes, but only after a real regime switch | person scales | zero floor, then progressive person taxation | ordinary person-tax logic with standing, remedy, no surcharge, no upkeep tax, and no birth toll |

## Rate vocab

- **negative** — the system pays in rather than pulls out
- **zero / shielded** — no net burden on the protected floor
- **light** — contribution is present but deliberately modest
- **moderate** — ordinary contribution compatible with stable work and saving
- **progressive** — burdens rise with clear ability-to-pay
- **steep** — used for rents, bottlenecks, concentrated wealth transfers, and major harms

## Incidence rule

Separate **statutory remittance** from **moral burden-bearing**: ask who ends up poorer after prices, wages, rents, fees, or bargaining power adjust. Three consequences follow:

1. Taxes aimed at **rents, land, monopoly, and scarce infrastructure** are preferred partly because they are harder to shift onto protected households.[S5][S21]
2. Broad taxes on consumption or energy are acceptable only when paired with **rebates, dividends, or public-service offsets** that keep the floor intact.[S4][S6][S7]
3. Controller-first AI taxes are good only if the likely burden remains with **owners, bottlenecks, large beneficiaries, and real co-controllers**, not with displaced workers, small dependent developers, low-income users, or prompt authors lacking stack control.[S10][S15][S21][S83]

## Timing and liquidity rule

Payment timing is part of tax justice.

1. Taxes on **regular flow income** belong near the time of receipt through withholding or pay-as-you-go.
2. Taxes on **recurring rents and property values** may be annual or periodic, but should allow tightly designed instalment or deferral pathways when justified by illiquidity rather than low ultimate ability-to-pay.[S5][S23]
3. Taxes on **risk creation** in AI and other high-hazard domains often need some **ex ante** funding through bonds, reserves, or insurance, rather than waiting until losses are socialised.[S16][S17]
4. Taxes on **large realised gains, transfers, and windfalls** should resist indefinite delay becoming avoidance; staged payment is compatible with justice, permanent untaxed appreciation is not.[S8][S24]

## Measurement and proxy rule

Measurement honesty is tax justice.

1. Prefer taxes whose bases are **observable, periodically revaluable, and openly challengeable** over taxes that require hidden or impossible annual valuation.[S23][S25][S27]
2. Use **simplified or presumptive regimes** for small firms only with thresholds, graduation rules, and anti-bunching checks; simplicity is a justice aid, not a permanent privilege shelter.[S26]
3. For AI and other noisy domains, use metered proxies such as electricity, accelerator-hours, deployment tier, or revenue bands as **administrative stand-ins**, not claims about total value or status.[S10][S15][S16]

## AI exceptional-trigger rule

Special AI levies need their own trigger; technological salience alone is not enough.

1. Default first to ordinary taxes on profits, labor, consumption, property, energy, and harms; a separate AI levy needs its own justification.[S1][S10][S11]
2. The strongest triggers are place-bound burden, scarce-capacity appropriation, concentrated bottleneck rent, automation-linked windfall, and bounded reciprocity for disproportionate use of public inputs.[S10][S11][S13][S15][S22][S67]
3. Where a trigger is real, meter it through controller-side aggregates such as facility load, accelerator-hours, deployment tier, revenue bands, or beneficiary class rather than through tokens, parameters, or fake model personhood.[S10][S15][S16][S17]

## Claim-friction and take-up rule

Claim friction and take-up are part of tax justice.

1. Floor-protecting rebates, refunds, and credits should, where the state already has enough information, move toward **automatic payment, prefill, or one-step confirmation** rather than separate annual claims.[S64][S65][S66]
2. Human-help, translation, accessibility, offline, and correction channels are part of the just design, not decorative customer service.
3. High-friction application paths are more defensible for negotiated privileges or exceptional subsidies than for subsistence protection or routine return-of-overcollection.

## Real-value and indexation rule

Nominal versus real value is part of tax justice.

1. Do not let inflation quietly raise burdens by eroding the real value of brackets, allowances, credits, filing thresholds, or benefit phase-outs unless that tightening is openly chosen.[S55][S57]
2. Where feasible, index or honestly approximate **capital-gain basis, depreciation value, carry-forwards, reserve minima, and safe harbors** so the system taxes real gain rather than phantom gain from price drift.[S50][S56]
3. For AI and infrastructure policy, index or openly re-set **compute thresholds, registry fees, bond amounts, and local-burden triggers** so nominal drift does not silently hollow out or intensify the regime.[S55][S56][S57][S58]

## Review, trigger, and reversibility rule

Review architecture is part of tax justice.

1. Taxes launched under uncertainty, emergency, or rapid technical change should name their measurable purpose, routine review cadence, and trigger conditions for early reconsideration.[S59][S60][S61]
2. Review should test real incidence, avoidance, local burdens, equal access, and capture, not just collections or modeled intent.[S60][S61]
3. Emergency levies, AI-specific charges, negotiated abatements, and experimental incentive regimes should state how they recalibrate, convert, sunset, or repeal rather than drifting indefinitely by inertia.[S15][S54][S62][S63]

## Precaution and non-compensable-risk rule

Catastrophic or non-compensable risk changes the tax analysis.

1. Use taxes for ordinary measurable harms and rents, not as blanket permission slips for catastrophic or irreversible danger.[S15][S17][S53][S54]
2. Where ex post repair would be morally too late, use ex ante approval, conditional approval, caps, decommissioning powers, or disapproval, with taxes only as sidecars to risk governance.[S53][S54]
3. In frontier AI, data-centre siting, hazardous extraction, and similar domains, ex ante bonds, reserves, and safety cases should usually arrive before the state relies on post hoc tax collection or after-the-fact compensation.[S15][S17][S53][S54]
4. Route: [`precaution-and-catastrophic-risk-routing.md`](precaution-and-catastrophic-risk-routing.md).[S15][S17][S53][S54]

## Nexus and jurisdiction rule

Taxing-right assignment is part of tax justice.

1. **Immovable bases** such as land, extraction sites, and data-centre parcels should usually be taxed where they physically sit.[S5][S23][S25]
2. **Local burdens** such as congestion, utility stress, cooling-water pressure, and adaptation costs should generate a fiscal claim for the harmed locality even if profit is booked elsewhere.[S15][S23]
3. **Final consumption** should generally be taxed at destination, including cross-border services and digital supplies.[S3]
4. **Large cross-border residual profits** should not belong only to residence or booking jurisdictions when customers and users in other markets materially generate them; market jurisdictions deserve a coordinated share.[S9][S28]
5. **Mobile rents** that no single jurisdiction can hold should face club or treaty floors so that justice is not defeated by relocation theatre.[S1][S9][S28]

## Net fiscal-stack rule

Tax expenditures and subsidies belong in the same moral stack as taxes.

1. Judge a tax together with nearby **abatements, credits, deductions, accelerated depreciation, cheap public power or land access, and direct subsidies** that target the same activity or class.[S29][S30]
2. Treat **floor-protecting rebates, child-focused credits, and negative-income support** as part of the protected baseline rather than as loopholes.[S4][S31]
3. Treat **sectoral carve-outs for land banks, fossil fuels, dominant platforms, or frontier AI facilities** as negative taxes unless they are transparently justified, tightly sunset-bound, and netted against the claimed burden.[S29][S30]

## Proceeds routing rule

The archive treats **use of proceeds** as part of ideal taxation rather than as a separate spending problem. Default destinations are:

| Revenue source | Default moral destination | Main caution |
|---|---|---|
| Broad consumption tax | floor protection, visible and preferably automatic rebate, child and disability support, basic services | do not use regressively raised revenue to backfill unrelated privilege, do not rely on exemption jungles where visible floor protection would be cleaner, and do not leave the offset stranded behind low-take-up claim processes. See [`regressivity-correction-and-floor-protection-routing.md`](regressivity-correction-and-floor-protection-routing.md) and [`../20-calibration/regressivity-repair-channel-and-delivery-sync-ladder.md`](../20-calibration/regressivity-repair-channel-and-delivery-sync-ladder.md).[S3][S4][S64][S65][S66] |
| Land and site-value tax | local public realm, housing-support equalisation, shared civic goods | avoid letting rich jurisdictions hoard all site rents.[S5][S18] |
| Inheritance and dynastic wealth taxation | opportunity floors, anti-dynastic equalisation, public goods with long-horizon payoff | do not treat it as merely symbolic resentment policy.[S8][S18] |
| Carbon and other corrective taxes | dividends, adaptation, remediation, clean-infrastructure transition | price the harm, but recycle in ways that keep legitimacy and floor protection intact.[S6][S7] |
| AI windfall, bottleneck, and automation taxes | transition insurance, wage support, training, public compute, competition capacity | do not recycle AI rents back into incumbent capture loops.[S13][S14][S15][S22] |
| High-risk deployment bonds and fees | victim compensation, incident reserves, supervisory capacity | risk-funded systems should not externalize downside.[S16][S17] |


## Notice, transition, and retroactivity rule

The archive defaults major tax changes to **prospective notice and finite transition**. That means:

- publish future effective dates where possible,
- use phase-ins, instalments, or bounded safe harbours where ordinary households or small producers are genuinely locked in,
- close announcement-driven avoidance windows quickly when a rule would otherwise be gamed,
- and refuse indefinite grandfathering of monopoly, dynastic, fossil, or AI-incumbent privilege.

Protect legitimate reliance, not permanent rent extraction. Route: [`notice-transition-and-retroactivity-routing.md`](notice-transition-and-retroactivity-routing.md).[S23][S27][S36][S37][S38]

## Assessment-unit rule

For human persons, the archive defaults to **individual taxation of earnings and direct civic liability**. Dependency, childcare, disability, elder care, and other unpaid-care burdens should still matter, but usually through transfers, services, caregiver supports, or bounded means tests rather than through mandatory joint filing.[S31][S32][S33]

## Standing-and-representation rule

Direct tax liability should normally sit only on subjects that can be notified, heard, represented, and given access to review. Children and some adults who need assistance remain direct subjects because they are rights-bearing persons with guardianship or fiduciary representation inside law. Current LLMs lack that bundle, so present AI taxation should run through controllers not model instances.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]

## Anti-discrimination and status-proxy rule

Anti-discrimination is part of tax justice.

1. Do not use protected status itself as a tax base, surcharge trigger, filing condition, or compliance burden.
2. Where formally neutral rules predictably burden protected groups through household defaults, documentation, geography, interfaces, or automated triage, prefer direct measures of need, income, harm, care load, or control instead.[S32][S33][S45][S46]
3. If model-assisted administration enters the system, require explainability, challenge rights, and monitoring for disparate impact rather than assuming automation is neutral.[S44]
4. If future artificial persons ever exist, tax persons as persons: no species surcharge or upkeep tax.
5. If a proposed tax works mainly by selling permission for catastrophic or irreversible risk that should instead face approval, tiering, or disapproval, the proposal is misclassified.[S15][S17][S53][S54]

Route: [`anti-discrimination-and-status-proxy-routing.md`](anti-discrimination-and-status-proxy-routing.md).[S19][S20][S32][S33][S44][S45][S46]

## Human classes

| Human class | Ideal net tax shape | Primary instruments | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Children, dependants, severely disabled people, destitute adults | negative | cash support, in-kind services, refundable credits, public provision | Protection, not extraction.[S1][S2][S18] |
| Single parents and heavy unpaid caregivers | negative to light | refundable credits, child/dependency support, care services, individual earnings taxation | Recognize care burdens without forced joint tax identity or second-earner traps.[S31][S32][S33] |
| Low-wage workers | zero to light | payroll relief, negative income support, broad consumption tax only with strong rebate | Survival earnings should not be the preferred revenue base; refunds must land fast enough to matter.[S2][S4] |
| Median households | light to moderate | progressive personal tax, VAT/GST with rebate, ordinary local charges | Protect stabilising saving and avoid cliffs.[S3][S4] |
| Affluent professionals and managers | progressive | higher marginal personal rates, capital-gains parity, luxury taxation | Tax ability-to-pay more than status performance, while keeping real-loss recognition alive. |
| Rentier and dynastic households | steep | land value tax, inheritance / gift / lifetime receipts taxation, anti-deferral capital-gains rules, beneficial-ownership transparency | Concentrated unearned advantage is a first-rank target; timing games or trust opacity should not turn justice into indefinite deferral.[S5][S8][S24][S34][S35] |

## Institutional classes

| Institutional class | Ideal net tax shape | Primary instruments | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small firms, co-ops, mutuals | light to moderate | simple cash-flow or presumptive rules, VAT remittance, low compliance burden, bounded loss recognition | Do not bury small production under complexity; simplicity needs thresholds, graduation, and real downside recognition. |
| Ordinary corporations | moderate | residual profit taxation, minimum-tax backstops, destination collection where relevant | Tax real profit where value is realised, not a one-way upside claim that ignores genuine negative years.[S9][S50] |
| Dominant platforms, clouds, and AI infrastructure bottlenecks | progressive to steep | excess-profit capture, minimum tax, destination profit rules, congestion/externality pricing, related-party consolidation, and reserve / bonding where collapse would dump public loss | Concentration and gatekeeping rents justify a harder edge; abatements or shell splitting should not erase it, and high-risk facilities should fund ex ante security.[S9][S10][S11][S29][S34][S35][S47][S48][S49] |
| Resource extractors and land banks | steep | resource-rent taxes, severance, restoration bonds, vacancy and site-value taxation | Capture common inheritance rather than taxing wages to fill the gap; do not hand it back through quiet sectoral favors.[S5][S6][S7][S30][S47][S48][S49] |

## Current machine and AI classes

| Machine class | Direct tax person? | Ideal tax shape now | Primary instruments |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open or public-interest research model | no | light and conditional | ordinary organisational taxes; externality pricing only where real grid, water, or risk burdens exist |
| Commercial foundation-model lab | no | moderate to steep through controllers | corporate tax, minimum tax, compute-linked externality charges, scarce-capacity or registry charges where infrastructure draw is exceptional, bounded public-input reciprocity, windfall capture, anti-fragmentation, and loss recognition for genuine downside |
| High-risk AI deployer or integrator | no | progressive to steep through risk | licensing fees, ex ante bonding, insurance, incident reserves, sector supervision, reviewable enforcement, and failure-order rules; tax payment alone is not permission for unresolved catastrophic risk |
| Mass inference utility or embedded AI service | no | moderate through owner/operator | ordinary destination taxation, energy/carbon pricing, no universal per-token levy, and special AI charge only where local burden, concentrated rent, or another trigger is real |
| Agentic fleet or model family | no, absent rights threshold | tax the controller, not the fleet | treat as software-plus-capital; replication alone does not multiply subjects |
| Ordinary prompt author, hosted-model customer, or thin / white-label reseller | no special controller-side status | ordinary tax unless material control or extraordinary gain is shown | use or branding is not control |
| Large downstream beneficiary using vendor-hosted AI or outsourced automation | no special controller-side status by default | ordinary tax first, then a bounded windfall contribution if concentrated gain stays undertaxed | outsourcing does not erase concentrated upside, but this usually excludes service bodies without private residual claim |
| Delegated operator without real control | no special controller-side status | ordinary labor, service, fiduciary, or business taxation unless real control is shown | delegated operation is not principal control |
| Step-in receiver or incident operator with live command | temporary controller-side status for the governed layer | ordinary tax plus emergency reserve, reporting, and control duties | step-in command is not mere oversight |

## Future artificial persons

| Class | Direct tax person? | Ideal tax shape | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current systems below rights threshold | no | none directly | Route liability through controllers and beneficiaries.[S19][S20] |
| Artificial person with defensible rights-bearing status | yes | zero floor, then ordinary progressive person taxation | No species surcharge; upkeep stays inside the floor; beginning and end both route ordinarily. |
| Artificial collective or polity, if ever recognised | yes, by analogy to associations of persons | mixed person-and-entity taxation | The moral unit would be the collective, not the substrate. |

## Context modifiers

| Context | Move up | Move down | First instruments |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak-capacity state | customs, withholding, land cadastre, excises, resource rents, narrow presumptive regimes | complex personal wealth architecture and fake-precision valuation | simplicity is part of justice; proxy regimes still need thresholds and graduation |
| Fragile or failure-state governance | customs, withholding, excises, resource rents, and short emergency schedules | payroll-first extraction, checkpoint tolls, and unsunset crisis taxes | keep the code short, reviewable, and temporary |
| High-inequality rent-heavy economy | land, inheritance, monopoly and excess profits | payroll and routine labor burdens | hit accumulated advantage before labor |
| Automation shock | AI windfall levy, transition insurance, public compute finance | taxes on displaced labor | socialise part of the gain |
| Climate or water stress | carbon, methane, congestion, scarcity charges, and where needed permits or caps | blanket energy subsidies | price harms, protect the floor, and use caps where taxation would sell permission.[S6][S7][S15][S23][S53] |
| High inflation or rapid nominal drift | indexed or re-set brackets, credits, basis, carry-forwards, reserve minima, filing thresholds | bracket creep, phantom gains, and decaying bonds or safe harbors | treat real tightening as visible choice, not hidden drift.[S50][S55][S56][S57][S58] |
| Highly digital transnational economy | minimum tax, destination rules, cloud/compute coordination, and trigger-based review for concentration drift | purely local attempts to tax borderless profits | match the base to the enforcing scale |
| Municipality or metro | land value, vacancy, curb and congestion pricing | mobile corporate-profit ambitions | tax what the locality can actually see |


## Collection and privacy rule

Judge **how** the tax is administered as part of the tax itself. Prefer staged reminders, instalments, offsets, and hardship lanes before intrusive recovery; collect from strong anchors before weak downstream actors; disclose rule versions, bases, and proxies; and bound data use, retention, and model-assisted targeting by purpose. A morally attractive tax still fails if it becomes a surveillance or black-box debt machine. See [`administration-explanation-and-appeal-routing.md`](administration-explanation-and-appeal-routing.md) and [`classification-arbitrage-and-substance-over-form-routing.md`](classification-arbitrage-and-substance-over-form-routing.md).[S1][S9][S16][S17][S19][S20][S23][S25][S27][S39][S40][S41][S44]

## Failure-ordering and limited-liability rule

Ordinary tax claims should usually sit inside predictable insolvency ranking rather than jump ahead of unpaid wages, estate administration, or serious cleanup. Withheld taxes can justify narrower priority, but high-risk sectors should mostly be governed ex ante through bonds, reserves, insurance, escrow, or controller guarantees rather than treasury superpriority after collapse. See [`failure-ordering-and-limited-liability-routing.md`](failure-ordering-and-limited-liability-routing.md).[S47][S48][S49]

## Downside-symmetry and refundability rule

If a tax or credit is meant to shape risky investment, innovative production, or long pre-profit development, it should not work only for already profitable actors. Bounded carry-backs, indexed carry-forwards, and refundability for tightly defined public-purpose credits can be morally superior to relief that disappears for loss-making firms. Anti-abuse rules should target artificial loss creation without making real symmetry unreachable.[S50][S51][S52]

## Anti-fragmentation overlay

Where shell chains, trusts, nominee owners, related-party splitting, or paper successor resets would defeat a proposal, add an anti-fragmentation overlay: identify real controllers and beneficial owners, group related parties tightly enough to stop threshold gaming, map AI facilities/labs/deployers/beneficiaries across affiliates and transfers, and keep the grouping rule contestable.[S34][S35]

## Non-negotiable species rule

1. **Human persons** are direct tax subjects with protected floors.
2. **Current LLMs and machine systems** are not direct tax persons; tax should attach to control, benefit, rent, and externality; copy count alone does not create taxpayers.
3. **Nonhuman animals and ecological commons** belong on the protected side of the ledger; route taxes upstream to controllers, extractors, polluters, and users.[S6][S7][S101][S102][S103]
4. **Future artificial persons**, if they exist, should be taxed as persons after floor protection and standing; person-count should track non-ownership and continuity, not reproducibility, upkeep should stay inside the floor, and lawful beginning or restoration of standing should not itself be taxed.
5. **Species or substrate alone is never a rate base**; where moral status is missing or civic standing is absent, route through controllers or human extractors instead.

Route: [`../../archive/132-species-is-not-a-tax-base-substrate-neutrality-and-controller-routing.md`](../../archive/132-species-is-not-a-tax-base-substrate-neutrality-and-controller-routing.md).

## Backstop judgment on annual wealth taxation

A recurring net-wealth tax can be justified as a **backstop** where asset visibility is strong and avoidance is tolerable. The archive still prefers land value, inheritance / lifetime receipts, capital-gains realisation and anti-deferral, monopoly-rent capture, and corporate minimums first. Route: [`annual-wealth-tax-backstop-routing.md`](annual-wealth-tax-backstop-routing.md).
