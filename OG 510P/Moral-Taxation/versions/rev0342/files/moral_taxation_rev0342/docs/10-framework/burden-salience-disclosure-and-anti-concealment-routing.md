# Burden salience, disclosure, and anti-concealment routing

This note answers a recurring design question the archive already half-settled elsewhere: **when may a tax stay ambient, and when does hiddenness itself become a justice defect?** The archive's stable answer is that low-friction withholding or embedded collection can be acceptable when the burden is ordinary, the rule is reviewable, and the design does not depend on ignorance; but nontrivial burdens, negotiated support, or politically contested transfers need line-item, summary, registry, or budget visibility strong enough to stop concealment theater.[S27][S29][S30][S31][S68][S71][S77]

## Core rule

1. **allow low-salience collection only when the real subject can still see, reconcile, and challenge the burden without heroic effort**,[S27][S31][S68][S77]
2. **require stronger disclosure as burdens grow, vary, stack, or become politically contestable**,[S27][S31][S71][S77]
3. **make tax-side support, abatements, and negotiated public favors visible enough to judge the net fiscal stack honestly**,[S29][S30][S71]
4. **treat one-sided invisibility as a defect when extraction stays hidden but relief, rebate, or correction requires active navigation**,[S27][S31][S64][S65][S66]
5. **and reject designs that work mainly because the payer, beneficiary, or public cannot tell what is happening**.[S27][S29][S68][S71][S77]

## Default routing table

| Live problem | Default visibility lane | Why this usually fits | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| routine wage withholding or ordinary payroll contribution | ambient collection plus clear pay-stub or year-end summary | ordinary collection can stay smooth if the subject can still reconstruct the claim.[S27][S31][S68] | do not let code-heavy payroll opacity hide rates, thresholds, or floor protection. |
| broad consumption tax with household repair | shelf or invoice visibility plus simple rebate / transfer explanation and periodic summary | the burden is diffuse, so repair and total burden need visible pairing.[S31][S64][S65][S66] | do not keep the tax automatic while making the repair path legible only to experts. |
| platform fee, utility charge, local surcharge, congestion charge, or special levy | line-item or bill-side visibility | non-ordinary or variable charges should be inspectable close to the event.[S27][S68][S77] | do not bury meaningful policy choices inside one blended service price. |
| tax expenditure, negotiated abatement, discounted public input, or controller-side siting package | public budget or registry visibility | hidden negatives can cancel the headline tax and corrupt comparison across firms or places.[S29][S30][S71] | do not call a sector highly taxed while quietly repaying it through side deals. |
| controller-side AI levy or scarce-capacity charge | upstream collection may stay operationally quiet, but triggers, rate logic, and public-favor offsets need visible registry-grade reporting | technical collection can sit upstream without becoming invisible public law.[S27][S68][S71][S77] | do not let only the controller and the state know whether the exceptional levy is real. |

## Routing rules

### 1. Ambient collection is acceptable only when invisibility is not doing the moral work

The archive does not demand theatrical line items for every routine withholding or embedded remittance path. It does demand that the person or firm carrying the burden can reconstruct the claim, see the main rules, and reach correction without paid-expert dependence becoming the price of comprehension.[S27][S31][S65][S68][S77]

### 2. Extraction and relief should not have opposite visibility defaults

A design is normatively lopsided when tax is automatic and nearly invisible, but rebates, hardship relief, or correction require active claiming, repeated login, or specialist help. Hidden extraction plus high-friction repair is not administrative elegance; it is asymmetric salience design.[S27][S31][S64][S65][S66]

### 3. Support that changes the real burden must become publicly visible

Abatements, credits, discounted utilities, accelerated depreciation, siting favors, and similar negative taxes should not stay hidden merely because they travel through contracts, procurement, or the tax code. When they materially change the stack, they need budget or registry visibility strong enough for outsiders to judge whether the public is taxing, subsidizing, or both.[S29][S30][S71]

### 4. The more novel, variable, or manipulative the charge, the nearer disclosure should move to the event

Routine payroll withholding can tolerate more ambient collection than a new local surcharge, special levy, dynamic congestion charge, platform-side extraction, or variable controller fee. As novelty, variance, or political contestability rise, disclosure should move closer to the transaction, invoice, bill, or decision point.[S27][S68][S77]

### 5. Low salience is especially suspect where burden-bearing is weak-side or politically displaced

When the likely burden falls on low-income households, tenants, workers, small firms, or diffuse customers, hiddenness becomes more dangerous because it weakens both private correction and public contest. The archive therefore treats concealed burden-shifting and concealed support as first-order justice defects, not as cosmetic communication problems.[S27][S31][S64][S65][S66][S71]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **headline-minus-reality theater** — the published tax is visible, but the offsetting favor is not.[S29][S30][S71]
2. **silent extraction / noisy repair** — collection is automatic while refunds or relief require active expertise.[S27][S31][S64][S65][S66]
3. **bundle concealment** — meaningful public charges disappear inside one blended bill or platform price.[S27][S68][S77]
4. **registry secrecy for negotiated favors** — side deals change the stack without public auditability.[S29][S30][S71]
5. **salience-by-class asymmetry** — strong actors see and price the whole stack while weak actors see only the residue.[S27][S31][S65][S66][S71]
