## Core rule

Average incidence is not enough. The archive requires distributional slices by income, wealth, household type, region, protected proxy, and public-service interaction whenever a fiscal rule plausibly shifts burdens onto people with less bargaining power, less liquidity, or historically weaker access to public remedies.

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| average tax rate is the main defense | average-theater gate | require decile/quintile, wealth, household, and regional incidence. |
| protected status is not directly measured | proxy-blindness gate | use lawful, privacy-preserving proxies and qualitative evidence rather than declaring neutrality. |
| place bears infrastructure, climate, or service burden | place gate | show local incidence, proceeds, and repair before aggregation hides loss. |
| benefits and taxes interact | net-fiscal gate | combine taxes, transfers, user fees, and public services where data allow. |
| data are missing | evidence-humility gate | use conservative defaults, targeted review, and sunset rather than certainty theater. |

## Anti-patterns

- **average-tax theater** — mean burden hides tails, cliffs, and concentrated place losses.
- **proxy blindness** — refusal to measure protected effects becomes a neutrality claim.
- **decile-only justice** — income slices hide wealth, race/place, disability, care, and household structure.
- **data absolutism** — missing data are used to ignore a plausible burden.
