Route: The moral tax base includes the full fiscal stack. The archive requires comparable disclosure of direct spending, tax expenditures, debt service, guarantees, public-private commitments, and fiscal risks so that a burden is not laundered by changing the accounting label.[S565][S566][S567][S568]

## Core rule

The moral tax base includes the full fiscal stack. The archive requires comparable disclosure of direct spending, tax expenditures, debt service, guarantees, public-private commitments, and fiscal risks so that a burden is not laundered by changing the accounting label.

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| tax break substitutes for spending | tax-expenditure gate | estimate cost, beneficiary, incidence, sunset, and alternative direct-spending design. |
| deficit or debt finances current benefit | intergenerational gate | state who receives now, who pays later, and how interest risk is managed. |
| guarantee or PPP hides exposure | fiscal-risk gate | book contingent liability, stress test, and public backstop repair. |
| resource or windfall revenue is earmarked | resource-revenue gate | publish deposit, withdrawal, stabilization, and dividend rules. |
| budget documents omit accessible explanation | citizen-budget gate | plain-language budget, tax-expenditure table, debt/fiscal-risk statement, and audit follow-up. |

## Anti-patterns

- **fiscal illusion** — the burden disappears because it is off-budget, deferred, or labeled a tax preference.
- **off-book burden shift** — guarantees, PPPs, or public entities carry fiscal risk without budget visibility.
- **debt camouflage** — current groups consume benefits while future taxpayers absorb interest and rollover risk.
- **tax-expenditure exceptionalism** — a subsidy escapes scrutiny because it is written into the tax code.
