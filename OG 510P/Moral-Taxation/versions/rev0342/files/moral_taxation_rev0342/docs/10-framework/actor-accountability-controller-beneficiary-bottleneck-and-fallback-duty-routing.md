# Actor accountability: controller, beneficiary, bottleneck, remitter, and fallback-duty routing

A route is not complete when it names the legal taxpayer, the instrument, or the apparent incidence. It must say who had practical control, who benefited, who held the channel bottleneck, who merely remitted or processed, who bears the burden, and who must provide a public fallback when a private rail fails.

## Core rule

Assign responsibility by **power plus duty plus benefit plus remedy capacity**, not by title alone. A person or institution may be a taxpayer, remitter, gatekeeper, beneficiary, controller, fiduciary, contractor, or public fallback provider. Those roles can overlap, but they must not be silently collapsed.

## Accountability chain

| Question | Route consequence |
|---|---|
| Who could prevent, collect, account for, or pay the charge? | Treat as a controller/remitter candidate; require authority evidence and appeal. |
| Who receives the rent, subsidy, backstop, exemption, or pass-through benefit? | Route burden, clawback, or proceeds conditions to the real beneficiary. |
| Who controls access to a necessary channel? | Add fallback-channel, fee cap, portability, and no-forfeiture guardrails. |
| Who is only a conduit, processor, payroll vendor, or interface? | Do not make them primary unless they have disbursement control, contractual duty, or willful failure. |
| Who is the protected burden bearer? | Repair must reach that person, not merely the statutory remitter. |
| What public institution owns the fallback duty? | The state cannot outsource the floor and then treat private-channel failure as taxpayer default. |

## Trust-fund and remitter analogy

Trust-fund tax rules show the useful distinction. Responsibility can follow the person with the duty and power to collect, account for, and pay trust taxes, but it should not become title-based guilt or automatic liability for every payroll-adjacent actor. The IRS frames the Trust Fund Recovery Penalty around responsible-person status plus willfulness, and Publication 15 anchors employer withholding, depositing, reporting, and payment duties.[S322][S324][S667]

## Anti-patterns

- **Actor smearing:** every adjacent actor is treated as responsible because the real controller is hard to reach.
- **Beneficiary invisibility:** the person receiving the rent, subsidy, or protection gap is absent from the remedy.
- **Conduit punishment:** a remitter or processor is made primary without power, duty, benefit, or willfulness.
- **Public fallback erasure:** the public body mandates a private channel but disclaims responsibility when the channel blocks access.
- **Title finality:** officer, owner, vendor, platform, or taxpayer labels substitute for a control record.

## Output requirement

Every answer should name: primary accountable actor, secondary accountable actors, non-responsible adjacent actors, real burden bearers, beneficiary/rent recipient, bottleneck actor, default liability move, fallback public duty, evidence required, and anti-misattribution rule.
