# Necessary private rail and channel-incidence routing

This note answers the question that comes **before** ordinary incidence analysis when the tax system uses a private channel: **is the burden or remedy passing through a bank, wallet, payroll processor, tax software vendor, insurer, telecom carrier, app store, cloud, platform, certification body, landlord, utility, or payment processor that people cannot realistically avoid?** A private rail can be a useful collection or delivery tool, but it must not become the only doorway to refunds, filing, payment, notices, appeals, benefits, or public repair.[S21][S27][S41][S64][S65][S66][S443][S444][S648]

## Core rule

Before choosing the final taxpayer, base, or rate, ask six channel questions:

1. who controls access to the rail,
2. who can pass through cost or delay,
3. who lacks a realistic exit,
4. whether a public, offline, assisted, or interoperable fallback exists,
5. whether the rail is using legal classification to avoid ordinary duties,
6. and whether the remedy should be price, rebate, duty, public option, fallback channel, or prohibition.

If the burdened person cannot reach the public claim without the private rail, channel design is part of tax justice rather than implementation detail.

## Rail taxonomy

| Rail | Common moral risk | First remedy |
|---|---|---|
| bank, direct deposit, wallet, payment processor | refund delay, payout skim, bankless exclusion, rejected-payment freeze | fallback channel, no-rent reissue, assisted correction |
| tax software, preparer, filing vendor | captive filing rent, eligibility screen, ghost-preparer or AI-agent error | public filing path, accountable signer, correction shelter |
| telecom carrier or utility bill | line-item surcharge, markup, shutoff, service degradation | affordability credit, markup bar, service condition |
| insurer or residual market | premium shock, policyholder assessment, coverage withdrawal | floor aid, mitigation, transparent assessment, buyout |
| app store, platform, marketplace, payroll processor | private toll on public benefit, status laundering, dashboard-only proof | public fallback, nondiscrimination, portable records |
| cloud, identity, certification, audit vendor | conformity toll, credential lock-in, source-of-truth capture | equivalent evidence, open schema, appeal, vendor independence |

## Decision output

Name the rail, the rail controller, the trapped group, the pass-through mechanism, the public fallback, and the review trigger. Then route to the specialized ladder: refund/payment rails, filing/preparer rails, telecom surcharge, insurance backstop, platform benefits, digital gatekeepers, certification, or release integrity.

Rev0293 adds one required handoff: identify the **access-rent beneficiary** separately from the public owner and the user. For tax-administration access routes, the answer must say whether the beneficiary is a preparer, tax-software partner, identity vendor, bank, wallet, processor, court collector, reporter, setoff recipient, escrow holder, or agency/program fund retaining taxpayer cashflow.

## Anti-patterns

- **necessary-channel toll** — a person must pay a private intermediary to exercise an ordinary tax duty or claim.
- **fallback fiction** — an exception exists on paper but is too slow, inaccessible, or discretionary to protect the floor.
- **rail-controller capture** — the intermediary controls records, eligibility, payment, correction, and appeal at once.
- **classification seam arbitrage** — the rail calls itself finance, software, telecom, insurance, or benefits to avoid the duty it performs.
- **availability incidence** — the real burden is denial, delay, withdrawal, or degradation rather than a visible price.
