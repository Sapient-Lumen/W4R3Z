# Court fines, fees, ability-to-pay, and civil-access routing

## Question in one sentence

When courts, municipalities, or agencies impose fines, court costs, filing fees, surcharges, warrants, license holds, or collection add-ons, how should the archive distinguish lawful sanction or access-cost recovery from revenue extraction and debtors-prison traps?[S515][S516][S517][S538]

Route: treat legal financial obligations as coercive fiscal instruments. They need public-purpose clarity, ability-to-pay review, waiver or sliding scale, willfulness findings before nonpayment sanctions, and uncoupling from court or police operating budgets.[S515][S517]

## Core rule

A fine may punish culpable conduct, and a filing fee may ration scarce adjudicative resources, but neither may be designed as a hidden tax on poverty or a toll on access to court. Nonpayment is not defiance until ability to pay, notice, alternatives, and willfulness have been tested.

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| city or court depends on fine/fee revenue | revenue-conflict gate | uncouple budgets and report proceeds. |
| low-income person faces fees, costs, or surcharges | ability-to-pay gate | waiver, reduction, sliding scale, or community alternative. |
| civil filing fee blocks a claim | access gate | in forma pauperis, fee waiver, or no-pay filing path.[S538] |
| nonpayment triggers jail, warrant, license hold, or collections | willfulness gate | hearing and written finding before coercive sanction.[S517] |
| collection add-ons exceed principal or floor capacity | compounding gate | cap, cancel, or convert to non-monetary compliance. |

## Anti-patterns

- **revenue trap** — the court or municipality funds ordinary operations from people least able to pay.
- **debtors-prison rent** — inability to pay is converted into jail, warrant, supervision, or license punishment.
- **access toll** — a filing fee or transcript fee blocks the remedy needed to contest liability.
- **surcharge cascade** — small obligations become unpayable through add-ons, interest, and collection fees.
