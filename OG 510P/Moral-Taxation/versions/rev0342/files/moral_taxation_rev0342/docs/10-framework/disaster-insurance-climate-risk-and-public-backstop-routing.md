# Disaster insurance, climate risk, and public-backstop routing

## Question in one sentence

When flood, wildfire, wind, drought, heat, climate-risk maps, insurance withdrawal, public insurers of last resort, premium subsidies, mitigation grants, or relocation programs are at issue, how should tax and public finance protect households without subsidizing repeated avoidable loss?[S491][S492][S493]

Route: risk-based pricing improves honesty, but public policy must protect low-income and legacy households, fund mitigation, require transparent hazard maps, and move repeated-loss subsidies toward buyout, elevation, hardening, or managed retreat.[S491][S493]

## Core rule

The archive rejects both climate denial discounts and abandonment pricing. Disaster finance should expose risk without making survival insurance unaffordable, and it should not use public backstops to reward new construction or repeated rebuilding in known danger zones.

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| actuarial premium rises | affordability gate | means-tested voucher, mitigation credit, or phase-in. |
| repeated-loss property | moral-hazard gate | buyout, elevation, relocation, surcharge, or redevelopment limit. |
| hazard map changes | notice/reliance gate | transition, appeal, disclosure, and adaptation funding. |
| insurer withdrawal | market-failure gate | public insurer, reinsurance backstop, or risk pool with conditions. |
| new risky development | land-use gate | no subsidy; require full risk price, bond, or prohibition. |
| disaster aid after loss | recovery gate | fast floor aid plus long-term mitigation and relocation option. |

## Anti-patterns

- **moral-hazard subsidy** — public insurance hides risk and encourages repeated avoidable loss.
- **retreat ransom** — residents are priced out without relocation, buyout, or adaptation support.
- **map shock** — new hazard data creates sudden unaffordable obligations without notice.
- **insurer exit externality** — private withdrawal shifts losses to taxpayers without risk discipline.
