# Instrument choice and non-tax alternatives routing

This note answers the question that comes **before** a proposal counts as tax design at all: **what should taxation do here, and what should instead be done by transfers, public provision, explicit subsidy, bonding, or ex ante governance?** The archive's stable answer is to use taxes mainly for **raising broad revenue, capturing rents, and pricing measurable harms**, while using **transfers and services to protect the floor**, **public provision or procurement where the goal is shared capacity or access**, **bonds / reserves / insurance where downside must be prefunded**, and **approval, caps, or refusal where catastrophe or non-compensability makes tax-as-permit morally defective**.[S1][S2][S3][S4][S5][S6][S15][S16][S17][S18][S21][S29][S30][S53][S54][S64][S65][S66]

A morally serious archive should not ask one instrument to do five contradictory jobs at once. When the real target is protection, repair, capacity-building, or prior control, the answer is often **tax plus another instrument** or **not tax first**.[S2][S4][S15][S18][S21][S29][S53][S54][S64][S69]

## Default lanes

| Problem | First instrument | Why this path usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subsistence risk, care load, disability burden, or child dependence | transfer, negative tax, automatic rebate, or service provision | the moral target is protection and capability, not price discipline | do not hide floor protection in exemption jungles or high-friction claims.[S4][S18][S31][S32][S33][S64][S65][S66] |
| Broad revenue from mass consumption | broad tax **plus** visible rebate / transfer repair | final consumption is visible and collectible, but justice depends on paired floor protection | do not ask checkout exemptions to do the work of a real floor.[S3][S4][S21][S64][S65][S66] |
| Land rent, resource rent, monopoly rent, or dynastic transfer | tax, royalty, auction, or similar rent-capture instrument | the base is concentrated, socially created, or weakly earned | do not subsidize or quietly exempt the same base through abatements, cheap public inputs, or tailored loopholes.[S5][S8][S21][S29][S30] |
| Routine measurable pollution, congestion, or scarcity burden | corrective tax or charge with visible recycling | the harm is recurrent and measurable enough that pricing can change behavior and fund repair | do not use pricing where the main harm is catastrophic or irreversible.[S6][S7][S21][S69] |
| Local infrastructure stress from data centres, compute clusters, or other place-bound heavy facilities | permit, siting conditions, local-burden charge, and infrastructure contribution | the issue is partly revenue, but also local capacity and host-burden governance | do not let distant general revenue stand in for local repair or approval discipline.[S15][S23][S69][S70] |
| Liquidity mismatch on a morally justified base | timing relief inside the tax: instalments, deferral, liens, or realization-linked settlement | the base may still be right even when payment timing is wrong | do not treat liquidity strain as proof that the base should be exempt forever.[S23][S24] |
| Shared capability, basic access, scientific capacity, or public-interest diffusion | public provision, procurement, prize, or explicit on-budget support | the target is capacity-building or universal access, not burden extraction | do not smuggle industrial policy into hidden tax expenditures when direct spending would be clearer.[S1][S2][S29] |
| Downside that could outrun ordinary ex post collection | bond, reserve, insurance, escrow, or controller guarantee | prefunding can protect workers, victims, and the public before failure | do not substitute insolvency fights for ex ante security.[S16][S47][S48][S49] |
| Catastrophic, irreversible, or non-compensable risk | approval, cap, moratorium, or refusal, with taxes only as sidecars if the activity is allowed at all | taxation cannot morally compensate harms that are too large or too late to repair | do not sell permission through a levy.[S15][S16][S17][S53][S54] |
| AI-linked displacement, bargaining shock, or concentrated automation gains | controller-side windfall contribution **plus** transition finance, training, and floor protection | the moral target is concentrated gain and social adjustment, not the worker who lost bargaining power | do not tax displaced labor to fund its own repair.[S11][S13][S14][S22][S67] |
| A sector claimed to be highly taxed while simultaneously receiving large abatements, credits, or discounted public inputs | net-stack disclosure, subsidy removal, or on-budget conversion before adding new headline taxes | hidden negative taxes can erase the real burden or turn the code into opaque industrial policy | do not treat tax expenditures as free or morally invisible.[S29][S30][S59][S60][S63] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Name the job before naming the tax

Say whether the proposal is mainly trying to **extract revenue, capture rent, price harm, protect the floor, build capacity, prefund downside, or gate a danger**. If this cannot be said plainly, the design is probably mixing instruments without discipline.[S1][S2][S18][S21]

### 2. Use spend-side tools for protection and capability, tax-side tools for rents and harms

The archive usually protects people through rebates, transfers, services, childcare, disability accommodation, and low-friction delivery rather than by making the base itself illegible. Conversely, it usually addresses concentrated advantage or measurable externality through the tax side rather than by hoping an after-the-fact transfer will undo the underlying rent structure.[S3][S4][S5][S6][S18][S64][S65][S66]

### 3. Prefer explicit subsidy or procurement to hidden subsidy-through-the-tax-code

Some positive goals really do justify public support. When they do, the archive prefers visible spending, procurement, or narrowly drawn refundable support over sprawling tax expenditures that obscure cost, incidence, and capture. The point is not "never subsidize"; it is **never pretend a subsidy is not a subsidy**.[S1][S2][S29][S30]

### 4. Keep tax and governance analytically separate

A tax can sit beside a permit, reserve duty, competition rule, or shutdown power, but it should not be mistaken for those things. This matters most in AI, extraction, and other high-risk domains where paying a charge does not itself settle whether the activity should proceed.[S15][S16][S17][S53][S54]

### 5. Fix timing problems with timing tools, not base abandonment

When the moral target is real but the cash flow is lumpy or illiquid, the first response is usually instalments, deferral, liens, escrow, or realization-linked settlement. That keeps the liability legible without converting every hard-to-pay case into a permanent exemption.[S23][S24][S47][S48]

### 6. Keep the protected floor out of collection by design

Where a tax is otherwise justified but likely regressive in raw form, pair it with automatic rebates, transfer repair, service provision, or other low-friction protection. A design that is easy to collect and hard to survive is not rescued by calling the burden "broad based."[S3][S4][S21][S64][S65][S66]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Tax instead of transfer** — using reduced rates, exclusions, or carve-outs where the real goal is to protect the floor.  
2. **Tax instead of governance** — trying to price a catastrophic or non-compensable risk.  
3. **Tax instead of visible subsidy** — hiding industrial policy inside abatements, exemptions, or preferential public-input pricing.  
4. **Tax instead of prefunding** — waiting for failure and then improvising from insolvency or weak ex post collection.  
5. **Tax instead of justice in incidence** — charging the easiest visible remitter while letting the real concentrated beneficiary escape.[S4][S15][S16][S21][S29][S47][S48][S49][S53][S54][S59][S60]

## Compression rule

If a proposal can be fixed by saying **"keep the tax, but pair it with a rebate / service / reserve / permit"** or **"this should not be tax-first"**, do **not** add a new special-case framework note. Route through this card and the relevant companion note instead.
