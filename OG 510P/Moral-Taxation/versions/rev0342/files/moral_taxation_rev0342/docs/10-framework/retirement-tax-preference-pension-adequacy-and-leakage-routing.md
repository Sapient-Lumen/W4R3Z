# Retirement tax preference, pension adequacy, and leakage routing

## Question in one sentence

When retirement saving, pension credit, deferral, exemption, or saver-match rules are proposed, how should the archive distinguish old-age adequacy from upside shelter and leakage traps?[S623][S624]

## Core rule

Retirement tax support is justified by old-age adequacy, longevity risk, care-interrupted work, and broad participation. It fails when deferral and exemption concentrate at the top, shelter inheritances, impose high fees, or let short-term liquidity stress destroy retirement security.[S623][S624]

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| tax expenditure is large and skewed | distributional gate | cap or convert to refundable match if adequacy is not served. |
| workers lack participation | participation gate | use automatic enrollment, public option, or simplified plans with fee caps. |
| caregiver or woman loses pension accrual | care-credit gate | credit unpaid care and interrupted work. |
| saver withdraws early under stress | leakage gate | protect liquidity without fee spirals. |
| high-balance accounts accumulate beyond adequacy | shelter gate | review caps, RMDs, estate interaction, and dynastic lockup. |

## Anti-patterns

- **retirement halo shelter** — old-age language hides high-end tax deferral.
- **fee leakage** — tax support is captured by asset managers or plan administrators.
- **care gap denial** — pension design ignores unpaid care and interrupted work.
- **liquidity trap** — emergency withdrawals destroy adequacy through penalties and fees.
