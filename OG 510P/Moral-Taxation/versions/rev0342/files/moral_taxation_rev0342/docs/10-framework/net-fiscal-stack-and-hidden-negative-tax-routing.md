# Net fiscal stack and hidden negative-tax routing

This note answers the question that comes **after** a tax looks defensible in base, rate, subject, and jurisdiction but **before** anyone should trust the headline burden: **what sits beside the tax, and does that surrounding package quietly cancel, reverse, or redirect the claimed burden?** The archive's stable answer is that abatements, credits, accelerated depreciation, subsidised public inputs, and direct subsidies belong on the same moral ledger as explicit taxation. A proposal should therefore be judged on its **net fiscal stack**, not on the headline levy taken in isolation.[S4][S15][S29][S30][S31]

## Core rule

Route evaluation through this order:

1. **name the headline tax or charge**,
2. **net in nearby negative taxes and public favors**,
3. **separate floor protection from incumbent privilege**,
4. **prefer visible spending or contracts where support is genuinely justified**,
5. and **time-limit, cost, and review any remaining carve-out**.[S4][S29][S30][S31]

A regime that taxes with one hand and quietly subsidizes the same rent, bottleneck, or harm with the other has not solved the moral problem; it has hidden it.

## Routing table

| Tax base or context | What must be netted in before moral judgment | What usually counts as legitimate relief | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Broad consumption taxes with rebates | VAT / GST plus offsets, cash benefits, child support, and timing of delivery | visible floor protection that keeps low-income households whole enough | do not call a tax progressive because rebates exist on paper when non-take-up, delay, or inflation leave the protected floor exposed.[S3][S4][S31][S64][S65][S66] |
| Land value, property, and immovable-base taxes | abatements, frozen assessments, infrastructure giveaways, and utility concessions tied to the site | bounded deferral for genuine liquidity stress, hardship relief, and equalisation rules | local rent taxes can be morally gutted by negotiated abatements sold as development policy.[S5][S23][S25][S29] |
| Corporate profit, excess-profit, and monopoly-rent taxes | credits, accelerated depreciation, tax holidays, transfer-pricing concessions, and below-cost public inputs | transparent, finite support for real public-purpose investment where ordinary budgeting would also justify it | dominant actors often look highly taxed in statute while the net stack remains gentle or even negative.[S9][S10][S29][S30] |
| Carbon, pollution, congestion, and scarcity pricing | fossil-fuel support, utility discounts, offset giveaways, and untargeted energy subsidies | visible household cushioning, transition support, and adaptation spending | a corrective tax paired with broad incumbent support weakens both the price signal and the justice story.[S6][S7][S30][S69] |
| Current AI facilities, model labs, and data-centre clusters | property-tax abatements, subsidised land, discounted power or water, accelerated depreciation, R&D credits, and bespoke infrastructure deals | tightly bounded public-interest support that widens access or resilience rather than subsidizing concentration | a controller-first AI tax can be morally nullified if siting packages quietly repay the taxed actors through another channel.[S10][S15][S29][S30][S67][S70] |
| Temporary transition or industrial-policy support | grants, contracts, guarantees, tax credits, cheap finance, and procurement preference | visible, time-bounded public-capacity building with review and exit rules | if the public-interest case is real, hiding it in the tax code often weakens scrutiny and extends it by inertia.[S15][S29][S59][S60][S63] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Net the stack before you judge the burden

Do not ask whether the stated rate is high, low, fair, or progressive until the full package is visible. A tax can be morally serious in isolation and morally hollow in net once abatements, utility contracts, subsidised land, or hidden expenditure-side favors are counted in.[S29][S30]

### 2. Distinguish floor protection from privilege

Household rebates, child-focused credits, disability support, and negative-income transfers are not morally equivalent to investor abatements, sector holidays, or dominant-firm input discounts. The archive treats the first class as possible floor protection and the second as presumptively suspect unless they clear a visible public-purpose test.[S4][S31]

### 3. Prefer visible support when support is genuinely justified

If the real policy goal is transition help, public capacity, regional adjustment, or basic-service affordability, a direct appropriation, contract, or grant is often morally cleaner than burying the same choice in a tax expenditure. Visible support can still be bad, but at least the public can see and contest it.[S15][S29]

### 4. Make the net stack legible across scales

Local abatements, state utility contracts, national credits, and treaty-level tax rules can interact. The archive therefore treats the relevant object as the **whole stack across levels of government**, not whichever slice a single office prefers to highlight.[S15][S28][S29][S70]

### 5. Cost, sunset, and review every carve-out

A carve-out that cannot name its fiscal cost, purpose, review cadence, and exit path is usually not a temporary instrument but a hidden distribution rule. This applies with special force to negotiated data-centre deals, fossil-fuel support, and permanent investment-code exceptionalism.[S29][S30][S59][S60][S63]

### 6. Do not let hidden negatives launder catastrophic or concentrated power

Where a sector raises concentration, local burden, or catastrophic-risk concerns, the presence of a headline tax should not distract from the possibility that the public is still subsidizing the very capacity it claims to discipline. In such cases the archive asks not only whether the tax is high enough, but whether the surrounding public favors should exist at all.[S15][S17][S29][S30][S53][S54]

## Default order of suspicion

When hidden negatives are present, the archive generally treats them in this order:

1. **most defensible** — household floor protection and targeted anti-hardship relief,
2. **possibly defensible** — time-bounded public-capacity or transition support with visible purpose,
3. **presumptively suspect** — sectoral abatements, discretionary utility discounts, bespoke infrastructure deals, and broad investor-side tax holidays,
4. **least defensible** — support that deepens dominance, weakens corrective pricing, or quietly repays actors for risks or rents the tax was supposed to discipline.[S4][S15][S29][S30][S31]

## Anti-patterns

1. **headline theater** — the public sees the levy, not the offsetting favor.
2. **development blackmail** — localities are told to compete away the base to keep or attract a project.
3. **correction reversal** — a harm charge exists, but surrounding subsidies still encourage the harm.
4. **relief confusion** — floor-protecting household support is rhetorically merged with incumbent carve-outs so both survive political attack or defense together.
5. **stack opacity across scales** — each level of government points to another while the overall burden remains low or negative.[S29][S30][S31][S70]
6. **support by replacement** — a visible tax-funded benefit is announced while baseline spending or grants are cut elsewhere, leaving the net package largely unchanged.[S29][S71][S78]
