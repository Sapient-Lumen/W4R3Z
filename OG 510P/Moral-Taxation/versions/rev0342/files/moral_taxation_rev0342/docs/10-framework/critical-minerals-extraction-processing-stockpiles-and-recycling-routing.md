# Critical minerals extraction, processing, stockpiles, and recycling routing

## Question in one sentence

When a tax credit, royalty, stockpile purchase, permitting preference, or processing subsidy is justified by critical-mineral security, how do we prevent extraction rents, monopoly bottlenecks, and sacrifice-zone burdens?[S599][S600][S601]

## Core rule

Criticality can justify resilience spending, stockpiles, recycling, and processing capacity, but not blank-check extraction. The claim must separate national or energy-security need from private mineral rent, local environmental burden, worker risk, and processing bottleneck power.[S599][S600][S601]

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| supply concentration or export-control risk | security gate | test diversification, recycling, substitution, and stockpile options before mine subsidy. |
| new mine or processing credit proposed | community gate | require water, tailings, labor, Indigenous, and reclamation proof. |
| strategic stockpile purchase proposed | price-support gate | buy resilience, not producer bailout or artificial price floor. |
| deep-sea or frontier extraction appears | precaution gate | do not monetize irreversible ecological uncertainty as ordinary rent. |
| recycling or recovery credit proposed | circularity gate | prefer high-additionality secondary supply when it reduces extraction burden. |

## Anti-patterns

- **sacrifice zone** — criticality language overrides local water, health, labor, or Indigenous claims.
- **stockpile price support** — public buying props up private balance sheets without resilience.
- **processing monopoly** — midstream bottleneck captures security subsidy.
- **green-transition camouflage** — clean-energy need hides unrepaired extraction harm.
