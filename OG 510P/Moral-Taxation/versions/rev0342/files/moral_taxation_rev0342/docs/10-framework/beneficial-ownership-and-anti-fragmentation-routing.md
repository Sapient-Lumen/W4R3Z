# Beneficial ownership and anti-fragmentation routing

This note answers the question that comes **after** a tax base looks morally justified and **before** legal form, threshold gaming, or affiliate theatre can dissolve it: **when should multiple paper taxpayers be treated as one real economic actor?** The archive's stable answer is to group only as far as needed to follow real control, benefit, and coordinated avoidance, while refusing to turn anti-fragmentation into a license for erasing genuinely separate persons or firms.[S9][S10][S20][S21][S28][S31][S32][S33][S34][S35]

## Core rule

Route grouping through this order:

1. **identify the natural persons or genuinely independent collective that ultimately controls or benefits from the base**,  
2. **aggregate related entities, trusts, nominees, or affiliates when paper separation would defeat the chosen tax base or threshold**,  
3. **keep ordinary personhood and anti-avoidance aggregation distinct so household equality is not sacrificed to enforcement convenience**,  
4. **use the narrowest grouping rule that still defeats shell splitting, threshold camping, and controller laundering**,  
5. and **keep grouping reviewable, evidence-based, and reversible when the claimed unity turns out to be fictitious**.[S21][S28][S31][S32][S33][S34][S35]

A good anti-fragmentation rule follows the real economic actor without pretending every family, contractor network, or supply chain is one taxpayer.

## Default grouping anchors by base or context

| Base or context | Best first grouping anchor | Why this anchor usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary human earnings | usually **no** aggregation beyond the individual person | ordinary labor liability is person-based, and household pooling assumptions are morally hazardous | do not smuggle compulsory jointness back in through anti-avoidance rhetoric.[S31][S32][S33] |
| Household wealth, trusts, gifts, and dynastic vehicles | beneficial owner, settlor / transferor, controlling trustee, protector, or dominant beneficiary map | dynastic control often hides behind formal trusteeship or layered family entities | do not let nominal fiduciaries or wrappers launder one family's continuing control into many fake taxpayers.[S8][S18][S34][S35] |
| Small-firm thresholds, presumptive regimes, and local-business relief | common-control anti-splitting rule | subdivision is an easy way to farm favorable thresholds | simplicity regimes should shelter genuinely small actors, not paper fragments of one larger business.[S21][S26][S27][S34][S35] |
| Large corporate groups and cross-border profit taxation | group accounts plus related-party and beneficial-ownership tracing | profit shifting and booking theatre usually run through affiliate networks rather than isolated entities | do not let invoice paths or transfer-pricing stories decide who the real taxpayer is.[S9][S21][S28][S34][S35] |
| Land banks, extractive webs, and immovable portfolios held through SPVs | controller chain across nominees, holding companies, and site vehicles | immovable bases are easy to wrap in many legal boxes while keeping one economic command structure | do not mistake property-form multiplication for real decentralization of benefit or control.[S5][S25][S34][S35] |
| Current AI stack split across landlord, utility, cloud, lab, deployer, and commercial beneficiary affiliates | controller map across the full stack | AI infrastructures are easy to fragment legally while remaining one coordinated rent / risk machine | avoid logo maps; identify who funds, controls, insures, directs, and captures the gains from the stack.[S10][S15][S16][S17][S20][S34][S35] |
| Controller-side and beneficiary-side AI gains split across related affiliates in one coordinated group | group-level control-and-benefit map before choosing the levy lane | one group can otherwise claim the controller sits elsewhere while the beneficiary sits elsewhere, leaving both lanes artificially thin | do not let vertical separation or transfer-pricing stories dissolve one coordinated surplus into nobody's windfall.[S9][S10][S20][S21][S28][S34][S35][S83] |
| Consortium-, foundation-, committee-, or DAO-like governed stack with no single dominant owner | governing coalition, voting control, treasury / reserve control, and stop / continue rights | distributed governance can be used to create a fake controller vacuum even when a real coalition still governs the live stack | do not let split command dissolve controller duties into nobody; use group accountability or designated filing where needed.[S20][S21][S34][S35][S41] |
| Future rights-bearing artificial person | no owner-side grouping once personhood and standing are real | once an artificial system is a rights-bearing subject, owner aggregation becomes domination rather than anti-avoidance | do not preserve property logic after personhood has displaced ownership.[S19][S20][S42][S44] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Group only where fragmentation defeats the chosen base

Grouping is not a moral good in itself. Use it where trusts, subsidiaries, nominees, SPVs, family vehicles, or AI-stack affiliates would otherwise split one tax base into many paper bases or many threshold-qualifying fragments.[S21][S34][S35]

### 2. Keep anti-avoidance aggregation distinct from ordinary human personhood

The archive can aggregate family wealth, coordinated gifts, or related-party businesses for anti-avoidance while still refusing compulsory joint taxation of ordinary earnings. Household anti-avoidance is not the same thing as household civic personhood.[S31][S32][S33]

### 3. Follow control, benefit, and coordinated risk-bearing, not just invoices

The real economic actor is often revealed by who can direct strategy, extract gains, absorb losses, pledge assets, move IP, dictate pricing, or decide whether the activity continues at all. That matters more than whichever affiliate sends the bill.[S10][S20][S28][S34][S35]

### 4. Use the lightest rule that still defeats paper splitting

Prefer disclosure, registry truth, common-control thresholds, group reporting, or rebuttable presumptions before universal consolidation. Over-aggregation can itself become domination or a barrier to entry for genuinely separate actors.[S21][S26][S27][S34][S35]

### 5. Group split-stack controller and beneficiary stories before lane choice

Where one coordinated group places controller-side command in one affiliate and concentrated downstream gain in another, screen the group-level facts before deciding that only a beneficiary-side levy or only a controller-side levy is morally available. The archive may still choose one lane after screening, but it should not let related-party choreography make each affiliate look too thin to tax.[S9][S10][S20][S21][S28][S34][S35][S83]

### 6. Keep grouping contestable and legible

If the state treats several entities or persons as one anti-avoidance unit, it should name the rule, identify the facts, and preserve a path for review, correction, and separation where common control is asserted too broadly.[S21][S27][S34]

### 7. Map AI stacks by control chain rather than by logo chain

For AI facilities and deployment chains, ask who owns the site, who holds the utility contract, who controls the accelerator or cloud allocation, who trains, who deploys, who finances the reserve or bond, and who captures the rents. Site ownership, metering, lending, or hosting is relevant, but not conclusive: the harder question is who can materially allocate scarce capacity, veto deployment, or set the risk posture for the stack. This is the level at which controller-first taxation survives affiliate theatre.[S10][S15][S16][S17][S20]

### 8. Collective governance can still be a real controller group

Where a consortium, foundation, steering committee, multisig, or DAO-like coalition can jointly pause, continue, update, finance, or economically direct the live stack, treat that coalition as a real controller group rather than a controller vacuum. Use the lightest workable remittance rule — designated filer, treasury remitter, lead controller, or joint-and-several filing — but keep substantive duties attached to the governing coalition.[S20][S21][S34][S35][S41]

### 9. Revisit grouping when facts change

Spin-offs, genuinely independent governance, broken control links, and changed beneficial ownership should loosen grouping. Anti-fragmentation should stop paper splitting, not permanently freeze yesterday's corporate map.[S21][S34][S35]

### 10. Distinguish anti-fragmentation from third-party collection shifts

Anti-fragmentation asks whether several paper boxes are really one controller or beneficiary map for measuring the tax base. Transferee, nominee, alter-ego, successor, and wrongful-levy questions ask a narrower collection question: whether this outsider, property item, transferred value, or successor interface can be reached for someone else's liability. The archive keeps the two related but separate. Beneficial-ownership opacity can justify stronger tracing, disclosure, grouping, or special-condition lien review, but it should not let collection jump from a real taxpayer to every nearby solvent title holder, payroll contact, customer, bank, relative, or purchaser without a theory-matching proof packet.[S34][S35][S39][S41][S42][S330][S331][S332][S334][S336]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Household blobbing** — using anti-avoidance language to erase individual earners into compulsory jointness.  
2. **Invoice worship** — assuming the contracting entity is the real taxpayer even when control and benefit sit elsewhere.  
3. **Threshold camping by subdivision** — multiplying entities only to stay under simplified-regime or rate thresholds.  
4. **Logo-map AI analysis** — treating a branded stack as many independent taxpayers because each layer sits in a different affiliate.  
5. **split-stack innocence** — parking controller command and concentrated downstream gain in sister affiliates so each claims the other is the real target.  
6. **Universal consolidation mania** — collapsing genuinely distinct actors into one group because grouping is easier to administer.[S9][S10][S20][S21][S28][S31][S32][S33][S34][S35][S83]

## Order of preference

When more than one grouping method is available, the archive usually prefers the one that is:

1. most closely tied to real beneficial ownership or control,
2. easiest to verify through existing registry, filing, or disclosure records,
3. narrow enough to avoid swallowing genuinely separate actors,
4. strong enough to defeat threshold gaming and shell splitting,
5. clearest about the boundary between anti-avoidance grouping and person-level civic status,
6. and easiest to revisit when ownership or control genuinely changes.

This is why beneficial-ownership tracing, related-party definitions, common-control anti-splitting rules, group reporting, designated-filer or lead-controller rules for real controller groups, and rebuttable controller presumptions usually beat both naive entity formalism and indiscriminate universal consolidation.
