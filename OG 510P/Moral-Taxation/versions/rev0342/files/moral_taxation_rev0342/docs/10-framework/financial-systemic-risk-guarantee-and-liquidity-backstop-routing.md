# Financial systemic-risk, guarantee, and liquidity-backstop routing

## Question in one sentence

When banks, nonbank financial intermediaries, exchanges, stable-value instruments, insurers, clearing systems, or market utilities benefit from explicit guarantees, implicit rescue expectations, central-bank liquidity, deposit insurance, resolution regimes, or crisis support, how should the archive separate ordinary finance from public-loss/private-upside rents?[S453][S454][S455]

Route: do not tax finance merely because it is finance. Tax or charge the public guarantee, runnable-liability risk, leverage externality, liquidity backstop, resolution cost, crisis support, and excess return created by public risk-bearing.[S453][S454]

## Core rule

Financial-sector taxation is justified when private actors receive value from public stability infrastructure or create systemic externalities that ordinary market prices do not carry. The base should track the public backstop and risk amplification, not gross transactions by reflex.

## Backstop ledger

Record at least:

- institution type, perimeter status, resolution authority, and systemic designation;
- insured deposits, guaranteed liabilities, runnable short-term funding, margin chains, collateral reuse, and stable-value promises;
- central-bank facilities, lender-of-last-resort eligibility, emergency programs, deposit-insurance fund exposure, and treasury guarantees;
- leverage, liquidity mismatch, maturity transformation, concentration, complexity, and cross-jurisdiction activity;
- links to households, SMEs, municipalities, pensions, insurers, clearing systems, payments, and critical markets;
- crisis aid, forbearance, asset guarantees, loss sharing, special facilities, or exceptional supervisory relief;
- dividends, buybacks, compensation, related-party transfers, and creditor payouts around support periods;
- proceeds route: resolution fund, deposit-insurance repair, public backstop repair, household/small-business credit continuity, or clawback.

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| ordinary lending without public backstop rent | ordinary income/rent stack | no special finance levy. |
| explicit deposit or liability guarantee | guarantee-benefit gate | risk-based premium, insurance contribution, or fund repair charge. |
| implicit too-big-to-fail value | systemic-risk gate | surcharge, resolution-fund contribution, capital/reserve rule, or clawback. |
| runnable nonbank liquidity chain | NBFI perimeter gate | disclosure, leverage/liquidity charge, margin/reserve rule, or non-tax regulation. |
| crisis support used | public-loss gate | support-conditioned clawback, compensation restraint, creditor haircut, or windfall recapture. |
| transaction tax proposed | incidence and market-function gate | reject if it impairs useful liquidity without capturing guarantee/risk rent. |

## Anti-patterns

- **public-loss/private-upside trap** — losses are socialized while dividends, bonuses, buybacks, and creditor gains remain private.
- **gross-transaction theater** — a financial transaction tax is used because it is visible, even though the moral base is guarantee value or systemic risk.
- **shadow-perimeter leak** — bank-like runnable liabilities migrate to nonbank entities without equivalent risk pricing.
- **bailout amnesia** — crisis aid is treated as history rather than a claim on future surplus.
