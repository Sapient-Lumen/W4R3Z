# Education, human capital, student debt, and credential-rent routing

## Question in one sentence

When tuition, grants, student loans, training credits, institutional tax preferences, and credential claims are at issue, how should taxation and public finance expand capability without subsidizing debt peonage or credential rents?[S503][S504]

Route: treat education finance as public capability and intergenerational infrastructure; support access through grants, income-contingent repayment, debt relief, training investment, and accountability, while refusing tax subsidies that capitalize into high tuition, low-quality credentials, or loan-servicing traps.[S503]

## Core rule

The student is not a rent source. Public finance may ask high lifetime earners to contribute back, but front-loaded debt, opaque quality, non-dischargeable distress, and tax subsidies to institutions require strict review. The base should be realized ability and institutional surplus, not hope, parental wealth, or credentials with weak labor-market value.

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| high tuition with weak completion or job outcomes | credential-rent gate | subsidy condition, disclosure, clawback, or price/quality control. |
| student debt distress | liquidity/floor gate | income-contingent relief, discharge, or payment pause. |
| broad public-skill need | capability gate | grants, training credits, and public provision before debt. |
| tax deduction/credit benefits high earners | incidence gate | retarget toward need, access, or completion. |
| aid decline replaced by household debt | public-finance gate | restore public finance before private loan expansion.[S504] |

## Anti-patterns

- **debt peonage** — human-capital investment is financed by long-term distress without realistic repayment capacity.
- **credential rent** — tax support raises prices or credentials without capability or labor-market value.
- **aid-cut substitution** — private debt or tax credits replace adequate public education finance.
- **servicer trap** — relief exists on paper but is lost through complex paperwork or errors.
