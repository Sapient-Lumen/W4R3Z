# Third-party reporting, correction, and bounded recipient-shelter routing

Use this card when the live tax dispute is not mainly about the rate, base, or threshold, but about **what should happen when liability or relief is being driven by a third-party statement, feed, meter, platform ledger, payroll report, account report, or controller-side packet that the affected person did not author and cannot fully control**. The archive's stable answer is that **upstream reporters must carry the main correction duty for their own reporting errors, weak recipients should usually get a usable counterstatement lane without having to reconstruct the whole upstream record, and collection or denial should stay issue-bounded while a material reporter-side mismatch is genuinely live**.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S42][S65][S66][S68][S83][S89]

This is not a special payroll rule or an AI-only rule. It applies wherever tax administration leans on third-party statements because they are efficient: wages and withholding, platforms and marketplaces, banks and payment rails, utilities and burden meters, registries and title systems, controller-side AI filings, or any other strong-record-holder report that may be partly wrong for the person downstream who must live with the result.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S42][S44][S65][S66][S68][S83][S89]

Archive note [`172`](../../archive/172-third-party-tax-reporting-errors-withholding-credit-and-information-return-correction-should-not-be-turned-into-mismatch-rents-or-reporter-hostage-traps.md) closes the no-rent version of this rule. Missing or incorrect W-2 / 1099 information, mistaken 1099-K gross proceeds, W-2c corrections, issuer-side information-return corrections, free public 1099 filing tools, and information-return penalties all point the same way: the recipient may owe a concrete counterstatement, but the stronger reporting layer cannot turn its own error into paid downstream cleanup or reporter-hostage delay.[S154][S155][S156][S157][S158][S159][S160]

## Default lanes

| Situation | Default correction lane | Why this lane usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employer, pension payer, or platform reports pay, withholding, or gross proceeds incorrectly | issuer-side correction first, with recipient counterstatement and issue-bounded shelter while the mismatch is live | the reporter usually holds the operative books and can fix the shared record most cheaply | do not force the worker, retiree, or seller to disprove the whole upstream ledger before any protection starts.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S65][S66][S68][S83] |
| Bank, payment processor, marketplace, or other intermediary report is incomplete, duplicated, or attributed to the wrong person | strongest reporter or registry must cure or annotate first, while the recipient can contest the named field without open-ended discovery | third-party reporting is justified by legibility gains; those gains collapse if error cost is pushed entirely downstream | do not let a visible feed become unreviewable truth merely because it arrived electronically.[S27][S39][S41][S42][S44][S68][S89] |
| Utility, facility, meter, or local-burden record overstates use, occupancy, congestion, or extraction | operator-side re-check and narrowed provisional billing before final recovery | the operator already sees the meter chain, event logs, or allocation method | do not make affected households or small firms carry industrial measurement error as a cash-flow loan to the state.[S15][S21][S23][S27][S39][S41][S79][S83] |
| Controller-side AI packet, registry filing, or usage report appears to assign the wrong actor, volume, or duty | visible controller-side signer or remittance anchor must amend, annotate, or true-up first, with narrow provisional routing for the disputed field | the strongest visible actor is usually best placed to repair shared machine-facing tax facts | do not let packet convenience turn a stale or wrong controller-side report into automatic downstream liability for users or weaker counterparties.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S39][S41][S44][S83][S89] |
| Rival third-party statements conflict | hold only the genuinely disputed slice, reconcile at the strongest common anchor, and release the clear remainder | conflicting reports do not justify freezing the whole case if most of the base is already clear | do not treat every mismatch as a reason for full-period suspension, omnibus holdback, or universal refiling.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S42][S66][S68][S89] |
| False wage report, unauthorized return, stolen identifier, or compromised refund coordinate appears in a tax record | let the recipient give a concrete identity-compromise counterstatement while the administration and stronger reporter preserve and repair the disputed field | identity theft often creates official-looking third-party facts the victim did not author and cannot fully trace | do not turn impostor records into automatic income, waiver, refund denial, or penalty facts against the victim.[S21][S27][S39][S40][S41][S42][S65][S66][S148][S150][S151][S152] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Upstream issuers should fix upstream errors

If the state relies on employer, platform, bank, registry, meter, or controller-side reports because those actors are better placed to observe the relevant facts, the same design should usually place the first correction duty on those reporters when material error appears. Efficient third-party reporting loses its moral justification if all of the repair burden is shifted to the weaker recipient after the fact.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S65][S66][S68][S83][S89]

### 2. The recipient usually owes a concrete counterstatement, not a full rival reconstruction

A worker, tenant, household, small seller, or downstream user should normally have to identify the disputed field, period, amount, or attribution error and provide the smallest workable contradiction. They should not usually have to rebuild the whole payroll system, platform ledger, registry chain, or controller map before the administration pauses reliance on the contested field.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S42][S65][S66][S68][S89][S91][S92]

### 3. Live mismatch should narrow crystallization

Where a third-party statement is materially contested, default to **issue-bounded shelter**: protect the recipient against full collection, full denial, or full sanction on the disputed slice while allowing the clear remainder to proceed. The archive rejects a crude choice between blind automated reliance and complete administrative freeze.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S42][S66][S68][S83]

If the mismatch plausibly comes from identity compromise, shelter should begin before the victim reconstructs the whole fraud path. The disputed return, wage report, refund coordinate, or authorization is a live record problem to investigate, not an immediate admission by the named subject.[S21][S27][S39][S40][S41][S42][S65][S66][S148][S150][S151][S152]

### 4. Corrections should propagate by reuse, not by repeated manual refiling

Once a reporter-side correction is accepted, it should update the shared tax fact where possible rather than forcing every affected downstream person to reopen the same mismatch separately. Reuse, portability, and delta updates apply here too: the cure for bad third-party reporting is not a new maze of repeated claims.[S27][S39][S41][S42][S68][S89][S91][S92]

### 5. Repeated or industrialized bad reporting should harden upstream, not merely downstream

Where a preparer, payroll firm, marketplace, platform, bank, controller group, or other upstream actor repeatedly generates false or stale reports at scale, sanctions, audit attention, or mandatory process repair should move upstream. A system that notices industrialized reporter error but keeps collecting only from weak recipients is mis-targeted.[S27][S39][S41][S42][S44][S83][S98]

### 5.5 Automated underreporter notices should not convert reporter feeds into final truth

CP2000 / CP2501-style notices are useful when they show the mismatch and invite a bounded response. They become reporter-hostage machinery when the standardized feed is treated as conclusive, the proposal is mistaken for a bill, or the recipient must buy help merely to compare the line item, attach documents, or preserve appeal / deficiency rights.[S154][S155][S156][S157][S158][S225][S226][S227][S228][S229]

### 5.6 Nonfiler reconstruction should not make reporter feeds conclusive

When a nonfiler case is built from W-2, 1099, platform, bank, or comparable reports, the reporter feed remains evidence, not final truth. The recipient should have a concrete counterstatement lane for wrong income, wrong person, missing withholding, duplicated reporting, basis omissions, and corrected payer documents even if a substitute return has already been proposed or assessed.[S154][S155][S156][S157][S158][S258][S261][S263][S264]

### 5.7 Worker-status disputes are more than typo correction

A 1099 versus W-2 dispute may be a status dispute, not just a wrong-form dispute. Once the recipient identifies a plausible employee / contractor misclassification, the case should route through worker-status determination, bounded worker-side payroll-tax reporting, and upstream payer response rather than treating the payer's information return as final.[S75][S76][S154][S155][S158][S160][S267][S268][S270][S271][S272][S273]



### 5.8 Broker and proceeds reports should not become gross-gain reports

A 1099-B, 1099-DA, 1099-S, exchange export, or substitute statement can show that a disposition occurred, but the weaker recipient still needs a no-rent way to correct missing or wrong basis, lot identification, transfer history, digital-asset wallet history, home-sale basis, or exclusion context. Where the reporter held the account, transfer statement, closing file, or broker feed, the first substantial correction duty belongs upstream after a concrete recipient counterstatement.[S27][S39][S41][S42][S65][S66][S154][S155][S158][S160][S291][S292][S294][S295][S296][S297][S298][S299][S300][S301][S302]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Electronic-truth theater** — treating a machine-delivered statement as effectively irrebuttable because it looks standardized.  
2. **Recipient reconstruction trap** — forcing the weaker recipient to rebuild the stronger actor's books before any shelter starts.  
3. **Whole-case freeze by tiny mismatch** — using one contested field to suspend undisputed relief or overcollect the rest of the period.  
4. **Correction non-propagation** — fixing the reporter's record but leaving every downstream recipient to clean up the same error alone.  
5. **Upstream immunity by scale** — excusing repeated false reporting by powerful intermediaries while sanctions land mainly on dependent end subjects.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S42][S44][S66][S68][S83][S98]
6. **Impostor record as truth** — official-looking third-party or filing records generated through identity compromise are used against the victim before a bounded recovery and correction path runs.[S21][S27][S39][S40][S41][S42][S65][S66][S148][S150][S151][S152]
7. **Mismatch rent / reporter hostage** — a recipient must buy paid help, wait indefinitely for the issuer, or accept a wrong match because the public correction lane treats the reporter's feed as effectively final.[S154][S155][S156][S157][S158]
8. **Underreporter proposal as proof** — a CP2000 / CP2501 mismatch notice is treated as final income, penalty, or collection fact before the recipient can use the response and appeal path.[S225][S226][S227][S228][S229]

9. **Reporter-fed default base** — third-party records are treated as conclusive because the recipient missed the original filing deadline, even though the recipient has a bounded correction or reconstruction.[S154][S156][S158][S258][S261][S264]
10. **Status-label finality** — a 1099, app category, or vendor label is treated as conclusive worker status before SS-8 / 8919-style correction or payer response is considered.[S267][S270][S272][S273]

## Compression rule

If the dispute mainly turns on **a tax fact being supplied by a stronger third-party reporter and challenged by the weaker recipient of that report**, use this card with the administration, proof-burden, refund, sanctions, reuse, or finality notes rather than creating a payroll-specific, platform-specific, or controller-specific doctrine file.
