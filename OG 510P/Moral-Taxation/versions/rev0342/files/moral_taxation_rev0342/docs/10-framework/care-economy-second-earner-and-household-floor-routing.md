# Care economy, second-earner, and household-floor routing

## Question in one sentence

When tax and benefit rules affect paid care, unpaid care, second earners, dependents, childcare, eldercare, disability care, or household assessment units, how should the archive recognize care without creating marriage penalties or caregiver invisibility traps?[S500][S501][S502]

Route: treat care as infrastructure, not as a private hobby or a reason to tax the second earner out of work. Assessment units may observe households for need and dependency, but liability and benefit design should preserve personhood, work incentives, caregiver support, and dependent dignity.[S500][S501]

## Core rule

The archive should not make households invisible when care needs are real, but it should also not collapse persons into household property. Tax/benefit rules must test effective marginal rates, childcare and eldercare costs, unpaid-care credits, social-insurance records, caregiver respite, and dependence without forcing marriage penalties, second-earner traps, or paperwork so complex that caregivers cannot claim support.

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| second earner faces high effective marginal rate | participation gate | individualization, taper repair, childcare support, or credit redesign.[S500] |
| unpaid care replaces paid work | care-recognition gate | caregiver credit, pension credit, allowance, or respite support. |
| paid care is low-wage and unstable | decent-work gate | wage floor, benefit record, public funding, and quality standards.[S501] |
| dependent support creates paperwork | automaticity gate | prefill, recurring verification, and low-friction renewal. |
| household means test punishes marriage or caregiving | assessment-unit gate | person-preserving transfer and cliff smoothing. |

## Anti-patterns

- **marriage penalty** — household assessment raises tax or cuts benefits without a defensible need measure.
- **caregiver invisibility** — unpaid care produces no income, credit, pension record, respite, or support.
- **second-earner trap** — childcare costs, joint taxation, and benefit withdrawal make work irrational.
- **care austerity loop** — underfunded public care shifts hidden costs to families, mainly women and low-income workers.
