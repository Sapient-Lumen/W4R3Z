# Insurance, reinsurance, protection-gap, and public-backstop routing

## Question in one sentence

When insurance, reinsurance, catastrophe funds, residual markets, or public backstops are used to manage risk, how should fiscal design distinguish real risk pooling from premium shock, hidden assessment, or bailout rent?[S493][S630][S631]

## Core rule

Insurance is legitimate risk pooling, not a license to privatize underwriting gains and socialize correlated loss after the fact. Public backstops may protect floors where private markets fail, but only with risk reduction, transparent assessments, policyholder affordability checks, and recovery from preventable retained upside.[S493][S630][S631]

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| coverage withdrawal or premium spike follows climate or catastrophe risk | protection-gap gate | separate actuarial risk, affordability floor, mitigation, and public-backstop need. |
| public catastrophe fund or reinsurance backstop is proposed | backstop gate | require pre-funded rules, attachment points, mitigation, and clawback from windfall beneficiaries. |
| residual-market deficit is assessed on all policyholders | assessment-incidence gate | trace who bears the surcharge and protect low-income policyholders. |
| insurer cites reinsurance cost pass-through | cost-pass-through gate | audit real reinsurance cost, capital position, and retained surplus before accepting the pass-through. |
| coverage conditions force retreat or upgrades | floor-and-transition gate | pair risk signals with adaptation aid and non-ransom relocation choices. |
