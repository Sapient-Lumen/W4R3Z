# User-fee, service-charge, utility, and public-access toll routing

## Question in one sentence

When governments or public utilities fund services through fees, charges, rates, disconnection rules, or access tolls, when does cost recovery become a hidden tax or essential-service ransom?[S573][S574][S575]

## Core rule

User fees can be legitimate when they price a special benefit, manage congestion, or recover avoidable service costs. They become tax-like and floor-sensitive when the charged item is a required compliance channel, a basic public right, or an essential utility whose loss threatens health, housing, safety, work, or civic access.

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| charge funds general public capacity | tax-label gate | treat as tax-like; require authorization, incidence, and rebate analysis. |
| fee blocks required compliance or appeal | rights gate | waive, rebate, or replace with public funding. |
| utility bill threatens shutoff | essential-service gate | affordability discount, arrears plan, seasonal or health moratorium, and no-rent reconnection. |
| rate increase funds infrastructure for a large user | cost-causation gate | assign interconnection and capacity costs to the cost causer before households. |
| fee revenue funds the charging office | self-funding gate | review conflict, cap, and independent audit. |

## Anti-patterns

- **hidden tax** — a broad public cost is shifted into compulsory fees.
- **essential-service ransom** — water, energy, filing, transit, or permit access is withheld from floor-risk households.
- **self-funding agency trap** — the collector depends on fee volume.
- **reconnection rent** — restoration of an essential service becomes a fee ladder.
