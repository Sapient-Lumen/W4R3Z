# Regressivity correction and floor-protection routing

This note answers the question that comes **after** a broad tax, corrective charge, or routine revenue instrument looks administratively workable but **before** it can count as morally justified: **how is regressivity corrected without turning the base into an opaque exemption jungle or leaving protected people to absorb the burden first and seek relief later?** The archive's stable answer is to keep the base comparatively broad where that improves legibility and collection, then correct regressivity through explicit floor protection, shared basic provision, and visibly timed offsets rather than through carve-out thickets that mainly reward better-documented or better-lawyered households.[S2][S3][S4][S6][S7][S21][S31][S32][S33][S64][S65][S66]

## Core rule

Correct regressivity in five steps:

1. **name the protected floor that must not be crossed**,  
2. **keep ordinary necessities affordable through cash, service, or bill-side protection rather than endless base fragmentation where possible**,  
3. **match relief timing to the burden's timing**,  
4. **separate floor protection from incumbent privilege**,  
5. and **treat low take-up, delayed offsets, or household-shadowing as justice failures, not side issues**.[S3][S4][S21][S31][S64][S65][S66]

A proposal fails when it calls itself progressive because relief exists on paper while protected households still pay first, wait too long, or miss the correction altogether.

For the archive's compact post-waist operating ladder on this question, start from [`../20-calibration/regressivity-repair-channel-and-delivery-sync-ladder.md`](../20-calibration/regressivity-repair-channel-and-delivery-sync-ladder.md) rather than reopening this routing rule.

## Default correction routing by base or context

| Base or context | Best first correction route | Why this route usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Broad consumption taxes | visible per-person or household-side rebate, dividend, or basic-service offset | transaction-side breadth is often administratively strong, but raw burden tends to lean regressive | do not treat exemptions for politically favored goods as a substitute for reliable floor protection.[S3][S4][S64][S65][S66] |
| Ordinary labor and payroll-linked taxation | tax-free or negatively taxed floor, wage subsidy, refundable earnings support, and child / disability / care support | subsistence labor is the weakest ordinary revenue source and should not shoulder public finance before rents and harms do | low nominal rates can still be unjust if payroll timing, withholding, or annual reconciliation push people below the protected floor.[S2][S4][S21][S31] |
| Carbon, congestion, water-stress, and other corrective taxes | dividend, bill credit, transit / retrofit help, and targeted transition support | corrective pricing is easier to defend when households can actually afford the transition path | do not announce the tax now and the compensation later, or promise a dividend that depends on weak take-up.[S6][S7][S21][S64][S65] |
| Housing, utilities, and locality-linked burden shifts | bill-side protection, public-service investment, or local equalisation rather than hidden exemptions | households experience these burdens as recurring essentials, so timing and delivery matter as much as nominal amount | do not let site-level revenue coexist with rent spikes, utility stress, or local service strain that are only notionally repaired.[S5][S6][S15][S21][S70] |
| Care-heavy, child-heavy, disability-heavy, or unstable-household contexts | direct support, refundable credits, services, and override-friendly household tests | care costs and dependency are real, but full household fusion often recreates domination or second-earner traps | do not force protection to depend on stale household assumptions, dominant-partner cooperation, or inaccessible documentation.[S31][S32][S33][S45][S46] |
| AI automation dividends or frontier-windfall finance | worker-side transition support, local repair, basic-capability provision, and floor-first sharing | AI-linked taxes lose legitimacy if gains are socialized only after vulnerable workers and localities absorb the disruption | do not route all sophistication to controller-side collection while leaving displaced workers and burdened places to navigate manual claims.[S17][S22][S67][S68] |
| Future rights-bearing artificial person, if threshold is genuinely crossed | person-level floor protection together with standing, remedy, and reciprocity | once a system becomes a rights-bearing subject, ordinary person-like floor logic should follow rather than owner-side extraction alone | do not impose direct tax obligations without the accompanying floor, remedy, and representation bundle.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Correct regressivity explicitly, not by shredding the base

The archive still prefers broad, legible bases when those improve honesty and collection. But breadth is only acceptable when explicit floor protection follows. Exemption jungles often hide who is protected, help higher spenders more than poorer households, and make later reform harder to read.[S3][S4][S21]

### 2. Put the strongest correction where the burden is most predictable

If the main burden lands through prices, use rebates, dividends, or bill offsets. If it lands through wages, use payroll-side or earnings-side support. If it lands through service scarcity or locality stress, use direct local fiscal repair or provision. The correction channel should mirror the burden channel closely enough that people are not asked to float the state or the transition themselves.[S4][S6][S7][S21][S68]

### 3. Separate protected-floor support from luxury favor or sector privilege

Child support, disability support, subsistence protection, and recurring anti-hardship transfers are not morally interchangeable with reduced rates for politically legible goods, home-asset favoritism, or bespoke business relief. The archive treats the first as potentially required and the second as presumptively suspect until justified independently.[S4][S21][S29][S30][S31]

### 4. Time relief with burden, not after hardship has already happened

A rebate that arrives long after rent, food, heating, or transport costs have risen is weaker than one that lands in-step. Timing is part of regressivity correction, not a separate implementation footnote. This is why automaticity, instalments, payroll syncing, and bill-side offsets often beat annual after-the-fact claiming.[S21][S23][S24][S64][S65][S66]

### 5. Keep care, disability, and household reality visible without letting the household swallow the person

Some floor protection must notice dependency, caring work, disability, or shared-resource facts. But the archive still defaults direct human earnings liability to the person and prefers direct support, services, refundable relief, or rebuttable household tests over blunt joint treatment that hides intra-household inequality.[S31][S32][S33][S45][S46]

### 6. Current AI-era taxation still needs ordinary human floor correction

Even when the archive routes AI taxes through controllers, facilities, clouds, and large beneficiaries, the distributive correction remains human-side: worker transition support, local repair, child and care support, and basic-capability provision. Present LLM systems do not become the moral substitute for protecting affected humans.[S17][S20][S22][S67][S68]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Exemption-jungle progressivity** — carving out politically sympathetic goods instead of protecting the floor directly.  
2. **Paper compensation** — calling a broad tax progressive because an offset exists in statute even though timing or non-take-up defeats it.  
3. **Household-shadow correction** — routing relief through a household unit that hides abuse, instability, separation, or unequal control.  
4. **Luxury masking** — defending affluent-favoring deductions or reduced rates as if they were anti-regressive necessities.  
5. **Controller-side sophistication / household-side maze** — collecting complex modern taxes upstream while making ordinary people perform the hardest procedural labor to be made whole.[S3][S4][S21][S29][S30][S31][S32][S64][S65][S66]

## Order of preference

When several ways of correcting regressivity are available, the archive usually prefers the one that is:

1. most direct at protecting subsistence and recurring essentials,
2. most visible rather than hidden in carved-up bases,
3. most tightly timed to the burden,
4. least dependent on a dominating household intermediary,
5. easiest to receive without heroic claiming,
6. and least confusable with incumbent privilege.

This is why broad taxes with visible, timely, low-friction floor protection usually beat elegant-looking rate schedules that leave the poor to self-insure through delay, documentation, or under-claiming.
