# Customer benefit and competitive pass-through routing

This note answers a question left too implicit between the archive's **beneficiary-side windfall** lane and its ordinary **incidence** rules: **if a downstream AI winner says the gain is already being competed away into lower prices, broader access, better service, shorter waits, or other user-facing improvements, should the archive still treat that gain as concentrated private windfall?** The archive's stable answer is **sometimes no, but only when the diffusion is real**. A gain that is durably passed through to broad customers or users is not the same moral object as retained concentrated surplus. But temporary discounts, lock-in subsidies, marketing theater, or quality claims that coexist with persistent supra-normal margins do not dissolve the beneficiary lane.[S3][S4][S10][S11][S21][S22][S28][S67][S83]

## Core rule

Route these cases in order:

1. **Ask whether the alleged user benefit is real, broad, and durable.** Temporary promotions, beta subsidies, selective discounts, or headline feature upgrades do not by themselves prove that the gain has actually diffused.[S4][S10][S11][S21][S22][S67][S83]
2. **Compare pass-through against retained margin.** Where prices fall, access expands, or quality rises **and** residual margins stay ordinary, the windfall story weakens. Where user benefit appears but margins remain persistently exceptional, the beneficiary lane stays live for the retained surplus.[S10][S11][S21][S22][S28][S67][S83]
3. **Do not confuse competitive diffusion with public-service routing.** Broad customer benefit can defeat or narrow a private windfall claim without turning the firm into a public body, mutual, or worker-benefit institution.[S11][S18][S21][S22][S67][S83]
4. **Keep other lanes intact.** Real customer diffusion does not erase controller-side duties, local-burden claims, monopoly-rent scrutiny, or protected-burden review where suppliers, workers, or users are still exposed.[S3][S4][S10][S15][S21][S22][S28][S67][S83]
5. **Mixed cases may split.** A firm can both pass through some gain to customers and retain enough exceptional surplus to justify a narrower beneficiary-side contribution on the remainder.[S10][S11][S21][S22][S67][S83]

## Routing table

| Case | Strongest first answer | Best fiscal shape | Must not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thin-margin or genuinely competitive market where AI savings are visibly flowing into lower prices, broader access, or materially better service while margins stay ordinary | ordinary tax plus incidence / user-benefit review | ordinary profit tax, ordinary consumption rules, and protected-burden review where needed | do not relabel a genuinely diffused gain as concentrated windfall merely because AI is involved.[S3][S4][S10][S11][S21][S22][S67][S83] |
| Large downstream firm claiming customer benefit while keeping persistent supra-normal margins, market power, or outsized labor-displacing surplus | beneficiary-side screening still applies for the retained surplus | ordinary tax first; add bounded beneficiary-side contribution if concentrated gain remains materially undertaxed | do not let modest price cuts or feature additions launder a real windfall.[S10][S11][S21][S22][S28][S67][S83] |
| Freemium, below-cost, or lock-in pricing justified as user benefit | skepticism first | ordinary tax plus market-power, classification, and beneficiary review | do not treat customer acquisition subsidies as proof that gain has already socially diffused.[S10][S11][S21][S22][S28][S67][S83] |
| Regulated or formula-pass-through sector where savings must appear in approved rates, fees, or user charges | public / mutual / regulated-service routing first | rate review, member or user relief, service integrity, worker transition, local repair, and ordinary tax on any real private extraction | do not duplicate a service-benefit lane as if it were an ordinary private windfall case.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S69][S83] |
| Mixed case with some broad customer diffusion and some concentrated retained surplus | split the story rather than force one label | ordinary tax plus narrower beneficiary-side charge only on the retained exceptional surplus; use incidence and proceeds tools for user-side fairness | do not force all-or-nothing classification when both diffusion and concentration are materially real.[S3][S4][S10][S11][S21][S22][S67][S83] |

## Four routing rules that matter most

### 1. Real diffusion weakens the windfall claim, but only if it is visible in substance

The archive does not assume that every downstream AI winner is keeping the upside. In some markets, competition, contract terms, or routine user pressure may force savings outward into lower prices, broader access, or better service. Where that happens durably enough that residual margins are not the main story, the beneficiary-side windfall case narrows or disappears.[S3][S4][S10][S11][S21][S22][S67][S83]

### 2. Customer benefit is not a free moral halo

A firm does not escape merely by naming some user benefit. The archive asks whether the benefit is broad, durable, and commensurate with the claimed AI gain. Small discounts, selective tiers, better marketing copy, or product upgrades that coexist with concentrated surplus do not by themselves change the lane.[S10][S11][S21][S22][S28][S67][S83]

### 3. Competitive pass-through is different from public-benefit or worker-benefit routing

Broad user benefit can morally matter without converting the institution into a public body, mutual, nonprofit, or labor-residual firm. The right answer is often simply that the gain has been competed away enough that ordinary taxation and incidence review are doing most of the work, not that the firm has become a service-benefit institution.[S11][S18][S21][S22][S67][S83]

### 4. Mixed cases should tax the retained exceptional surplus, not the gross activity

The cleanest design in mixed cases is to leave ordinary activity on the ordinary tax base, identify the part of the gain that remains exceptionally concentrated, and tax only that retained slice if ordinary taxation is missing it. The archive still rejects token taxes, user head taxes, or gross-usage levies as stand-ins for moral surplus.[S10][S11][S15][S16][S21][S22][S67][S83]

## Anti-patterns

1. **customer-halo laundering** — pointing to some user improvement as if it automatically erased concentrated retained gain,
2. **promo-diffusion theater** — treating temporary discounts, free tiers, or launch subsidies as proof of durable social diffusion,
3. **quality-mirage defense** — claiming broad benefit through quality or speed while still keeping exceptional margins,
4. **all-or-nothing lane forcing** — refusing to admit mixed cases where some gain diffuses and some remains concentrated,
5. **local-burden erasure** — acting as if customer benefit defeats place-bound repair claims where layoffs, strain, or adaptation burdens still land.[S3][S4][S10][S11][S15][S21][S22][S28][S67][S83]
