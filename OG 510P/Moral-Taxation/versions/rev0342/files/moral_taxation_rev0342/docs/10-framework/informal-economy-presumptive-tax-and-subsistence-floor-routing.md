# Informal economy, presumptive tax, and subsistence-floor routing

## Question in one sentence

When a state with limited administrative capacity taxes microenterprises, street vendors, cash businesses, subsistence traders, or hard-to-audit sectors through presumptive, turnover, license, or simplified regimes, how should the archive distinguish useful formalization from subsistence tolls and capacity overreach?[S451][S467][S468]

Route: simplification is legitimate when it lowers compliance cost, builds records, and graduates firms into ordinary regimes without invading the floor. It is illegitimate when a weak state taxes subsistence visibility because rent/harm bases are politically harder.[S467][S468]

## Core rule

The informal economy is not a single moral base. Some informality is subsistence survival; some is avoidance by capacity-rich actors. The route must separate microenterprise floor protection from professionalized evasion, and it must avoid formalization ransom.

## Routing table

| Signal | Start here | Default output |
|---|---|---|
| subsistence trader or microenterprise near floor | floor gate | exempt, rebate, ultra-light registration, or service-first formalization. |
| modest but growing business | simplified graduation gate | low-rate presumptive/turnover regime with taper and opt-in/opt-out path. |
| professionalized cash business with capacity | enforcement gate | ordinary audit/proxy and anti-evasion tools. |
| weak administration lacks income data | capacity gate | simple proxy only if floor-safe and contestable. |
| license fee blocks market access | access-rent gate | reduce, rebate, or convert to service fee with visible benefit. |
| formalization promised | trust gate | public services, records, credit access, social protection, and complaint route must materialize. |

## Anti-patterns

- **subsistence toll** — visible low-income activity is taxed because it is easier than rent extraction.
- **formalization ransom** — legal recognition, stall access, or business identity is sold as a revenue gate.
- **threshold cliff** — crossing a small line triggers a large compliance jump.
- **capacity excuse** — weak administration justifies blunt rules without floor protection, appeal, or trust-building.
