# Overlap, creditability, and non-substitution routing

Use this card when the live dispute is not whether a tax base exists at all but **whether several justified levies are charging the same moral object, whether one charge should credit another, or whether an "already taxed" objection is really just the wrong base talking**. The archive's stable answer is to **separate moral objects first, stack genuinely distinct claims, credit only true duplicates, and refuse broad-tax rhetoric that tries to erase earlier rent or harm pricing**.[S3][S4][S5][S8][S9][S10][S15][S21][S28][S29][S30][S73][S83][S87][S88][S93]

The fact that one activity, firm, asset, or place faces several levies does not by itself prove injustice. A data-centre campus can justly face land-value tax, local burden charges, carbon pricing, ordinary profit tax, and sometimes a residual-rent layer. What matters is whether those levies are reaching **different things** or merely charging the same thing twice under different names.[S5][S9][S10][S15][S21][S28][S83][S88][S93]

## Default lanes

| Situation | Default overlap answer | Why this path usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Land-rich housing or commercial wealth | let land / site-value taxes, inheritance or lifetime-receipts taxes, and ordinary capital-income rules all stand; use annual wealth tax only as a backstop | land rent, dynastic transfer, and ordinary capital return are related but not identical moral objects | do not let a wealth backstop become an excuse to skip land or inheritance reform, and do not tax the same stock slice twice without a clear duplicate screen.[S5][S8][S23][S73][S93] |
| Data-centre campus, frontier-model lab, or large AI deployer | stack site-value or host-burden charges, energy / water / carbon pricing, ordinary profit tax, and a narrow exceptional layer only if a distinct bottleneck or public-input claim remains | place-bound burdens, direct harms, and residual controller-side rents are different objects even when one actor pays them all | do not count the same kWh, emissions stream, or residual-rent slice twice under different labels.[S9][S10][S15][S22][S28][S83][S88] |
| Fossil, mining, or extractive activity | severance or resource-rent charges, carbon or pollution pricing, ordinary profit tax, and cleanup security can all coexist | extraction, ongoing harm, ordinary profit, and cleanup assurance answer different questions | do not use one charge to pretend the others are unnecessary, and do not ignore hidden support when claiming the sector is already burdened enough.[S6][S7][S9][S15][S29][S30][S88] |
| Broad household consumption | keep broad VAT / GST and repair regressivity through transfers or services; add narrower excises only for distinct harms or luxury claims | a broad consumption base and a targeted externality or positional claim need not collapse into one instrument | do not turn every distributive objection into a base exemption, and do not layer near-identical sales taxes with no distinct purpose.[S3][S4][S21][S64][S65][S66][S87] |
| Dominant digital, platform, or monopoly-rent business | let ordinary profit tax, minimum-tax floors, market-jurisdiction allocation, and narrow excess-profit or bottleneck-rent tools stand where each reaches a distinct slice | broad profit, anti-avoidance floors, and residual-rent capture are not automatically substitutes | do not call local payroll or sales exposure a reason to abandon separate rent capture, and do not relabel the same residual-profit claim twice.[S9][S10][S21][S28][S83] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Separate the moral objects before arguing about rates

Ask what each levy is actually trying to reach: land or location rent, extraction rent, direct harm, local burden, broad consumption, ordinary profit, residual monopoly or bottleneck rent, dynastic transfer, or wealth-stock backstop. Overlap is only morally suspicious when two instruments are really reaching the **same object**, not when they reach different objects attached to the same actor or activity.[S3][S5][S8][S9][S10][S15][S21][S28][S73][S83][S88][S93]

### 2. Stack genuinely distinct claims

The archive usually allows stacking when one levy is place-bound, another is harm-based, another is an ordinary broad fiscal claim, and a later one reaches a distinct residual rent. The same firm can therefore face several justified taxes without suffering a moral duplicate, because the taxes answer different public claims.[S5][S9][S10][S15][S21][S28][S87][S88][S93]

### 3. Credit, consolidate, or priority-order only true duplicates

Creditability is strongest where two instruments are both charging the **same emissions ton, the same measured stock, the same transaction base, or the same narrow rent slice**. In those cases the archive prefers explicit credits, narrow consolidation, or clear priority rules over rhetorical pile-on. But crediting should stay narrow enough that a carbon charge does not erase land rent, or an ordinary corporate tax erase monopoly rent, by category mistake.[S15][S21][S28][S83][S87][S88]

### 4. Broad taxes do not by themselves substitute for prior rent or harm pricing

A taxpayer cannot usually answer a land-rent, inheritance, pollution, congestion, or cleanup claim merely by saying it already pays ordinary income, profit, or sales tax. Broad taxes may interact through profit measurement or ordinary deductibility, but they are usually not moral substitutes for earlier claims that are more directly tied to exclusion power or imposed harm.[S3][S4][S5][S8][S9][S15][S21][S87][S88][S93]

### 5. Net out hidden negative taxes before judging pileup

Before declaring a sector or activity overtaxed, disclose abatements, cheap public inputs, tax expenditures, accelerated depreciation, utility discounts, and negotiated support. A statutory stack can look heavy while the net fiscal stack remains mild or even favorable.[S10][S15][S29][S30]

### 6. Hold backstops for last

Annual wealth taxes, bespoke windfall layers, and other broad backstops belong after cleaner land, inheritance, anti-deferral, harm-pricing, and ordinary profit tools have been used seriously. Backstops are justified when distinct concentrated advantage still escapes, not as a first-line substitute for clearer bases.[S5][S8][S10][S22][S73][S93]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Wrong-base already-taxed rhetoric** — ordinary income, payroll, sales, or profit tax is invoked to erase a distinct land, inheritance, pollution, or monopoly-rent claim.  
2. **Same-base pile-on** — two instruments charge the same measured stock, emissions stream, or rent slice and no one screens for duplication.  
3. **Subsidy-blind stacking** — nominal levies are added on top of hidden negative taxes and then misdescribed as heavy net burden.  
4. **Omnibus super-levy design** — one bespoke tax is asked to price harms, capture rents, and fund repair all at once, so the archive loses sight of what is being charged.  
5. **Backstop-first substitution** — designers reach for wealth or windfall backstops before cleaner first-line bases have been used seriously.[S5][S8][S10][S15][S21][S29][S30][S73][S83][S87][S88][S93]

## Compression rule

If a case mainly turns on **whether several justified taxes are really reaching different objects or merely duplicating one another**, use this card plus the base-ordering, net-stack, jurisdiction, or incidence notes rather than adding a new sector-specific overlap memo.
