# Record asymmetry, burden shifting, and adverse-inference ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that **heavy proof should follow superior access to the decisive facts rather than formal pleading order**, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when a concrete trigger is enough to shift burdens toward the stronger record-holder, when a narrow rebuttable presumption should run, and when selective incompleteness, sham fragmentation, or post-notice deletion justify harder adverse inference?**[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S39][S40][S41][S83][S89][S91][S92]

Route: once a weaker outsider points to a **concrete contradiction, governance signal, ownership mismatch, metering anomaly, or missing-record problem**, the archive should usually move proof toward the actor with superior access to the hidden facts, try the **narrowest rebuttable bridge first**, and harden toward adverse inference mainly when opacity looks strategic rather than ordinary.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S39][S40][S41][S83][S89]

## Option scan

| Option | Shape | Archive verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A — outsider reconstructs the hidden world first | keep full proof on the worker, household, tenant, locality, small supplier, or outside challenger until they can map the stronger actor's books, governance chain, or operating facts in detail.[S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S39][S41][S83] | Reject: turns record concentration into a structural litigation advantage. |
| B — any gap means automatic maximum inference | treat missing or incomplete records as enough for the harshest grouping, liability, or penalty inference immediately.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S39][S40][S41] | Reject: collapses ordinary incompleteness into sanction-max governance. |
| C — trigger, superior-access shift, narrow presumption, then harder inference only for strategic opacity | require a concrete trigger, shift the burden toward the stronger record-holder, use provisional or field-bounded presumptions first, and reserve hard adverse inference for selective incompleteness, sham fragmentation, or spoliation.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S39][S40][S41][S83][S89] | Adopt. |
| D — solve proof asymmetry with routine full surveillance | answer hidden-fact disputes by defaulting to bulk matching, broad discovery, or standing full-log retention.[S16][S17][S27][S39][S40][S41][S89][S91][S92] | Reject: defeats data-minimization and review-proportionality rules. |

## Five-rung ladder

1. **ordinary attestation lane** — where the paper story is coherent and no concrete mismatch is shown, accept ordinary attestation, ordinary shared records, or the existing packet rather than escalating proof demands reflexively,[S20][S21][S27][S39][S41][S83]
2. **trigger-and-answer lane** — once the weaker side points to a specific contradiction, hidden-governance signal, ownership loop, unexplained pass-through, metering anomaly, or missing record, require the stronger actor to answer that point first because it controls the relevant books or operations,[S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S39][S41][S83]
3. **narrow rebuttable-presumption lane** — if the answer stays incomplete, use the smallest workable provisional bridge: a grouped-fact presumption, field-bounded shelter, temporary allocation default, or issue-scoped filing bridge rather than whole-case collapse,[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S39][S40][S41][S83][S89]
4. **adverse-inference lane** — harden only when the pattern looks strategic: selective incompleteness, sham fragmentation, contradictory stories, refusal to produce core records, or post-notice deletion should justify stronger inference against the opaque actor,[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S39][S40][S41][S83]
5. **integrity-hardening lane** — repeated opacity, industrialized bad recordkeeping, or spoliation should trigger preservation orders, tighter signer or attestation duties, targeted sanctions, or stronger grouping rules on the opaque layer rather than a permanent surveillance default for everyone.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S39][S40][S41][S83][S89][S91][S92]

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt **Option C — trigger, superior-access shift, narrow presumption, then harder inference only for strategic opacity** as the archive's default calibration for ordinary proof asymmetry.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S34][S35][S39][S40][S41][S83][S89]

Presumption:

- demand a **concrete trigger**, not outsider omniscience,
- move the first serious answer burden toward the actor with **superior access**,
- prefer **field-bounded or issue-bounded presumptions** before whole-case conclusions,
- harden mainly for **strategic opacity, sham splitting, or spoliation**,
- and keep the cure for hard proof from becoming **routine broad surveillance**.

That is the narrowest workable setting because it preserves review proportionality and filing parity while still making opacity costly when it is being used as a strategic shield.

## Default context table

| Context | Default rung | Why this usually fits | Archive warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| worker, tenant, household, or small supplier needs relief while decisive records sit upstream | trigger-and-answer lane, then narrow rebuttable presumption if the stronger actor stays incomplete | payroll, billing, meter, and contract records usually sit with the stronger counterparty or system operator.[S21][S27][S31][S38][S64][S65][S66][S68][S89][S91][S92] | do not make floor protection depend on documents the claimant cannot reliably obtain. |
| beneficial ownership, related-party grouping, or anti-fragmentation dispute | trigger-and-answer lane, then adverse inference if shell layering or nominee opacity persists | the hidden affiliate chain is usually best known by the actor controlling it.[S21][S27][S34][S35][S39][S41] | do not require outsiders to prove the whole concealed chain before any grouping presumption runs. |
| cross-border profit, apportionment, or internal-allocation dispute | narrow rebuttable presumption after a concrete mismatch, then adverse inference if internal ledgers remain withheld | the multinational or platform group controls the relevant transfer and allocation facts.[S3][S9][S21][S27][S28][S30][S34][S35][S39][S41] | do not let cross-border complexity become an immunity cloak. |
| local burden, emissions, extraction, congestion, or facility-stress dispute | trigger-and-answer lane, usually anchored on the operator's own metering or logs | the operator already measures or can best reconstruct the live activity.[S15][S21][S23][S27][S69][S79][S83] | do not force affected residents to reverse-engineer an industrial process before repair begins. |
| controller or co-controller dispute in AI systems | use this memo first, then the controller-map-specific ladder if the live issue is packet design or controller proof settings | the general superior-access rule is already settled before controller-map calibration becomes relevant.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S39][S40][S41][S83][S89] | do not treat the controller memo as the only place ordinary burden-shifting doctrine lives. |
