# Land / site-rent netting / retained-surplus ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that **real outside site-rent or scarce-location absorption can narrow a downstream beneficiary-side windfall claim, but ordinary lease expense, related landlords, sale-leaseback choreography, and improvement-heavy stories cannot**, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when location-rent absorption is real, when the case is mixed, and when a beneficiary-side charge should still attach to retained exceptional surplus?**[S5][S15][S18][S21][S23][S25][S28][S34][S35][S67][S69][S70][S83]

## Minimal workable ladder

| Rung | Facts on the ground | Fiscal answer |
|---|---|---|
| 0. lease-expense theater | the story mostly rests on ordinary occupancy cost, fit-out recovery, bundled services, or vague rent inflation while exceptional margins remain strong | ignore the location-rent defense; keep the beneficiary-side lane on retained gain.[S18][S21][S23][S25][S67][S83] |
| 1. related-landlord lane | the rent claim runs through affiliates, sale-leasebacks, treasury landlords, controlled REITs, or bundled property-service wrappers | treat the case as retained private gain on the real grouped actor; run anti-fragmentation first.[S21][S23][S34][S35][S83] |
| 2. mixed site-rent case | some incremental gain is visibly being capitalized into real outside site value or scarce-location premia, but meaningful exceptional residual margins remain after netting | route the site-rent slice to land / host-burden tools, then tax only the retained exceptional residual through a narrower beneficiary-side charge.[S5][S15][S18][S21][S23][S25][S28][S67][S69][S70][S83] |
| 3. real outside site-rent absorption | outside scarce-location value is doing most of the work, residual margins are ordinary, and the gain mainly shows up as land value, lease premia, or host-place uplift | stand down any beneficiary-side windfall charge and use land-value, site-rent, host-burden, and equalization tools instead.[S5][S15][S21][S23][S25][S28][S69][S70][S83] |
| 4. host-burden override | the same place-bound AI site also creates housing, utility, transport, congestion, cleanup, or grid strain | do not let site-rent absorption erase local repair, equalization, or ex ante host-burden tools; those claims stay visible even when the beneficiary lane narrows.[S5][S15][S23][S25][S28][S69][S70][S83] |

## Settings that matter most

### 1. Net only real outside site-rent absorption

Count only the part of the gain that is really being absorbed by **outside scarce site value or location premia**. Do not count ordinary building service charges, tenant improvements, fit-out recovery, routine maintenance, financing cost, or other improvement-heavy expense as if they were immovable-rent absorption. The archive's calibration unit is net outside site-rent displacement of residual upside, not headline lease spend.[S5][S18][S21][S23][S25][S34][S35][S67][S83]

### 2. Keep the window place-linked and episode-linked

Location-rent relief is strongest when a visible AI siting or capacity episode is actually driving scarce-campus, logistics-node, or zoning-premium uplift. Use bounded windows and re-check residual margins so a temporary campus squeeze does not harden into permanent immunity for a downstream winner.[S5][S15][S18][S21][S23][S25][S28][S67][S69][S70][S83]

### 3. Mixed cases should split, not relabel

The clean answer is usually not to choose between “all downstream windfall” and “all location rent.” It is to identify the part of the gain that really capitalized into outside site value and the part that still sits as concentrated exceptional residual, then tax only the latter on the beneficiary lane.[S5][S18][S21][S23][S25][S67][S83]

### 4. Place-bound strain still needs visible repair

Where AI siting also strains housing, utilities, grid access, transport, water, or other host-local systems, the archive still prefers visible local repair and equalization. Real outside site-rent absorption can narrow a beneficiary-side charge; it does not excuse host-burden tools or richer-jurisdiction hoarding of place-based gains.[S5][S15][S23][S25][S28][S69][S70][S83]
