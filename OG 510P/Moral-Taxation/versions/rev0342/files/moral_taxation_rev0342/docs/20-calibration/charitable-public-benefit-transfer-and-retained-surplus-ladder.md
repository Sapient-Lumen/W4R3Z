# Charitable public-benefit transfer / retained-surplus ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that **only the truly outside, irrevocable, non-recapturable public-benefit slice can narrow a downstream beneficiary-side windfall claim, while CSR rhetoric, captive foundations, donor-advised shells, and substitutive giving cannot**, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when charitable transfer is real, when the case is mixed, and when a beneficiary-side charge should still attach to retained exceptional surplus?**[S18][S21][S22][S29][S34][S35][S47][S48][S49][S67][S69][S71][S83]

## Minimal workable ladder

| Rung | Facts on the ground | Fiscal answer |
|---|---|---|
| 0. theater | the claim mostly rests on CSR budgets, branding spend, donor-advised shells, captive foundations, reversible pledges, naming-rights vehicles, or giving rhetoric while exceptional margins, buybacks, executive extraction, or affiliate payouts continue | ignore the charitable-transfer defense; keep the beneficiary-side lane on retained gain.[S18][S21][S29][S34][S35][S67][S69][S71][S83] |
| 1. outside recipient but weak displacement | some money reaches an outside charity or public-interest recipient, but donor control, reputational recapture, substitutive behavior, or continuing exceptional residual means the transfer does not materially displace retained upside | ordinary tax plus ordinary proceeds review; beneficiary-side screening still applies to the exceptional residual.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S34][S35][S67][S69][S71][S83] |
| 2. mixed case | some incremental gain is genuinely transferred outside through independent, additive, non-recapturable public-benefit grants or trusts, but some exceptional residual remains after netting | tax only the retained exceptional residual after bounded netting of the truly outside irrevocable slice; do not tax gross activity.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S47][S48][S49][S67][S69][S71][S83] |
| 3. real outside public-benefit absorption | the incremental gain is genuinely being consumed by outside, irrevocable, additive public-benefit transfer, donor recapture is not the live story, and exceptional residual margins are no longer the main story | stand down or defer any beneficiary-side charge on that slice; stay with ordinary taxation, proceeds review, and anti-supplantation checks.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S47][S48][S49][S67][S69][S71][S83] |
| 4. duty-priority override | the same case also carries worker transition, cleanup, local repair, compensation, controller-side prefunding, or other direct obligations that should have been paid first | do not let charitable transfer outrank worker priority, local repair, settlement, compensation, ordinary tax, or prefunding duties; any relief is bounded by those prior claims.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S47][S48][S49][S67][S69][S71][S83] |

## Settings that matter most

### 1. Net only the truly outside and irrevocable slice

Count only transfer that has really left private option value: outside recipients, independent governance, legal segregation where relevant, non-recapturable use, and no live donor redirection right. Do not count captive foundations, donor-advised shells, marketing budgets, naming-rights trades, or grant structures that still buy private advantage as if they had already socialized the surplus.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S34][S35][S67][S69][S71][S83]

### 2. Keep additivity and substitution separate

Charity can narrow retained surplus only for the slice that is genuinely additive to baseline duties. Where the same firm is underpaying workers, skipping local repair, underfunding compensation, or using philanthropy to replace direct public obligations, the archive should still route first through those prior lanes.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S47][S48][S49][S67][S69][S71][S83]

### 3. Mixed cases should split, not moralize

Where some gain is truly outside and irrevocable and some is still concentrated private residual, split the case. The archive should net only the credible outside public-benefit slice and still tax the retained exceptional residual rather than forcing all-or-nothing labels.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S67][S69][S71][S83]

### 4. Use bounded windows and re-check recapture

Transfer claims are strongest when tied to actual grant or trust commitments in a bounded period rather than to an open-ended identity story about being a generous firm. Re-check whether donor control, related-party influence, executive extraction, or affiliate recapture has re-entered the picture before turning one period of genuine outside giving into lasting immunity.[S18][S21][S29][S34][S35][S67][S69][S71][S83]

## Three anti-patterns

1. **halo laundering** — pointing to modest or strategic giving as if it automatically erased concentrated residual gain,[S18][S21][S29][S67][S69][S71][S83]
2. **foundation-loop recapture** — treating donor-controlled foundations, donor-advised shells, affiliate charities, or reputational spend as if they already socialized the surplus,[S21][S29][S34][S35][S71][S83]
3. **charity-over-duty inversion** — using philanthropy language to crowd out worker transition, local repair, compensation, prefunding, or ordinary tax liability that should still be paid directly.[S18][S21][S22][S29][S47][S48][S49][S67][S69][S71][S83]
