# Annual wealth-tax backstop: visibility, grouping, and liquidity ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that an annual net-wealth tax is a **backstop rather than a master tax**, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when the archive should stay with land, inheritance, capital-income, and rent reform alone, when it should add a narrow annual wealth backstop, and when broader annual net-wealth taxation is justified only because visibility, controller grouping, valuation honesty, and bounded liquidity relief are all strong enough together?**[S5][S8][S18][S23][S24][S25][S34][S35][S73]

The constitutional part is already settled: **tax clearer concentrated bases first, use an annual net-wealth tax only as a high-threshold backstop where large fortunes remain materially under-taxed, and refuse to pretend that a wealth tax is just if controller visibility, valuation honesty, grouping, and bounded liquidity relief are missing.**[S5][S8][S18][S23][S24][S25][S34][S35][S73]

## Why calibration is still needed

The closed rule gives the direction, but not yet the smallest operating ladder. A usable calibration still has to avoid five distinct failures at once:

1. **master-tax substitution** where lawmakers announce an annual wealth tax instead of strengthening land, inheritance, capital-income, and anti-deferral rules that should have been doing most of the work first,[S5][S8][S23][S24][S73]
2. **registry fantasy** where broad annual wealth liability is promised without beneficial-ownership transparency, asset registries, or credible valuation protocols,[S25][S34][S35][S73]
3. **wrapper laundering** where one real fortune is split across trusts, shells, family partnerships, nominees, or cross-border affiliates and then presented as many smaller taxpayers,[S34][S35]
4. **forced-sale cruelty or illiquidity veto** where large concentrated wealth is either taxed on impossible cash terms or exempted altogether because some assets are hard to sell quickly,[S23][S24][S73]
5. and **threshold drift** where an anti-oligarchy backstop slides downward onto ordinary precautionary saving, small business tools, or lightly appreciated homes.[S8][S18][S23][S73]

## Small option set

### Option A — no annual net-wealth tax; strengthen front-line taxes instead

Rely on land value taxation, inheritance and gift taxation, capital-income reform, anti-deferral rules, and monopoly-rent capture while building better ownership visibility. This is the right answer where concentrated wealth is still being under-taxed mainly because clearer bases remain weak or because the administration cannot yet see the controllers or values honestly enough.[S5][S8][S23][S24][S34][S35][S73]

### Option B — narrow visible-fortune backstop

Use a very high-threshold annual backstop aimed first at **high-visibility financial wealth and other easy-to-value assets**, while hard-to-value or tightly held assets remain outside the annual base or face realization-linked settlement. This is less comprehensive, but it can do real anti-concentration work without pretending that universal annual valuation already exists.[S24][S25][S34][S35][S73]

### Option C — grouped broad backstop with honest valuation rules

Use a high-threshold annual net-wealth tax across a broader asset base, but only with controller-side grouping, rebuttable look-through, periodic market-value alignment, proxy rules for hard-to-value assets, and bounded payment relief for illiquid holdings. This is the archive's cleanest serious annual-wealth-tax design when the state can already see enough to tax the right fortunes rather than the easiest ones.[S23][S24][S25][S34][S35][S73]

### Option D — low-threshold or universal annual wealth taxation

Apply annual wealth taxation broadly across much of the population and most asset classes regardless of visibility or liquidity. The archive treats this as the worst ordinary option because it magnifies valuation problems, drags ordinary saving into an anti-domination instrument, and invites arbitrary relief jungles.[S18][S23][S24][S25][S73]

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt a **five-stage practical ladder**:

| Stage | When it should apply | Annual-wealth answer | Main guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. front-line-only lane | land, inheritance, anti-deferral, and rent taxes are still the main missing work; controller visibility is weak | **no annual net-wealth tax yet** | do not use a wealth tax headline to excuse weak first-line reform |
| 1. registry-first lane | concentrated wealth matters, but beneficial ownership, cadastre, or asset visibility is still patchy | **build disclosure, controller maps, and valuation protocols first** | do not levy a universal annual tax before the state can identify the real fortune |
| 2. narrow visible-fortune backstop | financial wealth and other easy-to-value holdings are visible, but broad annual valuation is still not | **high-threshold annual backstop on visible liquid wealth** | keep thresholds high and do not call this full wealth taxation |
| 3. grouped broad backstop | controller grouping is credible, major assets are visible enough, and valuation rules are honest though imperfect | **high-threshold grouped annual net-wealth tax with low rates** | use market values where possible and disclosed proxies where necessary |
| 4. illiquid-concentration settlement lane | large fortunes include tightly held firms, land-heavy portfolios, farms, or art / private business stakes | **preserve liability through instalments, capped deferral, liens, or realization-linked settlement** | illiquidity changes payment design, not whether command wealth counts |

The archive's provisional setting is therefore **stage 0 or stage 1 where front-line taxes and visibility remain weak, stage 2 where visibility is strong mainly for liquid fortunes, and stage 3 plus stage 4 only where controller grouping, valuation discipline, and bounded liquidity relief are all real enough to administer honestly**.[S5][S8][S18][S23][S24][S25][S34][S35][S73]

That is the narrowest workable recommendation because it preserves the archive's core ordering — clearer concentrated bases first — while leaving room for an annual backstop where dynastic or oligarchic wealth still compounds through low realization, sheltering, or weak transfer taxation. It also prevents the familiar mistake of treating every illiquid fortune as untaxable and every hard-to-value asset as either invisible or fully exempt.[S8][S23][S24][S25][S34][S35][S73]

For present AI-era fortunes, the practical consequence is that **controller-side equity, founder stakes, fund interests, and liquid financial claims can enter earlier stages of a wealth backstop sooner than hard-to-value private model, data, or affiliate chains, but the answer to opacity is stronger controller tracing and staged settlement rather than abandoning anti-concentration policy altogether**.[S10][S34][S35][S73]
