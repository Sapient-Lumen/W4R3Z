# Insurance, reinsurance, protection-gap, and public-backstop ladder

## Question in one sentence

When a private insurance market withdraws, reprices, or depends on public reinsurance, which costs should stay with risk creators, which need floor repair, and when does the backstop become a hidden tax or bailout rent?[S493][S630][S631][S650][S651]

## Option scan

| Option | Shape | Archive verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A — ordinary risk pricing | risk-reflective premiums with contestable maps and means-tested floor help | Accept only when coverage remains available, maps are reviewable, and affordability aid does not subsidize new avoidable exposure. |
| B — residual market or insurer of last resort | public or quasi-public pool carries risks private insurers decline | Use for continuity, but require transparent deficits, reserve rules, and assessment-incidence review. |
| C — reinsurance or catastrophe layer | state guarantee, bond, levy, or reinsurer absorbs tail losses | Justified only with attachment points, mitigation duties, and clawback/recoupment rules. |
| D — mitigation, buyout, or managed retreat | money goes first to risk reduction, relocation, or safer rebuilding | Preferred where continued coverage would lock households into danger or socialize repeated losses. |
| E — bailout of private underwriting loss | public money covers past underpricing without benefit or control tests | Reject unless temporary, conditional, and paired with public upside or consumer protection. |

## Parameter ladder

1. **attachment point** — Name the deductible, excess-loss layer, emergency trigger, or insolvency point at which public support begins.
2. **who caused, who benefits, who cannot exit** — Separate legacy homeowners, renters, new developers, insurers, mortgage lenders, and local governments before choosing an assessment base.
3. **premium-affordability floor** — Test low-income, renter, small-business, tribal, rural, and disability exposure before permitting broad policyholder assessments.
4. **residual-market assessment audit** — Show who pays assessments after insurers pass them through; do not call them actuarial if they function like a regressive tax.
5. **mitigation credit and no-new-risk rule** — Give premium/assessment relief for verified risk reduction and block subsidies for new avoidable high-risk development.
6. **reserve and reinsurance cost test** — Compare expected loss, reinsurance price, public guarantee value, and administrative costs before accepting deficit finance.
7. **buyout and retreat sequencing** — Offer relocation, buyout, or adaptation before repeated premium shocks become fiscal hostage-taking.
8. **renter and non-owner protection** — Treat rent pass-through and insurance-withdrawal effects as incidence, not as outside the insurance file.
9. **public-backstop proceeds claim** — Route recoupment to reserves, mitigation, relocation, and affected-community repair before general-fund absorption.
10. **review trigger** — Reopen after hazard-map update, insurer withdrawal, reserve breach, large disaster, reinsurance-market shock, or assessment passthrough spike.

## Default settings

| Parameter | Default | Redesign trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Public attachment point | Written threshold tied to modeled loss and reserve status | Open-ended guarantee, post-event emergency levy, or undisclosed deficit. |
| Assessment base | Risk/benefit-sensitive and floor-tested | Flat policyholder surcharge or utility-like assessment on low-income households. |
| Affordability aid | Means-tested and transition-limited | Permanent premium suppression for new risky development. |
| Mitigation link | Verified credits, buyouts, and rebuilding conditions | No credit for risk reduction or no penalty for risk creation. |
| Review cadence | Annual plus event-triggered after major losses | Backstop persists after market or hazard conditions change. |
