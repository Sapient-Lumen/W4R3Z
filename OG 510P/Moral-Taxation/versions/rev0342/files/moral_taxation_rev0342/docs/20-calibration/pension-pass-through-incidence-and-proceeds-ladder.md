# Pension pass-through / incidence / proceeds ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that **diffuse pension, retirement, and broad-saver pass-through does not by itself erase concentrated firm-side AI gain**, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when passive saver exposure needs no special adjustment, when it matters only through ordinary incidence checks, when it justifies visible retirement-adequacy or household-saver protection on the proceeds side, and when narrow wrapper stories deserve no saver-side concession at all?**[S3][S4][S10][S11][S18][S21][S22][S29][S34][S35][S64][S67][S83]

## Why calibration is still needed

Once the archive refuses retiree-halo immunity, four practical failures remain easy:

1. **incidence neglect** where ordinary retirees or household savers really do bear part of the burden through pension funds, annuities, or account balances but no one checks,
2. **proceeds drift** where policymakers invoke pensioners rhetorically yet route all proceeds elsewhere with no visible saver-side repair,
3. **double concession** where an operating firm gets a lighter levy *and* proceeds are still earmarked to saver relief for the same claimed diffusion,
4. **wrapper laundering** where narrow trusts, elite endowments, family vehicles, or thin retirement shells borrow broad-saver language to seek concessions meant for ordinary households.[S3][S4][S10][S11][S18][S21][S22][S29][S34][S35][S64][S67][S83]

## Small option set

### Option A — no special saver adjustment

Keep the operating-firm lane and use ordinary person-side tax and transfer rules only.[S3][S18][S21][S22]

This is strongest where passive saver exposure is trivial, speculative, or too diffuse to change protected-burden judgments. It is too thin when ordinary retirees are the likely final bearers of a meaningful share.

### Option B — incidence check only

Keep the full firm-side lane, but require explicit incidence testing for pensioners, retirees, and household savers before calling the design acceptable.[S3][S4][S18][S21][S22][S64][S83]

This is strongest where saver exposure is broad but the right remedy is not yet obvious. It is too weak when the archive already knows that protected households or pension beneficiaries will bear nontrivial burden without offset.

### Option C — proceeds-side saver protection

Keep the firm-side lane intact and route a bounded share of proceeds into retirement-adequacy, pension-floor, annuitant, or broad household-saver protection tools.[S3][S4][S18][S21][S22][S64][S67]

This is strongest where the diffusion is real and broad, but mediated: the operating firm still owes tax, yet visible reciprocity toward ordinary savers is morally warranted. It is too weak if it becomes a slogan for underfunding other real claims such as worker transition or local repair.

### Option D — layered integration

Use a layered answer:

1. **tax concentrated gain where it sits** under the already chosen firm-side lane,
2. **run a protected-burden screen** for retirees, annuitants, pension beneficiaries, and ordinary household savers,
3. **repair through person-side or pension-side tools first** where that is the cleanest channel,
4. **use a bounded proceeds share for retirement adequacy or saver protection only when broad mediated exposure remains a real moral fact**, and
5. **deny saver concessions entirely to narrow or elite wrappers.**[S3][S4][S10][S11][S18][S21][S22][S29][S34][S35][S64][S67][S83]

This is the archive's cleanest default because it keeps lane selection honest while still taking broad mediated household exposure seriously.

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt **Option D** as the default calibration ladder once the archive has already decided the case belongs on an ordinary or beneficiary-side operating-firm lane rather than in the direct public-benefit or worker-benefit exceptions.

That means:

1. **do not discount the operating-firm claim just because ownership is broadly intermediated** through funds or pensions,[S10][S11][S18][S21][S22][S29][S34][S35][S67][S83]
2. **distinguish protected ordinary savers from narrow asset wrappers** before offering any saver-side accommodation,[S18][S21][S29][S34][S35][S83]
3. **repair likely burden at the person or pension channel first** through progressivity, minimum pension design, benefit rules, or automatic credits rather than by weakening the firm-side levy,[S3][S4][S18][S21][S22][S64][S83]
4. **use earmarked proceeds only inside a named corridor** for retirement adequacy or broad household-saver protection when that burden is real and other claims have already been screened,[S4][S18][S21][S22][S64][S67]
5. **forbid same-slice double concessions** so the archive never lowers the firm-side claim and separately earmarks the same moral fact a second time.[S3][S10][S18][S21][S22][S83]

## Default saver-integration ladder

| Lane | When it should apply | Default answer | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. ordinary no-adjustment lane | passive saver exposure is trivial, uncertain, or morally remote | keep the ordinary firm-side lane plus ordinary person-side tax rules only | do not invent pension rhetoric where the exposure is too thin to matter |
| 1. incidence-check lane | broad household or retiree exposure is plausible but not yet shown to create protected burden | keep the firm-side levy and require explicit incidence review | do not let "everyone owns a little" substitute for evidence.[S3][S4][S18][S21][S22][S83] |
| 2. saver-protection lane | protected retirees or ordinary households are likely to bear some material burden through mediated holdings or payout channels | keep the firm-side levy, then use person-side progressivity, pension tax rules, automatic credits, or benefit floors | repair burden where it lands rather than pretending the operating gain disappeared.[S3][S4][S18][S21][S22][S64][S83] |
| 3. proceeds-integration lane | broad mediated saver exposure is real and the archive wants visible reciprocity beyond ordinary incidence repair | dedicate a bounded proceeds share to retirement adequacy, pension-floor support, or broad household-saver protection | do not let saver earmarks crowd out worker, local, or floor claims already established on stronger lanes.[S4][S18][S21][S22][S64][S67] |
| 4. layered integration lane | firm-side gain is concentrated, saver exposure is broad, and several claimant channels are simultaneously real | combine firm-side taxation, incidence repair, bounded saver-side proceeds, and other already-screened claims with clear publication of the split | publish the baseline and bar same-slice duplication.[S3][S4][S10][S18][S21][S22][S64][S67][S83] |
| 5. elite-wrapper override | the apparent saver claim comes mainly from narrow trusts, elite endowments, family vehicles, or thin retirement shells | no special saver accommodation; treat the case as ordinary private residual gain | do not let wrapper labels do the work of breadth or need.[S18][S21][S29][S34][S35][S83] |

## Four calibration rules that matter most

### 1. Mediated breadth matters mostly after lane selection

The operating firm can still be the right taxpayer even when broad passive owners exist in the background. Saver diffusion usually changes integration, not the moral existence of concentrated firm-side gain.[S10][S11][S18][S21][S22][S29][S67][S83]

### 2. Person-side repair usually beats firm-side immunity

When ordinary retirees or household savers are likely final bearers, the cleaner answer is usually credits, pension-floor rules, benefit support, or progressive proceeds routing rather than a weaker operating-firm levy. That keeps the tax aimed at concentration while protecting the floor where burden actually lands.[S3][S4][S18][S21][S22][S64][S83]

### 3. Broad-saver protection must stay broad and rules-based

If proceeds are routed toward saver protection, use broad public or quasi-public channels such as minimum pension support, annuitant protection, or household-saver credits. Do not build bespoke carve-outs that shadow particular firms, funds, or elite asset pools.[S3][S4][S18][S21][S22][S29][S64][S83]

### 4. Saver claims do not displace stronger direct claims

A mediated saver story is weaker than direct worker displacement, direct local burden, direct public-service obligation, or direct member relief. Saver-side calibration therefore comes after those direct claims are screened, not before.[S4][S11][S18][S21][S22][S64][S67][S69][S83]

Axes: `classification_or_label_arbitrage`, `retirement_halo_shelter`, `subsidy_lock_in`. Block pension pass-through theater where fees, employer capture, or high-balance sheltering absorb the subsidy.

Triggers: `distributional_skew`, `gender_pension_gap`, `high_balance_growth`, `protected_floor_or_incidence_shift`. Reopen on fee/employer capture, high-balance dominance, persistent gaps, or weak saver proceeds.
