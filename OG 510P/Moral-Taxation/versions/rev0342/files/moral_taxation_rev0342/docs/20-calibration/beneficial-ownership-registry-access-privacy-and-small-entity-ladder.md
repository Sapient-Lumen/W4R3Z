## Ten-gate ladder

1. **risk gate** — identify legal person, trust, nominee, real estate, bank account, cross-border structure, or low-risk entity.
2. **control gate** — define beneficial owner, substantial control, settlor, trustee, protector, and beneficiary.[S528][S529]
3. **availability gate** — ensure adequate, accurate, and up-to-date information is available to competent authorities.
4. **verification gate** — test ID, address, entity, and control claims without fake precision.
5. **access gate** — tier access by tax/AML authority, law enforcement, obliged entity, public-interest use, and public display.
6. **privacy gate** — protect minors, survivors, threatened owners, ordinary households, and low-risk small entities.
7. **burden gate** — simplify filing, assistance, updates, and correction for small entities.[S526][S527]
8. **gap gate** — compensate for exemptions with targeted high-risk triggers or alternative data.
9. **penalty gate** — reserve penalties for nonresponse, falsehood, or reckless disregard after correction opportunity.
10. **review gate** — track shell use, compliance cost, privacy incidents, and authority response time.[S530]

## Current-law/currentness warning

For United States BOI routing, Rev0287 treats the 2025 FinCEN interim final-rule posture as a volatile current-law anchor rather than a stable background fact: domestic entities and U.S. persons are currently exempted from BOI reporting under the narrowed rule, while foreign reporting-company obligations remain status-sensitive.[S526][S527] The cube should therefore refresh this route on final rulemaking, litigation, congressional amendment, or FinCEN guidance changes rather than treating either full registry access or broad domestic-company reporting as settled.
