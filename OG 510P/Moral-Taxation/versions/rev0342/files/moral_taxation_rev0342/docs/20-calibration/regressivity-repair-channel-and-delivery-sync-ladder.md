# Regressivity repair channel and delivery-sync ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that **broad or corrective taxes should usually stay legible and be repaired through explicit floor protection rather than exemption jungles**, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when no extra correction is needed, when cash, bill-side, payroll-side, service-side, or local repair must match the burden channel, and when the design should be rejected because relief is late, high-friction, or aimed at the wrong people?**[S3][S4][S21][S31][S64][S65][S66][S69][S87][S88]

Route: keep broad or corrective bases legible when that improves honesty and collection, but do not call a proposal justified unless floor protection arrives through the right channel, on the right timing, and with low enough friction to be real. Reduced rates, exemptions, and ad hoc price suppression can sometimes soften burdens, but they are not the archive's default when more targeted repair is available.[S3][S4][S21][S64][S65][S66][S69][S87][S88]

## Option scan

| Option | Shape | Archive verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A — reduced-rate and exemption default | repair regressivity mainly by narrowing the base, lowering rates on politically legible goods, or suppressing the visible charge at point of sale or billing.[S3][S87][S88] | Reject: often poorly targeted, obscures fiscal cost, and weakens the corrective signal. |
| B — broad tax plus annual or discretionary compensation | keep the base broad and rely mainly on later refunds, hardship funds, or case-by-case politics.[S4][S64][S65][S66] | Reject: leaves protected people floating the state or the transition. |
| C — burden-matched repair ladder | sort proposals into no-extra-correction, cash, bill/payroll, service-capability, or redesign/block lanes.[S3][S4][S21][S31][S64][S65][S66][S69][S87][S88] | Adopt. |
| D — universal local earmarking for all burdens | treat most regressive pressure as a reason to route proceeds into local or sector funds instead of direct household- or worker-side repair.[S69] | Reject: local sharing sometimes helps, but it is not a substitute for real floor protection where households or workers bear the burden. |

## Five-rung ladder

1. **no-extra-correction lane** — use only when the burden is genuinely expected to stay on concentrated rent, windfall, or monopoly surplus and protected humans are not plausible final bearers,[S21][S69]
2. **cash or dividend lane** — use when broad household price-side burdens are predictable and timely cash repair can preserve the floor without shredding the base,[S4][S69][S87]
3. **bill-side or payroll-side lane** — use when the burden arrives through recurring utility, transport, housing, or wage channels and repair needs the same cadence,[S21][S31][S65][S66][S88]
4. **service-and-capability lane** — use when the real hardship is access to childcare, disability accommodation, retrofit capacity, transit, relocation support, or similar non-cash bottlenecks,[S31][S69]
5. **redesign-or-block lane** — use when relief is late, discretionary, household-shadowed, hard to claim, or still leaves protected people below the floor.[S4][S31][S64][S65][S66]

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt **Option C — the burden-matched repair ladder** as the archive's default calibration for live regressivity disputes.[S3][S4][S21][S31][S64][S65][S66][S69][S87][S88]

Presumption:

- stay in the **no-extra-correction lane** only when burden is genuinely sticking to concentrated rents or supra-normal gains,
- move to the **cash or dividend lane** when broad price-side burdens are foreseeable and visible payments can land in time,
- use the **bill-side or payroll-side lane** when burdens arrive through recurring bills or wage packets,
- escalate to the **service-and-capability lane** when the real hardship is access rather than price,
- and move to **redesign-or-block** whenever relief is late, discretionary, household-shadowed, low take-up, or mainly captured by higher spenders.

That is the narrowest workable setting because it keeps the archive's preference for broad, legible bases while refusing to let “we offered some compensation” count when the compensation is mistimed, misrouted, or morally too weak.

For AI-era taxes, the implication is practical: **controller-side collection does not settle the distributive problem. When AI-linked burdens show up as household energy costs, local grid strain, worker displacement, or digital-access pressure, repair should match those channels directly — through dividends, bill credits, transition support, local repair, or services — rather than disappearing into generic future promises.**[S21][S67][S69]

## Default repair table

| Base or context | Default repair rung | Why this rung usually fits | Archive warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| narrow rent taxes on land, monopoly rents, windfalls, or inheritances | no-extra-correction lane unless indirect household pressure is still foreseeable | the tax is aimed at concentrated surplus rather than routine subsistence flows.[S21][S69] | do not mistake legal incidence for moral incidence if downstream markets are unusually captive. |
| broad VAT or sales-tax reform | cash or dividend lane | broad consumption taxes can stay legible if lower-income households get visible, timely cash repair rather than a maze of reduced rates.[S3][S4][S87] | do not treat reduced rates on politically legible goods as a full substitute for reliable floor protection. |
| carbon, fuel, and household-energy pricing | bill-side or cash lane, often with service-and-capability support on top | the burden is recurring, salient, and tied to transition constraints, so households need timely protection and viable alternatives.[S69][S88] | price suppression for everyone is often expensive and weakly targeted; do not leave low-income households to wait for annual claims. |
| payroll-side burdens, low-wage withholding, or work-related charges | payroll-side lane | the pressure arrives directly in take-home pay, so repair should often live in pay packets, earnings support, or immediate negative-tax treatment.[S21][S31][S65][S66] | annual reconciliation is too weak where routine pay already sits near the protected floor. |
| housing, utility, and locality-linked burden shifts | bill-side or local-repair lane | recurring essentials and place-bound strains are felt through bills and local service capacity, not just annual accounts.[S21][S31][S69] | do not call a place-based tax progressive if tenants or residents bear the strain while local repair remains speculative. |
| childcare, disability accommodation, eldercare, and other care-heavy contexts | service-and-capability lane, sometimes paired with cash support | access constraints and recurring care demands are often not solved by cash alone.[S31][S64][S65] | do not force care repair through household fusion or dominant-partner control. |
| AI-linked automation dividends or infrastructure levies | cash / dividend lane plus local-repair or transition-service support where displacement or host burden is visible | AI gains are controller-side, but the repair burden often falls on workers, households, and host localities.[S67][S69] | do not let controller-side sophistication coexist with worker-side or locality-side application mazes. |
