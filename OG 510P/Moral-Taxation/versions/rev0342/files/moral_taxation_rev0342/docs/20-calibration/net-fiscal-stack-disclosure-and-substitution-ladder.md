# Net-fiscal-stack disclosure and substitution ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that morally serious taxation must be judged on the **net fiscal stack** rather than on the headline levy alone, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when support may remain inside the tax code, when it should be moved on-budget or into an explicit public contract, and when it should be barred altogether because it cancels or reverses the claimed burden?**[S10][S15][S29][S30][S31][S59][S60][S63][S70][S71][S72]

The constitutional question is already settled: **tax expenditures, abatements, discounted public inputs, loans, guarantees, and direct subsidies belong on the same moral ledger as explicit taxes; household floor protection is not morally equivalent to incumbent privilege; and support that is genuinely justified should usually become visible, costed, reviewable public support rather than a hidden offset living beside the headline tax.**[S29][S30][S31][S59][S60][S63][S71]

## Why calibration is still needed

The closed rule settles the direction but not yet the smallest operating ladder. A workable calibration still has to avoid five distinct failures at once:

1. **headline-tax theater** where a levy looks serious only because the offsetting tax expenditure, guarantee, utility discount, or infrastructure giveaway is hard to see,[S29][S30][S71]
2. **tax-code warehousing** where targeted industrial policy is buried in tax relief even when an appropriation, contract, or auction would be more contestable and easier to review,[S59][S60][S63][S71]
3. **floor-protection confusion** where household rebates and care or low-income support are rhetorically fused with investor-side privilege so that both survive or both are attacked together,[S31][S71]
4. **AI and infrastructure contradiction** where governments claim to discipline concentrated or locally burdensome facilities while quietly repaying them through cheap land, power, water, grid access, tax holidays, or sovereign-risk absorption,[S10][S15][S70][S71]
5. and **permanent exceptionalism** where support introduced for transition, resilience, or place-based bargaining keeps running after the public-purpose case has weakened because no costing, sunset, or review path was built in.[S59][S60][S63][S71][S72]

## Small option set

### Option A — code-first permissiveness

Allow support to remain wherever it lands so long as some statute authorizes it: tax expenditures in the tax code, guarantees off to one side, utility discounts in a side agreement, and local infrastructure concessions in bargaining memos. This is the easiest arrangement for incumbents and the hardest for democratic control. It predictably understates real fiscal cost and lets the state claim toughness with one hand while quietly returning value with the other.[S29][S30][S71]

### Option B — disclosure only

Publish a tax-expenditure report and perhaps a list of guarantees, but leave the policy stack otherwise intact. This is better than opacity, and sometimes it is the first politically available step, but it still leaves hard choices scattered across tax law, utility regulation, development deals, and contingent-liability management.[S29][S59][S71]

### Option C — equal-footing substitution default

Treat tax expenditures, grants, subsidised inputs, loans, and guarantees as alternative policy instruments that should be compared on equal footing. Keep support in the tax code only where it is broad, rule-like, and closely tied to the ordinary base. Move targeted, actor-specific, or bargain-dependent support on-budget or into explicit public contracts with visible cost, conditions, review, and expiry.[S59][S60][S63][S71]

This is the cleanest general default because it preserves simple household-side integration where that is genuinely functional, but stops using the tax code as a shelter for hard distributive choices.

### Option D — contradiction and concentration bar

Where the surrounding support would directly undermine a corrective tax, deepen concentrated bottleneck power, or repay actors for burdens the public is already being asked to bear, do not merely reclassify the support; **bar it** unless the measure is narrowly structured for open access, public capacity, resilience, or clearly shared benefits that cannot be delivered more cleanly another way.[S10][S15][S30][S70][S71][S72]

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt a **five-step practical ladder**:

| Stage | When it should apply | Support answer | Main guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. protected-floor lane | household rebates, child support, disability support, low-income credits, care-side support | **may remain integrated with the tax-and-transfer system** | must be visible in the net stack, easy to receive, and defended as floor protection rather than investor privilege |
| 1. broad rule-like tax lane | simple, generally available relief tied closely to the ordinary base rather than bargaining with named actors | **may remain in the tax code** | keep it rule-bound, publicly costed, and non-negotiated |
| 2. targeted public-purpose lane | actor-specific or sector-specific support justified by public capacity, transition, resilience, or access goals | **move on-budget or into an explicit public contract** | require a stated objective, fiscal cost, review cadence, and expiry or conversion path |
| 3. contingent-liability / public-input lane | loans, guarantees, cheap public land, power, water, grid access, sovereign compute, or bespoke infrastructure concessions | **treat as fiscal support even if not booked as ordinary spending** | disclose risk, price the concession, and usually prefer contracts, auctions, or regulated access conditions over hidden tax-side accommodation |
| 4. contradiction bar | support that repays pollution, congestion, monopoly, fossil dependence, rent extraction, or concentrated AI bottleneck power | **bar the support, not merely disclose it** | exception only where the support is genuinely public-facing, open-access, and narrower than the harm or rent being corrected |

The archive's provisional setting is therefore **stage 0 for genuine floor protection, stage 1 only for broad rule-like relief, stage 2 as the default home for targeted public-purpose support, stage 3 for guarantees and public-input deals with explicit fiscal-risk treatment, and stage 4 wherever support would morally cancel or reverse the headline burden**.[S29][S30][S31][S59][S60][S63][S71][S72]

That is the narrowest workable recommendation because it keeps the tax code from becoming a warehouse for opaque industrial policy, preserves integrated delivery where the point is household protection rather than favoritism, and forces governments to admit when they are not taxing a sector so much as **taxing and reimbursing it at the same time**.

For present AI stacks, the practical consequence is straightforward: **broad household-side energy or access protection can live in stages 0 to 1; public compute or resilience support can sometimes justify stages 2 to 3 when access, interoperability, and review are explicit; but bespoke data-centre abatements, discounted electricity, water concessions, or guarantee-heavy siting packages for dominant controllers should usually be treated as stage-4 contradictions rather than as neutral development policy.**[S10][S15][S30][S63][S70][S71][S72]
