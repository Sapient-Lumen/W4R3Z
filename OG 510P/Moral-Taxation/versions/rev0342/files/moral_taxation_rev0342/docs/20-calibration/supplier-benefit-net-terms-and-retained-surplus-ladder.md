# Supplier benefit / creator-contractor terms / retained-surplus ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that **real upstream pass-through to outside suppliers, creators, contractors, licensors, franchisees, or other complementors can narrow a downstream beneficiary-side windfall claim, but only when the improvement is outside the group, durable enough, and net-positive**, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when counterpart benefit is real, when the case is mixed, and when a beneficiary-side charge should still attach to retained exceptional surplus?**[S10][S11][S14][S18][S21][S22][S26][S27][S28][S34][S35][S67][S69][S83][S104]

The constitutional answer is already closed: **tax retained concentrated gain where it remains, but do not pretend that real outside pass-through to independent counterparties is the same thing as retained private windfall.** What remains open is how much evidence and what kind of netting should be enough before the archive narrows or retires a beneficiary-side charge.[S10][S11][S14][S18][S21][S22][S26][S27][S28][S34][S35][S67][S69][S83][S104]

## Why calibration is still needed

Once the archive recognizes supplier, creator, and contractor pass-through as morally relevant, five practical failures remain easy:

1. **gross-not-net inflation** where higher payouts are counted while new fees, exclusivity, faster pace, or shifted risk quietly eat the gain back,
2. **affiliate laundering** where the apparent pass-through runs through related-party labels, captive vendors, or house marketplaces rather than real outside claimants,
3. **mixed-case denial** where some gain really diffuses outward but a large retained exceptional residual still exists,
4. **dependency romanticism** where counterparties are nominally paid more but remain structurally weak, easy to squeeze, or locally burdened,
5. **double-counting of benefit** where the same slice is treated both as vanished windfall and as new earmarkable proceeds.[S10][S11][S14][S18][S21][S22][S26][S27][S28][S34][S35][S67][S69][S83][S104]

## Small option set

### Option A — ignore supplier pass-through for calibration

Keep the ordinary or beneficiary-side firm levy and treat counterpart payments as morally irrelevant beyond ordinary income taxation of the recipients.[S10][S18][S21][S22]

This is strongest where the claimed pass-through is trivial, selective, or obviously strategic. It is too blunt where competition or bargaining has really pushed a meaningful share of the gain outward.

### Option B — recognize only proved net outside benefit

Keep the firm-side lane, but require proof that counterparties are independent outsiders and that the improvement remains positive after fees, lock-in, pacing, and shifted risk are netted out.[S10][S11][S14][S21][S22][S26][S27][S67][S83][S104]

This is strongest where pass-through is plausible but easy to fake. It is too thin when the archive already knows the case is mixed and needs a split rather than a yes-or-no answer.

### Option C — residual-only beneficiary charge

Where real outside pass-through is shown, narrow any beneficiary-side charge to the retained exceptional residual rather than gross activity or the full headline gain.[S10][S11][S21][S22][S28][S67][S83]

This is strongest where both diffusion and retention are materially real. It is too incomplete when dependency, local burden, or private-ordering abuse still require visible companion safeguards.

### Option D — layered net-pass-through ladder

Use a layered answer:

1. **screen group boundaries first** so affiliates and captive channels do not count as outside pass-through,
2. **measure net counterpart benefit rather than gross payment,**
3. **identify any retained exceptional residual** after the real outside share is counted,
4. **tax only that retained residual on the beneficiary lane,** and
5. **keep dependency, worker, local-burden, and controller-side review alive** where those companion claims are real.[S10][S11][S14][S18][S21][S22][S26][S27][S28][S34][S35][S67][S69][S83][S104]

This is the archive's cleanest default because it prevents both over-taxing genuinely diffused gain and under-taxing concentrated surplus hidden behind counterpart-payment rhetoric.

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt **Option D** as the default calibration ladder once the archive has already decided the case belongs in the supplier / creator / contractor pass-through lane.

That means:

1. **prove outsider status first** before any pass-through is credited,[S10][S18][S21][S27][S34][S35][S83]
2. **net the counterpart story** for fees, exclusivity, pace, shifted risk, tighter terms, and recapture rather than stopping at headline payouts,[S11][S14][S21][S22][S26][S27][S67][S83][S104]
3. **leave ordinary taxes on received income intact** for the counterparties while narrowing only the beneficiary-side charge on the retained residual,[S10][S11][S21][S22][S28][S67][S83]
4. **publish mixed-case reasoning explicitly** instead of forcing an all-or-nothing label,[S10][S11][S14][S21][S22][S67][S83]
5. **attach companion review where dependence or local strain remains visible** so better payments do not erase worker, supplier, or locality vulnerability.[S14][S21][S22][S26][S27][S67][S69][S83][S104]

## Default net-pass-through ladder

| Lane | When it should apply | Default answer | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. no-credit lane | claimed pass-through is trivial, too selective, too new, or too weakly evidenced | keep the ordinary or beneficiary-side firm lane unchanged | do not invent pass-through relief from ceremonial bonuses or isolated deals |
| 1. outsider-check lane | higher payments are visible but independence, breadth, or durability is still unclear | require group-screening, contract review, and evidence before narrowing the firm-side claim | do not let affiliates, shells, or captive marketplaces count as outside benefit.[S10][S18][S21][S27][S34][S35][S83] |
| 2. net-benefit lane | independent counterparties are broadly receiving better net terms and retained margins are no longer exceptional | ordinary tax plus counterpart-side income taxation is usually enough; no special beneficiary charge | do not confuse gross payouts with real net benefit.[S11][S14][S21][S22][S26][S27][S67][S83][S104] |
| 3. mixed residual lane | some real outside pass-through exists but a retained exceptional residual remains | ordinary tax plus a narrower beneficiary-side charge only on the retained exceptional slice | tax the residual, not the gross activity, and publish the split logic.[S10][S11][S21][S22][S28][S67][S83] |
| 4. dependency-sensitive lane | counterparties are being paid more but remain heavily dependent, locked in, or exposed to concentrated local strain | combine any narrowed firm-side claim with incidence, contract-integrity, and local-burden review | do not treat higher pay alone as a full answer to weak bargaining or local strain.[S14][S21][S22][S26][S27][S67][S69][S83][S104] |
| 5. recapture override | the apparent pass-through is being clawed back through fees, exclusivity, house channels, or related-party routing | treat the case as retained private gain on the real group actor | do not let fee recapture or dependency theater launder concentrated surplus.[S10][S18][S21][S27][S34][S35][S83][S104] |

## Four calibration rules that matter most

### 1. Net improvement matters more than headline payout

The archive cares about whether outside counterparties are actually better off, not whether a firm can point to a larger gross royalty line or contractor spend. If risk, lock-in, pace, or fee recapture rise at the same time, the net pass-through may be much smaller or even illusory.[S11][S14][S21][S22][S26][S27][S67][S83][S104]

### 2. True outside claimants matter more than contractual form

A payment is not social diffusion merely because a contract crosses an entity boundary. The archive credits pass-through only when the counterparties are real outsiders rather than affiliates, nominees, house labels, or captive vendors inside the coordinated group.[S10][S18][S21][S27][S34][S35][S83]

### 3. Mixed cases should isolate the retained exceptional slice

The cleanest answer is usually not to choose between "full windfall" and "no windfall." It is to identify the part of the gain that truly diffused outward and the part that still sits as concentrated exceptional surplus, then tax only the latter on the beneficiary lane.[S10][S11][S21][S22][S28][S67][S83]

### 4. Pass-through does not erase companion moral claims

Even real counterpart benefit can coexist with worker displacement, local service strain, supplier dependence, or controller-side duties. The supplier-pass-through lane narrows a beneficiary-side charge only to the extent it answers that specific concentration question; it does not automatically cancel the archive's other lanes.[S14][S21][S22][S26][S27][S67][S69][S83][S104]
