## Option scan

| Option | Shape | Archive verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A — pure gross-revenue tax | tax digital advertising receipts by platform size and user location | Risky unless base, apportionment, thresholds, and cascade relief are precise. |
| B — bottleneck/rent charge | target dominant gatekeepers, auction spreads, data advantage, or ad-tech intermediation rents | Stronger when market power rather than small publisher activity is the real base. |
| C — privacy/attention harm charge | price extraction of personal data, addictive attention design, or opaque targeting | Use only with data minimization and proceeds repair; do not license privacy harm. |
| D — invoice/pass-through speech rule | require or forbid certain disclosure of tax-related charges | Treat as speech/salience issue; disclosure can be required for clarity but bans on truthful explanation are suspect. |
| E — publisher or local-media support | route proceeds to journalism, privacy enforcement, or public-interest media | Possible only with viewpoint neutrality and anti-capture design. |

## Parameter ladder

1. **base definition** — Define digital advertising services, ad-tech intermediation, user location, affiliate revenue, and non-ad platform receipts separately.
2. **threshold and small-actor floor** — Exclude or credit small publishers, small advertisers, and low-margin intermediaries unless they are the rent holder.
3. **apportionment proof** — Use auditable user-location or market-sourcing rules with safe harbors and correction paths.
4. **gross-receipts cascade test** — Measure whether the tax stacks through multiple ad-tech layers and squeezes publishers/advertisers rather than platforms.
5. **speech and salience rule** — Do not ban truthful invoice or customer communication about tax-driven price changes; use neutral disclosure if clarity is needed.
6. **privacy extraction test** — Separate revenue from surveillance/data extraction and avoid making payment a license for rights-invasive targeting.
7. **pass-through evidence** — Trace price, commission, ad-rate, and publisher-payment changes after enactment.
8. **proceeds route** — Route to privacy enforcement, public-interest media, digital literacy, or affected users; record anti-supplantation.
9. **current-law label** — Mark challenged, enjoined, remanded, final, or proposed status for each jurisdiction.
10. **review trigger** — Reopen after court ruling, apportionment litigation, pass-through spike, publisher squeeze, or platform restructuring.

## Default settings

| Parameter | Default | Redesign trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Pass-through speech | Truthful itemization allowed; deception barred | Ban on truthful invoice explanation or compelled misleading label. |
| Base | Dominant ad/data rent with clear sourcing | All digital communication or publisher revenue swept in. |
| Thresholds | Target bottleneck scale and market power | Small publisher/advertiser squeeze. |
| Proceeds | Neutral media/privacy/public-capacity repair | General-fund absorption or viewpoint-selective media subsidy. |
| Review cadence | Litigation-triggered and annual incidence review | No update after court ruling or market redesign. |

## Anti-pattern definitions

- **invoice speech ban** — the state suppresses truthful explanation of tax-related price changes to hide incidence.
- **attention rent theater** — a tax claims to price attention harm while leaving targeting, privacy, and addiction design untouched.
- **gross receipts cascade** — multiple ad-tech layers pass the same burden through until non-rent actors bear it.
- **small publisher squeeze** — local or independent media lose ad revenue while dominant platforms keep bottleneck power.
- **apportionment fiction** — user-location or revenue-sourcing rules are too noisy to support coercive liability.
