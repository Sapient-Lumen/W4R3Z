# Controller-map verification, sampling, and review-intensity ladder

## Question in one sentence

Once the archive already knows **who owes a controller map**, **what it must contain**, **who may see it**, **how integrity and preservation work**, and **how provisional filing continues while disputes remain open**, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when facial completeness is enough, when targeted cross-checks or event samples are needed, and when stronger or sealed review is justified without defaulting to universal deep audit?**[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S39][S40][S41][S44][S83]

Route: verify only the **contested governance claim** at the lightest review intensity that can really test it. The archive should not collapse into either packet-trusting formalism or universal surveillance-by-audit.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S39][S40][S41][S44][S83]

## Option scan

| Option | Shape | Archive verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A — attestation-only lane | accept signed controller maps unless a whistleblower or catastrophic contradiction appears.[S20][S21][S27][S41] | Reject: makes elegant paperwork too close to dispositive. |
| B — universal deep-audit lane | demand broad logs, contracts, and operational replay for every serious controller map.[S16][S17][S39][S40][S41][S44] | Reject: too privacy-costly, too coercive, and too expensive as a default. |
| C — tiered verification lane | start with completeness checks, use targeted cross-checks and bounded event samples, and escalate only on contradiction, opacity, or high stakes.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S39][S40][S41][S44][S83] | Adopt. |
| D — complaints-only lane | verify only when weaker outsiders or affected parties reconstruct the contradiction themselves.[S16][S17][S27][S39][S41] | Reject: puts too much burden on the least informed parties. |

## Five-rung ladder

1. **facial-completeness lane** — confirm that the map is current, signed, period-bounded, internally complete, and not obviously inconsistent with the actor's prior packet, provisional filing posture, or declared redaction basis.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S41]
2. **targeted cross-check lane** — test only the named governance claims against already-available anchors such as deployment approvals, update gates, reserve authority, procurement scope, registry filings, or main payment / hosting / channel relationships.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S39][S41][S83]
3. **bounded event-sample lane** — where real control may turn on practice rather than titles, sample a small number of material events: updates, pauses, release approvals, capacity allocations, incident responses, successor handoffs, or revenue / loss decisions.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S39][S41][S83]
4. **contradiction-escalation lane** — if the sample clashes with the map, if material events are missing, or if redaction hides the decisive governance fact, escalate through stronger packet demands, harder presumptions, or provisional controller-group treatment rather than pretending the contradiction does not matter.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S39][S40][S41][S83]
5. **sealed or independent-review lane** — use supervised sealed inspection, third-party verification, or court / regulator-private review only for genuinely high-stakes, security-sensitive, or persistently contradictory disputes, and return the result as the shortest reviewable conclusion possible rather than a general disclosure dump.[S16][S17][S21][S27][S39][S40][S41][S44]

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt **Option C — tiered verification lane** as the archive's default review rule for controller maps.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S39][S40][S41][S44][S83]

Presumption:

- a current signed map with no concrete mismatch should usually face only **facial completeness** review,
- a live contest over named governance levers should trigger **targeted cross-checks** rather than broad discovery,
- practice-sensitive disputes about mission control, successor continuity, step-in command, or co-controller layering should use a **bounded event sample**,
- contradictions, strategic opacity, and sham redaction should **escalate** rather than staying in the light-touch lane,
- and security-sensitive or unusually consequential cases may justify **sealed or independent review**, but only after narrower lanes are inadequate.

This is the archive's narrowest workable setting because it preserves reviewability and correction without turning every controller dispute into either blind trust or permanent operational surveillance.

## Default verification matrix

| Live posture | Minimum review intensity | Why it usually fits | Archive warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| current signed map, low contradiction, routine filing | facial completeness | most ordinary disputes are about timeliness or missing fields, not hidden command.[S16][S17][S21][S27][S41] | do not manufacture deep audit just because the packet names many actors. |
| named dispute over who can approve, halt, deploy, update, or allocate capacity | targeted cross-check | the issue is a bounded governance claim that existing records can often test.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S39][S41][S83] | keep the check claim-specific; do not reopen the whole stack. |
| practice-sensitive conflict over co-control, successor continuity, or step-in command | bounded event sample | a few real events often show who actually governed the layer.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S39][S41] | sample material events, not entire communications archives. |
| stale, selectively incomplete, contradictory, or sham-redacted map | contradiction escalation | stronger review is justified because the packet has lost its correction-first presumption.[S16][S17][S20][S21][S27][S39][S40][S41][S83] | escalate proportionately; do not jump straight to maximal seizure of records. |
| high-stakes dispute with real security or trade-secrecy concerns | sealed or independent review | the archive still needs verification, but not general public disclosure.[S16][S17][S27][S39][S40][S41][S44] | return the shortest contestable conclusion possible. |
