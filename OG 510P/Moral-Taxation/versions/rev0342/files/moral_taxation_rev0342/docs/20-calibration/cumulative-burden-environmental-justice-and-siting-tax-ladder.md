# Cumulative-burden, environmental-justice, and siting-tax ladder

## Question in one sentence

When siting, abatements, credits, mitigation fees, or taxes affect an already overburdened place, when should the result be mitigation, denial, relocation, rebate, community ownership, or no-go rather than another average-incidence story?[S638][S639][S640]

## Option scan

| Option | Shape | Archive verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A — average burden review | evaluate the project or tax by jurisdiction-wide incidence or emission totals | Insufficient where a specific community already carries stacked burdens. |
| B — cumulative-load screen | use pollution, health, socioeconomic, infrastructure, and climate indicators before approval | Baseline requirement for overburdened places. |
| C — mitigation-first condition | approve only with enforceable exposure reduction and monitoring | Useful when harm is compensable and net burden falls. |
| D — community-benefit agreement | money, jobs, ownership, or services flow to the affected community | Supplement only; cannot purchase permission for non-compensable harm. |
| E — denial/no-go or relocation | block or relocate project/abatement when cumulative burden or rights harm is too high | Required when mitigation cannot protect health, housing, mobility, or sovereignty floors. |

## Parameter ladder

1. **screening threshold** — Set a cumulative-burden index or qualitative trigger before fiscal incentives are considered.
2. **burden inventory** — Count pollution, asthma, noise, freight, heat, water, utility arrears, tax burden, housing stress, and enforcement history.
3. **no-go categories** — Identify harms that cannot be priced: serious health deterioration, displacement, tribal/indigenous sovereignty breach, or irreversible ecological loss.
4. **abatement netting** — Net tax abatements, utility subsidies, infrastructure costs, and public services against promised benefits.
5. **mitigation enforceability** — Require measurable exposure reduction, deadlines, independent monitoring, and penalties.
6. **community voice** — Use affected-community consent/objection processes with language, disability, and anti-retaliation protection.
7. **proceeds and ownership** — Route funds to local repair, ratepayer relief, community ownership, or health monitoring with anti-supplantation.
8. **fallback when tools change** — If federal or state screening tools are withdrawn or revised, keep local burden evidence and independent review alive.
9. **distributional proof** — Use disaggregated place-based data, not average progressivity or county-level proxies.
10. **review trigger** — Reopen on exposure exceedance, monitoring failure, tool change, permit modification, benefit breach, or health-disparity spike.

## Default settings

| Parameter | Default | Redesign trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Initial gate | Cumulative screen before tax credit/abatement approval | Fiscal incentive considered before burden screen. |
| Approval mode | Mitigation-first or denial when cumulative load is high | Small grants substitute for exposure reduction. |
| Data | Tool plus local evidence plus community monitoring | One index used mechanically or no disaggregated data. |
| Proceeds | Local repair and anti-supplantation | General fund or project PR fund. |
| Review cadence | Permit/event-triggered plus annual monitoring | No review after construction or abatement award. |


## Failure-mode capsule

Axes: `abatement_for_pollution`, `harm_pricing_or_offset_theater`, `opacity_or_erasure`, `sacrifice_zone`. Sacrifice-zone accounting: incentives, permits, indices, or benefit money approve new exposure instead of reducing cumulative load.


## Recalibration trigger capsule

Triggers: `protected_floor_or_incidence_shift`, `screening_tool_removed`, `harm_or_resilience_threshold_breach`. Reopen when tools change, monitoring contradicts index data, thresholds breach, or proceeds substitute for exposure reduction.
