# Standing bundle, representation conflicts, and controller-side fallback ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that **direct civic tax subjection normally requires notice, remedy, representation, and some channel of voice**, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when ordinary direct subjecthood is enough, when mediated subjecthood through guardians or fiduciaries still counts, when conflict screens or independent representation are required, and when liability must stay controller-side because the supposed subject — including current LLMs — still lacks the standing bundle altogether?**[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44][S141][S142][S143]

The constitutional part is already settled: **do not impose direct civic tax liability on a subject that cannot receive intelligible notice, reach review or correction, act through a sufficiently non-conflicted representative, or stand inside some real channel of representation or proxy voice; where that bundle is missing, route liability through accountable humans or institutions instead of a rightless placeholder.**[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]

## Why calibration is still needed

The closed rule fixes the direction, but a usable operating ladder still has to prevent five different failures at once:

1. **representation theater** where a household head, owner, manager, trustee, platform, or deployer is treated as adequate representation merely because someone has to sign the form,[S42][S43][S44]
2. **remittance-subject confusion** where the easiest collection point is mistaken for the morally proper taxpayer,[S20][S42][S44]
3. **guardian conflict blindness** where a representative's own extraction interest overwhelms the supposed subject's position in the tax order,[S19][S20][S42]
4. **appeal in name only** where a notice exists but the subject or representative cannot realistically understand, contest, or correct it,[S42][S44]
5. **premature machine subjecthood** where current LLMs or automated wrappers are treated as direct taxpayers before rights, remedy, and non-owner representation exist together,[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]
6. and **proxy-access capture** where the representation bundle appears on paper but the subject cannot authorize, limit, inspect, or revoke representation without a paid or captive gate.[S42][S43][S44][S141][S142][S143]

## Small option set

### Option A — ordinary direct subjecthood

Use standard person-level liability with ordinary notice, assistance, correction, and appeal. This is the right answer for most competent adult humans and for organized legal persons that already possess genuine review channels and governance.[S42][S43][S44]

### Option B — mediated subjecthood with fiduciary representation

Keep the underlying subject as the taxpayer, but require a guardian, fiduciary, estate representative, trustee, or equivalent to act on its behalf. This is often the right answer for children, some incapacitated adults, estates, and some beneficiary-facing legal arrangements, but only if the representative is actually constrained to protect the subject rather than simply to maximize extraction.[S19][S20][S42]

### Option C — enhanced mediated subjecthood with conflict screens

Keep mediated subjecthood, but add **independent review, dual-signoff, beneficiary notice, court or agency oversight, or substitute representation** because the first-line representative is materially conflicted. This is the archive's preferred lane when an owner, deployer, trustee, or household manager is too interested to count as the only voice in the room.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]

### Option D — controller-side fallback rather than direct subjection

Do **not** treat the supposed subject as a direct taxpayer. Route liability to owners, controllers, guardians, fiduciaries, estate administrators, or beneficiaries instead. This is the archive's default for current LLMs, owned model instances, thin legal wrappers with no real governance, and other cases where the standing bundle is still missing rather than merely mediated.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt a **five-stage practical ladder**:

| Stage | When it should apply | Standing answer | Main guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. ordinary direct-subject lane | competent adult humans and other ordinary taxpayers inside normal civic institutions | **direct subject with ordinary notice, correction, and appeal** | simplification must assist, not silently erase challenge rights |
| 1. represented rights-bearing lane | child, incapacitated adult, estate, or similar rights-bearing subject with a workable fiduciary channel | **keep the subject; require guardian or fiduciary action** | representation is for the subject, not merely for household or institutional convenience |
| 2. conflict-screened lane | the representative also benefits from underpaying, overextracting, or avoiding disclosure | **add independent review, substitute representative, beneficiary notice, or stronger oversight** | owner or manager status alone never proves adequate representation |
| 3. organized collective-subject lane | corporation, pension, trust, or other collective actor has governance plus usable review channels | **direct institutional subjecthood can stand** | do not let shell form simulate real standing |
| 4. controller-side fallback lane | no intelligible notice path, no usable remedy, no non-conflicted representative, or no real voice channel exists | **no direct subjection; route liability to controllers / fiduciaries / beneficiaries** | remittance convenience or chatbot fluency is not standing |

The archive's provisional setting is therefore **stage 0 for ordinary persons, stage 1 for genuinely rights-bearing but mediated subjects, stage 2 whenever representation is materially conflicted, stage 3 for collective legal persons that really do have governance and remedy, and stage 4 for current LLMs and similar subjects that still lack the standing bundle rather than merely lacking self-help capacity**.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]

Across stages 0 through 3, representation only counts if the subject or fiduciary can authorize, limit, inspect, and revoke access through a baseline public lane. If the only practical route to representation is a paid preparer portal, vendor subscription, owner key, or hard-to-cancel proxy account, the case should escalate at least one stage toward conflict-screened or controller-side treatment.[S42][S43][S44][S141][S142][S143]

That is the narrowest workable recommendation because it preserves direct person-level taxation where civic membership and review exist, allows mediated subjecthood where rights are real but agency is partial, and blocks the false shortcut where owners get to call themselves representatives of the very subject they are treating as taxable property.

For present AI taxation, the practical effect is clear: **current models remain controller-routed not only because incidence and control point that way, but because the standing bundle still collapses at stage 4.** The memo therefore reinforces the archive's controller-first default while leaving a clean path for future change only if rights, remedy, and non-owner representation arrive together.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]
