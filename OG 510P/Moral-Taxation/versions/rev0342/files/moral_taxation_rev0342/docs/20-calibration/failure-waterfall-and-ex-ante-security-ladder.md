# Failure-waterfall and ex ante security ladder

## Question in one sentence

When ordinary insolvency priority, trust-like segregation, wages, victim repair, cleanup, bonds, reserves, insurance, controller guarantees, and shell regrouping collide, what is the smallest ladder that prevents predictable downside from being dumped on workers, victims, localities, or public backstops?[S15][S16][S17][S19][S20][S34][S35][S47][S48][S49]

## Practical rule

Routine tax debts should not become general kings in insolvency. Narrow custodial or trust-like collections can justify segregation or stronger priority; wages, estate-preserving administration, serious cleanup, and victim-repair claims need stronger protection than routine treasury recovery; and foreseeable high-downside operations should usually be secured before failure through bonds, reserves, insurance, escrow, decommissioning funds, or controller guarantees.[S15][S16][S17][S34][S35][S47][S48][S49]

The route therefore asks two questions before ranking claims: **was the amount collected or held for someone else, and was the downside predictable enough that ex ante security should have existed?** If the second answer is yes, ordinary post-collapse priority is already too late.[S15][S16][S17][S47]

## Five-stage ladder

| Stage | Use when | Default move | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. ordinary tax lane | routine assessed public claims with no custodial feature or unusual downside gap | ordinary insolvency ranking | no treasury superpriority by status alone |
| 1. custodial lane | withheld payroll, collected VAT/GST, escrowed levies, or similar held-for-another amounts | segregation, tracing, or narrow priority | do not let the custodial lane swallow all taxes |
| 2. floor-and-repair lane | wages, administration, cleanup, or victim repair would be subordinated to routine tax recovery | shield floor/repair claims | do not make workers or localities involuntary fiscal lenders |
| 3. prefunded-risk lane | hazardous, extractive, infrastructure-heavy, or frontier-AI downside can outrun the estate | bond, reserve, insurance, escrow, or decommissioning fund | size and reset security to real hazard, concentration, and scale |
| 4. controller-backstop lane | shell splitting, undercapitalization, affiliate extraction, or AI-stack fragmentation makes stage 3 unreliable | controller guarantee, regrouping, look-through, or denial of limited-liability shelter for the downside | escalate on control/extraction facts, not mere complexity |

## Default settings

| Posture | Instrument | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| routine assessed tax | ordinary ranking | no automatic treasury leapfrog. |
| withheld/collected amount | narrow trust/custodial priority | keep the category tight. |
| wage, cleanup, victim, or estate-preserving claim | floor-and-repair shield | do not subordinate real repair to routine revenue. |
| foreseeable high downside | ex ante security | do not wait for bankruptcy to discover underfunding. |
| thin shell or affiliate extraction | controller backstop | route responsibility to real control and upside capture. |

For AI, energy, infrastructure, extraction, and other stack-split projects, the practical consequence is simple: do not wait for a failed SPV, data-center subsidiary, deployment shell, or contractor tier to reveal unfunded repair, worker, victim, or public-backstop claims. Where concentrated compute, energy, decommissioning, cleanup, or safety burdens are predictable, the archive prefers stage-3 security or stage-4 controller backstops over broad post-collapse tax priority.[S15][S16][S17][S19][S20][S34][S35]

Triggers: `controller_or_accountability_drift`, `harm_or_resilience_threshold_breach`, `proceeds_or_surplus_trace_failure`, `public_backstop_used`. Reopen when security underperforms, priority crowds out repair, disclosure improves, a backstop is used, or correlated downside rises.
