# Threshold-cliff smoothing and graduation ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that **thresholds should protect dignity and administrative tractability but usually prefer slopes to cliffs**, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when to use a zero band, smooth taper, marginal step-up, graduation window, or hard cutoff without turning thresholds into pay-rise penalties, bunching magnets, or permanent privilege shelters?**[S26][S31][S80][S81][S82]

## Option scan

| Option | Shape | Archive verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A — hard cliffs as routine design | use sharp eligibility cutoffs, full allowance withdrawal points, and hard regime switches whenever they are easy to administer.[S80][S82] | Reject: predictably creates bunching, pay-rise penalties, and threshold gaming. |
| B — smooth everything | replace nearly every boundary with a continuous taper even where the underlying administrative or governance problem is genuinely categorical.[S26][S80] | Reject: can make the system opaque, expensive, and morally blurry about when an on-ramp ends or a stronger governance lane begins. |
| C — taper-and-graduation default | use de minimis screens, smooth tapers, graduation windows, marginal step-ups, and reserve hard cutoffs for narrow cases where the underlying need is genuinely discontinuous.[S26][S31][S80][S81][S82] | Adopt. |
| D — broad notches plus ex post hardship repair | keep sharp cliffs for speed, then patch harm through appeals, waivers, or discretionary relief.[S31][S80][S82] | Reject: converts design failure into an access-and-advice contest. |

## Five-rung ladder

1. **zero band or de minimis screen** — use where the point is simply to keep trivial liability or filing out of the system,[S31][S80]
2. **smooth taper or ordinary marginal band** — default for person-level income taxes, credits, and means-tested supports where work incentives and floor integrity matter,[S31][S80][S81][S82]
3. **graduation window** — use for simplified small-firm, small-landlord, or similar on-ramp regimes so crossing the line triggers managed migration rather than one-step punishment,[S26][S80]
4. **marginal step-up without retroactive bite** — use where a stronger schedule is justified but should apply only to the slice above the line,[S80][S81]
5. **narrow hard cutoff or notch** — reserve for genuinely categorical triggers such as filing obligations, permit classes, or governance gates, and usually pair with a small jump, buffer, or transition allowance.[S16][S17][S23][S25][S80]

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt **Option C — the taper-and-graduation default** as the archive's default calibration for threshold design.[S26][S31][S80][S81][S82]

Presumption:

- use **de minimis screens** for dignity and filing-cost reasons,
- use **smooth tapers or ordinary marginal bands** for recurring human-income and means-tested transitions unless a discontinuity is morally unavoidable,
- use **graduation windows** when moving simplified or small-scale actors into fuller compliance,
- allow **hard cutoffs** only where the triggered obligation is genuinely categorical and the jump is small, buffered, or explicitly justified,
- and reopen the rule whenever bunching, pay-rise penalties, or repeated threshold splitting show that the chosen boundary is doing too much moral work.[S26][S31][S80][S81][S82]

That is the narrowest workable setting because it protects the floor, preserves intelligible boundaries, and treats threshold discontinuities as exceptions rather than as the normal design language.

## Default boundary table

| Base or context | Default boundary shape | Why this shape usually fits | Archive warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| subsistence floor, filing minima, trivial liability screens | zero band or de minimis cutoff | the main task is to keep tiny obligations and floor claims out of unnecessary filing or collection.[S31][S80] | do not let a de minimis line become a broad pay-rise penalty when benefits disappear just above it. |
| ordinary labor-income schedules | marginal bands, not retroactive step taxes | progressive rates can rise without making the threshold dollar bear the whole schedule change.[S31][S80][S81] | do not stack allowance withdrawal, benefit withdrawal, and tax onset in one income band. |
| means-tested supports, in-work credits, and rebates | smooth taper with visible earnings disregard where needed | protection and work incentives both improve when support declines gradually rather than disappearing at one line.[S31][S81][S82] | do not defend formal targeting with cliff edges that leave households worse off after more work or a small raise. |
| simplified small-firm or presumptive regimes | graduation window | the regime is an on-ramp, so migration into fuller accounting should be staged rather than punished in one jump.[S26][S80] | do not let repeated bunching or splitting freeze the regime into permanent privilege. |
| permits, registry classes, or higher-governance AI / risk triggers | narrow hard cutoff, often with short transition buffer | some governance obligations are genuinely categorical and cannot be tapered indefinitely.[S16][S17][S80] | keep the jump tied to the governance need; do not smuggle ordinary tax extraction into a faux permit threshold. |
| annual wealth, valuation, or high-admin regimes | high hard entry threshold plus review and bounded transition | the administrative burden is real enough that entry can be categorical, but the line should be high, grouped honestly, and reviewed.[S23][S25][S73][S80] | do not use a hard threshold if controller splitting makes the line easy to farm. |
