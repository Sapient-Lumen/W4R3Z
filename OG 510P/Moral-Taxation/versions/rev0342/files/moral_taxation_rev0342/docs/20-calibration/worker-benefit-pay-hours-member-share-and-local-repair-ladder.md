# Worker benefit / pay / hours / member-share / local-repair ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that **worker-owned, broadly employee-owned, and other labor-residual firms using AI should usually route gains first through broad worker reciprocity rather than ordinary shareholder-windfall language**, **what is the smallest workable ladder for deciding when the gain should appear as higher pay, shorter hours, member distributions, training and transition support, resilient reserve, or local repair instead of dark retained surplus or thin paper ownership value?**[S10][S11][S13][S14][S18][S21][S22][S26][S27][S29][S67][S69][S83]

The constitutional answer is already closed: **where labor is genuinely the broad residual claimant, the first fiscal question is how AI-enabled gain becomes visible worker benefit rather than generic shareholder-style windfall or dark retained surplus, while real outsider extraction, real local burdens, and real controller duties still travel on their own lanes.**[S10][S11][S13][S14][S18][S21][S22][S26][S27][S29][S67][S69][S83]

## Why calibration is still needed

Once the archive routes these cases away from ordinary downstream windfall language, five failures remain easy:

1. **paper-worker benefit** where gains are celebrated as labor-owned but show up mainly as abstract share value or distant exit upside,
2. **pay-only tunnel vision** where every gain is forced into wages even when shorter hours, training, transition support, or safer pacing are the more urgent worker good,
3. **reserve-hoarding** where management keeps the gain as opaque retained slack while invoking resilience,
4. **local-erasure** where member gains are praised while burdened towns, suppliers, or displaced worker communities carry concentrated strain,
5. **outsider siphoning** where landlords, lenders, vendors, managers, or affiliates take the practical upside while the worker-owned form does reputational cover work.[S10][S11][S14][S18][S21][S22][S29][S34][S35][S67][S69][S83]

## Small option set

### Option A — pay-first

Push most visible gain into wages, cash bonuses, or member distributions.[S11][S14][S18][S21][S22][S26][S27][S67]

This is strongest where workers are underpaid, inflation-hit, or visibly bearing the live burden. It is too thin when exhaustion, overwork, unsafe pacing, retraining load, or local strain are the more urgent moral defects.

### Option B — time-and-conditions first

Push most gain into shorter hours, safer pace, staffing buffers, schedule stability, and similar non-cash worker benefit.[S11][S14][S18][S21][S22][S67]

This is strongest where AI is mainly removing drudgery or intensifying surveillance and pace rather than just lifting revenue. It is too weak when workers mainly need income security, transition cash, or member-side capital access.

### Option C — transition-and-resilience first

Keep most gain in training, redeployment, income smoothing, member reserve, cooperative capitalization, and similar resilience channels.[S11][S14][S18][S21][S22][S26][S27][S67]

This is strongest where automation is changing task mix quickly or where the firm needs a real buffer to avoid collapse or opportunistic outside refinancing. It is too weak when "future resilience" becomes an excuse for management hoarding or when current workers see no visible share.

### Option D — layered worker-reciprocity split

Use a layered answer:

1. **floor and burden repair first** where workers or nearby communities are absorbing the live cost,
2. **pay and time benefit next** where compensation, hours, or pace remain the clearest worker-facing injustice,
3. **member-share or distribution next** where the cooperative or employee-owned form is real and broad,
4. **training and resilient reserve last** inside a named anti-hoarding corridor with review triggers.[S10][S11][S14][S18][S21][S22][S26][S27][S29][S67][S69][S83]

This is the archive's cleanest default because it makes labor reciprocity visible without pretending every worker-owned firm should distribute gains in the same channel.

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt **Option D** as the default calibration ladder once the archive has already decided the case belongs in the worker-owned / labor-residual lane.

That means:

1. **screen outsider extraction first** so executive, landlord, lender, vendor, retiree-owner, or affiliate capture is taxed on its real private lane rather than smuggled into worker benefit,[S10][S18][S21][S29][S34][S35][S83]
2. **repair live worker burden first** where AI gains arrive alongside layoffs, intensification, unsafe pace, involuntary hour loss, or retraining load,[S11][S14][S18][S21][S22][S67]
3. **make at least one worker-facing gain channel highly visible** through pay, hours, schedule security, member distributions, or comparable present benefit,[S11][S14][S18][S21][S22][S26][S27][S67]
4. **attach local-repair shares where burden is actually concentrated** instead of treating internal member gain as the only moral claimant,[S11][S14][S21][S22][S67][S69]
5. **allow reserve and future-capacity retention only inside a named corridor** with maintenance-of-effort, member disclosure, and review triggers so worker-owned form does not become a new dark-treasury excuse.[S18][S21][S22][S26][S27][S29][S83]

## Default worker-reciprocity ladder

| Lane | When it should apply | Default use of gain | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. ordinary small-gain lane | gains are modest, uncertain, or mostly offsetting ordinary volatility | ordinary pay-setting, disclosure, and ordinary taxation only | do not invent ceremonial worker-sharing formulas for trivial or unclear gains |
| 1. burden-repair lane | layoffs, pace intensification, involuntary hour loss, retraining load, or local strain are already visible | transition support, wage floors, hour guarantees, training, and local repair first | do not celebrate member gain while current workers or burdened places absorb the shock.[S11][S14][S21][S22][S67][S69] |
| 2. pay-or-time lane | the firm is broadly stable and the clearest live injustice is worker undercompensation, overwork, or bad scheduling | higher pay, shorter hours, safer pace, schedule stability, or equivalent direct worker benefit | do not let all benefit vanish into paper share value or retained slack.[S11][S14][S18][S21][S22][S67] |
| 3. member-share lane | the labor-residual form is broad and real, and current workers already have decent pay/time protection | member dividends, patronage distributions, or similar broad worker-owner payout | do not confuse narrow elite partnership distributions or tradable paper gains with broad worker reciprocity.[S13][S18][S21][S22][S26][S27][S83] |
| 4. layered worker-benefit lane | several worker-facing claims are simultaneously real | blended split across burden repair, pay or time benefit, broad member share, training, reserve, and local repair | publish the split, baseline, worker coverage, outsider screens, and review cadence.[S10][S11][S18][S21][S22][S29][S67][S69][S83] |
| 5. extraction override | worker-owned form is present but the practical gain is being siphoned to managers, financiers, landlords, vendors, or affiliates | ordinary/private extraction taxes and controls on the real takers | do not let labor-residual branding shield outsider capture.[S10][S18][S21][S29][S34][S35][S83] |

## Four calibration rules that matter most

### 1. Worker reciprocity must become present benefit, not only asset rhetoric

If the archive is going to protect a case from ordinary shareholder-windfall language, some substantial share of the gain should become visible to current workers in present-tense terms such as pay, time, security, training, or broad member distribution. Pure appreciation in paper ownership claims is weaker evidence, especially where current working conditions worsen.[S11][S14][S18][S21][S22][S26][S27][S67][S83]

### 2. Time and pace can be as morally important as money

Automation often changes work by removing drudgery or by intensifying monitoring and pace. In those settings, shorter hours, safer staffing, and schedule security can be the morally central worker-facing output even when headline wages do not move first.[S11][S14][S18][S21][S22][S67]

### 3. Reserve is legitimate only when it is broad-worker protective and reviewable

Worker-owned firms may need retained capacity for downturns, capital replacement, or member transition support. But the archive accepts reserve retention only when the purpose is named, the corridor is bounded, outsiders are screened, and current workers still see visible benefit rather than an endless promise of future strength.[S18][S21][S22][S26][S27][S29][S83]

### 4. Local repair remains a real companion claim

A labor-residual firm can still impose concentrated burdens on a town, supplier base, or displaced worker pool. Worker reciprocity is therefore not the whole answer when burdens are externalized. Attach local-repair shares where the gain is riding on visible local cost.[S11][S14][S21][S22][S67][S69]

## Failure-mode capsule

Axes: `classification_or_label_arbitrage`, `opacity_or_erasure`, `worker_funded_fringe`. Block worker-reciprocity theater where managers, platforms, financiers, or affiliates retain the surplus.

## Recalibration trigger capsule

Triggers: `benefit_adequacy_failure`, `protected_floor_or_incidence_shift`. Reopen on missing pay, time, security, training, member share, or local repair.
