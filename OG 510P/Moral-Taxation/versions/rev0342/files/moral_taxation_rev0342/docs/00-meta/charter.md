# Charter

Moral Taxation exists to design a morally serious tax constitution for human and AI-shaped economies without collapsing together:

- **persons** and **tools**,
- **ability-to-pay** and **mere throughput**,
- **productive effort** and **scarcity rent**,
- **public finance** and **punitive symbolism**,
- **administrative legibility** and **bureaucratic ornament**.

## Primary aims

1. Preserve a compact but serious answer to what ought to be taxed first.
2. Separate tax **subjects** from tax **bases**.
3. Keep the archive honest about the difference between current LLMs and hypothetical artificial persons.
4. Prefer taxes that are morally defensible, economically coherent, and administratively possible at scale.
5. Keep the archive small enough for fast re-entry.

## What counts as progress

Progress means converting vague intuitions into stable surfaces:

- tax-ordering rules,
- class/context/species matrices,
- specific instrument notes,
- source-backed constraints,
- open questions with named decision criteria.

## Failure modes to resist

- taxing what is visible instead of what is responsible,
- taxing labor because it is easy while leaving rents lightly touched,
- treating current AI systems as quasi-persons for rhetorical effect,
- using complexity as camouflage for privilege,
- inflating the archive with secondary bulk instead of judgment.
