# Tax subjecthood and liability routing

This note answers the question that comes **before** rates: who is the direct civic tax subject, what base is being taxed, who remits first, who is likely to bear the real burden, and who must have standing to contest the result? The archive's stable answer is that current AI changes the **routing map** more than the **person map**.[S19][S20][S21]

## One-line thesis

Tax justice should route direct liability to rights-bearing persons, controller groups, and place-bound assets according to control, benefit, burden-bearing, and standing. Current LLMs are not yet direct tax persons; they are tax-relevant technologies inside human and firm liability chains.[S10][S19][S20][S21][S34][S35][S42][S43]

## The five roles

1. **Direct subject** — who is answerable in civic terms.
2. **Tax base** — what is actually being measured and taxed.
3. **Remitter** — which actor is used for first collection.
4. **Final bearer** — who is likely to end up poorer after pass-through and bargaining.
5. **Reviewer / representative** — who can receive notice, contest the claim, and speak for the subject where needed.[S21][S42][S43][S44]

Do not collapse these roles into one box. A tax can be well-aimed in base but misrouted in subject, collection point, or burden-bearing.

## Routing table

| Case | Direct civic subject | Best base or liability target | First remittance / collection anchor | Protected bearer | Main routing rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Human person below the floor | the person, but with zero or negative net burden | none directly beyond ordinary withholding mechanics for later reconciliation | payroll / transfer administration | subsistence, care, disability, and child-support floor must stay protected | when the state already knows enough, route support toward automatic or prefilled delivery rather than maze-like claiming.[S4][S31][S64][S65][S66] |
| Ordinary adult human earner | the individual person | earnings above the protected floor | wage withholding and pay-as-you-go filing | do not silently dump the burden onto dependents, weaker partners, or low-income consumers | default the assessment unit to the person, not compulsory jointness.[S2][S21][S31][S32][S33] |
| Household or couple | usually **not** the default direct civic subject | only the information needed for support targeting, care recognition, or anti-avoidance | transfer and filing systems as needed | intra-household inequality and second-earner independence should be protected | use household data as a modifier, not as a license to erase a weaker adult into a stronger adult's tax identity.[S31][S32][S33] |
| Small firm, cooperative, or thin-margin producer | the legal entity for remittance, but judged with attention to owners, workers, and loss years | simple cash-flow, withholding, and modest presumptive proxies where really needed | low-complexity filing and withholding | workers and fragile suppliers should not become the hidden cushion for collection difficulty | simplicity is for legibility and graduation, not permanent sheltering or threshold gaming.[S26][S27][S50][S51] |
| Large corporation, dominant platform, or coordinated controller group | the firm or grouped firms as legal remitters | residual profit, rent, anti-shifting, monopoly, and other controller-side bases | group reporting, minimum-tax backstops, and anti-fragmentation rules | protected workers, small suppliers, and low-income users should not be the silent residual payers | follow beneficial ownership and related-party control where shell splitting would fragment one moral target into many taxpayers.[S9][S21][S28][S34][S35] |
| Land parcel, extractive site, or other immovable base | the owner or controlling holder of the immovable base | site value, location rent, severance, depletion, and cleanup-related charges | cadastre, parcel roll, utility interface, or resource registry | illiquid but resident humans may need deferral, but immovable rent should not disappear | situs and local burden are stronger anchors than paper booking geography.[S5][S23][S25] |
| Current LLM or model as such | **not** a direct civic taxpayer now | none directly as a person; tax the controller-side stack instead | none directly on the model | do not make the model the bearer of liability merely because it is salient | present systems lack the standing bundle needed for direct civic subjection and remain embedded in human responsibility chains.[S19][S20][S42][S43] |
| Frontier AI stack: lab, cloud, facility owner, deployer, large beneficiary | humans and firms controlling the stack | compute scarcity, electricity, water, carbon, excess profits, funded downside risk, and windfall-like gains | facilities, utilities, controller groups, licenses, bonds, and reserve requirements | displaced workers and low-income users should not be the cleanup fund | route liability through the strongest control points in the order facility -> lab -> deployer -> beneficiary, while checking final incidence.[S10][S11][S15][S16][S17][S21][S22][S67] |
| Possible future artificial person | the artificial person itself, **only if** rights-bearing threshold plus standing are actually present | person-level ability-to-pay after a protected floor | direct administration only with independent representation and remedy | no ownership and no substrate surcharge once personhood is recognized | once the threshold is crossed, move from property logic to person logic rather than mixing both opportunistically.[S18][S19][S20][S42][S43][S44] |

## Hard defaults

### 1. Separate subject from remitter

The strongest collection point is not always the morally primary subject. Wage withholding may sit at the employer; VAT remittance may sit at the seller; controller-side AI levies may sit at a facility or cloud intermediary. That does not settle who is the true bearer or what justice requires.[S3][S21]

### 2. Protect the bearer before celebrating the bill

A tax aimed at a firm, platform, or utility is still badly routed if the predictable final burden lands mainly on people whose floor the archive is committed to protect. Rate design, rebates, dividends, competition checks, or re-routing may all be needed.[S4][S21][S31]

### 3. Household is usually context, not personhood

The archive uses household information for care, dependency, and means-testing only with caution. The default direct human tax subject for ordinary earnings remains the individual person.[S31][S32][S33]

### 4. Current LLMs change controller liability, not civic personhood

Present models can change where rents, harms, and bargaining power concentrate, but that still points the archive toward controllers, deployers, facilities, and beneficiaries rather than toward a machine head tax.[S10][S11][S15][S19][S20][S22][S67]

### 5. No direct tax subjection without standing

Where notice, contestability, representation, and some path of voice are absent, direct civic taxation is misrouted. Move liability to a guardian, estate representative, owner, controller, or beneficiary instead.[S42][S43][S44]

## Jurisdiction echo

The routing question also helps decide **where** liability belongs:

- situs for land, extractive sites, and other immovable bases,
- burdened locality for congestion, grid strain, cooling-water stress, and similar place-bound harms,
- destination for final consumption,
- market access for some share of large cross-border residual profit,
- and treaty or club coordination for mobile rents that otherwise outrun any one state.[S3][S5][S9][S15][S28]
