# Measurement, valuation, and proxy routing

This note answers the question that comes **after** choosing a morally plausible tax base but **before** rates, collection, or enforcement can honestly begin: **what evidence is good enough to measure the base without pretending to know more than the system really knows?** The archive's stable answer is to prefer observable bases, scheduled revaluation, disclosed proxy hierarchies, narrow safe harbors, and contestable estimates over fake precision or metaphysical accounting.[S10][S15][S16][S23][S25][S26][S27]

## Core rule

Route measurement through the strongest evidence stack in this order:

1. **direct observation of the base**,
2. **regularly updated valuation rules**,
3. **proxied measurement that is openly acknowledged as proxy**,
4. **narrow simplifications or safe harbors for genuinely small or noisy cases**,
5. and **reviewable challenge paths whenever a proxy, estimate, or model stands in for direct fact**.[S16][S23][S25][S26][S27]

A measurement regime is good when it is honest about uncertainty, cheap enough for ordinary compliance, hard enough to game, and revisable before approximation hardens into privilege.

## Default measurement anchors by base or context

| Base or context | Best first measurement anchor | Why this anchor usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wage and salary income | payroll records and third-party reporting | regular earned flows are already legible and cheap to verify | withholding convenience must not become proof that the worker is the right final burden-bearer.[S21][S27][S65][S66] |
| Broad consumption taxes | transaction records and seller-side remittance logs | the taxable event is visible at sale | exemption jungles should not be used to compensate for distributional problems better solved through rebates or transfers.[S3][S4][S21] |
| Land and immovable property | cadastre plus scheduled mass appraisal or other published valuation method | immovable bases suit standardized, reviewable valuation better than case-by-case improvisation | stale values quietly become privilege or overcharge if revaluation cadence collapses.[S5][S23][S25] |
| Small firms and informal or thin-record activities | simple cash-flow, turnover, or other presumptive proxy with graduation rules | some proxy is better than impossible bookkeeping when capacity is genuinely low | simplification for the small should not become permanent shelter for the larger or better-advised.[S16][S26][S27] |
| Large corporations and mobile profit | audited accounts, anti-fragmentation grouping, and minimum-tax / apportionment backstops | strong entities can bear fuller measurement and create larger gaming incentives | accounting visibility should not be mistaken for moral truth where rents are shifted through affiliates or booking theatre.[S9][S21][S28][S34][S35] |
| Pollution, energy, water stress, congestion, and other measurable harms | upstream fuel content, utility-scale meters, monitored use, or other physical indicators | physical traces are often more observable than downstream social value | do not confuse a measurable physical unit with the full moral account of harm.[S6][S7][S15][S16] |
| Current AI facilities, models, and deployers | electricity draw, accelerator-hours, facility class, deployment revenue tier, user-seat scale, incident reporting, and other disclosed proxies | current AI stacks are easier to meter through infrastructure and commercial traces than through claims about consciousness or social value | token counts, parameter counts, or compute proxies are administrative stand-ins, not direct measures of value, harm, or personhood.[S10][S15][S16][S17][S67] |
| Future rights-bearing artificial person | person-like account, income, asset, and transfer records only after rights-bearing status is real | once the subject becomes a civic person, measurement should track person-like reciprocity rather than equipment metrics | substrate or hardware proxies should not survive merely because they remain easier to meter than the rights-bearing subject.[S18][S19][S20][S42][S44] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Move outward from the hidden moral target to the nearest observable base

If the morally attractive target cannot be measured without fantasy accounting, move one level outward to a proxy that can actually be observed and explained. Site value often beats speculative annual wealth appraisal; upstream fuel content often beats diffuse downstream emissions guessing; controller-side AI infrastructure may beat end-user token flow.[S5][S6][S10][S15][S16][S25]

### 2. Treat revaluation cadence as part of justice

A good base can become unjust when the valuation rule stops moving. Land, property, thresholds, safe harbors, and administratively set amounts should be revalued, recalibrated, or indexed on a visible schedule rather than left to drift until politics notices.[S23][S25]

### 3. Use proxies as instruments, not revelations

A proxy is acceptable because it is administrable, not because it captures the whole moral reality. Electricity use can stand in for some AI burdens; turnover can stand in for small-firm capacity; market sales can stand in for capital value. None of these should be mistaken for the thing itself.[S10][S15][S16][S26]

### 4. Keep safe harbors narrow, reviewable, and anti-camping

Safe harbors and simplified regimes are justified when they lower compliance cost or handle noisy measurement. They fail when entry thresholds are easily gamed, when firms can camp inside them indefinitely, or when review never arrives.[S26][S27]

### 5. Publish the variables that matter and the path to challenge them

If an estimate, model, or mass-appraisal rule helps determine liability, the taxpayer should be able to see the main inputs, understand the broad logic, and challenge material mistakes without an expert priesthood. A hidden score is not yet fit to anchor coercive collection.[S16][S25][S27]

### 6. Do not let measurement convenience choose the tax subject

The easiest meter is not always the right taxpayer. Current LLMs remain controller-routed not because measurement is impossible, but because metering convenience does not settle personhood, standing, or final incidence.[S10][S19][S20]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Fake precision** — presenting a noisy or speculative base as exact.
2. **Stale valuation** — letting old assessments, frozen thresholds, or nominal drift do hidden distributive work.
3. **Proxy idolatry** — mistaking an administrable stand-in for the full moral target.
4. **Threshold camping** — building simplifications that reward bunching, shell-splitting, or permanent smallness theatre.
5. **Opaque coercion** — using vendor models, secret scoring, or unexplained estimates as if they were self-validating.[S16][S23][S25][S26][S27]

## Order of preference

When more than one measurement regime is available, the archive usually prefers the one that is:

1. most directly observable,
2. easiest to explain and challenge,
3. least likely to go stale without scheduled upkeep,
4. hardest to arbitrage through fragmentation or timing,
5. cheapest for genuinely small actors without becoming a shelter,
6. and least likely to be mistaken for evidence of machine personhood or total social value.

This is why payroll records, seller-side transaction logs, cadastres with regular revaluation, upstream harm meters, grouped corporate accounts, and controller-side AI proxies usually beat bespoke annual guesses, hidden risk scores, token poll taxes, or permanent small-firm presumptions.
