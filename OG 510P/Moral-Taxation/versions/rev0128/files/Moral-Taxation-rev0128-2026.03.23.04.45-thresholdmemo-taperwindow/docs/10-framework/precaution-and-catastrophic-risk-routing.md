# Precaution, catastrophic-risk, and non-compensable-harm routing

This note answers the question that comes **before** a corrective tax, risk fee, or AI levy is allowed to do moral work at all: **is the activity a priceable harm that taxation can help govern, a governable but high-risk activity that needs tax plus ex ante controls, or a catastrophic / non-compensable risk that should be gated before revenue logic?** The archive's stable answer is to separate **pricing ordinary burdens** from **selling permission for harms that would be morally too late to repair after the fact**.[S15][S17][S53][S54]

## Core rule

Route risky activities through this order:

1. **use ordinary taxation or pricing where the harm is measurable enough, reversible enough, and compensable enough that ex post correction is morally intelligible**,  
2. **use tax-plus-governance where the activity may continue only under ex ante approval, conditions, monitoring, reserve funding, or tiered constraints**,  
3. **use bonds, reserves, insurance, safety cases, and decommissioning powers before profits are freely distributed where downside could outrun ordinary compensation**,  
4. **treat taxes and fees as sidecars rather than moral licenses where the real issue is catastrophic, irreversible, or non-compensable risk**,  
5. and **prefer refusal, pause, or hard caps over tax-as-permit theater where no plausible charge would make the activity morally acceptable**.[S15][S16][S17][S47][S48][S49][S53][S54]

A levy can be technically collectible and still be morally defective if it mainly converts an approval decision into a revenue stream.

## Default routing by context

| Context | Best first fiscal / governance path | Why this path usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary pollution, congestion, or routine measurable externality | corrective tax or charge with visible household protection | the harm is recurrent, measurable enough to price, and partly remediable through behavior change plus revenue recycling | do not let distributive protection depend on weak take-up or hidden regressivity.[S6][S7][S64][S65][S66] |
| Landfill, toxic-storage, or extraction activity with serious but containable downside | permit plus charge, reserve fund, bond, and cleanup assurance | operation may continue, but ex post tax collection alone is too weak if cleanup is front-loaded or operator failure is plausible | do not wait until insolvency to discover that public cleanup outruns the tax base.[S16][S47][S48][S49][S53] |
| Data-centre, cooling-water, or grid-intensive siting with concentrated local burdens | siting approval, utility conditions, local-burden charges, and reserve / infrastructure contributions | local systems can be overloaded even where national gains look attractive | do not treat local fiscal pressure as fully answered by distant national revenue or vague future compensation.[S15][S23][S69][S70] |
| Current frontier-model lab or major deployer with serious deployment tail risk | approval, tiered deployment conditions, incident reserve, bond, insurance, and controller-side supervision, with taxes as sidecars | the core issue is not only energy or rent, but whether the system should scale under current mitigations at all | do not let controller-paid fees be rebranded as proof that the deployment is safe enough to proceed.[S10][S15][S17][S21][S53][S54] |
| Activity whose main downside is existential, irreversible, or not realistically compensable after the fact | refusal, moratorium, hard cap, or very narrow conditional approval | ex post pricing cannot repair the main wrong | expected-value pricing is a category mistake where catastrophe dominates the moral picture.[S15][S17][S53][S54] |
| Future rights-bearing artificial person engaged in ordinary economic activity | ordinary person-regime taxation plus whatever sectoral safety governance the activity itself requires | once rights-bearing status is crossed, substrate is not the tax reason; the activity is | do not use machine exceptionalism to impose blanket precaution taxes after personhood is recognized.[S18][S19][S20][S54] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Separate pricing from permission

A tax can price an ordinary burden without conferring moral authorization. The archive therefore treats taxation and approval as analytically distinct: some activities are taxable because they are allowed, while others are allowed only if separate approval conditions are met first.[S6][S7][S53][S54]

### 2. Use taxation first only where ex post correction is morally real

Taxation is strongest where the harm is observable enough to measure, delayed correction is not hopelessly late, and money can actually contribute to repair, adaptation, or deterrence. Where those conditions fail, taxation should recede behind ex ante governance.[S6][S7][S15][S53]

### 3. Move from tax to tax-plus-governance when downside can outrun ordinary compensation

Many activities sit between routine externality and outright prohibition. In that middle band, the archive prefers charges that sit beside permitting, monitoring, reserve funding, or tiered operating conditions rather than pretending a tax alone has answered the risk question.[S15][S16][S17][S53][S54]

### 4. Fund catastrophic downside before upside is distributed

Where public cleanup, victim repair, or emergency response may exceed the ordinary tax base, the morally earlier tools are bonds, reserves, insurance, escrow, decommissioning duties, or controller guarantees. Insolvency priority fights are a bad substitute for advance funding when the risk was visible ex ante.[S16][S47][S48][S49]

### 5. Keep controller-first AI taxation inside controller-first AI governance

Current LLM systems are still taxed through facilities, controllers, deployers, and beneficiaries, not as direct civic persons. The same controller-first logic applies to precaution: if frontier deployment, autonomous use, or facility concentration creates serious tail risk, the state should supervise the controller edge first through approval conditions, reserves, audits, and shutdown authority, with taxes and fees only supplementing that structure.[S10][S15][S17][S19][S20][S53][S54]

### 6. Keep precaution proportionate, reviewable, and non-rent-seeking

The archive rejects two equal mistakes: pretending catastrophic risk can be priced like any other externality, and using precaution as a blank check for arbitrary power or rent extraction. Ex ante controls should be tied to articulated risk thresholds, contestable evidence, review cadence, and visible exit or tightening paths.[S53][S54][S59][S60][S61]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Tax-as-permit theater** — a levy is treated as moral authorization for a risk that should have faced prior approval or refusal.  
2. **Revenue capture of oversight** — authorities keep a risky activity alive mainly because the fees are attractive.  
3. **Expected-value moral laundering** — catastrophic or irreversible harms are flattened into a price where money cannot really repair the wrong.  
4. **Failure-state substitution** — instead of requiring bonds, reserves, or controller guarantees up front, the system waits for collapse and then improvises from insolvency law.  
5. **Controller laundering** — labs, deployers, or site owners point to taxes paid as if fiscal contribution had discharged their duties of safety, testing, monitoring, or shutdown.[S15][S16][S17][S47][S48][S49][S53][S54]

## Order of preference

When more than one approach is available, the archive usually prefers the approach that is:

1. clearer about whether the activity is being **priced**, **conditionally allowed**, or **refused**,
2. less reliant on post hoc repair where the main harms are irreversible,
3. stronger at forcing ex ante reserves, bonds, or controller commitments before upside distribution,
4. less likely to convert public risk tolerance into a hidden revenue dependency,
5. and more reviewable as evidence changes.

This is why ordinary corrective taxes beat prohibition for routine emissions or congestion, permit-plus-charge beats tax-only design for serious but governable risk, and hard gating or disapproval beats revenue logic where catastrophe or non-compensability dominates the case.
