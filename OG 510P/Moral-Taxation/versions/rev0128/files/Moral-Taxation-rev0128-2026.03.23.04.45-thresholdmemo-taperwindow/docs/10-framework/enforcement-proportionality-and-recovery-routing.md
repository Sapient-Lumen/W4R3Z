# Enforcement proportionality and recovery routing

This note answers the question that comes **after** a tax base, subject, timing rule, and first remittance anchor look defensible but **before** the state escalates from assessment into recovery: **how should enforcement proceed without turning justified taxation into domination, standing surveillance, or hardship-blind extraction?** The archive's stable answer is to enforce through the least intrusive path that can still preserve compliance, protect the floor, reach strategic evaders, and keep model-assisted action reviewable and bounded by purpose.[S16][S17][S27][S39][S40][S41][S44]

## Core rule

Route recovery through this order:

1. **clarify the asserted liability in an intelligible notice before coercive recovery accelerates**,  
2. **prefer reminders, correction, offsets, instalments, and hardship-adjusted payment paths before seizure or exclusion**,  
3. **aim strong enforcement first at actors with capacity, concealment power, or strategic evasion rather than at weak diffuse subjects**,  
4. **bound data access, retention, matching, and model-assisted triage by purpose and review**,  
5. and **use irreversible or highly coercive measures only where proportionality, contestability, and floor protection still hold**.[S16][S17][S27][S39][S40][S41][S44]

A tax can be morally justified in base and still fail in enforcement if recovery depends on opacity, humiliation, unnecessary surveillance, or pressure that predictably strips the protected floor.

## Default routing by context

| Context | Best first enforcement path | Why this path usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary wage or salary underpayment | notice, explanation, withholding adjustment, and affordable payment plan | regular flows make correction easier than punitive escalation | do not convert small errors into cascading fees, account freezes, or floor-stripping garnishment.[S16][S27][S39][S41] |
| Broad consumption-tax non-compliance by households | seller-side correction or merchant remittance review rather than end-user pursuit | the stronger intermediary usually holds the records and can fix the problem upstream | do not externalize weak-system errors onto low-information consumers.[S3][S16][S27][S39] |
| Small-firm arrears with real distress | staged reminders, human review, instalments, temporary deferral, and simplified correction | viable firms should not be destroyed merely because timing was wrong | hardship lanes must not become incumbent favoritism or endless avoidance shelter.[S16][S27][S39] |
| Large-firm or cross-border evasion | large-taxpayer unit, controller-group audit, withholding backstops, and anti-fragmentation enforcement | strong actors can hide, shift, and delay at scale, so enforcement should reach real control first | do not reserve tough enforcement mainly for weak visible debtors while strategic evasion stays under-policed.[S9][S16][S17][S28][S34][S35][S39] |
| Land, property, or other immovable-base arrears | billing, lien, instalment, and sale only after visible notice and hardship screening | immovable assets support durable claims without immediate person-level coercion | avoid quick displacement where slower recovery or bounded deferral would preserve justice better.[S5][S23][S24][S27][S39] |
| Corrective taxes on fuel, extraction, carbon, congestion, or utilities | upstream enforcement at measured choke points | fewer monitored entities reduce diffuse downstream coercion | do not use environmental enforcement as a pretext for unrelated mass data expansion.[S6][S7][S15][S16][S39][S41] |
| Current frontier AI facility, cloud, or major deployer | controller-side audit, licensing consequences, reserve calls, bond replenishment, or utility / facility enforcement | concentrated AI infrastructure is easier to supervise at the controller edge than through end-user punishment | do not offload AI-linked recovery onto workers, small customers, or public victims after controller-driven risk creation.[S10][S15][S16][S17][S21][S41] |
| Future rights-bearing artificial person, if threshold is crossed | direct notice plus represented review path before coercive recovery | rights-bearing subjects require person-like contestability, not owner-substituted domination | no direct enforcement without real standing, remedy, and non-owner representation.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Escalate from explanation to coercion, not the reverse

A morally serious tax state begins with intelligible notice, correction opportunities, and predictable payment paths. Coercive recovery without that sequence is not administrative toughness; it is defective design.[S16][S27][S39][S44]

### 2. Protect the floor during recovery, not only at assessment

The protected floor can be defeated after liability is computed through garnishment, fee cascades, digital exclusion, or premature seizure. Hardship review, exempt minima, and affordable instalments are part of justice, not merely mercy.[S4][S16][S27][S39][S41]

### 3. Aim the strongest tools at strategic evasion and concentrated control

The archive prefers strong action against concealment, fragmentation, asset flight, large-controller opacity, and repeated bad-faith evasion over routine pressure on weak debtors who are easy to reach but not morally central. This matters for multinational firms, rentier asset holders, and current AI controller groups.[S9][S16][S17][S28][S34][S35][S39]

### 4. Keep model-assisted enforcement reviewable and purpose-bounded

Risk scoring, anomaly detection, and automated triage may assist enforcement, but they should not become hidden authority. The state should be able to explain the broad basis of the action, bound data use to the collection purpose, and preserve a human review path before irreversible harm occurs.[S16][S17][S40][S41][S44]

### 5. Prefer claim-preserving tools before person-destructive tools

Offsets, payment plans, liens on immovable or durable assets, reserve calls, bond replenishment, and future-flow withholding usually beat detention-like coercion, public shaming, sweeping account freezes, or default exclusion from ordinary civic life. Recovery should preserve collectability without gratuitous domination.[S16][S17][S27][S39][S41]

### 6. Separate enforcement convenience from surveillance entitlement

Available data is not automatically justified data. Cross-system matching, platform feeds, utility records, location traces, and model-generated inferences should be limited to what the tax task actually needs, retained no longer than justified, and kept contestable.[S16][S17][S40][S41]

### 7. In AI-heavy systems, keep controllers from externalizing failure-state collection

Where AI facilities, labs, or deployers create concentrated gains and concentrated risks, the archive prefers ex ante bonds, reserves, insurance, and controller guarantees over a downstream regime that waits for collapse and then chases workers, users, or local governments through improvised recovery.[S15][S17][S21][S41]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Easy-debtor bias** — enforcing hardest where records are easiest and power is weakest.  
2. **Floor stripping by process** — nominal floor protection defeated by garnishment, fees, freezes, or timing pressure.  
3. **Black-box coercion** — automated ranking or scoring that the subject cannot meaningfully understand or contest.  
4. **Surveillance creep** — treating every administratively available dataset as fair game for tax recovery.  
5. **Failure-state substitution** — skipping ex ante bonds, reserves, insurance, or strong-controller enforcement and relying on ex post extraction from weak or already harmed actors.[S15][S16][S17][S39][S40][S41]

## Order of preference

When more than one enforcement path is available, the archive usually prefers the path that is:

1. easier to explain and contest,
2. less likely to strip the protected floor,
3. stronger against strategic concealment and large-controller evasion,
4. less dependent on bulk surveillance or hidden scoring,
5. more compatible with payment over time where the base is real but liquidity is thin,
6. and less likely to displace recovery onto already injured workers, tenants, localities, or victims.

This is why staged notice, hardship review, instalments, controller-side enforcement, liens on durable assets, and ex ante risk funding usually beat fast punitive escalation, diffuse downstream extraction, or opaque algorithmic collection pressure.
