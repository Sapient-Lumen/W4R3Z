# Notice, transition, and retroactivity routing

This note answers the question that comes **after** a tax base, tax subject, and burden shape look morally defensible but **before** the state can impose the change justly: **when should new liability begin, how should people move into it, and when is any backward reach actually permissible?** The archive's stable answer is to default to prospectivity, protect legitimate reliance through bounded transition tools, use retroactivity only where a narrower answer would plainly fail, and refuse to turn certainty into permanent shelter for incumbent privilege.[S23][S27][S36][S37][S38]

## Core rule

Route major tax change through this order:

1. **published prospective notice for ordinary new liability**,
2. **bounded transition tools where real reliance, liquidity, or administrative readiness make them necessary**,
3. **narrow anti-abuse or emergency backdating only when prospectivity alone would be gamed or defeated**,
4. **time-limited grandfathering for vulnerable reliance only where simpler phase-ins or deferral will not do**,
5. and **a visible end-state so transition does not harden into indefinite incumbent privilege**.[S23][S27][S36][S37][S38]

A transition regime is good when ordinary people can understand what changes, when it starts, what happens to already-made commitments, and when temporary relief ends.

## Default routing by base or context

| Base or context | Best first notice / transition rule | Why this rule usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary personal-rate or bracket reform | future effective date plus indexed thresholds or clear phase-in | households need time to adjust payroll, estimated payments, and budgeting | do not use inflation drift or opaque withholding changes as stealth retroactivity.[S21][S27][S36][S38][S55][S58] |
| Land revaluation, site-value tax, or housing-tax reform | announced valuation date, staged revaluation, and liquidity relief where needed | the base is morally strong, but owner cash flow and stale valuations can make abrupt jumps arbitrary | do not turn illiquidity into a permanent exemption for site rent, and do not freeze old valuations forever in the name of certainty.[S5][S23][S25][S36] |
| Inheritance or wealth-transfer reform | published cutoff date plus finite anti-forestalling rule | wealthy actors can accelerate transfers once reform is announced | anti-forestalling should close the window, not license vague reach into long-closed ordinary family support.[S8][S24][S36][S37] |
| Broad corrective taxes such as carbon, congestion, or water-stress charges | visible phase-in with floor protection announced from the start | legitimacy improves when households can see both the price path and the compensating protection | do not announce the tax now and the dividend or rebate later.[S6][S7][S21][S69] |
| Small-firm simplification or graduation changes | future start date plus tapering and safe-harbor period | firms need time to adapt systems and to avoid cliff-driven bunching | do not leave a supposedly temporary threshold shelter in place indefinitely.[S26][S27][S38] |
| Current AI facility, compute, deployment, or controller-group charges | pre-announced reporting / registry obligations, then future charging date with short anti-avoidance cutoffs where needed | labs, utilities, clouds, and deployers often make long-lived siting and contract commitments | do not spring surprise charges on already-finished training cycles when a published forward rule could have reached the next cycle cleanly.[S10][S15][S17][S36][S37][S61] |
| Emergency or crisis taxes | immediate but clearly bounded start with declared trigger and review path | some crises require speed | emergency language is not a blank cheque for open-ended backdating or endless renewal.[S21][S36][S37][S59][S60][S61] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Start from prospectivity, not from administrative convenience

The archive defaults to future effective dates for major liability changes because tax coercion reaches into plans that people and firms cannot always unwind cheaply. Prospectivity is especially important where households have already committed to care, housing, schooling, retirement, or disability-related choices, and where firms or localities have already made long-lived infrastructure or compliance investments.[S23][S36][S37][S38]

### 2. Separate legitimate reliance from incumbent rent protection

The archive is more sympathetic where people are locked in by ordinary life structures or genuinely sunk productive commitments. It is less sympathetic where the main thing being preserved is speculative appreciation, monopoly privilege, fossil or extractive advantage, negotiated tax holidays, or AI-incumbent positioning built on public subsidy and entry barriers. Reliance is a discipline against arbitrary shock, not a veto on reform.[S23][S24][S36][S38]

### 3. Use transition tools that preserve direction

Good transition design uses phase-ins, installment plans, temporary safe harbors, elective realization windows, bounded deferral for illiquidity, or short anti-forestalling rules that protect legitimate adjustment without reversing the reform's moral direction. Blanket grandfathering is usually inferior because it protects the most entrenched incumbents most strongly.[S21][S23][S27][S36][S38]

### 4. Keep retroactivity narrow, named, and explainable

Backward reach is most defensible where an announcement would otherwise create an immediate gaming window, where a drafting defect or abuse scheme must be closed, or where an emergency measure is tied to a clearly declared event. Even then, the state should be able to say in ordinary language why purely prospective operation would fail and how far back the rule goes.[S21][S36][S37]

### 5. Grandfather vulnerable people, not permanent privilege

Where reliance protection is genuinely required, prefer time-bounded person-focused relief, staged phase-in, or liquidity accommodations over sector-wide indefinite carve-outs. A transition that never ends is usually not transition at all; it is a buried distributive choice to preserve incumbent advantage.[S23][S24][S36][S38]

### 6. Keep AI-era notice attached to the real control points

For present AI taxation, notice should usually attach first to registries, facility thresholds, utility interfaces, model-reporting duties, or grouped-controller obligations rather than to abstract model identities. This keeps the archive's controller-first stance intact while still giving labs, deployers, and beneficiary firms enough warning to adapt before the next major training, siting, or deployment cycle.[S10][S15][S17][S36][S61]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Midnight surprise** — liability appears only after the conduct or commitment is economically closed.
2. **Fake prospectivity** — the rule is formally future-dated but announced after the key irreversible commitment has already been made.
3. **Grandfathered oligarchy** — a reform exists on paper while powerful incumbents are exempted for years without moral justification.
4. **Emergency drift** — crisis language is used to justify vague, overlong, or repeatedly renewed exceptional rules.
5. **Split-timing hypocrisy** — the state accelerates collection now but delays transition relief, rebates, or adaptation support until much later.[S21][S36][S37][S59][S60]

## Order of preference

When more than one transition design is available, the archive usually prefers the one that is:

1. most prospective for ordinary new liability,
2. narrowest in any backward reach,
3. most protective of vulnerable reliance without freezing rentier privilege,
4. clearest about start date, end date, and review date,
5. least likely to create an announcement-driven avoidance window,
6. and most symmetric between the speed of collection and the speed of compensating protection.

This is why published future dates, finite phase-ins, visible cutoff rules, bounded anti-forestalling, and person-focused liquidity relief usually beat indefinite grandfathering, secret effective dates, or open-ended emergency taxation.
