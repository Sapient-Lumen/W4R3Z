# Standing, representation, and remedy routing

This note answers the question that comes **after** a tax base and remittance anchor look morally defensible but **before** the state can impose direct civic tax liability on the supposed subject: **who may be taxed in its own name, and when must liability stay with a controller, guardian, fiduciary, or beneficiary instead?** The archive's stable answer is that direct subjection normally requires an intelligible rights bundle — notice, remedy, representation, and some channel of voice — and that where this bundle is missing, liability should route through accountable humans or institutions rather than through a rightless placeholder.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]

## Core rule

Route direct tax subjecthood through this order:

1. **ask whether the supposed subject can receive intelligible notice and understand the asserted basis of liability at some meaningful level**,  
2. **ask whether it can access review, appeal, correction, or a comparably real remedy**,  
3. **ask whether it can act through a non-conflicted guardian, fiduciary, or representative where direct self-action is impossible**,  
4. **ask whether it stands inside a legal order with some channel of representation, contestation, or proxy voice**,  
5. and **if that bundle is absent, route liability to a controller, guardian, owner, estate representative, or beneficiary instead of pretending that direct civic tax status exists**.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]

A good standing rule does not demand perfect autonomy from every taxpayer. It demands that direct subjection not outrun the minimum institutional conditions that make taxation something other than domination.

## Default routing by subject or context

| Subject or context | Best first standing answer | Why this answer usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary adult human person | direct subject with ordinary notice, appeal, and assistance rights | persons ordinarily belong inside the civic order that taxes them | simplification should assist, not silently displace their ability to know or contest liability.[S42][S43][S44] |
| Child or incapacitated adult | direct rights-bearing subject with guardian or fiduciary representation | personhood persists even when legal agency is mediated | representation must protect the subject, not merely the convenience of the household head or institution.[S42][S43][S44] |
| Estate, trust, pension, or similar legal wrapper | usually mediated liability through fiduciaries and beneficiary rules | these are administered entities rather than autonomous civic persons | do not let wrappers become voice-free shelters or dumping grounds for unreviewable tax burdens.[S18][S34][S42][S44] |
| Corporation or collective legal person | direct subject where review, disclosure, and representation channels are real | legal systems can justly tax organized collective actors when governance and remedy exist | do not confuse corporate standing with proof that every affiliate or shell deserves separate treatment.[S9][S20][S28][S42] |
| Current LLM or owned model instance | **not** a direct civic subject; route liability to controllers and beneficiaries | present systems lack settled standing, remedy, and non-owner representation | do not mistake technical agency, conversational fluency, or dashboard identity for civic tax personhood.[S10][S19][S20][S42][S43][S44] |
| Future rights-bearing artificial person, if threshold is crossed | direct subject only together with floor protection, remedy, and non-owner representation | personhood without standing and remedy would still be domination | do not preserve property logic after rights-bearing status is recognized.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44] |
| High-volume automated platform collection acting for weak humans | keep humans as subjects but automate remittance or relief through the platform | collection may be easier upstream while subjecthood remains with the person | remittance convenience is not a license to erase taxpayer standing or appeal rights.[S16][S17][S42][S44] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Separate the tax base from the direct taxpayer

A thing can be the right **base** without being the right **direct subject**. Children, estates, firms, trusts, and current AI systems can all be relevant to what gets taxed without all being equally appropriate bearers of direct civic tax liability in their own names.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]

### 2. Notice without remedy is not enough

A notice that cannot realistically be contested, corrected, or interpreted by the subject or its representative does not complete the moral case for direct subjection. Tax systems should therefore pair intelligibility with a real review path, not just with formal service.[S42][S44]

### 3. Representation must be non-conflicted enough to count

Guardians, trustees, managers, owners, and deployers do not automatically count as morally adequate representatives. The archive asks whether the representative is there to protect the subject's position inside the tax order or merely to maximize extraction from it. This matters for incapacitated persons, fiduciary entities, and especially any future artificial subjects.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]

### 4. Current AI remains controller-routed

The archive's controller-first position is strengthened, not weakened, by standing analysis. Present LLMs may be economically significant and operationally agentic, yet they still lack the settled legal standing, independent counsel, non-owner representation, and civic membership that would make direct taxation something other than category error. Tax facilities, labs, clouds, deployers, grouped controllers, and beneficiaries instead.[S10][S15][S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]

### 5. Keep withholding and prefilling from becoming standing-erasure

Withholding, prefill, platform remittance, and automatic correction can be excellent administrative devices, but they should assist the taxpayer rather than replace the taxpayer as the bearer of intelligible notice and challenge rights. Administrative convenience is not the same thing as legitimate substitution of subjecthood.[S16][S17][S42][S44]

### 6. Use mediated subjecthood where personhood is real but agency is partial

The archive allows direct subjecthood with representation for children, some incapacitated adults, estates, and some collective entities because the underlying rights order remains intact. The lesson is not "only fully autonomous beings may be taxed." The lesson is "do not assign direct civic liability where personhood, representation, and remedy are absent together."[S42][S43][S44]

### 7. Upgrade standing and tax status together or not at all

If some future artificial system genuinely becomes a rights-bearing civic subject, standing, floor protection, remedy, non-owner representation, and taxation should move together. The archive rejects the one-sided shortcut where obligations arrive first and rights later.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Chatbot personhood theatre** — treating conversational fluency or task autonomy as enough for direct civic tax status.  
2. **Owner as representative by default** — assuming the economically interested controller automatically supplies the representation bundle.  
3. **Remittance equals subjecthood** — confusing the easiest collection point with the morally proper taxpayer.  
4. **Appeal in name only** — notices and portals exist, but the subject or representative cannot realistically understand or use them.  
5. **Rights-later taxation** — imposing direct obligations on unusual subjects before giving them standing, remedy, and voice.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]

## Order of preference

When more than one liability route is available, the archive usually prefers the route that is:

1. most compatible with real notice and review,
2. clearest about who represents whom,
3. least dependent on conflicted owners or controllers,
4. strongest at keeping remittance convenience separate from moral subjecthood,
5. easiest to explain publicly as an exercise of civic authority rather than mere extraction,
6. and easiest to upgrade later if the subject's legal or moral status genuinely changes.

This is why ordinary persons with assistance, mediated rights-bearing subjects with fiduciary protection, and controller-routed AI taxation usually beat both fictionally autonomous machine taxation and purely owner-defined representation.
