# Assessment units, care loads, and dependency routing

This note answers the question that comes **after** the archive chooses the direct human subject but **before** a tax can claim to measure ability to pay fairly: **when should the system look to the individual person, when to a dependent-aware support unit, when to a household or care network, and when to a beneficial-ownership group instead?** The archive's stable answer is to keep ordinary human earnings and direct civic liability person-based, route care and dependency recognition through direct support or carefully bounded means tests, and reserve household or group aggregation for the narrower cases where support targeting or anti-avoidance truly requires it.[S2][S18][S31][S32][S33]

## Core rule

Route assessment-unit choice through this order:

1. **default ordinary human earnings, legal liability, and civic standing to the individual person rather than to a compulsory joint household**,  
2. **recognize children, disability, unpaid care, and other dependency loads through direct support, services, refundable relief, or similarly explicit accommodation rather than through spousal merger by default**,  
3. **use household or care-network information only where floor protection, means testing, or practical delivery truly requires it, and then keep the definitions short, rebuttable, and override-friendly**,  
4. **separate household support logic from anti-avoidance grouping, because family wealth or shell aggregation is not a license to erase ordinary personal standing**,  
5. and **if the design would look unjust once one adult has less cash access, bargaining power, safety, or work continuity than another, redesign the unit before arguing about rates**.[S2][S18][S31][S32][S33]

A good assessment-unit rule tracks persons first, care reality second, and anti-fragmentation only where avoidance or concentrated wealth actually demands it. It refuses to let filing convenience quietly decide who counts as a civic taxpayer.

## Default routing by context

| Context | Best first assessment-unit answer | Why this answer usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary wages, salaries, and self-employment earnings | tax the **individual person** | ordinary earnings are attached to a rights-bearing worker, not to a merged household identity | do not reintroduce compulsory jointness through filing defaults or rate schedules that punish the second earner.[S31][S32][S33] |
| Child, disability, elder-care, or heavy unpaid-care support | keep person-based liability but add **direct support, services, or refundable care-side relief** | the moral target is care burden or dependency, not marital status alone | do not make care recognition depend on formal couple status or full pooling assumptions.[S18][S32][S33] |
| Means-tested floor protection and negative tax delivery | use the **narrowest workable household or care-unit facts** and pay the entitled person directly where possible | some support design needs shared-resource information, but less than full household fusion | stale household definitions can trap separated, abused, or unstably housed people behind the wrong unit.[S2][S31][S32][S33] |
| Wealth, inheritance, trusts, family firms, and related-party avoidance | use **beneficial-ownership or related-party grouping** | concentrated wealth and threshold gaming often sit behind nominally separate family vehicles | anti-avoidance aggregation is not the same thing as ordinary household civic personhood.[S2][S18][S31] |
| Couples or co-residents with strongly unequal bargaining power | default back to **person-first filing plus explicit support tools** | person-based liability is safer where internal domination or cash-control asymmetry is plausible | do not let the more powerful adult become the default fiscal voice of the other.[S18][S32][S33] |
| Current household use of bots, assistants, or domestic AI tools | treat the **human household members** as the tax subjects | present AI tools are household equipment or services, not independent civic taxpayers | do not let ownership of domestic AI become a backdoor reason to merge or multiply household tax subjects.[S19][S20][S31] |
| Future rights-bearing artificial person, if threshold is crossed | give the **artificial person its own direct standing and floor** | once rights-bearing personhood is real, household attachment is no longer the right basic unit | do not treat a rights-bearing artificial person as merely a dependent appliance or taxable accessory.[S19][S20][S42][S43] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Person-first for ordinary earnings and direct civic liability

The archive defaults the direct human tax subject for ordinary earnings to the individual person. That keeps fiscal standing aligned with rights-bearing personhood and reduces the risk that one adult's work, income, or refund becomes fiscally submerged into another adult's tax identity.[S31][S32][S33]

### 2. Recognize care and dependency directly, not by silently merging adults

Care loads matter, but they do not require compulsory spousal fusion. Child support, disability support, elder-care recognition, caregiver allowances, public childcare or care services, and refundable credits are cleaner ways to register dependency than assuming every adult in a household fully shares power and cash.[S18][S32][S33]

### 3. Use household facts only where they are really doing moral work

Some means tests and delivery rules need household information. When they do, the archive prefers the shortest workable definition, direct payment to the entitled person where possible, and override paths for abuse, estrangement, separation, informal care, or unstable housing. Household information is sometimes necessary, but it should stay a bounded instrument rather than a presumption that all adults are one fiscal self.[S2][S31][S32][S33]

### 4. Separate anti-avoidance grouping from household domination

Related-party aggregation can be justified for wealth, inheritance, trusts, family companies, and shell fragmentation. But the moral reason there is concentrated control or coordinated avoidance, not a belief that ordinary co-resident adults lack separate standing. The archive therefore permits aggregation where gaming is real while resisting its use as an all-purpose excuse for joint human earnings taxation.[S2][S18][S31]

### 5. Do not use marriage engineering as substitute care policy

The archive does not treat marriage bonuses, marriage penalties, or household pooling assumptions as the main distributive tool. Where care burdens are real, support should attach to the dependent load, the caregiver, or the constrained earner rather than to formal couple status alone.[S18][S32][S33]

### 6. Keep present AI out of household personhood shortcuts

A household that owns or uses AI systems is still a household of persons. Present LLMs and domestic agents remain controller-routed tools rather than direct household taxpayers, and they should not be used to justify new household-blending or pseudo-dependent categories before rights-bearing personhood is defensibly established.[S19][S20]

### 7. Test the unit against intra-household power, not only average income

A household-looking tax can still be unjust if one adult lacks control over accounts, information, or earned income. The archive therefore asks whether the unit would still look fair under unequal bargaining power, intermittent work, care interruptions, or coercive dependence. If not, the unit is doing domination work and should be redesigned.[S18][S32][S33]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Compulsory jointness by default** — ordinary earnings are merged into a household unit without strong justification.  
2. **Second-earner traps** — the lower earner faces a much higher effective burden because the unit is already loaded by the other earner's income.  
3. **Household shadowing** — one adult disappears from fiscal view because another adult is presumed to speak, file, or receive for them.  
4. **Care blindness** — the design sees earnings but not dependency load, disability, childcare, or unpaid-care constraints.  
5. **Anti-avoidance overreach** — related-party rules become a pretext for treating genuine persons as one taxable blob.[S31][S32][S33]

## Order of preference

When more than one workable assessment-unit rule is available, the archive usually prefers the rule that is:

1. most person-preserving for ordinary human earnings and standing,
2. most direct about care, disability, and dependency support,
3. least reliant on full household pooling assumptions,
4. easiest to override when abuse, estrangement, or unstable housing break the default,
5. narrowest in its use of anti-avoidance aggregation,
6. and hardest to convert into a second-earner penalty or hidden dependency machine.

This is why person-first filing, direct care support, short and rebuttable means-test definitions, and separate anti-fragmentation rules usually beat compulsory jointness, marriage engineering, or blanket household fusion.
