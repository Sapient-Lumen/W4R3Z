# Downside symmetry and loss-recognition routing

This note answers the question that comes **after** a tax is morally targeted and worth collecting in good years but **before** it can claim reciprocity across bad years: **how should genuine losses, negative years, and early-stage downside be treated so the state does not take upside on one logic and dismiss downside on another?** The archive's stable answer is to pair taxation of gains with a real, bounded, and anti-abuse-aware path for recognizing genuine downside through value-preserving carry-forwards, bounded carry-backs, refundability where the public purpose or neutrality case is strong, and ex ante funding where the relevant downside is catastrophic rather than compensable.[S21][S23][S50][S51][S52][S55][S56]

## Core rule

Route downside recognition through this order:

1. **ordinary current-year netting where the gain and loss are part of the same real base**,  
2. **carry-forwards that preserve enough real value to matter when current-year liability is insufficient**,  
3. **bounded carry-backs, refunds, or refundable credits where liquidity, cyclical neutrality, or public-purpose support makes waiting morally distortive**,  
4. **targeted anti-abuse rules against artificial losses, duplicate deductions, and trafficking rather than blanket suspicion of all downside**,  
5. and **ex ante bonds, reserves, or insurance instead of ex post loss relief where the real problem is catastrophic harm rather than ordinary business risk**.[S16][S17][S50][S51][S52][S56]

A downside regime is good when it treats gains and losses as parts of one fiscal relationship, preserves enough timing and real-value symmetry to matter in practice, and does not force only already-profitable actors to count as eligible participants in the tax design.

## Default downside anchors by base or context

| Base or context | Best first downside anchor | Why this anchor usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary labor income and routine household taxation | limited current-year netting plus floor protection rather than open-ended loss relief | the central justice task is protecting the floor, not turning wages into a speculative loss regime | do not let downside rhetoric become a back door for subsidizing ordinary consumption swings while genuine caregiving and hardship remain underprotected.[S4][S21][S31] |
| Small firms, sole proprietors, and volatile ordinary business income | carry-forward with simple rules and, where shocks are large, bounded carry-back or instalment refund | genuine cyclical losses otherwise count far less than gains and can destroy viable activity through timing alone | anti-abuse rules should target fabricated losses, not make only mature incumbents able to use relief.[S21][S23][S50][S52] |
| Risky long-horizon investment, entrepreneurship, and capital gains / losses | netting plus value-preserving carry-forward, with bounded carry-back where neutrality is central | risky activity is overtaxed when upside is current but downside is heavily delayed, capped, or left to inflation erosion | do not confuse neutrality with blanket subsidy for privileged asset holders; floors and anti-fragmentation still matter.[S21][S50][S55][S56] |
| Public-purpose innovation, research, and early-stage frontier work | refundable or transferable support only where the public-purpose case is explicit and tightly bounded | non-refundable relief works mainly for actors that already have tax capacity, leaving the intended encouragement asymmetrically available | do not let refundable design become a rent channel for weakly screened private claims.[S17][S22][S51] |
| Current frontier-model labs with long pre-profit periods and real externality exposure | distinguish controller-side harms from business-risk downside; allow genuine loss recognition for the business side, but fund tail-risk ex ante | AI labs can have genuine negative years before rent emerges, yet some major harms should never be left to later loss recognition | do not answer deployment risk with generous post hoc tax offsets when bonding, reserves, insurance, or gating should have come first.[S15][S16][S17][S22][S50] |
| Cyclical extractive, industrial, or infrastructure sectors | carry-forward with real-value preservation and bounded carry-back where swings are policy-relevant | cyclical sectors are prone to timing distortion if losses become stale or worthless between booms | do not let volatility become a story for permanent sector privilege, negotiated abatements, or cleanup-loss socialization.[S6][S7][S23][S50][S52] |
| Cleanup, victim repair, and catastrophic public losses | ex ante reserve, bond, insurance, or controller guarantee | some downside is not morally a tax-loss matter at all but a prior obligation to fund repair before failure | ordinary tax loss rules are a bad substitute for preparation against ruinous harm.[S15][S16][S17][S47][S48][S49] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Treat gains and losses as one fiscal relationship

A morally serious tax should not eagerly recognize upside while treating downside as a suspicious afterthought. If the state claims part of the gain because it accepts the activity as part of the ordinary taxable economy, it should usually recognize the matching genuine loss on terms that are not merely symbolic.[S21][S50]

### 2. Preserve enough value across time to make relief real

Carry-forwards that decay through inflation, strict nominal caps, or indefinite delay can be formally present yet morally hollow. Where downside is pushed across time, the archive prefers mechanisms that preserve enough real value or deliver enough bounded current relief to keep neutrality and reciprocity intelligible.[S50][S55][S56]

### 3. Use refundability selectively where current tax liability is the bottleneck

Some credits or incentives are meant to support activity before profit arrives. When that is the explicit public purpose, the archive can prefer bounded refundability, transferability, or carry-back over nominal relief that only profitable incumbents can use. But refundability should be screened, disclosed, and tailored to the actual public purpose rather than treated as a default prize for every favored sector.[S17][S22][S51]

### 4. Aim anti-abuse at artificial loss creation, duplication, and trafficking

The right answer to sham losses is not to deny that real downside exists. Block duplicate deductions of the same economic loss, basis inflation, wash-style timing games, and loss trafficking, but do not write the rules so broadly that only large or already-profitable actors ever receive effective symmetry.[S34][S35][S50][S52]

### 5. Separate ordinary downside from catastrophic or non-compensable harm

When the relevant "downside" is mass injury, irreversible ecological damage, model-enabled catastrophic failure, or cleanup that may outrun the firm, the archive does not treat ordinary tax-loss relief as the main tool. That domain belongs first to approval, bonding, reserves, insurance, decommissioning authority, and controller liability rather than to generous post-collapse tax accommodation.[S15][S16][S17][S47][S48][S49][S53][S54]

### 6. Keep downside symmetry compatible with the protected floor

Downside recognition is not a command to subsidize every private risk equally. The archive still protects subsistence first, still distinguishes rents from productive uncertainty, and still rejects designs that quietly privilege wealthy taxpayers' speculative losses while leaving routine human floor protection thin, late, or manual.[S4][S21][S31][S64][S65]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **One-way upside taxation** — gains are taxed promptly while losses are deferred, degraded, or practically unusable.  
2. **Paper symmetry** — carry-forward or credit relief exists in statute but loses most of its value through inflation, expiry, or chronic non-usability.  
3. **Incumbent-only downside** — the system recognizes losses mainly for actors already profitable enough to wait or large enough to plan around the rules.  
4. **Abuse panic overreach** — anti-avoidance rules aimed at sham losses are written so broadly that genuine downside hardly counts.  
5. **Catastrophe misclassification** — ruinous harms are treated as ordinary tax losses instead of being funded or constrained ex ante.[S15][S16][S17][S50][S51][S52][S55][S56]

## Order of preference

When more than one downside regime is available, the archive usually prefers the one that is:

1. most reciprocal between gain and loss treatment,
2. least likely to turn inflation or delay into silent loss denial,
3. most usable by genuinely loss-making but socially valuable activity,
4. hardest to exploit through trafficking, duplication, or shell games,
5. clearest about the boundary between ordinary business downside and catastrophic public risk,
6. and most consistent with protecting the human floor before subsidizing private speculative appetite.

This is why ordinary netting, real-value-preserving carry-forwards, bounded carry-backs where liquidity matters, screened refundability for genuinely public-purpose credits, and ex ante reserves for catastrophic domains usually beat pure no-refund regimes, nominally decaying carry-forwards, broad asymmetry between profitable incumbents and new entrants, or post-collapse pleas that tax law should somehow repair what bonding should have funded in advance.
