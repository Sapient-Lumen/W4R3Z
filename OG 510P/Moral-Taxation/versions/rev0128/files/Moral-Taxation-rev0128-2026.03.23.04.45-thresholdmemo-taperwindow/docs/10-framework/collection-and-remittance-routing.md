# Collection and remittance routing

This note answers the question that comes **after** choosing the tax subject and **before** escalation, audit intensity, or debt recovery: **where should collection begin?** The archive's stable answer is to collect at the strongest, most legible, least distortive anchor that already sees the taxable flow, asset, registry event, or controller relationship, while keeping the real subject's notice, correction path, and floor protection intact.[S21][S27][S39][S41][S65][S66][S68]

## Core rule

Route remittance through the **highest-capacity collection anchor** that:

1. already observes the taxable event or base,
2. sits close to real control or registry truth,
3. minimizes compliance burdens on weaker actors,
4. preserves a reviewable path for the real subject,
5. and does not quietly turn easy visibility into a moral reason to tax the wrong thing.[S21][S27][S39][S40][S41][S65][S66][S68]

## Default anchors by base or context

| Base or context | Best first collection anchor | Why this anchor usually wins | Main warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary wage and salary income | employer payroll withholding plus person-level reconciliation | regular visible flows are easiest to collect near receipt | payroll convenience must not erase the person-level floor, care recognition, or later correction rights.[S21][S31][S65][S66] |
| Broad consumption taxes | seller, platform, or other merchant remitter | transaction-point remittance is simpler than consumer self-reporting | protect the floor through rebates, transfers, or public provision rather than exemption jungles at the checkout line.[S3][S4][S21] |
| Land and immovable property | cadastre, assessment roll, and situs-linked billing | immovable bases create stable local registry anchors | illiquidity may justify instalments, deferral, or liens, not disappearance of site-rent claims.[S5][S23][S25] |
| Resource extraction, fuel, and measurable upstream emissions | extractor, terminal, utility-scale meter, or permit holder | upstream choke points are fewer and more legible than diffuse downstream users | do not leave burden review out of the picture merely because upstream collection is efficient.[S6][S7][S15] |
| Large corporate groups and cross-border profit claims | group reporting, large-taxpayer unit, withholding on relevant cross-border payments, and anti-fragmentation grouping | stronger entities already hold the books, payment rails, and controller map | do not let shell splitting, transfer-pricing theatre, or affiliate routing choose the collection point.[S9][S21][S28][S34][S35] |
| Small firms and thin-margin producers | simple filing plus withholding where feasible | simplicity matters, but direct entity remittance may still be needed | do not turn weak small firms into compliance shock absorbers for a policy that should have collected earlier upstream.[S26][S27][S39] |
| Current frontier AI facilities, clouds, and deployers | facility operator, cloud intermediary, utility-scale account, major deployer, or grouped controller | the concentrated infrastructure stack is more measurable than end-user token flow and sits closer to real control | avoid token poll taxes, parameter levies, or weak downstream collection that simply follows what is easy to meter.[S10][S15][S16][S17][S21][S67] |
| High-risk AI deployment fees, bonds, and reserve duties | licensing body, reserve custodian, insurer, or controller-side supervisor | ex ante risk finance works best before incident and before shell failure | do not rely on post-collapse collection from thin operating vehicles.[S15][S17][S41] |
| Future rights-bearing artificial person | direct account or fiduciary/guardian interface only if standing, notice, and remedy are real | once rights-bearing personhood exists, collection must respect it rather than route permanently through an owner | no indefinite owner-side substitution after personhood has displaced ownership.[S18][S19][S20][S42][S44] |

## Routing rules

### 1. Separate the collection anchor from the moral subject

The employer may withhold, the platform may remit, the cloud may be metered, and the large firm may file group accounts. None of that alone decides who the morally relevant subject is or who should bear the burden.[S21][S41]

### 2. Prefer strong intermediaries over weak downstream actors

Where the same revenue can be collected through a high-capacity intermediary rather than through many fragile households, workers, tiny firms, or low-information users, default to the stronger anchor first. This is especially important in AI stacks, gig platforms, and cross-border payment chains.[S27][S39][S41][S68]

### 3. Use natural records before bespoke reporting empires

Payroll systems, land registries, customs data, utility meters, payment rails, platform records, and large-controller accounts usually beat ad hoc declarations built from scratch. Prefer anchors that already exist in ordinary economic life.[S23][S25][S27][S65][S66][S68]

### 4. If collection is automatic, relief should not be manual by default

A regime that automates withholding, billing, or platform remittance while leaving rebates, refunds, and floor protection trapped behind separate claims is morally lopsided. Administrative power should reduce friction on both sides, not only on the state's side.[S64][S65][S66][S68]

### 5. Keep collection proportional and purpose-bounded

The strongest collection anchor is not a blank cheque for surveillance. Data use, retention, model-assisted targeting, and cross-system integration should remain bounded by purpose, review, and proportionality.[S40][S41][S44]

### 6. Group controllers before choosing the anchor where fragmentation is easy

If one real controller can split the activity across shell labs, landlords, utilities, affiliates, platforms, or family vehicles, choose the anchor only after grouping the real economic unit. Otherwise the system rewards fragmentation by design.[S34][S35]

### 7. Match collection cadence to the base

Frequent flow income often suits withholding or pay-as-you-go collection; illiquid assets may need billing cycles, instalments, liens, or deferral; risk-heavy activity may need ex ante bonds or reserve funding. The wrong cadence can make the right tax coercive.[S21][S23][S24][S39][S41]

## Five anti-patterns

1. **Visible-flow worship** — taxing the easiest meter rather than the right base.
2. **Weak-end extraction** — pushing compliance and cash-flow stress onto workers, users, tiny firms, or local victims because large upstream actors are politically harder to touch.
3. **Relief asymmetry** — seamless collection paired with maze-like refunds or rebates.
4. **Fragment-and-remit arbitrage** — letting shell entities or affiliate routing choose the collection point.
5. **Data-maximalist convenience** — assuming every available record should become tax-enforcement input.[S10][S21][S34][S35][S40][S41][S64][S68]

## Order of preference

When more than one anchor is available, the archive usually prefers the anchor that is:

1. closer to real control,
2. easier to verify with existing records,
3. less burdensome for weak actors,
4. less vulnerable to shell games,
5. more compatible with fast correction and review,
6. and less likely to induce surveillance spillover.

This is why payroll withholding, seller-side VAT remittance, cadastre-linked land billing, upstream resource collection, group-level corporate reporting, and controller-side AI collection often beat end-user self-reporting or diffuse downstream extraction.
