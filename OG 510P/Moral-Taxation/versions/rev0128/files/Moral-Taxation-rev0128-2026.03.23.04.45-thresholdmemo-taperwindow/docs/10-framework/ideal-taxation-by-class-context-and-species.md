
# Ideal taxation by class, context, and species

This is the archive's direct answer to the founding question: humans are the default direct tax persons; current LLM-shaped systems are taxed through controllers, bottlenecks, beneficiaries, rents, and harms rather than as civic subjects; future artificial persons switch into ordinary person-tax logic only after a real rights / standing / non-ownership regime change.[S4][S5][S8][S9][S10][S18][S19][S20][S21][S31][S42][S43][S44]

Start with the [`One-screen schedule`](#one-screen-schedule), then use [`class-context-species-matrix.md`](class-context-species-matrix.md) for the tight grid and [`default-tax-stack-by-scale.md`](default-tax-stack-by-scale.md) for the ordering answer. Expand into later class sections or routing notes only when the dispute turns on subject, timing, incidence, measurement, or jurisdiction.

### One-screen schedule

| Subject or class | Direct tax subject now? | Ideal burden shape | First instruments |
|---|---|---|---|
| Child, dependent, disabled person with exposed floor, or person below subsistence | yes | negative to zero net | automatic or low-friction income support, public provision, and floor-protecting transfers before extraction |
| Ordinary worker, low-income household, or heavy caregiver | yes | zero to light, then moderate only after the floor | person-based earnings taxation, visible rebates, care recognition, and payroll restraint |
| High-income human, rentier, or dynastic recipient | yes | progressive to steep | progressive personal taxation, inheritance / receipts taxation, luxury taxation, and anti-deferral capital-income rules |
| Landowner or natural-resource holder | yes or through firm shell, but anchored to the asset | moderate to steep | land-value taxation, site-rent capture, severance or resource-rent taxation, restoration bonds, and cleanup duties |
| Small firm, cooperative, or ordinary producer | no as a natural person, but yes as a remitting entity | light to moderate | simple cash-flow or presumptive regimes with graduation, withholding, and real loss recognition |
| Large corporation, dominant platform, or bottleneck intermediary | no as a natural person, but yes as an entity remitter | moderate to steep | minimum tax, residual-profit or excess-profit taxation, anti-base-erosion rules, destination allocation, and anti-fragmentation |
| Commercial frontier-model lab, mass AI utility, or agentic fleet controller | no direct machine subject; controller-side liability | moderate to steep through controllers, rents, burdens, and windfalls | ordinary corporate tax first, then exceptional AI levies only for real local burden, scarce-capacity draw, concentrated rent, automation windfall, or bounded public-input reciprocity |
| High-risk deployer creating catastrophic or non-compensable downside | no special machine personhood | supervision first, then steep sidecar charging where still allowed | licensing, ex ante bonds, reserves, insurance, incident funds, caps, and refusal where tax would otherwise become a permission slip |
| Current LLM or model as such | no | none directly as a civic person | tax controllers, owners, deployers, beneficiaries, and externalities instead |
| Future artificial person, if rights-bearing and non-owned | yes, but only after a real regime switch | zero floor, then ordinary progressive person taxation | ordinary person-tax logic with standing, remedy, representation, and no species surcharge |

## Rate-shape vocabulary

- **negative** — the system pays in rather than pulls out
- **zero / shielded** — no net burden on the protected floor
- **light** — contribution is present but deliberately modest
- **moderate** — ordinary contribution compatible with stable work and saving
- **progressive** — burdens rise with clear ability-to-pay
- **steep** — used for rents, bottlenecks, concentrated wealth transfers, and major harms

## Incidence rule

Separate **statutory remittance** from **moral burden-bearing**: the schedule asks who ends up poorer after prices, wages, rents, fees, or bargaining power adjust. Three consequences follow:


1. Taxes aimed at **rents, land, monopoly, and scarce infrastructure** are preferred partly because they are harder to shift cleanly onto protected households.[S5][S21]
2. Broad taxes on consumption or energy are acceptable only when paired with **rebates, dividends, or public-service offsets** that keep the floor intact.[S4][S6][S7]
3. Controller-first AI taxes are good only if the likely burden remains with **owners, bottlenecks, and large beneficiaries**, not with displaced workers, small dependent developers, or low-income users.[S10][S15][S21]

## Timing and liquidity rule

Payment timing is part of tax justice.

1. Taxes on **regular flow income** usually belong near the time of receipt through withholding or pay-as-you-go schedules.
2. Taxes on **recurring rents and property values** may be annual or periodic, but should allow tightly designed instalment or deferral pathways when justified by illiquidity rather than low ultimate ability-to-pay.[S5][S23]
3. Taxes on **risk creation** in AI and other high-hazard domains often need some **ex ante** funding through bonds, reserves, or insurance, rather than waiting until losses are socialised.[S16][S17]
4. Taxes on **large realised gains, transfers, and windfalls** should resist indefinite delay becoming avoidance; staged payment is compatible with justice, permanent untaxed appreciation is not.[S8][S24]

## Measurement and proxy rule

Measurement honesty is part of tax justice.

1. Prefer taxes whose bases are **observable, periodically revaluable, and openly challengeable** over taxes that require hidden or impossible annual valuation.[S23][S25][S27]
2. Use **simplified or presumptive regimes** for small firms only with thresholds, graduation rules, and anti-bunching checks; simplicity is a justice aid, not a permanent privilege shelter.[S26]
3. For AI and other noisy domains, use metered proxies such as electricity, accelerator-hours, deployment tier, or revenue bands only as **administrative stand-ins**, not as claims about total value or moral status.[S10][S15][S16]

## AI exceptional-trigger rule

Special AI levies need their own trigger; technological salience alone is not enough.

1. Default first to ordinary taxes on profits, labor, consumption, property, energy, and harms; a separate AI levy needs its own justification.[S1][S10][S11]
2. The strongest triggers are place-bound burden, scarce-capacity appropriation, concentrated bottleneck rent, automation-linked windfall, and bounded public-input reciprocity where controllers draw disproportionate value from shared public inputs.[S10][S11][S13][S15][S22][S67]
3. Where a trigger is real, meter it through controller-side aggregates such as facility load, accelerator-hours, deployment tier, revenue bands, or beneficiary class rather than through tokens, parameters, or fake model personhood.[S10][S15][S16][S17]

## Claim-friction and take-up rule

Claim friction and take-up are part of tax justice.

1. Floor-protecting rebates, refunds, and credits should, where the state already has enough information, move toward **automatic payment, prefill, or one-step confirmation** rather than separate complex annual claims.[S64][S65][S66]
2. Human-help, translation, accessibility, offline, and correction channels are part of the just design, not decorative customer service.
3. High-friction application paths are more defensible for negotiated privileges or exceptional subsidies than for subsistence protection or routine return-of-overcollection.

## Real-value and indexation rule

Nominal versus real value is part of tax justice.

1. Do not let inflation quietly raise burdens by eroding the real value of brackets, allowances, credits, filing thresholds, or benefit phase-outs unless that tightening is openly chosen.[S55][S57]
2. Where feasible, index or honestly approximate **capital-gain basis, depreciation value, carry-forwards, reserve minima, and safe harbors** so the system taxes real gain rather than phantom gain from price drift.[S50][S56]
3. For AI and infrastructure policy, index or openly re-set **compute thresholds, registry fees, bond amounts, and local-burden triggers** so nominal drift does not silently hollow out or intensify the regime.[S55][S56][S57][S58]

Route: [`real-value-and-indexation-routing.md`](real-value-and-indexation-routing.md).

## Review, trigger, and reversibility rule

Review architecture is part of tax justice.

1. Taxes launched under uncertainty, emergency, or rapid technical change should name their measurable purpose, routine review cadence, and trigger conditions for early reconsideration.[S59][S60][S61]
2. Review should test real incidence, avoidance, local burdens, equal access, and capture, not just collections or modeled intent.[S60][S61]
3. Emergency levies, AI-specific charges, negotiated abatements, and experimental incentive regimes should state how they recalibrate, convert, sunset, or repeal rather than drifting indefinitely by inertia.[S15][S54][S62][S63]

## Precaution and non-compensable-risk rule

Catastrophic or non-compensable risk changes the tax analysis.

1. Use taxes for ordinary measurable harms and rents, not as blanket permission slips for catastrophic or irreversible danger.[S15][S17][S53][S54]
2. Where ex post repair would be morally too late, use ex ante approval, conditional approval, caps, decommissioning powers, or disapproval, with taxes and fees only as sidecars to risk governance.[S53][S54]
3. In frontier AI, data-centre siting, hazardous extraction, and similar domains, ex ante bonds, reserves, and safety cases should usually arrive before the state relies on post hoc tax collection or after-the-fact compensation.[S15][S17][S53][S54]
4. Route: [`precaution-and-catastrophic-risk-routing.md`](precaution-and-catastrophic-risk-routing.md).[S15][S17][S53][S54]

## Nexus and jurisdiction rule

Taxing-right assignment is part of tax justice.

1. **Immovable bases** such as land, extraction sites, and data-centre parcels should usually be taxed where they physically sit.[S5][S23][S25]
2. **Local burdens** such as congestion, utility stress, cooling-water pressure, and adaptation costs should generate a fiscal claim for the harmed locality even if profit is booked elsewhere.[S15][S23]
3. **Final consumption** should generally be taxed at destination, including cross-border services and digital supplies.[S3]
4. **Large cross-border residual profits** should not belong only to residence or booking jurisdictions when customers and users in other markets materially generate them; market jurisdictions deserve a coordinated share.[S9][S28]
5. **Mobile rents** that no single jurisdiction can hold should face club or treaty floors so that justice is not defeated by relocation theatre.[S1][S9][S28]

## Net fiscal-stack rule

Tax expenditures and subsidies belong in the same moral stack as taxes.

1. Judge a tax together with nearby **abatements, credits, deductions, accelerated depreciation, cheap public power or land access, and direct subsidies** that target the same activity or class.[S29][S30]
2. Treat **floor-protecting rebates, child-focused credits, and negative-income support** as part of the protected baseline rather than as loopholes.[S4][S31]
3. Treat **sectoral carve-outs for land banks, fossil fuels, dominant platforms, or frontier AI facilities** as negative taxes unless they are transparently justified, tightly sunset-bound, and netted against the claimed burden.[S29][S30]

## Proceeds routing rule

The archive treats **use of proceeds** as part of ideal taxation rather than as a separate spending problem. Default destinations are:

| Revenue source | Default moral destination | Main caution |
|---|---|---|
| Broad consumption tax | floor protection, visible and preferably automatic rebate, child and disability support, basic services | do not use regressively raised revenue to backfill unrelated privilege, do not rely on exemption jungles where visible floor protection would be cleaner, and do not leave the offset stranded behind low-take-up claim processes. See [`regressivity-correction-and-floor-protection-routing.md`](regressivity-correction-and-floor-protection-routing.md) and [`../20-calibration/regressivity-repair-channel-and-delivery-sync-ladder.md`](../20-calibration/regressivity-repair-channel-and-delivery-sync-ladder.md).[S3][S4][S64][S65][S66] |
| Land and site-value tax | local public realm, housing-support equalisation, shared civic goods | avoid letting rich jurisdictions hoard all site rents.[S5][S18] |
| Inheritance and dynastic wealth taxation | opportunity floors, anti-dynastic equalisation, public goods with long-horizon payoff | do not treat it as merely symbolic resentment policy.[S8][S18] |
| Carbon and other corrective taxes | dividends, adaptation, remediation, clean-infrastructure transition | price the harm, but recycle in ways that keep legitimacy and floor protection intact.[S6][S7] |
| AI windfall, bottleneck, and automation taxes | transition insurance, wage support, training, public compute, competition capacity | do not recycle AI rents back into incumbent capture loops.[S13][S14][S15][S22] |
| High-risk deployment bonds and fees | victim compensation, incident reserves, supervisory capacity | risk-funded systems should not externalize downside.[S16][S17] |


## Notice, transition, and retroactivity rule

The archive defaults major tax changes to **prospective notice and finite transition**. That means:

- publish future effective dates where possible,
- use phase-ins, instalments, or bounded safe harbours where ordinary households or small producers are genuinely locked in,
- close announcement-driven avoidance windows quickly when a rule would otherwise be gamed,
- and refuse indefinite grandfathering of monopoly, dynastic, fossil, or AI-incumbent privilege.

Protect legitimate reliance, not permanent rent extraction. Route: [`notice-transition-and-retroactivity-routing.md`](notice-transition-and-retroactivity-routing.md).[S23][S27][S36][S37][S38]

## Assessment-unit rule

For human persons, the archive defaults to **individual taxation of earnings and direct civic liability**. Dependency, childcare, disability, elder care, and other unpaid-care burdens should still matter, but usually through transfers, services, caregiver supports, or carefully designed means tests rather than through mandatory joint filing that presumes full pooling or makes one adult fiscally derivative of another.[S31][S32][S33]

## Standing-and-representation rule

Direct tax liability should normally sit only on subjects that can be notified, heard, represented, and given access to review. Children and some adults who need assistance remain direct subjects because they are rights-bearing persons with guardianship or fiduciary representation inside law. Current LLMs do not yet satisfy that bundle, so present AI taxation should continue to run through controllers, owners, deployers, and other accountable beneficiaries rather than through owned model instances.[S19][S20][S42][S43][S44]

## Anti-discrimination and status-proxy rule

Anti-discrimination is part of tax justice.

1. Do not use protected status itself as a tax base, surcharge trigger, filing condition, or compliance burden.
2. Where formally neutral rules predictably burden protected groups through household defaults, documentation, geography, interfaces, or automated triage, prefer direct measures of need, income, harm, care load, or control instead.[S32][S33][S45][S46]
3. If model-assisted administration enters the system, require explainability, challenge rights, and monitoring for disparate impact rather than assuming automation is neutral.[S44]
4. If future artificial persons ever exist, do not impose a machine-species surcharge merely because of substrate; tax persons as persons.
5. If a proposed tax works mainly by selling permission for catastrophic or irreversible risk that should instead face approval, tiering, or disapproval, the proposal is misclassified.[S15][S17][S53][S54]

Route: [`anti-discrimination-and-status-proxy-routing.md`](anti-discrimination-and-status-proxy-routing.md).[S19][S20][S32][S33][S44][S45][S46]

## Human classes

| Human class | Ideal net tax shape | Primary instruments | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Children, dependants, severely disabled people, destitute adults | negative | cash support, in-kind services, refundable credits, public provision | The baseline is protection, not extraction.[S1][S2][S18] |
| Single parents and heavy unpaid caregivers | negative to light | refundable credits, direct child/dependency support, childcare/care services, individual earnings taxation | Recognize care burdens without forcing joint tax identity or second-earner traps.[S31][S32][S33] |
| Low-wage workers | zero to light | payroll relief, negative income support, broad consumption tax only with strong rebate | Survival earnings should not be the preferred revenue base; refund timing should be fast enough to matter.[S2][S4] |
| Median households | light to moderate | progressive personal tax, broad VAT/GST with rebate, ordinary local charges | Protect stabilising saving and avoid cliffs.[S3][S4] |
| Affluent professionals and managers | progressive | higher marginal personal rates, capital-gains parity, luxury consumption taxation | Tax ability-to-pay more than status performance, while keeping real-loss recognition from collapsing into pure upside taxation. |
| Rentier and dynastic households | steep | land value tax, inheritance / gift / lifetime receipts taxation, anti-deferral capital-gains rules, beneficial-ownership transparency | Concentrated unearned advantage is a first-rank tax target, and timing games or trust opacity should not turn justice into indefinite deferral.[S5][S8][S24][S34][S35] |

## Institutional classes

| Institutional class | Ideal net tax shape | Primary instruments | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small firms, co-ops, mutuals | light to moderate | simple cash-flow or presumptive rules, VAT remittance, low compliance burden, and bounded loss recognition | Do not bury small production under complexity; simplicity should come with thresholds and graduation rather than permanent shelter, and genuine downside should not be recognized only for mature incumbents with ample taxable income. |
| Ordinary corporations | moderate | residual profit taxation, minimum tax backstops, destination collection where relevant | The core goal is to tax real profit where value is realised, not to impose a one-way upside claim that ignores genuine negative years.[S9][S50] |
| Dominant platforms, clouds, and AI infrastructure bottlenecks | progressive to steep | excess-profit capture, minimum tax, destination profit rules, congestion/externality pricing, related-party consolidation, and reserve / bonding where collapse would dump public losses | Concentration and gatekeeping rents justify a harder edge, negotiated abatements or shell splitting should not quietly erase it, collection should lean on strong platform-side leverage before weak-user extraction, and high-risk facilities should not depend on broad tax priority after failure to cover what should have been bonded ex ante.[S9][S10][S11][S29][S34][S35][S47][S48][S49] |
| Resource extractors and land banks | steep | resource-rent taxes, severance, restoration bonds, vacancy and site-value taxation | The state should capture common inheritance rather than tax wages to make up the gap, should not give it back through quiet sectoral favors, and should not let cleanup or abandonment losses outrank bonded preparation only after the estate is empty.[S5][S6][S7][S30][S47][S48][S49] |

## Current machine and AI classes

| Machine class | Direct tax person? | Ideal tax shape now | Primary instruments |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open or public-interest research model | no | light and conditional | ordinary organisational taxes; externality pricing only where real grid, water, or risk burdens exist |
| Commercial foundation-model lab | no | moderate to steep through controllers | corporate tax, minimum tax, compute-linked externality charges, thresholded scarce-capacity or registry charges where exceptional infrastructure draw is real, bounded public-input reciprocity where disproportionate extraction from shared public inputs is shown, windfall capture where rents emerge, group-level anti-fragmentation where the stack is split across affiliates, and loss recognition that distinguishes genuine pre-profit downside from rent extraction |
| High-risk AI deployer or integrator | no | progressive to steep through risk | licensing fees, ex ante bonding, insurance, incident reserves, sector-specific supervision, reviewable enforcement with protected minima, explicit review triggers for incidents / near-misses / drift, and failure-order rules that do not subordinate workers or public repair to ordinary fiscal collection; do not treat tax payment alone as permission for unresolved catastrophic deployment risk |
| Mass inference utility or embedded AI service | no | moderate through owner/operator | ordinary destination taxation, energy/carbon pricing, no universal per-token levy, and any special AI charge only where local burden, concentrated rent, or other exceptional trigger is actually shown |
| Agentic machine fleet owned by a firm | no, unless rights threshold is met | tax the controller, not the fleet | treat as software-plus-capital until moral category changes |

## Future artificial persons

| Class | Direct tax person? | Ideal tax shape | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current systems below rights threshold | no | none directly | Route liability through controllers and beneficiaries.[S19][S20] |
| Artificial person with defensible rights-bearing status | yes | zero floor, then ordinary progressive person taxation | No species surcharge; ownership must break; see [`artificial-personhood-and-regime-switch-routing.md`](artificial-personhood-and-regime-switch-routing.md). |
| Artificial collective or polity, if ever recognised | yes, by analogy to associations of persons | mixed person-and-entity taxation | The moral unit would be the collective itself, not its substrate. |

## Context modifiers

| Context | Move up | Move down | First instruments |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak-capacity state | customs, withholding, land cadastre, simple excises, resource rents, narrow presumptive regimes | complex personal wealth architecture and fake-precision valuation | simplicity is part of justice, but proxy regimes need thresholds and graduation and should not become permanent favoritism by another name |
| High-inequality rent-heavy economy | land, inheritance, monopoly and excess profits | payroll and routine labor burdens | hit accumulated advantage before effort |
| Automation shock | AI windfall levy, transition insurance, public compute finance | taxes on displaced labor | socialise part of the gain |
| Climate or water stress | carbon, methane, congestion, cooling-water and scarcity charges, plus where needed ex ante permits or caps | blanket energy subsidies | price harms, then recycle proceeds fairly; phase in carefully where household liquidity is fragile, and do not rely on taxes alone where damage is severe, irreversible, or concentrated in weak local systems.[S6][S7][S15][S23][S53] |
| High inflation or rapid nominal drift | indexed or openly re-set brackets, credits, basis rules, carry-forwards, reserve minima, and filing thresholds | stealth bracket creep, phantom-gain taxation, and nominally decaying bonds or safe harbors | treat real tightening as a visible policy choice rather than as hidden drift.[S50][S55][S56][S57][S58] |
| Highly digital transnational economy | minimum tax, destination rules, cloud/compute coordination, and trigger-based review for concentration and pass-through drift | purely local attempts to tax borderless profits | match the base to the enforcing scale and revisit quickly when technology or bargaining structure moves faster than statute |
| Municipality or metro | land value, vacancy, curb and congestion pricing | mobile corporate-profit ambitions | local governments should tax what they can actually see |


## Collection and privacy rule

The archive also judges **how** the tax will be administered and collected. Prefer staged reminders, instalments, offsets, and hardship lanes before intrusive recovery; prefer collection from strong controllers and intermediaries before diffuse extraction from weak actors; disclose rule versions, measured bases, data sources, and proxy methods; and bound data access, retention, and model-assisted targeting by purpose, review, and proportionality. A morally attractive tax becomes defective when it is operationally realized as a surveillance or black-box debt machine, when liability cannot be explained and corrected without expert or vendor dependence, or when the same activity can cheaply escape into a softer category by relabeling labor, profit, controller chains, or deployment risk without any real change in substance. See [`administration-explanation-and-appeal-routing.md`](administration-explanation-and-appeal-routing.md) and [`classification-arbitrage-and-substance-over-form-routing.md`](classification-arbitrage-and-substance-over-form-routing.md).[S1][S9][S16][S17][S19][S20][S23][S25][S27][S39][S40][S41][S44]

## Failure-ordering and limited-liability rule

The archive also asks what happens when the taxpayer fails. Ordinary tax claims should usually live inside a predictable insolvency ranking rather than leapfrogging unpaid wages, estate administration, or serious cleanup claims by default. Withheld taxes can justify narrower priority because the debtor was holding money collected from others, but high-risk sectors should mostly be governed ex ante through bonds, reserves, insurance, escrow, or controller guarantees rather than through broad treasury superpriority after collapse. See [`failure-ordering-and-limited-liability-routing.md`](failure-ordering-and-limited-liability-routing.md).[S47][S48][S49]

## Downside-symmetry and refundability rule

The archive also asks how genuine downside is recognized. If a tax or credit is meant to shape risky investment, innovative production, or long pre-profit development, it should not work only for actors that are already profitable. Bounded carry-backs, indexed carry-forwards where relevant, and refundability or transferability for tightly defined public-purpose credits can be morally superior to nominal relief that disappears for loss-making firms. Anti-abuse rules should be aimed at artificial loss creation, not written so broadly that only incumbents ever receive real symmetry.[S50][S51][S52]

## Anti-fragmentation overlay

Where a proposal is easy to defeat by shell chains, trusts, nominee owners, or related-party splitting, the archive adds an anti-fragmentation overlay:

- identify beneficial owners and real controllers,
- group related parties narrowly enough to stop threshold gaming,
- map AI facilities, labs, deployers, and beneficiaries across affiliates,
- and keep the grouping rule contestable so anti-avoidance does not become arbitrary overreach.[S34][S35]

## Non-negotiable species rule

1. **Human persons** are direct tax subjects with protected floors.
2. **Current LLMs and machine systems** are not direct tax persons; tax should attach to control, benefit, rent, and externality.
3. **Future artificial persons**, if they exist, should be taxed as persons after floor protection and legal standing are recognised.

## Backstop judgment on annual wealth taxation

A recurring net-wealth tax can be justified as a **backstop** where asset visibility is strong and avoidance is tolerable. The archive still prefers land value, inheritance / lifetime receipts, capital-gains realisation and anti-deferral rules, monopoly-rent capture, and corporate minimums first. Route: [`annual-wealth-tax-backstop-routing.md`](annual-wealth-tax-backstop-routing.md).
