# Automation-dividend trigger ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that special AI levies need an exceptional controller-side trigger, **when should automation-linked windfall sharing activate rather than leaving the gain inside ordinary taxation alone?**[S11][S13][S14][S22][S67]

## Closed rules invoked

This memo does **not** reopen the archive's constitutional waist. It relies on:

- [`../10-framework/ai-exceptional-levy-trigger-routing.md`](../10-framework/ai-exceptional-levy-trigger-routing.md)
- [`../10-framework/incidence-and-protected-burden-routing.md`](../10-framework/incidence-and-protected-burden-routing.md)
- [`../10-framework/regressivity-correction-and-floor-protection-routing.md`](../10-framework/regressivity-correction-and-floor-protection-routing.md)
- [`../10-framework/proceeds-routing-and-fiscal-reciprocity.md`](../10-framework/proceeds-routing-and-fiscal-reciprocity.md)
- [`../10-framework/review-triggers-and-policy-reversibility.md`](../10-framework/review-triggers-and-policy-reversibility.md)

The constitutional question is already settled: **ordinary taxation remains the default, and a special automation-dividend contribution is justified only when a controller-side windfall is real enough to name and meter honestly.**[S11][S13][S14][S22][S67]

## Why calibration is still needed

The closed rule names **automation-linked windfall** as one exceptional trigger, but it does not yet specify the smallest workable activation ladder. A usable ladder has to avoid two failures at once:

1. **false positives** where every visible AI deployment is treated as windfall-rich even when margins are thin or downside remains genuine, and  
2. **false negatives** where controllers keep unusually large productivity gains while payroll, bargaining power, local resilience, or entry-level opportunity weaken and ordinary taxation does not catch up quickly enough.[S11][S13][S14][S50][S51][S67]

## Small option set

### Option A — ordinary-tax default only

Use ordinary profit, labor, consumption, land, and corrective taxes only. Do **not** activate a dedicated automation-dividend contribution unless the evidence for a controller-side windfall is weak, mixed, too early, or too noisy to separate from ordinary productivity variation.[S1][S9][S11]

### Option B — disclosure-and-review watchlist

Do not levy yet, but require a covered controller group or sector to publish a compact review packet when at least **two** signals move together over a review window:

- output, revenue, or deployment scale rises materially,
- payroll share, covered headcount, or entry-level hiring weakens,
- operating margins or residual profits rise,
- concentration or bargaining asymmetry visibly strengthens.[S11][S13][S14][S16][S17][S67]

This option is appropriate where displacement signals are visible but still too uncertain for immediate fiscal activation.

### Option C — provisional automation-dividend band

Activate a **bounded controller-side contribution** when at least **three** conditions hold over a named review window, with ordinary downturn and accounting noise screened out:

1. controller-side output, revenue, or deployment scale rises,  
2. labor share, headcount mix, or entry-level opportunity falls in a way plausibly linked to automation,  
3. margins, residual profits, or strategic bargaining power rise rather than merely staying flat,  
4. and the gain is not already being broadly recycled through worker-sharing, stronger wage floors, or comparable public-return channels.[S11][S13][S14][S22][S50][S51][S67]

The contribution should attach to an auditable controller-side aggregate such as excess margin band, excess profit band, or similarly reviewable gain measure, not to tokens, prompts, or raw inference throughput.[S15][S16][S17]

### Option D — strong windfall / concentrated-rent band

Use a stronger band only when Option C is already satisfied **and** one or more intensifiers are present:

- the gain is concentrated in bottleneck firms or cloud/controller groups,
- local labor-market or service burdens are visible,
- public-input reciprocity concerns are unusually strong,
- or ordinary tax and competition tools are visibly lagging the concentration event.[S10][S11][S15][S22][S67][S70]

This is not a universal AI surcharge. It is a temporary or review-bound rent-sharing band for concentrated automation windfalls.

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt a **three-stage ladder**:

1. **baseline ordinary taxation** by default,  
2. **mandatory disclosure / watchlist** when two trigger families move together over the review window,  
3. **bounded controller-side automation-dividend contribution** only when three or more trigger families cohere and ordinary tax/governance has not already captured the gain adequately.[S11][S13][S14][S15][S16][S17][S22][S67]

That recommendation is the archive's narrowest workable setting because it does four things at once:

- preserves the archive's ordinary-tax default,  
- requires windfall evidence rather than AI branding,  
- uses reviewable controller-side aggregates rather than fake precision,  
- and leaves room to intensify only when rent concentration and public burden are both clearer than in the baseline case.

## Default trigger-and-review matrix

| Stage | Minimum showing | Fiscal consequence | Required companion rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. ordinary-tax default | no coherent windfall evidence yet | no special levy | keep ordinary taxes, competition scrutiny, and labor-floor review active |
| 1. watchlist | two signal families moving together over the review window | disclosure, measurement, and earlier review cadence | publish rule version, covered entities, proxies, and error bounds.[S16][S17][S44] |
| 2. provisional contribution | three signal families plus evidence that ordinary tools are not already capturing the gain | bounded controller-side automation-dividend band | route proceeds to worker transition, local repair, public capacity, and floor protection rather than general incumbent subsidy.[S15][S22][S67][S68] |
| 3. strong band | stage 2 plus concentrated rent / local burden / public-input intensifier | stronger but still review-bound contribution | add explicit sunset or conversion path and stronger incidence checks.[S59][S60][S61][S62][S63] |

## Anti-patterns

- **Gross-activity theater** — taxing visible AI throughput as if it were windfall even when margins are absent.[S15][S50][S51]
- **Payroll drop alone** — treating labor contraction by itself as enough without checking where gains and losses actually land.[S11][S13][S14]
- **Permanent emergency mode** — activating a temporary contribution without review cadence, conversion rule, or exit path.[S59][S60][S61][S62][S63]
- **Deficit-fill drift** — routing proceeds into general incumbent subsidy loops instead of transition, floor protection, local repair, or public-interest AI capacity.[S15][S22][S67][S68]
- **Worker-side pass-through blindness** — imposing the levy while ignoring incidence on wages, prices, weak suppliers, or tenants.[S4][S21]

## What would change the recommendation

This ladder should be tightened, loosened, or replaced if any of the following become clearer:

- a better public measure of automation-linked gain than the current margin / labor-share / concentration family,  
- reliable evidence that ordinary corporate, rent, and competition tools are already capturing most of the gain,  
- strong evidence that the levy is predictably passing through to protected workers or low-income households,  
- or a shift from ordinary transition stress toward catastrophic or non-compensable risk, in which case governance and gating should dominate taxation.[S15][S17][S21][S53][S54]
