# Precaution threshold gating ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that taxation must yield where risk is catastrophic, irreversible, or not realistically repairable after the fact, **when should a domain move from ordinary pricing to pricing-plus-governance, and when should it move further to gating, refusal, or moratorium instead of relying on revenue logic?**[S15][S17][S53][S54]

The constitutional question is already settled: **ordinary harms can be priced, but catastrophic or non-compensable risk must be gated before revenue logic, and high-uncertainty domains often require a middle band where taxation sits beside approval, reserves, oversight, or caps rather than standing alone.**[S15][S16][S17][S53][S54]

## Why calibration is still needed

The closed rule names the boundary between **pricing** and **gating**, but it does not yet specify the smallest workable activation ladder. A usable ladder has to avoid two failures at once:

1. **permission-slip drift** where a tax is treated as enough even though ex post repair is unrealistic, uncertainty is high, or the loss surface is too large to remediate after deployment, and  
2. **moratorium inflation** where ordinary but manageable harms are treated as if they already justified refusal, freezing useful activity that should instead face pricing, reserves, disclosure, or bounded operating constraints.[S15][S17][S21][S53][S54]

## Small option set

### Option A — ordinary pricing default

Use ordinary taxes and corrective charges only where the relevant harm is still **bounded, measurable enough to price, and realistically repairable after the fact**. This is the default for ordinary pollution, congestion, ordinary profit, and other harms where ex post fiscal correction remains morally intelligible.[S1][S4][S21]

### Option B — pricing plus heightened watchlist governance

Keep taxation active, but require a covered controller group or domain to enter a **watchlist / review band** when at least **two** warning families move together over a review window:

- rapid scale-up into critical or high-dependence settings,
- uncertainty about harm pathways growing faster than measurement quality,
- evidence that downstream recovery would be slow, litigation-heavy, or politically unrealistic,
- or concentration and infrastructure dependency making failure more systemically consequential.[S10][S15][S16][S17][S44][S54]

This option is appropriate where the risk is not yet clearly catastrophic, but ordinary pricing alone is becoming too thin.

### Option C — tax plus governance band

Use a **combined regime** when at least **three** conditions hold:

1. the activity creates material downside risk or concentrated public burden,  
2. ordinary pricing alone cannot credibly contain the risk,  
3. ex ante controls such as approval, licensing, caps, reserves, bonds, red-teaming, or operating restrictions are available and administrable,  
4. and the domain still looks governable enough that a bounded activity path remains more justified than outright refusal.[S15][S16][S17][S44][S53][S54]

In this band, taxation can still matter — for congestion, scarce-capacity draw, local burden, or reciprocity — but only **beside** governance tools that reduce risk before loss materializes. Revenue logic is supplemental here, not sovereign.

### Option D — gating / refusal / moratorium band

Move to gating, refusal, or moratorium when Option C's middle path fails because one or more intensifiers are present:

- credible catastrophic, cascading, or non-compensable downside,
- no auditable containment or reserve structure strong enough to make ex post repair morally serious,
- system dependence so deep that ordinary administrative failure would be socially intolerable,
- or uncertainty so high that pricing would mostly function as a permission slip for risks the state cannot honestly measure or repair.[S15][S17][S53][S54][S61][S62]

This band does **not** mean taxation disappears forever. It means fiscal design must wait behind approval logic, tighter governance, or refusal until the activity returns to a governable risk class.

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt a **four-stage precaution ladder**:

1. **ordinary pricing default** where harms remain compensable and measurable enough to price,  
2. **watchlist / heightened review** when uncertainty, scale, or dependence grows faster than measurement quality,  
3. **tax plus governance** when ex ante controls are necessary but the activity remains governable,  
4. **gating / refusal / moratorium** when catastrophic or non-compensable risk makes tax-as-permit logic morally false.[S15][S16][S17][S21][S44][S53][S54][S59][S60][S61][S62]

That recommendation is the archive's narrowest workable setting because it does four things at once:

- preserves ordinary taxation where ordinary pricing is still honest,  
- creates an intermediate band where taxes and governance travel together rather than as substitutes,  
- reserves refusal for cases where repair and measurement are no longer credible,  
- and prevents emergency-style restrictions from becoming permanent by inertia rather than by reviewed evidence.

## Default trigger-and-review matrix

| Stage | Minimum showing | Fiscal consequence | Required companion rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. ordinary pricing | harms remain bounded, compensable, and measurable enough for ordinary pricing | ordinary taxes or corrective charges only | keep ordinary incidence checks and review cadence active |
| 1. watchlist | two warning families moving together over the review window | no new levy yet, but earlier review and disclosure | publish covered entities, review window, proxy limits, and failure modes.[S16][S17][S44] |
| 2. tax plus governance | material downside plus credible ex ante controls plus limits of pricing-alone | bounded levy may coexist with licensing, reserves, caps, or approval | add explicit rule version, operator duties, reserve / bond logic where needed, and visible review triggers.[S15][S17][S53][S54][S59][S60] |
| 3. gating / refusal | catastrophic or non-compensable risk, or no credible containment path | taxes do not substitute for approval / refusal; revenue logic stands back | use explicit trigger conditions, conversion tests, and re-entry rules rather than indefinite fog.[S53][S54][S59][S60][S61][S62][S63] |

## Anti-patterns

- **Tax-as-permit theater** — imposing a levy and pretending that priced risk is therefore acceptable even when repair is unrealistic.[S53][S54]
- **Threshold cosplay** — using token counts, parameter counts, or other visible numbers as if they settled risk class without checking actual capability, deployment context, or dependence.[S15][S16][S17]
- **Moratorium drift** — leaving a domain in emergency restriction without review cadence, conversion test, or exit path.[S59][S60][S61][S62][S63]
- **Ex post cleanup fantasy** — acting as if insolvency priority or later damages can substitute for ex ante reserves, caps, or refusal where harms are too large or diffuse.[S15][S17][S53]
- **Opaque safety theater** — relying on secret or proprietary risk scoring that subjects cannot contest and regulators cannot reconstruct.[S16][S17][S44]

## What would change the recommendation

This ladder should be tightened, loosened, or replaced if any of the following become clearer:

- a better public measure of governability than the current harm / dependence / containment family,  
- strong evidence that ordinary pricing plus ordinary sector regulation is already enough in a domain now sitting in the middle band,  
- strong evidence that a proposed levy is becoming a permission slip for risks that remain non-compensable,  
- or strong evidence that previously feared catastrophic pathways are now auditable, bounded, and governable enough to move down a band.[S15][S16][S17][S21][S53][S54][S59][S60][S61][S62][S63]
