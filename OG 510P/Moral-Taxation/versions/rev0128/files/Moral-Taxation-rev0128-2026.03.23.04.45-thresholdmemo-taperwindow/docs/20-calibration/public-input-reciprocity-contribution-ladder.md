# Public-input reciprocity contribution ladder

## Question in one sentence

Given the archive's closed rule that special AI levies need an exceptional controller-side trigger, **when should disproportionate commercial extraction from publicly financed research, data, compute, or shared knowledge infrastructure activate a bounded reciprocity contribution rather than leaving the gain inside ordinary taxation alone?**[S10][S11][S22]

## Closed rules invoked

This memo does **not** reopen the archive's constitutional waist. It relies on:

- [`../10-framework/ai-exceptional-levy-trigger-routing.md`](../10-framework/ai-exceptional-levy-trigger-routing.md)

- [`../10-framework/proceeds-routing-and-fiscal-reciprocity.md`](../10-framework/proceeds-routing-and-fiscal-reciprocity.md)
- [`../10-framework/jurisdiction-and-scale-routing.md`](../10-framework/jurisdiction-and-scale-routing.md)

The constitutional question is already settled: **ordinary taxation remains the default, and a public-input reciprocity contribution is justified only when controller groups derive disproportionate commercial advantage from publicly built knowledge or infrastructure in ways ordinary taxation, ordinary subsidy discipline, and existing public-return channels are not already handling well enough.**[S1][S10][S11][S22]

## Why calibration is still needed

The closed rule names **public-input reciprocity** as one exceptional AI trigger, but it does not yet specify the smallest workable activation ladder. A usable ladder has to avoid two failures at once:

1. **commons toll-road drift** where every use of open knowledge, public research, or public data is treated as billable as if the state owned every downstream output, and  
2. **free-rider enclosure** where highly concentrated controller groups build large private rents on top of public inputs while public-interest compute, shared research capacity, and open knowledge maintenance remain underfunded and ordinary taxation does not visibly return enough of that gain.[S10][S11][S22][S67]

## Small option set

### Option A — ordinary-tax default only

Use ordinary profit, labor, land, consumption, and corrective taxes only. Do **not** activate a dedicated reciprocity contribution unless the public-input claim is too diffuse, too ordinary, or too weakly tied to concentrated commercial extraction to justify separate treatment.[S1][S11][S22]

### Option B — disclosure-and-beneficiary watchlist

Do not levy yet, but require a covered controller group to publish a compact **public-input reliance packet** when at least **two** conditions move together over a review window:

- very large commercial deployment, revenue, or model-access scale,
- visible dependence on public research, open scientific infrastructure, public-interest datasets, or publicly financed compute / education pipelines,
- enclosure of those inputs inside proprietary access control, concentrated platform leverage, or strategic bottleneck position,
- weak visible return flow into public-interest compute, open research maintenance, or comparable shared-capacity support.[S10][S11][S16][S17][S22]

This option is appropriate where the reciprocity claim is plausible but still too noisy for immediate fiscal activation.

### Option C — bounded public-capacity contribution

Activate a **bounded controller-side reciprocity contribution** when at least **three** conditions hold over a named review window:

1. the controller group operates at large commercial scale,  
2. a meaningful share of the relevant knowledge or infrastructure stack is publicly financed or publicly maintained in a way that can be named by **beneficiary class or qualifying input bundle** rather than atomized output tracing,  
3. the group is monetizing that stack through proprietary access, concentrated market position, or other private enclosure rather than mainly circulating it back through open public benefit,  
4. and equivalent direct public-return channels are not already strong enough to make a new contribution redundant.[S10][S11][S22][S67]

The contribution should attach to a workable controller-side aggregate such as **revenue band, deployment tier, or covered beneficiary class**, with room to credit clearly verified direct support for public-interest compute, open research maintenance, or similar shared-capacity institutions. It should **not** rely on per-token public-data billing, per-paper royalties, or ownership claims over every model output.[S10][S16][S17][S22][S68]

### Option D — strong reciprocity / enclosure band

Use a stronger band only when Option C is already satisfied **and** one or more intensifiers are present:

- the controller group combines public-input dependence with infrastructure bottleneck rent or cloud concentration,
- the group also receives major tax expenditures, discounted public inputs, or other hidden negative taxation,
- the public-capacity gap is recurring rather than one-off,
- or the controller group is scaling in ways that weaken open and public-interest alternatives while drawing heavily on publicly built knowledge.[S10][S11][S22][S29][S30][S67]

This is not a universal knowledge toll. It is a review-bound reciprocity band for concentrated commercial appropriation of public inputs.

## Provisional recommendation

Adopt a **three-stage reciprocity ladder**:

1. **baseline ordinary taxation** by default,  
2. **public-input reliance disclosure / watchlist** when large commercial scale and public-input dependence become visible together,  
3. **bounded controller-side public-capacity contribution** only when large-scale commercial enclosure of publicly built inputs is clear and equivalent direct public return is not already strong enough.[S10][S11][S16][S17][S22][S67][S68]

That recommendation is the archive's narrowest workable setting because it does four things at once:

- preserves open knowledge circulation instead of turning every public input into a toll road,  
- uses controller-side tiers and beneficiary classes rather than impossible micro-accounting,  
- distinguishes broad social learning from concentrated commercial enclosure,  
- and keeps reciprocity tied to public-capacity rebuilding rather than symbolic punishment of a fashionable sector.

## Default trigger-and-review matrix

| Stage | Minimum showing | Fiscal consequence | Required companion rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. ordinary default | no coherent concentrated-reciprocity claim yet | no special levy | keep ordinary taxes, subsidy discipline, and public-capacity budgeting active |
| 1. watchlist | large commercial scale plus plausible public-input dependence | disclosure, beneficiary-class mapping, and earlier review cadence | publish covered entities, qualifying public-input bundles, proxy limits, and error bounds.[S16][S17][S44] |
| 2. reciprocity band | stage 1 plus clear private enclosure / weak public return | bounded controller-side public-capacity contribution | route proceeds to public-interest compute, open research maintenance, education / shared capability, or local/common repair rather than incumbent subsidy loops.[S22][S68] |
| 3. strong band | stage 2 plus bottleneck rent / hidden-negative-tax / recurring public-capacity gap intensifier | stronger but still review-bound reciprocity contribution | add explicit substitution, sunset, and conversion rules so the charge does not become permanent commons theater.[S29][S30][S59][S60][S61][S62][S63] |

## Anti-patterns

- **Per-token toll-road fantasy** — pretending every model output can be traced back and billed to atomized public inputs.[S10][S16][S17]
- **Universal open-knowledge tax** — taxing open, nonprofit, or ordinary educational use like concentrated proprietary extraction.[S11][S22]
- **Retroactive public ownership theater** — acting as if any public contribution makes all downstream value state-owned.[S10][S22]
- **Incumbent subsidy loop** — routing the contribution back into the same dominant controller groups instead of public-interest capacity.[S22][S29][S30]
- **No-return blindness** — ignoring verified direct support for shared public capacity when judging whether an extra reciprocity contribution is still needed.[S22][S68]

## What would change the recommendation

This ladder should be tightened, loosened, or replaced if any of the following become clearer:

- a better public measure of disproportionate public-input dependence than the current beneficiary-class / qualifying-input-bundle family,  
- strong evidence that ordinary taxation plus existing public-return channels are already capturing the reciprocity claim well enough,  
- strong evidence that the contribution predictably spills into protected workers, small researchers, public-interest institutions, or weak localities rather than concentrated controller groups,  
- or a shift from reciprocity questions toward catastrophic or non-compensable risk, in which case governance and gating should dominate taxation.[S15][S17][S21][S22][S53][S54]
