# 112 — Administration, visibility, appeals, and machine legibility

## One-line thesis

A morally good tax that is opaque, unappealable, or impossible to compute is not actually a morally good tax system.

## Why this matters

Tax justice lives partly in procedure. People need to know what they owe, why they owe it, how it was calculated, and how to contest it. This matters even more in AI-mediated taxation, automated withholding, or machine-assisted audit contexts.[S16][S17]

## Design rule

Treat administration as constitutional substance, not postscript.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Machine-readable but human-auditable

Every serious tax rule should be representable clearly enough for software while remaining inspectable by ordinary humans.

### 2. Notice and explanation floors

Taxpayers should receive:

- the rule invoked,
- the base measured,
- the rate used,
- the data sources relied on,
- and the review path,
- and the jurisdictional basis for the claim when cross-border or multi-level taxes are involved.

### 3. Appeals by right

Automated flags, AI-based scoring, or model-assisted enforcement should always feed a contestable human review lane.

### 4. Data minimization

A morally serious tax state should not collect every possible datum merely because analytics make it tempting.

### 5. Valuation honesty and proxy disclosure

Where taxes depend on appraisals, proxy variables, or machine-generated estimates, the administration should disclose the main variables, update cadence, and contest path. Hidden vendor models and stale assessments corrode legitimacy even when the nominal base is defensible.[S16][S23][S25][S27]

### 6. Continuous simplification

Complexity reduction is not a technocratic luxury; it is part of equal standing before the fiscal state.


## Guardrails

- No hidden model scores as final authority.
- Keep data retention proportionate.
- Publish rule versions and change logs.


## Failure modes

- **Black-box extraction**: people owe money because an unseen system says so.
- **Appeal by fiction**: a nominal review path exists but is unusable.
- **Complexity as class privilege**: only the well-advised can understand the code.


## Practical tests

1. Could an ordinary taxpayer reconstruct their liability?
2. Are AI-assisted determinations reviewable by humans with authority to reverse them?
3. Is compliance cost proportionate to the revenue and justice gained?
