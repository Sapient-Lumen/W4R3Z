# 117 — Measurement, valuation, proxies, and safe harbors

## One-line thesis

A tax can have the right moral target and still fail if its base cannot be measured honestly, valued regularly, or proxied without predictable injustice. The archive therefore prefers **observable bases, transparent valuation rules, periodic revaluation, and narrow safe harbors** over fake precision or metaphysical accounting.[S5][S10][S16][S25][S26][S27]

## Why this matters

Every tax system eventually confronts imperfect visibility:

- land must be valued,
- small firms may not keep full books,
- multinational profits can be shifted,
- AI harms are often probabilistic,
- and compute externalities are easier to meter than downstream social value.

When visibility is weak, states face a choice between impossible purity and workable approximation. The archive rejects both cynical guesswork and paralysis. It prefers the **least gameable approximation that can be explained, updated, appealed, and improved over time**.[S16][S25][S27]

## Archive rule

Use the strongest available measurement regime that preserves justice:

1. choose the most **observable and hard-to-fake base** consistent with the moral target,
2. publish the **valuation or proxy rule** in a form ordinary taxpayers can inspect,
3. revalue or recalibrate often enough that yesterday's proxy does not become today's privilege,
4. give small taxpayers simple paths without turning simplification into a permanent shelter for larger actors,
5. and disclose uncertainty instead of laundering it into pseudo-scientific confidence.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Prefer observable bases to romantic precision

If a morally attractive base cannot be measured except through fantasy accounting, move one level outward to a base that can actually be administered. It is often better to tax **site value, receipts, electricity draw, congestion, or realised gains** than to pretend a state can annually price every hidden stock of value with fine-grained moral accuracy.[S5][S15][S25]

### 2. Revalue property and land on a schedule

Property and site-value taxes degrade when valuations go stale. A just system does not leave assessments frozen for political convenience while market value drifts upward for decades. Revaluation cadence, mass appraisal quality, and appeal rights are part of the morality of the tax, not just its administration.[S23][S25]

### 3. Use simplified regimes as on-ramps, not shelters

Small firms and informal enterprises may justify turnover-based, fee-based, or otherwise simplified regimes when bookkeeping capacity is weak. But these regimes need **minimum thresholds, graduation rules, and anti-bunching checks**. A proxy that helps tiny firms comply becomes unjust when larger firms camp inside it to avoid ordinary taxation.[S26]

### 4. Distinguish proxy convenience from proxy truth

A proxy is a convenience instrument, not a metaphysical revelation. GPU-hours, electricity use, cooling-water demand, model-parameter counts, or deployment-tier categories may all be useful for pricing AI burdens, but none should be confused with value, consciousness, or total social harm. Use them because they are measurable, not because they exhaust the moral reality.[S10][S15][S16]

### 5. Safe harbors should be narrow, reversible, and visible

Safe harbors are justified when they lower compliance cost, stabilize expectations, or handle noisy measurement. They are not justified when they quietly become elite carve-outs. Good safe harbors have published entry conditions, review dates, and easy exit once better measurement is available.[S27]

### 6. Publish uncertainty and allow challenge

Where valuation bands, presumptions, or machine-generated estimates are used, the system should disclose the main variables, plausible error ranges, and appeal path. A number that cannot be interrogated is not yet fit to anchor coercive collection.[S16][S27]

## Guardrails

- no pretending token counts equal value or harm
- no permanent use of simplified regimes by firms that have outgrown them
- no stale cadastral values treated as morally neutral
- no annual valuation scheme that depends on hidden vendor models the taxpayer cannot challenge
- no direct machine-person taxation inferred from metering convenience alone

## Failure modes

- **fake precision** — impossible measurement presented as exactness
- **stale valuation** — old assessments turned into quiet privilege or quiet overcharge
- **proxy capture** — easy-to-measure quantity mistaken for the real moral target
- **threshold camping** — taxpayers distort activity to remain inside a simplified regime
- **opaque scoring** — machine estimates with no contestable explanation

## Practical tests

1. Can the taxpayer tell which facts determine liability?
2. Is the proxy materially easier to observe than the target it stands in for?
3. How quickly does the proxy go stale?
4. What anti-bunching or graduation rule prevents sheltering?
5. Can a normal person challenge an assessment without expert priesthood?
6. Would the archive still endorse the instrument if the measurement error were made public?
