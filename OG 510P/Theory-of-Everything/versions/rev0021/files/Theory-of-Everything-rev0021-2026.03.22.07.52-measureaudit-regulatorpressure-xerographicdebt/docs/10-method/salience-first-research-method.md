# Salience-first research method

## Salience test

A new addition is salient only if it does at least one of the following:
- reduces multiple tensions to one cleaner distinction,
- names a new invariant or recovery target,
- sharpens an open question into a discriminating test,
- compresses several documents into one canonical surface,
- or forces demotion of a previously loose claim.

## Non-salient additions

Avoid adding material that only:
- repeats known background,
- lists framework names without bridge structure,
- grows the bibliography without changing the theory map,
- or expands prose without changing a registry, workstream, or test.

## Archive growth rule

Prefer **one stronger document** over **many adjacent documents**.
A small archive that keeps its decision surfaces explicit is more valuable than a large archive with hidden repetition.
