# Cosmological-constant / vacuum-energy debt split audit

This document is the **first concrete application** of the archive's local-law / cosmological-boundary split.
Its job is not to solve the cosmological constant problem.
Its job is to stop the archive from calling unlike achievements by the same name.

## Compression verdict

The archive should now carry the cosmological-constant / vacuum-energy problem as **at least two burdens, not one**:

1. **local radiative burden** — why local EFT vacuum contributions and threshold shifts do not simply drag the effective curvature scale to the largest available UV-sensitive value,
2. **cosmological-boundary burden** — why the realized universe sits in a near-flat or tiny-`\Lambda` package rather than some other global state, vacuum sector, or measure-weighted history,
3. **mixed debt-shift burden** — whether a proposal really reduces one of those debts, or merely moves bookkeeping between them.

The archive should therefore stop calling the following one generic “solution”:
- local cancellation hope,
- integration-constant reparameterization,
- vacuum-selection story,
- anthropic / landscape selection,
- or global constraint package.

These are not all answering the same question.

## Why this document exists

The previous split surface installed the rule **do not smuggle local law and cosmological package into each other**.
But without a concrete case, the rule stayed abstract.
The cosmological constant problem is the right first stress test because it naturally invites explanatory laundering:
- local QFT bookkeeping gets retold as if it had already selected the actual universe,
- a global vacuum-selection story gets retold as if it had explained local radiative stability,
- or a reparameterization of `\Lambda` gets retold as if the radiative burden had disappeared.

This document blocks those moves.

## The three debt buckets

### 1. Local radiative burden

A proposal earns **local-law credit** here only if it says something real about one or more of the following:
- why vacuum contributions from matter sectors do not simply reappear as a large effective gravitational source,
- why integrating out thresholds does not continuously destabilize the small observed value,
- what symmetry, cancellation, dynamical adjustment, or constraint actually protects the small curvature response,
- and whether that protection survives ordinary EFT reasoning instead of being reintroduced by retuning at each scale.

This is the burden most tightly connected to local EFT honesty.
A proposal that does not reduce threshold sensitivity has probably not solved the local part, even if it says something interesting about the global part.

### 2. Cosmological-boundary burden

A proposal earns **cosmological-boundary credit** here only if it says something real about one or more of the following:
- why the realized universe inhabits a tiny-`\Lambda` state or sector,
- what global selection principle, measure, asymptotic package, or vacuum-weighting is being assumed,
- why one history, sector, or branch is preferentially realized,
- and how that package reaches empirical contact rather than remaining an unconstrained selection story.

This is where boundary conditions, initial state, vacuum landscape, and measure assumptions live.
A proposal can contribute here without having solved local radiative stability.

### 3. Mixed debt-shift burden

Some proposals are neither purely local nor purely global.
They modify the bookkeeping so that part of the problem migrates from “local source term” to “integration constant,” “global constraint,” or “history selection.”
That can be substantive.
But the archive should ask a sharper question:

**Did the debt shrink, or did it move?**

If a proposal reclassifies the constant without reducing threshold sensitivity, or fixes the observed value only by choosing a rare global package, then the burden has moved but not closed.

## Five anti-smuggling checks for vacuum-energy claims

Any future cosmological-constant claim should answer all five checks.

### 1. Which burden is being claimed?

Name the target explicitly:
- local radiative stability,
- global state / vacuum selection,
- measure weighting,
- integration-constant reclassification,
- or some mixed package.

If a claim cannot say which burden it is addressing, it is probably only changing vocabulary.

### 2. What local threshold sensitivity is actually reduced?

Say what happens when matter sectors are integrated out or when heavy thresholds move.
If the small observed value still requires retuning after ordinary EFT threshold shifts, then the local burden remains.

### 3. What global package is being assumed?

Name the required global ingredients:
- special boundary conditions,
- preferred vacuum sector,
- measure,
- anthropic weighting,
- global constraint,
- or special topology / ensemble assumptions.

If a proposal needs these, the archive should book that explicitly as cosmological-boundary credit rather than local-law credit.

### 4. What is the empirical discriminator?

A serious proposal should say whether it changes:
- local propagation or couplings,
- global history weighting,
- allowed vacuum sectors,
- large-scale boundary observables,
- or merely interpretation of the same measured `\Lambda`.

If no discriminator is named, the proposal may be a debt-reshuffling move rather than a sharper explanation.

### 5. Which debt is actually reduced?

The archive should score the outcome in one of four ways:
- **local burden reduced**,
- **global-package burden reduced**,
- **both reduced**,
- or **debt mainly reshuffled**.

That score should be more important than the proposal's branding.

## Current archive scoring by proposal class

### A. Plain EFT honesty

- **What it earns:** the clearest statement of the local radiative burden.
- **What it does not earn:** a reason the observed value is tiny.
- **Archive score:** honest baseline, not a solution.

### B. Local cancellation / symmetry hope

- **What it could earn:** genuine local-law credit if a robust protecting structure actually survives thresholds without retuning.
- **What the archive must ask:** is the protection explicit and portable, or only a slogan that fails once realistic sectors are included?
- **Archive score:** candidate local solution class, not yet promoted in generic form.

### C. Integration-constant / reparameterization moves

- **What they may earn:** a reclassification of where the constant appears in the equations.
- **What they often do not yet earn:** a demonstrated reduction of the ordinary radiative burden from matter thresholds.
- **Archive score:** often a mixed debt-shift move unless the local sensitivity problem is shown to shrink rather than merely relocate.

### D. Vacuum-selection / anthropic / measure packages

- **What they may earn:** cosmological-boundary credit about why a tiny observed value is selected or tolerated.
- **What they do not automatically earn:** local radiative protection.
- **Archive score:** global-package answers, not local-law closure.

### E. Global-constraint / sequestering-style packages

- **What they may earn:** a mixed attempt to connect global structure back onto local radiative bookkeeping.
- **What the archive must ask:** does the proposal really suppress threshold sensitivity, or does it only impose a new global accounting identity?
- **Archive score:** now split by later audits into a weaker bookkeeping lane and a stronger sequestering lane; still unresolved as a full two-burden closure until both local and global debts are reduced together.

## Family-by-family relevance

### Family A — EFT-first baseline

Family A remains the honesty baseline for the **local radiative burden**.
It does not by itself solve the smallness problem, but it blocks fake local-law victories that only rename the same sensitivity.

### Family B — Thermodynamic / entropy gravity

Family B is not currently the leading lane for this problem.
At present it offers at most indirect reconstruction pressure, not a sharp local radiative or global selection answer.
The archive should resist letting horizon or entropy language impersonate a vacuum-energy solution.

### Family C — Holography / entanglement geometry

Family C may eventually matter through boundary package or vacuum-sector structure, but the current archive should not let special boundary control masquerade as a generic local solution to vacuum energy.
The local-law / cosmological-boundary split matters here especially.

### Family D — Measurement / nonlocality pressure

Family D remains relevant only as a reminder that empirical contact and record structure still matter once a cosmological package is proposed.
It is not currently a direct vacuum-energy solution lane.

## What closes with this document

`FQ-0010` can now close in bounded form.

The archive now has a **first concrete debt map** for the cosmological-constant problem:
- local EFT radiative burden,
- cosmological-boundary / selection burden,
- and mixed debt-shift moves that must prove they reduce something rather than merely rename it.

## Net result

This is again a **compression move** more than a literature move.
It lets the archive say, more cleanly than before:

- not every “vacuum-energy solution” is solving the same problem,
- local-law credit and global-package credit must be booked separately,
- and the next useful audit is no longer abstract cosmology prose but a concrete proposal-class test of whether the debt really shrinks or merely moves.
