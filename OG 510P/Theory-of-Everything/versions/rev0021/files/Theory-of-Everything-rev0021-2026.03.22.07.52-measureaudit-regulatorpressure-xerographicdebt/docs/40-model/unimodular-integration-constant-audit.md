# Unimodular / integration-constant audit

This document is the **first concrete mixed-proposal-class pass** under the archive's cosmological-constant debt split.
Its job is not to settle unimodular gravity in general.
Its job is to stop the archive from calling a bookkeeping relocation a solved local radiative problem.

## Compression verdict

The current archive should treat **unimodular / integration-constant moves as real bookkeeping clarifications but not yet as generic local radiative closure**.

Bounded result:

1. **What they genuinely change:** they can move the cosmological constant from an ordinary local coupling-parameter role toward an integration constant or global datum.
2. **What they do not yet generically show:** that ordinary EFT vacuum contributions and threshold shifts stop mattering to the small observed curvature scale.
3. **What they therefore currently earn:** mixed debt-shift credit and some global-package clarification, not a promoted local-law solution to the old cosmological-constant problem.

This is already useful.
It means the archive can stop talking as if “`\Lambda` becomes an integration constant” and “local vacuum-energy sensitivity is reduced” were the same achievement.

## Why this document exists

The previous debt-split audit separated three burdens:
- **local radiative burden**,
- **cosmological-package burden**,
- **mixed debt-shift burden**.

Unimodular-style moves are the first compact test of that split because they are often narrated as a solution simply because the constant is reclassified.
The archive needs a smaller and harsher question:

**Did the local EFT burden shrink, or did the burden move into a global integration-constant package?**

## What the unimodular move actually buys

In the archive's current bounded reading, the unimodular / integration-constant lane can genuinely buy three things.

### 1. Cleaner variable bookkeeping

The cosmological constant can be treated as something other than a fixed bare coupling written once and for all in the local action.
That is not trivial.
It changes where the archive books explanatory burden.

### 2. Sharper separation of local equations from global data

The move makes it easier to say:
- the local field equations may look Einstein-like,
- while the effective `\Lambda` value is fixed by an integration constant, constraint sector, or initial-data choice.

That is conceptually aligned with the archive's local-law / cosmological-boundary split.

### 3. A more explicit mixed proposal class

The archive now has a cleaner example of a proposal that is neither purely local cancellation nor purely anthropic selection.
It sits in the mixed lane: part reclassification, part global package.

## What the unimodular move does **not** automatically buy

### 1. Local EFT threshold relief

A generic unimodular retelling does not by itself show why integrating out heavy matter sectors fails to reintroduce large vacuum-energy contributions.
If the same threshold sensitivity is still present in different bookkeeping clothing, the local radiative burden remains.

### 2. A free pass on quantum corrections

Changing how `\Lambda` appears classically does not automatically demonstrate quantum stability of a tiny observed value.
The archive should still ask what happens under ordinary EFT reasoning, loops, and threshold moves.

### 3. A distinctive empirical wedge

If the proposal is locally equivalent to GR with a cosmological constant across the relevant tested regime, then its gain may be conceptual or bookkeeping-level rather than a new observable discriminator.
That can still matter, but it should be scored honestly.

## Scoring unimodular-style proposals under the debt split

### Local radiative burden

**Current archive score:** not clearly reduced in generic form.

To earn local-law credit, a unimodular-style proposal would have to show more than reclassification.
It would need to say why heavy-sector vacuum contributions and threshold shifts do not simply reappear as effective curvature burden in a retuned form.
The current archive does not yet count that as shown in the generic class.

### Cosmological-package burden

**Current archive score:** partially clarified, not closed.

The proposal class often makes global data, four-volume constraint, or integration-constant selection more explicit.
That is real explanatory sorting.
But making the global package more explicit is not the same as showing why the realized value is tiny without delicate initial-data or constraint assumptions.

### Mixed debt-shift burden

**Current archive score:** genuinely active here.

This is where unimodular gravity currently earns most of its archive credit.
It is a useful demonstration that debt can move from “local coupling in the action” to “global datum / integration constant / constraint package.”
That movement is scientifically meaningful, but it is not yet the same as debt removal.

## Five checks the archive should now apply

### 1. Where do matter vacuum contributions go?

Do they cease to gravitate in a threshold-stable way, or are they simply absorbed into a differently named constant?
If the latter, the move remains largely classificatory.

### 2. What global package fixes the observed small value?

Name it explicitly:
- initial-data choice,
- four-volume condition,
- global constraint,
- sector choice,
- or some other ensemble input.

If that package is essential, the archive should count it as cosmological-boundary burden rather than local-law gain.

### 3. What happens under semiclassical / quantum reasoning?

If the proposal's classical elegance disappears once quantum vacuum contributions are tracked, the archive should not overcredit the classical reformulation.

### 4. Is there an observable discriminator?

If the proposal is empirically equivalent to GR plus `\Lambda` across the tested regime, the gain is explanatory sorting rather than a new empirical wedge.
That is allowed, but it should be labeled as such.

### 5. Did the smallness problem shrink, or only move?

The archive should now treat this as the decisive question.
A move can be mathematically clean, conceptually clarifying, and still mostly a re-routing of burden.

## Family-by-family relevance

### Family A — EFT-first baseline

Family A keeps the pressure honest here.
It asks whether threshold sensitivity actually changed or whether the same sensitivity was merely repackaged.

### Family B — Thermodynamic / entropy gravity

Family B is mostly peripheral in this pass.
Its current value here is only as a reminder not to let evocative vocabulary substitute for a local radiative mechanism.

### Family C — Holography / entanglement geometry

Family C matters mainly as a comparison class: a strong global package can be structurally rich without thereby solving the local radiative burden.
That is exactly the discipline the split was built to enforce.

### Family D — Measurement / nonlocality pressure

Family D stays indirect.
Its role here is only to keep empirical-contact standards visible when global-package stories start to drift away from observable-bearing records.

`FQ-0011` can now close in bounded form.

The archive now has a **first concrete mixed-class score** under the cosmological-constant debt split:
- unimodular / integration-constant moves can earn real bookkeeping and package-separation credit,
- but they do not yet generically earn local radiative closure,
- and they remain mostly debt-relocation rather than debt-elimination in the current archive posture.

## Net result

This is again a **compression move** more than a literature move.
It lets the archive say, more sharply than before:

- an integration constant is not automatically a threshold solution,
- a global package made explicit is not the same as a local-law mechanism,
- and the next useful audit is now a sequestering / global-constraint proposal-class pass rather than more unimodular prose.

That later pass is now installed separately so this document can stay narrow: unimodular bookkeeping remains the weaker mixed class against which stronger direct-targeting proposals are compared.
