# Anthropic / landscape-selection audit

This document applies the archive's cosmological-constant debt split to **anthropic / landscape-selection proposal classes**.
Its job is not to settle whether the landscape is real.
Its job is to stop the archive from confusing **conditional selection stories** with **local radiative solutions**.

## Compression verdict

The current archive should treat **anthropic / landscape-selection proposal classes as explicit cosmological-package answers that can earn real package credit, but not generic local radiative closure**.

Bounded result:

1. **What they genuinely change:** they make the small observed value of `Lambda` conditionally less surprising by combining a scan of possible vacuum energies with a selection rule tied to observer-friendly structure formation or habitability windows.
2. **What they do not thereby change:** they do not generically reduce ordinary local EFT threshold sensitivity or explain why vacuum contributions from matter sectors stop being large in each candidate vacuum.
3. **What they still owe:** a population mechanism, a measure or weighting rule, a reason to trust the relevant selection criterion, and some sharper empirical or cross-parameter pressure than pure post-selection bookkeeping.
4. **What they therefore currently earn:** explicit **cosmological-package credit**, not generic **local-law credit**.

That already changes archive state in a useful way.
It means the archive can now distinguish five non-identical vacuum-energy proposal modes:
- bookkeeping relocation,
- direct radiative targeting with residual global package,
- dynamical self-adjustment with branch or trigger cost,
- explicit anthropic / landscape selection,
- and any future class that might actually reduce both burdens without hiding the remainder in thresholds, branches, or measure assumptions.

## Why this document exists

The debt-split audit said that anthropic and landscape stories belong on the **cosmological-package** side unless they actually reduce local threshold sensitivity.
The self-tuning pass then made the contrasting point that some mixed classes at least try to alter the vacuum response dynamically.
So the next necessary stress test is the opposite extreme:

**What should the archive say about a class that openly accepts a broad vacuum scan or ensemble and tries to explain the observed small value mainly by conditional selection?**

This is the right place to block three common laundering moves:
- “there exist many vacua” being retold as “local vacuum energy is now explained,”
- “observers only arise in tiny-`Lambda` regions” being retold as if that had reduced threshold sensitivity,
- and “the landscape predicts our universe” being said before the population and measure rule are even specified.

## What the anthropic / landscape move actually buys

In the archive's current bounded reading, this proposal class can genuinely buy four things.

### 1. An explicit answer on the cosmological-package side

This is the class's strongest virtue.
It does not pretend that a tiny observed `Lambda` is generically forced by local dynamics alone.
Instead it says that the realized universe may be drawn from a large space of vacua or effective constants, and that observers preferentially appear only in a narrow window.

That is a real explanatory move on the **global-package** side.
The archive should count it as such.

### 2. A concrete scanning story rather than pure handwaving

The strongest compact version of this class is not just the slogan “maybe many universes exist.”
It is a more structured package in which vacuum energies can scan across a dense discretuum or landscape.
That matters because it gives the selection story an actual support space rather than an empty verbal ensemble.

In the archive's present bounded reading, this is the main gain of the landscape side over vague anthropic rhetoric.

### 3. A bounded reason the observed value can be conditionally unsurprising

Weinberg-style anthropic reasoning is load-bearing here because it sharpens the claim from “small values are nice for life” into a more disciplined point:
large positive `Lambda` can suppress structure formation, so the observed value can be discussed conditionally rather than only as a raw miracle.

That still does not make the explanation complete.
But it does mean the class is more than pure metaphysical preference.

### 4. Possible cross-parameter pressure in principle

If a proposal really specifies:
- what scans,
- how vacua are populated,
- how observers or structures are weighted,
- and what other parameters co-vary with `Lambda`,

then the class could in principle generate nontrivial conditional expectations beyond the single small-value fact.

The archive should count that as a possible future gain.
But it should not treat it as already earned by landscape language alone.

## What the anthropic / landscape move does **not** buy automatically

### 1. Generic local radiative burden reduction

This is the key discipline surface.
Anthropic selection can explain why we **observe** a small effective value in a subset of vacua.
It does **not** thereby explain why local EFT vacuum contributions became small, cancelled, or radiatively stable inside each vacuum.

So the archive should now say this explicitly:

**selection-conditioned observation is not the same as local threshold relief.**

### 2. Measure or population closure

A dense landscape is not enough by itself.
The class still owes answers to questions like:
- what populates the vacua,
- how frequently,
- with what cutoff or weighting,
- what counts as a typical observer or observer-proxy,
- and how predictions avoid pure measure arbitrariness.

Without that, the package is still underdetermined.

### 3. Unique realized-universe closure

Even with a discretuum and anthropic bound, the class often explains at most why the observed value is **compatible with selection**.
It does not yet tell the archive why this realized vacuum, this history, and this wider parameter package were selected rather than many nearby alternatives.

So the class is often better at bounded permissibility than at unique explanatory closure.

### 4. A thick empirical wedge by default

In practice the class can remain empirically thin.
Unless the measure, population rule, or parameter correlations become concrete enough to constrain more than “`Lambda` must be small enough for structure,” the class risks becoming post-selection bookkeeping rather than a sharp predictive program.

That does not make it useless.
It means the archive should score it honestly.

## Scoring anthropic / landscape proposals under the debt split

### Local radiative burden

**Current archive score:** not generically reduced.

This class does not currently earn local-law credit in generic form.
It can make the observed value conditionally unsurprising without showing why heavy thresholds, vacuum contributions, or low-energy EFT bookkeeping cease to create large local sensitivity.

### Cosmological-package burden

**Current archive score:** partially reduced, often more honestly than mixed classes that pretend to be local.

This is the class's real contribution.
If a proposal gives:
- a scan of possible values,
- a structured support space,
- and a bounded selection criterion,

then it earns explicit cosmological-package credit.
The archive should say that without embarrassment.

### Mixed debt-shift burden

**Current archive score:** lower mixedness than unimodular or self-tuning classes, because the class openly lives on the package side; still unresolved because measure and population rules often remain thin.

This is not mainly a local-to-global reshuffling move.
It is better understood as an open acceptance that the explanation may be primarily **ensemble-and-selection level**.
That honesty is a gain.
But the class still owes the archive a measure, a population rule, and a meaningful empirical wedge.

## Five checks the archive should now apply

### 1. What exactly scans?

Name the support space explicitly:
- `Lambda` alone,
- flux sectors,
- vacuum energies across a discretuum,
- wider parameter sets,
- or an ensemble of histories.

If the scan is left vague, the selection story has no stable target.

### 2. What exactly selects?

Name the selection criterion explicitly:
- galaxy formation,
- halo abundance,
- chemistry,
- observers,
- entropy production,
- or some other proxy.

If the criterion is changed ad hoc from argument to argument, the package debt is not reduced.

### 3. What populates and weights the space?

Say what populates the landscape or ensemble and how probabilities are assigned.
If the proposal has no explicit measure or typicality rule, the archive should count that as unresolved package debt rather than hidden closure.

### 4. What local burden remains untouched?

The proposal should say plainly whether it is leaving ordinary local vacuum-energy sensitivity intact.
If so, the archive should score that as **no local radiative relief** rather than letting cosmological-package success get mistaken for local-law success.

### 5. What is the empirical or cross-parameter wedge?

Does the proposal constrain anything beyond the observed smallness of `Lambda`?
For example:
- correlations with other scanned parameters,
- a specific population mechanism,
- a nontrivial measure consequence,
- or tension with a competing structure-formation proxy.

If not, the class may remain a bounded explanatory permission rather than a thick predictive framework.

## Family-by-family relevance

### Family A — EFT-first baseline

Family A is decisive here.
It keeps the local radiative burden explicit and prevents selection-conditioned observation from being mistaken for local threshold relief.

### Family B — Thermodynamic / entropy gravity

Family B is not currently a leading lane here.
Thermodynamic or horizon language should not be allowed to masquerade as a selection principle unless an actual population and weighting story is supplied.

### Family C — Holography / entanglement geometry

Family C may eventually matter if a concrete landscape or boundary-sector story yields a real measure or vacuum-counting advantage.
But the current archive should not let holographic control in special settings be retold as a generic anthropic closure story.

### Family D — Measurement / nonlocality pressure

Family D matters indirectly because any selection principle ultimately relies on what counts as an observer, record, or proxy for observational accessibility.
That does not solve the measure problem, but it prevents the archive from treating “observer selection” as a free primitive.

## What closes with this document

`FQ-0014` can now close in bounded form.

The archive now has a compact answer for anthropic / landscape-selection proposal classes:
- they can earn real cosmological-package credit,
- they do not generically reduce local radiative burden,
- and their remaining live debts are measure, population, typicality, and empirical thickness, now made more explicit by `docs/40-model/measure-population-typicality-audit.md`.

## Net result

This is again a **compression move** more than a literature move.
It lets the archive say, more cleanly than before:

- anthropic / landscape-selection proposals are not fake just because they are global-package answers,
- but they should not be credited with local radiative closure they did not earn,
- and the next useful audit is now a concrete worked example of a local / causal measure package rather than a broader essay about landscape selection in general.
