# Consensus elicitation and testimony discipline

Revision: `rev0273`

## Purpose

This surface treats expert consensus as testimony under a declared denominator, not as direct physical or formal evidence. It is useful only when the elicitation object, expert population, disagreement visibility, and aggregation rule are explicit.

## Structured consensus rule

A consensus row must say whether the signal comes from a structured Delphi-like process, expert panel, collaboration report, workshop, survey, citation network, or informal field uptake. Structured elicitation can reduce ambiguity in expert judgment, but it does not remove measurement, selection, contrast, semantic, or capacity debt.

## Dissent visibility

Dissent is route evidence too. Minority reports, unresolved negative controls, failed reanalyses, rival quotient interpretations, and domain objections must remain attached to the route unless a rollback or adjudication row explicitly retires them.

## Social testimony ceiling

Testimony can at most support review status, expert uncertainty, field uptake, and dispute structure. It cannot by itself create public-record closure, candidate-native observability, ontology identity, or S4/S5 authority.
