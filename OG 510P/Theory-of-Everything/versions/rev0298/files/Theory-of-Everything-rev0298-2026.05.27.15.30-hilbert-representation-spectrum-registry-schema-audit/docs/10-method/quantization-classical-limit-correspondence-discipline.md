# Quantization / classical-limit / semiclassical-correspondence discipline

This surface owns the rev0282 rule that a route may not spend quantum-completion, quantized-gravity, classical-limit, WKB, decoherence, backreaction, clock-recovery, or quantum-classical bridge language unless the route declares three objects:

1. a quantization map or a declared absence of one;
2. a classical-limit target and regime;
3. a semiclassical correspondence test connecting the quantum and classical descriptions.

A classical success is not a quantum completion. A formal quantization is not a physical Hilbert/state-space closure. A WKB or Born-Oppenheimer expansion is not a public-record bridge. Decoherence can explain classical-looking records without identifying the underlying ToE candidate. A route must keep ordering, measure, state-space, clock/time, backreaction, decoherence, and candidate-native observable gaps explicit.

## Noncompensation rule

A strong classical limit cannot compensate for a missing quantization map. A clean quantization map cannot compensate for a failed classical limit. A semiclassical bridge cannot compensate for missing public-record custody, gauge/observable quotient, sector control, matching, or viability rows.

## Required route questions

- What is the classical or prequantum domain?
- What is the quantum target object?
- Which quantization rule is used: canonical, path-integral, deformation, geometric, holographic dictionary, S-matrix, simulator, or explicit no-map state?
- Which ambiguity remains: ordering, measure, polarization, representation, contour, clock, state-space, dictionary, or simulator target?
- Which classical object is recovered, in which limit, and with which error or approximation bound?
- Which correspondence mechanism is claimed: WKB, decoherence, Born-Oppenheimer, large-N, backreaction, clock recovery, apparatus classicalization, or low-energy EFT matching?
- What rollback fires if the map, limit, or correspondence fails?
