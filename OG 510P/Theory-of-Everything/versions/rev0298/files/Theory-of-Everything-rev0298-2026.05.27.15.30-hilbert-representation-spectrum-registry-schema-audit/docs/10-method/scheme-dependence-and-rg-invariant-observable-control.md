# Scheme dependence and RG-invariant observable control

## Core distinction

The archive separates four objects that are often conflated:

```text
scheme coordinate
running parameter
RG trajectory / flow representation
physical invariant or matched public record
```

Only the last item can carry physical route credit, and even then only up to the route row's declared ceiling.

## Inessential versus essential structure

Some couplings, fields, coordinates, counterterms, or beta-function components can change under field redefinitions, regulator choices, subtraction conventions, basis changes, or truncation choices. A route may use them as diagnostics, but it may not treat them as ontology unless the corresponding `REG`, `RGF`, and `MAT` rows specify the quotient or invariant object.

## Fixed-point and universality language

A fixed point or universality class can be meaningful, but the archive requires the route to say:

- which theory space was searched;
- which regulator/truncation/subtraction scheme was used;
- which critical exponents or observables are scheme-independent;
- which inessential directions were quotiented;
- how the fixed-point result is matched to finite observed-sector records.

## EFT matching language

EFT support must state the source scale, target scale, degrees of freedom integrated out, Wilson coefficients or matching observables, power-counting rule, threshold corrections, and residual unmatched modes. A local EFT match is not a ToE-identification result.

## Cosmology and data-pipeline caution

Survey likelihoods, CMB maps, waveform models, and detector pipelines often carry parameterization and scale-setting choices. The archive therefore treats survey-scale, map-scale, catalog-scale, and detector-window choices as matching/regularization controls, not as direct candidate support.

## Rollback

If a result depends on a regulator, subtraction scheme, truncation, field redefinition, matching threshold, power-counting convention, counterterm choice, or untracked scale, the route must roll back to the most restrictive surviving row. Repair does not restore prior support automatically.
