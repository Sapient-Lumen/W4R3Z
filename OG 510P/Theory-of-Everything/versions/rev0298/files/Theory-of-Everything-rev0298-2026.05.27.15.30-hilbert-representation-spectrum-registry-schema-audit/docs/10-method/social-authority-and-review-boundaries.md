# Social authority and review boundaries

Revision: `rev0273`

## Purpose

This surface prevents social authority from becoming a hidden promotion channel. Peer review, publication, expert testimony, citation prestige, institutional hosting, registered-report status, replication claims, and community consensus can help route attention and expose scrutiny, but they are not themselves candidate-native evidence.

## Stop rule

No route may use `peer-reviewed`, `published`, `accepted`, `expert-endorsed`, `community consensus`, `replicated`, `registered report`, `highly cited`, `standard view`, `Nobel-level`, or `institutional authority` language unless the social-authority, review/replication, and consensus/elicitation rows are declared and current.

## Non-promotion rule

Social review can narrow, cap, or freeze claims. It can help decide whether a public carrier, acquisition protocol, or evidence unit has been inspected. It cannot replace the route denominator, observed-sector recovery, negative controls, measurement model, validity domain, causal mechanism, selection function, model-capacity bill, semantic binding, or rollback handle.

## Required denominator

Each social authority claim must name:

1. the authority signal being invoked;
2. the route-local target object inspected;
3. whether the review is independent of the route authors, data, priors, code, and school;
4. what failed reviews, null replications, dissent, and alternative readings remain visible;
5. the maximum authority effect allowed by the route row.

## Prohibited moves

- `peer reviewed` -> true;
- `widely accepted` -> candidate-native identity;
- `expert consensus` -> closure;
- `replicated benchmark` -> ontology;
- `registered report` -> positive result;
- `institutional data product` -> full ToE support;
- `citation count` -> independent evidence.
