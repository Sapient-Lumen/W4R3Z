# Ontology commitment and claim-language permission

Revision: `rev0272`

This surface separates four questions that the archive previously risked blending:

1. What term is being used?
2. What structure or operation does it name?
3. What ontological commitment, if any, follows?
4. What language may a claim use at the current route ceiling?

`ONTOLOGY-COMMITMENT-LEDGER.json` answers the second and third questions. `CLAIM-LANGUAGE-PERMISSION-LEDGER.json` answers the fourth.

## Key distinction

Structural retention, empirical adequacy, operational replay, formal equivalence, duality, and public-record agreement do not automatically imply ontology identity. `OQ-0058` already blocks duality/underdetermination laundering. `OQ-0069` adds the language-level stop rule: even when two descriptions are related, the archive must say whether the relation is label-equivalence, record-equivalence, model-equivalence, quotient-equivalence, structural retention, or full candidate identity.

## Permission rule

Allowed wording is capped by the weakest of:

- route state;
- promotion ceiling;
- public-record carrier and acquisition status;
- evidence credit and contrast/update rows;
- measurement, domain, causal, selection, and capacity rows;
- semantic-term row;
- ontology-commitment row;
- claim-language-permission row;
- rollback state.

No rhetorical polish, familiar vocabulary, realist idiom, or ontology label can raise that ceiling.
