# Independent-implementation versus independent-evidence-generation-closure gate

Purpose: split the archive's **witness-package progress** one step further.
A candidate should not earn the same witness credit merely because it can **let outsiders reach the same evidential class through genuinely separate code lineages answering to a public semantic specification** and because it can **let outsiders regenerate that evidential class through separately acquired, separately prepared, or blind / holdout evidence routes rather than by repeatedly analyzing one inherited evidence source**.
Those are related, but they are not the same achievement.

## Compression verdict

Inside the archive's witness package, once a lane has already moved beyond fresh-host reinstantiation and independent implementation, the current score should now distinguish five nested layers:

1. **public evidence-custody closure** — the decisive evidence bundle or reference object is stably extractable, versioned, fixity-checkable, preservable, citable, and reopenable beyond one privileged repository or operator chain;
2. **fresh-host reinstantiation closure** — outsiders can take that public bundle, identify the exact workflow / software / environment objects it depends on, materialize an executable environment on a fresh host or stack, and rerun or rehydrate the evidential process under declared equivalence rules without privileged manual setup;
3. **independent implementation closure** — outsiders can implement the relevant workflow semantics, protocol, model, or measurement logic through a genuinely separate code lineage, test it against a public conformance or agreement criterion, and still recover the same evidential class without carrying the originating executable bundle itself;
4. **independent evidence-generation closure** — outsiders can obtain the same evidential class through separately acquired, separately prepared, or blind / holdout record-generation routes, interlaboratory comparison pathways, or independent measurement chains rather than only through multiple implementations over one inherited evidence source;
5. **local public witness closure** — a lane can say where the physical records live, what stabilizes them, how they are reread, why they remain public evidence, and why that empirical loop is not mainly outsourced.

A lane may earn real credit on layer 3 while still being weak on layer 4.
That is progress.
It is just not yet independent evidence-generation closure, much less full witness closure.

## Why this gate exists

Shared witness-gate scaffolding now lives in `docs/40-model/witness-closure-gate-family-frame.md`. This document only names the pair-specific inflation move below.

**agreement across separate implementations being retold as if outsiders therefore already possessed fresh, independently generated evidence rather than repeated analysis of one inherited benchmark corpus, detector stream, scorer, simulator, or metrology chain.**

That inflation can happen in at least seven ways:
- a lane can publish multiple conforming implementations while every result still depends on one public benchmark corpus or one frozen detector stream;
- a lane can show cross-implementation agreement while the decisive labels, scores, or acceptance verdicts still come from one central evaluator or one private oracle;
- a lane can keep the code lineages separate while all implementations are tuned against one public practice set rather than challenged on hidden or fresh evidence;
- a lane can claim “independent replication” when later teams merely replay the same acquired records instead of acquiring, preparing, or transferring comparable records themselves;
- a lane can invoke interlaboratory rhetoric while laboratories never actually measure the same or comparable items under predetermined conditions;
- a lane can compare outputs from different implementations while still borrowing one undisclosed reference simulator, one shared synthetic-data generator, or one protected scoring rubric;
- or a lane can cite one comparison host's summary verdict while the underlying degree-of-equivalence, uncertainty, or challenge artifacts are too weak to show that the evidential class survived a genuinely separate record-generation route.

## The five-step independent-evidence-generation test

### 1. Separate record route, not just separate analyzers

Before awarding independent-evidence-generation credit, the archive should ask whether the later result depends on a genuinely separate evidence route rather than merely a separate implementation.
At minimum, a lane should disclose:
- whether later users acquired new measurements, prepared new comparison items, or exercised a disjoint hidden / holdout prompt or test set,
- whether the later route reuses the original raw records, labels, scorer, simulator, or benchmark prompts,
- which parts of the evidential chain are newly generated and which remain inherited,
- and what makes the later route comparable to the original one.

A lane that wins only here earns **independent implementation credit**, not yet independent evidence-generation closure.

### 2. Leakage control or blindness discipline

A lane earns more than implementation credit only when outsiders can tell that the later evidence route was not silently shaped by the original public artifacts.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- whether there is a hidden test set, blinded challenge item, sealed reference value, or other leakage-control device,
- whether public practice artifacts are explicitly separated from official evaluation artifacts,
- whether the scoring or adjudication path itself is shielded from training-time overfitting or retrospective tailoring,
- and what happens when the hidden or fresh route diverges from the public practice route.

Without that route, apparent fresh evidence may still be benchmark reuse in disguise.

### 3. Interlaboratory or inter-chain comparability rule

A lane earns stronger independent-evidence-generation credit only when separately generated evidence can still be compared under declared rules.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what counts as the same measurand, same evidential class, or same challenge target across chains,
- what protocol, transfer standard, or comparison artifact keeps the chains comparable,
- what uncertainty, tolerance, or degree-of-equivalence rule is used,
- and whether the later chain can disagree publicly without being normalized away by one host's narrative summary.

Without those answers, apparent independence may still be plurality without comparability.

### 4. Public disagreement handling over fresh evidence

A lane earns still stronger independent-evidence-generation credit only when disagreement across fresh evidence routes is itself informative rather than suppressible.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- whether divergences across blind / holdout or interlaboratory routes are public enough to expose under-specification,
- whether inconclusive outcomes are allowed when the comparison instrument is too weak,
- what public replacement, revision, or narrowing of claims follows from those divergences,
- and whether one central scorer, host, or coordinator can silently collapse the dispute back into a single privileged result.

Without that route, apparent fresh evidence may still be one chain with satellites.

### 5. Fresh evidence must still route back into public witness structure

A lane earns **independent evidence-generation closure** credit only when later users can connect separate implementations and separate evidence chains back to one public evidential class.
At minimum, it should name:
- the public semantic specification,
- the independent implementation lineages,
- the hidden / blind / holdout or interlaboratory evidence route,
- the comparison or degree-of-equivalence rule,
- and the public artifact path by which those fresh records remain challengeable, preservable, and reopenable.

This is still not the same thing as full local public witness closure.
A lane may support independent evidence generation very well while still borrowing the actual physical record carrier, stabilization story, or rereadability semantics from ordinary laboratory witness structure.

## Common failure modes

1. **same-dataset pluralism** — several implementations agree because they all analyze the same inherited evidence source.
2. **practice-set overfitting** — public practice artifacts quietly shape the later “independent” result.
3. **scorer monoculture** — supposedly independent routes still depend on one private evaluator, labeler, or summary oracle.
4. **reference-simulator capture** — different implementations inherit the same synthetic generator or reference model, so the evidence route is not genuinely separate.
5. **comparison-without-equivalence** — several labs or chains exist, but no declared rule says what counts as comparable agreement.
6. **fresh-evidence suppression** — divergences on hidden or blind evidence are normalized away rather than forcing revision, replacement, or inconclusive status.
7. **record-route amnesia** — the later route produces new records, but the archive cannot reconstruct how those records were generated, compared, and preserved.

## Compact current readout

- **MLCommons AILuminate's current methodology and FAQ** make the leakage burden explicit by splitting public practice prompts from hidden official prompts and warning that training on the practice set overfits it and destroys its diagnostic value relative to the hidden set.
- **ISO/IEC 17043:2023** makes the scheme burden explicit by treating proficiency testing as a competence-and-impartiality discipline for consistently operated comparison schemes rather than as ad hoc reruns.
- **ILAC P9:01/2024** makes the accreditation burden explicit by treating proficiency-testing and interlaboratory-comparison activities as part of the accreditation process rather than as optional theater.
- **BIPM's current CIPM MRA comparison guidance** makes the equivalence burden explicit by requiring comparison procedures that make results reliable and reproducible, are sufficient to demonstrate and maintain equivalence, and express degrees of equivalence quantitatively rather than by narrative reassurance alone.
- **NIST's current performance-monitoring glossary** makes the monitoring burden explicit by listing blind testing, interlaboratory comparisons, and proficiency testing as distinct ongoing checks rather than as optional embellishments after code agreement.

That does not refute current observer-relative or bridge-theory programs.
It just means they have not yet paid this stronger independent-evidence-generation debt.

## Net rule

Within the archive:
- independent implementation closure earns real witness-package credit;
- but it does **not** yet count as independent evidence-generation closure;
- and neither of those automatically counts as local public witness closure.

Any future completion bid or bridge-family claim that wants stronger witness credit should now say not only how outsiders can derive separate implementations from public semantics, but how separately generated records, blind / holdout routes, or interlaboratory evidence chains recover the same evidential class under declared comparison rules without leaning on one inherited evidence source.
