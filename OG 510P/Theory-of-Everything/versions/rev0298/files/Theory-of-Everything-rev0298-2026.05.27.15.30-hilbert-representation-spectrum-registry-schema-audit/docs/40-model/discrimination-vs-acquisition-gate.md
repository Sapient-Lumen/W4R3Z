# Discrimination-versus-acquisition gate

Purpose: split the **rival-discriminating** layer inside `docs/40-model/prediction-vs-discrimination-gate.md` one step further.
A completion bid should not earn the same empirical-traction credit merely because some consequence would **separate named rivals in principle** and because there is a **credible acquisition path** by which the archive could plausibly obtain that separation.
Shared family scaffolding and ordered routing live in `docs/40-model/completion-bid-comparison-gate-family-frame.md` and `docs/40-model/completion-bid-comparison-stack.md`; this file keeps only the pair-specific split between in-principle rival separation and acquisition-grade traction.

## Compression verdict

The archive should now score claimed empirical traction in **three acquisition layers** after rival separation is already established:

1. **in-principle rival separation** — if data of the stated kind existed at the stated precision, the outcome would materially reorder named nearby rivals;
2. **acquisition-path disclosure** — the lane names the actual observable channel, accessible regime, exposure / precision / systematic floor, and auxiliary assumptions required to obtain that separation;
3. **acquisition-grade discriminatory traction** — the archive can see a credible route by which a real experimental, observational, or computational campaign could plausibly obtain the separating evidence without silently relying on fantasy access, hidden background subtraction, or effectively unreachable regimes.

A lane may earn real credit on layer 1 while still being weak on layers 2 and 3.
That is still progress.
It is just not yet program-grade empirical traction.
Even that is not yet the same as stable theory attribution; the downstream split lives in `docs/40-model/acquisition-vs-attribution-gate.md`.

## Why this gate exists

`docs/40-model/prediction-vs-discrimination-gate.md` already blocked one inflation move:
**a risky forecast pretending to be a rival-discriminating wedge.**

A further inflation move remained too easy:
**a rival-discriminating wedge in principle pretending to be an actually acquirable empirical wedge.**

That confusion appears whenever a completion bid is praised because it:
- names an observable that would distinguish nearby rivals if measured with extreme precision,
- points to a very high-energy or very early-universe regime with little path to access,
- relies on background control, foreground subtraction, or environmental isolation that is never costed,
- or invokes a separating simulation / reconstruction target whose required inputs are themselves not credibly obtainable.

A wedge can be logically real and still be acquisition-thin.
This gate keeps those credits separate.

## The three-step acquisition test

### 1. Channel and regime disclosure

Before counting a discriminating wedge as stronger empirical traction, the archive should ask:
- which concrete observable channel carries the wedge,
- which regime supplies the signal,
- whether that regime is laboratory, astrophysical, cosmological, analog, or purely reconstructed,
- and which auxiliary assumptions are needed before the observable is even defined.

A lane that cannot name the channel or regime may still have an interesting idea.
It does not yet have acquisition-grade traction.

### 2. Precision and systematic honesty

A wedge earns more than in-principle credit only if the archive is told what it costs to see it.
At minimum, the lane should disclose:
- approximate separating precision or exposure,
- dominant noise / background / degeneracy sources,
- whether the wedge survives those systematics rather than only an idealized limit,
- and whether the required inference pipeline depends on fragile modeling choices that could wash the separation back out.

The archive should not demand perfect cost accounting.
It should demand enough honesty to tell the difference between **difficult** and **effectively unreachable**.

### 3. Campaign-grade decision value

Even after a channel and a nominal precision are named, the archive should still ask whether a plausible campaign could actually move theory choice.
A wedge earns **acquisition-grade discriminatory** credit only when:
- the relevant measurement path is at least articulable rather than magical,
- success or failure would materially reorder the named rival set,
- and the separating power is not swallowed by foreseeable degeneracies, null-systematics, or model-conditioned reinterpretation.

A wedge may be admirable and still not be the place the archive should currently expect decisive movement.
That is a planning fact, not a refutation.

## Common failure modes

1. **Regime fantasy** — the wedge lives only at energies, epochs, or isolation levels with no credible acquisition path.
2. **Precision laundering** — a separating threshold is invoked without naming the exposure, control, or systematic floor needed to reach it.
3. **Systematic silence** — the observable is defined only in an idealized pipeline whose real backgrounds or degeneracies are left unpriced.
4. **Decision inflation** — a wedge that would barely move program ranking under realistic uncertainty is narrated as imminent theory-choice leverage.

## Immediate archive rules

1. **Do not count a logically separating wedge as program-grade traction unless its acquisition path is named.**
2. **Require a concrete observable channel, regime, approximate separating resolution or exposure, and dominant systematic debt whenever a lane claims acquisition-grade discrimination.**
3. **Give extra credit only when the wedge would still materially reorder named rivals after realistic acquisition costs and foreseeable degeneracies are admitted.**
4. **Do not punish honest admission that a wedge is currently in-principle only.**
   Punish only the inflation move that treats such a wedge as already acquired empirical leverage.
5. **Keep this gate subordinate to the prediction-versus-discrimination gate and the canonical discriminator surfaces.**
   This surface sharpens comparison inside rival-discriminating success; it does not replace separate debts on recovery, observables, package disclosure, or witness closure.

## Net result

The archive now has a smaller and more honest sentence available:

> a completion bid may offer a genuinely rival-separating signal in principle and still be weak on acquisition-grade empirical traction.

That sentence blocks another prestige inflation move without widening the archive.
