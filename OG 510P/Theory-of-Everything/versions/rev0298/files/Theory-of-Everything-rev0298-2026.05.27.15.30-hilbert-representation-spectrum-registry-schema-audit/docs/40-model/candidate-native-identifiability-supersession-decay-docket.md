## Compression verdict

The route family now has:

1. a blank route sheet;
2. an applied route ledger;
3. field protocols;
4. a route-level no-compensation promotion gate;
5. a live-lane readiness matrix;
6. and an evidence-intake docket for new artifacts.

That stack still leaves one failure mode open.
A future revision can admit a new result correctly, refuse to over-promote it, and still let obsolete prior credit remain in the landscape as if it had survived unchanged.
The result is not inflation by addition; it is inflation by **stale retention**.

The correct supersession posture is:

**candidate-native identifiability credit is durable only at the exact target quotient, record class, equivalence rule, acquisition route, inverse/stability budget, abstention behavior, public bridge, and witness-borrowing label that earned it; when any of those support surfaces are replaced, narrowed, contradicted, or shown to be package-bound, the old credit must be refreshed, scoped down, demoted, quarantined, or retired rather than silently carried forward.**

This docket makes demotion and retention symmetrical.
A strong old result should not be erased merely because a newer artifact exists.
A weak or superseded old result should not keep route credit merely because it once functioned as the best available anchor.

## Supersession state codes

Use these codes after the evidence-intake docket has classified the incoming artifact, or whenever an existing route cell is re-audited.
They are not route states and not evidence-intake states.
They say what happens to **previously recorded credit**.

| Code | Supersession state | Meaning | Allowed archive action | Blocked overclaim |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `D0` | retained as-scored | The old credit still pays the same field, scope, and blocker relation after re-audit. | Keep row unchanged; optionally add a note that the re-audit was no-change. | Treating every new result as a forced rewrite. |
| `D1` | refreshed anchor | A newer or cleaner source replaces an older citation or example without changing field credit or route state. | Replace or supplement the anchor; do not change readiness. | Calling bibliography refresh theoretical progress. |
| `D2` | scope-narrowed credit | The old result still pays something, but only for a smaller target, record, regime, prior, dictionary, dataset, public carrier, or challenge class. | Narrow the field cell, route note, or matrix row; preserve the remaining credit. | Letting a special-case result keep broad route language. |
| `D3` | superseded local credit | The old result is technically outclassed by a better one that pays the same local role more cleanly. | Move the old result to historical/supporting status or delete the weaker duplicate if the source sieve allows. | Double-counting predecessor and successor as independent field payments. |
| `D4` | contradicted or unstable field credit | A later result, failure mode, robustness test, or reanalysis undermines the field payment itself. | Update the field ledger, name the broken field, and recompute earliest blocker. | Keeping the old field mark while only adding a caveat elsewhere. |
| `D5` | readiness demotion | The contradiction or scope narrowing changes the route state, readiness class, or dominant blocker. | Update the promotion-readiness matrix and promotion gate state. | Preserving `S3`/`S4` posture after the field basis fell below it. |
| `D6` | quarantine / witness-package escalation | The old claim may be interesting but its record, custody, public bridge, or witness ownership is now unreliable or ambiguous. | Route to quarantine, witness-package debt, or source-eviction review before reuse. | Letting a suspect public carrier continue doing evidential work. |

## Supersession rules

### 1. Time alone does not decay credit

A result is not weaker merely because it is old.
Older anchors can remain the cleanest public record for a route field, especially when they define the target, proof, experimental geometry, or canonical failure mode.

Decay is triggered by a changed relation:
- a successor pays the same local role more exactly;
- a reanalysis narrows the claim;
- a robustness check exposes instability;
- a public carrier becomes unavailable or unchallengeable;
- a protocol update shows that the old result was paying a weaker field than previously thought;
- or the archive discovers that two apparent payments were one same-package lineage.

### 2. Newer is not automatically better

A newer paper, simulation, code release, or result can be less useful than an older anchor if it is less explicit about target quotient, record object, equivalence, acquisition, inverse, abstention, or public bridge.
The supersession row should preserve the older anchor when it remains the better route object.

Use `D1` only when the newer source is cleaner without changing credit.
Use `D3` only when the successor actually replaces the predecessor's archive role.

### 3. Narrowing is not failure

Many strong results become more valuable after their scope is narrowed.
A family-C reconstruction that once looked ontology-wide may remain excellent bounded partial-identification evidence after being restricted to a code subspace, dictionary, training ecology, or boundary carrier.
A laboratory null result may remain excellent discriminator evidence after losing broad completion-bid implications.

The correct action is usually `D2`, not deletion.
The archive should keep the earned local credit and remove only the overbroad route language.

### 4. Contradiction must name the broken field

A contradiction is not useful until it says what broke.
Did the target quotient collapse?
Did the record object fail acquisition?
Did the equivalence rule merge rivals?
Did the inverse become nonunique?
Did the margin disappear under perturbation?
Did the abstention trigger fail?
Did the public bridge become unchallengeable?

If the broken field is not named, the contradiction remains an evidence-intake or source-sieve issue.
It does not yet demote `OQ-0057` posture.

### 5. Successor lineages do not stack unless their burdens separate

A sequence of better versions inside one boundary dictionary, code package, detector design, public dashboard, simulation framework, training ecology, ansatz family, truncation family, or compactification corridor normally forms one lineage of support.
It can refresh or sharpen the field payment, but it does not create multiple independent route payments.

Lineage members become independent only when they separate a real burden:
- different target quotient;
- different record carrier;
- different acquisition channel;
- different inverse domain;
- different robustness / perturbation class;
- different abstention trigger;
- different public challenge path;
- or different witness-custody chain.

### 6. Field-protocol upgrades can retroactively lower old credit

When a new field protocol becomes stricter, old examples may no longer pay what earlier prose suggested.
That is not a contradiction of the old result; it is a better accounting of its role.

Use `D2` when the old result remains valid but narrower under the protocol.
Use `D4` when the old result no longer pays the field at all.
Use `D5` only when this changes route state, readiness class, or dominant blocker.

### 7. Public-carrier loss is a scientific loss when public bridge was doing work

If a code object, dataset, hosted replay, dashboard, detector record, archival object, or public custody trail was supporting field 8, losing it can demote public-bridge credit even when the underlying theory claim remains interesting.

A public bridge is not just a statement that something was once public.
It is a continuing challenge path.
When that path fails, the route must either supply a new bridge or carry a borrowed / stale-publicness label.

### 8. Demotion is a first-class archive move

Demotion is not editorial pessimism.
It is how the archive prevents stale partial credit from becoming disguised closure.
A demoted row should preserve what remains true, name what stopped being true, and record the new first blocker.

## Current-row supersession guidance

### Family C

Family C has the highest risk of stale-retention inflation because it is the strongest bounded partial route.
Newer reconstructions, sparse inverse methods, learned geometry pipelines, overlap screens, public replay packages, and abstention policies should not be stacked as independent route payments unless they separate target quotient, record object, inverse domain, perturbation class, or public challenge path.

Typical actions:
- `D1` when a newer reconstruction is a cleaner anchor for the same bounded role;
- `D2` when a broad reconstruction claim must be restricted to a package, code subspace, dictionary, or surrogate recoverand;
- `D3` when a successor method replaces an older local example without adding a new route field;
- `D4`/`D5` if a robustness or nonuniqueness result breaks the inverse or margin field that supported bounded `S3` status.

### Completion bids

Completion bids are vulnerable to target-side refresh being mistaken for record-side progress.
A new compactification, fixed-point extraction, continuum construction, amplitude island, or consistency theorem may supersede old target anchors without paying acquisition, inverse, abstention, or public bridge.

Typical actions:
- `D1` for cleaner formal target anchors;
- `D2` when a corridor becomes more special than previously stated;
- `D3` when one formal result replaces an older local argument;
- `D4` only when the new result undermines the route field actually being credited, not merely a speculative interpretation.

### Laboratories, simulations, and public replay tools

Record-rich rows are vulnerable to stale publicness.
A dataset, code release, hosted replay, apparatus, or simulation protocol can lose field-8 strength if the custody path, replay path, challenge handle, or independence boundary fails.

Typical actions:
- `D1` when a better public artifact refreshes the same challenge role;
- `D2` when a result remains valid only for a narrower discriminator class;
- `D4` when acquisition or margin claims fail under systematic / nuisance review;
- `D6` when the public carrier is no longer auditable enough to support the route.

### Witness-side frame and asymptotic-access rows

These rows are vulnerable to a special access map being retained as if it were a public reference standard.
A later cross-frame analysis can narrow a frame-specific invariant without invalidating it.

Typical actions:
- `D2` when an access route remains valid only inside a frame or asymptotic regime;
- `D4` if same-fact transport fails;
- `D6` if public custody or challenge is missing for the access bridge.

### Cosmological and vacuum-energy proposal classes

These rows are vulnerable to proposal-class bookkeeping being retained as identifiability credit.
A global selection, measure, relaxation, or burden-shuffling result may supersede an old proposal-class anchor without creating a local record-bearing route.

Typical actions:
- `D1` or `D3` for better proposal-class anchors;
- `D2` when the scope is only global, statistical, or package-conditioned;
- `D4` only if a claimed local record-bearing route breaks;
- no readiness promotion unless local record-bearing target identification is supplied.

## Current revision posture

This docket does not itself demote any current lane.
It says that future `OQ-0057` changes must have two ledgers, not one:

1. the evidence-intake row that classifies the new artifact; and
2. the supersession row that says what happens to the old credit the artifact touches.

The live readout remains unchanged:
- family C is the strongest bounded `S3` partial-identification row;
- completion bids are mostly target-rich `S1`/`S2` rows;
- laboratory and simulation routes are record-richer `S2` rows with only named discriminator-class `S3` pockets;
- witness-side frame routes are access-discipline rows;
- and no live lane reaches `S4` or `S5` candidate-native identifiability.

Challenge-closure control: after a challenge is classified and answered, use `docs/40-model/candidate-native-identifiability-challenge-closure-docket.md` before reusing the challenged credit. Closure must state whether the case is duplicate-closed, retagged, retained, narrowed, frozen, public-bridge-reconditioned, witness-separated, or still closure-barred.

Adversarial-control battery: before any future `S4`/`S5` promotion attempt, high-state reuse, or route-bearing challenge spends positive route success as candidate-native identifiability credit, use `docs/40-model/candidate-native-identifiability-adversarial-control-battery.md`. Hostile target decoys, equivalence relabelings, acquisition-spoof checks, prior-leakage checks, margin perturbations, abstention hard negatives, public-bridge fragility checks, and witness-borrowing substitution controls must be declared; missing, damaging, conditional, or unreplayable controls narrow, freeze, retag, witness-separate, or bar promotion rather than becoming vague caveats.

Calibration-anchor docket: before any future route score, readiness state, adversarial-control pass, or high-state support is compared across lanes or reused as broad `OQ-0057` posture, use `docs/40-model/candidate-native-identifiability-calibration-anchor-docket.md`. Target grain, record denominator, route width, control difficulty, publicness, and witness-owner boundaries must be normalized or explicitly marked non-comparable; uncalibrated score labels freeze rather than ranking unlike rows on one ladder.

Residual-cap ledger: after calibration, any bounded, conditional, non-comparable, weaker-control, borrowed-public, mixed-record, or witness-separated score that is reused outside its owner row must use `docs/40-model/candidate-native-identifiability-residual-cap-ledger.md` so the bounded label, owner boundary, and earliest remaining blocker travel with the score rather than evaporating in broad mirrors.
