# Geometric-observable noncommutativity versus cross-frame portability audit

This document keeps one fresh witness-side frame brake load-bearing without widening the archive.
It asks what the archive should count when recent Lorentz-frame work stops being only a minimal-length slogan dispute and instead earns an explicit canonical obstruction to simultaneous sharp cross-frame geometry.

## Compression verdict

The archive should now hold one tighter sentence:

**recent cross-frame geometry work has crossed the line from a loose boost paradox to a first bounded incompatibility brake, because geometric observables associated with different inertial observers can be shown to generically fail to Poisson commute even in Minkowski spacetime; but that gain is still weaker than an explicit cross-frame fact-transport rule, public reference-standard closure, and local witness closure.** `REF-0182`

That means the honest archive move is neither:
- “different-frame geometry is just a trivial Lorentz redescription,”
- nor “noncommutativity by itself already solves public cross-frame witness transport.”

The right score is in between.

## What has genuinely improved

### 1. Cross-frame incompatibility is now explicit rather than gestural

Assanioussi, Kowalski-Glikman, Mäkinen, and Varrin compute the Poisson bracket of rod-length observables measured by two geodesic observers with different velocities and find that the observables generically do not Poisson commute, not even in Minkowski spacetime. `REF-0182`

That matters because the archive should now treat “the same geometric quantity in another Lorentz frame” as a potentially incompatible measurement context rather than as a frictionless portability story.

### 2. The brake is geometric and observer-attached, not only a Planck-scale slogan

The result is not phrased as a pure black-hole-resolution or phenomenology slogan about minimum length.
It is attached to observer-defined simultaneity surfaces and observer-indexed geometric observables themselves. `REF-0182`

That gives the archive a cleaner reason to distinguish:
1. one chosen frame measuring one sharp geometric quantity,
2. a true transport rule for the same coarse-grained fact across frames,
3. and a still stronger public standard that makes those comparisons operationally shared.

### 3. Same-theory frame change is not automatically a simultaneous public fact

If the relevant geometric observables for different observers do not Poisson commute, then a sharp fact in one observer package need not remain simultaneously sharp in another.
That does not yet tell us how real experiments coarse-grain, stabilize, or reread records, but it does block a cheap rhetorical promotion from “one frame works” to “the fact is already public across frames.” `REF-0182`

## What this does **not** buy

### 1. Not yet cross-frame witness portability

A non-commuting pair of observer-indexed observables is a brake on inflation, not yet a transport map.
The archive still needs:
- a declared same-fact or reidentification rule across frames,
- an explicit account of what is transported and what becomes frame-indexed,
- and a validity class that says where the comparison is operationally meaningful. `REF-0062`, `REF-0063`, `REF-0179`, `REF-0182`

So this gain belongs below genuine cross-frame witness portability.

### 2. Not yet public reference-standard closure

The result does not say how a shared standard of orientation, clocking, adequacy, or calibration becomes public enough for multi-observer use.
If anything, it makes that burden more visible by showing that frame change can come with genuine incompatibility rather than harmless relabeling. `REF-0066`, `REF-0182`

So this gain is also weaker than public reference-standard closure.

### 3. Not yet local witness closure

Nothing here says where durable records live, what stabilizes them, how they are reread, or why public evidence custody becomes candidate-native.
The gain is about observer-indexed geometry and incompatibility of sharp cross-frame measurement, not about public record carriers. `REF-0015`, `REF-0016`, `REF-0017`, `REF-0182`

## The archive score change

The witness-side frame lane should now remember one smaller internal distinction explicitly:

1. **one-frame geometric success** — one chosen observer package supports one sharp geometric observable;
2. **cross-frame incompatibility brake** — different observer packages can fail to support simultaneous sharp geometry, so portability is not free;
3. **cross-frame witness portability** — the same coarse-grained fact is transported and reidentified across observer packages under an explicit map;
4. **public reference-standard closure** — the comparison standard itself becomes public enough for multi-observer use;
5. **local witness closure** — records, stabilization, rereadability, and publicity are candidate-native.

The archive does **not** need to insert a new formal stage into the global witness ladder for this.
But it should keep this brake live inside `OQ-0040` and `OQ-0041`.

## False promotions to reject

1. **Lorentz covariance talk is not yet public witness portability**
   - A transformation law is weaker than an explicit same-fact transport rule.

2. **cross-frame noncommutativity is not yet public standard closure**
   - Exposing incompatibility does not itself tell us how a shared standard becomes public.

3. **observer-indexed geometric control is not record closure**
   - Geometry in two frame packages still does not tell the archive where durable records live or how they are reread.

4. **one sharp frame result is not a simultaneously sharp many-frame fact**
   - The new result gives the archive an explicit reason to reject that inflation move.

## What would move the archive further

A later revision should score the witness-side frame lane as materially stronger only if one compact route adds most of the following together:
1. an explicit same-fact transport or reidentification rule across frame packages,
2. a disclosed coarse-graining and calibration budget,
3. a public comparison-standard story rather than only observer-indexed simultaneity construction,
4. a record-carrier and rereadability route,
5. and a statement of where incompatibility remains irreducible versus where it is only artifact of description.

Without that package, the archive should preserve this gain as a bounded incompatibility brake and stop there.

## Net result

The archive now has a better brake sentence for `OQ-0040` and `OQ-0041`:

> one frame's sharp geometric fact is not yet a public cross-frame fact, because different observer-indexed geometric observables can already fail to Poisson commute even before record publicity, calibration, and shared-standard burdens are paid.

That keeps the gain load-bearing without letting it inflate. `REF-0182`
