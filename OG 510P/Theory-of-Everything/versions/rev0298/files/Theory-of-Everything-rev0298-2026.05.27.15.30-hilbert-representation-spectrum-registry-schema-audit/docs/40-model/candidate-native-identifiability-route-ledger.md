## Compression verdict

The current field-level verdict is sharper than the prior prose verdict:

**no current lane fails everywhere, but every current lane fails at least one decisive native-identifiability field.**

The failure pattern is not uniform.
Family C has the strongest partial route because it has named targets, record proxies, restricted inverses, stability screens, and abstention pressure inside controlled reconstruction packages.
But its decisive same/different, acquisition-realizability, inverse-completeness / stability, abstention / no-verdict, public-record, public-bridge, and route-portability fields remain partly borrowed from boundary packaging, dictionary choice, gauge/frame convention, finite-window control, simulation / training ecology, regularization choice, policy choice, software practice, or ordinary laboratory custody.

Formal completion bids often have the opposite shape.
They can name rich target spaces and strong internal consistency conditions, but they still lack a compact native route from actual public records to learnable candidate distinctions.
Low-energy and witness-side tool lanes often have real record acquisition or public comparison pressure, but they do not by themselves supply the candidate-native target, equivalence, and inverse-completeness account needed for final identifiability closure.

So the archive should keep the stronger current sentence:

**family C remains the strongest partial-identification lane; formal completion bids remain target-rich but acquisition-route / inverse-route / abstention-route / public-bridge-poor; laboratory and witness-side tools remain record-richer, sometimes margin- or null-result-richer, and sometimes public-bridge-richer but candidate-target-poor; no lane clears candidate-native identifiability closure.**

## Ledger codes

The route ledger uses four compact field codes.

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `N` | native enough to carry the field for the stated regime |
| `P` | partial, bounded, route-specific, or regime-limited payment |
| `B` | borrowed from an external laboratory, boundary, dictionary, gauge, model-selection, benchmark, or public-record ecology |
| `M` | missing, undeclared, or not yet the right object |

A row does not need all `N` entries to be scientifically useful.
But it cannot earn candidate-native identifiability closure while any decisive field remains `B` or `M`.
A `P` entry is useful only if the row names the boundary of the partial payment.

## Cross-field debts that now dominate

### 1. Equivalence is the main hidden bottleneck

Many lanes can name targets or records.
Fewer can say when two apparent targets are the same target under gauge, frame, branch, counterpart, coarse-graining, or empirical-collapse rules.
Without that rule, the archive should assume that an apparent candidate distinction may be a redescription or an underdetermined equivalence class.
`docs/40-model/candidate-native-equivalence-collapse-protocol.md` now turns that bottleneck into a required row: target token, variation axis, collapse status, invariant / carrier, boundary, and no-verdict outcome.

### 2. Acquisition is the next formal-observability bottleneck

Many lanes can define a record-like quantity.
Fewer can say how that quantity is actually prepared, accessed, obtained, stabilized, and compared under finite resources.
Without that route, a formal observable, boundary dictionary, simulation output, detector concept, or cosmological trace should remain formal-forward-map credit rather than acquired-evidence credit.
`docs/40-model/candidate-native-acquisition-realizability-protocol.md` now turns field 4 into a required row: candidate target, record object, access channel, regime / preparation assumptions, public acquisition path, finite-resource budget, nuisance controls, and no-acquisition outcome.

### 3. Inverse completeness and stability are the post-acquisition bottleneck

An acquirable record does not say what can be learned from that record.
A reconstruction, fit, or stable regularized output can still be rank-deficient, quotient-only, nuisance-confounded, policy-conditioned, or unstable under nearby admissible route choices.
`docs/40-model/candidate-native-inverse-completeness-stability-protocol.md` now turns fields 5 and 6 into a paired required row: target quotient, record domain, completeness claim, deficiency map, stability budget, separation / tie margin, robustness transport, no-inversion zone, and public audit handle.

### 4. Abstention is the post-inversion bottleneck

A route that can name a target, acquire a record, and run an inverse can still overclaim if it always reports one answer.
Without a native refusal rule, best-fit geometry, posterior mode, selected vacuum, classifier label, or policy-stable output should remain forced-answer credit rather than candidate-native identification.
`docs/40-model/candidate-native-abstention-no-verdict-protocol.md` now turns field 7 into a required row: target claim boundary, trigger state, returned evidence state, native trigger owner, margin / acquisition / equivalence relations, re-entry condition, and public challenge handle.

### 5. Public bridge is the route-to-community bottleneck

A lane can have a target, record, equivalence rule, acquisition path, inverse, margin, and no-verdict policy while still failing to export that route as public evidence.
Without a bridge object, native-to-public translation, custody unit, reread / replay path, challenge procedure, independence boundary, failure state, and witness-borrowing label, the route remains private, package-bound, or borrowed.
`docs/40-model/candidate-native-public-bridge-challenge-protocol.md` now turns field 8 into a required row.

### 6. Record-rich rows and target-rich rows fail differently

Formal completion rows tend to be target-rich but record-poor, abstention-poor, and public-bridge-poor.
Laboratory and simulation rows tend to be record-richer and sometimes null-result- or public-bridge-richer but target-poor.
Family C is strongest because it partially spans both sides, but it still borrows decisive field content from boundary, acquisition route, inverse route, abstention route, public bridge, and route-specific packaging.

### 7. Partial identification is the honest middle state

The archive should not erase partial identification.
A route that identifies one target subset, one equivalence class, one regime, or one discriminator-bearing difference is a real gain.
The mistake is to retell that gain as full candidate-native identifiability closure.

### 8. Abstention is a native competence, not politeness

A lane that always outputs one geometry, one UV story, one model, one frame fact, or one interpretation is not yet more informative than a lane that knows when the evidence collapses several targets.
Future route rows should treat explicit no-verdict, equivalence-class, calibrated-bundle, proxy-only, underdetermined, no-acquisition, no-inversion, and out-of-regime outputs as positive evidence of candidate-native discipline only when the abstention / no-verdict row says what triggers them and how outsiders can challenge them.

### 9. Public bridge remains below full witness closure

Even if a candidate had a strong field-8 bridge, it would still need stronger witness-package payment before claiming full local public witness closure.
Candidate-native identifiability and local public witness closure remain distinct debts.

## How future revisions should use this ledger

Do not add a new row merely because a lane is interesting.
Add or revise a row only when a claim changes one of the eight fields.
If the claimed change is in the empirical-equivalence field, apply `docs/40-model/candidate-native-equivalence-collapse-protocol.md` before changing the code. If the claimed change is in the forward / acquisition field, apply `docs/40-model/candidate-native-acquisition-realizability-protocol.md` before changing the code. If the claimed change is in inverse completeness or stability / margin, apply `docs/40-model/candidate-native-inverse-completeness-stability-protocol.md` before changing either field code. If the claimed change is in abstention, calibrated bundles, no-verdict, no-acquisition, no-inversion, or out-of-regime refusal behavior, apply `docs/40-model/candidate-native-abstention-no-verdict-protocol.md` before changing the field-7 code. If the claimed change is in public bridge, custody, replay, challenge, independence, or public terminal states, apply `docs/40-model/candidate-native-public-bridge-challenge-protocol.md` before changing the field-8 code. Then apply `docs/40-model/candidate-native-identifiability-promotion-gate.md` before saying that a field change promotes the route rather than only sharpening a local ledger cell.
The revision should say:

1. which row changed,
2. which field changed,
3. the old code and new code,
4. the basis for the change,
5. the remaining borrowed or missing fields,
6. whether the result changes the route promotion state or only one field code,
7. the earliest remaining dominant blocker,
8. and whether the result changes the claim registry or only clarifies debt.

A failed or incomplete row can still be valuable.
It localizes the debt and blocks inflation.
But it should not create an active followthrough item unless it exposes a genuinely earned next move.

## Net result

The archive now has a compact operational answer to the practical form of `OQ-0057`:

**which current lane is closest to candidate-native identifiability, and why is it still not closed?**

The answer is field-level rather than reputational.
Family C is closest because it pays the most fields partially.
