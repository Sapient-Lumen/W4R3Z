# Retirement versus exclusion gate

A completion bid should not earn the same archive-governance credit merely because some wedge would **justify retiring rivals from the active hedge set** and because that same improvement would **justify speaking as though those rivals were now impossible, excluded in principle, or no longer worth preserving even in memorial form**.

Shared family scaffolding and ordered routing live in `docs/40-model/completion-bid-comparison-gate-family-frame.md` and `docs/40-model/completion-bid-comparison-stack.md`; this file keeps only the pair-specific split between retirement-grade pruning and exclusion-grade closure.

**Pair-specific split:** a lane may deserve rival retirement before it deserves proof-grade exclusion.

## What this gate separates

A completion bid can now earn:
1. **allocation-grade steering** — some real concentration of scarce effort is justified;
2. **retirement-grade pruning** — some rivals can move to watch-state or below-normal maintenance without paying too much irreversibility cost;
3. **exclusion-grade closure** — the archive is now warranted in saying that a rival lane is not merely inactive for current governance reasons, but excluded strongly enough that impossibility-style language, memorial minimization, or theorem-adjacent closure claims are appropriate.

This surface exists only to block the inflation from (2) to (3).

## Why the distinction matters

Retirement is comparative and practical.
Exclusion is stronger, and can easily overclaim.
A lane may become a poor use of current effort while still preserving unresolved alternative assumptions, local no-go lessons, or cheap reopening triggers that matter for future theory change.

The archive therefore needs one more honesty layer:
**retiring a rival is not yet the same as proving that rival dead.**

## What exclusion-grade closure would have to cash out

### 1. Scope-of-exclusion disclosure

A lane does not earn exclusion-grade closure credit until the archive says exactly what is being excluded.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- whether the negative result touches one benchmark, one package subclass, one truncation, one cosmological dressing, one observable channel, or the broad lane itself;
- whether the pressure is local to a current implementation or genuinely lane-wide;
- whether the target is “not currently competitive,” “not worth active maintenance,” “ruled out under this assumption set,” or something much stronger;
- and whether the exclusion claim is temporary on the current archive basis or intended as durable cross-basis closure.

If scope is not named, retirement language can quietly inflate into fake impossibility.

### 2. Auxiliary-space and reinterpretation honesty

A rival lane should not be treated as excluded merely because one currently favored formulation has failed.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what auxiliary assumptions carried the claimed failure: boundary conditions, regulator choices, matter packaging, measure rules, truncation choices, or benchmark definitions;
- whether a nearby reinterpretation would preserve the lane while changing the failing surface;
- whether the claimed exclusion really lands on the lane's load-bearing commitments or only on one convenient representative;
- and whether the surviving alternative formulations are empty rescue rhetoric or still contain identifiable, bounded, and testable structure.

A lane that survives only by unbounded rescue maneuvers may deserve harsher language.
A lane that survives by one or two explicit nearby reinterpretations does not yet deserve proof-style closure language.

### 3. No-go grade versus comparative defeat

The archive should not blur together “currently loses” and “cannot work.”
At minimum, it should ask whether the closure claim comes from:
- a comparative evidence defeat on the current archive basis,
- a repeated failure to cash out promised recovery or observables,
- a bounded no-go theorem under named assumptions,
- or a contradiction that genuinely reaches the lane's core commitments.

Only the last two get close to exclusion-grade closure.
Comparative defeat and even durable retirement can still be weaker than impossibility.

### 4. Memorial floor and reopening-trigger preservation

Even a retired rival may still deserve compact memorial preservation.
At minimum, the archive should name:
- what minimal comparison memory remains: one claim entry, one failure-mode note, one discriminator note, one bibliography floor;
- what concrete conditions would reopen the lane: new theorem bypass, new observable wedge, new recovery map, or collapse of the leading lane's assumption package;
- whether the lane is memorialized as a failed live option, a theorem-bounded option, or a merely dormant hedge;
- and what language is prohibited meanwhile: “impossible,” “settled,” or “dead in principle,” unless the stronger standard has actually been met.

Without this memorial floor, retirement can silently become archive amnesia.

### 5. Exclusion-grade closure

Even after disclosure, exclusion-grade closure is earned only when:
- the scope of exclusion is stated tightly enough that the archive is not pretending a local failure is a lane-wide impossibility result,
- auxiliary-space escape routes have been checked and found either empty, ad hoc, or irrelevant to the lane's original load-bearing promise,
- the case rests on something stronger than current comparative defeat alone — ideally a bounded no-go or contradiction that reaches the lane's core commitments,
- and the archive can say exactly what remains in canon: memorial note only, theorem-bounded quarantine, or fully live reopening trigger.

A lane may deserve retirement while still failing this standard.
That is not indecision; it is scope discipline.

## Common failure modes

1. **Retirement-to-impossibility laundering** — a practical portfolio decision is retold as though a rival were now impossible in principle.
2. **Scope inflation** — one failed implementation is treated as though an entire lane were closed.
3. **Auxiliary amnesia** — the archive forgets which assumptions carried the claimed defeat.
4. **Comparative-defeat overreach** — current low rank is retold as theorem-like exclusion.
5. **Memorial erasure** — retired rivals lose even the compact notes that would make later reopening rational and cheap.

## Immediate archive rules

1. **Do not convert retirement-grade pruning directly into exclusion-grade closure.**
2. **Require explicit scope-of-exclusion disclosure before any impossibility, settled, or dead-in-principle language.**
3. **Ask whether the claimed defeat lands on a lane's core commitments or only on one implementation, truncation, or package.**
4. **Preserve a memorial floor and named reopening triggers even for retired rivals unless a stronger theorem-grade standard is met.**
5. **Keep this gate subordinate to the allocation-versus-retirement gate and upstream of any no-go or contradiction language.**
   This surface sharpens what retirement does *not* yet buy; it does not replace separate debts on recovery, package disclosure, observables, or witness closure.

## Net result

The archive now has a smaller and more honest sentence available:

> a completion bid may earn retirement-grade pruning and still be far from exclusion-grade closure once scope, auxiliary-space, no-go grade, and memorial-floor debt are faced honestly.

That blocks one more prestige inflation move without widening the archive.
