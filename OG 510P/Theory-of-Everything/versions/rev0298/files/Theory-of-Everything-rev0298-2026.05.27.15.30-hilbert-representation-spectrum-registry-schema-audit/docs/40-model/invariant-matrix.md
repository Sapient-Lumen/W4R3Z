# Invariant matrix

This is the archive's first compact pressure map across active bridge families.
It is **not** a winner declaration. It is a one-page way to see where each family currently carries explanatory weight, where it is only partial, and where it remains mostly pressure or silence.

## Legend

- `S` — strong current fit
- `P` — partial or regime-limited fit
- `R` — explicitly pressured by this invariant, but not yet a fit
- `U` — underbuilt or mostly silent

## Readout by family

### A — EFT-first gravity baseline
Best current anchor for `INV-0001`, `INV-0002`, and `INV-0004`.
Its main weakness is explanatory depth: it stabilizes low-energy recovery without yet resolving deep microstructure, horizon entropy, or record formation.

### B — Thermodynamic / entropy gravity
Strongest on `INV-0005` and interesting for `INV-0002`, but still owes a cleaner account of quantum correlations, low-energy field recovery, and record structure.
This family is most vulnerable when interpretation outruns derivation.

### C — Holography / entanglement geometry
Strongest current cross-domain bridge on `INV-0005` and `INV-0007`, with partial traction on `INV-0001`, `INV-0002`, `INV-0004`, and `INV-0008`.
Its main pressure is translation: how much survives outside especially symmetric or duality-friendly settings.

### D — Measurement / nonlocality pressure
Strongest on `INV-0006` and `INV-0008`.
This lane is essential because it keeps empirical contact honest, and it now has a first concrete gravity-induced-entanglement witness audit that separates discriminator-grade laboratory evidence from theory-independent mediator-quantumness closure.
But it is still not yet a standalone unification story.

## Consequence

The current archive should treat **A + one deeper bridge family + D** as the minimum serious comparison pattern.
Any candidate that performs well on geometry or entropy while staying weak on records and observables is incomplete.
Any candidate that handles records while staying silent on gravity or low-energy recovery is also incomplete.
