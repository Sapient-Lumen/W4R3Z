# Gravitational-environment state-structure versus quantization-closure audit

This document keeps one fresh low-energy gravity discriminator load-bearing without letting it inflate into premature quantum-gravity closure rhetoric.
It asks what the archive should count when recent open-system work says that a mesoscopic detector can distinguish a quantized graviton environment from a classical stochastic gravitational-wave background by the **structure** of the induced decoherence, even though both descriptions start from the same tidal quadratic detector coupling.

## Compression verdict

The archive should now hold one tighter sentence:

**state-selective decoherence structure should be scored as a bounded laboratory corridor for distinguishing a quantized graviton bath from a classical stochastic gravitational-wave background, not as quantization closure by itself: recent open-system work derives the same tidal quadratic detector coupling in both descriptions yet finds a protected low-phonon coherence sector at leading order for a quantized graviton environment, whereas a phase-randomized classical background decoheres even inside that subspace.** `REF-0184`

That means the archive should reject both extremes:
- "any decoherence-based gravity proposal is just raw noise rhetoric and buys nothing,"
- and "one structural decoherence discriminator already settles whether gravity is quantum."

The honest score is in between.

## What has genuinely improved

### 1. The lane now has one same-coupling quantum-versus-classical discriminator

`REF-0184` is useful because it does **not** compare unrelated detector models.
It keeps the microscopic detector-side interaction fixed as the same geodesic-deviation tidal coupling, so the claimed difference is not merely that one toy Hamiltonian decoheres and another one does not.
Within that common coupling, the difference comes from the statistical nature of the gravitational environment itself. `REF-0184`

### 2. The archive now has a structure-sensitive signal, not only a rate-sensitive one

The paper's main load-bearing gain is that the discriminator is about **which subspace stays coherent** rather than only how fast coherence decays overall.
A quantized graviton bath preserves coherence inside the lowest phonon-number manifold at leading order, while a classical phase-randomized background destroys it even there.
That is better archive material than a bare “more decoherence means more quantumness” slogan because it names a structural contrast rather than only a magnitude contrast. `REF-0184`

### 3. The lane now has one sharper mesoscopic-detector corridor

The source explicitly targets mesoscopic optomechanical or acoustic resonator regimes where direct displacement signals are too small to classicalize the device and where the detector remains genuinely quantum.
That matters for the archive because it turns a broad “gravitational waves as environment” slogan into one bounded experimental corridor with a specified detector class, protected-sector logic, and feasibility discussion. `REF-0184`

## What this does **not** buy

### 1. Not yet quantization closure

The source distinguishes one quantized graviton-environment model from one classical stochastic-background model inside a fixed detector framework.
That is real empirical-structure progress, but it is still weaker than theory-independent closure that gravity must be quantum in the strongest archive sense. `REF-0184`

### 2. Not yet graviton-state characterization

This lane is weaker than the stronger next rung already tracked in `docs/40-model/single-graviton-access-versus-quantization-closure-audit.md`.
There the archive already says that graviton counting statistics, second-order coherence, and tomography-grade state characterization would count as stronger nonclassicality credit than one dramatic detector headline alone. `REF-0176`, `REF-0184`

### 3. Not yet local witness closure

Even if the protected-subspace discriminator worked cleanly, the broader witness stack would still remain mostly unpaid: public record carriers, cross-site reproducibility, calibration lineage, and candidate-native witness transport are still separate burdens.
This audit therefore sharpens a laboratory quantumness corridor, not a later witness-package closure stage. `REF-0039`, `REF-0058`, `REF-0059`, `REF-0060`, `REF-0061`

## The archive score change

The archive should now keep one smaller distinction explicit inside the low-energy gravitational-quantumness lane:

1. **state-structure discrimination** — a detector distinguishes quantum versus classical gravitational environments by protected-subspace versus always-decohering behavior under the same microscopic coupling;
2. **discrete single-graviton access** — a detector registers graviton-scale energy exchange and begins probing interaction structure;
3. **graviton statistics / tomography credit** — detector data access coherence functions or state-reconstruction information that discriminate quantum radiation structure more sharply;
4. **local witness closure** — the lane carries the broader public record package rather than borrowing detector semantics from outside.

Current work adds stage 1 more sharply.
It does **not** yet reach stages 3 or 4, and it should not be mistaken for a shortcut around the existing witness ladder.

## False promotions to reject

1. **"Any structural decoherence distinction already proves gravity is quantum."**
   - The result is one bounded environment-discrimination corridor inside one detector regime, not a theory-independent closure theorem.

2. **"Because the effect is indirect open-system physics, it buys nothing."**
   - The same-coupling protected-subspace contrast is still real progress because it turns vague gravity-as-noise talk into a sharper operational discriminator.

3. **"This outranks graviton statistics or tomography."**
   - It does not.
   The archive should still treat state characterization and correlation-structure access as a stronger evidential rung than one protected-subspace versus decohering-subspace contrast alone.

## Net result

The archive now has a better sentence available:

> decoherence structure under a fixed tidal coupling can be real evidence for distinguishing a quantized graviton environment from classical stochastic gravitational noise, but that gain is still weaker than graviton-state characterization, local witness closure, or broad quantization closure.

That keeps the lane sharper without letting a new laboratory discriminator overclose the archive. `REF-0184`
