# Entanglement admission checklist

This document is the archive's small gate for admitting **entanglement-language claims** into canon.
It exists because entanglement is often genuinely informative, but is also one of the fastest ways for a theory archive to dissolve into prestige vocabulary.

Read this together with:
- `docs/40-model/discriminator-wedges.md`
- `docs/40-model/observer-record-minimum.md`
- `docs/40-model/candidate-bridges.md`

## Purpose

A future revision may use entanglement as a bridge quantity only if it says **which entanglement object**, **across what split**, **in what regime**, **with what observable consequence**, and **where the witnessing records live**.

The checklist is designed to sharpen both:
- `W-0002` — whether entanglement geometry earns anything beyond thermodynamic redescription,
- `W-0003` — whether record structure is explained or merely borrowed.

## Admission questions

A candidate entanglement claim must answer all of the following.

1. **Which quantity?**
   - Name the object: entanglement entropy, mutual information, relative entropy, modular Hamiltonian data, code-subspace relation, or another specific quantity.
   - “Entanglement” by itself is too coarse.

2. **Across which split?**
   - State the subsystem factorization, subregion choice, bipartition, algebra, or operational partition being used.
   - If the split is approximate, state the approximation.

3. **In which regime?**
   - State the domain of validity: vacuum or excited states, semiclassical limit, large-`N`, holographic setting, near-equilibrium setup, finite-temperature setting, laboratory many-body analog, or other bounded regime.
   - A regime-limited claim is admissible; a regime-free slogan is not.

4. **What is the claimed gain?**
   - Name exactly what the entanglement language is supposed to buy: geometric reconstruction, causal constraint, error-correcting redundancy, entropy bound saturation, or a calculable observable relation.
   - If the same result could be restated in generic thermodynamic or correlation language, the claimant must say what is uniquely gained by entanglement-specific structure.

5. **What is the observable-bearing consequence?**
   - Specify which correlators, response functions, geometric quantities, or operationally retrievable relations are supposed to change or become computable.
   - Elegant dictionary entries are not enough unless they bottom out in observable-bearing content.

6. **Where do records live?**
   - State the physical carriers of stable records relevant to the claim: boundary degrees of freedom, apparatus/environment channels, memory chains, horizon bookkeeping, or another explicit witness structure.
   - If record carriers are imported from a separate classical cut, the claim remains incomplete under `CL-0014`.

7. **What would demote the claim?**
   - Name a failure mode: dependence on a special background, collapse outside a narrow regime, inability to distinguish itself from coarse thermodynamic redescription, or inability to map formal observables into records.

## Decision states

Use the checklist to assign one of three compact states.

- **Admit**
  - all seven questions are answered with bounded but explicit content.

- **Provisional admit**
  - the quantity, split, and regime are clear, and there is a serious target consequence, but the record-carrier or observable-bearing story is still incomplete.

- **Reject / quarantine**
  - the claim uses “entanglement” as a prestige term without naming split, regime, distinctive gain, or witness structure.

### Family B — thermodynamic / entropy gravity

- **Current state:** not automatically admitted as an entanglement claim.
- **Why:** entropy language alone does not satisfy this checklist. Family B earns its own audit in `docs/40-model/thermodynamic-gravity-assumption-audit.md`.
- **Pressure:** if a B-claim relies on specifically entanglement-structured quantities, it must pass this checklist separately.

### Family D — measurement / nonlocality pressure

- **Current state:** outside the checklist's target, but load-bearing as a pressure source.
- **Why:** D is mainly about persistent records, witness agreement, and empirical closure. It becomes relevant here whenever a C-claim owes an account of re-readable evidence.

## Anti-solvent clauses

The archive will not admit the following moves:
- “spacetime is entanglement” without a named quantity and split,
- “geometry emerges from information” without saying which operationally retrievable relations are recovered,
- appeals to entanglement that merely rename thermodynamic gradients or generic correlations,
- record talk that quietly assumes a classical apparatus/environment decomposition while presenting itself as fundamental closure.
