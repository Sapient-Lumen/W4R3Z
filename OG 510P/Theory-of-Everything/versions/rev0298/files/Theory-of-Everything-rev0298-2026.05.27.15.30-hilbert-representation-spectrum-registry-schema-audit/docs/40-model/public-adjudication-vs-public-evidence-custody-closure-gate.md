# Public adjudication versus public-evidence-custody-closure gate

Purpose: split the archive's **witness-package progress** one step further.
A candidate should not earn the same witness credit merely because it can **support a bounded public challenge path with frozen replay bundles, declared decision rules, and public terminal dispute states** and because it can **place the challenged evidence bundle or reference artifact under public custody strong enough for outsiders to extract, identity-check, preserve, cite, transfer, and later reopen without depending on one privileged host's continuing goodwill**.
Those are related, but they are not the same achievement.

## Compression verdict

Inside the archive's witness package, once a lane has already moved beyond assertibility and public adjudication, the current score should now distinguish three nested layers:

1. **public adjudication closure** — outsiders can trigger a bounded challenge path, recover the committed replay bundle under declared rules, and obtain public uphold / replace / withdraw / undetermined / inconclusive outcomes;
2. **public evidence-custody closure** — the challenged evidence bundle, comparison artifact, or reference object is not merely reviewable through the host, but stably extractable, versioned, fixity-checkable, preservable, citable, and reopenable beyond one privileged repository or operator chain;
3. **local public witness closure** — a lane can say where the physical records live, what stabilizes them, how they are reread, why they remain public evidence, and why that empirical loop is not mainly outsourced.

A lane may earn real credit on layer 1 while still being weak on layer 2.
That is progress.
It is just not yet public evidence-custody closure, much less full witness closure.

## Why this gate exists

Shared witness-gate scaffolding now lives in `docs/40-model/witness-closure-gate-family-frame.md`. This document only names the pair-specific inflation move below.

**bounded public dispute rights being retold as if outsiders therefore already possessed durable public custody of the evidence-bearing artifact itself.**

That inflation can happen in at least six ways:
- a lane can let outsiders challenge a claim while all decisive materials remain view-only, rate-limited, or revocable through one host-controlled portal;
- a lane can preserve a challenge receipt while the underlying evidence bytes, calibration file, comparison report, or reference object are mutable, unlabeled, or silently replaced;
- a lane can expose a downloadable artifact once while failing to provide stable identifiers, version relations, checksums, or current certificates that would let later users know they still hold the same thing;
- a lane can name a reference material, comparison report, or standard object while leaving validity windows, storage conditions, or transfer requirements tacit, so later custody silently depends on insider memory;
- a lane can promise preservation in prose while lacking documented archival storage, disposition, or migration procedures that survive one provider, institution, or software stack;
- or a lane can support public adjudication only so long as the original host continues to answer, making the challenge surface hostage to one service endpoint rather than to the public record itself.

## The five-step public-evidence-custody test

### 1. Extractable artifact bundle rather than hosted inspection only

Before awarding custody credit, the archive should ask whether the decisive evidence object can actually leave the host.
At minimum, a lane should disclose:
- what the custody unit is: dataset, calibrated time series, comparison report, reference-material certificate, frozen model bundle, or other named artifact,
- whether challengers can retrieve that unit rather than merely inspect it through a portal,
- whether the downloadable object is the same object that governed adjudication rather than a nearby derivative,
- and which parts of the evidential object still remain trapped in private dashboards, undisclosed storage tiers, or non-exportable operator state.

A lane that wins only here earns **public adjudication credit**, not yet public evidence-custody closure.

### 2. Stable identity, version, and fixity

A lane earns more than adjudication credit only when outsiders can tell whether they still possess the same public evidence object.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what stable identifier, accession, DOI, or comparable identity attaches to the custody unit,
- what checksum, signature, certificate, or equivalent fixity route exists,
- how later updates become legible as new versions, amended certificates, or superseding reports rather than silent mutation,
- and whether version relations are public enough that a later user can reconstruct which object stood behind the original public claim.

Without that route, apparent custody may still be host-mediated inspection with better receipts.

### 3. Documented preservation and transfer path

A lane earns stronger custody credit only when the public evidence object has a declared route through time and across storage contexts.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what repository, archive, or storage authority assumes responsibility for the custody unit,
- what documented storage, migration, or preservation plan governs it,
- whether export, replication, or transfer to another repository is allowed without identity loss,
- and whether end users receive enough metadata to evaluate quality, provenance, and preservation risk for themselves.

Without those answers, apparent custody may still be temporary hosting rather than durable public possession.

### 4. Reference-object continuity and validity conditions

A lane earns still stronger custody credit only when reference objects remain usable outside the originating institution.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what current certificate, comparison report, validity window, or traceability statement governs the reference artifact,
- what storage or handling conditions must remain true for the artifact to keep its evidential role,
- how successor artifacts, renewed lots, or superseding reports are linked to the prior one,
- and whether a later outsider can tell that the reference object in hand is still the right public standard for reopening the claim.

Without that route, apparent custody may still be symbolic access to a standard that only insiders can actually keep alive.

### 5. Reopen path beyond one privileged host

A lane earns **public evidence-custody closure** credit only when later users can reopen the evidential question without depending on the originating host's continued special status.
At minimum, it should name:
- the extractable custody unit,
- its stable identity, version, and fixity route,
- the documented preservation and transfer path,
- the current validity / traceability conditions for any reference artifacts,
- and the remaining debt to full local record-carrier, rereadability, and candidate-native objectivity closure.

This is still not the same thing as full local witness closure.
A lane may preserve public evidence custody very well while still borrowing the actual record carrier, stabilization story, or rereadability semantics from ordinary laboratory witness structure.

## Common failure modes

1. **adjudication-to-custody inflation** — a bounded challenge route is retold as if outsiders therefore already possess the evidence object itself.
2. **host-locked replay** — challengers may rerun through the original service but cannot extract the decisive artifact bundle.
3. **mutable artifact shadow** — the downloadable object can change without a new identifier, version edge, or supersession notice.
4. **pointer-not-object rhetoric** — a URL or dashboard pointer is treated as custody even though the evidence-bearing bytes or reference artifacts are not actually in public hands.
5. **certificate drift** — reference materials or reports are cited without checking whether the current certificate, validity window, or renewal issue still matches the claim.
6. **preservation theater** — public artifacts exist today but have no documented migration, transfer, or repository-of-record path through time.

## Compact current readout

- **Current trustworthy-repository practice** makes the stronger burden explicit by treating repository trustworthiness as a matter not only of access, but of characteristics required for digital data and metadata stewardship, with extended guidance used to support objective review.
- **Current PID / DOI versioning practice** makes the identity burden explicit by recommending new DOIs for major changes, explicit version metadata for minor changes, and public version links between canonical and specific releases rather than silent mutation.
- **Current metrology practice at NIST** makes the custody burden concrete by requiring users of reference materials to ensure that the certificate or report they hold is current, rather than assuming that the named material alone carries the evidential role forever.
- **Current BIPM metrology infrastructure** makes the public side concrete by assuring public access to peer-reviewed comparison and calibration-and-measurement data in the KCDB rather than treating comparison standing as private institutional memory.
- **Current open-science data practice in gravitational-wave astronomy** shows what partial payment looks like through downloadable calibrated time series, supporting metadata, auxiliary channels, and analysis products that enable later public reanalysis rather than one-host-only inspection.

That does not refute current observer-relative or bridge-theory programs.
It just means they have not yet paid this stronger public-custody debt.

## Net rule

Within the archive:
- public adjudication closure earns real witness-package credit;
- but it does **not** yet count as public evidence-custody closure;
- and neither of those automatically counts as local public witness closure.

Any future completion bid or bridge-family claim that wants stronger witness credit should now say not only how outsiders can challenge and publicly revise a claim, but what evidence-bearing artifact they can actually take possession of, how they know it is still the same artifact, how it survives version changes and storage transfer, and how it can be reopened without depending on one privileged host's continued control.
