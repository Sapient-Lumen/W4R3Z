# Single-graviton access versus quantization-closure audit

This document keeps one fresh low-energy gravity gain load-bearing without letting it inflate into premature quantum-gravity closure rhetoric.
It asks what the archive should count when recent single-graviton detector proposals and follow-on counting-statistics work are advertised either as the first empirical corridor into linearized quantum gravity or as if they already settled the quantization question outright.

## Compression verdict

The archive should now hold one tighter sentence:

**stimulated single-graviton access should be scored as a bounded empirical corridor into linearized quantum gravity, not as quantization closure by itself: recent detector work argues that near-future quantum resonators could register stimulated absorption of single gravitons from classically measured gravitational waves and could test several basic single-graviton interaction properties, while newer graviton-counting work shows that stronger nonclassicality credit would come from quantum statistics, second-order correlations, and tomography rather than from one single-click event alone.** `REF-0175`, `REF-0176`

That means the archive should reject both extremes:
- "single-graviton detection already settles whether gravity is quantum,"
- and "because stimulated processes have semiclassical analogies, the whole lane is evidentially trivial."

The honest score is in between.

## What has genuinely improved

### 1. The archive now has a concrete low-energy graviton-access corridor

`REF-0175` argues that a massive quantum resonator, cross-correlated with independently detected gravitational waves, could in principle register stimulated absorption of a single graviton and thereby move the archive from generic graviton-detectability pessimism toward a bounded laboratory access corridor.
That is a real empirical-contact gain because it ties low-energy quantum-gravity talk to a concrete detector architecture, a concrete source class, and a concrete transition signal rather than to purely remote thought-experiment rhetoric. `REF-0175`

### 2. The lane now has a cleaner split between single-quanta access and stronger quantization evidence

The same source is explicit that stimulated single-quantum transitions are historically important without being the final benchmark for field quantization.
Its own proposed tests therefore aim not merely at a click/no-click event, but at probing energy quantization, universality, Einstein-coefficient structure, spin-2 coupling, and momentum transfer.
In archive terms, that means the right score is not "one graviton click equals closure," but "one graviton-click corridor makes stronger tests experimentally legible." `REF-0175`

### 3. The archive now has a sharper marker for what later promotion would require

`REF-0176` tightens the next rung.
It argues that graviton detection probabilities can distinguish coherent, squeezed, and thermal Gaussian radiation, that second-order correlation data can be transferred directly to the detector, and that phase-sensitive schemes could support tomography of Gaussian gravitational-wave states.
For the archive, this is valuable because it turns vague future-talk about "stronger evidence" into a more explicit promotion rule: quantum statistics and state-characterization claims are stronger than single stimulated absorption alone. `REF-0176`

## What this does **not** buy

### 1. Not yet quantization closure by itself

A stimulated single-graviton event is weaker than a full demonstration that the gravitational field must be quantum in the strongest archive sense.
`REF-0175` itself frames stimulated processes as historically suggestive but not by themselves definitive, and points toward additional tests rather than one-step closure. `REF-0175`

### 2. Not yet local witness closure

Even a clean graviton-click detector still leaves the broader witness stack mostly unpaid:
public record carriers, calibration lineage, cross-site reproducibility, intervention-grade control, and candidate-native witness transport remain separate burdens.
This revision therefore sharpens an empirical-access corridor, not a later witness-closure stage. `REF-0039`, `REF-0058`, `REF-0059`, `REF-0060`, `REF-0061`

### 3. Not yet broad ToE credit

A linearized-gravity graviton-access result would still be only one bounded low-energy witness gain.
It would not by itself settle UV completion, cosmological package debt, observed-sector fixation, or final theory ranking.
The archive should therefore keep this lane inside family-D / empirical-contact scoring rather than letting it silently overpay the local-law or broad-ToE books. `REF-0175`, `REF-0176`

## The archive score change

The archive should now keep one smaller distinction explicit inside the low-energy graviton lane:

1. **stimulated single-graviton access** — a detector can register discrete graviton-scale energy exchange in a gravity-sensitive setup;
2. **interaction-structure testing** — the lane can probe universality, absorption-versus-emission structure, spin, or momentum-transfer features of the exchanged quantum;
3. **quantum-statistics / tomography credit** — the lane can access graviton counting statistics, second-order coherence, or state reconstruction that discriminate genuinely quantum radiation structure more sharply;
4. **local witness closure** — the lane can stabilize the full public witness package rather than borrowing ordinary laboratory semantics and calibration stacks from outside.

Current work makes stage 1 look substantially less speculative and sketches routes into stages 2 and 3.
It does **not** yet reach stage 4.

## False promotions to reject

1. **"A single graviton click already proves quantization."**
   - Stimulated single-quantum exchange is a real gain, but the current papers themselves treat stronger statistical or structural tests as the more demanding evidence tier.

2. **"Because semiclassical analogies exist, the lane buys nothing."**
   - A concrete graviton-access corridor still matters because it turns a long-range impossibility story into an experimentally structured ladder.

3. **"One low-energy graviton detector would already close broad ToE competition."**
   - The lane is witness-side and linearized-gravity-side; it does not erase the archive's separate completion, cosmology, or candidate-native witness debts.

## What would move the archive further

A later revision should score this lane as materially stronger only if one compact package adds most of the following together:
1. detector-level confirmation of stimulated single-graviton access in a gravity-specific setup,
2. quantitative tests among alternative interaction rules such as universality, Einstein-coefficient structure, spin, or momentum transfer,
3. direct access to graviton statistics or tomography-grade state information,
4. cleaner separation between merely classical-wave-correlated events and genuinely quantum-radiation signatures,
5. and a route from detector success to broader witness-package reduction rather than a single prestige event.

Without that package, the archive should preserve the gain as a bounded **single-graviton access versus quantization-closure audit** and stop there.

## Net result

The archive now has a better low-energy graviton sentence available:

> stimulated single-graviton access would be real empirical progress on linearized quantum gravity, but stronger quantum-statistics or tomography evidence is still needed before the lane should be over-scored as quantization closure.

That keeps the lane live and sharper without letting one dramatic event headline overclose the archive. `REF-0175`, `REF-0176`
