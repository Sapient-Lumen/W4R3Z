# Ranking-versus-allocation gate

Purpose: split the **ranking-grade** layer inside `docs/40-model/attribution-vs-ranking-gate.md` one step further.
A completion bid should not earn the same archive-steering credit merely because some wedge would **materially and robustly improve the lane's comparative ranking** and because that same improvement would **justify concentrating scarce research attention, shrinking rival exploration, or pruning alternative lanes without losing too much option value**.
Shared family scaffolding and ordered routing live in `docs/40-model/completion-bid-comparison-gate-family-frame.md` and `docs/40-model/completion-bid-comparison-stack.md`; this file keeps only the pair-specific split between ranking-grade movement and allocation-grade steering.

## Compression verdict

The archive should now score claimed comparative progress in **three post-ranking layers** after a wedge already survives attribution and ranking stress:

1. **ranking-grade comparative movement** — the lane improves its standing across a named rival set under nearby reasonable prior-width and complexity choices;
2. **allocation-basis disclosure** — the lane states what research resource is being reallocated, what rival exploration slack is being reduced, what remaining value-of-information or misspecification risk is still carried, and what horizon the steering claim is about;
3. **allocation-grade steering** — the same broad movement survives strongly enough that the archive should actually tilt scarce effort toward that lane rather than merely updating its current ordering while preserving exploration reserve elsewhere.

A lane may earn real ranking-grade movement while still being weak on layers 2 and 3.
That is still progress.
It is just not yet allocation-grade steering.

## Why this gate exists

`docs/40-model/attribution-vs-ranking-gate.md` already blocked one inflation move:
**attribution-grade support pretending to be an archive-moving comparative verdict.**

A further inflation move remained too easy:
**ranking-grade movement pretending to settle how the archive should spend scarce attention.**

That confusion appears whenever a completion bid is praised because it:
- moves upward in the current ordering and is then treated as if rival exploration can be safely collapsed,
- earns one better comparative score while the option value of unresolved wedges in other lanes is left uncounted,
- uses ranking movement to justify concentration even though acquisition, attribution, or prior-width debt remains fragile enough that misspecification could still reorder the field,
- or ignores that research steering is a sequential decision under bounded budget, where preserving some exploration diversity can still be rational even after a current ranking update.

A lane can rise meaningfully and still not deserve monopoly attention.
This gate keeps **verdict movement** separate from **resource concentration**.

## The three-step allocation test

### 1. Allocation basis disclosure

Before counting ranking movement as steering guidance, the archive should ask:
- what resource is actually being moved: reading time, modeling effort, formal development, phenomenology effort, or discriminator design;
- whether the claim concerns a mild tilt, a strong concentration, or effective pruning of rival lanes;
- what decision horizon is being used: next-pass archive effort, medium-run workstream weight, or near-closure posture;
- and which unresolved debts remain outside the currently favorable wedge.

A lane that cannot name this basis may still have improved its comparative rank.
It does not yet have allocation-grade support.

### 2. Exploration-reserve and option-value honesty

A ranking gain earns more than verdict credit only if the archive is told what would be lost by concentrating too early.
At minimum, the lane should disclose:
- whether nearby rival lanes still carry unresolved high-value wedges or cheaper discriminator opportunities,
- whether the leading lane's gain depends on fragile package, prior, or attribution choices that could still move,
- whether misspecification risk makes concentrated effort unusually brittle,
- and whether a diversified next step would buy more information per unit effort than simple concentration.

The archive should not demand one exact value-of-information calculation.
It should demand enough honesty to tell the difference between **a lane currently ranked first** and **a lane that already justifies shrinking exploration reserve elsewhere**.

### 3. Allocation-grade steering

Even after ranking movement and disclosure, the archive should still ask whether effort should actually move.
A wedge earns **allocation-grade steering** credit only when:
- the comparative gain survives nearby reasonable misspecification and basis changes strongly enough to matter for near-term portfolio choice,
- the remaining option value in rival lanes is small enough, or slow enough, that preserving broad exploration is no longer the better buy,
- and the lane can say what steering follows: light tilt, strong concentration, or no more than continued broad watchfulness.

A wedge may justify updated belief without justifying aggressive concentration.
That is a decision fact, not a demotion.

## Common failure modes

1. **Rank-to-budget laundering** — a lane improves in the ordering and is immediately treated as deserving most scarce attention.
2. **Exploration amnesia** — rival lanes still carry cheap unresolved wedges or hedge value, but that option value is ignored.
3. **Misspecification blindness** — a rank move built on fragile acquisition, attribution, or prior assumptions is treated as if portfolio concentration were robust.
4. **Horizon smuggling** — short-run reading or synthesis triage is quietly retold as long-run pruning guidance.
5. **Monopoly rhetoric** — “best current lane” is retold as “only serious lane worth working on.”

## Immediate archive rules

1. **Do not convert ranking-grade movement directly into portfolio concentration.**
2. **Require explicit mention of the resource being reallocated and the rival exploration slack being reduced.**
3. **Ask what option value would be destroyed by concentrating early, especially under acquisition, attribution, or misspecification fragility.**
4. **Allow a lane to be first-ranked yet still justify only a mild tilt rather than a strong concentration.**
5. **Keep this gate subordinate to the attribution-versus-ranking gate and upstream of the allocation-versus-retirement gate.**
   This surface sharpens decision posture after ranking movement and before any rival-retirement logic; it does not replace separate debts on recovery, observables, package disclosure, or witness closure.

## Net result

The archive now has a smaller and more honest sentence available:

> a completion bid may earn ranking-grade comparative movement and still be too early for allocation-grade concentration once exploration reserve, option value, and misspecification risk are faced honestly.

That sentence blocks another prestige inflation move without widening the archive.
