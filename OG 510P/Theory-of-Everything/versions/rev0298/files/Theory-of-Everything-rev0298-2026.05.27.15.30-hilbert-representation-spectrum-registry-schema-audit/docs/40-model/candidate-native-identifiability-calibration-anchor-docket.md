## Compression verdict

The candidate-native identifiability stack can now describe a route, score fields, prevent route-level compensation, classify new evidence, retire stale credit, propagate dependencies, adjudicate conflicts, trace decisions, replay or roll back traces, answer and close challenges, and test positive route success against hostile controls.
That still leaves a final inflation path: **score drift**.

A live lane can avoid an obvious overclaim while still changing the denominator behind a familiar score label:

- a family-C package calls a bounded boundary / simulator / dictionary route `S3`;
- a lab route calls a named discriminator class `S3`;
- a completion bid calls an internally rich but publicly unacquired target corridor `S2`;
- an observer / frame route calls a same-fact transport improvement `S2`;
- a cosmological proposal calls a burden-reduction story local-law progress.

Those labels may all be honest locally, but they are not interchangeable unless the archive states the target grain, record denominator, route width, control difficulty, publicness condition, and witness-owner boundary.
Without that normalization, a later summary can compare unlike scores as if they were one shared ladder.

The correct calibration posture is:

**before a route score, readiness row, high-state reuse, adversarial-control pass, or live-lane comparison is allowed to support broad candidate-native identifiability posture, the archive must name the calibration anchors that make the score comparable or else carry an explicit non-comparable / bounded label.**

This docket is deliberately narrower than the promotion gate.
The promotion gate asks whether a route may move upward.
The calibration docket asks whether the number or state being carried forward means the same thing as another row's number or state.

Use it when:
- a lane claims or reuses `S2`, `S3`, candidate-`S4`, `S4`, or `S5` language;
- the promotion-readiness matrix is updated;
- a field-local result is compared to a route-level result;
- a lab / simulation discriminator is compared to a completion bid or family-C route;
- a hostile-control pass is reused as cross-lane support;
- a summary surface says one lane is stronger, weaker, closer, or more ready than another;
- a new revision changes target grain, record class, public bridge, decoy set, abstention boundary, or witness-owner boundary without otherwise changing the route score.

Do not use it for prose-only repairs, bibliography trimming, or source-sieve edits that do not carry route score or readiness posture.

## Calibration state codes

These codes are not evidence-intake states, supersession states, propagation states, adjudication states, trace states, replay states, challenge states, closure states, adversarial-control states, or promotion states.
They say whether a route score is calibrated for reuse or comparison.

| Code | Calibration state | Meaning | Required archive action | Blocked overclaim |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `K0` | no calibration required | The edit does not reuse, compare, promote, demote, or summarize route-score / readiness posture. | Leave the score surface unchanged; do not create fake calibration rows. | Turning every local note into a score-normalization exercise. |
| `K1` | calibration anchor missing | A score, readiness row, promotion attempt, or comparison is being reused without target grain, record denominator, control difficulty, and publicness anchors. | Bar broad comparison or high-state reuse until anchors are supplied. | Treating `S3` or `candidate-S4` as self-explanatory. |
| `K2` | wrong calibration owner | The proposed anchor belongs to witness-package, local-law, cosmological-package, method-quality, or source-sieve credit rather than candidate-native identifiability. | Retag to the correct owner and preserve the identifiability cap. | Letting non-identifiability progress recalibrate an identifiability score. |
| `K3` | target-grain mismatch | The score uses a narrower or broader candidate quotient than the comparison row or summary implies. | Narrow the score label, split the row, or mark the comparison non-comparable. | Comparing a named discriminator class to a whole candidate theory. |
| `K4` | record-denominator mismatch | The score swaps formal, simulated, boundary, lab, public, or candidate-native record classes without declaring the denominator change. | Normalize the record class or reroute through acquisition / public-bridge protocols. | Counting a formal observable like an acquired public record. |
| `K5` | control-difficulty mismatch | The score survived easier decoys, fewer hard negatives, friendlier priors, or weaker public reruns than the comparison row. | Carry the weaker-control label or rerun adversarial controls before reuse. | Treating a friendly-package pass as equal to a hostile-control pass. |
| `K6` | public / witness boundary mismatch | The score borrows publicness, custody, replay, objectivity, or witness-package credit from another owner. | Witness-separate, public-bridge-condition, or cap the route state. | Letting ordinary public evidence or witness progress recalibrate route identifiability. |
| `K7` | calibrated bounded reuse | The score is comparable only after its target grain, record denominator, control difficulty, publicness condition, and witness boundary are carried with it. | Reuse the score with the bounded label and residual cap; do not erase the qualifier in broad mirrors. | Forgetting that calibrated comparability was conditional and bounded. |
| `K8` | calibration failure / freeze | Anchors conflict, cannot be replayed, are missing after challenge, or show that the compared rows are not on one ladder. | Freeze broad comparison, roll back reused score, split rows, or route through conflict / challenge closure. | Ranking unlike score labels as if they measured one route-state quantity. |

## Calibration anchors

### 1. Target-grain anchor

A score must say what size object it is scoring.
`S3` for a named discriminator class is not `S3` for a whole completion bid.
`S3` for a family-C bounded boundary / dictionary / simulator package is not `S3` for generic candidate-native geometry identification.
`S2` for an internally coherent target corridor is not `S2` for a route with acquired public records.

Default failure: `K3`.
Default repair: split the row or carry the target-grain qualifier into every reuse.

### 2. Record-denominator anchor

A score must say what kind of record pays for it.
Formal observables, boundary dictionary outputs, simulation artifacts, lab records, public custody objects, independently generated evidence, and candidate-native records are different denominators.
A later summary may not compare them as one route-state quantity unless the record class is normalized or the mismatch is visible.

Default failure: `K4`.
Default repair: reroute to acquisition-realizability and public-bridge protocols, then relabel the row.

### 3. Route-width anchor

A score must say whether it covers one example, one benchmark ecology, one regime, one package, one quotient class, one candidate family, or a generic target class.
Narrow route success may be real while still failing generic posture.

Default failure: `K3` or `K5` depending on whether the mismatch is target width or control difficulty.
Default repair: carry the bounded scope into the normalized label.

### 4. Control-difficulty anchor

A score must say what it survived.
A route that passes friendly decoys, handpicked hard cases, one simulator ecology, or one public replay does not have the same calibration as a route that passes hostile target decoys, acquisition-spoof checks, prior-leakage checks, margin perturbations, abstention hard negatives, public-bridge fragility controls, and witness-borrowing substitution tests.

Default failure: `K5`.
Default repair: rerun the adversarial-control battery or mark the score weaker-control / not comparable.

### 5. Publicness anchor

A score must say whether publicness is internal, published, hosted, custody-preserved, independently replayable, challengeable, reconditioned after challenge, or witness-package dependent.
A row may be calibrated for internal competence without being calibrated for public identifiability.

Default failure: `K6`.
Default repair: carry the public condition or witness-separate the support.

### 6. Owner-boundary anchor

A score must say which owner surface gets the credit.
Candidate-native identifiability credit is not recalibrated upward because witness-package, local-law, cosmological-package, detector, simulation, or source-quality credit improved.
Those gains can matter, but they must keep their owner labels.

Default failure: `K2` or `K6`.
Default repair: retag to the correct owner and preserve the route cap.

## Current live-lane calibration

### Family C

Family C remains the strongest bounded `S3` partial-identification row only under a package-grain calibration.
Its present credit is not calibrated as generic candidate-native geometry identification, because the record denominator still includes boundary / dictionary / simulator / code-subspace / public-replay package assumptions and the public bridge remains conditional.
Normalized label: **bounded package-grain `S3`, record-denominator mixed / borrowed-public, control difficulty incomplete**.

### Completion bids

Completion bids remain target-rich but record-poor.
Their strongest rows should not be compared directly to family-C `S3` rows unless the denominator says formal target-side corridor rather than acquired route.
Normalized label: **mostly `S1`/`S2` target-grain credit, no acquired public route denominator unless a specific record route is supplied**.

### Laboratory and simulation routes

Lab and simulation routes may be record-richer than completion bids, but the target grain is often an externally chosen contrast, effective-model discriminator, emulator behavior, or named benchmark class.
Their `S3` pockets are calibrated only to those named discriminator classes.
Normalized label: **record-richer `S2`, named-class `S3` only when the target quotient is explicitly bounded**.

### Witness-side frame and observer routes

Frame / observer / asymptotic routes can calibrate equivalence and public-transport pressure, but they should not be ranked as candidate-native identifiability routes unless acquisition, inverse, stability, abstention, and public bridge are also present for a target quotient.
Normalized label: **equivalence / public-transport discipline below route closure**.

### Cosmological proposal classes

Vacuum-energy, measure, population, anthropic, relaxation, and global-selection rows usually live under local-law / cosmological-package / witness-package owner splits.
They are not calibrated as candidate-native identifiability unless they supply a local target-to-record route.
Normalized label: **burden-accounting or proposal-class credit, not route-state credit, unless a local record-bearing quotient is declared**.

## Minimal update policy

- If the result is `K0`, do not change route posture.
- If the result is `K1`, do not reuse or compare the score until anchors are supplied.
- If the result is `K2`, retag to the correct owner and preserve the identifiability cap.
- If the result is `K3`, split or relabel the target grain before comparing.
- If the result is `K4`, normalize the record denominator or route through acquisition / public bridge.
- If the result is `K5`, rerun hostile controls or carry the weaker-control label.
- If the result is `K6`, witness-separate or public-condition the score.
- If the result is `K7`, reuse only with the bounded normalized label.
- If the result is `K8`, freeze broad comparison and route through conflict, replay / rollback, challenge response, or challenge closure before reuse.
