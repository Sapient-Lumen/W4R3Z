# Matter-sector recovery gate

Purpose: split the **recovery axis** inside `docs/40-model/completion-bid-cashout-sieve.md`.
A completion bid should not earn the same recovery credit merely because it can point to **some** low-energy or semiclassical regime and because it can recover something close to the **observed matter sector**.
Shared family scaffolding and ordered routing live in `docs/40-model/completion-bid-comparison-gate-family-frame.md` and `docs/40-model/completion-bid-comparison-stack.md`; this file keeps only the pair-specific split between regime recovery and observed-sector recovery.

## Compression verdict

The archive should now score recovery in **two layers**:

1. **regime recovery** — a candidate shows that local QFT and semiclassical / classical gravity reappear in some controlled domain;
2. **observed-sector recovery** — a candidate shows how the actual gauge / chiral / generation structure of the observed matter sector is recovered, or at least sharply constrained, without hiding that result inside a hand-picked package.

A lane may earn real credit on layer 1 while remaining weak on layer 2.
That is still progress.
It is just not the same progress.

## Why this gate exists

The atlas and the cash-out sieve already blocked one common inflation move:
**UV ambition pretending to be integrated closure.**

A second inflation move remained too easy:
**generic recovery pretending to be observed-sector recovery.**

That confusion shows up whenever a completion bid is praised because it:
- has some semiclassical limit,
- can produce gauge theories in broad outline,
- or can build many model classes,

and those real gains are then narrated as if the observed matter architecture had already been recovered.

This gate keeps those credits separate.

## The two-layer recovery test

### Layer 1 — regime recovery

A lane earns **regime recovery** credit when it can state, in a controlled way:
- how ordinary local QFT behavior reappears,
- how semiclassical or classical geometry reappears,
- what approximation, truncation, compactification, coarse-graining, or control window is doing the work,
- and where that recovery is known to fail or remain unproved.

This is necessary.
But it is not yet observed-sector closure.

### Layer 2 — observed-sector recovery

A lane earns **observed-sector recovery** credit only when it says something specific about the actual matter architecture we observe.
At minimum, the archive should ask for three separate sub-questions.

#### 2a. Architecture
Can the lane recover or sharply constrain:
- the gauge-sector structure,
- chirality,
- family / generation structure,
- anomaly-safe matter assignment,
- and the broad low-energy field content it is claiming to explain?

#### 2b. Pattern
Can it say something nontrivial about:
- symmetry breaking,
- hierarchies,
- mixing / coupling structure,
- neutrino / dark-sector extensions if claimed,
- or why the allowed low-energy possibilities are not arbitrarily broad?

#### 2c. Portability
Does the gain survive beyond one especially selected package?
The archive should ask whether the matter result is:
- generic,
- robust across a family of admissible packages,
- or still heavily tied to a special compactification, truncation, asymptotic choice, or auxiliary construction.

A lane strong on architecture but weak on pattern or portability still earns **partial observed-sector credit**, not full closure.
If a lane is strong on architecture and partial pattern but still weak on actual observed-parameter fixation, route the remaining question through `docs/40-model/parameter-fixation-gate.md` rather than silently scoring it as finished.

## Common failure modes

1. **Semiclassical inflation** — a controlled low-energy limit is mistaken for recovery of the observed matter sector.
2. **Model-class inflation** — the existence of many candidate constructions is mistaken for a portable explanation of the one observed sector.
3. **Constraint inflation** — sharper consistency or fixed-point constraints are mistaken for a realized matter architecture.
4. **Extension inflation** — enlarging the formalism to higher dimensions, supersymmetry, or extra sectors is mistaken for ordinary matter-sector closure.

## Immediate archive rules

1. **Do not let "some low-energy limit exists" count as observed-sector closure.**
2. **Do not let a family of model constructions count as portable recovery without package disclosure.**
3. **Do not let predictive restriction count as realized matter architecture unless the low-energy target is named explicitly.**
4. **Score architecture, pattern, and portability separately before calling the matter sector "recovered."**
5. **Route any claim about actual observed couplings, hierarchies, or mixings through `docs/40-model/parameter-fixation-gate.md`.**
6. **Keep this gate subordinate to the main completion-bid sieve.**
   This surface sharpens axis 2; it does not replace the separate debts on package disclosure, observables, or wedge thickness.

## Net result

The archive now has a smaller and more honest sentence available:

> a completion bid may be strong on regime recovery while still being weak on observed-sector recovery.

That sentence is enough to block a large amount of framework inflation without widening the archive.
