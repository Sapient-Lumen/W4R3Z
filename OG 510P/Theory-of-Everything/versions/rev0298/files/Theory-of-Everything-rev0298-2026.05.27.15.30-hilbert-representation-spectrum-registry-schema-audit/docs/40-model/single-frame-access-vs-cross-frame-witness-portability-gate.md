# Single-frame-access versus cross-frame-witness-portability gate

Purpose: split the archive's **observer-relative witness progress** one step further.
A candidate should not earn the same witness credit merely because it offers **one successful observer- or frame-relative access construction** and because it supports **cross-frame, publicly portable witness structure**.
Those are related, but they are not the same achievement.

## Compression verdict

Inside the archive's witness package, the current score should now distinguish three nested layers once a lane has already moved beyond pure asymptotic access:

1. **single-frame observer-relative access** — a lane can define or compute quantities relative to one observer, one rod/clock package, one boundary-frame convention, or one perspective choice;
2. **cross-frame witness portability** — a lane can say how the *same coarse-grained fact* is transported, reidentified, and kept agreement-capable across nearby physical frame or observer changes, without a silent external referee;
3. **local public witness closure** — a lane can say where the physical records live, what stabilizes them, how they are reread, and why agreement is not confined to one privileged perspective.

A lane may earn real credit on layer 1 and even make bounded progress on layer 2 while still remaining weak on layer 3.
That is progress.
It is just not yet public witness closure.

## Why this gate exists

Shared witness-gate scaffolding now lives in `docs/40-model/witness-closure-gate-family-frame.md`.

The archive already had:
- a minimum record standard,
- a witness-borrowing ladder,
- a three-book broad-credit split,
- and a witness-package subgate separating asymptotic / observer-relative access from local witness closure.

What it still lacked was one compact warning against a narrower inflation move:

**one successful frame-relative or observer-relative construction being retold as if public witness portability were already solved.**

That inflation can happen in at least four ways:
- one privileged observer description is retold as if it already carried intersubjective agreement,
- one quantum-reference-frame transformation is retold as if the same fact had already been stably reidentified across ordinary physical frame changes,
- coarse-graining or resolution dependence is ignored even though observer agreement can fail when the description is changed,
- or hidden synchronization, calibration, or thermodynamic subsidy is doing the real work while the theory only names the observable itself.

This gate keeps those credits separate.

## The four-step portability test

### 1. Privileged-frame disclosure

Before awarding portability credit, the archive should ask which physical frame package is doing the work.
At minimum, a lane should disclose:
- which observer, clock, rod, boundary frame, detector package, or perspective choice is privileged,
- whether the claim works only after one special gauge-fixing / perspective-taking step,
- and which parts of the evidential loop still depend on that privileged setup.

A lane that wins only here earns **single-frame access credit**, not yet portability.

### 2. Cross-frame transport map

A lane earns more than single-frame access only when it can say how the same coarse-grained fact is tracked across nearby physical frame changes.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what counts as the same fact before and after a frame change,
- what the translation or switching map is,
- what gets preserved and what becomes frame-indexed,
- and whether the transport works only in one toy sector or across a broader physically relevant class.

Without that map, the lane still owes major portability debt.

### 3. Calibration and finite-resource disclosure

A lane earns stronger portability credit only when it discloses the physical price of making the comparison.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what synchronization, calibration, or side-information the observers need,
- whether agreement survives only at one chosen resolution or coarse-graining,
- what finite-resource or thermodynamic limits constrain the comparison,
- and whether an external laboratory convention is still quietly supplying the public standard.

Without those answers, apparent agreement may be a calibration artifact rather than candidate-native witness structure.

### 4. Public-portability test

A lane earns **cross-frame witness-portability** credit only when it can say how multiple physical observers or frame choices can still recover and agree on the same coarse-grained fact without one silent external referee.
At minimum, it should name:
- the fact-identity condition,
- the translation / reidentification route,
- the agreement condition,
- the resolution or coarse-graining floor,
- and the remaining debt to full record carrier / stabilization / rereadability closure.

This is still not the same thing as full local witness closure.
But it is stronger than one successful privileged description.

## Common failure modes

1. **Privileged-frame laundering** — one observer package is retold as if it had already become generic public evidence.
2. **Switch-map silence** — a frame switch exists in principle, but the archive is not told what invariant fact survives the switch.
3. **Calibration subsidy** — hidden synchronization, coarse-graining choice, or laboratory convention supplies the apparent agreement.
4. **Resolution fragility** — apparent objectivity holds only at one measurement resolution, while coarser or translated descriptions lose agreement.
5. **Portability-to-closure inflation** — cross-frame agreement is retold as if record carrier, stabilization, and rereadability had already been solved.

## Compact current readout

- **Quantum-reference-frame and relational-dynamics work** is real progress because it makes frame changes and relational observables explicit rather than hiding them in fixed external coordinates.
  Recent frame-conditioned Hamiltonian work sharpens that credit one step further: one chosen frame package can now support an exact relational evolution rule derived directly from quantum constraints and frame conditions, so the archive no longer has to score every dynamical reference-frame claim as mere rhetoric.
  But that is still weaker than ordinary public evidence or a transported multi-observer fact. `REF-0120`, `REF-0179`
- **New observer-dependent-entropy invariant work** sharpens that progress one step further: some ideal-frame entropy quantities are now invariant across frame changes and non-ideal disagreement can be quantitatively bounded, so the archive no longer has to score every frame-relative entropy claim as mere one-frame rhetoric.
  But even that gain is still weaker than a transported public fact with declared calibration, agreement, and rereadability conditions. `REF-0161`
- **Closed-universe and observer-relative quantum-gravity constructions** are real progress because they show that nontrivial local physics can survive away from pure asymptotic control.
  But they still do not by themselves tell the archive how the same coarse-grained fact is transported and agreed across nearby physical observer packages.
- **A new intersubjectivity coarse-graining audit** sharpens the target: public observable status is stronger than one-description or one-resolution agreement, because complete intersubjectivity is a coarse-graining-stable criterion rather than a one-setting success condition.
- **The same audit** also sharpens the price: ideal intersubjectivity is finite-resource impossible, so any portability or public-witness claim should disclose calibration, cooling, and coarse-graining subsidy rather than hiding that debt behind the word “observable.” `REF-0064`, `REF-0065`
- **A new geometric-observable noncommutativity audit** sharpens the same boundary from the other side: even in Minkowski spacetime, observer-indexed geometric observables attached to different Lorentz frames can fail to Poisson commute, so one frame's sharp geometric fact is not automatically a simultaneously sharp public cross-frame fact. `REF-0182`
- **A new perspectival entanglement-transference audit** sharpens the same lane from inside the resource bookkeeping package: one chosen perspective can now come with an explicit global↔perspectival map in which entanglement loss is rebooked as coherence gain for simple sectors, but that is still weaker than a public same-fact transport rule with declared calibration and agreement conditions. `REF-0187`

## Immediate archive rules

1. **Do not count one privileged observer/frame construction as public witness portability.**
2. **Require an explicit cross-frame transport / reidentification map before promoting single-frame access to portability credit.**
3. **Require calibration, coarse-graining, and finite-resource disclosure before promoting portability claims to public-witness rhetoric.**
4. **Do not treat a Lorentz transformation law or same-theory frame change as free simultaneity of sharp geometric facts when the relevant observer-indexed observables are non-commuting.**
5. **Do not count cross-frame portability itself as full local witness closure unless record carrier, stabilization, rereadability, and objectivity are still answered in the candidate's own language.**

## Net result

The archive now has a smaller and more honest sentence available:

> a lane may earn one-frame observer-relative access while still owing both same-fact transport and simultaneous sharp cross-frame comparability, and it may clear some of that burden without coming close to public witness portability or local witness closure.

That blocks another common inflation move without widening the archive.
