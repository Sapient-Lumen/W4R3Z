# Public-reference-standard versus diachronic-witness-lineage-closure gate

Purpose: split the archive's **witness-package progress** one step further.
A candidate should not earn the same witness credit merely because it can **make a comparison standard public enough for several observers at one time** and because it has **closed the diachronic lineage that lets records, calibrations, and observer comparison stay the same evidence through time**.
Those are related, but they are not the same achievement.

## Compression verdict

Inside the archive's witness package, once a lane has already moved beyond one privileged frame and beyond a silently borrowed public comparison standard, the current score should now distinguish three nested layers:

1. **public reference-standard closure** — a lane can say how the shared standard of comparison becomes public enough for use: axes, clocks, orientations, adequacy thresholds, fragment-selection rules, or other comparison standards are no longer silently borrowed;
2. **diachronic witness-lineage closure** — a lane can say how the *same evidential standard and record lineage* persist across later rereads, apparatus drift, recalibration, observer succession, archive retrieval, or device replacement;
3. **local public witness closure** — a lane can say where the physical records live, what stabilizes them, how they are reread, why they remain the same evidence through time, and why public evidence is not mainly outsourced.

A lane may earn real credit on layer 1 while still being weak on layer 2.
That is progress.
It is just not yet diachronic lineage closure, much less full witness closure.

## Why this gate exists

Shared witness-gate scaffolding now lives in `docs/40-model/witness-closure-gate-family-frame.md`. This document only names the pair-specific inflation move below.

**a public standard that works now being retold as if durable empirical witness lineage through time were already solved.**

That inflation can happen in at least four ways:
- several observers can share a standard only because the theory silently borrows a stable observer identity or memory continuity that it does not itself explain;
- agreement can hold only because calibration traceability, transfer standards, metadata lineage, or uncertainty envelopes are inherited from an external laboratory chain;
- a theory can explain public comparison at one time slice without saying how later observers or successor devices reidentify the same record token, threshold, or detector response as *the same evidence*;
- or archive-ready evidence can be asserted in prose without naming how recalibration, drift, missing-data periods, or channel substitutions preserve or break the evidential lineage.

## The four-step diachronic-lineage test

### 1. Time-extended lineage disclosure

Before awarding diachronic-lineage credit, the archive should ask what has to stay the same across time for the evidence to remain the same evidence.
At minimum, a lane should disclose:
- which record token, calibration chain, observer memory, metadata bundle, or threshold convention is being carried forward,
- which later rereads, reanalyses, or successor devices are supposed to count as accessing the same evidential content,
- and which parts of that continuity are fixed externally rather than arising inside the candidate's own language.

A lane that wins only here earns **public-standard credit**, not yet diachronic lineage closure.

### 2. Persistence and reidentification route

A lane earns more than public-standard credit only when it can say how the evidential lineage itself persists.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what keeps the relevant record or standard stable across time,
- how later agents or devices reidentify it as the same evidential carrier,
- what transformations, replacements, or recalibrations preserve lineage and which ones break it,
- and whether the route works only in one toy setup or across a broader physically relevant class.

Without that route, the lane still owes major diachronic debt.

### 3. Traceability and uncertainty disclosure

A lane earns stronger diachronic credit only when it discloses the chain by which later comparison remains trustworthy.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what transfer standards, calibration links, uncertainty budgets, or provenance metadata keep the record comparable through time,
- how drift, missing data, hardware replacement, or changed coarse-graining are tracked,
- whether the same conclusion survives nearby recalibration choices or uncertainty envelopes,
- and which parts of the trust chain still live outside the candidate.

Without those answers, apparent durability may be a reporting artifact rather than candidate-native witness lineage.

### 4. Closure boundary

A lane earns **diachronic witness-lineage closure** credit only when it can say how a public comparison standard becomes a temporally durable evidence chain without a silent external continuity guarantor.
At minimum, it should name:
- the record / standard lineage being preserved,
- the persistence and reidentification route,
- the traceability / uncertainty chain,
- the remaining observer-succession or metadata debt,
- and the remaining debt to full local record carrier / stabilization / rereadability closure.

This is still not the same thing as full local witness closure.
But it is stronger than merely making the comparison standard public at one time.

## Common failure modes

1. **Momentary-publicity inflation** — a standard is public now, so the lane is retold as if durable evidence through time were solved too.
2. **Observer-persistence laundering** — stable memory or observer continuity is silently imported from ordinary practice rather than candidate-native structure.
3. **Traceability silence** — calibration chains, transfer standards, metadata provenance, or uncertainty envelopes are doing the real evidential work but remain unnamed.
4. **Replacement blindness** — a lane never says which device substitutions, recalibrations, or archive transformations preserve the same evidence.
5. **Lineage-to-closure inflation** — diachronic witness lineage is retold as if local record carrier, stabilization, and full objectivity were already solved too.

## Compact current readout

- **Public-reference-standard work** is real progress because it stops observer agreement from silently borrowing one common axis, clock, or adequacy threshold.
- **But diachronic lineage is a stricter demand.** A theory of everything should not merely explain how several observers compare notes now; it should say why those notes remain the same evidence after delay, reread, recalibration, archival storage, or successor-observer use.
- **Current observer-relative and quantum-reference-frame work** still leans heavily on borrowed continuity at this step.
- **Current laboratory data practice** makes the missing structure visible: practical public evidence depends on explicit traceability chains, calibrated transfer standards, metadata provenance, and stated uncertainty envelopes.

That does not refute the theory programs.
It just means they have not yet paid this witness debt.

## Net rule

Within the archive:
- public reference-standard closure earns real witness-package credit;
- but it does **not** yet count as diachronic witness-lineage closure;
- and neither of those automatically counts as local public witness closure.

Any future completion bid or bridge-family claim that wants stronger witness credit should now say not only how observers compare a fact, but how that fact remains the *same public evidence through time* without a silently borrowed laboratory lineage.
