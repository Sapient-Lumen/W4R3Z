# Assertibility versus public-adjudication-closure gate

Purpose: split the archive's **witness-package progress** one step further.
A candidate should not earn the same witness credit merely because it can **bind categorical public claims to challengeable entitlement artifacts, declared scope, and mandatory abstention semantics** and because it can **support a bounded public challenge path in which outsiders can trigger review under a frozen evidence / policy bundle, force reruns or comparison checks, and obtain a public terminal status rather than a narrative correction**.
Those are related, but they are not the same achievement.

## Compression verdict

Inside the archive's witness package, once a lane has already moved beyond public auditability and assertibility, the current score should now distinguish three nested layers:

1. **assertibility closure** — categorical public claims are issued only under publicly inspectable entitlement artifacts, declared scope, and mandatory abstention or undetermined semantics when entitlement is missing;
2. **public adjudication closure** — outsiders can actually trigger a bounded challenge path, recover the committed artifact bundle, force replay, rerun, or comparison under declared rules, and obtain a public outcome such as upheld, replaced, withdrawn, undetermined, or inconclusive rather than a private settlement or vague correction note;
3. **local public witness closure** — a lane can say where the physical records live, what stabilizes them, how they are reread, why they remain public evidence, and why that empirical loop is not mainly outsourced.

A lane may earn real credit on layer 1 while still being weak on layer 2.
That is progress.
It is just not yet public adjudication closure, much less full witness closure.

## Why this gate exists

Shared witness-gate scaffolding now lives in `docs/40-model/witness-closure-gate-family-frame.md`. This document only names the pair-specific inflation move below.

**assertibility-disciplined public claims being retold as if outsiders therefore already had a bounded power to challenge, rerun, and publicly revise them under fixed rules.**

That inflation can happen in at least six ways:
- a lane can publish declared scopes, entitlement certificates, and an undetermined state while leaving challenges to private operator channels or institution-specific goodwill;
- a lane can permit review in principle while failing to preserve a frozen replay bundle of the exact evidence, policy, model, thresholds, calibration state, or comparison target that governed the original public claim;
- a lane can accept disputes while silently changing the criteria or artifact set midstream, so the rerun does not actually test the published claim;
- a lane can re-evaluate challenged claims under insider discretion without a public decision rule naming what counts as uphold, replace, withdraw, or inconclusive;
- a lane can publish corrections without a bounded dispute horizon, so challenge rights remain indefinite rhetoric or vanish without notice;
- or a lane can settle a challenge narratively while never emitting a durable public resolution artifact that future users can inspect, cite, and reuse.

## The five-step public-adjudication test

### 1. Standing, trigger, and dispute horizon

Before awarding adjudication credit, the archive should ask whether outsiders can actually trigger a challenge.
At minimum, a lane should disclose:
- who may challenge: any observer, registered participant, bounded oversight set, or only insiders,
- what artifact, receipt, record bundle, or comparison result is challengeable,
- what time window or dispute horizon governs challenge rights,
- and whether challenge initiation is public enough to leave a durable event rather than only a private support ticket.

A lane that wins only here earns **assertibility credit**, not yet public adjudication closure.

### 2. Frozen replay / comparison bundle

A lane earns more than assertibility credit only when the challenged public claim is tied to a recoverable bundle rich enough for replay or comparison.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what exact evidence, policy, thresholds, model or theory version, calibration state, metadata, and timing context are frozen for review,
- whether those materials are durably available to challengers rather than reconstructed later from memory,
- whether the challenge rerun checks the same public claim rather than a nearby reformulation,
- and which parts of the original public claim still depend on tacit lab practice, operator memory, or unexported internal state.

Without that route, apparent adjudication may still be assertibility with a better inbox.

### 3. Public decision rule over narrative discretion

A lane earns stronger adjudication credit only when disputes terminate under a declared rule rather than ad hoc discussion.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- who adjudicates: auditor, committee, referee, comparison protocol, or other named decision surface,
- what rule decides the case: byte equality, threshold vote, statistical criterion, comparison metric, or explicit qualitative rubric,
- whether inconclusive status exists when the challenge instrument is too weak or the transfer standard is inadequate,
- and whether the rule is public before the challenge outcome rather than reverse-engineered afterward.

Without those answers, apparent adjudication may still be assertibility plus informed discretion.

### 4. Bounded public state transition

A lane earns still stronger adjudication credit only when a dispute changes public standing in a bounded, legible way.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what terminal public outcomes exist: upheld, replaced, withdrawn, downgraded to undetermined, or inconclusive,
- how those outcomes become visible on the same public surface as the original claim,
- whether a successful challenge can actually replace or downgrade the original public standing rather than merely append commentary,
- and how challenge costs or penalties are structured so that the pathway is usable but not free to spam.

Without that route, apparent adjudication may still be transparent but non-binding review.

### 5. Durable dispute memory

A lane earns **public adjudication closure** credit only when dispute outcomes become durable infrastructure rather than one-off fixes.
At minimum, it should name:
- the standing and dispute horizon,
- the frozen replay or comparison bundle,
- the public decision rule,
- the terminal public outcome states,
- the durable resolution artifact: replaced record, amended comparison report, regression test, or standing benchmark revision,
- and the remaining debt to full local record-carrier, rereadability, and objectivity closure.

This is still not the same thing as full local witness closure.
A lane may be strongly adjudication-disciplined while still borrowing the public record carrier, rereadability, or objectivity side from ordinary laboratory witness structure.

## Common failure modes

1. **assertibility-to-adjudication inflation** — a challengeable certificate is retold as if outsiders therefore already have bounded rerun power.
2. **challenge theater** — a public complaint channel exists, but the challenger cannot reach the committed artifact bundle or trigger a rule-bound review.
3. **moving-target review** — the challenge uses a modified model, revised threshold, or altered calibration bundle rather than the one that governed the original public claim.
4. **criterion laundering** — reviewers discuss the challenge but no public decision rule says what would count as uphold, replace, withdraw, or inconclusive.
5. **non-binding correction** — a dispute produces commentary or apology while the original public standing remains intact.
6. **memoryless adjudication** — the same failure can recur because the dispute never becomes a revised report, regression artifact, or benchmark update.

## Compact current readout

- **Recent factuality-benchmark work** makes the stronger burden explicit by releasing claim-level auditable rationales, versioning disputed items, and reporting that expert or agent auditors can adjudicate disagreements rather than only label one-shot outputs.
- **Recent public-verification protocol work** makes the stronger burden operational by adding a fixed challenge window, low-cost audits, formal full challenges, deterministic re-execution, threshold decision rules, and public replacement of disputed outputs when the original claim fails.
- **Current international metrology infrastructure** makes the institutional side concrete by hosting key and supplementary comparison reports as public technical artifacts that underpin recognized calibration and measurement capabilities rather than treating disputes as informal side correspondence.
- **Recent NIST comparison-analysis work** makes the statistical side explicit by warning that familiar pass / fail criteria can be too weak, by naming comparison power as part of the challenge surface, and by preserving an inconclusive outcome where the comparison instrument or transfer standard is not strong enough.

That does not refute current observer-relative or bridge-theory programs.
It just means they have not yet paid this stronger public-adjudication debt.

## Net rule

Within the archive:
- assertibility closure earns real witness-package credit;
- but it does **not** yet count as public adjudication closure;
- and neither of those automatically counts as local public witness closure.

Any future completion bid or bridge-family claim that wants stronger witness credit should now say not only how public categorical claims are entitlement-disciplined, but who can challenge them, for how long, against which frozen replay or comparison bundle, under which public decision rule, with which terminal public states, and with what durable memory so that a successful dispute changes the archive-visible standing rather than merely annotating it.
