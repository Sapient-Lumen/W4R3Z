## Compression verdict

The archive should now hold one tighter sentence:

**inside low-energy gravity EFT reasoning, positivity / dispersion constraints should be scored as running-sensitive envelope tools rather than static sign verdicts by default. Recent one-loop work shows that certain non-minimal three-point interactions can drive leading scalar, photon, and graviton Wilson coefficients downward toward the infrared, while graviton-aware dispersion bounds still preserve consistency and tie the allowed negativity to gravitational contributions plus species / coupling bounds.** `REF-0178`

That is a real gain.
It gives the archive a cleaner way to score what low-energy amplitude sign arguments buy and where they stop.
But it is still not the same thing as observable closure, stable lane attribution, or broad ToE credit.

## What has genuinely improved

### 1. The archive now has an explicit running-sensitive sign rule instead of static positivity rhetoric

Earlier archive posture already treated amplitude / positivity language as valuable but easy to overread.
`REF-0178` sharpens that caution by showing that, once dynamical gravitons are included, some leading EFT coefficients can decrease toward the infrared rather than behaving like simple scale-free positivity mascots. `REF-0178`

That matters because it turns a broad methodological warning into a smaller operational rule: a low-energy sign claim should name the scale, the running corridor, and the package that carries the dispersion argument before it is scored as lane pressure.

### 2. The archive now has a cleaner graviton-loop envelope rule

The same source does not dissolve positivity discipline.
Instead, it shows that graviton contributions also enter the ultraviolet dispersion bounds needed to keep the envelope consistent, so the right read is not “positivity failed,” but “the admissible envelope moved once gravity was treated honestly.” `REF-0178`

For the archive, that is valuable because it blocks two opposite inflation moves at once: one where any negative running is narrated as a death blow to consistency, and one where a single positive coefficient sign is narrated as a scale-free victory.

### 3. The archive now has an explicit species / coupling honesty rule

`REF-0178` also ties the allowed negativity to bounded numbers of non-minimally coupled states or bounded coupling-weighted scalar content, rather than leaving the whole effect as free prestige rhetoric. `REF-0178`

That gives the archive a more concrete discipline sentence: sign-level low-energy support is only as clean as its disclosure of graviton loops, non-minimal couplings, species counting, and measurement scale.

## What this does **not** buy

### 1. Not a collapse of positivity / causality methods

The new source does **not** say that dispersive or causality-based EFT reasoning has become useless in gravity.
It says that the archive should stop treating one coefficient sign as a static monotone verdict when running, graviton poles, and infrared structure matter. `REF-0178`

### 2. Not yet observable or attribution closure

Even a better sign-envelope story still sits upstream of on-shell observable extraction, stable rival separation, and attribution-grade support.
This revision therefore sharpens low-energy amplitude honesty, not witness-package debt or observed-sector closure. `REF-0039`, `REF-0058`, `REF-0166`, `REF-0177`, `REF-0178`

### 3. Not a unique-lane victory

The source is useful because it improves the archive's low-energy discipline rule, not because it crowns one completion bid.
It should tighten how several lanes talk about positivity, dispersion, or EFT sign constraints whenever those are used as empirical pressure or theory-selection rhetoric. `REF-0166`, `REF-0177`, `REF-0178`

## The archive score change

The archive should now keep one smaller distinction explicit whenever a lane advertises low-energy sign-based gravity support:

1. **sign snapshot** — one coefficient sign is quoted at one scale or in one preferred package;
2. **running-aware envelope** — the sign claim is placed inside a stated RG / dispersion corridor with graviton-loop honesty;
3. **species / package disclosure** — the lane states what field content, non-minimal couplings, and hierarchy assumptions carry the bound;
4. **attribution-grade support** — nearby admissible scale choices, nuisance packages, or inference pipelines do not erase the same lane credit;
5. **witness-package relief** — the resulting observable can ride a durable public evidence stack.

Current archive posture should allow real credit at stages 1-3.
It should not let stages 1-2 silently impersonate stages 4-5.

## False promotions to reject

1. **"Negative infrared running kills positivity-based consistency talk outright."**
   - The new result instead shows a graviton-aware envelope in which some negativity is consistent once the dispersion story is upgraded.

2. **"One positive low-energy coefficient sign is already a scale-free lane verdict."**
   - The archive should now demand scale, loop, and species disclosure before scoring a sign claim as serious pressure.

3. **"Low-energy amplitude-envelope success already buys observable or witness closure."**
   - The source is about disciplined EFT envelope reading, not about public record carriers or stable attribution by itself.

## What would move the archive further

A later revision should score this lane as materially stronger only if one compact package adds most of the following together:
1. explicit scale-tagged observables rather than coefficient-sign rhetoric alone,
2. stability across nearby admissible loop, species, or non-minimal-coupling packages,
3. a clearer route from those observables to rival separation rather than one-lane prestige talk,
4. and a route from that separation to public witness-bearing evidence.

Without that package, the archive should preserve the gain as a bounded **gravitational positivity running audit** and stop there.

## Net result

The archive now has a better sentence available:

> low-energy gravity positivity constraints remain valuable, but one coefficient sign is weaker than a graviton-aware running envelope with explicit scale, species, and package disclosure — and that envelope is still weaker than attribution-grade support or witness-package relief.

That keeps amplitude / dispersion discipline load-bearing without letting sign rhetoric overclose the archive. `REF-0178`
