# Diachronic-witness-lineage versus cross-site-reproducibility-closure gate

Purpose: split the archive's **witness-package progress** one step further.
A candidate should not earn the same witness credit merely because it can **preserve the same evidence through time inside one inherited lab / provider / apparatus lineage** and because it has **closed cross-site reproducibility strongly enough that the same coarse-grained evidential claim can be reinstantiated across independently maintained sites or stacks**.
Those are related, but they are not the same achievement.

## Compression verdict

Inside the archive's witness package, once a lane has already moved beyond one-time public comparison and has named a durable evidential lineage through time, the current score should now distinguish three nested layers:

1. **diachronic witness-lineage closure** — a lane can say how the same evidential standard and record lineage persist through reread, recalibration, archive retrieval, device replacement, and observer succession *within one managed lineage*;
2. **cross-site reproducibility closure** — a lane can say how comparable preparation, measurement, metadata, and acceptance conditions are reinstantiated across independently maintained laboratories, providers, hardware stacks, or experimental campaigns, so the same coarse-grained evidential claim survives outside one inherited chain;
3. **local public witness closure** — a lane can say where the physical records live, what stabilizes them, how they are reread, why they remain the same evidence through time, and why public evidence is not mainly outsourced.

A lane may earn real credit on layer 1 while still being weak on layer 2.
That is progress.
It is just not yet cross-site reproducibility closure, much less full witness closure.

## Why this gate exists

Shared witness-gate scaffolding now lives in `docs/40-model/witness-closure-gate-family-frame.md`. This document only names the pair-specific inflation move below.

**a durable evidential chain inside one inherited stack being retold as if independent public reproducibility across sites or providers were already solved.**

That inflation can happen in at least four ways:
- the same result is reproduced only because the same software stack, control scripts, calibration recipes, or vendor conventions are silently shared across all runs;
- protocol transport depends on tacit local operator knowledge that never becomes part of the candidate's explicit witness story;
- a theory can explain how evidence persists through time in one archive while saying little about what has to be fixed, versioned, or re-instantiated elsewhere for another site to count as seeing the same evidence;
- or cross-platform agreement can be asserted in prose without naming the public benchmark / schema / reporting backbone that makes the claims comparable rather than merely similar.

## The four-step cross-site reproducibility test

### 1. Site and stack disclosure

Before awarding cross-site reproducibility credit, the archive should ask what actually changes from site to site.
At minimum, a lane should disclose:
- which preparation steps, control scripts, instrument backends, calibration conventions, data schemas, and reporting rules are shared,
- which of those are versioned and transportable rather than tacitly local,
- and which parts of the evidential loop still depend on one inherited stack or operator culture.

A lane that wins only here earns **diachronic-lineage credit**, not yet cross-site reproducibility closure.

### 2. Protocol transport route

A lane earns more than lineage credit only when it can say how the evidential protocol itself travels.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what separates measurement intent from site-specific instrument realization,
- what public protocol, schema, or benchmark definition lets different stacks instantiate the same coarse-grained claim,
- which parts of the route require manual retuning or hidden expert intervention,
- and whether the transport works only across trivial near-clones or across genuinely heterogeneous setups.

Without that route, the lane still owes major reproducibility debt.

### 3. Cross-site comparability and versioning

A lane earns stronger reproducibility credit only when it discloses how agreement remains comparable across independently maintained stacks.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what metadata, versioned benchmark definitions, calibration disclosures, and acceptance criteria travel with the claim,
- whether the same conclusion survives nearby changes in provider, hardware family, software release, or reporting convention,
- how longitudinal reruns and cross-platform curation are handled,
- and which parts of the comparability chain still live outside the candidate.

Without those answers, apparent reproducibility may be a same-stack artifact rather than candidate-native public evidence.

### 4. Closure boundary

A lane earns **cross-site reproducibility closure** credit only when it can say how a diachronic evidential lineage becomes a multi-lineage public result across independently maintained sites or stacks without a silent common-lab inheritance.
At minimum, it should name:
- the coarse-grained evidential claim being transported,
- the public protocol / schema / benchmark backbone,
- the site-specific realization freedom and its limits,
- the versioning / metadata / calibration chain that preserves comparability,
- and the remaining debt to full local record carrier / stabilization / rereadability closure.

This is still not the same thing as full local witness closure.
But it is stronger than merely preserving one evidence chain through time.

## Common failure modes

1. **One-lineage inflation** — a durable evidence chain in one institution or provider stack is retold as if broad public reproducibility were already solved.
2. **Same-stack laundering** — nominally different runs still share hidden software, calibration, operator, or vendor infrastructure.
3. **Tacit-operator borrowing** — reproducibility depends on undocumented expert practice rather than transportable protocol.
4. **Versionless protocol drift** — benchmark definitions, schemas, or reporting conditions move while the lane keeps talking as if the same evidence were being compared.
5. **Reproducibility-to-closure inflation** — cross-site reproducibility is retold as if local record carrier, stabilization, and full objectivity were already solved too.

## Compact current readout

- **Recent metrology / standardization work** sharpens the target by insisting that results become reproducible across laboratories only when reporting conditions, timing conventions, fit models, acceptance criteria, and environmental disclosures are explicit enough to travel.
- **Recent portable measurement-stack work** makes the transport structure visible by separating algorithmic or measurement intent from instrument realization, standardizing schemas, and exposing reusable protocol templates rather than relying on one local control stack.
- **Recent open cross-platform benchmarking work** sharpens the audit language by forcing public tooling, versioned data curation, longitudinal reruns, and provider-spanning comparison instead of one-shot same-stack demonstrations.

That does not refute current observer-relative or bridge-theory programs.
It just means they have not yet paid this stronger reproducibility debt.

## Net rule

Within the archive:
- diachronic witness-lineage closure earns real witness-package credit;
- but it does **not** yet count as cross-site reproducibility closure;
- and neither of those automatically counts as local public witness closure.

Any future completion bid or bridge-family claim that wants stronger witness credit should now say not only how the same evidence persists through time, but how the same coarse-grained evidential claim is reinstantiated across independently maintained sites, providers, or hardware stacks without a silently borrowed common lab.
