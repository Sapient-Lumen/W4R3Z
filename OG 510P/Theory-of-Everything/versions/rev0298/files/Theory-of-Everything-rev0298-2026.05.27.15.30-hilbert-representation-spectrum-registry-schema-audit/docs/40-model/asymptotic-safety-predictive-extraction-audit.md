# Asymptotic-safety predictive-extraction audit

This document keeps one conservative completion-bid strength from inflating into a broader low-energy cash-out claim.
It asks what the archive should count when asymptotic safety is praised not only for ultraviolet fixed-point seriousness, but for supposedly already delivering concrete predictive control at the amplitude or Wilson-coefficient level.

## Compression verdict

The archive should now hold one tighter sentence:

**inside asymptotic safety, ultraviolet fixed-point evidence is stronger than portable predictive extraction. Recent amplitude work shows that fixed-point existence alone does not guarantee bounded scattering amplitudes, that massless infrared gravitational logarithms can dominate, and that derivative-expansion / standard-RG-improvement routes can misstate low-energy momentum dependence.** `REF-0166`

That is not a demotion of the lane.
It is a sharper cash-out rule.
The lane keeps its conservative completion-bid seriousness, but any stronger prediction rhetoric now owes a more explicit momentum- or field-dependent transport story.

## What has genuinely improved

### 1. The archive now has a concrete amplitude-level transport warning, not only general truncation rhetoric

Earlier archive posture already treated asymptotic safety as a serious completion bid with real UV fixed-point ambition but lingering truncation / scheme debt.
`REF-0166` sharpens that posture by computing a simple scalar scattering amplitude analytically enough to say something more precise: the fixed point by itself does **not** guarantee amplitude boundedness, and low-energy cash-out can fail for reasons that do not show up in coarse fixed-point prose. `REF-0166`

That is useful because the archive prefers one explicit transport failure mode over a large amount of generic caution language.

### 2. The massless infrared regime is now a named predictive hazard

The new paper argues that in massless theories gravitational logarithms can dominate the infrared regime.
That matters for the archive because a lane can look ultraviolet-clean while still transporting its claimed prediction through the infrared badly. `REF-0166`

So the archive should now distinguish more sharply between:
- **UV completion seriousness**,
- **formal low-energy extraction in one truncation**,
- and **robust momentum-sensitive predictive transport**.

### 3. Derivative expansion and standard RG improvement are now weaker default cash-out stories than the archive previously allowed

`REF-0166` argues that in the massless case a derivative expansion can fail quantitatively for the relevant Wilson coefficients and that standard RG-improvement can fail qualitatively on momentum dependence.
For theories with massive fields the paper is more forgiving, but it still reports residual failure modes for classically marginal and purely gravitational couplings. `REF-0166`

That does not kill the lane.
It does block a common prestige jump:
from *fixed point plus one truncation* directly to *portable low-energy predictive extraction*.

## What this does **not** buy

### 1. Not a refutation of asymptotic safety

The new result is a transport-discipline gain, not a lane-collapse result.
It says the cash-out route is harder and more momentum-sensitive than a fixed-point headline suggests.
It does **not** say the ultraviolet completion bid fails. `REF-0166`

### 2. Not a reason to erase benchmark-level predictive structure

The archive should still allow bounded benchmark credit when a truncation or restricted matter sector yields a real structural relation.
The new brake is narrower: benchmark-level structure is weaker than portable predictive transport, especially when the route is IR-sensitive or depends on coarse derivative expansion alone. `REF-0166`

### 3. Not yet attribution-grade theory support

Even if one observable-looking relation survives a chosen extraction route, the archive still needs to ask whether nearby admissible momentum treatments, regulator choices, or forward-model packages preserve the claim.
So this revision sharpens **prediction transport** and **attribution fragility**, not ranking or closure. `REF-0166`

## The archive score change

The archive should now keep one smaller distinction explicit for asymptotic safety:

1. **fixed-point seriousness** — the lane remains a live conservative completion bid;
2. **bounded extraction in one momentum/truncation package** — a concrete calculation yields a structured low-energy relation or amplitude result;
3. **portable predictive transport** — the same relation survives explicit momentum/field dependence, nearby admissible extraction choices, and the relevant IR structure;
4. **attribution-grade support** — the resulting empirical traction still points stably back to the lane rather than to one fragile extraction ecology.

Current archive posture is solidly at stage 1, with bounded activity at stage 2.
`REF-0166` says the jump from stage 1 or 2 to stage 3 is now harder than broad fixed-point rhetoric suggests.

## False promotions to reject

1. **"A UV fixed point already implies well-behaved low-energy amplitudes."**
   - The new amplitude result says no: boundedness and correct momentum dependence still need explicit transport work.

2. **"A derivative expansion is good enough by default for predictive cash-out."**
   - In massless cases the new result says no; the approximation can miss the relevant coefficients quantitatively.

3. **"Standard RG improvement already captures the important low-energy momentum story."**
   - The new result says that can fail qualitatively in the massless setting.

4. **"One successful asymptotic-safety extraction already earns portable observed-sector credit."**
   - The archive should now require stronger transport and attribution checks before granting that score.

## What would move the archive further

A later revision should score this lane as materially stronger only if one compact package adds most of the following together:
1. explicit momentum- or field-dependent transport beyond coarse derivative expansion where the regime demands it,
2. stability of the extracted relation across nearby admissible truncation / regulator / matching choices,
3. a clear split between massless-IR-sensitive and massive-sector cash-out claims,
4. theory-separated observable consequences that survive those transport checks,
5. and a cleaner route from extraction success to attribution-grade support.

Without that package, the archive should preserve the gain as a bounded **predictive-extraction audit** and stop there.

## Net result

The archive now has a better conservative-completion sentence available:

> asymptotic safety may keep its ultraviolet fixed-point seriousness while still owing a harder momentum-sensitive low-energy cash-out story than fixed-point rhetoric suggests.

That keeps the lane load-bearing without letting fixed-point prestige overclose the archive. `REF-0166`
