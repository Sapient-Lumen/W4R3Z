## Compression verdict

The archive should now preserve six compact points at once:
- family C is strong enough that **abstention competence** must now be scored separately from mere route existence, accuracy, or even bundle-aware rhetoric, because current lanes increasingly output explicit bulk geometries, effective potentials, or model functions. `REF-0142`, `REF-0143`, `REF-0145`
- recent conformal-PINN work shows a stronger calibration pattern than heuristic uncertainty language: a route can attach prediction intervals to a calibration set and obtain **distribution-free finite-sample coverage guarantees**, even with spatially adaptive intervals. `REF-0146`
- but recent conformal-operational work also shows that coverage alone does not determine the deployed behavior people actually care about, because **commitment frequency, deferral, and decisive error exposure** can still differ materially across rules with similar coverage. `REF-0147`
- and current scientific-ML OOD work makes the regime-shift pressure explicit: neural physics solvers often degrade outside training support, so abstention competence now requires a declared **shift trigger** rather than confidence language that is only meaningful in-distribution. `REF-0148`
- recent inverse-design work goes one step further by treating abstention and OOD detection as **route-level capabilities** rather than after-the-fact interpretation, which is exactly the epistemic direction family C now needs even if not through the same machinery. `REF-0149`
- so family C now earns a sharper screen: **soft confidence, nominal intervals, or one uncertainty score are weaker than a route with declared coverage semantics, an audited deployment profile, and an explicit regime-shift trigger that can abstain rather than always return a bulk story.** `REF-0146`, `REF-0147`, `REF-0148`, `REF-0149`

## Why this document exists

The archive already blocks several overpromotions in the family-C lane:
- restricted inverse success is not yet identifiability closure,
- route breadth is not yet common-target convergence,
- thin-data recovery is not yet acquirable witness closure,
- conditioned robustness is not yet portable inverse stability,
- good route-local recovery is not yet candidate-native target recovery,
- and one confident answer is not yet a calibrated solution-bundle or honest uncertainty object.

What it still lacked was one short rule for the next inflation move:

> this route reports uncertainty or a bundle-like object, therefore it already knows when not to identify the bulk target.

That step is still too fast.
A route can emit error bars, posterior mass, confidence scores, or a best-answer ranking while still lacking:
- a declared **coverage** meaning for those intervals,
- a declared **deferral / abstention** rule,
- a declared **audit profile** over commitment and decisive error,
- or a declared **regime-shift trigger** for when the whole inverse ecology has left the trusted package.

The archive therefore needs one explicit distinction between:
- a route that attaches soft confidence to every answer,
- a route that supplies a calibrated family of admissible answers,
- a route that knows when to **abstain**,
- and a route that knows when the input itself sits outside the regime where any of those objects should be trusted.

## The five checks the archive should now require

### 1. Heuristic uncertainty is weaker than abstention semantics

It is now easy for a family-C route to output one answer plus a confidence-like quantity.
But a score attached to every prediction is not yet evidence that the route knows when it should refuse to identify a bulk target.
The archive should therefore require an explicit output grammar such as:
- predict one answer,
- return a bundle / interval of admissible answers,
- or abstain.

Without that grammar, the route may still be *obliged* to emit a geometry-like object even when the data / prior stack do not justify one.

### 2. Coverage semantics are stronger than soft confidence

Yu, Ho, and Wang are useful here because they show one route from scientific-ML uncertainty talk to a genuinely stronger object: conformal prediction for PINNs with a calibration set, finite-sample distribution-free coverage guarantees, and locally adaptive uncertainty bands. `REF-0146`

The archive should not silently import their exact machinery into holography.
But it should import the epistemic lesson:
**a route that wants abstention-ready credit should say what its intervals mean, how they were calibrated, and under what sampling semantics they are supposed to cover the truth.**

### 3. Coverage is still weaker than an audited deployment profile

Zwart matters because he makes an additional point that the archive now needs.
Even once coverage is calibrated, deployment still depends on quantities like **commitment frequency, deferral, and decisive error exposure**, and these are not fixed by coverage alone. He therefore introduces a calibrate-and-audit pattern where the rule is calibrated and then audited on an independent split to estimate the operational profile actually induced by deployment. `REF-0147`

That is exactly the next distinction family C now requires.
A route can have nominally good uncertainty semantics while still being too eager to commit, too reluctant to abstain, or too exposed to decisive error in the cases that matter for candidate-level same/different claims.

### 4. Shift triggers are part of abstention competence

Wei, Ooi, Wong, Gupta, Chiu, and Ong are useful because they state the distribution-shift problem without euphemism: scientific machine learning often fails once the test scenario moves outside the training distribution, and robust out-of-distribution generalization remains a central challenge. Their NOVA framework is valuable to the archive not because it solves family C, but because it forces the right question into the open: **what tells the route that it has left the regime where ordinary confidence language is even meaningful?** `REF-0148`

Family C now needs that same discipline.
A route that is only calibrated inside one ansatz, one cutoff family, one training ecology, or one observer-map package should not be scored as abstention-ready unless it also declares the trigger by which it detects regime shift and exits rather than overcommitting.

### 5. Inverse routes can treat abstention as a native capability

De Campos, Krebs, and Gottschalk are valuable because they frame abstention inside inverse design as a route-level capability tied to intrinsic uncertainty metrics, where abstaining from unreliable predictions and detecting out-of-distribution targets are explicit goals rather than interpretive afterthoughts. `REF-0149`

Again, the archive should not import their exact architecture into holography.
But it should import the stronger lesson:
**abstention should be a designed capability of an inverse route, not a charitable reading of confidence scores after the route has already committed itself.**

## False promotions the archive should reject

1. **one uncertainty score is not yet an abstention rule**
   - A route can quantify uncertainty while still being forced to answer every time.

2. **nominal interval output is not yet declared coverage semantics**
   - An interval without calibration semantics is still softer than finite-sample or otherwise explicit coverage meaning.

3. **coverage is not yet a deployment profile**
   - Similar coverage can coexist with very different commitment, deferral, and decisive-error behavior.

4. **in-distribution calibration is not yet regime-shift competence**
   - A route can be well behaved on its calibration ecology while still failing silently under ansatz, cutoff, observer-map, or data-distribution shift.

5. **OOD detection is not yet candidate-native identifiability closure**
   - Detecting that a route has left its trusted regime is helpful, but it still does not close same/different rules for the candidate-native bulk target.

6. **abstention-ready inverse machinery is not yet candidate-native closure**
   - Even an honest defer-or-abstain route can still borrow the gauge, map, recoverand, and witness assumptions that keep family C below closure.

## What would change archive state

A future family-C lane should earn stronger identifiability credit only if it adds, in one compact route:
1. a declared output grammar that distinguishes point prediction, calibrated bundle, and abstention,
2. declared coverage or calibration semantics for the uncertainty object it reports,
3. an independently audited operational profile over commitment, deferral, and decisive error,
4. a declared regime-shift or OOD trigger that can route the system into abstention,
5. a declared mapping from abstention / bundle / point-output behavior to candidate-level same/different claims,
6. and portability checks showing that this abstention behavior survives nearby ansatz, cutoff, observer-map, filtering, and training-package changes.

Without that six-part upgrade, family C should be scored as stronger at **speaking uncertainty language** and at guarding against forced overcommitment, not yet as offering abstention-ready candidate-native identification.
