# Identifiability worked-example battery

## Purpose

The state machine should be run, not merely admired. This battery gives toy cases that future revisions can use to test whether route states, residual caps, negative controls, and public bridges are being applied consistently.

## Example 1 — Dual descriptions with the same public observables

- Positive pattern: two descriptions produce the same public correlators.
- State: `S2` unless the target quotient says the descriptions are intentionally identical.
- Negative control: dual-description decoy.
- Failure caught: empirical equivalence being laundered into ontology identification.
- Required upgrade: declare the quotient under which the two descriptions are the same candidate, or keep ontology claims capped.

## Example 2 — Entanglement wedge / island reconstruction

- Positive pattern: interior or bulk operators become reconstructable from boundary or radiation data.
- State: bounded `S3` when code-space, target grain, public record, and residual error are declared.
- Negative control: reconstruction decoy with hidden state / decoder / cutoff prior.
- Failure caught: reconstruction access being laundered into public witness ownership or full ToE identifiability.
- Required upgrade: fill the record denominator and distinguish physical possession, reconstructive access, and candidate-native record definition.

## Example 3 — BMV-style gravity-mediated entanglement discriminator

- Positive pattern: an experiment constrains whether a gravitational mediator can carry quantum correlations.
- State: `S2`, with named discriminator-class `S3` pockets if the mediator class is sharply narrowed.
- Negative control: lab-discriminator decoy that rules out a mediator class while leaving many ToE candidates alive.
- Failure caught: low-energy quantumness evidence being counted as full quantum-gravity ontology.
- Required upgrade: show candidate-specific inverse identification, not merely class discrimination.

## Example 4 — Causal-set reconstruction route

- Positive pattern: order / volume / sprinkling information reconstructs continuum-like structure.
- State: `S2` unless inverse stability, manifoldlikeness, quantum dynamics, and public record production are declared.
- Negative control: discrete-order decoy with matching large-scale pattern but wrong dynamics or matter recovery.
- Failure caught: structural reconstruction being counted as completion-bid closure.
- Required upgrade: define acquisition, inverse deficiency, and observed-sector recovery.

## Example 5 — Frame-dependent record transport

- Positive pattern: two relational or quantum-reference-frame descriptions each produce a coherent record.
- State: access credit until a same-fact transport law is declared.
- Negative control: frame decoy with frame-local agreement but no public equivalence rule.
- Failure caught: observer-relative success being counted as public reference-standard closure.
- Required upgrade: fill the frame-transport schema and public challenge route.

## Example 6 — Simulator of an assumed equation

- Positive pattern: hybrid, analogue, or numerical system faithfully executes a candidate equation.
- State: tooling or acquisition credit, generally `S1`/`S2`.
- Negative control: simulator decoy that inherits the equation rather than identifying it.
- Failure caught: execution fidelity being counted as attribution-grade empirical support.
- Required upgrade: independent public records that could distinguish the assumed equation from nearby alternatives.

## Maintenance rule

Each future candidate-native promotion attempt should add or update one worked example when the control class is new. If no example can be written, the proposed promotion likely lacks a replayable denominator.
