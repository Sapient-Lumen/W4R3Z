# Duality equivalence versus candidate identity audit

## Purpose

Dualities are both a strength and a trap. They can reveal that apparently different descriptions are one candidate quotient, or they can hide underdetermination by making many presentations look like one coherent package.

## Audit questions

| Question | Failure if missing |
|---|---|
| What are the two descriptions being compared? | no adjudication object |
| What is the claimed invariant carrier? | presentation-level change mistaken for candidate difference |
| Is there a duality, gauge, frame, regulator, or functorial map? | duplicate credit or false conflict |
| What public record experiment distinguishes them? | no candidate-difference claim |
| What regime boundary limits equivalence? | local equivalence exported globally |
| What residual cap survives the quotient? | closure rhetoric by erasing degeneracy |

## Route effect

This audit is especially relevant to string / M-theory, holography, amplitudes, locally covariant bridges, and frame-relative witness routes. It also applies to any future “same theory in different language” claim.

## Stop rule

If the archive cannot decide whether two descriptions are `presentation-same`, `duality-same`, `operationally-same`, `regime-split`, `candidate-different`, or `unknown`, the route cannot spend candidate-uniqueness language. It may spend only quotient-bounded or discriminator-bounded credit.
