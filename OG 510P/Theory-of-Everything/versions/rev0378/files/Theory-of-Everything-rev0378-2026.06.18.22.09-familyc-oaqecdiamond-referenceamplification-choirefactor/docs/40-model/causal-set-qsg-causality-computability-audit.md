# Causal-set QSG causality-computability audit

This document keeps one real causal-set gain from inflating into a broader claim that the minimalist discrete-causality lane already has a rich, computable quantum dynamics in hand.
It asks what the archive should count when quantum sequential growth is praised not only for being conceptually native to causal order, but for supposedly already having escaped the simplicity / computability tradeoff.

## Compression verdict

The archive should now hold one tighter sentence:

**inside causal-set / discrete-causality programs, native quantum-growth ambition is now sharper than before, but still weaker than an earned non-commutative dynamics story or portable completion-bid cash-out. Recent quantum sequential growth work shows that two natural operator-ordering implementations of quantum Bell causality collapse the transition-operator algebra back to a commutative one, while the remaining precursor-size-ordered option adds constraints without yielding a general computable form; the simplest `d=2` Pauli realization fails, so any non-trivial realization would have to be higher-dimensional if it exists at all. That is a real causality/computability audit, not yet a settled quantum dynamics.** `REF-0169`

That is not a demotion of the lane.
It is a sharper honesty rule.
The minimalist completion / substrate bid now has one explicit native quantum-growth stress test, but stronger credit still owes a workable non-commuting dynamics, continuum-recovery transport, matter coupling, and witness-bearing observables.

## What has genuinely improved

### 1. The archive now has one explicit native quantum-growth stress test

Earlier posture already treated causal-set programs as the sharpest minimalist pressure on continuum emergence, but it was easy to talk as though “order plus number” naturally came with a suitably quantum growth law waiting just offstage. `REF-0169`

`REF-0169` turns that vague optimism into a more usefully compressed picture: the main obstruction is no longer just “quantum dynamics is hard”, but a more specific tension between Bell-causality-style naturalness, non-commutativity, and computability. `REF-0169`

### 2. Two natural quantum Bell-causality orderings collapse back toward the already-simple lane

The paper's strongest negative result is also its most clarifying one: assuming non-singular dynamics, the two most natural operator-ordering choices for quantum Bell causality reduce the transition-operator algebra to a commutative one. `REF-0169`

That matters because the archive prefers one explicit collapse result over generic remarks that the causal-set lane “probably has richer quantum versions somewhere.”

### 3. The surviving option is better viewed as a bounded unresolved corridor than as achieved quantum richness

A third, precursor-size-ordered choice yields additional commutation relations without forcing full commutativity, but the algebra remains too complicated to produce a general transition-operator form, which in the paper's own terms hinders computability. The attempted Pauli-matrix representation also fails, pushing any non-trivial realization beyond the simplest `d=2` setting. `REF-0169`

That is exactly the middle layer the archive needs to preserve:
- **minimalist native kinematics**,
- **bounded native quantum-growth pressure**,
- **workable non-commuting dynamics**,
- **continuum / observed-sector cash-out**.

`REF-0169` materially strengthens the second layer.
It does not collapse the third or fourth.

## What this does **not** buy

### 1. Not a realized non-commutative QSG dynamics

The new result does not deliver a general, computable non-commuting transition-operator dynamics.
It clarifies where the difficulty lives. `REF-0169`

### 2. Not continuum or observed-sector recovery

Nothing in the new audit by itself solves manifoldlike emergence, matter coupling, ordinary low-energy transport, or observed-sector architecture.
So the gain is still upstream of the main completion-bid recovery gates. `REF-0169`

### 3. Not empirical or witness closure

Even a successful higher-dimensional non-commutative QSG realization would still need observable carriers, record formation, and a wedge against nearby rivals before the archive could score it beyond atlas seriousness.
The present result is earlier than all of that. `REF-0169`

## False promotions to reject

1. **"Causal-set theory now has a rich native quantum dynamics."**
   - The new result earns an explicit obstruction map, not a settled non-commuting dynamics.

2. **"Natural Bell-causality quantization automatically preserves both causal discipline and quantum richness."**
   - Two natural orderings collapse back to commutativity, and the remaining path is still algebraically under-solved.

3. **"A clarified QSG algebra already upgrades the causal-set lane into broad completion-bid cash-out."**
   - Continuum recovery, matter transport, observables, and witness-bearing wedges remain much later debts.

## What would move the archive further

A later revision should score this lane as materially stronger only if one package adds most of the following together:
1. a non-trivial non-commuting QSG realization that stays causally disciplined without collapsing back to the commutative case,
2. a computationally workable transition-operator family rather than only partial algebraic constraints,
3. a controlled route from that dynamics toward manifoldlike continuum behavior,
4. some explicit matter or low-energy observable transport,
5. and at least one bounded empirical or inter-framework wedge that does not disappear into witness borrowing.

Without that package, the archive should preserve the gain as a bounded **causal-set QSG causality-computability audit** and stop there.

## Net result

The archive now has a better causal-set sentence available:

> causal-set / discrete-causality programs now have one explicit native quantum-growth stress test, but that is still weaker than a workable non-commuting dynamics, continuum recovery, or integrated completion-bid cash-out.

That keeps the lane genuinely sharpened without letting minimalist quantum-growth rhetoric overclose the archive. `REF-0169`
