# Candidate-native counterfactual versus candidate-native threshold closure gate

This document separates two stronger witness-package conditions that are still easy to conflate once a candidate starts looking genuinely experimental in its own variables.

1. **candidate-native counterfactual closure** — the candidate's own bridge variables define a menu of admissible alternative interventions, say when two interventions are the same or different across branches / frames / labs, and commit to contrastive record classes for experiments of our choosing rather than only one showcased intervention path;
2. **candidate-native threshold closure** — the candidate's own bridge variables also define the resolution, tolerance, coarse-graining, uncertainty, and observer-capacity budgets that say when those contrastive record classes are actually empirically distinguishable, adequately stable, or still under-resolved rather than borrowing those decisive thresholds from ordinary detector or metrology language;
3. **full local public witness closure** — beyond both, the candidate can sustain publicly durable local record production, rereadability, comparison, and challenge without silently outsourcing the decisive witness work.

A lane may earn real credit on layer 7 while still be weak on layer 8.
That is progress.
It is just not yet candidate-native threshold closure, much less full local public witness closure.

## Why this gate exists

Shared witness-gate scaffolding now lives in `docs/40-model/witness-closure-gate-family-frame.md`. This document only names the pair-specific inflation move below.

**a native menu of experiments and contrastive outcomes being retold as if the candidate therefore already knows, in its own variables, how sharp the distinctions are, what counts as adequate agreement, and when an apparent difference is still below the resolution budget.**

That inflation can happen in at least seven ways:
- a lane can specify alternative interventions while the decisive error bars, tolerance bands, and coarse-graining rules still live in ordinary detector or metrology language;
- a lane can describe different record classes while the criterion for saying they are empirically distinct, approximately the same, or under-resolved remains external;
- a lane can rely on exact formal distinctions in the candidate while any realistic observer has finite bandwidth, finite thermodynamic resources, or backreaction limits that are not internalized natively;
- a lane can present one preferred coarse-graining without saying why that coarse-graining, rather than another nearby one, is the empirically relevant native partition;
- a lane can showcase a native operator construction while the dynamic range, bias budget, or systematic-shift budget remains borrowed from ordinary sensor-side analysis;
- a lane can redescribe one experiment's outcomes in native variables while the adequacy threshold for agreement or reproducibility is still inherited from external practice;
- or a lane can advertise native contrastive commitments while staying silent about the observer-capacity or approximation regime in which those commitments are supposed to be read.

## The five-step candidate-native-threshold test

### 1. Native resolution variables, not just native alternatives

Before awarding candidate-native-threshold credit, the archive should ask whether the candidate says what sets its **empirical resolution scale** in its own variables rather than merely listing different admissible interventions.
At minimum, a lane should disclose:
- which native parameters, spectra, algebras, coarse observables, or state partitions set the relevant notion of distinguishability,
- which apparent differences are exact versus only effective or approximate,
- which parts of the resolution story still live in external detector-side terms,
- and what would make a finer distinction physically meaningless or unavailable.

A lane that wins only here still earns **candidate-native counterfactual credit**, not yet candidate-native threshold closure.

### 2. Native tolerance and coarse-graining budget

A lane earns more than counterfactual credit only when the candidate explains what tolerance or coarse-graining budget makes a record class stable enough to count as the same evidence-bearing fact.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what native coarse-graining map or equivalence class defines the rereadable record,
- whether that map is exact, approximate, state-dependent, branch-dependent, or regime-limited,
- how the candidate avoids smuggling tolerances back in through external detector calibration or analyst convention,
- and what breakdown regime makes the coarse-graining cease to support stable witness claims.

Without that route, apparent native contrastive structure may still inherit its decisive tolerance budget from outside the candidate.

### 3. Native uncertainty and significance rule

A lane earns stronger candidate-native-threshold credit only when the candidate says how uncertainty, bias, or finite-resource spread should be read in the candidate's own variables.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what native source of spread, fluctuation, or observer-capacity limit is expected,
- how the candidate distinguishes systematic shift from acceptable noise or approximation error,
- what native rule says that an observed difference is large enough to count against one branch of the counterfactual story,
- and which parts of the significance rule are still borrowed from ordinary laboratory statistics or metrology guidance.

Without those answers, the lane may have native alternatives but not a native story about when alternative outcomes are actually evidentially different.

### 4. Native under-resolution and breakdown declaration

A lane earns still stronger candidate-native-threshold credit only when it says which apparent differences are **under-resolved, regime-dependent, or observer-capacity-limited** in native terms.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- which distinctions fail because of finite thermodynamic resources, backreaction, or observer-capacity limits,
- which exact-looking differences become merely approximate after the candidate's own coarse-graining,
- which contexts force the theory to return an under-resolved or tolerance-bound verdict rather than a crisp contrast,
- and what empirical differences would expose an error in that under-resolution story.

Without that boundary, a candidate can look sharper than it is by quietly borrowing laboratory thresholds for when a distinction is taken seriously.

### 5. Native-to-lab threshold bridge must be explicit

A lane earns **candidate-native threshold closure** credit only when later users can connect actual laboratory uncertainty budgets, acceptance criteria, or reread tolerances back to one native threshold story.
At minimum, it should name:
- the native resolution variables,
- the native tolerance / coarse-graining budget,
- the native uncertainty or significance rule,
- the native under-resolution / breakdown declaration,
- the mapping from those native structures to actual external calibration, bias, and comparison practice,
- and the residual debt to full local public witness closure.

This is still not the same thing as full local public witness closure.
A lane may support candidate-native threshold closure quite well while still depending on ordinary laboratory infrastructure for the final public record ecology.
But that is a much smaller debt than merely having a native menu of interventions while borrowing the decisive “different enough to count” rule from outside.

## Common failure modes

1. **menu-without-metric inflation** — a native menu of experiments is retold as if it already fixed the evidential threshold for distinguishing their outcomes.
2. **imported-errorbar smuggling** — decisive tolerances, uncertainty bands, or adequacy thresholds still live in ordinary detector or metrology language outside the candidate.
3. **exactness theater** — exact formal distinctions are advertised even though the candidate itself only supports approximate localization, approximate clocks, or regime-limited observables.
4. **coarse-graining silence** — the account names record classes but never says which coarse-graining makes them stable or why nearby coarse-grainings do not matter.
5. **observer-capacity laundering** — finite resource, finite bandwidth, or backreaction limits are pushed into informal lab-side caveats rather than being represented in the candidate.
6. **threshold-free contrast** — the candidate says what would be different under another intervention but never says when that difference becomes evidentially decisive.
7. **theory-external adequacy fallback** — when pressure rises, the account reverts to ordinary calibration, fit, or acceptance language, revealing that the candidate's own threshold budget is not yet closed.

## Compact current readout

- **Giddings' observables review** makes the split visible by saying that gravity gives only approximate localization through standard dressing / gravitational splitting, which means a native contrast between alternatives is weaker than a native account of how sharply that contrast can be resolved.
- **Geng's quantum rods and clocks construction** sharpens the burden by noting that the reference system itself has quantum fluctuations, so a native local-operator story is weaker than a native tolerance story for how those fluctuations limit evidential distinctions.
- **Thiemann's relational-reference-frame analysis** makes the interpretation burden explicit by arguing that physical predictions require an operational definition of the reference frame in mathematics and by asking how precisely gauge-dependent fields are interpreted through relational observables.
- **Candeloro, Debarba, and Binder's intersubjectivity analysis** shows that finite thermodynamic resources bound agreement and bias and that perfect intersubjectivity is impossible, so native contrastive stories are weaker than a native resource-bounded threshold story.
- **Geiger et al.'s quantum metrology review** makes the laboratory side precise by showing that bias, variance, dynamic range, and systematic-error characterization are all part of substantiating an accuracy claim, so contrastive outcomes alone are weaker than explicit threshold and uncertainty budgets.

That does not refute current bridge programs or completion bids.
It just means they have not yet paid this stronger candidate-native-threshold debt.

## Net rule

Within the archive:
- candidate-native counterfactual closure earns real witness-package credit;
- but it does **not** yet count as candidate-native threshold closure;
- and candidate-native threshold closure does **not** yet count as full local public witness closure.

Any future completion bid or bridge-family claim that wants stronger witness credit should now say not only what alternative experiments and contrastive outcomes the candidate natively supports, but also how the candidate's own bridge variables define the resolution scale, tolerance budget, uncertainty rule, and under-resolution boundary that make those contrasts evidentially meaningful without collapsing back into borrowed detector or metrology language.
