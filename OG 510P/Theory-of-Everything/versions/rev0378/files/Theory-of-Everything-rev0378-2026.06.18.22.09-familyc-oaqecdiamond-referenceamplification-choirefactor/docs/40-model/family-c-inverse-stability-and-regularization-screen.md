## Compression verdict

The archive should now preserve four compact points at once:
- family C has real **route-local inverse-stability progress**, because current reconstruction work no longer stops at a bare existence proof; some routes now analyze injected measurement noise, filtering, or learned generalization behavior explicitly. `REF-0130`, `REF-0142`
- but route-local robustness is still weaker than **portable inverse stability**, because the success can depend on one filtering procedure, one training-noise recipe, one metric ansatz, one geometry family, one observable package, or one validity window. `REF-0130`, `REF-0142`
- and portable inverse stability is still weaker than **candidate-native identifiability closure**, because a stable output under one reconstruction protocol does not yet supply a native same/different rule, a native abstention rule, or a native account of what distinctions remain collapsed after regularization and prior choice are varied. `REF-0124`, `REF-0061`
- so family C now earns a sharper screen: **conditioned inverse success is real progress, but filtered or trained stability inside one route should not be retold as stable candidate-native identification.** `REF-0124`, `REF-0130`, `REF-0142`

## Why this document exists

The archive already blocks several over-fast promotions inside family C:
- restricted inverse success is not yet identifiability closure,
- route breadth is not yet common-target convergence,
- and thin-data recovery is not yet acquirable witness closure.

What it still lacked was one short rule for a different inflation move:

> this inverse route works under noisy data or after one learned / filtered regularization package, therefore the underlying candidate-level distinctions are now stably identified.

That step is still too fast.
A route can be informative, robust, and even practically useful while remaining **conditioned** on one reconstruction ecology.
This document prevents the archive from mistaking **route success under one stabilizer** for **native identifiability under admissible perturbation and prior variation**.

## The three checks the archive should now require

### 1. Noise handling is real progress, but still route-conditioned

Fan and Yang do more than show formal inversion from boundary correlators.
They explicitly study noisy measurements, truncation effects, and an iterative filtering procedure using the real and imaginary parts of the correlator tied together by Kramers-Kronig structure. `REF-0130`
That matters: family C now has at least one inverse route that tries to face noisy witness conditions rather than assuming exact data.

But the right archive score is still bounded.
Improvement after one filtering pipeline is evidence of **route-conditioned robustness**, not yet of candidate-native stable identification across admissible reconstruction ecologies.
The archive should ask what happens when the filter family, truncation rule, noise model, or observable preprocessing package is varied.

### 2. Learned inverse success is still prior / training-distribution conditioned

Kim's Transformer result is a second real advance.
It shows that the inverse RT relation can be learned with high accuracy inside a controlled `AdS_3` black-hole package, and it reports meaningful generalization beyond the exact training instances. `REF-0142`
But the same paper also makes the conditioning debt visible rather than removable:
- reliability is reported only inside a stated training range,
- prediction quality degrades sharply outside that range,
- the learned output can become unstable near some regions,
- and additive noise in training is described as crucial for generalization. `REF-0142`

So the archive should not translate “the model learned the inverse map” into “family C now has stable candidate-native identification.”
What it has instead is a new class of **training-conditioned inverse success**.
That is real progress.
It is still weaker than a route whose identifiability claim survives explicit variation of training distribution, architecture, regularizer, and ansatz family.

### 3. Stable outputs are still weaker than native same/different rules

Even if a family-C route survives the first two checks, the archive should still ask a stronger identifiability question:
what candidate-level distinctions are stably learnable, and which are still empirically collapsed once finite resolution, noise, filtering choice, regularization, training prior, and observer-map choice are all disclosed?

This is where the general identifiability gate still bites.
Saini et al. show that inverse success needs more than separability; it needs explicit inversion-stability control. `REF-0124`
Nomura and Ugajin show that stable predictions can coexist with unresolved microscopic structure. `REF-0061`
So family C should not receive candidate-native identifiability credit merely because one route appears numerically or statistically stable.

## False promotions the archive should reject

1. **filtered reconstruction is not yet route-independent stability**
   - Better output after one denoising or consistency filter does not yet show that the recovered bulk distinctions survive nearby reconstruction choices.

2. **good in-distribution performance is not yet portable inverse closure**
   - A learned inverse map that works inside one training window does not yet show stable recovery outside that window or across nearby ansatz families.

3. **stable output is not yet native same/different closure**
   - Numerically smooth predictions are still weaker than a native rule for which candidate-level differences are real, gauge, or empirically collapsed.

4. **regularized success is not yet abstention discipline**
   - A route that always outputs a geometry may still lack the right rule for when it should answer “non-identifiable under this data / noise / prior package.”

## What would change archive state

A future family-C lane should earn stronger inverse-stability or identifiability credit only if it adds, in one compact route:
1. a declared admissible perturbation model for noise, truncation, and finite resolution,
2. a declared variation study over nearby filters, regularizers, or training-prior families,
3. a declared in-distribution / out-of-distribution boundary with abstention behavior,
4. a declared statement of which candidate-level distinctions remain stable across those variations,
5. a declared statement of which distinctions collapse or become gauge / map / prior dependent,
6. and a native bridge from that stability statement back to candidate-level same/different claims rather than only route-local geometry output.

Without that six-part upgrade, family C should be scored as having **conditioned inverse stability gains** rather than stable candidate-native identification.

## Net result

The archive should now stop moving directly from “this family-C inverse route tolerates noise, filtering, or learned generalization” to “family C has stable candidate-native identification.”
That is too fast.
The stronger and still disciplined statement is:

**family C now has a first explicit inverse-stability / regularization screen: some current routes show meaningful robustness under one reconstruction ecology, but conditioned stability under one filter, training distribution, ansatz, or validity window is still weaker than portable inverse stability, abstention-ready identification, or candidate-native identifiability closure.** `REF-0124`, `REF-0061`, `REF-0130`, `REF-0142`
