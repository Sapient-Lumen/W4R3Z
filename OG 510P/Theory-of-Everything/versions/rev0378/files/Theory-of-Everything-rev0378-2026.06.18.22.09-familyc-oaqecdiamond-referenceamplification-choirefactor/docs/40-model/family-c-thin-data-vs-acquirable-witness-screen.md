## Compression verdict

The archive should now preserve four compact points at once:
- family C has broadened further toward **thin-data inverse reach**, because current routes can recover geometry-sensitive structure not only from entanglement variations, boundary correlators, or finite-window Green-function data, but now also from an analytic two-point-function-to-geometry construction that does not assume symmetry matching or asymptotically AdS geometry; `REF-0128`, `REF-0130`, `REF-0131`, `REF-0140`
- that broadening is real progress, because it shows family C is not confined to one heavily symmetry-protected inverse anecdote; `REF-0130`, `REF-0140`
- but thinner-data reconstruction is still weaker than **candidate-thick identification**, because the recovered bulk object remains route-built from a chosen sparse record class plus a declared dictionary / model package rather than from a closed candidate-native same/different rule; `REF-0128`, `REF-0130`, `REF-0140`
- and route existence is still weaker than **acquirable witness closure**, because some of the entanglement or geometry-sensitive quantities needed for holographic readout may already become computationally or operationally costly at the precision where the semiclassical story matters. `REF-0132`, `REF-0141`

So the correct archive move is now:
**award family C thinner-data inverse breadth, but do not promote that to acquirable witness closure, candidate-thick discrimination, or candidate-native identifiability closure.**

## Why this document exists

The archive already says that family C now has:
- real restricted inverse progress,
- a small method-diverse inverse portfolio,
- an internal triage for non-uniqueness,
- and an overlap-class screen for deciding when several routes are even talking about one recoverand.

What remained underspecified was a different inflation move:

> if geometry can be reconstructed from a very thin or selected data class, the witness burden must now be almost paid.

That step is still too fast.
A route may become more analytically impressive while still depending on sparse data classes, phenomenological dictionaries, route-specific observables, or precision regimes that do not yet amount to acquirable public evidence.
This document installs that missing brake.

## The new pressure: thin-data inverse reach

Nebabu, Qi, Tang, and Wang push family C in an important direction.
Starting from two-point data of an interacting Majorana generalized free field, they derive an analytic formula for a dual bulk geometry without requiring the usual symmetry-matching setup of AdS/CFT and without assuming the geometry is asymptotically AdS. `REF-0140`
That is genuine widening of family-C inverse ambition.
It means the archive can no longer summarize family C as if its inverse routes lived only inside one standard entanglement-wedge or symmetry-heavy package.

Taken together with Jokela et al., Fan and Yang, and Ran-Wu-Xian, the picture is now sharper:
family C can extract geometry-sensitive structure from several relatively thin or selected data classes. `REF-0128`, `REF-0130`, `REF-0131`, `REF-0140`

That is progress.
But it is still not the same thing as thick candidate-level identification.

## Why thin-data recovery is weaker than thick identification

A reconstruction route can be thin-data and still be highly nontrivial.
The archive should still ask what has actually been identified.

Entanglement-area variations near a fixed AdS background, boundary two-point correlators interpreted through inverse scattering, finite-window Green-function data, or generalized-free-field two-point data do not yet by themselves fix one fully declared candidate-level ontology. `REF-0128`, `REF-0130`, `REF-0131`, `REF-0140`
They recover some geometry-sensitive structure inside a route-specific package.
That is weaker than saying the lane has closed:
- which candidate-level differences count as the same target,
- which differences are only gauge or dictionary drift,
- which differences remain empirically collapsed,
- and which part of the recovered object is robust outside the chosen sparse record class.

So the archive should distinguish:
- **thin-data inverse reach** — a route extracts real geometry-sensitive structure from a sparse or selected record class,
- from **candidate-thick identification** — the lane declares what candidate-level distinctions are thereby fixed, which are not, and why.

Family C is now stronger on the first than on the second.

## Why route existence is weaker than acquirable witness closure

Even if a thin-data inverse route is mathematically explicit, the archive should still ask whether the needed data can actually be acquired at the relevant precision under realistic witness conditions.
Terashima already provides one brake: finite-`N` subregion reconstruction can fail beyond a logarithmic cutoff, so route-local reconstruction stories are weaker than regime-portable witness access. `REF-0132`
Wang, Jin, and Liu add a different brake: in their holographic entropy / cryptanalytic analysis, estimating Ryu-Takayanagi geodesic lengths or extracting the relevant entropy information can require exponential measurement or processing cost in the number of boundary degrees of freedom. `REF-0141`

Archive consequence:
a route that exists on paper is not yet the same as one that delivers a public, acquirable witness channel.
That matters because identifiability debt is about learnability under witness routes, not just about formal invertibility.

## Decision rule the archive should apply

When a future family-C result advertises geometry recovery from a thin or selected data class, apply this sequence before scoring it:

1. **data-class declaration** — what record class is actually being used: entanglement variations, two-point correlators, finite-window Green functions, generalized-free-field data, or something else?
2. **package declaration** — what dictionary, model class, asymptotic choice, symmetry assumption, or phenomenological ansatz is required before inversion starts?
3. **candidate-thickness check** — does success identify candidate-level differences, or only one route-local recoverand inside that package?
4. **acquisition check** — can the required data be acquired at the precision that makes the bulk conclusion meaningful, or is the route still only formal / computational / idealized?
5. **residual-collapse check** — after the previous steps, what still remains gauge, map-side drift, or empirically collapsed?

This sequence is intentionally conservative.
It prevents family C from converting sparse-data elegance directly into witness or identifiability credit.

## False promotions the archive should reject

1. **two-point or entropy access is not yet thick identification**
   - A sparse record class can support real inversion without yet fixing the candidate-level same/different rule.

2. **analytic inversion is not yet public witness access**
   - A closed-form or constructive route may still rely on precision, cost, or setup assumptions that make the witness channel non-acquirable.

3. **broader asymptotic reach is not yet portability closure**
   - Going beyond symmetry-matched AdS packages still leaves regime, map, and acquisition debt alive.

4. **geometry-sensitive output is not yet rival discrimination**
   - Recovering one effective bulk object from a thin data class is weaker than separating nearby candidate-level stories.

5. **thin-data breadth is not identifiability closure**
   - Family C can broaden its inverse reach while still failing candidate-native learnability and empirical-collapse closure.

## What would change archive state

A future family-C lane should earn stronger witness or identifiability credit only if it adds, in one compact route:
1. one declared sparse or thin data class,
2. one explicit package statement for the dictionary and model class being assumed,
3. one declared candidate-level same/different rule for what that data can identify,
4. one precision / noise / acquisition-cost statement for obtaining the required data,
5. one fixed-package residual-collapse statement,
6. and one public bridge from that route back to acquirable witness-bearing records.

Without that six-part upgrade, family C should be scored as wider on inverse reach than on witness acquisition or identifiability.

## Net result

The archive should now stop moving directly from “family C can reconstruct geometry from thin or selected data” to “family C is close to witness or identifiability closure.”
That is too fast.
The sharper and still conservative statement is:

**family C now has broader thin-data inverse reach, including a recent two-point-function-to-geometry route beyond symmetry-matched AdS settings, but that same broadening makes it even more important to distinguish sparse-data geometry extraction from candidate-thick identification and from acquirable witness closure.** `REF-0128`, `REF-0130`, `REF-0131`, `REF-0132`, `REF-0140`, `REF-0141`
