# Observer-dependent entropy invariants audit

This document keeps one fresh witness-side gain load-bearing without letting it sprawl.
It asks what the archive should count when observer-relative entropy assignments stop being pure slogan and gain explicit invariants plus bounded disagreement structure across quantum reference frames.

## Compression verdict

The archive should now hold one tighter sentence:

**frame-relative entropy work has crossed the line from one-description rhetoric to a first bounded transport discipline, because recent quantum-reference-frame results identify frame-independent diagonal Rényi invariants for ideal frames and state-independent bounds on observer disagreement for non-ideal frames; but that gain is still weaker than cross-frame public witness portability, public reference-standard closure, and local witness closure.** `REF-0161`

That means the right archive move is neither:
- "observer-dependent entropy is too frame-relative to score at all,"
- nor "entropy invariants solve witness portability."

The honest score is in between.

## What has genuinely improved

### 1. Some observer-relative entropy content is now invariant rather than purely perspective-phrased

For ideal quantum reference frames, de la Hamette identifies a family of diagonal Rényi entropies that are invariant under frame change for arbitrary subsystems containing a frame.
That means at least one class of entropy-bearing quantities can now be tracked across ideal frame changes without being reduced to "one observer said this, another observer said that." `REF-0161`

This is real progress over a bare single-frame construction.
It gives the archive a first compact example of a quantity class whose frame-relative behavior is structured strongly enough to support a bounded sameness claim.

### 2. Observer disagreement is now quantitatively decomposed rather than only asserted

For ideal frames, the observer-dependence of subsystem entropy is not merely noted; it is decomposed into single-frame coherence and inter-frame correlation terms.
So the archive now has a sharper description of *why* two observers can disagree about entropy assignments inside the same relational setup. `REF-0161`

That is stronger than generic prose about perspective dependence.
It gives a first compact bookkeeping rule for where the disagreement lives.

### 3. Non-ideal frames no longer break the story into total silence

For non-ideal frames, the same paper does not claim exact invariance, but it does give a state-independent bound on how large the entropy disagreement can become, in terms of effective relational Hilbert-space dimensions determined by the frame representation structure. `REF-0161`

That matters because the archive should score non-ideal-frame progress separately from exact ideal-frame symmetry.
A bounded disagreement claim is weaker than an invariant, but it is still stronger than unstructured observer relativity.

## What this does **not** buy

### 1. Not yet public witness portability

An invariant entropy family is not yet a transported *public fact* in the archive's stronger sense.
The candidate still owes:
- what physical observers are comparing,
- what calibration or synchronization package makes the comparison possible,
- what resolution floor keeps the comparison meaningful,
- and how those observers recover and reread the same public evidence rather than only computing frame-relative state functionals. `REF-0062`, `REF-0063`, `REF-0120`, `REF-0161`

So this gain belongs above bare one-frame access but below cross-frame public witness portability.

### 2. Not yet public reference-standard closure

Even if some entropy quantities are invariant across ideal frame changes, the archive still needs an account of how the shared comparison standard itself becomes public enough for multi-observer use.
The invariant alone does not tell us how clocks, axes, fragment adequacy, or comparison conventions stop being silently borrowed. `REF-0066`, `REF-0161`

So this gain is also weaker than public reference-standard closure.

### 3. Not yet local witness closure

Nothing in the new result says where durable records live, what stabilizes them, how they are reread, or why ordinary laboratory objectivity is now candidate-native rather than borrowed.
The result is about structured observer dependence of entropy assignments, not about record carriers or evidential custody. `REF-0015`, `REF-0016`, `REF-0017`, `REF-0161`

## The archive score change

The witness-side ladder should now keep one smaller distinction explicit:

1. **single-frame observer-relative access** — one observer package can define the relevant quantity;
2. **bounded frame-transport discipline** — some quantity class is invariant or quantitatively bounded under declared frame changes;
3. **cross-frame public witness portability** — multiple physical observers can recover and agree on the same coarse-grained public fact without a silent external referee;
4. **public reference-standard closure** — the shared comparison standard itself becomes public enough for multi-observer use;
5. **local witness closure** — records, stabilization, rereadability, and publicity are candidate-native.

The archive does **not** need to insert a new formal stage into the global subgate ladder for this.
But it should remember that stage 1 can now be sharpened internally by a first real transport-style result rather than a flat "some observer saw something" description.

## False promotions to reject

1. **frame-invariant entropy is not public evidence**
   - A mathematical invariant across ideal frame changes is not yet a public measurement standard.

2. **bounded disagreement is not solved objectivity**
   - A state-independent ceiling on disagreement is weaker than an account of how ordinary observers stably agree in practice.

3. **relational entropy structure is not record closure**
   - The result gives entropy bookkeeping, not record-carrier, stabilization, reread, or custody closure.

4. **gravitational relevance is not gravitational closure**
   - Potential implications for observer-dependent gravitational entropy do not yet settle witness debt in quantum gravity more broadly.

## What would move the archive further

A later revision should count the witness-side frame lane as materially stronger only if one compact route adds most of the following together:
1. an explicit fact-identity rule that survives declared frame changes,
2. a calibration / synchronization budget,
3. a coarse-graining or adequacy floor for comparison,
4. a public comparison-standard story rather than a merely invariant quantity,
5. and a record-carrier / rereadability route that turns the entropy object into public evidence.

Without that package, the archive should preserve the new gain as a bounded invariants-and-bounds result and stop there.

## Net result

The archive now has a better brake sentence for `OQ-0040` and `OQ-0041`:

> structured frame-relative entropy progress is real, but it still sits below public witness portability and well below public reference-standard or local witness closure.

That keeps the gain load-bearing without letting it inflate. `REF-0161`
