## Compression verdict

The archive should now preserve six compact points at once:
- family C is strong enough that **candidate ordering must now be scored together with policy stability**, because one route may look decisive only under one abstention / action wiring while nearby reasonable policies reorder or defer the same cases; `REF-0147`, `REF-0153`, `REF-0154`
- recent operational-profile work makes the first warning explicit: calibration rules with similar coverage can still induce materially different commitment, deferral, and decisive-error profiles, so “same coverage” is not yet “same decision behavior”; `REF-0147`
- recent decision-theoretic conformal work makes the second warning explicit: prediction sets do not by themselves determine the right downstream action, because the optimal action depends on the loss convention and on how costly out-of-set failure is; `REF-0154`
- recent conformal policy-control work sharpens the third warning: downstream policy choice is itself the object that must be controlled, and even safe interpolation between reference and optimized policies is a real statistical problem rather than a harmless presentation choice; `REF-0153`
- recent multi-distribution conformal work makes the fourth warning explicit: pooled calibration is valid only for the induced mixture distribution, while source-wise or ecology-wise reliability is a stronger requirement that can change the efficient accepted set materially; `REF-0155`
- the archive should therefore award family C a new screen: **a same/different verdict or candidate ordering earns stronger credit only if it remains stable across a declared reasonable policy family and across the heterogeneous source ecologies the route claims to cover, rather than appearing only under one tuned decision package.** `REF-0147`, `REF-0153`, `REF-0154`, `REF-0155`

## Why this document exists

The archive already distinguishes:
- restricted inverse success from identifiability closure,
- route breadth from common-target convergence,
- thin-data recovery from acquirable witness closure,
- conditioned robustness from portable inverse stability,
- surrogate recovery from candidate-native target recovery,
- point estimates from calibrated bundles,
- soft confidence from real abstention competence,
- and safer selective answering from retained discriminator-bearing hard-case coverage.

What it still lacked was one short rule for the next inflation move:

> this route has calibrated uncertainty, a defensible abstention rule, and some hard-case coverage, therefore its bulk ordering is now decision-ready and closer to candidate-native identifiability.

That step is still too fast.
A route can preserve coverage and still have its **candidate order, same/different verdict, or preferred bulk interpretation depend materially on the policy wiring** used to consume the uncertainty object.
Change the abstention threshold, loss asymmetry, deferral cost, or source weighting, and the “best” action can move.
If the ordering only survives under one such package, the archive has gained a useful decision ecology, not yet policy-grade identification.

The archive therefore needs one explicit distinction between:
- a route that is informative under one declared policy package,
- a route whose candidate ordering remains stable across a reasonable neighborhood of those policy choices,
- and a route that can additionally preserve that stability across the heterogeneous source ecologies it claims to cover.

## The five checks the archive should now require

### 1. One calibrated interface is weaker than policy-stable candidate order

Operational calibration is not yet action stability.
A family-C route may emit the same uncertainty object while different abstain / act conventions convert it into materially different candidate choices.
The archive should therefore require every route that claims stronger family-C identification to declare the **policy family** under which its candidate order is being read.

### 2. Similar coverage is weaker than similar operational behavior

Zwart matters here because the paper says the quiet part plainly: deployed systems are judged by more than coverage alone, and calibration choices with similar coverage can still induce materially different operational profiles in commitment, deferral, and decisive-error exposure. `REF-0147`

That is exactly why the archive needs the next screen.
Two family-C routes, or one route under two threshold choices, may both look “valid” while only one actually commits on the cases that determine the bulk order.
So the archive should now require a **policy-stability readout**, not only a coverage or selective-risk readout.

### 3. Prediction-set validity is weaker than decision-stable action quality

Wang and Dobriban matter because they make the decision-theoretic gap explicit: coverage by itself does not specify how to act, and the minimax-optimal downstream action depends on the loss convention and on the penalty carried by outcomes outside the set. `REF-0154`

That is exactly the family-C lesson here.
If one bulk candidate is preferred only because one loss profile punishes abstention, or because another profile heavily penalizes rare out-of-set failures, then the archive has learned something real about a policy package but not yet about policy-invariant candidate identification.
The route should therefore name which action / loss convention is doing the work.

### 4. One tuned policy is weaker than controlled policy selection

Prinster et al. matter because they push the issue from evaluation to policy choice itself: the downstream policy is often the real object of interest, and calibrating a more aggressive policy against a safe reference is a nontrivial statistical task rather than a cosmetic presentation choice. `REF-0153`

Family C now needs that same honesty.
If a bulk-ordering claim appears only after tuning one selector, acceptance threshold, or decision interpolation against the same ecology where the route is later praised, then the archive should score a **tuned policy package**, not yet policy-grade candidate identification.

### 5. Pooled-source validity is weaker than source-wise order stability

Yang and Jin matter because they make the multi-ecology warning exact: standard conformal calibration on pooled data is valid only for the induced mixture distribution, while source-wise uniform validity across heterogeneous environments is stronger and can force materially different prediction-set geometry. `REF-0155`

That is exactly the next family-C warning.
If a bulk ordering is stable only on a pooled boundary-data mixture, but changes when one source, observer ecology, cutoff class, or protocol family dominates, then the archive has not yet earned ecology-robust candidate ordering.
It has one mixture-valid readout.

## False promotions the archive should reject

1. **one calibrated action policy is not yet policy-stable candidate order**
   - A route can look decisive only because one specific abstain / act convention was chosen.

2. **similar coverage is not yet similar downstream choice**
   - Coverage-equivalent rules can still commit, defer, and err in materially different ways.

3. **one loss convention is not yet decision-grade identification**
   - A bulk verdict that flips when abstention cost or out-of-set penalty changes is still policy-fragile.

4. **pooled-source validity is not yet ecology-robust order stability**
   - A mixture-valid ordering can break when one source or protocol family dominates.

5. **policy-stable candidate order is not yet candidate-native identifiability closure**
   - Even stable downstream ordering can still borrow gauge, map, recoverand, and witness structure.

## What would change archive state

A future family-C lane should earn stronger identifiability credit only if it adds, in one compact route:
1. a declared action / loss convention and abstain / act wiring rather than only uncertainty output,
2. candidate-order or same/different reporting that remains stable across a declared reasonable neighborhood of those policy choices,
3. source-wise or ecology-wise reporting rather than only pooled-mixture validity,
4. evidence that the hard discriminator-bearing regimes remain covered under those nearby policy choices rather than only under one tuned selector,
5. a declared rule for what order reversals mean: empirical collapse, unresolved underdetermination, or a need for witness reacquisition,
6. and, ideally, one compact result showing that the same candidate order survives both policy variation and nearby source-ecology variation.

Without that six-part upgrade, family C should be scored as stronger at **policy-conditioned decision support**, not yet as stronger at candidate-native identification.

## Bottom line

The archive should now stop moving directly from “family C has calibrated abstention plus retained hard-case coverage” to “family C now has decision-ready candidate ordering.”

The stronger compact posture is:

**family C now has a sharper policy discipline, but that discipline earns real identification credit only if the resulting candidate order or same/different verdict survives reasonable action / loss choices and source-ecology variation rather than appearing only under one tuned decision package.** `REF-0147`, `REF-0153`, `REF-0154`, `REF-0155`
