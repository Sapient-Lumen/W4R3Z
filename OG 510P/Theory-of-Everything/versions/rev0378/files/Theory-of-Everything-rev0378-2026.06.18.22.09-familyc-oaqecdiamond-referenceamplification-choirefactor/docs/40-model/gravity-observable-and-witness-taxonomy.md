# Gravity observable and witness taxonomy

## Purpose

A route that says “observable,” “record,” or “witness” in gravity must say which kind. This taxonomy extends the gravitational-locality and frame-transport surfaces into a reusable checklist.

## Witness classes

| Class | Public strength | Main obstruction | Typical route ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| fixed-background local QFT observable | high in nongravitational QFT | fails as exact gravity-local gauge-invariant object | `S1`/`S2` import only |
| gravitationally dressed quasi-local observable | perturbatively meaningful | long-range dressing and subsystem non-independence | bounded `S2`/`S3` |
| relational observable | candidate-native potential | reference-system fluctuations and frame dependence | `S2` until transport closes |
| asymptotic observable | strong in scattering/boundary settings | finite-region/local witness may be absent | `S2`/`S3` corridor |
| subregion / edge-mode algebra | sharp finite-region structure | boundary/corner modes, type changes, gluing, complement subtleties | structured `S2`/`S3` |
| QES / island / code-subspace record | strong reconstruction corridor | code-space, state, cutoff, and decoder/publicness dependence | bounded Family-C `S3` |
| laboratory detector record | strong ordinary publicness | mediator-class, subsystem, and target-quotient underdetermination | discriminator `S3` pocket |
| frame-relative record | strong inside one frame | same-fact transport and public reference standard | `S2` access discipline |

## Use

Every route row should classify its `record_object` under this taxonomy or explicitly say why the taxonomy is inadequate. Failure to classify the witness object is an `earliest_blocker` for any route that invokes public records in gravity.

## Speculative pressure

The mature public witness in gravity may be an equivalence class across relational, dressed, asymptotic, edge, and detector-history descriptions rather than a sharply local object. That is not a license to weaken publicness. It makes the equivalence relation the central object.
