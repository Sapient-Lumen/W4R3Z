# Assimilation versus convergence gate

A completion bid should not earn the same archive-governance credit merely because a preserved fragment has been **assimilated** as recipient-native or shared-spine support and because that same overlap now licenses a **convergence-grade** claim that several lanes are approaching one deeper theory or common core.

Shared family scaffolding and ordered routing live in `docs/40-model/completion-bid-comparison-gate-family-frame.md` and `docs/40-model/completion-bid-comparison-stack.md`; this file keeps only the pair-specific split between assimilation-grade domestication and convergence-grade unity credit.

**Pair-specific split:** a fragment may become native in more than one home without yet proving that those homes are globally the same theory, or even that their local overlap extends beyond one bounded chart.

## What this gate separates

A completion bid can currently earn:
1. **assimilation-grade domestication** — an imported fragment may now be treated as recipient-native support or shared-spine property rather than as a tracked foreign dependency;
2. **convergence-grade unity credit** — the overlap now materially supports the stronger claim that several lanes are approaching one deeper theory, common core, or globally coherent atlas rather than merely sharing a bounded reusable chart.

This surface exists only to block the inflation from (1) to “the lanes are therefore really the same thing underneath.”

## Why the distinction matters

Shared fragments are common in deep theory search.
Multiple lanes can recover the same constraint, symmetry trace, effective structure, dual window, or benchmark map and still disagree sharply about ontology, dynamical closure, observables, or how the fragment extends outside the overlap patch.
A fragment can therefore be native in several homes while the homes themselves remain only partially coordinated.

Without a convergence gate, the archive can make three opposite mistakes.
It can treat any multi-host native overlap as evidence of one looming final theory.
It can collapse a bounded chart correspondence into global equivalence without asking what fails outside the chart.
Or it can overreact and refuse to learn from real structural overlap that should raise the pressure for a cleaner common-core map.

The archive therefore needs one more honesty layer:
**assimilation-grade domestication is not yet convergence-grade unity credit.**

## What convergence-grade unity credit would have to cash out

### 1. Overlap-scope disclosure

The archive should not call several lanes convergent until it says what actually overlaps.
At minimum, it should ask:
- which fragment, observable structure, constraint package, or dynamical relation is shared,
- which regimes, boundary conditions, approximations, or benchmark families define the overlap patch,
- whether the overlap concerns ontology, dynamics, observables, reconstruction, or only calculational convenience,
- and whether the shared piece remains load-bearing once nearby package choices are varied.

A native fragment with narrow scope can still matter.
It is just not yet deep convergence.

### 2. Translation-completeness disclosure

The archive should not treat overlap as convergence until the lanes can say how the overlap is translated.
At minimum, it should ask:
- whether the mapping is bidirectional or only one-way,
- whether states, observables, and dynamics all translate or only a selected sector,
- whether the mapping preserves explanatory role or merely reproduces outputs,
- and whether the translation is local / patchwise, asymptotic, approximate, or genuinely global.

Patchwise translation can still be valuable.
It is just weaker than unity.

### 3. Residual remainder disclosure

The archive should not narrate shared fragments as one-theory evidence while the disagreement remainder remains invisible.
At minimum, it should ask:
- what each lane still says differently outside the shared fragment,
- whether rival ontological commitments remain materially live,
- which auxiliary assumptions are still lane-specific,
- and whether the shared fragment leaves a large underdetermined remainder untouched.

Large remainder means the overlap is real but not yet identity-bearing.

### 4. Global-gluing disclosure

The archive should not turn local chart overlap into global convergence without asking how the patches glue.
At minimum, it should ask:
- whether several local correspondences compose consistently,
- whether transition rules are mutually compatible across more than one patch,
- whether the alleged common core extends beyond one benchmark family,
- and whether any candidate bundle structure remains nontrivial rather than collapsing to one global theory only by wishful compression.

Local overlap is often the beginning of convergence work, not its completion.

### 5. Rival-survival disclosure

The archive should not award unity credit while nearby rivals can host the same overlap equally well.
At minimum, it should ask:
- whether more than one serious lane still supports the fragment natively,
- whether the shared fragment lowers or preserves rival underdetermination,
- whether the overlap differentiates donor, recipient, and third-party lanes at all,
- and whether the right routing is still “shared infrastructure” rather than “deep unification.”

If rival survival stays wide, convergence language should stay weak.

### 6. Convergence-grade unity credit

An assimilated fragment earns convergence-grade unity credit only when:
- overlap scope is explicit enough that the archive knows which patch is shared and which is not,
- translations are complete enough that the shared structure is more than output mimicry,
- the disagreement remainder is bounded enough that the overlap meaningfully reduces underdetermination,
- local correspondences show some credible route to global gluing rather than isolated chart luck,
- and rival survival is narrowed enough that the overlap points beyond generic shared infrastructure.

A fragment may deserve assimilation while still failing convergence.
That is not a loss; it is chart honesty.

## Common failure modes

1. **Overlap-to-unity laundering** — several lanes host one fragment and the archive calls that deep convergence by default.
2. **Patch-to-global inflation** — a local or approximate correspondence is narrated as full theory identity.
3. **Remainder erasure** — the unexplained disagreement outside the shared fragment disappears from the story.
4. **Shared-core capture** — common infrastructure is mistaken for proof that one lane has won or that all lanes are secretly one.
5. **Bundle wishcasting** — the archive speaks as if local transition maps already glue into one coherent global model space.

## Compact current readout

| Convergence question | What the archive should ask | Failure mode if skipped |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Overlap scope** | Exactly which fragment and regime are genuinely shared? | tiny overlap retold as deep agreement |
| **Translation completeness** | Do states, observables, and dynamics translate, or only outputs in one patch? | mimicry retold as identity |
| **Residual remainder** | What still differs materially outside the overlap? | underdetermination erased |
| **Global gluing** | Do local correspondences compose into a broader coherent atlas? | chart luck retold as unity |
| **Rival survival** | Can nearby rivals host the same overlap equally well? | shared infrastructure mistaken for convergence |

## Immediate archive rules

1. **Do not convert assimilation-grade domestication directly into convergence-grade unity credit.**
2. **Require overlap-scope and translation-completeness disclosure before shared fragments count as evidence that several lanes are approaching one deeper theory.**
3. **Keep residual disagreement visible; a bounded common patch is not yet lane identity.**
4. **Route multi-host overlap first to shared infrastructure or common-core pressure unless global-gluing evidence is actually named.**
5. **Keep this gate subordinate to the import-versus-assimilation gate and upstream of any claim that cross-lane overlap now supports one deep ontology, one final common core, or inevitable theoretical merger.**

## Net result

The archive now has a smaller and more honest sentence available:

> a completion bid may assimilate the same fragment in several lanes while still failing convergence-grade unity credit until overlap scope, translation completeness, disagreement remainder, global gluing, and rival survival are faced honestly.

That blocks one more route by which local overlap can be mistaken for final unification without widening the archive.
