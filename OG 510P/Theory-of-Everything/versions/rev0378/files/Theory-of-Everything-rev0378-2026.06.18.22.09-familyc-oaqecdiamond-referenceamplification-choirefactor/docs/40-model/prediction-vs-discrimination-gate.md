# Prediction-versus-discrimination gate

Purpose: split the **exposed risky-surplus** layer inside `docs/40-model/fit-vs-prediction-gate.md` one step further.
A completion bid should not earn the same observed-parameter credit merely because it leaves **some risky low-energy consequence exposed** and because that consequence would **actually discriminate the lane from nearby rival completion bids, packages, or benchmark families**.
Shared family scaffolding and ordered routing live in `docs/40-model/completion-bid-comparison-gate-family-frame.md` and `docs/40-model/completion-bid-comparison-stack.md`; this file keeps only the pair-specific split between exposed risky surplus and rival-discriminating surplus.

## Compression verdict

The archive should now score claimed predictive success in **three distinct test-comparison layers**:

1. **exposed surplus** — after fitting or selection, the lane still leaves additional nontrivial relations, exclusions, or forecast windows genuinely vulnerable;
2. **rival-discriminating surplus** — at least some of that exposed surplus would materially change the relative standing of the lane against named nearby rivals, rather than remaining a shared or generic consequence;
3. **rival-set disclosure** — the lane states plainly which rival packages, benchmarks, or background baselines are being compared, which consequences are shared, and what experimental resolution would actually separate them.

A lane may earn real credit on layer 1 while still being weak on layers 2 and 3.
That is still progress.
It is just not decisive theory-choice traction.

## Why this gate exists

`docs/40-model/fit-vs-prediction-gate.md` already blocked one inflation move:
**an accommodated fit pretending to be a risky prediction.**

A further inflation move remained too easy:
**a risky prediction pretending to be a discriminating wedge.**

That confusion appears whenever a completion bid is praised because it:
- leaves some parameter relation, spectrum window, or cosmological forecast exposed,
- survives one future test better than a null baseline,
- or can tell a story about what would count as empirical contact,

and that real but limited gain is then narrated as though it would uniquely favor the lane over nearby rivals.

Often the exposed consequence is still:
- shared by many nearby packages,
- too coarse to separate rival mechanisms,
- dependent on experimental precision not yet specified,
- or only discriminating against a straw baseline rather than the strongest nearby alternatives.

This gate keeps those credits separate.

## The three-step discrimination test

### 1. Rival accounting

A lane earns **exposed-surplus** credit when it leaves something genuinely vulnerable after the fitting path is disclosed.
Before turning that into stronger credit, the archive should ask:
- which nearby rivals or benchmark families are actually under comparison,
- which of them would survive the same outcome,
- and whether the comparison is against a real rival set or only against an empty / background baseline.

A prediction without named adversaries may still be interesting.
It is not yet strong discriminatory evidence.

### 2. Discriminating wedge

A lane earns **rival-discriminating** credit only when some exposed consequence would materially separate it from nearby alternatives.
At minimum, the archive should ask whether the lane leaves behind any of:
- a sign, ordering, or correlation that nearby rivals would not share,
- an excluded window that actually removes live competitors,
- cross-sector transport that forces a relation absent in nearby packages,
- or a forecast whose confirmation or failure would visibly reorder the rival set.

A lane should get more credit for such wedges than for exposed surplus alone.
A risky forecast that many rivals also allow is still exposure, but not yet decisive separation.

### 3. Rival-set disclosure

If the lane does **not** yet provide a strong discriminating wedge, it should say so explicitly.
The archive should ask for:
- which rivals were compared,
- which advertised consequences are shared across those rivals,
- which projected measurements would actually have separating power,
- and which package, truncation, or benchmark choices still decide whether the wedge survives.

The archive should not punish honest disclosure.
It should punish adversary laundering.

## Common failure modes

1. **Generic-surplus inflation** — a risky but widely shared consequence is narrated as unique support for one lane.
2. **Adversary opacity** — the lane never states which nearby rivals are actually under comparison.
3. **Null-result overclaim** — exclusion of a broad background or straw baseline is retold as decisive support for one completion bid.
4. **Resolution blindness** — an alleged wedge depends on measurement precision or auxiliary assumptions that are never named.

## Immediate archive rules

1. **Do not let risky-but-rival-shared consequences count as decisive support.**
2. **Do not let exclusion of a null baseline count as lane-specific confirmation unless real rivals are also separated.**
3. **Require a named rival set, comparison observable, and approximate separating resolution whenever a lane claims discriminating evidence.**
4. **Give extra credit only for wedges that survive nearby package, truncation, or benchmark variation and would actually reorder the rival set.**
5. **Keep this gate subordinate to the fit-versus-prediction gate and the canonical discriminator surfaces.**
   This surface sharpens comparison inside claimed predictive success; it does not replace the separate debts on package disclosure, observables, witness closure, or cosmological burden.

## Net result

The archive now has a smaller and more honest sentence available:

> a completion bid may leave risky consequences exposed and still be weak on genuinely rival-discriminating empirical traction.

That sentence blocks another prestige inflation move without widening the archive.
