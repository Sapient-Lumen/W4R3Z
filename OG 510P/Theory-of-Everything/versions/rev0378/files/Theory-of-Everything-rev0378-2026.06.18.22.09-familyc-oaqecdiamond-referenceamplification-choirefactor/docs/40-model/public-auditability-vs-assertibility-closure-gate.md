# Public auditability versus assertibility-closure gate

Purpose: split the archive's **witness-package progress** one step further.
A candidate should not earn the same witness credit merely because it can **make the reasons, thresholds, state context, and action lineage behind interventions public enough for outside audit and replay** and because it can **treat categorical public claims as assertable only when backed by a publicly inspectable, contestable entitlement certificate, with a first-class abstention state when that certificate is missing**.
Those are related, but they are not the same achievement.

## Compression verdict

Inside the archive's witness package, once a lane has already moved beyond drift-resilient autonomy and public auditability, the current score should now distinguish three nested layers:

1. **public auditability closure** — outsiders can reconstruct why a particular intervention, escalation, remapping, or refusal happened under the policy, thresholds, state snapshot, and action lineage that existed at decision time;
2. **assertibility closure** — the lane's public assert / deny / report interface is itself disciplined by publicly inspectable and contestable entitlement artifacts, declared scope, and a mandatory abstention or undetermined state when entitlement is missing;
3. **local public witness closure** — a lane can say where the physical records live, what stabilizes them, how they are reread, why they remain public evidence, and why that empirical loop is not mainly outsourced.

A lane may earn real credit on layer 1 while still being weak on layer 2.
That is progress.
It is just not yet assertibility closure, much less full witness closure.

## Why this gate exists

Shared witness-gate scaffolding now lives in `docs/40-model/witness-closure-gate-family-frame.md`. This document only names the pair-specific inflation move below.

**auditable decision provenance being retold as if categorical public claims were therefore already warranted, challenge-ready, and properly abstention-bounded.**

That inflation can happen in at least five ways:
- a lane can expose replayable provenance for past actions while still presenting categorical public verdicts without a public rule stating when assert / deny / undetermined is allowed;
- a lane can publish explanation artifacts or claim-linked evidence while the entitlement threshold for categorical speech remains hidden in private dashboards, tacit norms, or non-exported scoring logic;
- a lane can let outsiders inspect why a claim was made after the fact while denying them a challengeable certificate that could have blocked the claim at the time of issue;
- a lane can support revisions internally while leaving the public interface binary, so unresolved or contradictory evidence still gets laundered into fluent yes / no language;
- or a lane can treat abstention as a cosmetic UX choice rather than a first-class epistemic status required whenever no forcing witness is available.

## The five-step assertibility test

### 1. Public claim unit and declared scope

Before awarding assertibility credit, the archive should ask whether the public output unit is explicit enough to carry entitlement.
At minimum, a lane should disclose:
- what the assertable public unit is: claim, verdict, alarm, classification, fit report, or theory-side conclusion,
- what declared scope, operating assumptions, or boundary conditions govern that unit,
- whether the scope is public at issuance time rather than reconstructed later,
- and which parts of the output remain commentary, speculation, or advisory prose rather than entitlement-bearing claims.

A lane that wins only here earns **public auditability credit**, not yet assertibility closure.

### 2. Entitlement certificate over mere explanation

A lane earns more than auditability credit only when it can bind each public categorical claim to a publicly inspectable and contestable entitlement artifact.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what evidence bundle, certificate, bound, verifier output, or warrant object licenses assertion or denial,
- whether the artifact is rich enough for an outsider to inspect rather than just trust a narrative explanation,
- how claim–evidence links expose contradictions, insufficiencies, or unresolved branches rather than only supporting snippets,
- and which parts of the justification still live in hidden heuristics, tacit operator judgment, or unexported internal scores.

Without that route, apparent assertibility may still be audit-friendly rhetoric rather than warranted public speech.

### 3. Mandatory abstention or undetermined semantics

A lane earns stronger assertibility credit only when lack of entitlement blocks categorical public speech.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what public status vocabulary exists beyond assert / deny,
- under what conditions the system must abstain, defer, or return undetermined,
- whether abstention is mandatory when the entitlement artifact is missing or contradictory rather than merely optional,
- and whether the public interface preserves the difference between insufficient warrant and negative evidence.

Without those answers, apparent assertibility may still be overconfident output with better post-hoc logs.

### 4. Challenge, revision, and repair path

A lane earns still stronger assertibility credit only when public challenge can revise public standing.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- how an outside challenge, contradiction, or newly surfaced artifact reaches the entitlement layer rather than only a help desk or private operator channel,
- whether the lane can replay or re-evaluate the same public claim under changed evidence or tighter policy without hand reconstruction,
- how revised standing is surfaced publicly: corrected, withdrawn, downgraded to undetermined, or left unchanged with reasons,
- and whether repair artifacts become durable regression checks rather than one-off manual exceptions.

Without that route, apparent assertibility may still be transparent overclaiming rather than challenge-ready public speech.

### 5. Closure boundary

A lane earns **assertibility closure** credit only when it can say how public categorical claims remain answerable to challengeable warrants rather than fluent confidence.
At minimum, it should name:
- the public claim unit and declared scope,
- the entitlement certificate,
- the abstention / undetermined rule,
- the challenge and revision path,
- the repair / regression route for preventing repeated overclaim,
- and the remaining debt to full local record-carrier, rereadability, and objectivity closure.

This is still not the same thing as full local witness closure.
A lane may be strongly assertibility-disciplined while still borrowing the public record carrier, rereadability, or objectivity side from ordinary laboratory witness structure.

## Common failure modes

1. **auditability-to-assertibility inflation** — a replayable log is retold as if categorical public claims were therefore warranted.
2. **certificate-free confidence** — public yes / no outputs appear without an inspectable entitlement artifact.
3. **scope laundering** — a claim is asserted broadly while the actual entitlement only covers a narrow regime, benchmark, or declared context.
4. **abstention laundering** — the system could have returned undetermined, but the public interface suppresses that state in favor of a forced verdict.
5. **private-challenge bottlenecks** — disputes can be raised only through insider channels, not through the same public evidence path that justified the claim.
6. **repair without memory** — a challenged claim is corrected once, but the correction never becomes a durable regression or contract check.

## Compact current readout

- **Recent epistemic-interface work** makes the stronger burden explicit by arguing that categorical public outputs should appear only when a publicly inspectable and contestable certificate of entitlement exists, and otherwise must surface an explicit undetermined state rather than a forced verdict.
- **Recent claim-level auditability work** clarifies what partial payment looks like by treating claim–evidence links, contradiction transparency, and audit effort as first-class objects rather than fluent summaries plus generic logs.
- **Recent contestability work in explainable AI** makes clear that explanation is not enough: challenge, review, revision, and recourse must be designed as explicit mechanisms rather than hoped-for side effects of transparency.
- **Recent verified decision-pipeline work** shows a stronger practical pattern in which missing or contradictory support triggers targeted repair or abstention rather than confident emission, and where repair artifacts become part of the ongoing check surface.

That does not refute current observer-relative or bridge-theory programs.
It just means they have not yet paid this stronger assertibility debt.

## Net rule

Within the archive:
- public auditability closure earns real witness-package credit;
- but it does **not** yet count as assertibility closure;
- and neither of those automatically counts as local public witness closure.

Any future completion bid or bridge-family claim that wants stronger witness credit should now say not only how the reasons for interventions become public enough for audit, but how categorical public claims are gated by declared scope, challengeable entitlement certificates, mandatory abstention when support is missing, and a revision path that can publicly change standing rather than merely annotate the past.
