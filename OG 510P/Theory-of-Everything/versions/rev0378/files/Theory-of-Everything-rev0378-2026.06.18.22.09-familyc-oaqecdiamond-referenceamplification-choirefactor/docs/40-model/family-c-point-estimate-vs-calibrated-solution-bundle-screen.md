## Compression verdict

The archive should now preserve four compact points at once:
- family C has reached a stage where **calibration discipline** matters, not just existence or accuracy, because current routes increasingly return explicit bulk geometries or effective model functions from boundary-side data through learned inverse RT maps, physics-informed reconstructions, or data-constrained neural-ODE calibrations. `REF-0142`, `REF-0143`
- but point confidence under strong physical priors is not yet the same as calibrated identification, because recent Bayesian holography / PINN work shows that physical constraints can sharply collapse the posterior and generate **apparent overconfidence** that standard confidence language does not interpret cleanly. `REF-0144`
- and recent UQ work on physics-informed inverse problems shows a stronger pattern than forced point output: inverse routes can instead represent **families of admissible solutions** and their uncertainty through solution bundles and Bayesian uncertainty estimates rather than pretending one answer is already forced. `REF-0145`
- so family C now earns a sharper screen: **one geometry-like point estimate inside one reconstruction ecology is weaker than a calibrated solution bundle or an explicit abstention rule, and should not be retold as candidate-native identifiability closure.** `REF-0142`, `REF-0144`, `REF-0145`

## Why this document exists

The archive already blocks several overpromotions in the family-C lane:
- restricted inverse success is not yet identifiability closure,
- route breadth is not yet common-target convergence,
- thin-data recovery is not yet acquirable witness closure,
- conditioned robustness is not yet portable inverse stability,
- and good route-local recovery is not yet candidate-native target recovery.

What it still lacked was one short rule for the next inflation move:

> this inverse route returns one sharp geometry-like answer with good accuracy, therefore the underlying bulk verdict is now calibrated and identified.

That step is still too fast.
A route can be impressive, useful, and even generalizing while still producing a **forced point estimate** from one posterior-collapse mechanism, one prior stack, or one training ecology.
The archive therefore needs one explicit distinction between:
- a route that outputs one answer because the data genuinely force one answer,
- a route that should instead return a calibrated **bundle** or interval of admissible answers,
- and a route that should simply **abstain** because the data / regime / prior package does not settle the question.

## The four checks the archive should now require

### 1. Point output is not yet calibrated identification

Kim's Transformer paper is a real advance: after one training phase, it reconstructs the blackening function inside a declared `AdS_3` metric ansatz, performs well on unseen examples, and even extrapolates to some horizonless cases. `REF-0142`
But the same paper also makes the boundedness clear rather than removable:
- the training data are generated inside one declared ansatz and one black-hole package,
- added white noise is part of what makes the inverse relation learnable,
- reliability is stated only within a training range,
- outside that range prediction quality degrades sharply,
- and instability appears in some extrapolation regions. `REF-0142`

So the archive should no longer move directly from “the route returns one accurate geometry” to “the route now has calibrated identifiability.”
A point estimate may still be the output style of one trained inverse machine rather than the justified epistemic object.

### 2. Physics-constrained confidence can hide posterior collapse

Landgren and Taylor are especially useful here because they study a holography-adjacent PINN problem — entangling surfaces with limited boundary data — and then ask what apparent overconfidence means under strong physical priors. They argue that standard overconfidence language is not automatically appropriate in B-PINNs, because soft or hard physics constraints can collapse the posterior distribution and make uncertainty artificially small even when what is really happening is **physics-driven posterior pinching** rather than data-forced identification. They therefore introduce information-density and physics-constraint-coupling diagnostics to tell where confidence is warranted and where it is merely prior-induced. `REF-0144`

That matters for family C now.
Several current routes live inside strong geometry, asymptotics, differential-equation, or regularity priors.
So apparent confidence can no longer be read naively as evidence that the candidate-native bulk target itself has been identified.

### 3. Inverse maturity should include solution bundles, not just one winner

Flores et al. are valuable because they state the stronger alternative explicitly.
They note that PINNs are not naturally equipped with robust uncertainty quantification, and then combine Bayesian neural networks, error-aware variance, and **solution bundles** so inverse problems can be expressed as statistical analysis over multiple admissible solutions and parameter values rather than one forced trajectory. `REF-0145`

The archive should not silently import their exact machinery into holography.
But it should import the epistemic lesson:
**a maturing inverse lane should increasingly know when the right object is a calibrated family of admissible bulk answers, not a single geometry-like point estimate.**

### 4. Calibrated bundles are still weaker than candidate-native same/different rules

Even after the first three checks, a calibrated bundle is still weaker than candidate-native identifiability closure.
A route may provide a good posterior family, confidence interval, or abstention zone while still borrowing:
- the observer / dictionary map,
- the gauge / frame equivalence rule,
- the surrogate recoverand,
- the measurement-access assumptions,
- or the criterion for which bulk differences count as empirically distinct.

So the stronger archive move is not “bundle equals closure.”
It is only this:
**forced point output no longer counts as the default epistemic target; family C should increasingly be scored by whether it can return calibrated bundles, disclosed uncertainty regions, or explicit abstention when the data / prior package does not determine one candidate-level answer.** `REF-0124`, `REF-0061`, `REF-0144`, `REF-0145`

## False promotions the archive should reject

1. **one confident geometry is not yet calibrated identification**
   - A route can output one best-fit bulk story while uncertainty remains strongly prior-shaped.

2. **physics-driven posterior collapse is not yet data-forced uniqueness**
   - Hard or soft constraints can narrow uncertainty without showing that the data alone fix the candidate-level answer.

3. **good in-range performance is not yet bundle-aware epistemics**
   - Reliability inside one declared domain is weaker than a declared uncertainty family or abstention rule outside that domain.

4. **point accuracy is not yet honest non-identification behavior**
   - A route that always returns a geometry may still be weaker than a route that sometimes says “several answers remain admissible” or “underdetermined under this package.”

5. **solution bundle is not yet candidate-native identifiability closure**
   - Even a well-calibrated posterior family can still borrow same/different criteria from outside the candidate-native bridge variables.

## What would change archive state

A future family-C lane should earn stronger identifiability credit only if it adds, in one compact route:
1. a declared calibration study for the route's predictive intervals, posterior family, or confidence regions,
2. an explicit distinction between data-driven uncertainty and prior / physics-constraint-induced posterior collapse,
3. a declared bundle or interval of admissible recoverands when one unique answer is not forced,
4. a declared abstention or underdetermination rule when the route leaves the trusted regime,
5. a declared mapping from that bundle / abstention structure to candidate-level same/different claims,
6. and a declared statement of which ambiguities are gauge, map drift, surrogate debt, or genuine empirical collapse.

Without that six-part upgrade, family C should be scored as stronger at producing disciplined point estimates inside one ecology, not yet as offering calibrated candidate-native identification.

## Net result

The archive should now stop moving directly from “family C can output one accurate bulk geometry-like answer” to “family C has therefore identified the candidate-native bulk story.”
That is too fast.
The stronger and still disciplined statement is:

**family C now needs a point-estimate-versus-calibrated-solution-bundle screen: current inverse routes can return impressive bulk outputs inside declared reconstruction ecologies, but recent Bayesian / PINN work shows that physics constraints can collapse posteriors and create overconfidence while solution-bundle methods point toward families of admissible answers rather than one forced geometry, so point output alone is still weaker than calibrated abstention-ready identification or candidate-native identifiability closure.** `REF-0124`, `REF-0061`, `REF-0142`, `REF-0144`, `REF-0145`
