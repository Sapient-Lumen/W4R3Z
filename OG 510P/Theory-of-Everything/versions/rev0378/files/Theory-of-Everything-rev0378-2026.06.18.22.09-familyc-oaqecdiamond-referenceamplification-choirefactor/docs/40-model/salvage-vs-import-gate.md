# Salvage versus import gate

A completion bid should not earn the same archive-governance credit merely because some lane has left behind a **salvage-worthy fragment** and because that same fragment may now be **imported into another lane or the shared spine as native explanatory credit**.

Shared family scaffolding and ordered routing live in `docs/40-model/completion-bid-comparison-gate-family-frame.md` and `docs/40-model/completion-bid-comparison-stack.md`; this file keeps only the pair-specific split between salvage-grade retention and import-grade transfer.

**Pair-specific split:** a fragment may survive defeat without yet being licensed to cross the border cleanly.

## What this gate separates

A completion bid can currently earn:
1. **salvage-grade retention** — after lane-level defeat or demotion, some bounded fragment still deserves preservation as a reusable tool, benchmark, constraint, or mechanism relic;
2. **import-grade transfer** — that fragment can be brought into another lane, bridge family, or the shared spine with its role, scope, and inherited debt stated explicitly.

This surface exists only to block the inflation from (1) to “the fragment now belongs to canon.”

## Why the distinction matters

A retained relic is not automatically portable.
Something can survive because it is mathematically elegant, historically useful, or locally constraining while still failing to travel cleanly into a different explanatory package.
Import can fail for several reasons:
- the fragment only works inside the defeated lane's ontology or global package,
- the fragment carries hidden approximations or regime restrictions that the recipient lane has not inherited honestly,
- the fragment's explanatory role changes when moved, so what survives is no longer the same achievement,
- or the fragment is now generic shared infrastructure rather than proprietary credit for any one recipient.

Without an import gate, the archive can make two opposite mistakes.
It can preserve fragments but then quietly canonize them without a transfer account.
Or it can refuse transfer altogether and lose real cross-lane inheritance that should sharpen the spine.

The archive therefore needs one more honesty layer:
**salvage-grade survival is not yet import-grade canonization.**

## What import-grade transfer would have to cash out

### 1. Provenance disclosure

The archive should not import a fragment without saying where it came from and what was defeated around it.
At minimum, it should ask:
- which lane the fragment came from,
- whether the fragment was preserved as an active tool, benchmark relic, negative-constraint note, or memorial residue,
- which parts of the parent lane were rejected, retired, or left open,
- and whether the fragment was already shared infrastructure before the parent lane weakened.

If provenance is blurry, import usually becomes prestige laundering.

### 2. Transfer mechanism disclosure

A surviving fragment should not be treated as portable until the archive says how it crosses lanes.
At minimum, it should ask:
- whether the transfer is by approximation, limiting recovery, reinterpretation, benchmark inheritance, or pure mathematical reuse,
- whether the recipient lane reproduces the fragment natively or merely borrows it instrumentally,
- whether the fragment keeps the same subject matter after transfer,
- and whether any extra assumptions are needed to make the transfer work.

Import without transfer mechanics is usually just analogy wearing formal clothes.

### 3. Debt carry-over discipline

The archive should not let fragments migrate while leaving their unpaid burdens behind.
At minimum, it should ask:
- which domain limits, calibration choices, or hidden package assumptions traveled with the fragment,
- which debts were paid off by the recipient lane and which remain merely borrowed,
- whether the fragment's old witness, observable, or parameter debts still matter after transfer,
- and whether the transfer quietly reintroduces exactly the assumptions that helped defeat the parent lane.

A fragment can be useful and still arrive with quarantined debt.
That is still transfer, but not clean canonization.

### 4. Credit-routing discipline

The archive should not let import blur who now deserves explanatory credit.
At minimum, it should ask:
- whether the fragment now supports the recipient lane's native claims or only its tooling,
- whether multiple recipient lanes can equally inherit the same fragment,
- whether the imported item changes any lane ranking or only enlarges common infrastructure,
- and whether the archive is mistaking imported leverage for evidence that the donor lane was broadly right after all.

Cross-lane reuse can be real without restoring donor-lane prestige.

### 5. Import-grade transfer

A salvaged fragment earns import-grade transfer only when:
- provenance is explicit enough that the archive knows what was inherited and what was not,
- the transfer mechanism is named precisely enough that the fragment's role in the recipient lane is intelligible,
- carried debt is either paid, bounded, or explicitly quarantined,
- and explanatory credit is routed cleanly: donor lane, shared infrastructure, recipient lane, or mixed benchmark inheritance.

A fragment may deserve preservation while still failing import.
That is not waste; it is border discipline.

## Common failure modes

1. **Salvage-to-canon laundering** — a retained relic is treated as part of the shared spine with no transfer account.
2. **Prestige backflow** — the donor lane regains broad credit merely because one fragment remains useful elsewhere.
3. **Debt amnesty by relocation** — regime limits, witness debt, or hidden package assumptions vanish when a fragment changes owners.
4. **Instrumental/native confusion** — a recipient lane borrows a tool but narrates that tool as native explanatory achievement.
5. **Shared-infrastructure capture** — a broadly reusable mathematical device is presented as proprietary confirmation for one lane.

## Compact current readout

| Transfer question | What the archive should ask | Failure mode if skipped |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Provenance** | What exactly survived, from which donor lane, and at what preservation level? | donor-lane prestige laundering |
| **Mechanism** | Is the fragment rederived, reinterpreted, approximated, or merely borrowed instrumentally? | analogy posing as inheritance |
| **Debt carry-over** | Which regime, witness, package, or parameter debts travel with the fragment? | hidden debt amnesty |
| **Credit routing** | Does the fragment support donor memory, recipient claims, or only shared infrastructure? | false native credit |

## Immediate archive rules

1. **Do not convert salvage-grade retention directly into import-grade canonization.**
2. **Require provenance and transfer-mechanism disclosure before any salvaged fragment enters another lane or the shared spine.**
3. **Carry over unpaid debts explicitly; relocation is not debt forgiveness.**
4. **Route explanatory credit explicitly: donor lane, recipient lane, shared infrastructure, or bounded benchmark inheritance.**
5. **Keep this gate subordinate to the exclusion-versus-salvage gate and upstream of any claim that a surviving fragment now belongs to canon or shifts lane ranking.**
   This surface sharpens what salvage still does *not* buy; it does not erase donor-lane defeat or automatically promote the recipient lane.

## Net result

The archive now has a smaller and more honest sentence available:

> a completion bid may leave salvage-grade relics that still fail import-grade transfer until provenance, transfer mechanism, debt carry-over, and credit routing are faced honestly.

That blocks one more route by which defeated-lane fragments can be smuggled back into canon without widening the archive.
