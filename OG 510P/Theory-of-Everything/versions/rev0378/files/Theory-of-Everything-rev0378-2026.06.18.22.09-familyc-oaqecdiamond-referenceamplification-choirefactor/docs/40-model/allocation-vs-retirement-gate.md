# Allocation versus retirement gate

A completion bid should not earn the same archive-governance credit merely because some wedge would **justify concentrating more scarce effort on that lane** and because that same improvement would **justify retiring rivals, shrinking their maintenance floor toward zero, or treating later reopening as irrational**.

Shared family scaffolding and ordered routing live in `docs/40-model/completion-bid-comparison-gate-family-frame.md` and `docs/40-model/completion-bid-comparison-stack.md`; this file keeps only the pair-specific split between allocation-grade steering and retirement-grade pruning.

**Pair-specific split:** a lane may deserve more work before it deserves rival death.

## What this gate separates

A completion bid can currently earn:
1. **ranking-grade movement** — attributable support robustly improves the lane's comparative standing;
2. **allocation-grade steering** — that movement is strong enough to justify some real concentration of scarce effort, relative to rivals, once option value and misspecification debt are disclosed;
3. **retirement-grade pruning** — the case is now strong enough that rival lanes can be pushed to a minimal watch state, removed from normal active hedge preservation, or made reopening-exceptional without paying too much irreversibility cost.

This surface exists only to block the inflation from (2) to (3).

## Why the distinction matters

Research concentration is reversible.
Retirement is only partly reversible.
Once a lane is pruned too hard, the archive can lose tacit comparison memory, benchmark continuity, local expertise, and cheap future reopening paths.
Under deep misspecification, that loss can matter more than a short-run gain from pushing all effort into the current frontrunner.

The archive therefore needs one more honesty layer:
**concentrating effort is not yet the same as collapsing the hedge set.**

## What retirement-grade pruning would have to cash out

### 1. Reopening-cost disclosure

A lane does not earn retirement-grade pruning credit until it states what would actually be lost by letting rivals fall below a maintenance floor.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what would have to be rebuilt later: conceptual comparison state, technical competency, benchmark maps, bibliography continuity, or explicit discriminator surfaces;
- how expensive later re-entry would be relative to keeping a small live hedge now;
- whether the rival lane contains unresolved but cheap discriminator opportunities that would become harder to recover after pruning;
- and whether the proposed retirement is about a local archive cycle, a medium-run research program, or something close to durable archival deactivation.

A lane that cannot name this cost may still deserve more resources.
It does not yet deserve rival retirement.

### 2. Correlated-failure and misspecification honesty

Retirement asks more than “which lane looks best now?”
It also asks whether the leading lane and the lagging lanes might fail together or whether the leading lane's current edge could collapse under one shared hidden mistake.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- whether the leading lane and the proposed retirees share a common weak assumption package,
- whether the current frontrunner's edge is partly inherited from the archive's current observable, cosmological, or comparison basis,
- whether nearby model-class misspecification could make today's frontrunner systematically over-favored,
- and whether the rival lane still carries orthogonal hedge value even if its present rank is lower.

Retirement-grade pruning requires more than confidence.
It requires confidence that the remaining hedge is not cheaply buying protection against correlated failure.

### 3. Maintenance-floor disclosure

The archive should not jump from “allocate more here” to “drop everything else.”
It should ask what minimal preservation floor must remain even after concentration.
At minimum, that means naming whether rivals keep:
- a watchful bibliography floor,
- a benchmark / comparison floor,
- one live discriminator note,
- or only a fully dormant standby threshold.

If that floor is not stated, apparent retirement language is usually just concentration rhetoric wearing a stronger costume.

### 4. Retirement-grade pruning

Even after disclosure, pruning is earned only when:
- the leading lane's advantage survives nearby admissible ranking, allocation, and misspecification changes strongly enough to matter for more than one local cycle,
- the remaining hedge value in rival lanes is small enough, slow enough, or too correlated to justify even a lightweight maintenance floor beyond watchfulness,
- reopening cost is genuinely low relative to the concentration gain,
- and the archive can say exactly what governance change follows: mild concentration with hedge preservation, strong concentration with watch-state rivals, or actual retirement-level pruning.

A lane may deserve a stronger budget share while still failing this test.
That is not weakness; it is irreversibility discipline.

## Common failure modes

1. **Concentration-to-retirement laundering** — extra effort for one lane is quietly retold as a reason to make rivals effectively disappear.
2. **Reopening-cost amnesia** — the archive forgets that comparison memory and technical re-entry pathways are themselves scarce assets.
3. **Shared-assumption blindness** — correlated failure risk is ignored because the frontrunner currently looks cleaner on the archive's present basis.
4. **Floor erasure** — rivals lose even a tiny maintenance floor without anyone stating that this is what happened.
5. **Monoculture prestige** — “best current lane” is retold as “the only lane that remains rational to preserve.”

## Immediate archive rules

1. **Do not convert allocation-grade steering directly into retirement-grade pruning.**
2. **Require explicit reopening-cost and maintenance-floor disclosure before any rival-retirement language.**
3. **Ask whether the leading lane's edge could fail together with the lanes it is trying to retire.**
4. **Prefer watch-state or thin-hedge preservation over faux closure whenever reopening cost is material.**
5. **Keep this gate subordinate to the ranking-versus-allocation gate and the canonical atlas surfaces.**
   This surface sharpens governance posture after concentration; it does not replace separate debts on recovery, observables, package disclosure, or witness closure.

## Net result

The archive now has a smaller and more honest sentence available:

> a completion bid may earn allocation-grade steering and still be far from retirement-grade pruning once reopening cost, correlated failure, and hedge-floor disclosure are faced honestly.

That blocks another prestige inflation move without widening the archive.
