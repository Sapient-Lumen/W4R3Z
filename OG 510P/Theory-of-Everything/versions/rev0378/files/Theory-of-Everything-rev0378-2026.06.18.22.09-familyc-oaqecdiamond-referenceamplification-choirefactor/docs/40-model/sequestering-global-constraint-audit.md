## Compression verdict

The current archive should treat **sequestering / global-constraint moves as the strongest mixed-class vacuum-energy proposals yet audited, but not as full closure of the cosmological-constant problem**.

Bounded result:

1. **What they genuinely change:** unlike plain unimodular reclassification, they directly target matter-sector vacuum contributions and aim to make the residual curvature radiatively stable rather than merely renaming `\Lambda`.
2. **What they still owe:** they continue to rely on an auxiliary global or topological constraint package, and they still leave a finite residual curvature or constant-setting burden that has to be fixed by boundary data, history, measurement, or a special global sector.
3. **What they therefore currently earn:** stronger mixed-class credit than unimodular proposals, plus bounded partial local-radiative credit, but not generic local-law closure and not cosmological-package closure.

That is already a sharper archive state.
It means the archive can now distinguish three non-identical mixed proposal classes:
- **pure bookkeeping relocation**,
- **direct radiative targeting with residual global package**,
- and any future class that might actually shrink both local threshold sensitivity and cosmological-package burden at once.

## Why this document exists

The previous audit closed the unimodular pass by saying:
- bookkeeping can improve,
- the local/global split can sharpen,
- and the debt can move,
- without local threshold sensitivity being generically reduced.

Sequestering-style proposals are the next necessary stress test because they aim higher.
They do not just reclassify `\Lambda`.
They try to **cancel or neutralize vacuum-energy contributions from matter sectors through extra constraint structure** and to leave behind a radiatively stable residual curvature. That makes them the right place to ask the archive's harder question:

**Does this class really reduce local EFT burden, or does it mostly trade local sensitivity for a more elaborate global accounting package?**

## What the sequestering move actually buys

In the archive's current bounded reading, sequestering / global-constraint proposals can genuinely buy four things.

### 1. A direct attack on matter-loop vacuum energy

This is the most important difference from the unimodular pass.
The sequestering class is not content merely to move `\Lambda` into an integration constant.
Its explicit aim is to stop vacuum-energy contributions from matter loops from sourcing curvature in the ordinary way.

That means the archive should now score sequestering as a **stronger mixed class than pure reparameterization**.

### 2. A tighter radiative-stability claim

The local sequestering formulation is designed so auxiliary sectors cancel vacuum-energy contributions while leaving a finite residual curvature that is claimed to be radiatively stable.
That is a real local-pressure move even if it is not yet full closure.

The archive should therefore stop saying that all mixed proposal classes are equal.
They are not.
Sequestering is a more ambitious and more structurally local-facing move than unimodular bookkeeping alone.

### 3. A cleaner statement of where the residual burden lives

The proposal class also makes the leftover burden easier to see.
Even if large vacuum contributions are neutralized, the final small effective curvature is still not derived for free.
A residual finite piece remains to be fixed by some combination of:
- global constraint sector,
- boundary or history data,
- topological or four-form structure,
- or measured residual constant.

That means the archive can now name the remaining debt more honestly.
The local radiative burden may shrink, but the cosmological-package burden does not disappear.

### 4. A better comparator for future mixed proposal classes

Sequestering now becomes the archive's benchmark for any future mixed class that says it does more than re-route burden.
A future proposal should not merely beat unimodular bookkeeping.
It should now have to beat **the stronger sequestering threshold**:
- direct radiative targeting,
- honest residual-package naming,
- and a nontrivial empirical or structural discriminator.

## What the sequestering move still does **not** buy automatically

### 1. Full local-law closure

A bounded local-radiative gain is not yet the same as a complete local-law solution.
The archive should still ask:
- which sectors are actually sequestered,
- which loops are covered,
- what auxiliary fields or constraint sectors must be introduced,
- and whether threshold sensitivity has really disappeared rather than being absorbed into a protected residual constant whose value is still external to the low-energy local story.

Current archive verdict: **partial local burden reduction is plausible and stronger than unimodular reclassification, but generic full local closure is still not earned.**

### 2. Cosmological-package closure

Even sympathetic formulations still leave the archive with a real global-package question:
- why this residual value,
- why this history or boundary package,
- why this auxiliary-sector configuration,
- and what sets or constrains the final small curvature in the realized universe.

The archive should therefore not let “radiatively stable residual curvature” be retold as “the actual observed tiny value is now fully explained.”
Those are not the same accomplishment.

### 3. A thick empirical wedge

Across the relevant tested regime, the payoffs of sequestering may remain mostly structural unless the proposal yields a clear cosmological or gravitational discriminator beyond GR plus a small effective constant.
If the model's gain is mainly how it distributes bookkeeping and residual burden, the archive should say so.

That still counts as progress.
It just counts as **cleaner explanatory sorting**, not automatic empirical victory.

## Scoring sequestering-style proposals under the debt split

### Local radiative burden

**Current archive score:** partially reduced, not generically closed.

This is the first audited class that earns more than bookkeeping credit.
By construction it aims to neutralize matter-sector vacuum-energy contributions and protect the residual curvature from ordinary radiative destabilization.
That is a real improvement over the unimodular pass.

But the archive still does not count the class as a finished local-law victory in generic form because the mechanism comes attached to extra constraint structure and still leaves a finite residual curvature whose small realized value is not thereby fully explained.

### Cosmological-package burden

**Current archive score:** still open.

The proposal class sharpens where this burden lives, but it does not erase it.
A small residual curvature still has to be fixed or interpreted within a broader global package.
That means the archive should continue to score the class as incomplete on the cosmological side.

### Mixed debt-shift burden

**Current archive score:** strongly active here.

This remains a mixed proposal class.
Its virtue is that it does more than merely relabel the debt.
Its limitation is that it still does not close all the burdens it rearranges.

So the current archive posture should now be:
- **stronger than unimodular bookkeeping**,
- **stronger than pure global selection talk**,
- **not yet a two-burden closure**.

## Five checks the archive should now apply

### 1. Which vacuum contributions are actually neutralized?

Name the target explicitly:
- matter loops,
- phase transitions,
- graviton loops,
- or only a subset.

A proposal should not get full local-radiative credit for canceling one contribution class while leaving others vague.

### 2. What residual constant or curvature remains, and who fixes it?

If the mechanism leaves a finite radiatively stable residual term, say what determines it:
- measurement,
- global constraint,
- boundary history,
- topological sector,
- or other package.

If that residual fixing remains external to the local low-energy story, the cosmological-package burden is still present.

### 3. Which auxiliary or global sectors are indispensable?

The archive should now make the added machinery explicit:
- global Lagrange multipliers,
- four-form sectors,
- topological sectors,
- finite-volume or global-history assumptions,
- or other non-ordinary local ingredients.

This does not disqualify the proposal.
It simply prevents the mechanism from being scored as if it were a bare local EFT modification with no package cost.

### 4. What is the empirical discriminator?

Does the class predict any distinctive:
- cosmological history,
- global constraint consequence,
- phase-transition residue,
- collapse or boundary behavior,
- or departure from plain GR plus a tiny constant?

If not, its main gain remains structural rather than strongly observational.

### 5. Did both burdens shrink, or only one?

This remains the decisive audit check.
The archive should now score sequestering-style proposals as:
- more than debt relabeling,
- less than complete closure,
- and still incomplete unless both local threshold sensitivity and cosmological-package burden are reduced together.

## Family-by-family relevance

### Family A — EFT-first baseline

Family A keeps the scoring honest.
It asks whether sequestering really changes ordinary threshold sensitivity or merely protects a residual constant with additional constraint structure.

### Family B — Thermodynamic / entropy gravity

Family B remains mostly peripheral here.
Its role is still only disciplinary: do not let evocative gravitational language impersonate a vacuum-energy solution.

### Family C — Holography / entanglement geometry

Family C again matters mainly as a comparison class.
A strong global package or boundary sector can be structurally rich without thereby solving the local radiative burden.
That is the same anti-smuggling rule now applied to sequestering.

### Family D — Measurement / nonlocality pressure

Family D remains indirect but useful.
If a proposal's residual constant is effectively fixed by measurement or by special history-selection language, the archive should say so instead of pretending the local dynamical story has become self-sufficient.

This is again a **compression move** more than a literature move.
It lets the archive say, more sharply than before:

- not every mixed proposal class is equal,
- sequestering is stronger than mere bookkeeping relocation,
- direct radiative targeting still is not the same as complete explanation,
- and the next useful audit should now test self-tuning / relaxation proposal classes rather than reopening the same sequestering promises in broader prose.
