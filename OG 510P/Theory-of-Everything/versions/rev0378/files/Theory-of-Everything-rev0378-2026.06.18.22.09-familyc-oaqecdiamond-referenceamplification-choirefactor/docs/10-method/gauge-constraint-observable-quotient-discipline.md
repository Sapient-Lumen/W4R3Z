# Gauge, constraint, and observable-quotient discipline

`OQ-0075` controls a recurring laundering path: a route proves, simulates, fits, or reconstructs a quantity in a gauge-fixed or representative-dependent presentation, then prose treats that quantity as a physical observable.

The archive now separates three questions. First, what redundancy is present: gauge symmetry, diffeomorphism, frame convention, duality presentation, field redefinition, regulator coordinate, relabeling, or package-only naming? Second, does the constraint structure close without anomaly, broken Ward identity, hidden second-class conversion, regulator artifact, or failed propagation? Third, what is the observable after quotienting: Dirac observable, BRST/BV cohomology class, relational record, S-matrix invariant, public detector/catalog statistic, or merely a nonphysical metadata object?

A route may use physical-observable, gauge-invariant, representative-independent, reduced, cohomological, Dirac-observable, or candidate-native record language only when the owning rows in `GAUGE-SYMMETRY-LEDGER.json`, `CONSTRAINT-CLOSURE-LEDGER.json`, and `OBSERVABLE-QUOTIENT-LEDGER.json` license that wording. Gauge-fixed representatives, coordinate expressions, raw perturbation variables, simulator labels, and package metadata are allowed as diagnostic or custody artifacts; they do not carry physical-observable authority.

## Stop rule

No route may convert gauge-fixed support into physical support unless it declares the redundancy, reduction/cohomology rule, constraint-closure status, representative-dependence test, residual anomaly risk, observable quotient, rollback handle, and maximum authority effect.

## Non-promotion rule

Passing this layer can repair or clarify a route. It cannot promote a route beyond its state-machine ceiling, public-record carrier ceiling, observed-sector obligations, or residual cap.
