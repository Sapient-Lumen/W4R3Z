# Candidate-native threshold versus candidate-native identifiability closure gate

This gate separates two stronger witness-package achievements that are still easy to conflate:

1. **candidate-native threshold closure** — the candidate's own bridge variables define the resolution, tolerance, coarse-graining, uncertainty, and under-resolution budgets that say when apparent outcome differences count as evidence rather than approximation chatter;
2. **candidate-native identifiability closure** — the candidate's own bridge variables also say which underlying states, couplings, geometries, frame-choices, or history classes are actually learnable from those thresholded record classes, which distinctions are gauge-equivalent or empirically collapsed, and where inversion is incomplete, unstable, or setup-dependent;
3. **local public witness closure** — a still stronger condition in which public local records, rereadability, objectivity, and experimental closure are all carried without substantial witness borrowing.

Candidate-native threshold closure is real progress.
It is just not yet candidate-native identifiability closure, much less full local public witness closure.

## Why this gate exists

Shared witness-gate scaffolding now lives in `docs/40-model/witness-closure-gate-family-frame.md`. This document only names the pair-specific inflation move below.

**a candidate that says when different records count as evidence being retold as if it therefore already says which theory-level distinctions are actually learnable from those records.**

That inflation can happen in at least seven ways:
- a lane can state native thresholds while several distinct candidate states or geometries still collapse onto the same admissible record class;
- a lane can advertise native coarse-graining while the inverse map from records back to candidate variables is imported from ordinary tomography or parameter-estimation language;
- a lane can talk as if two theories or histories are distinguishable while they differ only by gauge or by a counterpart convention the candidate itself has not fixed;
- a lane can achieve predictive stability under partial observability while leaving microscopic structure radically underdetermined;
- a lane can rely on informationally incomplete witness routes that separate some alternatives but not the candidate distinctions that matter for broad ToE credit;
- a lane can produce a formally exact map while its inversion is numerically unstable or wildly setup-dependent;
- or a lane can hide its residual underdetermination behind external model-selection, fit-quality, or benchmark conventions.

## The five-step candidate-native-identifiability test

### 1. Native target space, not just native thresholds

Before awarding candidate-native-identifiability credit, the archive should ask whether the candidate says **what is being identified** in its own variables rather than only what counts as an evidential difference.
At minimum, a lane should disclose:
- which candidate-level states, couplings, geometries, frame-choices, or histories are the intended targets of identification,
- which of those targets are exact, effective, approximate, or regime-limited,
- which target distinctions are already known to collapse under symmetry, coarse-graining, or observer limits,
- and which parts of the target space are still described only in external EFT, detector, or phenomenology language.

A lane that wins only here still earns **candidate-native threshold credit**, not yet candidate-native identifiability closure.

### 2. Native forward map and empirical-equivalence classes

A lane earns more than threshold credit only when the candidate explains how native target classes map to native record classes and where that map ceases to be injective.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what native observables or record classes each target class feeds into,
- which different targets are intentionally treated as the same because they lie on one gauge orbit or empirical-equivalence class,
- what counterpart or sameness convention is needed to compare targets across branches, frames, or histories,
- and what would count as evidence that two supposedly collapsed target classes should instead be treated as distinct.

Without that route, the lane may have thresholds but not a native account of what those thresholds can actually discriminate.

### 3. Native informational completeness and inversion stability

A lane earns stronger candidate-native-identifiability credit only when the candidate says whether its witness route is informative enough to separate the target distinctions it claims to separate.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- which witness route is informationally complete for the candidate distinction under discussion,
- which distinctions are only partially identified,
- how inversion from record classes back to native targets behaves under finite resolution and noise,
- and whether numerical instability, degeneracy, or setup dependence destroys the claimed identification.

Without those answers, a lane may tell us when differences count as evidence while still lacking a stable native inverse path from evidence to theory-level distinctions.

### 4. Native underdetermination and abstention rule

A lane earns still stronger candidate-native-identifiability credit only when it says where native evidence leaves multiple candidate distinctions unresolved.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- which target distinctions are only bounded, bracketed, or equivalence-class identified,
- which microscopic structures remain hidden under partial observability,
- when the candidate should return an explicitly collapsed, underdetermined, or non-identifiable verdict rather than a unique reconstruction,
- and what empirical additions would move that verdict from underdetermined to identified.

Without that boundary, the candidate can look more informative than it is by quietly borrowing an external uniqueness convention.

### 5. Native-to-lab identification bridge must be explicit

A lane earns **candidate-native identifiability closure** credit only when later users can connect actual public records back to one native account of learnability.
At minimum, it should name:
- the native target space,
- the native record map,
- the native empirical-equivalence or gauge-collapse rule,
- the informational-completeness or partial-identification condition,
- the inversion-stability rule,
- the underdetermination / abstention rule,
- and the mapping from those native structures to actual laboratory reconstruction or comparison practice.

This is still not the same thing as full local public witness closure.
A lane may support candidate-native identifiability closure quite well while still depending on ordinary laboratory infrastructure for the final public record ecology.
But that is a much smaller debt than merely having native thresholds while borrowing the decisive account of what the evidence can actually teach us about the candidate.

## Common failure modes

1. **threshold-without-inverse inflation** — native tolerances are named as if that already fixed what can be learned.
2. **hidden-gauge laundering** — apparently different candidate states are treated as distinct even though the candidate has not supplied a native sameness or orbit rule.
3. **one-record-many-model collapse denial** — several candidate targets generate the same record class, but the archive is asked to score them as uniquely identified anyway.
4. **setup-description smuggling** — unique reconstruction quietly depends on external knowledge of the apparatus, preparation map, or benchmark configuration that the candidate never internalizes.
5. **unstable inversion theater** — a formally invertible map is advertised while finite-resolution data make the inverse numerically unstable or regime-fragile.
6. **partial-observability amnesia** — statistically stable predictions are retold as if they uniquely fix microscopic structure.
7. **empirical-equivalence silence** — residual equivalence classes are left unnamed so the candidate appears sharper than its own witness route allows.

## Compact current readout

- **Thiemann's 2026 reference-frame analysis** makes the bridge burden explicit by saying that without an operational translation of reference frames into mathematics the physical interpretation of theory calculations in terms of observational data is not possible, and by asking how precisely gauge-dependent fields are interpreted through relational observables.
- **Kabel et al.'s quantum-hole-argument analysis** makes the identifiability burden explicit by treating models on the same symmetry orbit as physically equivalent and by showing that counterpart conventions are needed to say what counts as the same or different event across models.
- **Puliyil, Leppäjärvi, and Ziman's channel-identification result** sharpens the witness side by showing that differentiating any two channels requires informational completeness and that unique characterization also needs a complete description of the set-up.
- **Saini et al.'s completeness-stability result** sharpens the inverse side by showing that measurement quality is not just about separability but about estimation-error bounds and numerical stability of the inversion used for reconstruction.
- **Yang, Sun, and Zhou's practical homodyne-shadow result** makes the finite-resolution burden concrete by showing that even realistic finite-setting and finite-bin protocols need explicit necessary-and-sufficient informational-completeness conditions plus variance analysis.
- **Nomura and Ugajin's closed-quantum-gravity prediction framework** makes the physics-side caution explicit by showing that partial observability can restore statistically stable predictions without thereby uniquely fixing the microscopic structure behind them.

That does not refute current bridge programs or completion bids.
It just means they have not yet paid this stronger candidate-native-identifiability debt.

## Net rule

Within the archive:
- candidate-native threshold closure earns real witness-package credit;
- but it does **not** yet count as candidate-native identifiability closure;
- and candidate-native identifiability closure does **not** yet count as full local public witness closure.

Any future completion bid or bridge-family claim that wants stronger witness credit should now say not only what native thresholds, tolerances, and coarse-grainings it supports, but also which candidate distinctions those witness routes can actually identify, which remain collapsed or gauge-equivalent, and where the native inverse map becomes incomplete, unstable, or underdetermined without falling back into borrowed tomography, model-selection, or gauge-fixing language.
