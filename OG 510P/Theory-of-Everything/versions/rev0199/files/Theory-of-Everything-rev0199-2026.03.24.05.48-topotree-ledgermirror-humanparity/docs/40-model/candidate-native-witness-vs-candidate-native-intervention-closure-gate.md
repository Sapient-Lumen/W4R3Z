# Candidate-native-witness versus candidate-native-intervention-closure gate

Purpose: split the archive's **witness-package progress** one step further.
A candidate should not earn the same witness credit merely because it can **say, in its own bridge variables, where the record carriers live, what stabilizes them, how they are reread, and why publicity is not just borrowed from ordinary detector-side prose** and because it can **also say, in those same variables, what it means to prepare the relevant evidential state, choose and trigger the interaction, treat selective outcomes as state updates, and deliberately write, perturb, reroute, or erase the record rather than merely reread it**.
Those are related, but they are not the same achievement.

## Compression verdict

Inside the archive's witness package, once a lane has already moved beyond fresh-host reinstantiation, independent implementation, independent evidence generation, and candidate-native witness language, the current score should now distinguish seven nested layers:

1. **public evidence-custody closure** — the decisive evidence bundle or reference object is stably extractable, versioned, fixity-checkable, preservable, citable, and reopenable beyond one privileged repository or operator chain;
2. **fresh-host reinstantiation closure** — outsiders can take that public bundle, identify the exact workflow / software / environment objects it depends on, materialize an executable environment on a fresh host or stack, and rerun or rehydrate the evidential process under declared equivalence rules without privileged manual setup;
3. **independent implementation closure** — outsiders can implement the relevant workflow semantics, protocol, model, or measurement logic through a genuinely separate code lineage, test it against a public conformance or agreement criterion, and still recover the same evidential class without carrying the originating executable bundle itself;
4. **independent evidence-generation closure** — outsiders can obtain the same evidential class through separately acquired, separately prepared, or blind / holdout record-generation routes, interlaboratory comparison pathways, or independent measurement chains rather than only through multiple implementations over one inherited evidence source;
5. **candidate-native witness closure** — the candidate's own bridge variables name the record carrier, stabilization story, reread path, and objectivity / publicity conditions closely enough that the empirical loop is no longer mostly redescribed in borrowed detector-side or boundary-side language;
6. **candidate-native intervention closure** — the candidate's own bridge variables also say what counts as preparing, perturbing, selectively updating, branching, writing, steering, or erasing those evidence-bearing states rather than leaving experiment-choice semantics in ordinary external apparatus language;
7. **local public witness closure** — a lane can say where the physical records live, what stabilizes them, how they are reread, how they are prepared and deliberately manipulated, why they remain public evidence, and why that empirical loop is not mainly outsourced.

A lane may earn real credit on layer 5 while still being weak on layer 6.
That is progress.
It is just not yet candidate-native intervention closure, much less full local public witness closure.

## Why this gate exists

Shared witness-gate scaffolding now lives in `docs/40-model/witness-closure-gate-family-frame.md`. This document only names the pair-specific inflation move below.

**native record-carrier language being retold as if the candidate therefore already supplies native experiment-choice, measurement-trigger, selective-update, and write / erase semantics.**

That inflation can happen in at least seven ways:
- a lane can identify native record carriers while the actual preparation of those records still depends on an external lab script, apparatus setting, or boundary source that the candidate only assumes;
- a lane can define observables or record algebras while the selective state update associated with a readout still arrives from imported Born-rule / Lüders language with no native bridge account of what physically changed;
- a lane can introduce observers, clocks, rods, or worldlines internally while the meaning of an experiment “of our choosing” remains hand-set from outside the candidate;
- a lane can describe what an observer can measure while saying little about what the observer can deliberately influence, reroute, reset, or erase in the same native vocabulary;
- a lane can map native witness structure to external evidence after the fact while leaving the write-side of the measurement loop tacit;
- a lane can redescribe ordinary apparatus interventions in geometric, algebraic, or relational language without deriving the intervention semantics in that language;
- or a lane can show update rules in one special holographic or detector construction while the candidate-wide preparation / update story remains underdescribed.

## The five-step candidate-native-intervention test

### 1. Native preparation semantics, not just native record semantics

Before awarding candidate-native-intervention credit, the archive should ask whether the candidate says what counts as **preparing** the relevant evidential state in its own variables rather than merely naming what a later record looks like.
At minimum, a lane should disclose:
- which native configuration, subspace, algebraic sector, or coarse-grained state is supposed to be prepared,
- what operations or constraints count as preparing that state,
- which parts of the preparation recipe still live in ordinary apparatus or boundary-source language,
- and what would distinguish preparation failure from later readout failure.

A lane that wins only here earns **candidate-native witness credit**, not yet candidate-native intervention closure.

### 2. Native trigger and coupling semantics

A lane earns more than witness credit only when the candidate explains what it means, in native terms, to **turn on** the interaction that produces the record.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what native interaction or coupling constitutes the measurement or probe,
- how experiment choice is represented without silently appealing to an external operator hand,
- whether the interaction is background-, boundary-, observer-, or frame-dependent,
- and what breakdown regime makes the trigger semantics cease to be well-defined.

Without that route, apparent native witness structure may still be passive rereadability draped over borrowed lab intervention.

### 3. Native selective-update semantics

A lane earns stronger candidate-native-intervention credit only when selective outcomes lead to an explicit **native update** or branch-conditioning story rather than a merely imported one.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what physical change in the candidate corresponds to conditioning on a realized outcome,
- whether the update is effective, approximate, relational, algebraic, or fundamental,
- how the update avoids collapsing back into purely external textbook talk,
- and which parts of the post-selection or conditioning rule are still borrowed from ordinary measurement lore.

Without those answers, the lane may have native observables but not a native account of what measurements do.

### 4. Native write / steer / erase path

A lane earns still stronger candidate-native-intervention credit only when deliberate manipulation of the evidential state is described natively as well.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what counts as writing or perturbing the evidence-bearing state in the candidate,
- whether the same native structures support conditional rerouting, stabilization, reset, or erasure,
- how control differs from mere passive correlation,
- and what empirical differences would appear if the candidate's intervention story were wrong.

Without that path, apparent native witness closure may still be read-only semantics.

### 5. Native-to-lab action bridge must be explicit

A lane earns **candidate-native intervention closure** credit only when later users can connect actual laboratory choices and updates back to one native intervention story.
At minimum, it should name:
- the native preparation object,
- the native trigger / coupling rule,
- the native selective-update rule,
- the native write / steer / erase path,
- the mapping from those native structures to actual external interventions,
- and the residual debt to full local public witness closure.

This is still not the same thing as full local public witness closure.
A lane may support candidate-native intervention closure quite well while still depending on ordinary laboratory infrastructure for the final public record ecology.
But that is a much smaller debt than merely naming native records while borrowing the entire action side of the experiment.

## Common failure modes

1. **read-only inflation** — native record carriers are retold as if they already imply native actuation.
2. **borrowed-preparation laundering** — the candidate names the record but the initial-state or source-setting semantics remain entirely external.
3. **update black-boxing** — conditioning on an outcome is handled by imported textbook language with no candidate-native account of what changed.
4. **experiment-choice smuggling** — the decisive “which probe was applied?” content still lives in a human / apparatus-side narrative outside the candidate.
5. **write-side silence** — a lane can reread a native record but cannot say how the same record would be deliberately written, stabilized, rerouted, or erased.
6. **special-construction overcrediting** — one observer or detector model yields an update rule, but the candidate-wide intervention semantics remain unpaid.
7. **theory-external action fallback** — when pressure rises, the argument retreats to ordinary lab-control language, showing that the candidate's own actuation story is not yet closed.

## Compact current readout

- **Witten's observer algebra paper** makes the split visible by distinguishing the minimal worldline model from a realistic observer who also carries measuring equipment and a recording device, and by explicitly separating the region an observer can see from the region the observer can both see and influence.
- **Abdalla, Antonini, Iliesiu, and Levine's observer-path-integral framework** pushes beyond bare observables by developing a non-perturbative description of the observables an observer could measure in generic spacetimes, but it still leaves open how far that observer-relative formalism closes the whole intervention burden.
- **Harlow, Usatyuk, and Zhao's closed-universe observer framework** sharpens the stake by asking what calculation should predict the likely outcome of an experiment of our choosing inside a closed universe, making clear that “having observables” is weaker than “having experiment-choice semantics.”
- **Balasubramanian, Keski-Vakkuri, and Pranzini's detector-based state-update analysis** shows in a holographic setting that selective measurements should update the bulk gravity state, and that extensions of ordinary update rules to relativistic and gravitating systems are subtle rather than automatic.

That does not refute current bridge programs or completion bids.
It just means they have not yet paid this stronger candidate-native-intervention debt.

## Net rule

Within the archive:
- candidate-native witness closure earns real witness-package credit;
- but it does **not** yet count as candidate-native intervention closure;
- and candidate-native intervention closure does **not** yet count as full local public witness closure.

Any future completion bid or bridge-family claim that wants stronger witness credit should now say not only how the candidate natively identifies record carriers, stabilization, rereadability, and publicity, but also how the candidate's own bridge variables represent preparation, trigger, selective update, and deliberate write / erase paths without collapsing back into borrowed laboratory action language.
