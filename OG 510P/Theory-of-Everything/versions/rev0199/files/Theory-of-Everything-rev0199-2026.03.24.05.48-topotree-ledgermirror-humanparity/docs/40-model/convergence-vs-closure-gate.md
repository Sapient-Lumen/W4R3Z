# Convergence versus closure gate

A completion bid should not earn the same archive-governance credit merely because several lanes now support a **convergence-grade** common core and because that same overlap now licenses a **closure-grade** claim that the search has effectively settled on one unique final theory or successor completion.

Shared family scaffolding and ordered routing live in `docs/40-model/completion-bid-comparison-gate-family-frame.md` and `docs/40-model/completion-bid-comparison-stack.md`; this file keeps only the pair-specific split between convergence-grade unity credit and closure-grade final-theory credit.

**Pair-specific split:** a common core may be real, important, and even highly constraining while still leaving more than one live successor theory, completion route, or final explanatory package on the table.

## What this gate separates

A completion bid can currently earn:
1. **convergence-grade unity credit** — several lanes now support a bounded common core, deeper shared structure, or genuinely tightening atlas overlap rather than merely a reusable fragment;
2. **closure-grade final-theory credit** — the shared core now narrows the search enough that one uniquely privileged successor, completion route, or final explanatory package deserves search-closing treatment.

This surface exists only to block the inflation from (1) to “the archive has therefore effectively found the final theory.”

## Why the distinction matters

A common core does not automatically choose its own completion.
Several lanes can agree on a structural center while still disagreeing about ontology, observables, cosmological package, record carriers, parameter-fixation route, or what the theory looks like outside the overlap patch.
Even exact or near-exact overlap can therefore leave a nontrivial successor space alive.

Without a closure gate, the archive can make three opposite mistakes.
It can treat any serious convergence as if the search were basically over.
It can compress “common core” and “unique successor theory” into one blurred win.
Or it can overreact and refuse to treat genuine convergence as progress simply because final closure has not yet been earned.

The archive therefore needs one more honesty layer:
**convergence-grade unity credit is not yet closure-grade final-theory credit.**

## What closure-grade final-theory credit would have to cash out

### 1. Core-identity disclosure

The archive should not call convergence “closure” until it says what has actually converged.
At minimum, it should ask:
- whether the shared core concerns ontology, dynamics, observables, or only a narrower structural kernel,
- which invariants or burdens the common core really settles,
- which parts of the current atlas remain outside that core,
- and whether the alleged core is itself stable across nearby package choices.

A real core can still be narrow.
It is just not yet final closure.

### 2. Extension-multiplicity disclosure

The archive should not narrate a common core as final-theory closure until it says how many live extensions remain.
At minimum, it should ask:
- whether more than one inequivalent successor theory can extend the same core,
- whether the core underdetermines observables, ontology, or cosmological package,
- whether the same shared center admits several completion routes with distinct external commitments,
- and whether any supposed uniqueness comes from the core itself or from extra selection assumptions.

A shared center with many live extensions is convergence, not closure.

### 3. Burden-closure disclosure

The archive should not treat a core as search-closing until it says which standing burdens are now actually closed.
At minimum, it should ask:
- whether low-energy recovery, observed-sector recovery, and observed-parameter burdens are settled by the core or merely deferred,
- whether observables and record / witness burdens are settled natively rather than promised downstream,
- whether cosmological-boundary and selection burdens are reduced rather than relocated,
- and whether the core closes explanatory debt or only compresses notation.

A shared core that leaves major burdens open can still be progress.
It is just not yet final-theory credit.

### 4. Successor-selection disclosure

The archive should not move from convergence to closure until it says how one successor, if any, is selected.
At minimum, it should ask:
- whether there is a principled rule from core to successor rather than a retrospective preference,
- whether the selection is empirical, mathematical, interpretive, or package-conditioned,
- whether the same selection rule would survive nearby admissible rival sets,
- and whether any remaining choice is small enough to count as bounded completion rather than open-ended underdetermination.

A core without a named successor-selection rule is not yet a final theory.

### 5. Reopening-rights disclosure

The archive should not call a search “closed” until it says what could reopen it.
At minimum, it should ask:
- which observations, constructions, or no-go results would revive rival successors,
- whether alternatives remain preserved as standby rivals or only as memorial fragments,
- whether closure would survive new bridge imports or new observable wedges,
- and whether the current result is robust enough to shrink reopening rights without pretending they vanished.

If reopening rights remain broad, closure language should stay weak.

### 6. Closure-grade final-theory credit

A convergent common core earns closure-grade final-theory credit only when:
- the core is explicit enough that the archive knows what has converged and what has not,
- extension multiplicity is narrow enough that only a tightly bounded successor space remains live,
- burden closure is broad enough that major explanatory debt is not merely pushed downstream,
- successor selection is principled enough that one route is not being chosen by convenience alone,
- and reopening rights are narrow enough that the archive can honestly talk about a practically settled final-theory search rather than a promising common center.

A lane may deserve convergence while still failing closure.
That is not stagnation; it is finality honesty.

## Common failure modes

1. **Common-core-to-final-theory laundering** — a genuine shared center is retold as if the search is basically over.
2. **Successor wishcasting** — the archive acts as if the common core mechanically picks one completion when no such rule has been shown.
3. **Burden deferral erasure** — unresolved observable, cosmological, or witness debt disappears behind “deep structure” rhetoric.
4. **Uniqueness by compression** — rival successors are ignored because the story became cleaner when they were not named.
5. **Closure by fatigue** — portfolio exhaustion or sociological focus is mistaken for earned final-theory closure.

## Compact current readout

| Closure question | What the archive should ask | Failure mode if skipped |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Core identity** | What exactly has converged and which burdens does that core really settle? | narrow overlap retold as final theory |
| **Extension multiplicity** | How many inequivalent successors still extend the same core? | plurality erased |
| **Burden closure** | Which major debts are actually closed rather than deferred? | compression retold as explanation |
| **Successor selection** | What picks one successor rather than another? | convenience retold as uniqueness |
| **Reopening rights** | What would still reopen rival successors? | provisional closure retold as finality |

## Immediate archive rules

1. **Do not convert convergence-grade unity credit directly into closure-grade final-theory credit.**
2. **Require extension-multiplicity and burden-closure disclosure before common-core rhetoric can count as search-closing progress.**
3. **Keep successor-selection debt explicit; a shared center does not yet choose its own final completion.**
4. **Keep reopening rights visible unless the rival-successor space has actually collapsed.**
5. **Keep this gate subordinate to the assimilation-versus-convergence gate and upstream of any claim that the archive has effectively found one final theory.**

## Net result

The archive now has a smaller and more honest sentence available:

> several lanes may genuinely converge on a common core while still failing closure-grade final-theory credit until core identity, extension multiplicity, burden closure, successor selection, and reopening rights are faced honestly.

That blocks one more route by which deep overlap can be mistaken for final search closure without widening the archive.
