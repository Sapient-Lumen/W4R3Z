# Cross-frame-portability versus public-reference-standard-closure gate

Purpose: split the archive's **cross-frame witness progress** one step further.
A candidate should not earn the same witness credit merely because it can **transport the same coarse-grained fact across nearby observer or frame changes** and because it has **closed the public reference-standard problem** inside its own language.
Those are related, but they are not the same achievement.

## Compression verdict

Inside the archive's witness package, once a lane has already moved beyond one privileged frame, the current score should now distinguish three nested layers:

1. **cross-frame fact portability** — a lane can say how the *same coarse-grained fact* is translated, reidentified, and kept agreement-capable across nearby observer or frame changes;
2. **public reference-standard closure** — a lane can say how the shared standard of comparison itself becomes public enough for use: axes, clocks, orientations, fragment adequacy, or other comparison standards are no longer silently borrowed from an external laboratory convention;
3. **local public witness closure** — a lane can say where the physical records live, what stabilizes them, how they are reread, and why public evidence is not mainly outsourced.

A lane may earn real credit on layer 1 while still being weak on layer 2.
That is progress.
It is just not yet public standard closure, much less full witness closure.

## Why this gate exists

Shared witness-gate scaffolding now lives in `docs/40-model/witness-closure-gate-family-frame.md`. This document only names the pair-specific inflation move below.

**portable fact agreement being retold as if the shared public standard of comparison were already solved.**

That inflation can happen in at least four ways:
- observers can translate a fact between frames only because a classical orientation, clock standard, or detector convention is silently imported from outside the candidate;
- agreement can hold only after an undeclared adequacy threshold, fragment choice, or coarse-graining rule is fixed by hand;
- a theory can say “all observers see the same thing modulo a transformation” without saying how that transformation class becomes public, stable, and operationally available;
- or redundancy can be asserted in prose without naming how many channels or fragments actually clear the comparison standard.

## The four-step public-standard test

### 1. Standard disclosure

Before awarding public-standard credit, the archive should ask what shared standard of comparison is doing the work.
At minimum, a lane should disclose:
- which axes, clocks, orientations, detector conventions, fragment-selection rules, or adequacy thresholds are assumed,
- whether those standards are fixed externally or arise inside the candidate's own language,
- and which parts of the evidential loop still depend on that borrowed standard.

A lane that wins only here earns **cross-frame portability credit**, not yet public-standard closure.

### 2. Standard-objectification route

A lane earns more than portability only when it can say how the comparison standard itself becomes publicly usable.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- whether the standard is redundantly accessible or only privately held,
- whether different observers can recover the same standard modulo the stated transformation law,
- what physical carriers or channels support that recovery,
- and whether the route works only in one toy setting or across a broader physically relevant class.

Without that route, the lane still owes major public-standard debt.

### 3. Adequacy and redundancy disclosure

A lane earns stronger public-standard credit only when it discloses the operational floor for agreement.
At minimum, the archive should ask:
- what adequacy criterion says a fragment, observer, or channel carries enough information to count,
- how many channels or fragments clear that bar,
- whether the result survives overlap corrections, coarse-graining changes, or nearby threshold choices,
- and what resource or thermodynamic limits constrain the redundancy.

Without those answers, apparent publicity may be a reporting artifact rather than candidate-native objectivity.

### 4. Closure boundary

A lane earns **public reference-standard closure** credit only when it can say how the shared standard of comparison itself becomes public enough for multi-observer use without a silent external referee.
At minimum, it should name:
- the comparison standard,
- the objectification route,
- the adequacy / redundancy condition,
- the remaining calibration and resource debt,
- and the remaining debt to full record carrier / stabilization / rereadability closure.

This is still not the same thing as full local witness closure.
But it is stronger than merely transporting one fact across nearby frames.

## Common failure modes

1. **Borrowed-standard laundering** — a hidden classical standard is imported, then retold as if the candidate had generated it.
2. **Threshold silence** — publicity depends on one undeclared adequacy bar, fragment choice, or overlap convention.
3. **Modulo-transformation inflation** — “same up to transformation” is retold as if public operational agreement were already solved.
4. **Redundancy rhetoric** — many-observer access is asserted without naming channel count, fragment adequacy, or resource limits.
5. **Standard-to-closure inflation** — public reference-standard closure is retold as if record carrier, stabilization, and rereadability were already solved too.

## Compact current readout

- **Quantum-reference-frame and observer-relative gravity work** is real progress because it makes frame change, relational access, and agreement conditions far more explicit than a fixed-background story does.
  But most current lanes still borrow the shared public standard that lets those comparisons count as ordinary evidence.
- **Recent objectivity-of-reference-frame work** sharpens the target by showing that a reference standard itself can become objective only in a stronger, transformation-aware sense.
  That target is stricter than mere cross-frame fact transport.
- **Recent operational quantum-Darwinism work** sharpens the audit language by forcing publicity to name adequacy thresholds and redundancy counts rather than simply claiming “many observers can agree.”
  That does not by itself solve quantum gravity, but it gives the archive a cleaner brake against witness inflation.

## Immediate archive rules

1. **Do not count cross-frame fact portability as public reference-standard closure.**
2. **Require an explicit account of the shared comparison standard before promoting portability claims to public objectivity rhetoric.**
3. **Require adequacy-threshold, redundancy, and resource disclosure before treating a standard as publicly closed.**
4. **Do not count public reference-standard closure itself as full local witness closure unless record carrier, stabilization, rereadability, and objectivity are still answered in the candidate's own language.**

## Net result

The archive now has a smaller and more honest sentence available:

> a lane may earn cross-frame fact portability while still remaining weak on public reference-standard closure and local witness closure.

That blocks another common inflation move without widening the archive.
