# Exclusion versus salvage gate

A completion bid should not earn the same archive-governance credit merely because some lane has been **excluded as a live candidate for full theory-of-everything closure** and because that same exclusion would **justify discarding the lane's reusable mathematics, effective structures, discriminator lessons, or bounded mechanism fragments**.

Shared family scaffolding and ordered routing live in `docs/40-model/completion-bid-comparison-gate-family-frame.md` and `docs/40-model/completion-bid-comparison-stack.md`; this file keeps only the pair-specific split between exclusion-grade closure and salvage-grade retention.

**Pair-specific split:** a lane may fail as a final contender while still leaving reusable relics.

## What this gate separates

A completion bid can currently earn:
1. **retirement-grade pruning** — rivals can leave the active hedge set while memorial notes and reopening triggers remain;
2. **exclusion-grade closure** — the archive can say, with bounded scope, that a lane is no longer a live candidate for full ToE completion under the named basis;
3. **salvage-grade retention** — even after exclusion, some bounded fragment of the lane still deserves active preservation as reusable mathematics, effective structure, negative constraint, benchmark map, or bridge ingredient.

This surface exists only to block the inflation from (2) to “nothing survives.”

## Why the distinction matters

Whole-lane defeat does not automatically erase part-level value.
A superseded lane can still leave behind:
- a calculational technology that later lanes reuse,
- an effective theory with a real restricted domain,
- a negative result that permanently narrows later search,
- a benchmark family that future claims still have to beat,
- or a structural clue that survives reinterpretation at a different scale or level.

Without a salvage gate, the archive can make two opposite mistakes.
It can either preserve too much and quietly smuggle a failed lane back into live prestige.
Or it can erase too much and lose the very fragments that later progress would rationally inherit.

The archive therefore needs one more honesty layer:
**lane-level defeat is not yet fragment-level uselessness.**

## What salvage-grade retention would have to cash out

### 1. Fragment identity disclosure

The archive should not say “something survives” without naming what the surviving thing actually is.
At minimum, it should ask:
- whether the retained fragment is mathematics, an effective reduced-domain model, a benchmark family, a no-go lesson, a bridge mechanism, or a phenomenological fit surface;
- what domain restriction keeps that fragment honest;
- whether the fragment depends on the lane's defeated global ontology or survives reinterpretation without it;
- and whether the retained item is actually load-bearing for later work or only culturally familiar.

If the fragment cannot be named tightly, “salvage” is usually just nostalgia.

### 2. Reinterpretation and domain honesty

A failed lane can leave something behind only if the archive says how that inheritance works.
At minimum, it should ask:
- whether the retained fragment survives as an approximation, an abstraction, a limit, a benchmark, or a methodological lesson;
- whether later lanes use it with the same meaning or only after reinterpretation;
- whether the surviving fragment has a narrower subject matter than the defeated lane claimed;
- and whether the fragment's remaining success depends on exactly the parts of the old lane that were *not* excluded.

Salvage without reinterpretation honesty can quietly launder a failed ontology back into canon.

### 3. No-credit-transfer discipline

The archive should not let surviving fragments rebuild lane-level prestige automatically.
At minimum, it should ask:
- whether the retained fragment supports the old lane itself or only later lanes that reuse it;
- whether the fragment is now common infrastructure available to multiple rivals rather than proprietary credit for the excluded lane;
- whether retaining the fragment changes any current atlas ranking, or only preserves a local tool / lesson;
- and whether the archive is mistakenly turning part-level usefulness into whole-lane resurrection.

A salvageable fragment may deserve preservation while the parent lane stays excluded.
That is not inconsistency; it is granularity discipline.

### 4. Salvage-grade retention

Even after exclusion, retention is earned only when:
- the surviving fragment is named precisely enough that the archive knows what is being preserved and what is not,
- the fragment has a bounded domain or reinterpretation story that does not depend on silently restoring the defeated lane's global claims,
- the fragment still performs distinctive explanatory, calculational, comparative, or constraining work for later search,
- and the archive can state the preservation level exactly: active reusable tool, benchmark relic, negative-constraint note, or memorial-only historical residue.

A lane may deserve exclusion while still failing full erasure.
That is not backsliding; it is disciplined salvage.

## Common failure modes

1. **Exclusion-to-erasure laundering** — a lane loses, so every fragment is thrown away with it.
2. **Relic prestige smuggling** — a retained fragment is used to sneak whole-lane credit back into the atlas.
3. **Domain amnesia** — a surviving effective or benchmark fragment is reused outside the restricted regime that made it successful.
4. **Reinterpretation evasion** — the archive pretends a fragment survives unchanged when it actually survives only under a new ontology or level description.
5. **Nostalgia preservation** — familiar mathematical or historical material is kept without any current explanatory, comparative, or constraining role.

## Immediate archive rules

1. **Do not convert exclusion-grade closure directly into fragment erasure.**
2. **Require explicit fragment identity and bounded-domain disclosure before claiming that anything from a defeated lane survives.**
3. **Do not let a salvaged fragment restore lane-level prestige unless a separate lane-level reopening rule is actually met.**
4. **Preserve reusable relics as one of four levels only: active tool, benchmark relic, negative-constraint note, or memorial-only residue.**
5. **Keep this gate subordinate to the retirement-versus-exclusion gate and upstream of any archive claim that a defeated lane left “nothing of value.”**
   This surface sharpens what exclusion still does *not* buy; it does not reopen lanes automatically or replace separate debts on recovery, observables, package disclosure, or witness closure.

## Net result

The archive now has a smaller and more honest sentence available:

> a completion bid may earn exclusion-grade closure and still leave salvage-grade relics once fragment identity, reinterpretation, and no-credit-transfer debt are faced honestly.

That blocks one more prestige / amnesia oscillation without widening the archive.
