# Design: Ecosystem Priority Ladder 2026

## Thesis
At this point in Rust’s evolution, the ecosystem is missing **reviewable control-plane layers** more than it is missing another pile of domain-specific wrappers.

The sharpest near-term contributions are the ones that make people faster, safer, and less dependent on folklore when they:
- build and iterate,
- choose crates and stacks,
- learn and teach,
- publish and review packages,
- and move between human, CI, editor, and agent workflows.

That does **not** mean domain kits stopped mattering.
It means the archive should default to ranking **cross-cutting evidence and handoff layers above narrower domain seams** unless a domain seam is clearly unlocking multiple frontier bands at once.

## Why this ranking now
Official signals are unusually aligned:
- The 2025 State of Rust survey says resource usage remains a major productivity problem, debugging is still high in the list of non-trivial problems, online docs remain the preferred canonical reference, and learning behavior appears to be shifting toward LLM/editor mediation.
  https://blog.rust-lang.org/2026/03/02/2025-State-Of-Rust-Survey-results/
- The 2025 compiler-performance survey says long rebuilds remain a real blocker, `cargo check` versus `cargo build` duplication is painful, editor/IDE latency matters, and users still lack good explanations for why builds are slow.
  https://blog.rust-lang.org/2025/09/10/rust-compiler-performance-survey-2025-results/
- Cargo’s current work makes build analysis, build-dir layout, target-dir locking, and workspace/config discovery first-class live problems rather than background wishes.
  https://rust-lang.github.io/rust-project-goals/2025h2/cargo-build-analysis.html
  https://rust-lang.github.io/rust-project-goals/2025h2/cargo-build-dir-layout.html
  https://blog.rust-lang.org/inside-rust/2026/02/18/this-development-cycle-in-cargo-1.94/
  https://blog.rust-lang.org/2026/03/13/call-for-testing-build-dir-layout-v2/
- crates.io now exposes materially richer review inputs: Security-tab advisory surfacing, Trusted Publishing with provider expansion and locked-down modes, SLOC, and `pubtime` in the index.
  https://blog.rust-lang.org/2025/02/05/crates-io-development-update/
  https://blog.rust-lang.org/2025/07/11/crates-io-development-update-2025-07/
  https://blog.rust-lang.org/2026/01/21/crates-io-development-update/
- Rust’s 2025 vision work recommends better crate guidance, more supportive diagnostics/guidance from crates, and more extensibility across the compilation workflow.
  https://blog.rust-lang.org/2025/12/19/what-do-people-love-about-rust/
- Rust’s 2026 goals keep pushing higher-level workflows and supply-chain clarity through `cargo script`, public/private dependency control, SBOM support, and other adoption-facing work.
  https://rust-lang.github.io/rust-project-goals/2026/flagships.html
- The Rust Foundation’s 2026–2028 strategy centers stable infrastructure, sustainable maintenance, and responsible adoption growth, which means the archive should keep ranking contributions that reduce ecosystem friction while making review and maintenance more durable.
  https://rustfoundation.org/strategic-plan/
  https://rustfoundation.org/media/annual-report-strategy-2025/

## Ranking rule
When choosing what to deepen next, score candidate contributions against five questions:
1. **Breadth of pain:** does it reduce recurring pain for a large share of Rust users or teams?
2. **Official momentum:** is Rust/Cargo/crates.io/Foundation already moving in this direction?
3. **Compositional leverage:** does it improve many downstream lanes rather than one domain only?
4. **Reviewability delta:** does it replace folklore or screenshots with portable artifacts and explicit checks?
5. **Companion-tool fit:** can it ship as a thin layer instead of demanding a winner-take-all platform rewrite?

The default move is to pick work that wins on at least **four** of those five questions.

What not to build:
- another cache daemon,
- another timings dashboard,

What not to build:
- a crates.io replacement,
- a permanent risk score,
- or a binary “secure package” badge.

- it has broad leverage for human onboarding, editor correctness, CI, and automation.

- no silent secret capture.

What not to build:
- another workspace manager,
- a forced Nix/devcontainer migration,
- or a fake “environment works” badge.

## Frontier-selection consequences for this archive
### Default next-cut ordering
When the archive has no urgent contrary signal, prefer this order:
1. Build-State Evidence
2. Adoption Decision
3. Canonical Learning
4. Package Admission
5. Workspace Environment
6. Maintenance Reality
7. Compatibility Claims
8. Atlas / Async Commons / Cargo Report
9. narrower substrate/domain kits

### What counts as a justified exception
Override the default order only when at least one of these is true:
- the official Rust/Cargo/crates.io roadmap has turned the seam into a live adoption blocker;
- a lower-layer domain kit is required to stop many later revisions from flattening an important reality;
- or the archive can make a small lane-map correction that materially strengthens several higher layers.

## Practical interpretation
The deepest missing thing in “ideal Rust” is increasingly **not** one more library family.
It is the ability to move between:
- ecosystem evidence,
- project decisions,
- canonical learning surfaces,
- package/release trust,
- and workspace/operator reality,

without losing freshness, authority, or reviewability.

That is why this ladder should steer the archive.
