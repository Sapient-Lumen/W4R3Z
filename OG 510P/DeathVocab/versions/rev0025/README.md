# DeathVocab — rev0025

## Begin with the scene, then ask what the setting permits

A remembered room, a phone call or an object left behind can be the beginning of a question before someone knows the term to search for. DeathVocab's [witnessed-moment method](files/DeathVocab-rev0025-2026.05.26.15.45-environmentatlas-exposuredeaths-hazardaudit/docs/10-method/witnessed-moment-as-primary-unit.md) starts there. It separates a witness's observation from their interpretation, the editor's interpretation and what remains unknown. A useful vocabulary should help someone find related material without making resemblance into an explanation of their particular experience.

The [setting note](files/DeathVocab-rev0025-2026.05.26.15.45-environmentatlas-exposuredeaths-hazardaudit/docs/20-setting/setting-is-not-background.md) adds a second question: who was able to be there, speak, receive information or leave a record? Place is part of the conditions under which an account becomes possible. The note asks for those differences to be retained, rather than ranking settings as though one kind of room could stand for every death.

This edition's [environment and exposure layer](files/DeathVocab-rev0025-2026.05.26.15.45-environmentatlas-exposuredeaths-hazardaudit/docs/00-meta/rev0025-environment-exposure-layer.md) widens that concern beyond the room. It connects exposure, witnessing, discovery, families' waiting and the way deaths enter a count. Its contribution is a set of questions and source-bound research structures, not a hazard-response manual. The scene-first approach and the wider scale belong together: neither a vivid individual account nor an aggregate category should silently replace the other.

For worked records, vocabulary mappings and the three proposed reader views, continue to the [main collection guide](../../README.md). The status below matters to that reading: the supplied records remain research pilots and scaffolds, not collected private testimony or an enabled public advice service.
