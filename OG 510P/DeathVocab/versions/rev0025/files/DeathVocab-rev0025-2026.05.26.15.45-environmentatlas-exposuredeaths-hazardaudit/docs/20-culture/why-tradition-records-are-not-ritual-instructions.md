# Why tradition records are not ritual instructions

Rev0009 opens the cultural/tradition axis, but only in quarantine. A public page may eventually help a reader recognize a word, understand why a family is asking for time or a person, and know which local authority to contact. It may not teach a rite.

The archive is vulnerable to a specific failure: because death practices are meaningful, a clean summary can feel respectful while actually stealing authority. The cube therefore separates five things:

1. A term exists.
2. A family used the term in a room.
3. A public source describes the term.
4. A tradition/community holder authorizes a surface.
5. A reader may act in a local situation.

Only the first three are present in this revision. The fourth is not yet present. The fifth is outside the archive.

The right action for the cube is usually: ask the family what matters, document preference, route to the correct person, and do not extract what is not offered.
