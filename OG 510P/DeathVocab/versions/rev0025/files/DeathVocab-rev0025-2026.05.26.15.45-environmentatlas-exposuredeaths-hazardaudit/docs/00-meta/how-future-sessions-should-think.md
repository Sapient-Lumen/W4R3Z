# How future sessions should think

## Do not inherit the latest revision as destiny

The most recent revision is only the current head. It is not the final ontology.

A future session may discover that “witnessed moment” is too narrow, that “practice” needs to be split from “craft,” that “afterward” deserves its own cube, that “setting” should be subordinated to “room authority,” or that “sensory channel” should be a primary axis. That is allowed.

The archive should preserve such disagreements rather than silently overwriting them.

## Prefer explicit uncertainty to smooth prose

Smooth prose is dangerous here. It can hide source weakness, cultural flattening, clinical ambiguity, jurisdictional variation, and grief coercion.

A good record should be able to say:

- this was observed in this setting;
- this term is used by these sources or roles;
- this may be interpreted differently;
- this is not a forecast;
- this is not advice;
- this is not ours to explain;
- this is currently unknown.

## Keep the dream bigger than the schema

Schemas are for preserving retrievability and safety. They are not the dream.

The dream is that the archive becomes a place where people can meet death’s real textures without being abandoned to panic, mystification, sales, or euphemism.

## Use revision work as a form of care

A revision can add records. It can also clarify a boundary, prune overclaim, improve a gateway, write a refusal standard, change a tone rule, or create a better re-entry packet. Those are not lesser work.

In this cube, sometimes the deepest progress is making it harder to publish prematurely.
