# What we are trying to earn

DeathVocab is not a product roadmap. It is an earned-authority project.

## The first earned right: quarantine

The cube has earned the right to hold low-risk public-source pilot records in quarantine. It has not earned the right to publish them. Quarantine is not a release queue; it is a laboratory where source, schema, tone, review, and nonclaims can be tested without exposing readers to immature authority.

## The second earned right: asking

The archive eventually needs oral and witnessed accounts. But asking for them is not neutral. To ask a hospice aide, funeral director, chaplain, dispatcher, spouse, dying person, or bereaved parent for a record is to ask them to reopen a room. The archive must earn the right to ask by providing consent, credit, anonymity, edit, withdrawal, and aftercare boundaries.

## The third earned right: public language

A public page may be read in shock. It may be read at 3 a.m. beside a body. It may be read by someone who cannot call a clinician, by someone who has already lost trust in institutions, or by someone whose culture has been misrepresented before. Public language therefore has to be slower than internal language.

It must not say “normal” when it means “seen before.” It must not say “common” when it only has a few public sources. It must not say “do this” when it means “some care teams do this.” It must not say “this means death is near” when it means “this can happen near death in some trajectories.”

## The fourth earned right: tradition

The archive can learn vocabulary from public sources. It cannot infer authority from that. A term like tahara, ghusl, Viaticum, tukdam, Antam Sanskar, or ofrenda may be visible in public sources and still not be the archive’s to explain in full.

The archive earns tradition material through relationship, permission, and the right of refusal.

## The fifth earned right: hard domains

The seed demands that ugly, political, institutional, sudden, pediatric, suicide, overdose, custody, disaster, war, and unclaimed deaths not be skipped. But naming them is not the same as publishing scenes from them.

These domains need higher gates because they are easier to sensationalize, easier to identify, easier to misread, and often connected to legal, institutional, or family harm.

## The sixth earned right: scale

Ten thousand revisions will not be earned by record count alone. Scale requires memory: why a source was rejected, why a phrase was softened, why a community refused, why a record was withdrawn, why a public page failed review, why an axis was replaced.

The cube should grow like an accountable workshop, not like a content farm.
