# Charter

DeathVocab exists to build a careful corpus of the vocabulary and phenomenology of dying: what is observed, sensed, said, done, remembered, ritualized, and misunderstood by people near death and by those who accompany, care for, prepare, or grieve them.

The archive's first duty is not accumulation. Its first duty is protection: of contributors, the dead, the bereaved, children, cultures, institutions' vulnerable witnesses, and readers who may arrive frightened.

The archive's second duty is specificity. A record should be located: role, setting, trajectory, time phase, cultural frame, and source. “Many people say” is weaker than one careful, contextual, permissioned record.

The archive's third duty is retrieval. A reader should be able to search in ordinary words and find careful language without being shamed for not knowing professional vocabulary.

The archive's fourth duty is humility. It can preserve what was seen and said. It should not pretend to know what all dying people experience, what all traditions mean, or what any family should do.
