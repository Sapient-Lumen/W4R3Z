# Inside-view evidence and nonproof

The cube needs an inside-view layer because the seed asks for what dying people experience when we can know. But interiority is ethically dangerous: the archive can easily overread silence, flatten spiritual language, pathologize meaning, or convert survivor testimony into spectacle.

Use the evidence verbs:

- **reported** — the person said it or later remembered it.
- **witnessed** — someone saw/heard something externally.
- **inferred** — a witness interpreted from role, relationship, or pattern.
- **clinically described** — a public clinical/professional source names the phenomenon.
- **unknown** — the archive has no access.

A public sentence must never silently upgrade one verb into another.

Do not say “she was peaceful” when the evidence is “her body was still.”
Do not say “he was hallucinating” when the evidence is “he spoke to his dead mother.”
Do not say “near-death experiences show what death is like” when the evidence is a recovered-survivor report.
Do not say “they could not hear” when the evidence is silence.

The inside-view layer is a humility machine.
