# Three public surfaces, one governed corpus

Status: product-shape sketch, not implementation.

DeathVocab probably needs three outward surfaces sharing one governed archive.

## 1. Plain-language witness surface

Audience: a person who saw something and is trying to understand it.

Design rule: start from the words a shocked person uses, not from expert terms. Return orientation, not diagnosis.

## 2. Practitioner apprenticeship surface

Audience: hospice workers, nurses, aides, doulas, chaplains, funeral directors, first responders, medical students, volunteers, and family carers learning the work.

Design rule: preserve the small practical knowledge that rarely enters textbooks: phrasing, pacing, room-reading, mouth care explanations, family dynamics, institutional handoffs, and what experienced people notice before they can fully explain it.

## 3. Provenance and scholarship surface

Audience: researchers, editors, tradition holders, clinical reviewers, contributors, and long-term maintainers.

Design rule: every public-facing sentence should be traceable back to record state, source type, consent, axis locations, review status, nonclaims, and rollback dependencies.

## Shared archive rule

The same underlying record can appear differently across surfaces. A family-facing page may omit graphic or restricted detail. A practitioner-facing page may include more context but still avoid advice or prediction. A scholar-facing page may expose source and review metadata. The corpus should model these as views, not separate truths.
