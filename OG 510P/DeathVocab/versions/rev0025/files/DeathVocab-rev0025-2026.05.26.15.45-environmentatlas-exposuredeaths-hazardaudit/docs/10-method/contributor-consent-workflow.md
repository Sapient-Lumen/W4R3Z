# Contributor consent workflow

DeathVocab cannot be built by scraping grief. The corpus needs living witnesses, but living witnesses need control.

## Consent is layered

A contributor can agree to a private interview but not publication. They can permit paraphrase but not quotation. They can allow a practitioner-facing training note but not a lay public page. They can allow a record to remain inside the archive while refusing use in search, glossary, or examples.

The workflow therefore separates:

- collection consent
- storage consent
- quotation consent
- paraphrase consent
- role attribution or anonymity
- publication surface
- future reuse
- edit rights
- withdrawal rights

## Branches

### Public source

No personal consent is required for ordinary public institutional, scholarly, or patient-facing sources, but this does not make the source automatically safe. Medical content still needs clinical safety review, and public social-media visibility should not be treated as consent for extraction.

### Professional witness

Professionals may carry institutional risk, patient-confidentiality obligations, and moral injury. The intake should ask for role and setting without asking for identifying patient details. A record should not name an institution unless explicit permission and review exist.

### Family or bereaved witness

Family accounts can be the most useful and the most dangerous. A relative may be entitled to tell their own experience, but the record may still expose the dead person, other relatives, a conflict, a diagnosis, or a stigmatized circumstance. Publication requires a de-identification and family-impact review.

### Dying-person first-person account

First-person accounts from people facing death require special care around voluntariness, capacity, timing, future use, and whether the person wants posthumous revision or removal to be possible.

### Tradition holder

Some knowledge may only be named as existing. Some may be kept in a restricted room. Some may not belong in the corpus at all. Permission must come from authorized holders, not from an outsider who has read about the practice.

## Withdrawal

Withdrawal is not a courtesy; it is part of the architecture. If a contributor withdraws, derived surfaces must be found and repaired: search aliases, glossary examples, summaries, teaching pages, and related-record prose.
