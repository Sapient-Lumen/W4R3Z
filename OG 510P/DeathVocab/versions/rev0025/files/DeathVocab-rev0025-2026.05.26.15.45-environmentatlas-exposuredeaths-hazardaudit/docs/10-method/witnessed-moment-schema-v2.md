# Witnessed-moment schema v2

`rev0002` named the north star: DeathVocab should preserve the witnessed scene before attaching a label. `rev0003` turns that into an operational schema.

## The record is not a term

A term-first archive asks: “What is death rattle?” A witnessed-moment archive asks: “What did the room hear, when did it happen, who heard it, what words did they search later, what did a source say, what cannot be inferred, and what safety boundary belongs on the page?”

The term still matters, but it becomes an index handle. The record keeps the moment intact enough that a reader can arrive from ordinary language and a practitioner can still see the professional crosswalk.

## Required layers

Every record now has these layers:

1. **source packet** — source ids, source ladder level, collection method, consent state, and limitations.
2. **moment** — plain scene, bounded time, witness position, sensory channels, ordinary-language queries, and professional terms.
3. **axes** — phenomenology, timeline, trajectory, setting, perspective, cultural frame, safety domain, stability.
4. **content layers** — observation, source synthesis, interpretation, what-this-is-not, uncertainties, lay orientation, practitioner notes.
5. **provenance** — why the record exists, how it was made, what it depends on, and what could invalidate it.
6. **safety/review** — clinical, cultural, child, suicide, identifiability, graphicness, institutional-retaliation, and public-interface gates.
7. **links** — related glossary terms, seed cues, search aliases, related records, and contrasting records.

## First constraint

A record can be useful before it is publishable. The five `rev0003` records are therefore `quarantined_public_source_pilot`: they test the schema against public sources, but are not declared public pages and are not clinical guidance.
