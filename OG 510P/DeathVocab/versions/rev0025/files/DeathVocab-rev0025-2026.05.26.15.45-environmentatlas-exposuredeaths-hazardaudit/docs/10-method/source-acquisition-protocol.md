# Source acquisition protocol

The corpus should begin with public, citable, low-risk sources not because they are the deepest sources, but because they let the machinery fail safely. Private bedside testimony should enter only after the source packet, consent workflow, withdrawal path, de-identification policy, and review lanes are ready.

## Acquisition sequence

1. **Public source pilots**: family-facing, clinical, scholarly, and organizational sources. These test extraction, crosswalks, and safety notes.
2. **Professional witness interviews**: hospice and palliative workers, aides, doulas, chaplains, funeral workers, first responders, and related roles. These add lived craft knowledge.
3. **Family and bereaved witness interviews**: entered slowly, with de-identification and family-impact review.
4. **Tradition-governed rooms**: added only with permission from authorized holders, and sometimes held as “exists but details withheld.”
5. **Institutional and political deaths**: added with retaliation and legal/context review, not flattened into neutral uncertainty.

## Source packet minimum

Every source candidate needs:

- source id
- source type
- source ladder level
- collection method
- consent or permission state
- citation or locator
- what it supports
- what it does not support
- safety domains triggered
- correction or withdrawal path

## Rejection and held-source logging

Not every source should become a record. Rejected and held sources should still be logged privately enough that future builders understand what was considered and why it was not used.

Common reasons to hold or reject:

- seed cue only, no independent source
- identifiable private story without permission
- public social media that is too intimate to extract ethically
- suicide method detail
- pediatric detail without highest gate
- restricted cultural detail without authority
- sensational or voyeuristic framing
- unclear source chain
- active legal or institutional-retaliation risk
