# Witnessed moment as the primary unit

Status: method note for future schema work.

## Why this matters

A term-first corpus will over-flatten the material. People do not usually arrive with the vocabulary. They arrive with a remembered scene: the breathing, the reaching hand, the smell, the silent television, the phone call, the mouth, the cat, the estranged sibling, the nurse's phrase, the body leaving the house.

A witnessed-moment-first corpus can still support glossary entries, but it keeps the lived observation from being consumed by the label.

## Definition

A **witnessed moment** is a bounded observation or practice around dying, death, care, ritual, institutional handling, or aftermath, recorded with enough context to support search, comparison, restraint, and ethical review.

## Search crosswalk examples

- Family phrase: “reached for the air” → possible terms: picking/plucking, reaching, terminal restlessness, visioning; nonclaim: not enough to diagnose cause or inner experience.
- Family phrase: “she wanted to go home but she was home” → possible terms: going-home language, transition metaphors, disorientation, symbolic language; nonclaim: not proof of a single spiritual or neurological explanation.
- Family phrase: “he sounded like he was drowning” → possible terms: terminal secretions, death rattle, air hunger, respiratory disease; nonclaim: not a medication or care instruction.

## Publication posture

The stronger the scene, the stronger the gate. Beauty, poignancy, or pedagogical usefulness never substitutes for consent, de-identification, cultural permission, and harm review.
