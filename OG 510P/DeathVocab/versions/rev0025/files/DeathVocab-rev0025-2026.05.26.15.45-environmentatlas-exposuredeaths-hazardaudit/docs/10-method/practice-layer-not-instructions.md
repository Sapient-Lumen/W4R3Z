# Practice layer, not instructions

Revision: rev0005

The seed asks for actual hospice and bedside practice — mouth care, positioning, speaking in the room, handling the body after death, phone calls, and the little verbal toolkit — but it also says the corpus is observational rather than prescriptive. Rev0005 therefore adds care/practice records as witnessed-moment companions, not as how-to pages.

A practice record may say that a kind of care move exists, where it appears, what words surround it, what public sources support it, and what review it needs. It may not tell a reader what to do for the person in front of them unless that instruction has been reviewed, localized, and placed in an appropriate surface.

Each practice record has four mandatory guardrails:

1. A source packet with named source IDs.
2. A `what_this_is_not` list.
3. A `clinical_boundary` or domain boundary.
4. A route back to local professionals, care plans, law, or tradition holders where appropriate.
