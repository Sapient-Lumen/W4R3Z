# Closed-room evidence and negative proof

Closed institutional deaths create a special evidence problem. The witnesses may be staff; the first words may be official; the body may be inaccessible; the family may receive data categories before narrative; and later reviews may be partial, delayed, confidential, or contested.

The cube must therefore separate five streams: family memory, staff witness, official trace, public data, and advocacy or oversight claims. None is automatically the whole truth.

The archive may record access gaps. It may record that a family hears words such as pending, under review, restraint, seclusion, detainee death, death in custody, critical incident, root cause analysis, duty of candour, or mortality dashboard. It may not infer culpability from absence, infer exoneration from official silence, or publish case-specific allegations.
