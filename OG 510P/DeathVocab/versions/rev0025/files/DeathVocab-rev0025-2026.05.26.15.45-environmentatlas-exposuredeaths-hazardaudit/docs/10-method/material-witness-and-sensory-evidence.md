# Material witness and sensory evidence

A material witness is not a person. It is the chair, cup, swab, blanket, monitor, call bell, oxygen concentrator, ring, pet, lamp, window, phone, tissue box, stained sheet, or room condition that carries part of the death forward.

DeathVocab should learn to ask about material witnesses because many bedside scenes are remembered through them. The archive should also learn not to publish them casually. Objects can identify people, expose family conflict, reveal medical details, disclose religious practice, and turn a private room into spectacle.

## Extraction prompts

Ask contributors, with consent and review:

- What could be heard in the room?
- What could be smelled, and what should not be published?
- What did people touch or avoid touching?
- What ordinary objects became important?
- What machines, alarms, phones, or doorways interrupted the room?
- What was removed after death?
- What detail came back later in grief?

## Rendering rule

Sensory detail is only useful when it serves orientation, truthfulness, dignity, and care. If a detail mainly makes the reader stare, cut it.
