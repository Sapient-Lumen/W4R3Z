
# Utterance atlas notes

Rev0006 begins the language layer with sixteen quarantined records. The layer separates five things that are often collapsed:

1. **Dying-person utterances** — what the person says or seems to say.
2. **Family search phrases** — what a bereaved person types afterward.
3. **Practitioner response language** — what staff or volunteers say in the room.
4. **Interpretation frames** — clinical, spiritual, cultural, relational, metaphorical, or unknown.
5. **Archive wording** — the public surface's own careful language.

The same words can move across these lanes. “I want to go home” may be a direct request, a dementia phrase, a spiritual phrase, a distress phrase, or a family memory sharpened by grief. The archive should not flatten it. It should route the reader to context and uncertainty.

The utterance atlas also adds a silence lane. A dying person may stop speaking hours or days before death. That absence can be as important to a family as a spoken sentence. The corpus must not imply that a death without last words is incomplete.
