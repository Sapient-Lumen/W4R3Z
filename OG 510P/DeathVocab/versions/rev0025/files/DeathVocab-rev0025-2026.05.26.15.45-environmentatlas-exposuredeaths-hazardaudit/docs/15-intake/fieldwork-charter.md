# Fieldwork charter — rev0015

DeathVocab is not ready to collect private testimony. It is ready to rehearse how it would protect testimony.

The core shift in this revision is from **source expansion** to **consent-bearing fieldwork design**. The cube now has 186 quarantined public-source records, but the seed premise is that the living knowledge of dying is largely oral, distributed across roles, culturally fragmented, and rarely written with bedside texture. Public sources can keep the office honest; they cannot replace the corpus.

## Governing line

**The archive may ask for memory only after it can protect the person offering it.**

## The first eligible pilot is not a family grief harvest

The first plausible pilot is low-risk practitioner craft with no patient identifiers: how a hospice nurse explains noisy breathing, how an aide thinks about mouth care as presence, how a funeral director describes the second goodbye without naming a family, how a chaplain or social worker hears euphemisms without spiritualizing them.

Freshly bereaved family accounts, children's words, suicide scenes, custody deaths, restricted cultural material, disaster/war scenes, domestic violence deaths, body images, 911 calls, and active investigations remain blocked or policy-only.

## What this revision earns

It earns only a dry run: fictional or harmless material can be used to test invitation, consent, recording-start language, stopping, transcript review, quote release, de-identification, withdrawal, and protected data custody.

It does **not** earn real testimony collection, public quote, public paraphrase, or release-ready records.
