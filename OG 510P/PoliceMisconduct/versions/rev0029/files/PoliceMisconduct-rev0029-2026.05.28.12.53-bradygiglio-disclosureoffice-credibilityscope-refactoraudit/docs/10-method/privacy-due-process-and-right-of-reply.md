# Privacy, due process, and right of reply

The corpus serves accountability, not punishment by database. Its legitimacy depends on preserving due process and minimizing harm to civilians and families.

## Privacy before display

A person can appear in a public record and still deserve minimization. Minors, sexual assault survivors, medical/mental-health crisis subjects, undocumented people,
informants, witnesses, and bereaved families receive protective defaults.

## Due process before aggregation

Officers under investigation, cleared officers, exonerated officers, acquitted officers, and officers with reversed findings must carry that status clearly.
Aggregation should not hide exculpatory or status-changing information.

## Right of reply

A future public corpus needs a correction and contest process. Corrections should not become a backdoor for manipulation, but absence of a process is unacceptable.
Correction requests should be tracked as records with status, evidence, disposition, and rollback effects.
