# Source conflict lifecycle

A source conflict is not a failure of the cube. It is one of the main things the cube exists to preserve.

## Lifecycle

1. **Observation** — a source page, document label, press release, docket item, monitor report, or local page is observed.
2. **Weak event** — the observation becomes a source-scoped nonclaim.
3. **Conflict trigger** — another source family disagrees, lags, narrows, or broadens the status.
4. **Reconciliation ticket** — the cube records the conflict and the missing carrier needed to resolve it.
5. **Accession** — the relevant order/docket/agreement/report is frozen or locator-pinned.
6. **Candidate** — a current-status candidate may be drafted with uncertainty and rollback triggers.
7. **Display gate** — public copy is reviewed for due process, history retention, and overclaim risk.
8. **Rollback** — if a later source contradicts or clarifies the claim, the candidate changes and dependent views update.

## Rev0005 examples

- New Orleans and Newark: source-page label vs press-release terminal event.
- Cleveland: motion signal vs enforcement page label.
- Orange County: mixed-agency row and child-agency status.
- Louisville/Minneapolis: closure/retraction language and history-retention guard.

## Public posture

Public display should prefer phrasing like:

> Official sources differ or are incomplete. DOJ's cases page lists X; a DOJ press release says Y; the cube has not yet verified the current court status.

That sentence is more honest than a premature badge that says `Closed` or `Enforcement`.
