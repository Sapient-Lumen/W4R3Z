# History retention after closure, dismissal, termination, completion, or retraction

Closure is not erasure.

The cube must preserve historical public-record carriers even after a consent decree terminates, a lawsuit is dismissed, an agreement completes, or a findings report is retracted.

This matters for Louisville and Minneapolis especially, because DOJ closure/retraction language creates a risk that earlier public records will be treated as if they never existed. The archive must instead keep dated carriers with exact source states.

A public page may say: “This source family later changed federal posture.” It must not say: “There was no prior public record.”
