# Not-overly-constrained design note

The project needs guardrails, but it also needs a horizon.

The danger of this corpus is obvious: defamation, privacy harm, misidentification, source drift, extraction from grieving families, and misuse by hostile actors. The previous revisions correctly built gates before people.

A subtler danger is overlearning caution. A cube that never leaves source inventory will be safe but too weak. It will not make wandering impossible, it will not help defense lawyers at the point of testimony, it will not surface repeated settlement costs, and it will not let families find records in one place.

Rev0015 therefore separates three kinds of constraint.

## Permanent constraints

These are identity conditions for the project:

- source-bound claims;
- adjudication-state precision;
- privacy and dignity for civilians/families/witnesses/vulnerable groups;
- officer due process and correction route;
- no name-only entity merge;
- public record does not equal public display;
- no police-intelligence drift against civilians;
- rollback and defeaters remain first-class.

## Phase-bound constraints

These are current safety choices, not destiny:

- no live person records yet;
- DOJ SLS first;
- department/source pages before public officer lookup;
- no summaries before capture/privacy;
- no status banners before proof;
- no canonical agencies before identifier gates.

A future revision may relax a phase-bound constraint only by naming what it earned first.

## Unknown constraints

Some issues are not yet settled:

- what role-gated access should look like;
- whether public defender workflows need separate governance;
- how families can participate in incident narrative boundaries;
- how open data should be limited to avoid misuse;
- when international or adjacent justice-system surfaces should begin.

Unknown constraints should become open questions, not hidden assumptions.

## The operating formula

**Speculation belongs in horizon space. Implementation belongs behind gates. Public display belongs behind source, privacy, due-process, freshness, and correction review.**
