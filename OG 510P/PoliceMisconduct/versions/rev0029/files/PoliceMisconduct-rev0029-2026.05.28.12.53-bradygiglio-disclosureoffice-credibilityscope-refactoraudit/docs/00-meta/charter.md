# Charter

PoliceMisconduct exists to construct a normalized, source-bound, ethics-forward public-record substrate for police misconduct accountability.

The archive is not built to win an argument by rhetoric. It is built to preserve the records, distinctions, corrections, and uncertainty
that allow journalists, litigators, defenders, researchers, oversight bodies, communities, and families to reason from evidence.

## Primary aims

1. Normalize records across jurisdictions without flattening legal, procedural, or source differences.
2. Make officer mobility, repeat-defendant patterns, settlement costs, discipline outcomes, decertification, and Brady/Giglio-relevant credibility issues discoverable when lawfully and ethically supportable.
3. Protect civilians, families, witnesses, minors, survivors, and vulnerable communities from extractive or dangerous publication.
4. Protect officer due process by preserving allegation/finding/outcome distinctions and correction routes.
5. Create an archive that can absorb thousands of revisions without losing its source boundaries.

## What counts as progress

Progress means one of:

- a clearer source carrier;
- a safer privacy/display rule;
- a better adjudication vocabulary;
- a stronger entity-resolution threshold;
- a reproducible acquisition protocol;
- a rollback rule for a known failure mode;
- a smaller, clearer pilot scope;
- a validated schema fixture;
- a resolved open question;
- or a recorded non-take that prevents future loops.

## Failure modes to resist

- allegation laundering;
- settlement overreading;
- name-only identity merges;
- official-source monoculture;
- privacy harm hidden behind public-record legality;
- data-product drift toward police targeting;
- archive bloat without stronger truth;
- justice-flavored rhetoric replacing careful records.
