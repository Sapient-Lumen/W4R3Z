# Rev0015 mile-high telos charter

Rev0015 pauses ingestion and looks back down from altitude.

The project is not merely a database of police misconduct. It is an attempt to build a public-record accountability substrate: a durable layer where source custody, claim states, entity resolution, privacy, missingness, correction, and rollback are strong enough that downstream civic users can do work they cannot safely or cheaply do now.

The project should remain legible as a dream before it becomes legible as a product.

## The deepest telos

The cube should make democracy less forgetful.

Police accountability records are fragmented by jurisdiction, record type, law, court system, local newsroom survival, union contract, disclosure regime, agency structure, and time. The seed names the core consequence: there is no national database of police misconduct, and officers can move across jurisdictions after serious misconduct because the public record is scattered, inconsistent, sealed, stale, or inaccessible.

The cube's mature job is to make that kind of fragmentation less protective.

Not by shouting. Not by flattening. Not by treating allegation as finding or settlement as admission. Not by extracting families' grief into a public spectacle. Not by making a police-facing intelligence product.

It should make public records source-bound, normalized, preserved, contestable, and usable.

## What we are trying to earn

We are trying to earn the right to do dangerous useful things:

- name an officer publicly when the source support, identity evidence, adjudication state, privacy review, freshness, and correction route are strong enough;
- connect an officer's career across departments without name-only merging;
- show repeat-defendant and repeat-settlement patterns without turning litigation into guilt;
- show department-level costs and policies without hiding individual recurrence;
- help defense lawyers locate credibility/disclosure records without creating a tool for harassment;
- help families find every public reference to a loved one's death or injury without claiming ownership of the story;
- show when reform ended, changed, failed, or was rolled back without erasing historical public findings;
- make missingness visible when records are sealed, destroyed, denied, stale, paywalled, paper-only, or politically revised.

The word is **earn**. The cube does not get these capabilities because they are important. It earns them through custody, gates, nonclaims, privacy, due process, and rollback.

## Current caution is scaffolding, not destiny

The current no-person posture is correct for the source-graph pilot. It is not the telos.

The DOJ SLS pilot is useful because it teaches status drift, source custody, public display restraint, monitor-report archives, and institutional patterns. It is not the whole project.

Department-level source inventory is a safe early public surface. It is not the final product.

The cube must eventually become brave enough to represent people, incidents, lawsuits, settlements, and officer mobility. But it should become brave by becoming accurate, source-bound, and correctable, not by becoming reckless.

## Dream big, gate hard

A future session should be allowed to propose a wandering-officer alert, a Brady/Giglio workbench, a family incident packet, an arbitration tracker, a FOIA engine, a union-contract graph, a death-in-custody layer, or an international comparison surface.

Those ideas belong in the cube as dreams or horizon entries before they become implementation commitments. The point is not to suppress them. The point is to preserve them in a state that cannot accidentally become a public claim.

## The design center

The design center is not a single website. It is a set of linked civic views over a shared substrate:

1. source graph;
2. preservation/fixity graph;
3. claim graph;
4. agency graph;
5. incident graph;
6. officer/career graph;
7. lawsuit/settlement graph;
8. policy/union/monitor graph;
9. public and role-gated civic interfaces;
10. correction, contestation, and rollback layer.

The public interface should be narrower than the archive. The archive should be narrower than everything technically obtainable. The telos should remain wider than the current pilot.

## What future sessions should inherit

Future sessions should inherit permanent commitments:

- public record does not mean public display;
- allegation, finding, settlement, adjudication, exoneration, reversal, and correction stay distinct;
- source rows are not misconduct claims;
- name-only identity resolution is prohibited;
- civilians, families, witnesses, minors, survivors, undocumented people, people in crisis, and other vulnerable groups need special protection;
- officer due process is a condition of trustworthy accountability;
- missingness is a fact, not an absence claim;
- rollback and correction are first-class;
- the cube must not become a police intelligence tool against civilians.

Future sessions should not inherit as permanent:

- no person records forever;
- DOJ SLS as the only pilot;
- official sources as sufficient;
- department-level public pages as the final product;
- current status caution as current status silence forever;
- JSON ledgers as the only possible data shape;
- U.S.-only scope as a philosophical boundary;
- source inventory as the whole civic value.

## Short maxim

**Dream big. Gate hard. Claim carefully. Correct visibly. Remember why.**
