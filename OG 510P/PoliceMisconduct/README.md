# PoliceMisconduct

## Accountability without laundering an allegation into a finding

This project asks what a public-record archive would need in order to support careful accountability. Its charter includes discovering patterns, but also preserving the differences that make a pattern claim defensible: identities, jurisdictions, source types, procedural states, corrections and uncertainty.

Those obligations extend in several directions. Civilians, families and vulnerable witnesses can be harmed by publication; officers can be harmed by a false merge or by treating an allegation as an adjudicated outcome. Public availability alone does not settle the ethics of redisclosure.

## The supplied material is not a public accusation service

The revision’s own entry page blocks live Brady/Giglio records, officer list status, case-specific nondisclosure claims and person-specific public credibility labels. Its schemas, synthetic fixtures and scope definitions should be read as a design argument about what evidence would be needed, not as evidence against anyone.

The most useful reading question is how the archive permits correction. A dataset that makes an accusation easy to find but its changed status difficult to discover would reproduce the failure it claims to address. The charter’s attention to rollback, source boundaries and recorded decisions not to proceed is therefore substantive rather than decorative.

This guide offers no legal conclusion, new entity identification or expanded access. Historical acquisition and review protocols are preserved text; none has been executed for this editorial work.

Read beside [Radical Governance](../Radical-Governance/README.md) for usable remedy and [MissingKnowledgeHalf](../MissingKnowledgeHalf/README.md) for the importance of retaining failed or withdrawn interpretations.
