# Kernel Operators

This file states the smallest operator set the archive currently regards as indispensable. The point is compression: future revisions should work through this kernel rather than adding ever more metaphysical nouns.

## The kernel at a glance

| Operator | Core question | What it explains | What breaks without it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Form | What makes this the kind of thing it is? | individuation, constitution, kindhood, organization | parts-lists replace entities |
| Process | How can a thing persist while changing? | becoming, maintenance, development, decay | stasis becomes the default picture |
| Power | What can this thing do or undergo? | modality, tendency, production, constraint | laws and capacities become underdescribed |
| Structure | How can relations be constitutive? | networks, ordering, systemic identity | reality looks like a heap of atoms |
| Grounding | In virtue of what does this obtain? | constitutive dependence and ontological order | dependence collapses into causation or disappears |

**Emergence** is not a sixth peer operator. It is a **threshold notion** used when form, process, power, structure, and grounding jointly support a real higher-level profile.

## Operator-by-operator notes

### 1. Form

Form answers the question of **this-thinghood**: not merely what parts are present, but what organization makes them one thing of a certain kind.

Retain form because without it the archive cannot explain:

- why organisms differ from heaps,
- why artifacts have constitution conditions,
- or why persistence often tracks organization rather than matter-token identity.

### 2. Process

Process answers the question of **being-in-time**: how a thing can remain itself through activity, replacement, maintenance, and development.

Retain process because without it the archive cannot explain:

- living turnover,
- eventful persistence,
- temporal passage,
- or why some entities are better modeled as stabilized activity than as static stuff.

### 3. Power

Power answers the question of **modal profile**: what a thing can do, undergo, resist, or make possible.

Retain power because without it the archive struggles to explain:

- lawfulness,
- tendencies and capacities,
- productive constraint,
- and the difference between a merely actual profile and a real dispositional one.

The fuller typed treatment now lives in `54-powers-dispositions-abilities-and-manifestation.md`.

### 4. Structure

Structure answers the question of **relational constitution**: when what something is depends on the system of relations in which it stands.

Retain structure because without it the archive cannot adequately explain:

- systems,
- networks,
- role-defined entities,
- and domains where organization outruns intrinsic microprofile.

The fuller typed treatment now lives in `55-structure-patterns-networks-and-relational-constitution.md`.

### 5. Grounding

Grounding answers the question of **ontological order**: what obtains in virtue of what.

Retain grounding because without it the archive cannot distinguish:

- causal production from constitutive dependence,
- derivative reality from unreality,
- or layered being from flat inventory metaphysics.

## Joints among the operators

The operators are not independent islands.

- **Form** often determines which **powers** a thing has.
- **Process** often sustains the **form** of a thing across time.
- **Structure** may be the relational side of **form** in complex systems.
- **Grounding** orders how forms, structures, and processes depend on more basic realities.
- **Emergence** is licensed only when these relations stabilize a genuinely new explanatory and modal profile.

## Compression rules derived from the kernel

A future addition should normally do one of four things:

1. sharpen one kernel operator,
2. show that two operators can be compressed,
3. demonstrate a missed indispensable operator,
4. or provide a hard case the current kernel mishandles.

If a proposed concept does none of these, the archive should probably not keep it.
