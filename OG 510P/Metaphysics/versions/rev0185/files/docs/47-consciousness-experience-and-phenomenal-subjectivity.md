# Consciousness, Experience, and Phenomenal Subjectivity

## Why this file exists

The archive already has files on emergence, levels, representation, centeredness, agency, personhood, life, and mental-causation pressure. But it still lacked a direct rule for **consciousness** itself.

Without a dedicated file here, the archive risks six opposite mistakes:

- collapsing **consciousness** into mere access, report, discrimination, or control,
- inflating consciousness into a detached **mental substance** whenever third-person explanation feels incomplete,
- treating **conscious state**, **conscious subject**, **self-conscious subject**, and **person** as if they were one flat kind,
- treating **phenomenality**, **intentionality**, **attention**, and **representation** as if one of them automatically settled all the others,
- assuming that a neural correlate or functional role is already a finished metaphysical answer,
- or treating consciousness as either obviously fundamental everywhere or obviously unreal anywhere without a typed intermediate profile.

This file gives the archive a compact **consciousness profile** so future revisions can say whether a case is chiefly about phenomenal state, conscious subjecthood, self-consciousness, access, reportability, intentional presentation, or only an informational or behavioral proxy.

## Working default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Consciousness is real as a centered, subject-involving mode of manifestation or appearing, borne by organized beings or other genuine subjects, and not exhausted by access, report, or third-person functional description; but it need not, by default, be treated either as a separate substance or as mentality all the way down.**

This fits the archive's wider commitments:

- **process** still matters, because consciousness is episodic, temporally extended, stream-like, interruptible, and developmentally variable,
- **form** still matters, because conscious life is not a heap of atomized feels but an organized field or stream with structure,
- **power** still matters, because conscious states alter salience, guidance, memory, aversion, deliberation, and attention,
- **structure** still matters, because binding, integration, subject-pole organization, and world-presentation are not ontologically idle,
- **grounding** still matters, because conscious episodes should not float free of organisms, subjects, or sustaining realizers,
- and **life and personhood** still matter, because many conscious cases are neither mere metabolic organization nor already full personhood.

## Core distinctions

### 1. Conscious state, conscious subject, self-conscious subject, and person are not the same

The archive should distinguish at least four things:

- **conscious state** — an episode or condition with experiential character,
- **conscious subject** — the locus or bearer of such states,
- **self-conscious subject** — a subject that can in some way be aware of itself as itself,
- **person** — a thicker profile involving some integration of subjecthood, first-personality, agency, normative standing, and diachronic organization.

Without this distinction the archive will keep sliding from “there is experience here” to “there is a self here”, and from “there is a self here” to “there is a full person here”.

### 2. Phenomenality, access, report, attention, and wakefulness are not the same

The archive should keep apart:

- **phenomenality** — what it is like, or the manifest character of experience,
- **access** — availability for use in reasoning, control, memory, or report,
- **reportability** — what can be verbally or behaviorally expressed,
- **attention** — selective amplification or prioritization,
- **wakefulness / arousal** — background enabling condition rather than consciousness by itself.

These often travel together in ordinary adult waking life. But they can come apart in dreams, anesthesia edge cases, blindsight-style cases, absorbed skill, automatic behavior, and degraded-report conditions.

### 3. Phenomenality and intentionality are deeply related but not identical

Some experiences seem essentially world-presenting: seeing, hearing, feeling, imagining, remembering, anticipating, caring. But the archive should not assume that **aboutness** automatically explains **what-it-is-like-ness**, or that phenomenality is just an inner paint detached from world-presentation.

So future revisions should ask:

- whether the conscious state is chiefly sensory, affective, imagistic, thought-like, bodily, or mixed,
- whether its intentional target is determinate, vague, objectual, situation-like, bodily, self-directed, or none,
- and whether the state's phenomenal profile is supposed to be identical with, realized by, or only systematically linked to its intentional profile.

### 4. Neural correlate is not yet realization, identity, or grounding

The archive should not infer metaphysics too quickly from neuroscience.

A neural correlate can show that a conscious state depends on a neural organization or is lawfully associated with it. But that still leaves open whether the relation is best modeled as:

- type-identity,
- token-identity,
- realization,
- grounding,
- emergent dependence,
- aspect-duality,
- neutral-basis manifestation,
- or some mixed story.

So correlation is evidentially powerful, but metaphysically underdetermining.

### 5. Consciousness can be derivative but robust

The archive should resist two temptations:

- **flat reductionism**, where consciousness is only a convenient gloss on information processing, access, or disposition to report,
- **flat dualism**, where consciousness is treated as a second realm whose relation to nature remains largely mysterious.

Many cases are better treated as **derivative but robust conscious reality**. A conscious episode may be:

- dependent without being unreal,
- realized in living or otherwise organized subjects without being exhausted by external description,
- causally relevant without needing magical exemption from the causal order,
- and centered without being merely private in the sense of metaphysical arbitrariness.

### 6. Mental causation is a pressure test, not an automatic refutation

The archive should treat mental causation as a live explanatory burden.

If pain, perception, intention, attention, and conscious deliberation matter, then the archive must say how they matter. But it should not assume that the only options are:

- **epiphenomenal impotence**,
- **mysterious overdetermination**,
- or **strict reduction on pain of exclusion**.

A more promising default is to ask whether conscious states are causally efficacious **through** their realized organization, control role, and integration into wider subject-level processes.

### 7. Keep rival packages live

Future revisions should keep live at least these rival packages:

- **reductive / illusion-friendly views** — experience-talk is thinner than it seems, or explained away through function, access, report, or self-modeling,
- **non-reductive emergence / realization views** — consciousness is real and dependent, but not straightforwardly reducible,
- **property-dualist or dual-aspect views** — consciousness is irreducible and may require a second aspect or property family,
- **panpsychist or cosmopsychist views** — mentality is fundamental and widespread, with combination or constitution carrying the burden,
- **neutral-monist / Russellian views** — the basal layer is neither merely mental nor merely physical, and conscious/physical descriptions capture different organizational or aspectual profiles.

The archive need not settle the rivalry yet. But it should stop speaking as if one word like **mind**, **experience**, or **awareness** had already chosen among them.

## Current archive answer

The archive should adopt a **typed phenomenal realism** inside its wider layered process realism:

> **Conscious experience is real as a centered, organized mode of manifestation in a subject; it is typically dependent on living or otherwise richly integrated organization, but it is not well modeled either as a mere reporting disposition or as a detached mental substance.**

In compressed form:

> **Consciousness is neither an eliminable user-illusion nor, by default, a separate ontological kingdom. It is a real subject-involving mode of being that demands typed dependence discipline.**

This does **not** yet settle whether the best deep underpinning is emergentist, dual-aspect, panpsychist, neutral-monist, or something else. But it does rule out two cheap moves:

- dismissing phenomenality because access or report can be studied,
- and postulating a separate substance merely because third-person explanation is incomplete.

## Consciousness-profile rule for future revisions

Before inferring a metaphysical verdict from language like **conscious**, **experience**, **aware**, **feels like**, **subjective**, **phenomenal**, **sentient**, **what it is like**, **qualitative**, **mental presence**, **attention**, **report**, **self-conscious**, **stream of consciousness**, or **qualia**, specify:

1. **target kind** — phenomenal state, stream of experience, conscious subject, self-conscious subject, person-level consciousness, animal consciousness, machine-analog case, or mixed case;
2. **phenomenal profile** — sensory, affective, bodily, imagistic, thought-like, multimodal, minimal, absent, or mixed;
3. **subject profile** — no genuine subject proposed, minimal subject, unified subject, self-conscious subject, socially articulated subject, or still unsettled;
4. **access/report profile** — unavailable, behaviorally manifested, access-available, reportable, self-reportable, linguistically articulated, or mixed;
5. **intentional profile** — object-directed, world-presenting, bodily, self-directed, nonintentional, or mixed;
6. **integration basis** — neural, organismic, computational, informational, field-like, enactive, socially scaffolded, or mixed;
7. **dependence claim** — identity, realization, grounding, emergence, aspect-duality, neutral-basis manifestation, panpsychist constitution, or still unsettled;
8. **causal profile** — epiphenomenal, causally efficacious through realization, organizationally control-relevant, overdetermination-risking, or still unsettled;
9. **persistence basis** — momentary episode, stream continuity, organismic continuity, memory-linked continuity, processual integration, or still unsettled;
10. **failure profile** — dreamless sleep, anesthesia, blindsight-style dissociation, split-stream, depersonalization, locked-in expression failure, attentional absence, or mixed;
11. **reality verdict** — fundamental mentality, derivative but robust conscious reality, thin access-only profile, merely representational gloss, illusion-friendly reinterpretation, or still unsettled.

Without this step, the archive will keep sliding from information processing to consciousness, from consciousness to selfhood, and from selfhood to personhood as if one term settled all three.

## Stress cases

### Pain without articulate report

The archive should ask whether report failure shows absence of consciousness, absence of access, absence of communicative channel, or only degraded self-interpretation.

### Blindsight, masking, and partial-awareness cases

The archive should ask whether the case involves absent phenomenality, degraded phenomenality, informational uptake without consciousness, or mixed profiles that separate access from feeling.

### Dreaming, dreamless sleep, anesthesia, and sedation

The archive should ask whether consciousness is absent, merely inaccessible, intermittently present, or still present in altered form.

### Split-brain, fragmentation, and unity pressure

The archive should ask whether one stream, many streams, one subject, many subjects, or merely fractured access is the best verdict.

### Animal and non-linguistic consciousness

The archive should ask what evidential and metaphysical role is played by behavior, affect, integration, learning, bodily regulation, and environmental responsiveness when language-report is unavailable.

### Artificial or artifact-supported cases

The archive should ask whether the target is mere task competence, access architecture, self-modeling, genuine subjecthood, or still unsettled.

## Default archive stance

The current archive default should be:

1. **Be realist about consciousness.** Do not explain it away merely because it is difficult.
2. **Be distinction-heavy.** Keep phenomenality, access, attention, report, intentionality, and personhood apart.
3. **Be dependence-disciplined.** Correlation does not yet settle realization, grounding, or identity.
4. **Prefer derivative-but-robust consciousness where possible.** Treat conscious episodes as real without prematurely making them fundamental or detachable.
5. **Keep deep rival packages live.** Panpsychism, neutral monism, dual-aspect views, and non-reductive emergence all remain serious under pressure.
6. **Treat mental causation as an explanatory burden, not a veto.**
7. **Keep consciousness tied to subject-profile questions.** Do not infer a full person wherever a conscious state appears, and do not infer no subject wherever report is missing.

## Working conclusion

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Consciousness is a real, centered, subject-involving mode of manifestation: dependent, structured, causally significant enough to matter, and not adequately handled either by flat report-functional reduction or by default appeal to a separate mental substance.**

That conclusion is strong enough to guide future revisions, but restrained enough to keep consciousness, subjecthood, selfhood, personhood, and mentality-at-large from collapsing into one another.
