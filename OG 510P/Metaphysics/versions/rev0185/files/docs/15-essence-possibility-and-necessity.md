# Essence, Possibility, and Necessity

This file gives the archive a compact modal policy.

The archive had already become committed to powers, forms, laws, kinds, and persistence conditions. But it still lacked a direct answer to three linked questions:

1. **what makes a possibility genuinely metaphysical rather than merely imaginable,**
2. **what makes a necessity hold of a thing rather than merely of a sentence,**
3. **and what role possible worlds should play in the archive's ontology.**

Without an answer, the archive risks three opposite mistakes:

- letting every modal claim float free as a bare intuition,
- turning possible-worlds talk into an automatic ontology of extra worlds,
- or treating essence as nothing more than a bookkeeping shadow of modal profiles.

## Core archive thesis

The archive should now adopt the following default:

> **Metaphysical modality is best treated as essence- and structure-disciplined possibility. Possible worlds are useful representations of ways reality could be, but they are not the archive's default ontological furniture.**

In compressed form:

> **Essence first, worlds second.**

That means:

- what a thing is helps determine what it could or must be,
- powers and structures help determine what developments and organizations are possible,
- laws articulate derivative modal constraints,
- and possible-worlds talk is primarily a modeling framework unless a later revision earns a heavier ontology.

## Four distinctions the archive should keep explicit

### 1. Logical possibility is not yet metaphysical possibility

A description may be free of contradiction and still fail to fit the natures, powers, or identity conditions of the things involved.

The archive should therefore resist moving too quickly from:

- conceivability,
- formal consistency,
- linguistic coherence,
- or model-theoretic satisfiability

straight to **metaphysical possibility**.

### 2. Metaphysical modality is not yet nomic modality

Some things are impossible given the actual laws, but not impossible full stop.

The archive should distinguish:

- what is impossible because it violates the current laws or stable modal structure of this world,
- from what is impossible because it conflicts with the essence, identity, or constitutive profile of the things in question.

This matters because laws may be derivative while essence reaches deeper into what a thing is.

### 3. De dicto necessity is not yet de re necessity

A sentence may be necessary without telling us much about the nature of a thing. Conversely, a thing may have necessary or essential features that are better stated de re than by merely marking a proposition as necessary.

The archive should ask whether a modal claim is about:

- a proposition,
- a thing,
- a kind,
- a relation,
- or a structured system.

### 4. Possible-worlds semantics is not yet possible-worlds ontology

Possible-worlds talk is often extremely useful for clarifying modal space, counterfactual comparison, and the difference between necessity and contingency.

But the archive does **not** need to infer from that usefulness that there exist, in the same ontological sense as this world, countless concrete worlds.

The current default is lighter:

- use worlds as a representational framework,
- keep actualist and essence-first options live,
- and demand extra argument before inflating ontology.

## Current archive default: essence-first modal realism without world-plenitude

The archive should now work with the following picture unless a later revision clearly improves on it.

### 1. Essence helps ground metaphysical modality

The archive should treat some modal truths as flowing from what things are.

That does **not** mean every necessity is simply an essence-claim. It means that when the archive asks why something is metaphysically necessary or impossible, it should look first to:

- the nature of the thing,
- the form of the kind,
- the powers belonging to it,
- the structures required for its identity,
- or the constitutive relations without which it would not be that thing at all.

### 2. Powers and structure mediate concrete possibility

Essence by itself can become too static if it is treated only as a definitional list.

For this archive, essence must remain connected to:

- powers,
- dynamic capacities,
- sustaining organization,
- and structural constraints.

That fits the archive's process-friendly stance better than a purely classificatory essentialism.

### 3. Laws are derivative articulations of modal reality

The archive should continue to say that laws articulate objective modal-structural constraints, but it should now place them more clearly:

- **essence and identity** constrain what something can be,
- **powers and structure** constrain what it can do or sustain,
- **laws** articulate stable patterns within that deeper modal order.

### 4. Worlds are maps of modal space, not default residents of the archive

Possible worlds remain useful for:

- distinguishing necessity, possibility, and contingency,
- handling counterfactual comparison,
- testing origin and persistence claims,
- and clarifying de re/de dicto ambiguity.

But unless a future revision shows otherwise, the archive should treat worlds as **representational surrogates for modal space**, not as default additional concrete domains.

## How this connects to earlier files

### With kinds and properties

Kinds help fix what belongs to a thing's nature and what counts as accidental variation.

### With persistence

If persistence is kind-sensitive, then possibility of survival is also kind-sensitive. A thing's persistence conditions constrain its modal profile.

### With laws, powers, and structure

The laws file explains how the world is modally ordered. This file explains how to rank the modal sources: essence and structured powers first, laws as articulation rather than external imposition.

### With grounding

Some necessities are best understood not merely as brute modal facts but as grounded in the essences, structures, or constitutive profiles of entities and kinds.

### With truthmakers

Modal truths should not automatically generate an ontology of abstract possible entities. The truthmaker file's general discipline still applies.

## Archive decision rule

When a future revision uses words like **possible**, **necessary**, **could**, **must**, **impossible**, or **essence**, it should answer five questions:

1. **Which modality is at issue — logical, epistemic, nomic, metaphysical, practical, or normative?**
2. **Is the claim de dicto or de re?**
3. **What grounds the modal status — essence, power, structure, law, institution, or something else?**
4. **Is possible-worlds language being used representationally or ontologically?**
5. **Would the claim survive if conceivability came apart from metaphysical possibility?**

If a revision cannot answer those questions, its modal vocabulary is under-disciplined.

## Working conclusion

The archive should now adopt the following modal policy:

> **Treat metaphysical possibility as constrained by the natures, powers, and structures of things; treat necessity as more than mere global truth across worlds; and treat possible worlds as a powerful framework whose semantic usefulness does not by itself settle ontology.**

That is the current archive default on essence, possibility, and necessity.
