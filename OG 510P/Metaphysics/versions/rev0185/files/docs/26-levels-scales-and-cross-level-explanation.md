# Levels, Scales, and Cross-Level Explanation

The archive already speaks of **higher-level reality**, **emergence**, **derivative but robust domains**, **realization**, and **fundamentality**. What it lacked was a single rule for when those are the same issue and when they are not.

This file adds that rule.

## Core claim

The archive should reject the assumption that every use of **higher** and **lower** picks out one global ontological ladder.

There are at least six different ideas that often get collapsed:

1. **metaphysical fundamentality** — what is least derivative or most basic,
2. **explanatory level** — the level at which a phenomenon is best explained,
3. **mechanistic level** — the part-whole stratification local to a mechanism,
4. **scale** — micro/macro, fast/slow, fine/coarse, near/far, dense/sparse,
5. **resolution or descriptive grain** — how finely or abstractly we represent a system,
6. **domain or theory-level** — the organized subject matter of a science or ontology.

These do not automatically align.

## Why this matters

A theory can be:

- **more fundamental** without being the best explanatory level for a target phenomenon,
- **mechanistically decomposing** a phenomenon without eliminating the target phenomenon,
- **coarser-grained** without being less real,
- **autonomous in explanatory practice** without being metaphysically basic,
- and **cross-scale** without positing a new ontological tier.

Many archive disputes become cleaner once this is said out loud.

## Default archive stance

The archive should prefer a **typed, local, phenomenon-indexed stratification**.

That means:

- reality may well have a foundational order,
- but not every organized domain has to fit one universal layer-cake,
- mechanistic levels are often **local to a phenomenon**,
- explanatory autonomy can coexist with ontological dependence,
- and scale changes or modeling changes do not by themselves settle what is real.

This default sits well with the archive's existing commitments to grounding pluralism, real emergence under discipline, and organized wholes.

## Four distinctions to keep explicit

### 1. Fundamentality is not explanatory adequacy

A more basic base need not yield the best explanation for every target.

Microphysical description may underwrite a system without being the right explanatory level for:

- metabolism,
- ecological resilience,
- institutional stability,
- skillful action,
- or legal authority.

Do not infer explanatory sufficiency from metaphysical depth.

### 2. Mechanistic levels are often local, not global

A mechanism can have higher and lower levels relative to the phenomenon it realizes or sustains.

Those levels are often:

- part-whole organized,
- constitutively relevant,
- phenomenon-indexed,
- and variable with the explanatory target.

Do not infer a universal stratification of all reality from a local mechanistic decomposition.

### 3. Scale is not ontology

Fine-grained and coarse-grained descriptions may track the same reality at different scales or resolutions.

A macro-description can be:

- more stable,
- more explanatory,
- more projectible,
- or more intervention-relevant

without thereby being more fundamental.

Conversely, a micro-description can be more basic without being the only real one.

### 4. Reduction is not the same as elimination

A higher-level theory or ontology may reduce in one sense while remaining indispensable in another.

Reduction may support:

- derivability,
- explanation,
- justification of earlier success,
- or limited-domain recovery.

That does not automatically mean that the reduced domain was unreal, dispensable for all explanation, or merely verbal.

## Cross-level linkage types

When the archive compares levels, it should ask which linkage is actually in play.

### A. Constitutive / mechanistic linkage

A whole-level phenomenon is explained by organized components and activities.

Typical form:

- heart pumping ↔ coordinated tissues and flows,
- memory process ↔ organized neural activity,
- thermostat regulation ↔ arranged parts and feedback.

### B. Realizational linkage

A type, role, or function is sustained by a lower-level configuration that may vary across realizers.

Typical form:

- a computational or institutional role realized by different substrates or organizations.

### C. Grounding or metaphysical-priority linkage

A derivative fact obtains in virtue of more basic facts.

Typical form:

- social, legal, or set-theoretic cases where the issue is constitutive priority rather than causal production.

### D. Emergence linkage

A higher-order pattern earns ontological seriousness because organized dependence plus explanatory autonomy survive compression.

Typical form:

- organisms, minds, ecosystems, institutions, and some collective patterns.

### E. Cross-level causal or constraint linkage

Higher-level organization constrains component behavior, not by spooky reverse causation, but through boundary conditions, control architecture, selection regimes, or structured environments.

Typical form:

- developmental canalization,
- institutional rule-constraint,
- engineered control systems,
- organism-level regulation.

### F. Representational / intertheoretic linkage

Two theories or models connect through approximation, limiting procedures, bridge principles, renormalization, or effective description.

Typical form:

- thermodynamics and statistical mechanics,
- continuum and particle models,
- effective field theories and higher-energy theories.

## What the archive should now prefer

### 1. No mandatory global layer-cake

The archive should not assume that all levels line up in one clean, universal hierarchy.

Reality may be ordered, but organized domains can cross-cut each other.

### 2. Real higher-level domains under discipline

The archive should continue to allow:

- biological levels,
- mental or agential levels,
- social and institutional levels,
- infrastructural and ecological levels,
- and some mathematical or structural levels of description,

provided the case states its dependence, unity, causal, modal, and category profile.

### 3. Autonomy and dependence can coexist

A domain can be:

- derivative in metaphysical priority,
- locally autonomous in explanation,
- objective in lawlike or modal profile,
- and irreducible for intervention or control

without becoming absolutely basic.

### 4. Top-down language should be translated, not banned

The archive should neither ban nor romanticize “top-down causation”.

Instead it should ask whether the case is really about:

- boundary conditions,
- selection and retention,
- control architecture,
- rate-limiting organization,
- institutional norm enforcement,
- or a mixed constitutive-causal profile.

## Levels-profile rule

When a future revision uses language like **higher-level**, **lower-level**, **more basic**, **autonomous**, **reducible**, **emergent**, **micro**, **macro**, **coarse-grained**, or **multiscale**, it should state:

1. whether the issue is **fundamentality**, **explanatory level**, **mechanistic level**, **scale**, **resolution**, **theory-relation**, or **cross-level constraint**;
2. what the **target phenomenon** is;
3. whether the levels are **local** to that phenomenon or intended as a **global hierarchy**;
4. whether the linking relation is chiefly **constitutive**, **realizational**, **grounding-based**, **causal/constraint-based**, **intertheoretic**, or **mixed**;
5. whether the higher-level domain is best treated as **basic**, **derivative but robust**, **thinly dependent**, or **merely representational**;
6. what explanatory loss follows if the higher-level vocabulary is discarded.

If a proposal does not do this, its level-talk is under-disciplined.

## Default stress cases

This file should be used first when the archive discusses:

- organisms and development,
- minds and cognition,
- ecosystems and climates,
- institutions, law, and markets,
- infrastructure and cities,
- thermodynamics/statistical mechanics,
- continuum/particle or field/effective-theory cases,
- and any dispute that says “really only the lower level exists”.

## Compression payoff

This file is meant to reduce slippage across at least seven existing regions of the archive:

- **emergence**, because autonomy must now be typed,
- **dependence**, because not every interlevel relation is grounding,
- **mereology**, because some levels are local part-whole levels,
- **causation**, because cross-level influence must be translated,
- **laws/powers/structure**, because some laws are level-specific without being illusory,
- **ontological commitment**, because derivative robustness differs from mere representational convenience,
- **relations**, because stratification can be local and typed rather than one flat hierarchy.

## Current archive default in one sentence

**Reality may be stratified, but stratification is best treated as local, typed, and cross-linked rather than as one universal layer-cake in which explanatory level, scale, and metaphysical fundamentality always coincide.**
