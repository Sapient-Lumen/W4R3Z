# Equivalence, Reformulation, and Duality

## Why this file exists

The archive now has explicit files on **symmetry**, **representation**, **contrast classes**, **reference**, **ontological revision**, and **idealization**. But it still lacked one compact file saying how the archive should treat **equivalence**, **reformulation**, and **duality**.

Without a dedicated file here, the archive risks eight recurrent mistakes:

- treating every formally different presentation as if it described a **different world**,
- treating mere **empirical equivalence** as if it automatically settled deeper theoretical or ontological equivalence,
- treating **gauge redundancy** or surplus structure as if it were automatically part of the world rather than part of a representational package,
- treating every strong **duality** as if it proved one final ontology rather than preserving several live interpretive options,
- treating local, regime-bound, or approximation-limited correspondences as if they were **global sameness**,
- treating one reused vocabulary as if it guaranteed one stable target and one unchanged ontology,
- treating reformulation success as if it erased the difference between **vehicle**, **interpretation**, and **worldly target**,
- or treating every preserved invariant as if it exhausted what is real.

This file gives the archive a compact **equivalence profile** so future revisions can say what sort of sameness is being claimed, what is preserved across reformulation, what varies, what the target is, and what sort of ontological verdict—if any—has actually been earned.

## Working default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Equivalence is typed: not every sameness claim is the same, and formal, empirical, interpretive, structural, and ontological equivalence should not be collapsed.**

And more specifically:

> **Reformulation and duality often reveal preserved target-structure across different presentations, but they do not by themselves decide whether the ontology is identical, thinner, layered, or still unsettled.**

This fits the archive's wider commitments:

- **symmetry** matters because some presentation differences are gauge-like or coordinate-style redundancies,
- **representation** matters because theory vehicles and targets must not be collapsed,
- **reference** matters because continuity of terms can come apart from continuity of target,
- **revision** matters because later inquiry can preserve structure or intervention profiles without preserving every old posit,
- **contrast classes** matter because some “rivals” are really alternative carvings or presentations,
- **idealization** matters because some equivalences are regime-bound, approximate, or asymptotic rather than global,
- and **access architecture** matters because what is preserved under reformulation may be access-stable structure, not a complete inventory of being.

## Core distinctions

### 1. Empirical equivalence is not automatically theoretical equivalence

The archive should distinguish:

- **empirical equivalence** — same observable or measurement-level deliverances over a relevant range,
- **predictive equivalence** — same answers to a class of questions,
- **model-equivalence** — paired model structures or solution-spaces,
- **interpretive equivalence** — same worldly target under a shared interpretation,
- and **ontological equivalence** — same claims about what there is and what is basic.

Otherwise it will keep letting shared output do all the metaphysical work.

### 2. Formal equivalence is not automatically sameness of content

The archive should distinguish:

- **notational or coordinative reformulation**,
- **definitional or translation-style equivalence**,
- **isomorphic or structure-preserving recoding**,
- **category-theoretic or model-theoretic correspondences**,
- and **pragmatically similar but content-divergent formalisms**.

Otherwise it will keep moving too quickly from mathematical mapping to sameness of theory-content.

### 3. Gauge redundancy is not the same as worldly multiplicity

The archive should distinguish:

- **surplus mathematical structure**,
- **gauge-related descriptions of one physical situation**,
- **choice of coordinates or presentation**,
- **genuinely different boundary conditions or sectors**,
- and **interpretive packages that disagree about what the formal surplus means**.

Otherwise it will keep mistaking descriptive freedom for extra ontology.

### 4. Duality is not automatically identity

The archive should distinguish:

- **strong duality claims** that preserve many structures across formulations,
- **local or regime-bound correspondences**,
- **approximate or asymptotic dualities**,
- **interpretive dualities** where the same formalism invites different ontological readings,
- and **heuristic analogies** that are useful without being equivalences.

Otherwise it will keep treating every powerful correspondence as if it forced one final reading of reality.

### 5. Preserved invariants do not automatically exhaust reality

The archive should distinguish:

- what is **preserved across formulations**,
- what is **artifact of one presentation**,
- what is **lost or hidden** under translation,
- what is preserved only **locally or approximately**,
- and what remains **ontologically underdetermined** despite strong formal matching.

Otherwise it will keep turning invariance into a complete inventory of being.

### 6. Same target is not always same ontology

Two reformulations can agree on:

- the **target domain**,
- the **successful interventions**,
- the **measurement structure**,
- the **robust invariants**,
- or the **effective regime**,

while still differing on whether the world fundamentally contains fields, particles, structures, spacetime points, potentials, states, processes, or only equivalence classes thereof.

Otherwise the archive will keep assuming that one shared target settles every ontological question.

### 7. Rivalry and coexistence must be typed

The archive should distinguish:

- cases where two formulations are **really the same package in different dress**,
- cases where they are **equivalent for one purpose but not another**,
- cases where they share a target but differ in **explanatory articulation**,
- cases where one carries **surplus ontology** relative to another,
- and cases where genuine empirical or interpretive divergence remains.

Otherwise it will keep flattening selection, coexistence, and suspension into one verdict.

## Four live options

### 1. Presentation inflationism

**Picture:** every formally distinct formulation carries a distinct ontology.

**What it gets right:**
- formal differences can matter,
- and some reformulations are not innocent.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it overmultiplies ontology from coordinates, gauges, encodings, and regime-limited variants,
- and it underdescribes preserved targets and invariants.

### 2. Flat equivalence optimism

**Picture:** once two packages are formally or empirically equivalent enough, all deeper ontological questions are settled.

**What it gets right:**
- many disputes are merely about presentation,
- and some differences should not count ontologically.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it collapses empirical, formal, interpretive, and ontological equivalence,
- and it underdescribes cases where preserved structure still leaves ontology open.

### 3. Instrumentalist underreading of reformulation

**Picture:** reformulations matter only for convenience; if outputs match, there is no deeper metaphysical lesson.

**What it gets right:**
- reformulation can be pragmatic,
- and not every duality or equivalence proves ontology.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it underdescribes what preserved invariants, translation maps, intervention profiles, and target continuity can teach us about reality.

### 4. Typed equivalence realism

**Picture:** equivalence, reformulation, and duality are philosophically important, but only in typed ways. Some reveal genuine preserved structure or target continuity; some reveal gauge redundancy or surplus formalism; some leave ontology underdetermined; some support selective structural realism; and some are local or regime-bound rather than global.

**What it gets right:**
- it keeps reformulation epistemically and metaphysically serious without inflation,
- it fits the archive's selective and corrigible realism,
- and it lets the archive distinguish same-target, same-structure, same-predictions, same-ontology, and still-open cases.

**What it risks if left alone:**
- it can become vague unless each case states what is preserved, what varies, and what kind of equivalence is actually at stake.

## Archive commitments

### 1. Ask what kind of sameness is being claimed

Do not let “equivalent”, “same theory”, “dual”, “only a reformulation”, or “just notation” do work without a typed profile.

### 2. Keep vehicles, translations, and targets distinct

A successful map between formalisms is not yet a verdict about ontology.

### 3. Treat gauge and surplus structure as live but case-sensitive

Some formal surplus is representational freedom; some surplus may encode choices, sectors, or boundary-sensitive content; and the archive should not decide this a priori.

### 4. Allow structural retention without whole-ontology retention

Sometimes what survives reformulation is best read as preserved structure, law-pattern, intervention profile, or effective regime rather than a full inventory of entities.

### 5. Keep dualities ontologically disciplined

A duality may support layered coexistence, selective structural realism, or suspension rather than a single forced ontology.

### 6. Refuse the shortcut from strong correspondence to final metaphysics

Formal power, explanatory reach, and unification matter, but they do not automatically settle fundamentality or exhaustive ontology.

## Equivalence profile rule for future revisions

Before inferring a metaphysical verdict from language like **equivalent theory**, **same content**, **mere reformulation**, **duality**, **translation map**, **definitional equivalence**, **same observables**, **same models**, **gauge-related**, **surplus structure**, **different variables for the same world**, or **alternative formulation**, future revisions should answer at least these questions:

1. **target domain** — what worldly target, regime, process, or structure is at issue;
2. **packages compared** — what formulations, models, representations, or interpretive packages are being compared;
3. **equivalence type** — empirical, predictive, formal, definitional, model-theoretic, structural, interpretive, ontological, or mixed;
4. **preserved content** — observables, intervention profile, law-structure, symmetries, invariants, target domain, boundary conditions, or mixed content;
5. **variation profile** — what differs: variables, coordinates, gauge, explanatory articulation, ontology, regime, or semantics;
6. **scope** — global, local, bounded-range, asymptotic, approximate, or merely heuristic;
7. **translation basis** — explicit map, isomorphism, categorical equivalence, interpretive bridge, operational bridge, or mixed relation;
8. **surplus-structure risk** — whether any package carries descriptive freedom not obviously fixed by worldly differences;
9. **ontological lesson** — same ontology, thinner ontology via quotienting or equivalence classes, selective structural retention, layered coexistence, underdetermined ontology, pragmatic reformulation only, or still unsettled.

Otherwise the archive will keep sliding from formal correspondence to ontological identity, from shared outputs to one metaphysical package, and from powerful dualities to uncontrolled realism or uncontrolled deflation.

## Compression payoff

This file is meant to stabilize six already-live archive fault lines at once:

- **symmetry** without turning every formal freedom into ontology,
- **representation** without collapsing vehicle and target,
- **contrast** without mistaking alternative formulations for automatic rivals,
- **revision** without confusing preservation of structure with preservation of all posits,
- **idealization** without mistaking local or approximate correspondence for global sameness,
- and **reference** without treating reused labels or shared measurement success as sufficient for one settled target and one settled ontology.

## Default archive verdict

The archive should now prefer a **typed realism about equivalence and reformulation**:

> **Different formulations can preserve one target, one structure, one intervention profile, or one effective regime without thereby forcing one complete ontology; equivalence and duality are real philosophical achievements, but the metaphysical lesson must be stated in typed form rather than read straight off from formal success alone.**
