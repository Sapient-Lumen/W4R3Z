# Fundamentality, Grounding, and Metaphysical Explanation

## Why this file exists

The archive already has files on dependence, levels, supervenience, architectural direction, being, laws, life, personhood, and consciousness. But it still lacked a direct rule for **fundamentality**, **grounding**, and **metaphysical explanation** together.

That omission matters because the archive already says all of the following:

- some things are more basic than others,
- grounding is real and useful,
- explanation is not exhausted by efficient causation,
- higher-level domains can be derivative but robust,
- and architectural direction matters even when local reciprocal organization is allowed.

Without a dedicated file here, the archive risks six opposite mistakes:

- treating **grounding** as a one-word solvent for every dependence question,
- treating **fundamentality** as identical with mere existence, naturalness, or explanatory usefulness,
- treating **metaphysical explanation** as whatever remains once ordinary causation is set aside,
- assuming that the **most causally powerful** or **most scientifically central** domain must also be absolutely fundamental,
- assuming that **ungroundedness**, **independence**, and **complete basis** are always the very same achievement,
- or treating every hierarchy dispute as already settled once one says “in virtue of”.

This file gives the archive a compact **grounding/fundamentality profile** so future revisions can say whether a case is chiefly about ontological priority, complete basis, constitutive explanation, dependence-family choice, local or absolute fundamentality, or only explanatory salience.

## Working default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Reality has genuine priority structure. Grounding is a real form of constitutive determination, fundamentality is real but not one flat notion, and metaphysical explanation is plural but answerable to typed dependence rather than reducible either to causation or to a single universal building relation.**

This fits the archive's wider commitments:

- **process** still matters, because priority does not erase becoming, maintenance, or temporal production,
- **form** still matters, because what counts as a basis is often kind-sensitive and not merely a heap of lower items,
- **power** still matters, because explanatory depth is not exhausted by static constitution,
- **structure** still matters, because some grounded facts depend on organization and relation rather than bare inventory,
- **grounding** still matters, because some facts obtain in virtue of others and not merely after them,
- and **metaontology** still matters, because existence, robustness, commitment, and fundamentality do not collapse into one verdict.

## Core distinctions

### 1. Existence is not fundamentality

The archive should keep apart at least four verdicts:

- **exists and is fundamental**,
- **exists and is derivative but robust**,
- **exists only thinly or in a heavily dependent way**,
- **is merely representational or pragmatic**.

Without this distinction the archive will keep sliding from “real” to “basic”, or from “not basic” to “not real”.

### 2. Absolute fundamentality and relative fundamentality are not the same

The archive should distinguish:

- **absolute fundamentality** — nothing more basic in the relevant sense,
- **relative fundamentality** — one fact, entity, or domain is more basic than another,
- **local fundamentality** — a target is basic only relative to a restricted dependence family or domain,
- **basal-candidate talk** — a proposed base picture not yet earned as the final metaphysical bottom.

Some disputes are really about **priority ordering** within a domain. Others are about whether there is any absolute base at all. Those should not be run together.

### 3. Ungroundedness, independence, and complete basis can come apart

A target may be proposed as fundamental because it is:

- **ungrounded**,
- **ontologically independent**,
- **a complete minimal basis** for everything else,
- or simply treated as **primitive** in a theory.

These ideas often travel together, but they are not guaranteed to coincide. A proposal may be independence-first, basis-first, or primitive-first. The archive should demand that future revisions say which of these jobs is actually being claimed.

### 4. Grounding is not the whole dependence family

The archive already distinguishes composition, constitution, realization, grounding, and causation. This file adds a stricter rule:

> **Do not infer grounding merely because one thing depends on another.**

Some cases are better modeled as:

- **composition** — whole/part make-up,
- **constitution** — one thing materially constituting another here and now,
- **realization** — a type, role, or state being realized by a lower-level configuration,
- **ontological dependence** — existence-conditional or priority-linked dependence,
- **supervenience** — no-difference constraint,
- **causation** — temporal production, sustaining, inhibition, or triggering,
- **grounding** — constitutive in-virtue-of order.

Grounding is central, but it is not the whole map.

### 5. Ontological dependence is not automatically priority

Some dependence relations are modal-existential or historical without clearly conferring metaphysical priority. The archive should therefore ask whether the case really carries:

- simple existential dependence,
- asymmetric priority,
- constitutive determination,
- basis-completeness,
- or only an explanatory or evidential connection.

Without this step the archive will keep treating every necessitation or dependence condition as a building relation.

### 6. Metaphysical explanation is not identical with grounding, though grounding often helps generate it

The archive should now say explicitly:

- many metaphysical explanations are **grounding-involving**,
- some are **essence-involving**, **realization-involving**, **constitution-involving**, or otherwise mixed,
- and metaphysical explanation should not be defined merely as “whatever is non-causal”.

A useful rule is:

> **Ask first what relation carries the explanatory burden, and only then ask whether the result counts as a metaphysical explanation.**

Without this step the archive will keep using “metaphysical explanation” as a prestige label without saying how the explanation works.

### 7. Explanatory importance is not yet fundamentality

A domain can be indispensable to explanation without being absolutely fundamental. Organisms, agents, institutions, fields, or conscious subjects may be explanatorily serious while still being derivative in important ways.

So future revisions should not argue:

- this level explains a lot,
- therefore it is fundamental.

That shortcut is too quick. What matters is whether the domain is explanatory because it is basic, because it is an indispensable organized level, because it carries autonomous laws or powers, or because it is a privileged modeling grain.

### 8. Directional priority is the archive default, but not every acceptable structure must be a simple terminating ladder

This file should inherit the architectural discipline already adopted elsewhere:

- prefer **directional priority** where grounding is genuinely at issue,
- keep open whether all acceptable dependence chains must be globally well-founded,
- distinguish **local reciprocal organization** from **symmetric grounding**,
- and allow that the archive may someday accept an anchored infinitist or other non-simple architecture if the gain is clear enough.

The default remains priority-friendly without forcing a crude ladder picture on every domain.

## Current archive answer

The archive should adopt a **priority-without-imperialism** stance:

> **Some parts of reality are more fundamental than others, and some facts obtain in virtue of more basic facts; but the right metaphysics is not one that says “grounding” and stops. It must say what the relata are, what dependence relation is in play, whether the issue is absolute or relative fundamentality, and whether the explanatory job is grounding, realization, constitution, essence, or a mixed profile.**

In compressed form:

> **Be realist about priority, non-imperialist about grounding, and pluralist about metaphysical explanation.**

This rules out three cheap moves:

- treating all dependence as one flat building relation,
- treating all reality-talk as a hunt for one absolutely basic inventory,
- and treating every non-causal explanation as already a finished grounding explanation.

## Grounding/fundamentality profile rule for future revisions

Before inferring a metaphysical verdict from language like **fundamental**, **basic**, **ground-level**, **ultimate**, **ungrounded**, **independent**, **in virtue of**, **because**, **explains why**, **metaphysically explains**, **depends on**, **ontological priority**, **building relation**, **more real**, or **nothing over and above**, specify:

1. **target kind** — entity, fact, property, relation, law, power, structure, process, level, kind, institution, or mixed case;
2. **fundamentality target** — absolute fundamentality, relative fundamentality, local/domain-restricted fundamentality, complete-basis claim, primitive posit, or still unsettled;
3. **relata choice** — facts, objects, properties, structures, event-types, law-statements, or mixed relata;
4. **dependence relation** — grounding, ontological dependence, composition, constitution, realization, supervenience, causation, essence-link, or mixed profile;
5. **directionality profile** — asymmetric, reciprocal, locally reciprocal within a wider order, well-founded, infinitary but anchored, or still unsettled;
6. **basis claim** — single privileged base, plural base, layered basis, no complete basis claimed, or still unsettled;
7. **explanation type** — grounding-based, essence-based, realizational, constitutive, law/structure based, causal, mixed, or merely heuristic;
8. **evidence source** — conceptual analysis, truthmaking pressure, scientific structure, indispensability, modal argument, explanatory unification, or mixed support;
9. **failure profile** — overgeneration, explanatory vacuity, collapse into mere covariance, illicit slide from dependence to priority, illicit slide from explanation to fundamentality, or still unsettled;
10. **reality verdict** — fundamental, derivative but robust, thinly dependent, merely representational, or still unsettled.

Without this step, the archive will keep sliding from dependence to grounding, from grounding to absolute fundamentality, and from explanatory usefulness to basic reality as if one term settled all three.

## Stress cases

### Set/member, whole/part, and constitution cases

The archive should ask whether the target relation is mereological, constitutive, or genuinely grounding-like. A whole's parts, a statue's clay, and a set's members need not all carry the same priority story.

### Laws, powers, and structures

The archive should ask whether laws are fundamental, grounded in powers or structures, or only represent stable regularity. It should not assume that modal indispensability by itself fixes the grounding profile.

### Organisms and living systems

The archive should ask whether organism-level facts are grounded in biochemical organization, realized by lower-level activity, or partly explained by higher-level organizational closure. Not every multi-level explanation here is a simple bottom-up grounding story.

### Consciousness and mindedness

The archive should ask whether conscious facts are grounded, realized, emergent, aspectual, or neutral-basis manifestations. “Mind depends on brain” does not by itself tell the archive which metaphysical relation is doing the work.

### Social and institutional reality

The archive should ask whether money, law, office, platform status, borders, or corporations are grounded in recognition, records, enforcement, material infrastructure, or some mixed basis. Institutional dependence is real, but not every such case is well described by one bare grounding slogan.

### Physics-adjacent cases

The archive should ask whether a scientific level is being treated as fundamental because it is ontologically basic, because it is the finest available description, because it offers widest explanatory scope, or because current theory happens to privilege it. Those are not the same claim.

## Default archive stance

The current archive default should be:

1. **Be realist about priority.** Do not flatten reality into one ontological level.
2. **Separate existence from fundamentality.** Derivative reality can still be real.
3. **Use grounding carefully.** Do not let it erase composition, realization, constitution, or causation.
4. **Be pluralist about metaphysical explanation.** Grounding matters, but essence, realization, constitution, and structure can matter too.
5. **Demand a basis claim.** If something is called fundamental, ask whether the claim is independence-based, basis-based, or primitive-first.
6. **Do not infer fundamentality from explanatory success alone.** Higher-level indispensability is not automatic absolute priority.
7. **Keep architecture explicit.** If a hierarchy, regress, or loop is proposed, state what kind.

## Working conclusion

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Fundamentality is real but not one flat notion; grounding is real but not an imperial solvent; and metaphysical explanation is real but must be tied to typed dependence and explicit basis claims rather than used as a decorative synonym for depth.**

That conclusion is strong enough to guide future revisions, but restrained enough to keep dependence, grounding, fundamentality, explanation, and reality-status from collapsing into one another.
