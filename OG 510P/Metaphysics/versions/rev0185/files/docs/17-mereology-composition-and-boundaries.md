# Mereology, Composition, and Boundaries

## Why this file exists

By `rev0009`, the archive had become much better at handling **dependence**, **emergence**, **persistence**, **truthmaking**, **modality**, and **category discipline**. But one major source of compression pressure was still under-specified:

> **When do parts amount to a whole that deserves ontological seriousness, and how do boundaries matter to that answer?**

Without a dedicated file on this, the archive risks several recurrent confusions:

- treating any **mereological sum** as if it were already an **integrated individual**,
- treating **composition** as if it settled **constitution, unity, or persistence** by itself,
- treating **co-location** as if it forced **identity**,
- treating **boundary vagueness** as if it erased all entityhood,
- and treating **part replacement** as if it had one flat significance across organisms, artifacts, clouds, institutions, and processes.

This file therefore adds a compact rule-set for **parts, wholes, composition, and boundaries**.

## Archive default

The archive now favors **integration-sensitive mereological realism**:

- **parthood is real** and should not be dissolved into mere language,
- but **not every sum is an ontologically serious whole**,
- **composition** is a real relation that answers one question only — what parts make up this whole,
- **unity, constitution, and persistence** remain further questions,
- and **boundaries** may be material, functional, operational, institutional, or vague without thereby becoming unreal.

The archive should therefore distinguish at least four things:

1. **mereological sum** — whatever parts are taken together,
2. **integrated whole** — a sum whose organization is doing real unifying work,
3. **constituted individual** — an entity whose kind, form, or role is not exhausted by enumerating parts,
4. **bounded system** — a target whose boundary conditions matter for powers, persistence, or explanation.

This is meant to block two opposite mistakes:

- **universalist inflation**: every arbitrary fusion counts as a serious object in the same sense,
- **nihilist thinning**: only minimal particles or simples are real, and all ordinary wholes are derivative bookkeeping.

The archive default is more selective: some sums are real only in a weak mereological sense, while some wholes are real in a stronger organizational, explanatory, and modal sense.

## Core distinction map

### 1. Parthood is not yet unity

To say that X has parts is not yet to say what makes X **one** thing. A heap, organism, clock, border patrol, storm system, and court all have parts, but their unity conditions differ.

Parthood answers a structural question. Unity answers a further metaphysical question.

### 2. Composition is not yet constitution

The archive already distinguishes composition from constitution. This file sharpens the point.

- **composition** asks what parts make up the candidate whole,
- **constitution** asks how some material or lower-level basis constitutes an entity of a certain kind,
- **unity** asks what makes it one,
- **persistence** asks how it survives change,
- **grounding** asks in virtue of what it obtains,
- **causation** asks what brings about or sustains it.

A statue may be composed of clay parts, constituted by that clay under a certain form, and persist or fail according to conditions not captured by part-listing alone.

### 3. Sum is not yet integrated whole

The archive should distinguish:

- **mere sum** — whatever is formed by taking these parts together,
- **integrated whole** — something whose organization, coordination, or closure is doing real work,
- **system** — something whose boundary conditions matter to what powers and processes it can sustain.

This matters because a living cell, legal corporation, hurricane, and chess set are not alike merely because each has many parts.

### 4. Boundaries are real even when not razor-sharp

The archive should reject the shortcut:

> vague boundary, therefore no real thing.

Some boundaries are sharp and engineered. Others are graded, negotiated, porous, or context-sensitive. A cloud, coastline, immune system, ecosystem, body, or institution may have a real but not perfectly crisp boundary.

Boundary variation should therefore be treated as a **type-sensitive question**:

- **material boundary** — skin, wall, shell, membrane,
- **functional boundary** — where a process or control loop is internally organized,
- **operational boundary** — what counts as inside the working system for explanation,
- **institutional boundary** — who or what is inside a legal or normative structure,
- **vague boundary** — edge cases where membership is indeterminate or graded.

### 5. Co-location is not identity

The archive should continue resisting the inference:

> same place, same matter, therefore same object.

Constitution puzzles matter because a lump, statue, organism, and institution can share matter, region, or participants without thereby being one and the same entity under one description.

### 6. Part turnover has different significance in different domains

Part replacement does not have a single metaphysical meaning.

- In **organisms**, organized metabolic continuity often matters more than strict material retention.
- In **artifacts**, function and design history may matter more than full material continuity.
- In **clouds** and **storms**, dynamic profile may matter more than stable composition.
- In **institutions**, participants may change while roles, rules, and enforcement structures persist.

This confirms the archive's prior commitment that persistence is kind-sensitive. Mereology must therefore be integrated with sortals, form, process, and category discipline.

## Non-collapse rules

### Rule 1: Do not infer serious objecthood from arbitrary summation

A fusion may exist in a weak mereological sense without earning the same ontological status as an organism, tool, person, or institution.

### Rule 2: Do not let composition do the work of unity, constitution, or grounding

Composition answers what parts are present. It does not by itself answer what the thing is, why it is one, or in virtue of what it obtains.

### Rule 3: Do not treat every boundary as a hard line in the same sense

Some boundaries are surfaces, some are control interfaces, some are legal-normative demarcations, and some are vague margins.

### Rule 4: Do not let boundary vagueness automatically erase entityhood

The fact that a cloud or organism has borderline cases does not show there is no real cloud or organism. It shows that boundary metaphysics must be more careful than a cartoon outline model.

### Rule 5: Do not identify location, parthood, and dependence

Something can be located in a region without being identical to that region. Something can be part of a whole without grounding it in the same sense. Something can be depended on without literally being a part.

### Rule 6: Do not treat open systems as failures of individuality by default

Many real individuals sustain themselves only through exchange with an environment. Permeability is not the same as non-entity.

## Boundary-and-composition profile for future revisions

Any new substantial claim about a candidate whole should, where relevant, answer these six questions:

1. **Part basis** — What are the relevant parts or contributors?
2. **Mode of integration** — Heap, mechanism, organism, process, institution, network, or something else?
3. **Boundary type** — Material, functional, operational, institutional, vague, or mixed?
4. **Persistence under turnover** — What part changes can it survive?
5. **Dependence profile** — Is the case mainly composition, constitution, realization, grounding, causation, or a layered combination?
6. **Explanatory level** — Does explanatory work come chiefly from part-listing, organization, dynamic regulation, role structure, or some combination?

This is meant to stop the archive from confusing a **sum**, a **system**, and an **individual**.

## Default archive applications

### Organisms

Treat as **integrated open systems** whose boundaries are materially and functionally real but often metabolically permeable. Material turnover does not by itself defeat identity.

### Artifacts

Treat as wholes whose composition matters, but whose identity may also depend on form, intended function, repair history, and use-context.

### Clouds, flames, and storms

Treat as cases where process profile and dynamic continuity often matter more than stable matter-token retention or razor-sharp boundaries.

### Institutions

Treat as wholes with participant parts only in a loose sense; their more important boundaries are often role-structured, legal, and operational rather than merely spatial.

### Mereological sums

Allow them where the theory needs them, but do not let them silently displace integrated wholes as the archive's default model of entityhood.

## What this file changes in the archive's style

From this revision onward, the archive should avoid sentences of the form:

- “These parts are there, so the whole is already explained.”
- “The boundary is vague, so there is no real entity.”
- “The same matter occupies the place, so there is only one thing here.”
- “Open exchange with an environment means the system is not a genuine individual.”
- “Composition alone settles persistence.”

These are exactly the shortcuts this file is meant to block.

## Current archive posture in one line

> **Parts are real, but wholes are not exhausted by parts: composition must be integrated with form, process, kind, and boundary discipline before a candidate whole earns full ontological seriousness.**
