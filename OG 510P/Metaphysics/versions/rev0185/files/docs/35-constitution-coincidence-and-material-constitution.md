# Constitution, Coincidence, and Material Constitution

## Why this file exists

The archive already had files on mereology, location, identity, substance, supervenience, and ontological commitment. But it still lacked a direct policy for cases where **one thing seems to be made from, colocated with, or materially continuous with another without simply being identical to it**.

Without a dedicated rule here, the archive risks three opposite mistakes:

- treating every coincidence case as impossible because two things cannot be “in the same place,”
- treating every colocated or materially continuous pair as two objects by default,
- or flattening **constitution**, **composition**, **identity**, **realization**, **grounding**, and **co-location** into one mixed relation.

## Core distinctions

### 1. Constitution is not composition, identity, or grounding

The archive should distinguish at least four questions:

- **composition** — what parts compose a whole,
- **constitution** — what materially or organizationally makes up a thing here and now,
- **identity** — whether two candidates are numerically one,
- **grounding** — in virtue of what a fact or entity obtains.

A lump may compose itself from particles, constitute a statue, coincide with it for a time, and ground various truths about it, without those all being one relation.

### 2. Coincidence is a clue, not yet a verdict

Shared place, overlap, or material continuity does not by itself settle whether there are:

- two things,
- one thing under two descriptions,
- one thing and one derivative phase,
- or no genuine higher-level object at all.

Co-location therefore creates metaphysical pressure, but it is not itself the answer.

### 3. Constitution is typically asymmetric and domain-typed

The archive default is that constitution, where real, is usually better treated as:

- **asymmetric**,
- **dependence-laden**,
- **domain-sensitive**,
- and often **time-indexed**.

The clay may constitute the statue while the statue exists, but not vice versa. The living body may materially constitute the organism without being a second thing in exactly the same way a clay lump is. An institution may be constituted by organized practices without being spatially colocated in a simple material sense at all.

### 4. Persistence and modal profiles matter

Many constitution debates are really disputes about **persistence**, **kind**, and **counterfactual profile**.

The archive should therefore ask whether the apparent coincidents differ in:

- coming-into-being conditions,
- going-out-of-being conditions,
- persistence through alteration,
- modal dependence on structure or history,
- or kind-sensitive explanatory role.

If there is no disciplined divergence anywhere, a two-object verdict becomes expensive.

### 5. The archive default: constitution without easy duplication

The archive should keep **constitution without identity** as a live and often useful option, but not as an automatic multiplication rule.

Its working default is:

- allow constitution where a formed individual or organized object is materially made up by something with a different persistence or modal profile,
- prefer **one-object readings** where the putative difference is only verbal, phase-relative, or explanatorily idle,
- and treat constitution as a **typed dependence profile** rather than as a universal solution to every case of shared matter or shared place.

## A decision rule for future revisions

Before adding or rejecting a constitution claim, state six things:

1. **Constituted candidate** — statue, organism, artifact, institution, event-like continuant, or some mixed case.
2. **Constituting basis** — lump, body, matter, process, practice, structural arrangement, or layered base.
3. **Coincidence mode** — exact co-location, overlap, diachronic continuity, material continuity, organizational embodiment, or non-spatial constitution.
4. **Divergence test** — what differs: persistence, modal profile, kind, historical origin, destruction condition, explanatory role, or nothing serious.
5. **Dependence type** — constitution, composition, realization, grounding, identity, or mixed profile.
6. **Verdict level** — one thing, two coincident things, one thing plus derivative phase/profile, or underdescribed.

If those six items are not stated, the archive should treat the case as underdescribed.

## Default verdicts by case-type

### Lump/statue style cases

Default: keep **constitution without identity** live, because kind, persistence, and modal profiles often diverge in a disciplined way.

### Organism/body style cases

Default: do **not** assume the clay/statue model transfers cleanly. Often the better question is whether the organism is a process-stabilized substantial individual whose body is its constituting material organization rather than a second coincident object of the same general sort.

### Artifact/material basis cases

Default: constitution often earns explanatory use where design history, function, and persistence conditions diverge from those of the raw material basis.

### Institutional embodiment cases

Default: many institutional entities are constituted by organized practices, roles, records, and enforcement structures rather than by simple material coincidence, so “constitution” here should be typed rather than spatially flattened.

This file tightens six older files at once:

## Current archive default

The archive should therefore say:

- **coincidence is not yet duplication**,
- **constitution is not identity, but neither is it automatic object multiplication**,
- **persistence, kind, and modal profile do much of the real work in constitution cases**,
- **spatial overlap and material sameness are clues rather than verdicts**,
- and **constitution should be treated as a typed dependence relation whose use must be earned domain by domain**.
