# Contrast Classes, Rival Carvings, and Comparative Ontological Choice

## Why this file exists

The archive now has explicit files on **naturalness**, **grain**, **evidence**, **abduction**, **indispensability/promotion**, **objectivity**, **access architecture**, **reference**, and **ontological revision**. But it still lacked one direct file saying what the archive means when it compares:

- two allegedly rival ontological packages,
- two carvings of the same target,
- two grains or scales of one domain,
- one realist and one cautious reconstruction,
- or one apparently plural situation in which not all successful articulations are genuinely incompatible.

Without a dedicated file here, the archive risks seven recurrent mistakes:

- treating every difference in **description** as a difference in **ontology**,
- treating every difference in **grain** as a contradiction,
- treating every explanatory or representational **contrast class** as fixed in advance,
- treating one successful carving as if it had already refuted all differently aimed or differently scaled articulations,
- treating limited pluralism as if it were either incoherent or automatically anti-realist,
- treating comparative superiority as if it automatically promoted one whole package rather than one typed aspect,
- or treating unresolved comparison as if it forced skepticism rather than disciplined suspension or local coexistence.

This file gives the archive a compact **contrast profile** so future revisions can say when two packages are genuine rivals, when they are only differently carved handles on one target, when they are complementary but non-equivalent, and what sort of comparative verdict—selection, layering, splitting, coexistence, or suspension—has actually been earned.

## Working default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Comparative ontology is contrastive, grain-sensitive, and category-sensitive rather than winner-take-all by default.**

And more specifically:

> **Do not treat two ontological articulations as genuine rivals until the archive has fixed the target, grain, category scheme, contrast class, and decision criteria; many apparent disputes turn out to be redescription, scale-shift, selective retention, or legitimate layered coexistence.**

This fits the archive's wider commitments:

- **naturalness** matters because some carvings track joint structure better than others,
- **grain** matters because rival descriptions often differ in determinacy or scale rather than truth-value,
- **evidence and abduction** matter because comparative choice is often inferentially mediated,
- **promotion** matters because success may support only one process, mechanism, structure, or status profile inside a wider package,
- **reference** matters because packages may share a target while redescribing it,
- and **revision** matters because later inquiry often preserves one target under a recarved ontology rather than replacing a whole world-picture.

## Core distinctions

### 1. Rivalry is not mere difference

The archive should distinguish:

- **genuine incompatibility**,
- **difference in grain**,
- **difference in aim**,
- **difference in category choice**,
- **difference in representational vehicle**,
- and **difference in scope**.

Otherwise it will keep mistaking alternative descriptions, models, scales, or purposes for flat contradiction.

### 2. Same target, different carving is not target plurality by default

The archive should distinguish:

- one target under **different descriptive articulations**,
- one target under **different scales of resolution**,
- **overlapping target families**,
- and genuinely **distinct targets**.

Otherwise it will keep sliding from different handles on one reality to “two worlds”, or from one stable label to falsely assumed target identity.

### 3. Category conflict is stronger than stylistic contrast

The archive should distinguish:

- cases where one package says **object** and another says **process**,
- cases where one says **entity** and another says **structure**,
- cases where one says **fundamental** and another says **derivative but robust**,
- and cases where the difference is merely verbal or pragmatic.

Otherwise it will keep underestimating serious ontological disagreement and overestimating superficial redescription.

### 4. Local choice is not global victory

The archive should distinguish:

- **local superiority** in one domain,
- **regional superiority** across one family of cases,
- **cross-domain pressure** toward one package,
- and a supposed **global metaphysical settlement**.

Otherwise it will keep letting one hard-won local success legislate the whole ontology.

### 5. Ontological economy is not representational economy

The archive should distinguish:

- fewer **posits**,
- fewer **categories**,
- fewer **parameters or variables**,
- cleaner **description**,
- and genuinely leaner **ontology**.

Otherwise it will keep treating simple bookkeeping as simple being.

### 6. Comparative criteria are plural and typed

The archive should distinguish at least:

- **world-fit**,
- **evidential support**,
- **causal or mechanistic articulation**,
- **modal articulation**,
- **naturalness/joint-carving**,
- **cross-case integration**,
- **manipulability or intervention sensitivity**,
- **historical retention**,
- and **representational manageability**.

Otherwise it will keep collapsing all comparative choice into one flat appeal to simplicity, elegance, or usefulness.

### 7. Suspension and layered coexistence are real comparative verdicts

The archive should distinguish:

- **choosing one package over another**,
- **layering two compatible articulations**,
- **splitting the domain**,
- **keeping plural handles under active comparison**,
- and **refusing decision pending stronger discrimination**.

Otherwise it will keep forcing premature monism or lazy anything-goes pluralism.

## Four live options

### 1. Winner-take-all monism

**Picture:** once one ontological package is explanatorily or evidentially superior, its rivals should generally be treated as defeated.

**What it gets right:**
- some metaphysical packages really are incompatible,
- and comparison would be pointless if no package could ever lose.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it overstates the frequency of genuine contradiction,
- and it underdescribes scale-shifts, category shifts, and layered coexistence.

### 2. Permissive pluralism

**Picture:** many successful carvings can coexist without pressure to choose among them, because ontology is largely interest-relative.

**What it gets right:**
- one domain can support more than one fruitful articulation,
- and not every difference in carving needs elimination.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it weakens contrast classes too much,
- and it underdescribes when one package really is better world-tracking than another.

### 3. Pragmatic localism

**Picture:** comparative choice is always local to aims, practices, and purposes; metaphysical comparison should rarely go further.

**What it gets right:**
- aims and practices matter,
- and local success should not be inflated into a cosmic verdict too cheaply.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it leaves too little room for cross-domain ontological discipline,
- and it risks treating all serious metaphysical comparison as disguised engineering choice.

### 4. Typed comparative realism

**Picture:** comparison is real, but typed. Some packages are genuine rivals, some are scale-variants, some are layered complements, and some are representationally useful without earning ontology. The archive should choose, layer, split, or suspend only after making the contrast class and criteria explicit.

**What it gets right:**
- it preserves real disagreement,
- it preserves real pluralism where earned,
- and it prevents both premature unification and premature fragmentation.

**What it risks if left alone:**
- it can become slogan-like unless each case states exactly what the rival packages are and what would count as a discriminating result.

## Archive commitments

### 1. Make the contrast class explicit before announcing a winner

“Better ontology” is empty until the live rivals and their aims are fixed.

### 2. Distinguish rivalry from recarving

Do not treat one target under a new grain, category, or explanatory embedding as automatically a new target.

### 3. Let comparison be category-sensitive

Sometimes the real issue is whether a domain is best understood in object-, process-, mechanism-, structure-, status-, or response-dependent terms.

### 4. Prefer local verdicts to global overreach

A package can win in one domain, or for one aspect of a domain, without settling the whole metaphysics.

### 5. Use plural criteria without flattening them

Simplicity, naturalness, explanatory articulation, robustness, manipulability, and historical retention do not all track ontology in the same way.

### 6. Keep suspension and layering live

When comparison has not yet earned a single package, the archive should say so rather than force a premature total verdict.

## Contrast profile rule for future revisions

Before inferring a metaphysical verdict from language like **better ontology**, **rival package**, **same target under another description**, **more natural carving**, **mere redescription**, **competing frameworks**, **equally good models**, **different levels of description**, **only a pragmatic choice**, **one real disagreement**, or **plural but compatible**, future revisions should answer at least these questions:

1. **target domain** — what phenomenon, domain, or candidate reality is under comparison;
2. **package A / package B** — what the rival or allegedly rival articulations actually say;
3. **grain and scale** — whether the packages differ in determinacy, level, scale, or resolution;
4. **category scheme** — object, process, mechanism, structure, status profile, centered fact, response-dependent property, or mixed package;
5. **contrast class** — which alternatives are genuinely live and which are not;
6. **comparison criteria** — evidential support, world-fit, explanatory depth, mechanism, naturalness, economy, manipulability, historical retention, practice fit, or mixed criteria;
7. **shared content** — what both packages preserve, if anything;
8. **real incompatibility** — what exactly cannot be true together, if anything;
9. **decision type** — choose, layer, split domain, keep plural handles, or suspend;
10. **final reality verdict** — typed superiority, selective preference, layered coexistence, pragmatic-only use, or still unsettled.

## What this file does **not** say

It does **not** say:

- that all metaphysical disputes are verbal,
- that one victorious package must always replace all others,
- that plural successful articulations are automatically equally good,
- that practical usefulness is the same as ontological superiority,
- or that undecided comparison licenses skepticism about the world.

## Bottom line

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Many apparent ontological disputes are really disputes about grain, category, target-fixation, or contrast class; real comparative ontology begins only once those are fixed.**

And more specifically:

> **When rival packages remain after that discipline, compare them with typed criteria and let the verdict be equally typed: choose, layer, split, or suspend—but do not let one loose contrast or one useful description settle the metaphysics by itself.**
