# Causation, Production, Counterfactuals, and Intervention

This file gives the archive a compact policy for **causation**.

## Why this file exists

The archive already treats:

- **powers** as real,
- **laws** as derivative articulations of modal structure,
- **dependence** as plural,
- and **absences/omissions** as a genuine metaphysical pressure point.

But without a direct causal file, the archive risks sliding among at least four different things as if they were one:

1. **production**,
2. **difference-making**,
3. **counterfactual dependence**,
4. and **intervention-sensitive modeling**.

A compact non-reductive metaphysics needs all four in view, but it should not confuse them.

## Four live options

### 1. Regularity-first accounts

**Picture:** causes are patterns of constant or lawlike succession.

**What they get right:**
- causal discourse is often supported by stable patterns,
- type-level science needs repeatable associations,
- and causal judgment is frequently inferential.

**What they get wrong for this archive:**
- they make causation too thin,
- underdescribe production,
- and fit badly with the archive's powers-first resistance to a lawless mosaic.

### 2. Counterfactual / difference-making accounts

**Picture:** a cause is something such that, had it not occurred, the effect would not have occurred, or would have differed.

**What they get right:**
- causation is tied to explanation and control,
- they illuminate preemption, overdetermination, and probabilistic cases,
- and they scale well to token, type, and variable-level talk.

**What they get wrong if left alone:**
- simple counterfactual dependence is not enough for all causation,
- model choice and similarity metrics matter,
- and difference-making can outrun metaphysical production.

### 3. Intervention / causal-model accounts

**Picture:** causal structure is what supports the right intervention-sensitive counterfactuals.

**What they get right:**
- they clarify variable-level causation,
- they are extremely useful in science,
- and they force precision about pathways, confounding, and control.

**What they get wrong if turned into the whole metaphysics:**
- they can become too model-relative,
- and they risk treating epistemic access conditions as the ontology itself.

### 4. Production / powers / process accounts

**Picture:** causes genuinely bring about, sustain, prevent, or modify effects in virtue of powers unfolding in processes under structural constraints.

**What they get right:**
- they preserve causal efficacy,
- fit well with powers and process metaphysics,
- and explain why causation feels thicker than bare regularity.

**What they get wrong if left alone:**
- they can become obscure if they do not connect to disciplined counterfactual or intervention tests,
- and they can overstate production where the real issue is constitutive or grounding dependence.

## Current archive answer

The archive should adopt a **hybrid but asymmetric** answer:

> **Causation is objective worldly difference-making realized through productive or preventing processes of power-bearing entities under modal-structural constraints.**

That means:

- causation is **not merely regular succession**,
- causation is **not exhausted by counterfactual semantics**,
- counterfactuals and interventions are **diagnostic and model-guiding tools**,
- production belongs in the metaphysics,
- and causal claims are often true because a structured process made, sustained, blocked, or redirected a difference.

In compressed form:

> **production grounds the causal reality; counterfactual and intervention structure tracks it.**

## Archive commitments

### 1. Do not collapse causation into grounding or constitution

Grounding answers a constitutive question: **in virtue of what does this obtain?**

Causation answers a productive or dynamic question: **what made, sustained, altered, or prevented this?**

A single case may involve both, but the archive should not let one relation impersonate the other.

### 2. Causal relata vary by domain

The archive should allow causal claims among:

- events,
- processes,
- variables,
- states,
- omissions or blocked sustainers,
- and institutionally organized actions.

Do not assume in advance that all causal discourse has one privileged relatum type.

### 3. Counterfactuals are indispensable but not self-standing ontology

Counterfactual analysis remains one of the archive's best tools for testing whether a putative cause really made a difference. But the archive should treat counterfactuals as **tracking** causal structure, not automatically **constituting** it.

### 4. Intervention is a privileged epistemic window, not necessarily the metaphysical core

Interventions are often the cleanest way to reveal causal structure. But it does not follow that causation is metaphysically nothing over and above manipulability.

### 5. Production includes sustaining, preventing, and redirecting

The archive should not restrict causation to one-shot pushes. Causes may:

- initiate,
- sustain,
- inhibit,
- enable,
- channel,
- or interrupt processes.

This is especially important for life, social systems, maintenance processes, and omission cases.

### 6. Absences are causally serious when anchored in process failure or blocked support

The archive should continue to resist treating absences as basic entities. But it should also resist saying that omissions are unreal. The current default is:

> **causally salient absences are usually derivative facts about disrupted, missing, or blocked sustaining processes in an already structured field of dependence and expectation.**

### 7. Causation is level-sensitive

Higher-level causal claims need not be metaphysically illicit just because they are not fundamental. In many domains, macro-organized processes are the right causal level because that is where stable difference-making and control variables live.

## Causal profile rule

When a future revision uses causal vocabulary, it should say which of the following is doing the work:

1. **token production** — this event/process brought about that event/process,
2. **type-level causal tendency** — C-type conditions tend to produce E-type outcomes,
3. **variable influence** — manipulating X would change Y,
4. **sustaining or enabling causation** — ongoing support keeps a state/process in being,
5. **preventive or inhibitory causation** — a factor blocks or damps a process,
6. **omissive causation** — a structured failure to sustain or intervene made the difference,
7. **apparent causation masking another relation** — what is really at issue is grounding, constitution, or realization.

If a proposal does not state this, its causal talk is under-disciplined.

## Archive default on causal explanation

The archive should presently prefer a **production-plus-difference** view:

- **powers and process** explain how causal efficacy is possible,
- **structure** explains pathway dependence, system constraints, and level-specific organization,
- **counterfactuals** test whether a factor made a difference,
- **interventions and causal models** help identify causal profile,
- and **laws** summarize or articulate the stable modal organization within which causation unfolds.

So the current order is:

1. **modal powers and structured processes make causal production possible,**
2. **counterfactual and intervention structure reveal how the difference runs,**
3. **causal models are disciplined representations of that structure.**

## Working conclusion

The archive should now adopt the following causal policy:

> **Be realist about causation, production, and efficacy; use counterfactuals and interventions as clarifying tests; keep causal relata plural by domain; and distinguish dynamic making-from, sustaining, preventing, and omissive failure from constitutive dependence.**

That is the current archive default on causation.
