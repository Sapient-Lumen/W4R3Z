# Things, Stuff, Fields, and Distributed Being

## Why this file exists

The archive already has files on categories, mereology, location, substance, identity, levels, nonexistents, and constitution. But it still lacks a direct rule for a recurrent question:

> **Is the target best treated as a thing, a stuff/mass, a field, a medium, a network-like distributed entity, or some disciplined mixed case?**

Without a direct answer, the archive keeps risking six predictable mistakes:

- forcing all concrete reality into **countable object ontology**,
- treating **stuff** talk as just sloppy thing talk,
- treating **fields** as either merely mathematical or automatically object-like,
- assuming that **distributed presence** is metaphysically thin merely because it is not compact,
- confusing **location**, **constitution**, and **occupancy mode**,
- and sliding from one clear case to another unlike case merely because both are “in space”.

This file gives the archive a compact **distributed-being profile** so future revisions can say what ontological mode a target has before making further claims about unity, counting, persistence, causation, or commitment.

## Working default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Concrete reality is not exhausted by compact countable objects. Some concrete domains are better modeled as stuff-like, field-like, medium-like, network-like, or otherwise distributed presences. Those modes are not automatically less real; they are differently unified, located, counted, and explained.**

This fits the archive’s wider commitments:

- **form** still matters, because the relevant organization may be objectual, mass-like, field-like, or infrastructural,
- **process** still matters, because many distributed entities persist through ongoing transmission, flow, or regulation,
- **power** still matters, because fields, media, and stuffs often earn reality through structured causal capacity,
- **structure** still matters, because distributed order is often constitutive rather than ornamental,
- and **grounding** still matters, because not every distributed profile is fundamental, even when it is ontologically serious.

## Core distinctions

### 1. Thing / object profile

A target is **thing-like** when it is best treated as a countable individual with relatively determinate unity and persistence conditions.

Typical marks:

- countable as **one object** rather than as an amount,
- has a salient unity condition,
- often has relatively stable boundaries,
- often has sortal-governed persistence conditions.

Paradigm uses:

- organisms,
- artifacts,
- many ordinary objects,
- some institutions under office-like or corporation-like descriptions.

### 2. Stuff / mass profile

A target is **stuff-like** when it is better modeled as matter, material, or amount rather than as a countable individual.

Typical marks:

- measured by amount, quantity, or portion more naturally than by unit-count,
- divisibility does not automatically destroy category membership,
- persistence is often not the persistence of one individual thing,
- explanatory work often turns on constitution, supply, admixture, phase, or material continuity.

Paradigm uses:

- water,
- clay,
- iron,
- smoke,
- blood,
- sand,
- fuel,
- informational or institutional “stuff” only by clear analogy, not by default.

### 3. Field profile

A target is **field-like** when it is distributed across a region or structure in a way not well captured by compact object occupancy.

Typical marks:

- distributed intensity or influence,
- occupancy not exhausted by one neat bounded body,
- location often better handled by region, spread, reach, or coupling profile,
- causal or explanatory work often turns on patterned distribution rather than discrete-object collision.

Paradigm uses:

- physical field-like cases,
- force or influence profiles,
- some ecological or infrastructural spread cases,
- some socially distributed position structures when treated analogically and with discipline.

### 4. Medium profile

A target is **medium-like** when it is what carries, transmits, sustains, or channels processes without always being the main countable individual under discussion.

Typical marks:

- supports propagation, transfer, immersion, or embedding,
- may be stuff-like or field-like rather than object-like,
- often matters because other entities or processes depend on it.

Paradigm uses:

- air as a transmission medium,
- water as a sustaining or propagating medium,
- infrastructural media,
- institutional or symbolic media only when the analogy is explanatory rather than decorative.

### 5. Network / distributed-entity profile

A target is **distributed-entity-like** when its unity depends on linked organization across places, nodes, roles, or channels rather than on compact material adjacency.

Typical marks:

- persistence through regulated connectivity or role structure,
- partial material turnover without loss of identity,
- often mixed spatial / functional / institutional boundaries,
- counting is possible, but only with domain-typed unity conditions.

Paradigm uses:

- supply chains,
- power grids,
- digital infrastructures,
- corporate structures,
- ecosystems under some descriptions,
- diasporic or translocal institutions under disciplined conditions.

## What this file does **not** say

It does **not** say:

- that every field is fundamental,
- that every stuff is more basic than every thing,
- that distributed presence dissolves individuality,
- that object ontology is wrong,
- or that all concrete categories reduce to one master spatial form.

The point is only that **different modes of concrete being need different ontological profiles**.

## Archive default

The current archive default is:

1. **Do not force thing ontology everywhere.** Some domains are better handled as stuff, field, medium, or distributed entity.
2. **Do not treat stuff as ontologically second-rate by default.** Some constitution and continuity questions are really about material or amount structure rather than about individual identity.
3. **Do not treat field-like spread as merely mathematical by default.** Sometimes distributed profile is part of the worldly ontology, even if its best theory is highly mathematical.
4. **Do not infer one metaphysical verdict from one locative pattern.** Shared region, distributed occupancy, and diffuse presence do different work in different domains.
5. **Keep mixed cases explicit.** An organism may be a thing with field-like operational reach; an institution may be a distributed entity with thing-like legal identity; a statue may be a thing constituted by stuff; a storm may be a process with field-like and medium-dependent features.

## Distributed-being profile rule for future revisions

Before inferring a metaphysical verdict from a target’s spread, materiality, or occupancy, specify:

1. **ontological mode** — thing, stuff, field, medium, distributed entity, process, or mixed case;
2. **counting mode** — unit-count, amount-measure, intensity-profile, node-pattern, or mixed;
3. **location mode** — compact occupancy, exact/weak location, regional spread, operational reach, distributed node-location, or mixed;
4. **unity mode** — sortal unity, material continuity, field-coherence, transmission medium, role-network organization, or mixed;
5. **persistence mode** — object persistence, continuing amount, pattern maintenance, field continuation, infrastructure continuity, or mixed;
6. **causal/explanatory mode** — collision/interaction, transmission, diffusion, regulation, coupling, boundary condition, or mixed;
7. **ontological verdict** — fundamental, derivative but robust, derivative and profile-like, merely representational, or still unsettled.

Otherwise the archive will keep mistaking diffuse reality for unreality, and countability for ontological privilege.

## Stress cases

### Clay and statue

The clay is often better treated partly as **stuff-like material basis**, while the statue is a **thing-like constituted artifact**. This helps explain why constitution, counting, and persistence can diverge.

### Organism and metabolism

The organism is often **thing-like**, but it persists through the continued regulation of flows of **stuff** and through processual organization. Treating it as only a lump obscures the archive’s process realism.

### Storms, fires, and clouds

These are often better treated as **processual distributed entities** with medium- and field-like features than as ordinary compact objects.

### Electromagnetic or force-like cases

These are often better treated as **field-like** or at least not automatically as compact individuals, even when they couple to object-like sources or receivers.

### Institutions and infrastructures

Some are best treated as **distributed entities** whose persistence turns on role structure, records, recognition, enforcement, and material support rather than on one bounded body.

## Compressions earned by this file

This file compresses several earlier tensions:

- the **location** file can now distinguish compact from distributed presence more directly,
- the **constitution** file can now distinguish constituted things from constituting stuff more cleanly,
- the **substance** file can now avoid forcing one bearer model onto fields and distributed infrastructures,
- the **identity** file can now treat individuality and reidentification as typed by ontological mode,
- the **levels** file can now separate scale from ontological mode,
- and the **teleology** file can now treat function-bearing distributed systems without pretending they are all ordinary compact objects.

## Current practical default for the archive

When in doubt:

- begin with the **lowest-inflation profile** that still preserves real explanatory structure,
- allow **thing**, **stuff**, **field**, and **distributed entity** to remain distinct unless compression is clearly earned,
- and prefer **typed mixed profiles** over forcing all difficult cases into one master image of what a concrete entity must be.
