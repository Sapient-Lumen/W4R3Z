# Naturalness, Joint-Carving, and Ontic Selectivity

## Why this file exists

The archive already talks about:

- **properties, repeatables, and kinds**,
- **relations and structure**,
- **category discipline**,
- **levels and cross-level explanation**,
- **identity and individuation**,
- **ontological commitment and seriousness**,
- and **vagueness / borderline ontology**.

But it still lacks one direct rule for a pressure point running through all of them:

- why some distinctions seem to track the world better than others,
- why some predicates and categories are more explanatory than their gerrymandered rivals,
- how the archive should speak of **naturalness** or **joint-carving** without pretending that every domain has one unique final vocabulary,
- and when structural, kind, or level-talk is tracking real organization rather than merely convenient description.

Without that discipline, the archive risks sliding among:

1. abundant property talk that makes every predicate equally ontologically serious,
2. kind realism that assumes one unique carve of reality in every domain,
3. structural realism that treats all selectivity as purely relational without saying which relations matter,
4. ontological-commitment talk that inflates every useful classification into reality,
5. and pluralism that becomes too permissive to explain why some classifications are better than others.

A compact non-skeptical, non-reductive metaphysics needs a better rule-set than that.

## Core claim

The archive should adopt **typed naturalness realism with local joint-carving pluralism**.

Compressed:

> **Reality is not equally well described by every true predicate or classification. Some properties, relations, structures, and kinds are more elite, projectible, explanatory, or constitutively important than others. But this need not force one unique global carve of being for every domain.**

This means:

- do **not** treat all predicates as equally ontologically revealing,
- do **not** assume there is one universal final vocabulary for every scale and domain,
- do **not** collapse naturalness into mere linguistic convenience,
- and do **prefer** classifications that earn their keep through explanation, induction, modal stability, structural role, or kind-sensitive individuation.

## Five distinctions the archive now needs

### 1. Abundant predication vs ontic selectivity

Many predicates may be meaningful, useful, and true. But it does not follow that they all cut reality at equally significant joints.

This matters because the archive already distinguishes:

- existence,
- fundamentality,
- derivative reality,
- and mere representational convenience.

It should now also distinguish **mere predicability** from **ontic selectivity**.

### 2. Naturalness vs fundamentality

A feature can be derivative yet still track a real joint in the world.

This matters because:

- biological kinds,
- institutional roles,
- mechanistic organization,
- and some higher-level explanatory variables

may be far from fundamental while still being more natural than arbitrary disjunctions or convenience classes.

The archive should therefore not equate **natural** with **fundamental**.

### 3. Joint-carving vs one unique final carve

Some domains may admit more than one non-arbitrary, reality-responsive classification.

This matters because chemistry, biology, psychology, infrastructure, institutions, and social kinds may support:

- overlapping explanatory partitions,
- scale-relative but objective classifications,
- and domain-typed naturalness rather than a single once-and-for-all partition of being.

The archive should therefore allow **local joint-carving pluralism** under discipline.

### 4. Naturalness vs mere usefulness

A predicate may be pragmatically useful without tracking a real metaphysical pattern; but some useful classifications are useful precisely because they latch onto real structure.

The archive should therefore ask whether a classification succeeds because of:

- convenience only,
- epistemic tractability,
- causal/explanatory projectibility,
- modal robustness,
- structural role,
- or kind-sensitive organization.

### 5. Elite structure vs authoritarian ontology

The archive should neither say:

- every category is equally good,
- nor that only one sparse microphysical vocabulary is metaphysically respectable.

A non-reductive metaphysics needs room for **elite structure above the base** where organization, powers, relations, and lawfulness earn it.

## Archive default

The current default is:

1. **be realist about comparative naturalness or ontic selectivity**;
2. **treat naturalness as domain-typed rather than flatly global by default**;
3. **allow more than one legitimate joint-carving in some domains**, while rejecting arbitrary classificatory permissiveness;
4. **let explanatory, modal, structural, and projective success count as evidence of naturalness**;
5. **keep global one-carve monism and anything-goes pluralism as live rivals, not defaults**.

Compressed again:

> **Some ways of dividing reality are better because reality itself is more structured, stable, projectible, and explanatorily cooperative along those lines; but those lines need not all belong to one final all-purpose classificatory scheme.**

## What the archive should now say

### 1. Not every true predicate tracks equal reality

The archive should resist the lazy move from:

- “this can be truly said”,
- or “this variable is useful in a model”,
- or “this category appears in discourse”,

straight to:

- “this therefore marks a natural property or kind”.

### 2. Selectivity can be real above the base

The archive should allow that some derivative domains carve real joints because of:

- stable organization,
- mechanistic decomposition,
- powers,
- modal constraints,
- relational structure,
- or teleofunctional roles.

This matters for organisms, artifacts, institutions, infrastructures, and some abstract or formal structures.

### 3. Naturalness should be typed

Different domains may earn selectivity differently.

For example:

- **physics-adjacent cases** may lean on sparse law/power/structure,
- **chemistry and biology** may lean on stable organization, causal profiles, and projectible clustering,
- **social ontology** may lean on rule-structured and enforcement-backed roles,
- **mathematical or formal cases** may lean on structural indispensability rather than spatiotemporal occupancy.

So the archive should ask not only whether something is natural, but **natural in virtue of what profile**.

### 4. Joint-carving is not exhausted by language

The archive should resist both:

- pure conventionalism about kinds and categories,
- and overreaction toward one final metaphysical dictionary.

Reality can push back against classification without dictating only one respectable conceptual scheme for every purpose.

### 5. Naturalness helps explain other archive distinctions

This file should now help the archive say why some classifications are better suited than others for:

- persistence criteria,
- explanation,
- modal inference,
- counterfactual support,
- identity and individuation,
- level and scale distinctions,
- and ontological seriousness.

### 6. Gerrymander warnings should become explicit

When a proposal relies on a category, variable, property, or kind, the archive should ask whether the posit is:

- gerrymandered,
- merely convenience-based,
- multiply realizable but still projectible,
- structurally elite,
- or genuinely kind- or form-sensitive.

### 7. Naturalness is evidentially important, not magically decisive

Evidence of naturalness can come from:

- explanatory indispensability,
- projectibility across cases,
- modal robustness,
- resilience across theory change,
- structural integration,
- and successful cross-context tracking.

But no single one of these automatically settles the issue in every domain.

## Naturalness-profile rule

When a future revision uses language like **natural**, **joint-carving**, **elite**, **projectible**, **gerrymandered**, **arbitrary classification**, **more objective kind**, **structural selectivity**, or **better variable**, it should state:

1. **what category and domain are at issue** — property, relation, kind, variable, structure, level, social role, abstractum, or something else;
2. **whether the issue is naturalness, fundamentality, explanatory usefulness, or ontological commitment**;
3. **what is doing the selectivity work** — law, power, form, structure, projectibility, mechanism, teleology, institution, mathematical role, or some mixed basis;
4. **whether the case supports one privileged carve or several disciplined local carves**;
5. **what the rival classifications are** and why they are less natural, less stable, less explanatory, or more gerrymandered;
6. **what explanatory loss follows** if the target classification is treated as merely convenient rather than reality-responsive.

If a proposal does not do this, its naturalness or joint-carving talk is under-disciplined.

## What this file is not saying

- It is **not** saying only fundamental physics tracks real joints.
- It is **not** saying every science or practice gets equal ontological status.
- It is **not** saying there is always one unique best carve for every domain.
- It is **not** saying naturalness is only linguistic elegance.
- It is **not** saying higher-level kinds are natural just because they are familiar.

It is only saying that the archive now needs a **typed naturalness-and-selectivity discipline**.
