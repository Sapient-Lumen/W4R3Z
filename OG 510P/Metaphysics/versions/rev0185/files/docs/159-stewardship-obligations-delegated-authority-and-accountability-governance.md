# Stewardship Obligations, Delegated Authority, and Accountability Governance

## Why this file exists

`158-public-reliance-citation-dispute-withdrawal-and-retraction-governance.md` prevents publication, citation, teaching uptake, review language, or package polish from becoming unrestricted permission to rely. It asks what public-use status a claim has, what audience and use are permitted, what warnings must travel, how it should be cited, how disputes enter the archive, and how reliance is narrowed, withdrawn, retracted, or historically retained.

That still leaves a further failure mode. A claim can have an appropriate public-use status and still become irresponsible because no one knows **who owes what after reliance is allowed**.

A maintainer may publish a reliance packet and then leave disputes orphaned. A reviewer may permit narrow wording and then watch it become broad public authority. A teacher may compress the archive honestly at first and then reuse slides after the source version has been withdrawn. A fork maintainer may import a term register without importing the deprecation rules. A citation user may quote a verdict without preserving its version, status, and warning. A domain adapter may operationalize a source-neutral metaphysical distinction inside law, medicine, engineering, policy, or institutional practice without domain review. A derivative tool may summarize a packet while hiding the return path. A public user may rely on a claim beyond its allowed use and then blame the archive for an inference the archive explicitly forbade.

The danger is **orphaned responsibility**: every permission is formally scoped, but the duties attached to custody, citation, transmission, review, correction, update, warning, and withdrawal are left implicit.

This file adds a stewardship layer after public reliance: **obligations, delegated authority, role accountability, update duties, notice duties, fork duties, teaching duties, source-domain duties, and high-stakes reliance constraints**. Its job is not to create moral decoration around the archive. Its job is to say which role must preserve which limits when archive material is maintained, cited, taught, forked, summarized, automated, operationalized, disputed, corrected, withdrawn, or entrusted to another steward.

## Compressed default

The archive should now say:

> **Reliance permission creates stewardship duties. State the responsible role, the obligation class, the delegated authority, the update/watch duty, the warning duty, the correction path, and the accountability consequence before treating public use as responsible.**

In shorter form:

> **Permission without stewardship becomes abandonment.**

And more carefully:

> **Every public-facing or derivative use of archive material should identify the stewarding role, the authority actually held, the obligations accepted, the obligations explicitly refused, the version and source watch required, the warnings that must travel, the disputes that must be routed, the corrections that must be noticed, the handoffs that must preserve limits, and the failures that trigger demotion, quarantine, withdrawal, or do-not-reuse status.**

## The basic unit: the stewardship packet

A **stewardship packet** records who is responsible for a public or derivative use and what obligations follow. It is required when a package, file, application dossier, precedent, teaching derivative, fork, migration, public citation, review claim, source-dependent example, machine-generated summary, institutional adaptation, or operationalized use asks to rely on archive material beyond private reading.

A packet should include:

- **Stewardship ID** — stable identifier for the stewardship relation.
- **Artifact or claim** — version, package, file, section, packet, derivative, fork, teaching output, source-bound example, citation, public notice, or operational use.
- **Role accepting stewardship** — maintainer, contributor, reviewer, citation user, teacher, derivative author, fork maintainer, source-domain adapter, public relying user, institutional adopter, automated-system operator, dispute handler, or successor custodian.
- **Authority basis** — authorship, custody, review appointment, domain expertise, institutional role, fork ownership, teaching context, citation use, technical operation, maintenance responsibility, or explicit delegation.
- **Authority limits** — what the role may not decide, certify, update, withdraw, operationalize, or represent.
- **Public-use status inherited from `158`** — U0 through U9, including required warnings.
- **Obligation class** — one of the O-classes below.
- **Accepted duties** — citation duty, warning duty, version watch, source watch, dispute routing, correction notice, update propagation, derivative migration, withdrawal notice, or refusal duty.
- **Declined duties** — duties explicitly not accepted so others do not assume them.
- **Delegation relation** — whether any duty is delegated, to whom, under what limit, and what remains with the original steward.
- **Return path** — where disputes, corrections, appeals, source changes, version changes, and withdrawal requests go.
- **Escalation trigger** — what moves the case to `153`, `154`, `157`, `158`, or a future revision.
- **Accountability consequence** — warning update, status downgrade, derivative quarantine, citation correction, teaching withdrawal, fork notice, public hold, do-not-reuse designation, or release rollback.
- **Expiry or review trigger** — version change, source change, dispute, review expiry, fork divergence, public teaching use, high-stakes adaptation, or changed audience.

## Permission and obligation are different

A public-use packet may permit citation, teaching, summary, or controlled reliance. That permission does not automatically assign all obligations to the original archive maintainer, the reviewer, the citer, the teacher, the fork maintainer, or the public user. Different roles carry different duties.

Do not infer:

- from **public citation permitted** to **maintainer owes unlimited downstream support**,
- from **teaching reuse permitted** to **teacher may ignore update status**,
- from **source-neutral governance file** to **domain adapter may operationalize without domain review**,
- from **reviewed for integration** to **reviewer accepts responsibility for public reliance**,
- from **fork allowed** to **fork inherits current-archive authority**,
- from **withdrawal rule stated** to **all old derivatives are automatically corrected**,
- from **citation warning included once** to **future uses need no update watch**.

Stewardship governance exists to prevent both extremes: unbounded liability for any archive author and unowned responsibility for public use.

## Obligation classes

Use these classes to state the duty profile attached to a public or derivative use.

### O0 — no stewardship claim

The item is private scratch, abandoned, or explicitly not stewarded. It should not be cited as a maintained archive object. If it circulates, it carries a warning: no maintained stewardship relation.

### O1 — attribution-only duty

The role must identify version, file, packet, and status but does not accept update watch, dispute handling, migration, or correction duties. This may fit historical references, casual orientation, or bibliographic mention.

### O2 — warning-preservation duty

The role must preserve required warnings, forbidden-use statements, non-verdicts, source limits, review limits, and public-use limits. Most public summaries and teaching glosses require at least O2.

### O3 — citation-and-return-path duty

The role must preserve citation form and provide a route back to the controlling file or packet. This is the minimum class for controlled public citation, derivative notes, and reusable teaching material.

### O4 — update-watch duty

The role must check whether the relevant version, source, public-use packet, withdrawal status, or deprecation status has changed before reuse. This is required for living teaching artifacts, forks, source-bound examples, and public-facing derivative materials.

### O5 — dispute-routing duty

The role must receive or forward disputes to the appropriate archive route: public reliance disputes to `158`, review disputes to `157`, provenance disputes to `156`, release/lineage disputes to `154`-`155`, reception signals to `153`, and substantive classification disputes to `144`-`150`.

### O6 — correction-and-notice duty

The role must issue or propagate corrections when a relied-on claim is narrowed, clarified, held, withdrawn, retracted, or superseded. This duty belongs to maintainers of derivative artifacts, public teaching sets, citation guides, fork releases, and institutional adaptations.

### O7 — migration-and-deprecation duty

The role must provide a migration note, deprecation notice, or replacement path when older material is no longer safe to reuse as stated. This is required for forks, major derivatives, public course packs, institutional adaptations, and any artifact that represents itself as tracking the current archive.

### O8 — domain-responsibility duty

The role must obtain or state domain review before using archive material in law, medicine, engineering, safety, finance, clinical practice, governance, institutional policy, or other high-stakes operational settings. Source-neutral metaphysical permission does not satisfy domain responsibility.

### O9 — entrusted-steward duty

The role accepts ongoing custody for a package, fork, public derivative, teaching registry, public errata page, source-refresh branch, or institutional implementation. O9 requires explicit authority limits, update cadence or trigger rules, dispute path, deprecation procedure, successor plan, and exit/transfer conditions. Without those fields, the claim should be downgraded to O4-O7 rather than treated as entrusted stewardship.

## Role matrix

### Archive maintainer

The maintainer has responsibility for package integrity, front-door coherence, control-stack integration, release packets, lineage/provenance/review/public-reliance packets, and archive-level correction when a revision is issued. The maintainer does not automatically owe domain validation, legal approval, clinical safety, institutional implementation, external peer review, or maintenance of every third-party derivative.

Minimum duties: O3 for released packages; O4 for current-version maintenance; O5-O7 when disputes, withdrawals, deprecations, or migrations are opened; O8 only when the maintainer explicitly creates a domain-bound or high-stakes adaptation.

### Contributor or proposed-revision author

A contributor owes target clarity, source disclosure, version identity, non-verdict preservation, and update-footprint honesty. A contributor may propose, but does not automatically certify, release, publish, withdraw, or grant public reliance.

Minimum duties: O2 for proposal language; O3 when the proposal is circulated; O4 when the proposal cites current archive status; O5 if the contributor receives a dispute about the proposal.

### Reviewer or certifier

A reviewer owes scope discipline. Review language must not exceed the actual evidence inspected, authority basis, independence status, and permitted claim language. A reviewer who sees review laundering should either correct the wording or withdraw the review claim.

Minimum duties: O2 for warning preservation; O3 for review citation; O5 for review disputes; O6 if the review finding is materially misstated; O8 when domain certification is claimed.

### Citation user

A citation user owes version identity, file or packet identification, public-use status, and warning preservation. A citation user is not a maintainer, but may become responsible for correction if their citation is public, derivative, instructional, or operational.

Minimum duties: O1 for historical mention; O3 for controlled public citation; O4 if the citation claims currentness; O6 if the user publicly corrects or retracts a cited use.

### Teacher or pedagogical derivative author

A teacher may simplify, but must not make simplification look stronger, more current, more settled, or more public than the controlling packet permits. Teaching artifacts should state compression level, public-use status, omitted limits, update trigger, and return path.

Minimum duties: O2 for one-time classroom orientation; O3 for reusable teaching notes; O4 for living course materials; O6-O7 if old teaching material remains public after a relevant withdrawal or deprecation.

### Fork maintainer or derivative-project maintainer

A fork maintainer inherits no automatic current-archive authority. The fork must state lineage relation, compatibility class, imported controls, dropped controls, local changes, public-use status, and maintenance responsibility.

Minimum duties: O3 for fork citation; O4 for compatibility claims; O6-O7 for migration, deprecation, or supersession; O9 only if the fork claims ongoing maintained stewardship.

### Source-domain adapter

A source-domain adapter imports archive distinctions into legal, clinical, scientific, engineering, policy, historical, textual, institutional, or professional settings. The adapter owes source currency, domain review, and domain-specific warning, not merely archive citation.

Minimum duties: O4 for source watch; O5 for domain disputes; O6 for source corrections; O8 for high-stakes operational use.

### Public relying user

A public relying user may rely only within the public-use status and required warnings. Reliance beyond scope is not converted into archive permission by need, convenience, repetition, institutional adoption, or citation frequency.

Minimum duties: O1-O3 for citation; O4 before claiming currentness; O8 before high-stakes operational use.

### Automated-system operator or generated-output steward

If a system summarizes, retrieves, ranks, teaches, converts, or answers from archive material, the operator or output steward must preserve version identity, compression level, public-use status, warnings, return path, and update trigger. Automation does not dissolve stewardship; it relocates it.

Minimum duties: O2 for generated summaries; O3 for public outputs; O4 for current-answer claims; O6 if generated outputs are corrected; O7 if a deployed derivative must migrate.

### Successor custodian

A successor custodian accepts custody after a maintainer exit, fork transfer, institutional adoption, or archival handoff. The handoff must state inherited obligations, refused obligations, open debts, deprecations, source-refresh needs, review limits, reliance limits, and successor authority.

Minimum duties: O7 for migration/deprecation memory; O9 when ongoing stewardship is accepted.

## Delegation rule

A role may delegate tasks but not silently erase responsibility. Delegation requires:

- named delegate,
- delegated task,
- retained responsibility,
- authority limit,
- evidence or packet transferred,
- update/notice trigger,
- dispute path,
- and exit condition.

If those fields are missing, the delegation should be treated as informal help, not as a governance handoff.

## Handoff packet

A handoff packet should be used when stewardship moves from one role to another. It should contain:

1. prior steward,
2. successor steward,
3. artifact scope,
4. version scope,
5. accepted duties,
6. declined duties,
7. open debts,
8. unresolved disputes,
9. deprecations or withdrawals in force,
10. source-refresh obligations,
11. review and public-use statuses inherited,
12. warnings that must travel,
13. compatibility limits,
14. evidence transferred,
15. effective date or trigger,
16. exit or successor condition.

Without a handoff packet, later users should not infer that stewardship was cleanly transferred merely because the artifact moved.

## Accountability matrix

When a failure occurs, do not ask only whether the archive sentence was correct. Ask which stewardship duty failed.

| Failure mode | Likely failed duty | First corrective action |
|---|---|---|
| Claim cited without version | O1 / O3 | correct citation and add version/file/packet identity |
| Warning dropped from summary | O2 | restore warning and mark derivative as lower-trust until fixed |
| Currentness claimed from old version | O4 | check lineage and public-use status; add migration note |
| Dispute ignored or sent nowhere | O5 | route to `158`, `157`, `156`, `154`-`155`, `153`, or `144`-`150` as appropriate |
| Public derivative continues after withdrawal | O6 / O7 | issue correction, hold, migration, or do-not-reuse notice |
| Fork claims archive authority without compatibility record | O7 | require lineage packet and compatibility class under `155` |
| Source-bound example used after source change | O4 / O8 | refresh source, narrow example, or withdraw source-bound claim |
| Teaching simplification treated as doctrine | O2 / O3 / O6 | add compression level, omitted limits, and return path |
| Reviewer quoted beyond scope | O2 / O5 / O6 | correct claim-warrant wording under `157` |
| High-stakes operational use from source-neutral file | O8 | require domain review or mark use forbidden |
| Maintained derivative has no successor plan | O9 | create handoff packet or downgrade stewardship status |

## High-stakes boundary

This archive is a metaphysical research archive. It may inform conceptual diagnosis, teaching, philosophical argument, source interpretation, and internal archive governance. It is not by itself a legal, medical, clinical, engineering, financial, safety, security, policy, or institutional authority.

Any use in a high-stakes domain needs domain-specific review, current sources, responsible professional judgment where relevant, and explicit separation between metaphysical diagnosis and operational decision. A public-use status of U4 or U5 does not create O8 satisfaction. A self-reviewed package does not become domain-reviewed because a domain user finds it useful.

High-stakes use should therefore ask:

- What operational decision is being affected?
- Which archive claim is being used?
- What public-use status does it have?
- What source-domain review exists?
- What professional or institutional authority is required?
- What warnings must travel?
- What would count as misuse?
- Who carries correction and update duty?
- What happens if the archive later narrows, withdraws, or retracts the claim?

If these questions cannot be answered, the use should remain conceptual orientation, not operational reliance.

## Source-dependent stewardship

Source-dependent claims create extra duties because the archive's metaphysical lesson may depend on a legal text, scientific result, engineering standard, clinical classification, historical document, institutional rule, empirical dataset, or current practice.

A source-dependent stewardship packet should include:

- source identity,
- source version, date, edition, or access date,
- role of the source in the claim,
- source stability estimate,
- source-refresh trigger,
- domain-review status,
- warning required if the source changes,
- public-use status,
- obligation class,
- and responsible role for source update.

If no one accepts source-watch duty, the public-use status should be downgraded or the example should be marked orientation-only.

## Stewardship of negative space

Sometimes the responsible act is to refuse stewardship. A role should explicitly decline when it cannot maintain currentness, cannot provide domain review, cannot monitor disputes, cannot issue corrections, cannot migrate a derivative, or cannot preserve required warnings.

A refusal is not failure if it is stated clearly. Hidden refusal is failure. An honest packet can say: this derivative is historical only; this teaching note is not maintained; this fork is experimental; this citation is orientation-only; this source-bound example has no refresh guarantee; this result is not suitable for operational reliance.

## Relation to public reliance governance

`158` grants or refuses public reliance. `159` assigns duties around that grant or refusal.

The default relation is:

- U0 usually pairs with O0 or O1 only.
- U1 usually requires O1-O2.
- U2 usually requires O1-O3, plus clear historical status.
- U3 usually requires O2-O3.
- U4 usually requires O3 and often O4.
- U5 usually requires O3-O4 and sometimes O6 for reusable teaching artifacts.
- U6 usually requires O4-O6 and source-specific duties.
- U7 usually requires O4-O8.
- U8 requires O6-O9 depending on the public reliance claim.
- U9 means no public reliance and usually requires O0 plus warning or quarantine duties if the item has already circulated.

These pairings are defaults, not automatic upgrades. Public-use permission should not be strengthened merely because a steward is willing. Stewardship makes permitted use responsible; it does not enlarge the underlying warrant.

## Stewardship anti-patterns

### Obligation laundering

A public-use permission is cited as if someone else has accepted all update, correction, source-watch, and dispute duties. Fix: name the steward and obligation class.

### Authority outsourcing

A maintainer, teacher, reviewer, or fork maintainer points to another role as if that role accepted responsibility. Fix: require delegation or handoff packet.

### Silent high-stakes upgrade

A conceptual distinction is used in law, medicine, engineering, policy, or institutional decision-making without domain review. Fix: downgrade public use or require O8.

### Warning attrition

Required warnings gradually disappear across summaries, slides, generated outputs, or citations. Fix: classify as O2 failure and repair the derivative.

### Orphaned derivative

A derivative remains public after the steward stops maintaining it. Fix: add historical-only warning, transfer stewardship, or withdraw the derivative.

### Fork prestige borrowing

A fork keeps archive names and file numbers while dropping lineage, review, public-use, and withdrawal controls. Fix: require fork status and compatibility statement under `155` plus stewardship packet here.

### Review-to-reliance slippage

A review packet is cited as if it creates public reliance and stewardship duties. Fix: separate `157`, `158`, and `159` fields.

### Correction without notice

A package is corrected but public derivatives, teaching uses, citations, or forks are not notified when they claim currentness. Fix: O6/O7 action.

### Steward capture

A steward claims authority to block all criticism, appeal, fork, or public correction because they maintain a derivative. Fix: separate stewardship authority from doctrinal authority.

### Infinite duty inflation

Every public mention is treated as imposing unlimited support, correction, monitoring, and liability on the archive. Fix: state limited obligation class and declined duties.

## Stewardship worksheet

Before allowing a public or derivative use, answer:

1. What exact artifact, claim, packet, or derivative is being stewarded?
2. Which version and file does it come from?
3. What public-use status under `158` applies?
4. Which role accepts stewardship?
5. What authority does that role actually have?
6. What obligations are accepted?
7. What obligations are declined?
8. What warnings must travel?
9. What update or source-watch trigger applies?
10. What dispute route applies?
11. What correction or withdrawal action is required if the status changes?
12. Is this a high-stakes or domain-bound use?
13. Is delegation or handoff involved?
14. What accountability consequence follows if the duty fails?
15. When should the stewardship packet expire, refresh, or be migrated?

## Future expansion rule

Do not add another responsibility-governance file merely because more roles can be imagined. Add one only if actual archive use reveals a recurring duty failure not handled by stewardship packets, public-use statuses, review packets, provenance packets, lineage packets, release gates, reception packets, transmission packets, precedent packets, application dossiers, or source notes.

Likely concrete future infrastructure would be more useful than another abstract layer: a public stewardship ledger, maintained teaching-derivative registry, source-watch table, citation-template bundle, fork-maintainer declaration, public errata queue, or machine-readable duties file.

## Closing formulation

A mature archive should not only know what it says, how it was built, how it was reviewed, what the public may rely on, and how public reliance may be disputed or withdrawn. It should know **who must carry the limits forward**.

Stewardship governance is the archive's resistance to false authority by abandoned responsibility.
