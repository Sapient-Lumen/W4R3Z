# Metametaphysics, Substantiveness, Verbalism, and Worldly Difference

## Why this file exists

The archive now has explicit files on **being/existence/ontological commitment**, **ontological status**, **naturalness**, **reference**, **contrast classes**, **revision**, **equivalence**, and **regimes/scope conditions**. But it still lacked one direct file saying how it should treat claims like:

- this dispute is **merely verbal**,
- that package is only a **different language** or **framework**,
- these rivals are **not really disagreeing about reality**,
- this is only a dispute about **what counts as an object**,
- ordinary talk says one thing while metaphysics says another,
- or both sides are right because they are using **different ontological standards**.

Without a dedicated file here, the archive risks eight recurrent mistakes:

- treating every ontological disagreement as if it were automatically a disagreement about the world's furniture,
- treating every successful paraphrase as if it automatically dissolved the original ontology,
- treating ordinary-language permissibility as if it automatically fixed foundational ontology,
- treating quantifier variance or framework tolerance as if they automatically neutralized metaphysical seriousness,
- treating naturalness or joint-carving considerations as if they automatically settled every apparently verbal dispute,
- treating different **status verdicts** as if they were disagreements about bare existence,
- treating local coexistence of differently aimed descriptions as if it forced a permissive anything-goes pluralism,
- or treating stubborn disagreement as if the only options were global realism or global deflation.

This file gives the archive a compact **substantiveness profile** so future revisions can say whether a case is chiefly about:

- worldly difference,
- language or framework choice,
- category or counting policy,
- scope or regime,
- status rather than existence,
- target-splitting,
- or a mixed case.

## Working default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Many metaphysical disputes are substantive, but none should be treated as merely verbal until a real translation, preservation, and loss-profile is shown.**

And more specifically:

> **A diagnosis of “mere verbalism” is earned only when the archive can show that rival formulations preserve the same worldly, modal, explanatory, and naturalness-relevant content, with no remaining difference in target, status, scope, or dependence profile.**

This fits the archive's wider commitments:

- **ontological commitment** matters because not every existential idiom is innocent,
- **status** matters because disputes about fundamental vs derivative reality are not always disputes about whether anything exists,
- **naturalness** matters because some packages track more elite or explanatorily privileged structure than others,
- **reference** matters because two speakers may fail to disagree if their terms latch onto different targets,
- **contrast classes** matter because one package may answer a different question than its apparent rival,
- **revision** matters because later theory may preserve a target while changing category, scope, or status,
- and **ordinary language** matters because linguistic categories are evidence about how we talk and carve, but they do not automatically settle foundational metaphysics.

## Core distinctions

### 1. Worldly disagreement is not the same thing as verbal variance

The archive should distinguish:

- disagreement about **what there is**,
- disagreement about **what is fundamental**,
- disagreement about **how to count or classify one target**,
- disagreement about **status** rather than existence,
- disagreement about **scope or regime**,
- and disagreement that is largely **terminological or framework-relative**.

Otherwise it will keep treating every rival sentence-form as one flat metaphysical fight.

### 2. Paraphrase is not automatic elimination

The archive should distinguish:

- paraphrase that preserves the same target and explanatory work,
- paraphrase that changes **category** while preserving part of the work,
- paraphrase that preserves truth at the cost of modal or explanatory depth,
- paraphrase that changes the **status verdict**,
- and paraphrase that only works by silently dropping what made the original discourse important.

Otherwise it will keep mistaking a clever redescription for a full ontological discharge.

### 3. Ordinary-language ontology is not foundational ontology

The archive should distinguish:

- the categories implicit in ordinary language,
- the categories useful for theory construction,
- the categories best suited for final ontological verdicts,
- and cases where these diverge but remain connected.

Otherwise it will keep sliding from how we ordinarily speak of objects, events, holes, persons, institutions, or absences to the claim that metaphysics is already settled by grammar.

### 4. Quantifier or framework tolerance is not automatic anti-realism

The archive should distinguish:

- harmless variation in notation or regimenting language,
- permissive alternative ways of counting one domain,
- genuine framework choice with preserved target-contact,
- and cases where one package has a better **naturalness**, **status**, or **dependence** profile than its rival.

Otherwise it will keep treating every framework-sensitive dispute as if no worldly issue remained.

### 5. Status disputes are not always existence disputes

The archive should distinguish:

- “X exists” from “X is fundamental”,
- “X exists” from “X is derivative but robust”,
- “X is real enough for explanation” from “X is basic furniture”,
- and “X is only representationally useful” from “X is unreal in every sense”.

Otherwise it will keep turning many archive disagreements into fake yes/no questions about bare existence.

### 6. Coexistence is not the same thing as permissive pluralism

The archive should distinguish:

- two packages that are genuinely compatible because they answer different questions,
- two packages that are compatible only after **scope restriction**,
- two packages that share a target but disagree about **status**,
- and two packages that cannot both be right because they differ about worldly dependence or privileged carving.

Otherwise it will keep confusing layered articulation with indiscriminate tolerance.

### 7. Underdetermination is not the same thing as verbal equivalence

The archive should distinguish:

- rival packages that currently fit the evidence equally well,
- packages related by exact reformulation,
- packages related by partial translation but different metaphysical loading,
- and packages that simply leave the key issue unsettled.

Otherwise it will keep collapsing epistemic stalemate into semantic harmlessness.

## Four live options

### 1. Strong deflationism

**Picture:** many traditional metaphysical disputes are empty, framework-bound, or merely verbal.

**What it gets right:**
- grammar and regimenting language can mislead,
- and some apparent disputes really do evaporate under translation or paraphrase.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it too quickly neutralizes naturalness, dependence, status, and truthmaker pressure,
- and it risks treating real ontological difference as language-choice.

### 2. Unrestricted heavyweight realism

**Picture:** every persistent metaphysical disagreement is a disagreement about reality itself.

**What it gets right:**
- many metaphysical disputes are not merely verbal,
- and language should not be allowed to swallow ontology.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it overstates the significance of category shifts, paraphrases, and scope-sensitive rearticulations,
- and it risks turning bookkeeping differences into deep metaphysical conflicts.

### 3. Framework pluralism

**Picture:** multiple frameworks may be equally acceptable for describing one domain, with no uniquely privileged ontology always required.

**What it gets right:**
- some domains admit more than one successful carving,
- and local coexistence is sometimes better than winner-take-all conquest.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it can underdescribe when one framework tracks more elite structure,
- and it can become too relaxed about explanatory loss, dependence differences, and status demotion.

### 4. Typed substantiveness realism

**Picture:** some disputes are genuinely substantive, some partly verbal, some scope- or status-relative, and some framework-plural. The task is not to choose one global slogan, but to diagnose which kind of disagreement is actually in front of us.

**What it gets right:**
- it preserves metaphysical seriousness without assuming that all disagreement is equally deep,
- it preserves sensitivity to language and frameworks without flattening reality into talk,
- and it makes coexistence, rivalry, demotion, and suspension all live but disciplined options.

**What it risks if left alone:**
- it can become evasive unless each case states exactly what remains substantive after translation, paraphrase, and category repair.

## Archive commitments

### 1. Earn “merely verbal” diagnoses

Do not call a dispute merely verbal unless the archive can show a translation or redescription that preserves the key worldly, modal, explanatory, and naturalness-relevant work.

### 2. Diagnose the disagreement type before issuing a verdict

Ask whether the disagreement is chiefly about:

- target,
- category,
- counting policy,
- dependence profile,
- status,
- scope,
- framework,
- or language only.

### 3. Let ordinary language provide evidence, not final authority

Ordinary-language ontology can reveal stable categorial habits and pressure points. It does not by itself determine what is fundamental, derivative, merely representational, or best treated as an effective-regime posit.

### 4. Treat status disagreements as real disagreements

A dispute over whether something is **fundamental**, **derivative but robust**, **thinly dependent**, or **merely representational** can be metaphysically substantive even when both sides agree that the discourse is true in some ordinary sense.

### 5. Let naturalness and explanatory loss matter

When two formulations are extensionally or inferentially close, ask whether one better captures elite structure, explanatory proportion, counterfactual depth, or cross-domain stability.

### 6. Allow local coexistence without global permissivism

Two packages may both survive if they are typed to different domains, scales, regimes, or explanatory aims. But coexistence should be argued, not assumed.

### 7. Do not let underdetermination masquerade as verbal peace

A case can remain unresolved because the archive lacks enough grip, not because the rivals are saying the same thing.

### 8. Use meta-level diagnosis to compress, not to evade

The point of metametaphysical discipline is to reduce fake disagreement and preserve real disagreement, not to turn every hard case into a diplomatic shrug.

## Substantiveness profile for future revisions

Before inferring a metaphysical verdict from language like **merely verbal**, **just a framework**, **only a different way of speaking**, **same ontology really**, **nothing deep is at stake**, **this is only about counting**, **ordinary language says**, **quantifier variance**, **conceptual scheme**, **metaphysically lightweight**, **same world under another description**, or **only a status difference**, future revisions should answer at least these questions:

1. **target domain** — what domain or candidate is actually at issue?
2. **rival packages** — what do the apparently rival views really say?
3. **translation relation** — can one package be translated into the other, and at what cost?
4. **preserved content** — what worldly, modal, explanatory, inferential, and naturalness-relevant content is preserved under translation?
5. **remaining difference** — what still differs: target, category, counting, scope, status, dependence, or nothing substantial?
6. **ordinary-language role** — is the dispute driven by natural language, formal regimentation, scientific model choice, or foundational ontology?
7. **comparison cost** — what explanatory, modal, or ontological work is lost if one package is paraphrased away?
8. **final verdict** — substantive rivalry, status dispute, scope-relative coexistence, framework-relative reformulation, largely verbal variance, or still unsettled?

## Compression rule for future revisions

Future revisions should preserve a standing discipline:

> **Do not call a dispute “merely verbal” until you can say what survives translation, what is lost, and why no worldly, modal, explanatory, or naturalness-relevant difference remains.**

That rule is intentionally stricter than ordinary conversational dismissal. It keeps the archive non-skeptical without making it gullible about every inherited ontology fight.
