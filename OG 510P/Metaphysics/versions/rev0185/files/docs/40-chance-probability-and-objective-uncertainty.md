# Chance, Probability, and Objective Uncertainty

## Why this file exists

The archive already had files on laws, powers, causation, modality, levels, representation, symmetry, and explanatory grain. But it still lacked a direct rule for **chance**, **probability**, and **objective uncertainty**.

Without a dedicated file here, the archive risks four opposite mistakes:

- treating every probability claim as merely subjective credence or ignorance,
- treating every useful stochastic model as evidence for irreducible metaphysical indeterminism,
- treating observed frequencies as if they were automatically identical to chances,
- or treating all objective probabilities as one flat ontological kind.

This file gives the archive a compact **chance profile** so future revisions can say what kind of probabilistic case is at issue, what process or setup carries it, what relation it has to possibility and frequency, and whether any metaphysical chance is really being proposed.

## Working default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Some probabilities are objective and explanatorily serious, but not all probability-talk is one ontological kind, and not every objective probability is a basic chance.**

This fits the archive's wider commitments:

- **form** still matters, because a chance often belongs to a typed process or setup rather than to a bare outcome-description,
- **process** still matters, because chanciness concerns how outcomes may unfold from an organized trial or evolving situation,
- **power** still matters, because chances often concern what a thing or system can do, undergo, or transition into,
- **structure** still matters, because symmetries, measures, ensembles, and probabilistic laws may constrain outcome-space,
- and **grounding** still matters, because credence, frequency, model-fit, and objective chance should not be collapsed.

## Core distinctions

### 1. Probability is plural

The archive should distinguish at least:

- **credence / degree of belief** — subjective confidence,
- **evidential or logical probability** — support given evidence,
- **frequency** — actual or limiting outcome ratios in repeatable trials,
- **objective chance** — mind-independent probabilistic structure attached to a process, setup, or law,
- **model parameter probability** — a probability used inside a model, simulation, idealization, or inference pipeline,
- **risk profile** — probability combined with stakes, cost, vulnerability, or decision relevance.

The archive should not let “probability” function as a one-word answer to several metaphysical questions at once.

### 2. Chance is process-typed, not outcome-floating

A chance claim should usually be read as belonging to a **trial**, **process**, **mechanism**, or **setup**, not to a bare outcome considered in isolation. A coin's landing heads by chance is different from its merely being placed heads-up even if the outcome-description is the same.

The archive should therefore prefer process-typed formulations such as:

- the chance of this decay,
- the chance of this toss under these conditions,
- the chance of this transition given this state,
- or the chance-profile of this evolving setup.

This keeps chance aligned with powers, laws, and process rather than with detached outcome labels.

### 3. Chance relates to possibility and frequency, but is neither identical to either

A positive chance should normally imply some relevant modal openness of the outcome-profile. But possibility alone is too thin to give a numerical chance. Likewise, frequencies are often our best evidence for chances, yet a fair process can produce misleading short-run frequencies, and some genuine single-case chances need not come from many actual repetitions.

The archive should therefore keep three claims apart:

- **possible**,
- **frequent**,
- **chancy**.

### 4. Objective probability need not always be basic chance

Some objective probabilities may be treated as:

- **irreducible stochastic transition-chances**,
- **propensities or dispositions of setups**,
- **best-system statistical laws**,
- **ensemble or measure-theoretic structure over possible states**,
- **coarse-grained or scale-relative probabilities in deterministic systems**,
- or **effective higher-level probability profiles** that are objective enough for science without being fundamental.

The archive should not assume in advance that only one of these is legitimate in every domain.

### 5. Determinism and chance must be typed, not sloganized

The archive should not simply say that determinism excludes chance or that probability proves indeterminism. That depends on what kind of probability is at issue and what view of laws is doing the work.

Some worlds may contain:

- irreducibly probabilistic laws,
- deterministic microdynamics plus robust statistical structure,
- deterministic laws plus objective higher-level probabilities,
- or explanatory practice that uses probabilities mainly as epistemic or model-relative tools.

So the archive should treat **chance vs determinism** as a typed architectural question, not a slogan duel.

### 6. Credence should answer to chance where genuine chance is known

Where a genuine objective chance is known, rational credence should normally defer to it absent defeating information. But credence and chance are not identical.

The archive should therefore distinguish:

- **what the world's chance-profile is**,
- **what an agent reasonably believes about that profile**,
- and **what evidence is admissible or inadmissible** relative to the outcome.

This matters because the archive already distinguishes worldly structure from representational and epistemic states elsewhere.

## Archive default

The current archive default is:

1. **Be realist but typed about probability.** Some probabilities are objective and explanatory, but probability-talk is not one ontological kind.
2. **Do not collapse credence, evidence, frequency, and chance.** These are linked, but not identical.
3. **Allow single-case chance where process and law earn it.** Repeatability helps evidence, but is not the only metaphysical template.
4. **Allow effective and higher-level objective probabilities.** Not every objective probability must be fundamental to be serious.
5. **Do not infer irreducible chanciness from every stochastic model.** Some probabilistic descriptions are coarse-grained, epistemic, best-systemic, or merely instrumental.
6. **Do not infer mere ignorance from every probability claim either.** Some probabilities track real modal structure, transition propensity, or lawlike statistical order.
7. **Keep chance connected to process.** Ask what setup, mechanism, or law-profile grounds the chance before escalating metaphysics.

## Chance-profile rule for future revisions

Before inferring a metaphysical verdict from language like **chance**, **probability**, **risk**, **random**, **stochastic**, **likely**, **uncertain**, **distribution**, **measure**, **ensemble**, **credence**, or **propensity**, specify:

1. **target type** — event, outcome, transition, process, law, ensemble, population, model, institution, agent-choice, or mixed case;
2. **probability type** — credence, evidential probability, frequency, objective chance, propensity, best-system statistical law, effective higher-level probability, or model parameter;
3. **time and reference profile** — single-case, repeat-trial, long-run, time-indexed chance, stage-relative chance, population-level distribution, or mixed profile;
4. **grounding basis** — law, power, symmetry, measure, ensemble, coarse-graining, ignorance, inference procedure, institutional rule, or mixed basis;
5. **modal relation** — genuine openness, deterministic evolution with probabilistic description, underdetermination by evidence, or still unsettled;
6. **evidence relation** — observed frequencies, calibration, measurement, simulation, Bayesian update, intervention, or none yet;
7. **reality verdict** — fundamental chance, derivative but robust objective probability, practice-relative but objective statistical profile, merely epistemic probability, merely instrumental model parameter, or still unsettled.

Otherwise the archive will keep sliding from uncertainty to ignorance, from useful models to ontology, and from stochastic language to brute metaphysical indeterminism.

## Stress cases

### Radioactive decay and quantum transition cases

These are paradigm pressure points because they make single-case chance vivid. But the archive should still ask whether the probability is being treated as an irreducible transition-chance, a collapse-specific rule, a statistical summary of hidden dynamics, or an effective predictive profile.

### Statistical mechanics and macro-regularities

Here the archive should ask whether the probabilities are fundamental, measure-theoretic, best-systemic, coarse-grained, or tied to typicality assumptions. Deterministic microdynamics does not by itself settle the higher-level probability question.

### Chaotic systems and prediction-limited determinism

Where systems are highly sensitive to initial conditions, probability language may reflect finite-agent limitation, coarse-graining, or stable ensemble structure rather than irreducible metaphysical chance. The archive should not treat unpredictability as automatically chancy.

### Social, biological, and institutional rates

Population-level transition probabilities, behavioral propensities, epidemiological rates, and institutional risk profiles may be objective and indispensable even when they are not fundamental chances. The archive should ask whether the relevant profile is causal, statistical, normative, or mixed.

## What this file does **not** say

It does **not** say:

- that every probability is subjective,
- that every probability is a fundamental chance,
- that long-run frequencies are identical to chances,
- that determinism and objective probability are automatically incompatible,
- or that every uncertainty estimate reveals metaphysical openness in reality itself.

The point is only that **chance and probability need a typed metaphysical profile rather than a flat choice between “mere ignorance” and “fundamental indeterminism everywhere.”**
