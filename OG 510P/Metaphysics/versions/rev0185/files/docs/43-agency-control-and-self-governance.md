# Agency, Control, and Self-Governance

## Why this file exists

The archive already had files on causation, teleology, potentiality, chance, social ontology, representation, and indexicality. But it still lacked a direct rule for **agency**, **control**, and **self-governance**.

Without a dedicated file here, the archive risks six opposite mistakes:

- treating every intentional or purposive episode as if it were just another event in a causal chain,
- treating every reason-explanation as if it were automatically an ontologically complete account of agency,
- treating every control claim as if it required maximal metaphysical freedom,
- treating every agent as if it had to be either an irreducible unmoved source or a mere passenger of underlying processes,
- treating agency as if it were a purely inward private phenomenon rather than often embodied, scaffolded, role-mediated, or institutionally organized,
- or treating chance, spontaneity, and indeterminism as if they by themselves generated self-governance.

This file gives the archive a compact **agency profile** so future revisions can say whether a case is chiefly about intentional action, initiation, guidance by reasons, control, policy-level organization, delegated or distributed agency, or the stronger question of freedom.

## Working default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Some organized beings are real agents when what happens is not only causally produced but also guided by the being's own organized powers, reasons-responsive states, policies, intentions, or standing modes of self-regulation.**

This fits the archive's wider commitments:

- **form** still matters, because agency depends on organized capacities rather than bare motion,
- **process** still matters, because acting, deciding, inhibiting, correcting, and sustaining are temporal unfoldings,
- **power** still matters, because agency involves capacities for initiation, control, adjustment, and restraint,
- **structure** still matters, because agential order often includes plans, roles, representations, bodily channels, and institutional scaffolds,
- and **grounding** still matters, because agential reality need not float free of lower-level processes in order to be real.

## Core distinctions

### 1. Behavior, action, agency, and control are not the same

The archive should distinguish:

- **mere behavior** — what a system does,
- **intentional action** — behavior guided under some description by aims, reasons, or policies,
- **agency** — the organized capacity to act, guide, inhibit, revise, or initiate in ways attributable to the agent,
- **control** — the degree to which the resulting course is governed by the agent's own organized capacities rather than by mere accident, compulsion, or external override,
- **self-governance** — the stronger case in which the agent's action is integrated with its standing policies, commitments, or endorsed motivational order.

Without this distinction, the archive will keep sliding from motion to action, from action to agency, and from agency to freedom as if one word settled all three.

### 2. Reasons-explanation and causal production are connected but not identical

The archive should keep distinct:

- **reason-giving explanation** — what made the action intelligible from the agent's point of view,
- **causal production** — what brought the event about,
- **control structure** — what kept the process on the agent's track rather than on a deviant path,
- **normative assessment** — whether the action was justified, rational, competent, or blameworthy.

A reason can illuminate what the agent was doing without by itself settling whether the action was under genuine control, and causal production can occur without yet amounting to acting for a reason in the right way.

### 3. Agency should not be reduced too quickly to spectator causation

The archive should keep open at least three live pictures:

- **event-causal agency** — action is produced in the right way by agent-involving states and events,
- **agent-causal agency** — the agent as persisting individual exercises an irreducible initiating or settling power,
- **organized-control views** — agency consists in the integrated guidance, monitoring, inhibition, and policy-sensitive regulation of behavior even if no special extra ingredient beyond organized powers is added.

The current archive default should not force an early verdict among these. But it should reject the flat view that once lower-level causal history is given, the agential question is automatically exhausted.

### 4. Self-governance is stronger than mere intentionality

The archive should distinguish:

- **intentional action**,
- **skilled controlled action**,
- **self-controlled action**,
- **wholehearted or policy-integrated action**,
- **free action** in the stronger sense tied to alternative possibilities or self-determination.

A being can act intentionally without being fully self-governing. It can be pushed by a motive, divided within itself, manipulated, or operating under narrow compulsion while still acting under some intentional description.

### 5. Agency can be embodied, scaffolded, delegated, or distributed

The archive should not assume that all genuine agency is sealed inside one punctual inner source. Some agency is:

- **embodied** — depending on bodily skill, posture, timing, and sensorimotor organization,
- **scaffolded** — relying on tools, interfaces, prompts, memory aids, records, or procedural supports,
- **role-mediated** — exercised through offices, permissions, and institutional position,
- **distributed** — spread across coordinated persons, subsystems, or socio-technical arrangements.

The archive should therefore ask what exactly is doing the controlling work, rather than assuming either a simple inner homunculus or a purely external mechanism.

### 6. Chance and spontaneity do not by themselves generate agency

Indeterminism, spontaneity, or stochasticity can matter to agency, but they do not automatically create control. The archive should distinguish:

- **mere unpredictability**,
- **objective chance within a process**,
- **open alternatives under the agent's guidance**,
- **self-determining control**,
- **luck-sensitive deviations**.

Otherwise the archive will keep mistaking looseness in the causal order for freedom, or deterministic organization for automatic loss of agency.

### 7. Agency is compatible with dependence and levels

The archive should now say more clearly that agency can be **real without being fundamental**. Agents may be organisms, persons, institutions, or organized systems whose reality depends on lower-level processes while still exerting distinctive explanatory, modal, and normative pressure.

So the archive should:

- allow **higher-level agential reality** where control profiles are real,
- resist both **flat reduction** and **mystified exemption**,
- distinguish **causal dependence** from **agential disappearance**,
- and treat agency as a domain where level, grain, centeredness, teleology, and social structure often interact.

## Archive default

The current archive default is:

1. **Be realist but typed about agency.** Some beings are genuinely agents, not just event-sites, when organized control, guidance, and answerability to reasons or policies are part of what explains what happens.
2. **Do not identify agency with mere behavior.** Movement, output, reaction, and even purposive-looking success are not enough by themselves.
3. **Do not identify agency with maximal freedom.** A domain may contain genuine agency even if stronger questions about alternative possibilities or irreducible self-determination remain open.
4. **Treat control as graded and structured.** Guidance, inhibition, monitoring, repair, responsiveness, and policy-integration can vary across cases.
5. **Allow mixed agential architectures.** Some cases are more bodily, some more representational, some more institutional, and some more distributed.
6. **Keep luck distinct from freedom.** Chance may loosen determination without yet yielding self-governance.
7. **Keep agency distinct from mere causal description.** The question is not just whether the action had causes, but whether the causes were organized in the right way as the agent's own guidance structure.

## Agency-profile rule for future revisions

Before inferring a metaphysical verdict from language like **acts**, **chooses**, **decides**, **controls**, **guides**, **initiates**, **self-governs**, **autonomous**, **voluntary**, **under one's own steam**, **institutional agent**, **collective actor**, or **automated decision**, specify:

1. **target type** — organism, person, subsystem, office-holder, institution, collective, interface-mediated arrangement, or mixed case;
2. **activity type** — mere behavior, intentional action, skilled performance, deliberative choice, omission, inhibition, policy execution, role-performance, or mixed case;
3. **guidance basis** — reflex structure, learned skill, belief-desire-intention structure, standing policy, norm-governed role, algorithmic procedure, social coordination, or mixed basis;
4. **control profile** — minimal, graded, robust, fractured, externally overridden, distributed, delegated, or still unsettled;
5. **causal profile** — event-causal, agent-causal, volitional, organizationally controlled, stochastic, deterministic, or mixed;
6. **freedom load** — none, weak voluntariness only, strong self-control, alternative-possibility demanding, self-determination demanding, or still unsettled;
7. **reality verdict** — genuine agent-level reality, derivative but robust agential organization, merely as-if control, or still unsettled.

Otherwise the archive will keep sliding from reasons to causes, from causes to control, and from control to freedom as if a single description settled them all.

## Stress cases

### Habit, reflex, and skill

The archive should ask whether the case is mere automaticity, learned embodied control, action under an intention, or a hybrid profile in which agency is real but thinly reflective.

### Compulsion, fragmentation, and manipulation

The archive should ask whether the agent acts, is partly bypassed, is divided, or is only a channel for another structure's control.

### Delegated and institutional agency

The archive should ask whether the relevant agent is a person, an office-holder, a team, an institution, or a socio-technical arrangement whose agency is role-bound and record-mediated.

### Automation and interface-mediated action

The archive should ask whether a system's apparent agency is only programmed output, genuinely controlled adaptive behavior, delegated human agency, or some mixed control profile.

### Determinism, chance, and self-determination

The archive should ask whether the dispute is really about causal closure, about single-case openness, about reasons-responsiveness, about sourcehood, or about a stronger metaphysics of the will.

## What this file does **not** say

It does **not** say:

- that every agent must be fundamentally irreducible,
- that every intentional action requires libertarian freedom,
- that reasons explanation replaces causal explanation,
- that distributed or scaffolded agency is unreal,
- that agency is only inward mental privacy,
- or that mere randomness can substitute for control.

The point is more disciplined and more compact: the archive should now treat **agency** as a typed mode of organized control and guidance that can be real without being fundamental, stronger than mere behavior but weaker than some maximal pictures of freedom, and often dependent on embodiment, form, history, roles, and scaffolding.
