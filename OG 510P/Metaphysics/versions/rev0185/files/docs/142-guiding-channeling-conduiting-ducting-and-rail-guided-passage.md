# Guiding, Channeling, Conduiting, Ducting, and Rail-Guided Passage

## Why this file exists

The archive now has explicit files on **circulation / throughput / recirculation / organized throughflow**, **rerouting / diversion / bypass / shunting**, **hosting / lodging / carriage / guest occupancy**, **attachment / anchoring / tethering / mooring / docking**, **embedding / insertion / implantation / insetting**, **enclosure / encasement / casing / housing / encapsulation**, and **support / bearing / suspension / hanging / bracing / load-path carriage**, but it still lacks one direct file saying what to do when a case is described as **guided through**, **run along**, **channeled within**, **ducted through**, **routed in a raceway**, **sleeved through**, **fed along rails**, **slid along guides**, or **constrained to a path**.

That omission matters because the archive still risks nine opposite mistakes:

- treating every real **guided-through** case as if it were already ordinary **hosting** or **containment**,
- treating every **channeled** case as if it were only generic **circulation** rather than path-constrained passage,
- treating every **ducted** case as if the duct were automatically identical with the guided flow,
- treating every **raceway** or **conduit** case as if the contents were parts of the guiding structure in the same sense as welded components,
- treating every **rail-guided** case as if it were already mere support or mere contact,
- treating every **same channel** as if it meant **same guided target**,
- treating every **same guided target** as if it meant **same guide, same route, or same corridor**,
- treating every **routing support** case as if the explanatory center were load-bearing support rather than constrained passage,
- or smuggling the differences among **guiding**, **channeling**, **conduiting**, **ducting**, **sleeving**, and **rail-guided passage** into neighboring files without typing them.

This file gives the archive a compact **guiding / channeling / conduiting / ducting / rail-guided-passage profile** so future revisions can say whether a case is chiefly about directed passage, constrained transit, protected routing, guided sliding, airflow corridor formation, or stronger incorporation only in harder cases.

## Compressed picture

> **One thing can genuinely guide another without thereby becoming numerically identical with it.**

And more carefully:

> **Guiding, channeling, conduiting, ducting, sleeving, and rail-guided passage are typed ways in which one target constrains, directs, organizes, protects, or regularizes the path of another without that alone settling identity, parthood, hosting, embedding, or fusion.**

## Why the default view should absorb this operator-family

The archive's working view already says:

- **process** matters, because guidance relations are maintained, obstructed, redirected, chafed, fouled, widened, narrowed, and repaired across time,
- **powers and functions** matter, because channels direct movement, ducts shape airflow, rails constrain rotation, conduits protect passage, and sleeves regularize path,
- **structure** matters, because the same target can move through ducts, pipes, guide rails, raceways, sleeves, trays, grooves, or temporary corridors,
- **dependence pluralism** matters, because guidance should not be collapsed into hosting, support, embedding, attachment, or constitution,
- **boundary discipline** matters, because one thing can be inside or along a guide while remaining distinct from the guide,
- **failure-mode discipline** matters, because jamming, chafing, misrouting, rotation, snagging, leakage, and corridor collapse reveal real guidance architecture,
- and **artifact-risk discipline** matters, because language like *in the duct*, *in the raceway*, *on the rails*, or *through the channel* often overstates metaphysical intimacy.

So the default view should absorb guidance-language without flattening it.

## What this operator-family is for

This operator-family is for cases where one target is **directed through, along, or within another**.

The relation may involve:

- **wires, cables, or busbars run within a raceway or conduit**, 
- **airflow established by ducts, grilles, exhaust geometry, or temporary directional-airflow corridors**,
- **a vehicle, module, or payload sliding along rails or guides that constrain its path**,
- **a catheter, tube, or sleeve guiding something through a passage**,
- **a moving member routed through trays, ducts, or conduits to avoid interference or damage**,
- or **one larger organization continuing while the guide, rail, or corridor changes**.

The key question is not simply whether one item is located in another, but **whether the explanatory center is constrained path, organized passage, and direction-governed movement rather than residence, load-bearing support, regulated throughput, bond-making, or identity**.

## Why the archive needs it

### 1. Guidance is not the same as hosting

A thing can be guided through another without being hosted by it in the thicker guest-or-tenant sense.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **guided through or along**,
- **hosted in or on**,
- **temporarily enclosed within**,
- **supported by**,
- and **stronger incorporation only in harder cases**.

Otherwise it will keep flattening guided passage into residence.

### 2. Guidance is not the same as support

A rail, conduit, sleeve, or raceway can constrain path without the main explanatory burden being load-bearing support.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **load-bearing support**,
- **path-constraining guidance**,
- **friction-limited holding**,
- **ordinary attachment**,
- and **same guide only where separately earned**.

Otherwise it will keep mistaking constrained passage for suspension or bearing.

### 3. Guidance is not the same as embedding or enclosure

Something can move within a channel or guide corridor without being implanted, encapsulated, or made constitutively one with the surrounding structure.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **guided passage through a corridor**,
- **persistent embedding in a substrate**,
- **protective enclosure**,
- **sealed containment**,
- and **same guided relation only where separately earned**.

Otherwise it will keep treating all inside-relations as one flat ontology.

### 4. Same guide does not mean same guided target

One rail, raceway, conduit, or corridor can successively guide different targets across time.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **same raceway under changed wiring**,
- **same rail guide under changed payload**,
- **same duct under changed airflow episode**,
- **same route standard under changed tokens**,
- and **same guide-target pair only where that relation itself is the target**.

Otherwise it will keep letting route continuity masquerade as bearer continuity.

### 5. Same guided target does not mean same route or guide

A continuing target can remain the same while moving through different channels, ducts, conduits, or guide rails at different times.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **same target under changed route**,
- **same cable under re-routing through a new raceway**,
- **same airflow objective under changed ductwork**,
- **same CubeSat standard under changed dispenser guide geometry**,
- and **same guide architecture only when separately earned**.

Otherwise it will keep letting route change masquerade as bearer change.

### 6. Guidance can be passive, active, temporary, or release-governed

A guidance relation may be passive as in a raceway, active as in forced directional airflow, temporary as in a deployment guide, or only relevant during one phase of movement.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **static corridor guidance**,
- **actively generated directional guidance**,
- **deployment-phase rail guidance**,
- **protective routing without active forcing**,
- and **mixed architectures**.

Otherwise it will keep treating guidance as one flat state.

## Typology of guidance-like cases

### A. Raceway and conduit cases

The key feature is that a path-bearing structure is designed expressly to hold and route another target.

**Examples:** electrical raceways, conduits, sleeves, protected runs for wires or busbars.

### B. Ducting and directional-flow cases

The key feature is that airflow or fluid movement is organized by geometry, pressure, exhaust placement, or attached ductwork.

**Examples:** clean-to-less-clean directional airflow, exhaust ducts, temporary ducts, in-room HEPA ductwork.

### C. Rail-guided sliding cases

The key feature is that motion is constrained by rails, tracks, grooves, or guides that regularize path and prevent unwanted rotation or departure.

**Examples:** CubeSat rails in dispensers, drawer or carriage rails, guided deployment systems.

### D. Routed-and-supported bundle cases

The key feature is that passage is constrained and protected through clamps, troughs, ducts, conduits, or other routing architecture.

**Examples:** wire bundles routed to prevent chafing or jamming; cables guided through protective channels.

### E. Sleeve and passage-lining cases

The key feature is that one structure creates a constrained corridor through which another target passes or moves.

**Examples:** sleeves, liners, guide tubes, protective feed-throughs.

### F. Continuity theater through route labels

The key feature is that language of *same channel*, *same line*, *same route*, *same guide*, or *same corridor* hides what is really route reuse, target replacement, or changed guide architecture.

**Examples:** “same line” talk that tracks only one tray or conduit after cable swaps; “same deployment path” talk that hides changed guide tolerances; “same airflow route” talk that hides changed active exhaust architecture.

## Cross-domain pressure

### Raceways and expressly designed channels

Electrical-routing cases pressure the archive because a raceway is explicitly defined as a channel for holding and routing wires, cables, or busbars without those contents thereby becoming numerically identical with the raceway.

### Directional airflow and ductwork-attached corridors

Ventilation cases pressure the archive because a desired airflow direction can be established by supply/exhaust placement, pressure differential, or ductwork attached to filtration units without making the air, the duct, and the room one undifferentiated bearer.

### Rail-guided deployment systems

Spaceflight cases pressure the archive because rails and guides can prevent rotation and constrain deployment path while the guided payload remains distinct from the guide architecture.

### Routed bundles and anti-chafing design

Aircraft wiring cases pressure the archive because the same wires can remain the same wires while routing, support intervals, ducts, troughs, or conduits change, and because the explanatory center is often protected passage rather than mere support.

## False moves to avoid

- Do **not** infer identity from every case of being **guided through** or **run within**.
- Do **not** infer hosting from every case of being **in a conduit**, **in a raceway**, or **in a duct**.
- Do **not** infer embedding from every case of moving **through a sleeve** or **along rails**.
- Do **not** infer support from every case of being **on guides** or **routed and supported**.
- Do **not** infer same guided target from same guide corridor.
- Do **not** infer same guide from same guided target.
- Do **not** infer fusion from constrained path intimacy.

## Default diagnostic questions

When a case is described as **guided through**, **channeled within**, **ducted through**, **routed in**, **sleeved through**, **fed along rails**, **slid along guides**, or **constrained to a path**, ask:

1. What exactly is the **target** — one wire, cable, flow, payload, module, vehicle, body, beam of traffic, or mixed organization?
2. Is the case chiefly about **raceway routing**, **conduit passage**, **ducted flow**, **rail-guided sliding**, **sleeved passage**, **temporary guide corridor**, or a mixed architecture?
3. What exactly is the **guiding basis** — one channel, conduit, raceway, duct, rail, guide slot, sleeve, tray, groove, or pressure-shaped corridor?
4. What is doing the explanatory work — **path constraint**, **rotation prevention**, **protected passage**, **flow-direction shaping**, **anti-chafing routing**, **release-governed sliding**, or mixed basis?
5. Does the guide remain the same under changing guided targets, does the guided target remain the same under changed guides, or is the case really about one changing **guide pair**?
6. What is the relevant **grain** — one local guide point, one duct run, one raceway segment, one corridor, one rail system, or mixed levels?
7. What is the relevant **comparison class** — hosting, support, valving/throttling, enclosure, embedding, rerouting, or mere location?
8. What is the relevant **artifact-risk profile** — slot-tracking confusion, route-reuse inflation, hidden guide change, changed tolerance disguised as continuity, or mixed?
9. Is the guidance **passive**, **actively generated**, **phase-limited**, **release-governed**, or **permanently maintained**?
10. Whether the best verdict is **typed channel guidance**, **typed conduit passage**, **typed ducted directional flow**, **typed rail-guided passage**, **typed sleeved transit**, **stronger incorporation only in harder cases**, or **still unsettled**.

## Compression rule

Future revisions should normally treat **guiding / channeling / conduiting / ducting / rail-guided passage** as one operator-family whenever the explanatory burden is: **one target directed through, along, or within another under a typed path-constraining relation; unresolved question whether the relation is hosting, support, valving/throttling, enclosure, embedding, rerouting, or fusion; and the need to distinguish continuing guide architecture from continuing bearer identity rather than letting guided-passage language settle the ontology by itself.**

## Anchor references

- Occupational Safety and Health Administration, **29 CFR 1910.399** — defines a **raceway** as an enclosed channel of metal or nonmetallic materials designed expressly for holding wires, cables, or busbars, useful for distinguishing typed guidance channels from generic hosting or enclosure.  
  https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.399

- Centers for Disease Control and Prevention / NIOSH, **Ventilation FAQs** — explains that **directional airflow** is a protective ventilation concept where air movement flows in a clean-to-less-clean direction and that it can be created by supply/exhaust placement, pressure differences, or ductwork attached to in-room HEPA filtration units, useful for distinguishing ducted or corridor-shaped guidance from generic containment.  
  https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/ventilation/faq/index.html

- National Aeronautics and Space Administration, **State-of-the-Art of Small Spacecraft Technology, Integration, Launch, and Deployment** — explains that in a rail-type CubeSat dispenser the rails prevent rotation inside the dispenser and then slide along guides during deployment, useful for distinguishing rail-guided passage from generic support or attachment.  
  https://www.nasa.gov/smallsat-institute/sst-soa/integration-launch-and-deployment/

- Federal Aviation Administration, **AC 25.1701-1** — states that wires should be supported by suitable clamps or other devices at suitable intervals and notes abrasion and chafing risks for wires contained in troughs, ducts, or conduits, useful for distinguishing routed-and-protected passage from mere co-location or support-only language.  
  https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_25_1701-1.pdf
