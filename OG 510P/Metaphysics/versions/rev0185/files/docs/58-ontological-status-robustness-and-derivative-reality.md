# Ontological Status, Robustness, and Derivative Reality

## Why this file exists

The archive already had a file on **being, existence, and ontological commitment**, and many later files ended with a **final reality verdict**. But those verdicts were still operating more as repeated habit than as one explicit discipline.

Without a dedicated file here, the archive risks six recurrent mistakes:

- treating **existence** as if it settled **status**,
- treating **derivative** as if it meant **unreal**,
- treating **representational usefulness** as if it either guaranteed reality or precluded it,
- treating **idealization** as if it were simply fiction,
- treating **institutional, normative, centered, or biological reality** as if all had the same mode of ontological seriousness,
- or treating every domain as if the only options were **fundamental furniture** or **error**.

This file gives the archive a compact **status profile** so future revisions can say, with one shared discipline, whether a candidate is fundamental, derivative but robust, thinly dependent, idealized but world-tracking, fictional, merely representational, or still unsettled.

It should now be used together with `60-indispensability-representation-and-ontological-promotion.md` whenever a status verdict depends on explanatory indispensability, model success, or the temptation to promote a useful posit directly into ontology.

The fuller treatment of when **objective** means mind-independent, response-dependent but real, institutionally objective, centered but objective, or merely private now lives in `59-objectivity-mind-independence-and-response-dependence.md`.

## Working default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Use one ordinary notion of existence, but many typed verdicts about ontological status.**

And more specifically:

> **Do not multiply senses of being; instead, distinguish what exists from how it exists, how heavily it depends, what explanatory work it does, and whether its role is worldly, idealized, fictional, or merely representational.**

This fits the archive's wider commitments:

- **fundamentality** matters because some things are ontologically prior,
- **dependence** matters because derivative domains can still be real,
- **truth** matters because some discourse is world-answerable even when idealized,
- **appearance** matters because partial disclosure is not automatic illusion,
- **social ontology** matters because constructed or enacted domains can still be real,
- and **metaontology** matters because grammar, quantification, and modeling do not settle status by themselves.

## Core distinctions

### 1. Existence is not the same question as status

The archive should distinguish:

- **existence** — whether there is such a thing at all,
- **status** — whether it is fundamental, derivative but robust, thinly dependent, idealized, fictional, merely representational, or some mixed case,
- **commitment** — what reality must contain or make obtain for the relevant claims to be true,
- and **seriousness** — how much explanatory, modal, causal, normative, or organizational work would be lost by elimination.

Otherwise the archive will keep treating "is there?" and "what kind of reality is this?" as one flat question.

### 2. Derivative does not mean unreal

The archive should preserve a standing distinction between:

- **ontological priority**,
- **ontological dependence**,
- **derivative but robust reality**,
- and **mere heuristic convenience**.

An organism, person, institution, centered fact, or norm-governed role may be non-fundamental and still be fully real enough to matter.

### 3. Status is not fixed by grammar alone

The archive should not move too quickly from:

- existential idiom,
- nominalization,
- useful classification,
- indispensable discourse,
- or model success,

straight to a verdict about what exists and how seriously.

Natural language, practical discourse, and formal modeling all supply evidence. None of them alone settles status.

### 4. Idealization is not the same thing as fiction

The archive should distinguish:

- **strictly literal target description**,
- **approximate but world-tracking representation**,
- **idealized target characterization**,
- **useful fictional scaffold**,
- and **mere bookkeeping convenience**.

Otherwise the archive will keep sliding from inexactness to unreality.

### 5. One notion of existence can support many status verdicts

The archive should stay resistant to multiplying existential quantifiers or modes of being too quickly. It does not need one deep sense of existence for concreta, another for abstracta, another for institutions, and another for absences.

What it needs is a more disciplined status vocabulary layered on top of an ordinary notion of existence.

### 6. Robustness is domain-typed

A domain earns **robust** status when eliminating it would cost too much explanatory, modal, causal, normative, organizational, or persistence-sensitive work relative to the best alternatives.

But robustness is not one flat test. What counts as robust for:

- an organism,
- a legal office,
- a centered fact,
- a conscious subject,
- a scientific model target,
- or a mathematical structure,

need not be identical.

## The archive's status lattice

The archive should normally use the following status lattice.

### 1. Fundamental / basic reality

Use this verdict when the candidate is least dependent, ungrounded in the relevant sense, or part of the archive's best current basis story.

Do **not** use it merely because the candidate is explanatorily important or often mentioned.

### 2. Derivative but robust reality

Use this verdict when the candidate depends on other realities but still earns serious explanatory, modal, causal, normative, organizational, or persistence work that is not well captured by paraphrase alone.

Paradigm pressure cases include:

- organisms,
- persons,
- institutions,
- many processes,
- many organized wholes,
- and some centered or normative structures.

### 3. Thinly dependent or aspectual reality

Use this verdict when the candidate is real, but only in a highly dependent, aspectual, bounded, privative, role-relative, or projection-sensitive way.

Paradigm pressure cases include:

- holes,
- shadows,
- boundaries,
- absences,
- omissions,
- statuses that depend almost entirely on enacted role-space,
- and some perspectival or interface-bound entities.

### 4. Idealized but world-tracking posit

Use this verdict when the candidate is not best taken as straightforward furniture of the world, but still tracks real structure closely enough that elimination would destroy scientific, mathematical, or organizational understanding.

This category matters for:

- some model entities,
- effective-level variables,
- equilibrium constructions,
- idealized continua,
- and some statistical or geometric abstractions.

### 5. Fictional, erroneous, or merely representational posit

Use this verdict when the candidate does not earn enough worldly, modal, organizational, or explanatory work once the case is described better.

Paradigm pressure cases include:

- pure calculational placeholders,
- some interface-only icons,
- some narrative entities outside the fiction,
- and some merely convenient reifications.

### 6. Still unsettled

Use this verdict when the archive lacks enough grip on category, dependence, truth conditions, robustness, or elimination cost to issue a stronger verdict.

This is not failure. It is disciplined suspension.

## Four live options

### 1. Flat existence realism

**Picture:** once something exists, there is little more to say metaphysically except whether it is fundamental.

**What it gets right:**
- it resists equivocal being-talk,
- and it keeps ontology from becoming baroque.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it underdescribes derivative but robust domains,
- and it leaves the archive's repeated status verdicts with no common rule.

### 2. Fundamentalist austerity

**Picture:** only the fundamental is fully real; derivative domains are at best shorthand.

**What it gets right:**
- it preserves ontological seriousness about basis,
- and it resists inflation.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it makes organisms, institutions, persons, and many structured wholes look metaphysically weightless,
- and it confuses dependence with illusion.

### 3. Permissive discourse realism

**Picture:** any indispensable or systematically successful discourse earns reality in roughly the same way.

**What it gets right:**
- it notices that many non-fundamental domains are explanatorily indispensable,
- and it resists eliminativist cheapness.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it blurs the difference between robust domains, thin profiles, idealizations, and mere representational scaffolds,
- and it can become ontologically indiscriminate.

### 4. Typed status realism

**Picture:** existence is not multiplied into many senses, but ontological status is explicitly typed; derivative but robust reality is real, thin dependence is real in a lighter way, idealized but world-tracking posits are distinguished from fiction, and representational convenience is not automatically promoted.

**What it gets right:**
- it preserves ordinary realism,
- it preserves derivative reality,
- it preserves anti-bloat discipline,
- and it gives the archive a shared final-status vocabulary across domains.

**What it risks if left alone:**
- it can become impressionistic unless each case states what kind of dependence, what elimination loss, and what truth-contact is doing the work.

## Archive commitments

### 1. Keep one ordinary notion of existence

The archive should not introduce many senses of "exists" unless a domain forces that move. The default should remain one ordinary existential notion plus a typed status discipline.

### 2. Treat derivative reality as genuine where the work is real

The archive should reject the shortcut:

> derivative -> unreal

This is especially important for social, normative, biological, agential, and centered domains.

### 3. Keep representational success and ontological status distinct

A model, map, interface, or discourse can be useful without earning a robust ontological posit. But usefulness is also not irrelevant: sometimes it tracks a real target through approximation or idealization.

### 4. Make robustness answerable to elimination cost

A candidate earns more robust status when its removal causes serious loss across explanation, modality, organization, causation, normativity, persistence, or truthmaking.

### 5. Let different domains earn robustness differently

The archive should not demand the same evidence pattern from every domain. Institutional reality, centered reality, organismic unity, and model-based scientific realism may each earn robust status in different ways.

### 6. Preserve the distinction between thin reality and mere representation

Holes, shadows, omissions, boundaries, and some roles may be real in a thin or aspectual way. The archive should not treat these as identical either to robust individuals or to pure fiction.

## Status-profile rule for future revisions

Whenever a revision ends with a **final reality verdict**, it should now state explicitly:

1. **target candidate** — what exactly is under assessment,
2. **category profile** — object, process, event, fact, relation, structure, role, organization, abstractum, privation, or mixed,
3. **dependence profile** — grounding, constitution, realization, composition, institution, representation, abstraction, privation, or mixed,
4. **truth-contact profile** — direct worldly basis, broad correspondence, structural fit, idealized but world-tracking, fiction-internal only, or mixed,
5. **robustness basis** — explanatory, modal, causal, normative, organizational, persistence, mathematical, or mixed,
6. **elimination cost** — what would actually be lost under elimination or demotion,
7. **idealization/error profile** — literal, approximate, idealized, fictional, erroneous, or mixed,
8. **status verdict** — fundamental, derivative but robust, thinly dependent, idealized but world-tracking, merely representational/fictional, or still unsettled.

Without this step, the archive will keep using verdicts like **real**, **robust**, **thin**, **objective**, **constructed**, **idealized**, and **merely representational** as if they all belonged to one unarticulated scale.

## Where this matters most

This file is especially meant to steady the archive's verdicts about:

- organisms and living systems,
- persons and first-personal subjects,
- consciousness,
- institutions and statuses,
- norms and reasons,
- centered facts,
- absences and privations,
- structured wholes,
- model entities,
- and mathematical or structural abstractions.

## Compression rule

Future revisions should not invent a fresh status vocabulary for each domain unless the existing lattice clearly fails.

A good revision should usually do one of four things:

1. place the candidate inside the existing status lattice,
2. show that two statuses were being confused,
3. show that one status should be split because it hides a real recurrent difference,
4. or show that the present lattice misclassifies an important family of cases.

Otherwise the archive should prefer reusing the existing status discipline.

## Current archive lean

The archive now leans toward the following verdict:

> **Reality is status-graded without being equivocal: some things are fundamental, many things are derivative but robust, some are thinly dependent or aspectual, some are idealized but world-tracking, and some are merely representational or fictional — and these differences should be stated explicitly rather than hidden behind one loose word like “real.”**

This keeps the archive realist, anti-bloat, and non-reductive at the same time.

## What this file does not settle

This file does **not** settle:

- whether there are genuine irreducible modes of being,
- whether abstracta deserve one unified treatment,
- whether some domains should be reconstructed rather than literally accepted,
- whether every idealization should receive the same ontological verdict,
- or whether robustness always comes with causation or sometimes only with structure, normativity, or mathematics.

It settles only this much: the archive should stop issuing final reality verdicts without one shared discipline for what those verdicts mean.

> **Keep one ordinary notion of existence; type ontological status explicitly; let derivative reality be real where elimination would cost too much; distinguish thin dependence from robust derivative being; and keep idealized world-tracking posits separate from both literal furniture and mere fiction.**
