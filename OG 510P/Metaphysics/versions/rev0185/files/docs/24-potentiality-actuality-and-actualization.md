# Potentiality, Actuality, and Actualization

## Why this file exists

The archive already relies on **powers**, **process**, **modality**, **teleology**, **persistence**, and **development**, but it still lacked a direct policy for one of the oldest and most useful metaphysical contrasts:

- what something **can be**,
- what it is **now**,
- what it is **in the course of becoming**,
- and what it is when a capacity is **fulfilled, expressed, blocked, or frustrated**.

Without that discipline, the archive risks sliding among:

1. **bare logical possibility**,
2. **real disposition or power**,
3. **developmental potency**,
4. **current operative manifestation**,
5. **completed realization or fulfillment**,
6. **mere representational possibility**.

A compact non-skeptical, non-reductive metaphysics needs those apart.

## Archive default

The current default is **typed potentiality realism**.

That means:

- treat some potentialities as real features of things rather than as mere linguistic convenience,
- distinguish **standing powers** from **ongoing actualizations**,
- distinguish **ongoing actualization** from **completed manifestation or fulfillment**,
- allow that some potentials are **developmental** or **teleological** rather than merely trigger-response dispositions,
- and refuse to let all “can”, “is able”, “is ready”, “is becoming”, and “has become” language collapse into one flat modal status.

Compressed:

> **Potentiality is real where powers, capacities, or developmental tendencies belong to what a thing is; actuality is plural, ranging from standing organized readiness through ongoing manifestation to achieved fulfillment.**

## Four live options

### 1. Deflationary possibility-first views

**Picture:** talk of potentiality adds nothing over modal semantics, counterfactual truth, or predictive convenience.

**What they get right:**
- not every possibility claim tracks a real feature of the world,
- some “can” talk is merely epistemic or practical,
- and metaphysics should not multiply potencies just to paraphrase ordinary language.

**What they miss for this archive:**
- they underdescribe real capacities, liabilities, and developmental tendencies,
- they flatten the difference between a merely conceivable state and a power-bearing system,
- and they fit poorly with the archive's realism about powers, process, teleology, and organized becoming.

### 2. Bare dispositionalism

**Picture:** reality is fundamentally powers or dispositions; potentiality is just the standing dispositional profile of things.

**What it gets right:**
- ontology should include what things can do, undergo, sustain, or resist,
- unmanifest powers can still be real,
- and modality is better anchored in the world than in detached possibility spaces.

**What it misses if left alone:**
- it can underdescribe form, readiness, organization, and fulfillment,
- it can treat all powers as alike when developmental, agential, and teleological cases differ,
- and it can blur the difference between having a power and being in the midst of its actualization.

### 3. Aristotelian act-potency views

**Picture:** change is the actualization of a potential as such; form, matter, power, and fulfillment belong in one typed metaphysical scheme.

**What they get right:**
- they connect change, persistence, and directedness in one framework,
- they distinguish incomplete from complete actuality,
- and they fit naturally with hylomorphic and process-friendly ontology.

**What they risk if left alone:**
- they can become too schematic unless tied to contemporary distinctions about dispositions, abilities, control, and levels,
- and they may tempt the archive into overextending teleology beyond domains that earn it.

### 4. Abilities / control / developmental views

**Picture:** many important potentialities are not mere stimulus-response dispositions but organized abilities, powers of self-maintenance, or developmental trajectories.

**What they get right:**
- not all capacities are passive or binary,
- some potentials require enabling conditions, training, maturation, or organization,
- and actualization can be gradual, controlled, partial, or frustrated.

**What they risk if left alone:**
- they can diffuse the ontology of power into functional description,
- and they need help distinguishing genuine power from merely projected competence.

## Current archive answer

The archive should adopt a **layered answer**:

> **Potentiality is not exhausted by bare possibility; actuality is not exhausted by occurrent state; and actualization is often a real process of organized becoming.**

That means the archive should distinguish at least seven statuses:

1. **mere possibility** — logical, epistemic, practical, or representational room,
2. **standing power / disposition** — a real capacity, liability, or tendency,
3. **developmental potency** — a capacity whose fulfillment unfolds through growth, organization, or staged realization,
4. **readiness or first actuality** — an organized state of being able, equipped, or in a position to act,
5. **ongoing actualization** — the process of a power being exercised or fulfilled,
6. **achieved manifestation / completion** — the completed or settled realization of what was potential,
7. **blocked or frustrated potential** — a real power whose normal or fitting actualization is prevented, interrupted, or corrupted.

The archive does **not** need one universal technical vocabulary for every domain. But it does need these distinctions.

## Archive commitments

### 1. Not every possibility is a real potency

The archive should not infer a worldly power from every coherent description or counterfactual scenario.

A real potency usually requires more than bare consistency. It normally requires:

- a bearer,
- an enabling organization,
- a modal profile,
- and some connection to what the bearer can genuinely do, become, undergo, or sustain.

### 2. Powers are real even when unmanifested

The archive should remain realist about standing capacities. Fragility does not wait to exist until the glass shatters. Linguistic competence does not vanish while the speaker is silent. Institutional authority can be real even when not being exercised.

This matters because a purely occurrent ontology underdescribes what many entities are.

### 3. Actuality is layered

The archive should not treat “actual” as one flat status.

A thing may be:

- actually organized so as to exercise a power,
- actually engaged in exercising it,
- or actually at the completed state toward which the process was directed.

This layered view is especially important for life, learning, agency, development, repair, and teleology.

### 4. Actualization is often processual and incomplete while underway

A change is often best understood not as a jump from non-being to being, but as a **real ongoing actualization** with its own structure.

That matters because:

- some realities exist only in their enactment,
- some powers manifest gradually,
- and many metaphysical mistakes come from attending only to pre-state and post-state while ignoring the mode of becoming.

### 5. Developmental and teleological potencies are real where organized systems earn them

A seed, embryo, immune system, trained musician, court, or scientific institution may possess powers whose actualization depends on:

- maturation,
- environmental support,
- coordinated sub-capacities,
- norms of success and failure,
- or role-specific organization.

These are not always well modeled as simple trigger-response dispositions.

### 6. Frustrated, blocked, and corrupted actualizations are metaphysically revealing

Some of the best evidence for real potentiality comes from failure:

- a damaged heart can fail at pumping well,
- a silenced speaker can retain the ability to speak,
- a blocked enzyme pathway can reveal the process it would otherwise complete,
- a corrupted institution can show the end relative to which it is malfunctioning.

So the archive should treat frustrated actualization as diagnostically serious, not as a mere negative remainder.

### 7. Potentiality connects modality, causation, teleology, and time

This file should tighten four neighboring archive regions at once:

- with **modality**, because real powers narrow live possibilities;
- with **causation**, because production often involves the exercise of powers rather than bare sequence;
- with **teleology**, because some potentials are directed toward completion, function, or role-fulfillment;
- with **time**, because actualization is often an ordered process rather than a static state.

## Potentiality-profile rule

When a future revision uses language like **can**, **able**, **ready**, **latent**, **disposed**, **developing**, **in the process of becoming**, **fulfilled**, or **frustrated**, it should state which of these is doing the main work:

1. **mere modal space** — logical, epistemic, practical, or representational possibility,
2. **standing disposition or power**,
3. **developmental potency**,
4. **readiness / organized ability / first actuality**,
5. **ongoing actualization**,
6. **achieved manifestation or fulfillment**,
7. **blocked, frustrated, or corrupted actualization**.

Then it should also state:

- what the bearer is,
- what enables or disables the potency,
- whether the case is passive, active, developmental, agential, institutional, or mixed,
- how the potency relates to kind, form, and boundary,
- and whether the actuality in question is occurrent activity, achieved end-state, or merely current readiness.

If a proposal does not do this, its power and possibility vocabulary is under-disciplined.

## Default archive stances by domain

### Organisms

Default stance: **developmental and self-maintaining potencies are real**.

Living things often are not merely bundles of local dispositions. They are organized systems whose capacities unfold, repair, regulate, and mature over time.

### Artifacts

Default stance: **abilities are design- and maintenance-sensitive**.

A tool can be functional, broken, ready for use, currently in use, or fulfilled in successful operation. Those statuses should not be collapsed.

### Agents

Default stance: **ability exceeds mere occurrent performance**.

An agent can retain a capacity while not exercising it, can exercise it poorly, and can develop or lose it through training, injury, or social enablement.

### Institutions

Default stance: **role-powers and authority-potentials are real but derivative**.

An office or institution can possess capacities it is not currently exercising. These powers depend on rules, uptake, procedures, and collective recognition.

### Abstracta and models

Default stance: **usually representational unless independently earned**.

Do not reify every possibility-space or state-space into a domain of genuine potencies. Some are only tools for tracking what concrete or socially real systems can do.

## Working summary

The archive's current answer is neither:

- a thin modalism where all potentiality reduces to semantic possibility,
- nor a flat dispositionalism where every power is the same kind of thing,
- nor a crude actualism where only the occurrent is real.

It is instead:

> **a non-skeptical, non-reductive realism about typed potentialities and layered actuality in a world of forms, powers, structures, and organized processes.**

That answer should help the archive stay sharper about development, manifestation, readiness, frustration, and fulfillment without inflating ontology beyond what those distinctions earn.
