# Unity, Sortals, and Persistence

This file addresses a prior question to grounding and emergence: **what counts as one thing, and when is it the same thing through change?**

## Why this file exists

The archive already says that many entities are **stabilized processual organizations**. But that slogan remains under-disciplined unless the archive can answer four older metaphysical questions:

1. **What kind of thing is this?**
2. **How many things are here?**
3. **What unifies these parts or phases into one entity?**
4. **What changes can occur without the entity ceasing to exist?**

Without a compact answer, the archive risks two failures at once:

- process talk that blurs genuine objects,
- and object talk that ignores change, turnover, and organization.

## Central archive thesis

The archive should now adopt the following default:

> **Questions of unity and persistence are usually kind-sensitive, form-sensitive, and process-sensitive. They should not be settled first by brute matter-token continuity, nor by a single global formula for all entities.**

In compressed form:

> **Same thing later? Ask: same what, by what form, under what persistence condition?**

## Sortals as the archive's main compression device

A useful way to compress the issue is through **sortals**.

A sortal does at least four jobs:

- tells us **what a thing is**,
- helps **individuate** it,
- helps answer **how many** there are,
- and supplies or constrains a **persistence criterion**.

This matters because metaphysical identity questions are often empty or misleading when the kind of thing is left unspecified.

The archive should therefore ask not only whether `a` is the same as `b`, but whether `a` is the same **organism**, **artifact**, **institution**, **whirlpool**, **person**, or **office** as `b`.

## Phase sortals and substance sortals

The archive should retain the distinction between:

- **phase sortals** — ways something can be for a time without ceasing to exist when it leaves that phase,
- **substance or persistence sortals** — kinds whose loss is normally the end of the thing.

Examples:

- **child** is a phase sortal,
- **organism** is closer to a persistence sortal,
- **office-holder** is a phase or role description,
- **corporation** is a persistence sortal only under norm-governed continuity conditions.

This distinction matters because many metaphysical confusions come from treating role, stage, phase, and bearer as if they posed the same identity question.

## Archive default on persistence

The archive should now prefer the following broad rule:

### 1. Organisms

Persistence normally tracks **organized life-process continuity**, not strict material sameness. Metabolic turnover is compatible with persistence when the integrated living organization continues.

### 2. Artifacts

Persistence often tracks **form-and-function continuity** more tightly than organisms do. An artifact may survive part replacement, but only within a tolerance fixed by its organization, use, and constitution conditions.

### 3. Social and institutional entities

Persistence tracks **norm-governed and recognitional continuity** sustained by practices, rules, records, and enforcement structures, not merely by material continuity.

### 4. Pure processes or events

Persistence questions here often become **continuation questions** about the stability of a pattern or occurrence rather than identity questions about an enduring bearer.

## Unity rubric: when is there one thing here?

Before the archive admits an entity as one thing rather than a mere aggregate, it should ask for most of the following:

1. **Boundary condition** — what distinguishes this entity from its surrounding matter, activity, or environment?
2. **Coordination condition** — what internal organization makes the parts or phases function together rather than merely coexist?
3. **Powers condition** — what capacities belong to the whole in virtue of that organization?
4. **Persistence condition** — what kinds of change are survivable?
5. **Failure condition** — what would count as the entity's dissolution, death, destruction, or replacement?

If those questions cannot be answered even approximately, the archive should hesitate before promoting the candidate to full entityhood.

## What this does to the current synthesis

This file strengthens **Layered Process Realism** in a very specific way.

It lets the archive say:

- objects are not illusions,
- processes are not all there is,
- and object persistence is often the continued success of an organized process in sustaining the relevant form.

That is more disciplined than saying either:

- objects are just static lumps,
- or objects are merely convenient names for flux.

## Endurance vs. perdurance

The archive should keep both **endurantist** and **perdurantist** models live, but it should not let that dispute become the first question.

A useful archive rule is:

> **First identify the entity-kind and its persistence conditions; only then ask whether endurance, perdurance, or a mixed picture models it best.**

This keeps the archive from importing a one-size-fits-all temporal ontology too early.

## Main failure modes

### 1. Matter-token fetishism

Treating sameness of material constituents as the decisive criterion for persistence obscures organisms, repaired artifacts, and institutions.

### 2. Process washout

Treating every entity as merely a flowing occurrence dissolves ordinary and medium-scale unity.

### 3. Coincidence inflation

Treating every slight difference in sortal, phase, constitution, or modal profile as automatically multiplying entities can overpopulate the ontology.

### 4. Category confusion

Not every persistence question belongs to the same category. The identity conditions of a cat, a storm, a marriage, and a legal office should not be expected to line up under one simple rule.

## Working conclusion

The archive should now use the following order of questions when handling a candidate entity:

1. **What kind of thing is it?**
2. **What unifies it?**
3. **What is its persistence condition?**
4. **Only then: what grounds or constitutes it?**

In compressed form:

> **Category first, unity second, persistence third, grounding fourth.**

That is the current archive default. It is especially important for life, mind, artifacts, and institutions.
