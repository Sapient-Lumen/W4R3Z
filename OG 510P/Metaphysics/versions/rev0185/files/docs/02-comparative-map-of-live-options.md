# Comparative Map of Live Options

This file compares the strongest nearby metaphysical families. The archive is not trying to preserve every historical school equally; it is trying to identify what must survive compression.

## 1. Substance ontology

### Strengths

- Preserves the intuition that there are enduring things.
- Helps with bearer-property structure.
- Keeps individuation from dissolving into flux.

### Weaknesses

- Often underdescribes becoming, development, and maintenance.
- Tends to treat process as secondary.
- Can make organization seem accidental.

### Archive judgment

Retain as one ingredient, but not as a sufficient total picture.

## 2. Process metaphysics

### Strengths

- Handles change, activity, and development directly.
- Better fits living systems and temporally extended phenomena.
- Corrects static ontologies.

### Weaknesses

- Risks making entities too derivative.
- Can blur the difference between a thing and an occurrence.
- Needs an account of stabilization and individuation.

### Archive judgment

Indispensable, but must be paired with form and objecthood.

## 3. Hylomorphism / form-first views

### Strengths

- Explains why organization is not reducible to a parts-list.
- Helps with constitution, persistence, and kind-membership.
- Preserves explanatory room for life and artifacthood.

### Weaknesses

- Can become verbally rich without a clear modal profile.
- Needs care to avoid sounding like re-labeled common sense.
- Must explain how form interacts with powers, matter, and structure.

### Archive judgment

Central ingredient. The archive is increasingly hylomorphic in spirit, though not doctrinally confined to Aristotelian vocabulary.

## 4. Grounding / fundamentality frameworks

### Strengths

- Sharpens constitutive dependence.
- Distinguishes ontological order from causation.
- Clarifies what it means for something to be derivative yet real.
- Encourages explicit hierarchy talk.

### Weaknesses

- Can become too schematic.
- Does not by itself tell us what kinds of things there are.
- Leaves open whether grounding bottoms out, and in what.
- Risks imperialism if composition, constitution, and realization are redescribed too quickly as grounding.

### Archive judgment

Indispensable as an ordering framework, but never sufficient as a standalone metaphysics.

## 5. Powers / dispositional essentialism

### Strengths

- Gives modality and production ontological purchase.
- Helps explain lawfulness and tendency.
- Resists a world of inert categorical bits.

### Weaknesses

- Needs pairing with a theory of form, bearer, and level.
- Risks circularity if every property is explained by further powers alone.

### Archive judgment

Retain as part of the modal core.

## 6. Structural realism / relation-first views

### Strengths

- Fits domains where relation outruns intrinsic profile.
- Makes good contact with modern physics.
- Undermines simplistic atomism.

### Weaknesses

- Can make individual objects seem derivative in an unhelpfully abstract way.
- Must explain what is structured, or how free-standing structure is intelligible.

### Archive judgment

Retain as a major corrective against substance atomism, but pair with form and powers.

## 7. Priority monism

### Strengths

- Makes global structure explanatorily central.
- Offers a principled way to think of local entities as dependent on a wider whole.
- Helps with cases where part-whole organization seems metaphysically basic.

### Weaknesses

- Risks making ordinary and medium-scale entities overly derivative.
- Can inherit a scale-bias: the most comprehensive need not always be the most explanatory.
- Must explain how local powers and forms remain genuine.

### Archive judgment

Serious live option for the basal picture. Best treated as a candidate underpinning of the current synthesis, not yet its official foundation.

## 8. Neutral monism / dual-aspect baselines

### Strengths

- Offers a path for integrating mind and world without crude reduction or substance dualism.
- Encourages a less parochial account of what the basic level might be like.
- Can preserve dependence while avoiding a purely physical base.

### Weaknesses

- Often underdeveloped about concrete individuation and levels.
- Can become promissory when the “neutral” basis is left too obscure.
- Needs better articulation of laws, powers, and structure.

### Archive judgment

Keep live, especially as a mind-integration option, but do not promote to archive default without a clearer basal theory.

## 9. Flat reductive physicalism

### Strengths

- Ontological economy.
- Tight coupling to empirical science.
- Often useful as a methodological discipline.

### Weaknesses

- Frequently confuses dependence with identity.
- Has difficulty preserving the reality of mind, normativity, life, social institutions, and formal structure.
- Underdescribes explanation above the microlevel.

### Archive judgment

Rejected as a full metaphysics, though retained as a pressure source against hand-waving.

## 10. Idealism and anti-realism

### Strengths

- Keeps epistemic access and conceptual mediation in view.
- Can illuminate perspective, intelligibility, or normativity.

### Weaknesses

- Risks collapsing ontology into conditions of thought or experience.
- Often fails the archive's non-skeptical realism constraint.

### Archive judgment

Use diagnostically, not as archive default.

## Present ranking

### Tier A: indispensable ingredients

- hylomorphism/form
- process
- grounding/fundamentality
- powers/dispositions
- structure/relations

### Tier B: serious live base-options

- substance ontology
- priority monism
- neutral monism / dual-aspect baselines

### Tier C: pressure tests rather than destination views

- reductive physicalism
- idealism / anti-realism

## Current synthesis rule

Prefer theories that can jointly answer:

1. What is a thing?
2. What makes it the kind of thing it is?
3. What grounds it?
4. What can it do?
5. How can it persist through change?
6. When is a higher level genuinely real?
7. What, if anything, is globally fundamental?

Any candidate that leaves two or more of these permanently obscure should not become the archive default.

## 11. Property realism, trope theory, and nominalism

### Strengths of sparse property realism

- Explains real similarity without reducing it to mere language.
- Helps the archive connect form, powers, lawfulness, and natural kinds.
- Fits the thought that some predicates track objective repeatables while many do not.

### Strengths of trope theory

- Preserves the particularized character of concrete features.
- Helps with this-instance constitution, resemblance, and non-shareable profiles.
- Fits process-friendly metaphysics that wants features instantiated in concrete situations rather than floating free.

### Strengths of nominalist pressure

- Forces ontological economy.
- Exposes the danger of reifying every predicate.
- Helps test whether a proposed property does any explanatory work beyond language.

### Weaknesses across the space

- abundant realism can bloat ontology,
- trope-only approaches can make repeatability and lawfulness harder to state,
- austere nominalism can underdescribe objective similarity, induction, and natural kind structure.

### Archive judgment

Retain the whole dispute as live, but prefer a **sparse immanent realism with trope-permissive local description and domain-sensitive kind realism** over both maximal universals and aggressive nominalist thinning.


## Added pressure from identity and individuality

Any live option in this archive should now say whether it treats individuality as primitive, qualitatively grounded, relationally grounded, hylomorphically grounded, historically grounded, or domain-typed. Views that leave this implicit now lose points for under-discipline.
