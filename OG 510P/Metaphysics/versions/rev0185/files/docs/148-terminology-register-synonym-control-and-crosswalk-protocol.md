# Terminology Register, Synonym Control, and Crosswalk Protocol

## Why this file exists

The archive now has a disciplined growth sequence:

1. `144-operator-family-map-and-triage-grid.md` routes candidate cases.
2. `145-adversarial-stress-tests-and-revision-protocol.md` attacks the routed verdict.
3. `146-diagnostic-case-ledger-calibration-set-and-benchmark-protocol.md` records reusable hard cases.
4. `147-revision-dependency-graph-update-propagation-and-drift-control.md` propagates accepted changes through the archive.
5. In `rev0143`, `149-claim-status-register-maturity-levels-and-commitment-governance.md` assigns commitment status after terminology audit.
6. In `rev0144`, `150-application-dossier-decision-record-and-verdict-report-protocol.md` records reusable verdicts after status assignment.
7. In `rev0145`, `151-precedent-reuse-appeal-transfer-and-review-governance.md` governs how those verdicts may later be cited, taught, transferred, appealed, superseded, or marked do-not-reuse.
8. In `rev0146`, `152-transmission-excerpt-compression-and-pedagogical-governance.md` governs how those verdicts and terms may be summarized, excerpted, diagrammed, taught, or exported without losing their term status and forbidden inferences.

Before status assignment and application reporting can be safe, the sequence still has to handle one major source of hidden drift: **terminology**. A case can be routed, stress-tested, ledgered, and propagated while still leaving behind uncontrolled words that later readers treat as doctrine. This happens when ordinary terms such as **same**, **part**, **in**, **support**, **control**, **real**, **ground**, **cause**, **open**, **closed**, **channel**, **replacement**, **system**, or **function** are allowed to carry more metaphysical weight than the archive has licensed.

The archive is especially vulnerable because it deliberately uses clusters of near-synonyms in file names. Those clusters are helpful entry points, but they can also mislead. **Guiding**, **channeling**, **conduiting**, and **ducting** are not always interchangeable. **Valving**, **throttling**, **metering**, and **aperture control** are adjacent but not identical. **Attachment**, **joining**, **clamping**, **latching**, **threading**, **meshing**, and **gripping** all sound like ways of holding together, but they answer to different bases and failure modes.

This file adds a control layer for **canonical terms, aliases, neighboring terms, deprecated terms, quarantined metaphors, and crosswalks**. Its job is not to police ordinary language for its own sake. Its job is to prevent vocabulary from silently deciding ontology.

## Compressed default

The archive should now say:

> **No term should be allowed to do operator-work until its target, basis, grain, aliases, rivals, and forbidden inferences are explicit.**

In shorter form:

> **Words enter as clues; only registered contrasts become doctrine.**

And more carefully:

> **Every stable technical term in the archive should have a status: canonical operator term, alias, family umbrella, neighboring rival, domain-specific term, diagnostic prompt, source-specific term, deprecated term, or quarantined metaphor. If the status is not clear, the term should not be used to settle identity, dependence, continuity, status, or category.**

## Place in the control sequence

The enlarged governance sequence is:

1. **Route** the case or proposal with `144`.
2. **Stress-test** the routed verdict with `145`.
3. **Ledger** reusable or precedent-setting cases with `146`.
4. **Propagate** the accepted change through the archive with `147`.
5. **Register or audit terminology** with this file so the accepted change does not leave behind uncontrolled aliases, metaphors, or overloaded words.
6. **Assign commitment status** with `149` so terminology-safe claims are not overread as stronger doctrine than intended.
7. **Write an application dossier** with `150` so terminology-safe and status-assigned verdicts remain portable, bounded, and reopenable.
8. **Govern precedent reuse and appeal** with `151` so terminology-safe verdicts do not later carry aliases, metaphors, source terms, or unsafe shortcuts beyond their licensed scope.
9. **Govern transmission and compression** with `152` so summaries, tables, diagrams, teaching examples, and public outputs do not turn aliases, metaphors, source terms, or unsafe shortcuts into canonical language by repetition.

This fifth step matters because a large archive can drift by vocabulary even when its file map is current; the sixth step matters because a large archive can drift by commitment strength even when its words are safe; the seventh step matters because a large archive can drift by application prose even when its terms and statuses are correct; the eighth step matters because a large archive can drift by precedent reuse even when the original dossier carried correct term status. A new revision may update every expected file yet still teach a misleading term if the revision leaves readers unsure whether a phrase is canonical, merely illustrative, domain-specific, deprecated, metaphorical, or a neighbor to be contrasted.

## The terminology problem

### 1. Same word, changed basis

A word can remain stable while the basis changes. **Support** can mean load-bearing support, explanatory support, social support, legal support, evidential support, or scaffold support. The same word does not guarantee the same metaphysical relation.

Default rule: when one word occurs across families, require target, basis, and grain before treating it as a stable operator.

### 2. Same basis, changed word

A basis can remain stable while vocabulary changes. The same aperture-control structure may be called valving, throttling, metering, regulation, partial closure, dose control, or flow control. The changed word does not guarantee a changed operator.

Default rule: when different words track the same basis and failure mode, prefer alias treatment or crosswalk treatment before adding a new file.

### 3. Family umbrella mistaken for local operator

A broad term can name a family rather than a local file. **Control**, **interface**, **continuity**, **access**, **status**, **support**, and **process** often point to a region of the archive rather than one verdict.

Default rule: family umbrellas should route inquiry; they should not settle local diagnosis.

### 4. Domain term mistaken for metaphysical primitive

Engineering, law, biology, medicine, social theory, mathematics, physics, and everyday practice all bring domain terms. A domain term may be precise within its practice without being a metaphysical primitive.

Default rule: source-specific or domain-specific terms must be translated into target, basis, grain, relation, failure mode, and comparison class before they become archive terms.

### 5. Metaphor mistaken for mechanism

The archive often uses physical vocabulary because interface, flow, resistance, support, and control metaphors are useful. But metaphors can overrun their scope. A legal bottleneck, epistemic filtering, institutional support, or social scaffolding may preserve a structural analogy without sharing the same material mechanism.

Default rule: metaphors are allowed as entry points only after the literal basis and the analogical basis are distinguished.

### 6. Label mistaken for target

A file name, administrative category, readout label, route marker, measurement bucket, model variable, or diagnosis code can be mistaken for the thing itself.

Default rule: do not let a label become the bearer unless the archive has separately earned a status, reference, or institutional-reality verdict.

## Term statuses

Every durable term introduced by a revision should be assigned one or more of the following statuses.

### 1. Canonical operator term

A canonical operator term names the main diagnostic relation of a file or stable section. It can be used in headings and method rules.

Requirements:

- target class is clear,
- basis is explicit,
- grain is stated,
- nearest rivals are named,
- artifact risks are known,
- and forbidden inferences are recorded.

Example pattern: **valving** as the canonical term for regulated passage through aperture, resistance, setting, or metered admission.

### 2. Alias

An alias is a term that may point to the same operator in many cases but should not be treated as identical in every context.

Requirements:

- say which canonical term it usually routes to,
- say what basis would make it diverge,
- say which neighboring file should receive divergent cases.

Example pattern: **flow-controlled** may be an alias for valving in a metered-aperture case, but may route to gating, filtering, bottlenecking, or feedback if the basis differs.

### 3. Family umbrella

A family umbrella names a region of inquiry rather than a single file.

Examples: **interface**, **control**, **continuity**, **support**, **access**, **status**, **process**, **stock/flow**.

Rule: an umbrella term should trigger triage with `144`; it should not be used as a final verdict.

### 4. Neighboring rival

A neighboring rival is a term close enough to tempt misclassification.

Examples: **sealing** as a rival to valving, **hosting** as a rival to embedding, **clamping** as a rival to gripping, **turnover** as a rival to substitution.

Rule: neighboring rivals must be named in the local file, method rule, frontier test, and stress-test protocol when the boundary is unstable.

### 5. Domain-specific term

A domain-specific term is authoritative inside a practice or source context but not automatically a canonical metaphysical term.

Examples: raceway, dispenser rail, gasket, butterfly valve, load path, tenancy, status function, truthmaker, possible world, realization.

Rule: domain-specific terms need translation into the archive's target/basis/grain grammar before promotion.

### 6. Diagnostic prompt

A diagnostic prompt is a useful question-word or entry-word that points to a test rather than a conclusion.

Examples: same, real, part, cause, in, through, open, closed, supports, represents, controls, replaces.

Rule: diagnostic prompts should be treated as invitations to analysis, not as results.

### 7. Source-specific anchor term

A source-specific anchor term appears in an external source and is used to stabilize an example.

Rule: record the anchor in `05-source-citations.md` when the term supports a source-dependent claim. Do not expand its domain without a new source or a generic abstraction note.

### 8. Deprecated term

A deprecated term once served a useful role but now misleads or has been replaced by a clearer term.

Rule: deprecation should say whether the term is forbidden, retained as historical label, or allowed only as a quoted ordinary expression.

### 9. Quarantined metaphor

A quarantined metaphor is useful rhetorically but dangerous as doctrine.

Examples: social valve, epistemic friction, legal scaffolding, institutional metabolism, conceptual gravity, semantic leakage.

Rule: a quarantined metaphor must be translated before it is allowed to support a metaphysical inference.

### 10. Unsafe shortcut

An unsafe shortcut is a phrase the archive should resist because it collapses distinctions.

Examples: **same role, therefore same thing**; **inside, therefore part**; **controlled, therefore agentive**; **measured, therefore real**; **no output, therefore absent**; **changed name, therefore changed entity**; **same label, therefore same bearer**.

Rule: unsafe shortcuts should be flagged in method rules, stress tests, or ledger entries when they repeatedly mislead.

## Term-record schema

When a future revision introduces or stabilizes a term, it should record at least:

1. **Term:** the word or phrase under review.
2. **Status:** canonical operator, alias, umbrella, neighboring rival, domain-specific term, diagnostic prompt, source-specific anchor, deprecated term, quarantined metaphor, unsafe shortcut, or mixed.
3. **Canonical destination:** which file or family should receive ordinary cases.
4. **Target:** what the term is usually about.
5. **Basis:** what makes the term non-accidental when it is used correctly.
6. **Grain:** token, type, role, system, lineage, model, institution, regime, or mixed.
7. **Aliases:** near-synonyms that often route to the same place.
8. **Nearest rivals:** terms that look similar but route elsewhere.
9. **Forbidden inferences:** what the term must not be allowed to settle by itself.
10. **Artifact risks:** labels, readouts, metaphors, source terms, or administrative categories that can mislead.
11. **Translation rule:** how the term should be converted into target/basis/grain language.
12. **Update footprint:** which files must change if the term becomes canonical, deprecated, or a controlled alias.

## Alias discipline

### When aliases are acceptable

Aliases are acceptable when:

- they help readers enter the archive,
- they route to the same canonical destination under the same basis,
- they do not erase a live boundary,
- and they come with known divergence conditions.

Example: **metering** can be treated as an alias within the valving family when the case is about calibrated admission through an aperture or setting. It should not be treated as a mere alias when the case is about measurement without control, bookkeeping, evidential registration, or statistical estimation.

### When aliases are dangerous

Aliases are dangerous when:

- the same word crosses families,
- a local synonym hides a different failure mode,
- an ordinary expression carries a heavy identity or dependence claim,
- a source-specific technical term is exported outside its source domain,
- or a metaphor becomes more memorable than the contrast it was meant to illustrate.

Default rule: dangerous aliases should be crosswalked, not promoted.

## Neighbor-rival discipline

A term becomes a neighbor-rival when it repeatedly competes with another term for the same case. Neighbor rivalry is not a defect. It is how the archive identifies where its diagnostic pressure lives.

For each unstable rivalry, future revisions should ask:

1. What target do the terms share?
2. What basis differs?
3. What grain differs?
4. What failure mode differs?
5. What continuity consequence differs?
6. What ordinary example would tempt collapse?
7. What hard negative prevents collapse?

The late operator family already contains many such rivalries:

- hosting versus embedding,
- embedding versus enclosure,
- enclosure versus wrapping,
- coating versus wrapping,
- wrapping versus lamination,
- sealing versus valving,
- joining versus attachment,
- clamping versus latching,
- threading versus joining,
- meshing versus gripping,
- support versus hosting,
- guiding versus support,
- guiding versus valving,
- valving versus gating,
- valving versus bottlenecking.

A future revision that adds a new neighbor should update `144` and this file. A future revision that resolves a rivalry should update the affected local files, `03`, `04`, `145`, `146` if the case is precedential, and `147` for propagation.

## Metaphor quarantine protocol

A metaphor is quarantined when it is useful enough to retain but risky enough to prevent from doing direct ontological work.

Before using a metaphor doctrinally, specify:

- the **literal source domain**,
- the **target domain**,
- the **shared structure** that makes the analogy helpful,
- the **non-shared structure** that would mislead,
- the **operator family** the metaphor might route to,
- the **nearest literal term** in that family,
- the **forbidden inference** the metaphor tempts,
- and the **translation** into target, basis, grain, failure mode, and comparison class.

Example pattern:

- **epistemic filter** may be useful if the issue is selective transmission of evidence, but it should not automatically inherit physical filtering, shielding, aperture, or valving logic.
- **institutional scaffold** may be useful if the issue is external enabling structure, but it should not automatically mean literal support, parthood, or constitution.
- **social valve** may be useful if a practice regulates admission or release, but it should not automatically imply hardware-like aperture control, automatic feedback, or fluid flow.

Metaphor quarantine is not metaphor banishment. It is controlled translation.

## Renaming, deprecation, and consolidation protocol

A term should be renamed, deprecated, or consolidated when its continued use creates more drift than clarity.

### Rename when

- the current canonical term points readers to the wrong family,
- a better term captures the basis rather than the ordinary appearance,
- or an alias has become more accurate than the old heading.

Rename only after recording the old term as a deprecated or historical alias so old file references remain intelligible.

### Deprecate when

- the term repeatedly invites a forbidden inference,
- it duplicates a better-controlled term,
- it is too source-specific for generic archive use,
- or it carries a metaphor the archive can no longer quarantine safely.

Deprecation should say whether the term is forbidden, allowed as an ordinary quote, allowed only in historical notes, or allowed as an alias under strict basis conditions.

### Consolidate when

- several terms track the same target, basis, grain, failure mode, and continuity consequence,
- the differences are mainly stylistic or domain-specific,
- and hard negatives cannot be found.

Consolidation should update `144`, `03`, `04`, `145`, and `146` if any calibration cases depend on the older separation.

## Cross-domain translation protocol

When a term moves from one domain to another, future revisions should use a translation ledger:

1. **Source domain:** where the term is native.
2. **Target domain:** where the term is being applied.
3. **Source basis:** what makes the term true in the source domain.
4. **Target basis:** what is supposed to play the analogous role.
5. **Shared structure:** what legitimately transfers.
6. **Non-shared structure:** what must not transfer.
7. **Nearest archive files:** where the translated case should be routed.
8. **Status after translation:** alias, metaphor, domain-specific term, canonical extension, or rejected transfer.
9. **Source need:** whether a source anchor is required in `05`.
10. **Stress test:** which same-word/changed-basis and same-basis/changed-word variants from `145` must be run.

The archive should especially require this protocol for legal, institutional, biological, clinical, engineering, computational, and mathematical terms, because those domains often have precise local meanings that tempt overextension.

## Terminology audit worksheet

Before closing a revision, ask:

1. Did the revision introduce any new durable term?
2. Did it promote an ordinary word into a technical role?
3. Did it use an alias without naming its canonical destination?
4. Did it create or resolve a neighbor rivalry?
5. Did it rely on a source-specific technical term?
6. Did it import a metaphor from another domain?
7. Did it use **same**, **part**, **in**, **support**, **control**, **real**, **cause**, **ground**, **open**, **closed**, **evidence**, **model**, or **function** in a way that could settle too much?
8. Did any term require a source anchor in `05`?
9. Did any term require a method rule in `03` or frontier test in `04`?
10. Did any term require an adversarial variant in `145`?
11. Did any term require a calibration entry in `146`?
12. Did any term require a propagation note in `147`?
13. Which terms are canonical, aliases, umbrellas, neighboring rivals, domain-specific, deprecated, quarantined metaphors, or unsafe shortcuts?
14. Which non-updates are intentional?

A revision that cannot answer these questions may still be useful, but it is not terminology-safe.

## Relation to `144`

`144` routes cases by family. This file routes words by status. A case may be correctly routed while its vocabulary remains unsafe. When a term appears across several families, use `144` to identify the families and this file to say whether the term is canonical, alias, umbrella, rival, metaphor, or unsafe shortcut.

## Relation to `145`

`145` already includes vocabulary quarantine as a stress-test gate. This file makes that quarantine persistent. A term that fails same-word/changed-basis or same-basis/changed-word testing should not disappear from view; it should be registered as an alias, neighbor rival, quarantined metaphor, deprecated term, or unsafe shortcut.

## Relation to `146`

`146` records hard cases as calibration instruments. This file records the terms those cases stabilize or defeat. A calibration case should now say not only which verdict it supports, but also which vocabulary it licenses, which vocabulary it blocks, and which aliases remain unsafe.

## Relation to `147`

`147` says accepted changes must propagate. This file says terminology changes are among the things that may need propagation. If a revision canonicalizes, aliases, deprecates, quarantines, or renames a term, the update footprint should include the front door, synthesis, method rule, frontier test, family map, adversarial protocol, ledger cases, source notes, index, version marker, and manifest as appropriate.

## Relation to source citations

This file adds no new external source anchors by itself. It is a methodological and terminological control file. However, it increases the archive's source discipline: when a term's authority comes from a legal, engineering, clinical, scientific, textual, or historical source, the source-specific status of that term should be clear in `05-source-citations.md`.

## Anti-patterns

### 1. Glossary theater

A revision adds a list of terms but does not record basis, grain, rivals, forbidden inferences, or update footprint.

Correction: convert the glossary entry into a term record.

### 2. Alias laundering

A revision treats a near-synonym as evidence that the same operator applies.

Correction: run same-word/changed-basis and same-basis/changed-word tests.

### 3. Metaphor promotion

A vivid metaphor becomes the basis for a new operator.

Correction: quarantine the metaphor and translate it before promotion.

### 4. Domain capture

A source-specific technical term silently becomes a metaphysical primitive.

Correction: record the source domain, target domain, and translation rule.

### 5. Umbrella collapse

A family term such as **control**, **interface**, **support**, or **status** is used as a local verdict.

Correction: route through `144` and assign local destination.

### 6. Forbidden-inference drift

A term is used correctly in one paragraph but later allowed to imply identity, parthood, grounding, causation, or reality without argument.

Correction: add forbidden inferences to the term record and update the relevant method rule or frontier test.

## Default verdict

The default verdict of this file is linguistic but not merely linguistic:

> **Terminology is part of metaphysical method because uncontrolled words become uncontrolled commitments.**

The archive should therefore treat terms as governed entry points. Ordinary vocabulary may start inquiry. Source vocabulary may anchor examples. Metaphor may reveal a pattern. But only a term whose target, basis, grain, aliases, rivals, artifact risks, and forbidden inferences are explicit may carry stable operator-work.


## Revision-integration note: terminology status and claim status

`149-claim-status-register-maturity-levels-and-commitment-governance.md` distinguishes term status from claim status. A term can be canonical while the claim using it remains provisional. A term can be an alias while the underlying distinction is mature. A source-specific term can be well registered while the metaphysical claim remains domain-local. A quarantined metaphor can still be useful as an M1 prompt without becoming doctrine.

Future terminology entries should therefore avoid a common mistake: **registered vocabulary is not the same as accepted commitment**. When a term is allowed to carry operator-work, ask next what status and maturity level the resulting claim has under `149`.


## Revision-integration note: terminology and application dossiers

`150-application-dossier-decision-record-and-verdict-report-protocol.md` makes term status visible in the final verdict. This matters because a term can be correctly registered in `148` and still disappear from the application report, allowing the reader to treat an alias as canonical, a metaphor as doctrine, a source-specific term as archive-wide, or an umbrella as a local verdict.

Future terminology entries that support a reusable case should therefore specify how they should appear in the dossier: canonical term, controlled alias, neighboring rival, family umbrella, domain-specific term, source-specific anchor, deprecated term, quarantined metaphor, unsafe shortcut, or translation required.
