# Habituation, Sensitization, Desensitization, and Response Recalibration

## Why this file exists

The archive now has explicit files on **repair and resilience**, **masking and blocked manifestation**, **onset and return**, **negative feedback and homeostasis**, **synchronization and timing breakdown**, **gating and selective responsiveness**, and **saturation and capacity limits**, but it still lacks one direct file saying what to do when a case is said to become **habituated**, **sensitized**, **desensitized**, **tolerant**, **primed**, **allostatically recalibrated**, **less responsive after repetition**, **more reactive after repetition**, or **history-shaped in its later responsiveness**.

That omission matters because the archive still risks eight opposite mistakes:

- treating every **response decrease after repetition** as if it were mere fatigue, saturation, or disappearance,
- treating every **response increase after prior exposure** as if it were just stronger ordinary activation,
- treating every **desensitization** case as if the relevant power or bearer had been lost,
- treating every **habituation** case as if it were contentless wear rather than structured history-sensitive change,
- treating every **stimulus-specific recalibration** as if it were a whole-system global shift,
- treating every **generalized retuning** as if it were only local habituation,
- treating every **allostatic recalibration** as if it were simple homeostatic return or already terminal pathology,
- or treating every **lasting change in responsiveness** as if it settled identity, regime change, or replacement too quickly.

This file gives the archive a compact **response-recalibration profile** so future revisions can say whether a case is chiefly about habituation, sensitization, desensitization, priming, tolerance, allostatic retuning, or some mixed history-shaped responsiveness.

## Working default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Changed responsiveness after repetition is not one flat metaphysical lesson.**

In compressed form:

> **History-shaped responsiveness is real and typed.**

And more carefully:

> **Repeated or prolonged encounter with a stimulus, stressor, signal, demand, or task can recalibrate later responsiveness so that the same bearer becomes habituated, sensitized, desensitized, primed, tolerant, or allostatically retuned without thereby disappearing, becoming numerically new, or proving that the earlier response profile was unreal.**

This fits the archive's wider commitments:

- **powers and manifestation** still matter because a standing power may persist while its trigger threshold, gain, or expression profile changes,
- **repair, resilience, and plasticity** still matter because recalibration can be adaptive continuity rather than replacement,
- **masking, inhibition, and blocked manifestation** still matter because reduced later response can reflect inhibitory retuning rather than absence,
- **amplification and positive feedback** still matter because heightened later response is not always one more case of ordinary stronger activation,
- **negative feedback and homeostasis** still matter because allostatic recalibration can preserve viability by shifting the response profile rather than restoring one previous baseline,
- **gating, coupling, and tuning** still matter because repetition can retune windows, thresholds, and sensitivity,
- **saturation and capacity limits** still matter because response decrement can arise from ceiling, depletion, or refractoriness rather than habituation,
- **onset, cessation, and return** still matter because changing responsiveness does not by itself create or end a bearer,
- **malfunction and pathology** still matter because sensitization, tolerance, and stress retuning can be adaptive, pathological, or mixed,
- and **comparative ontological choice** still matters because some cases favor habituation, others desensitization, others allostatic retuning, and others genuine damage, overload, or replacement.

## Core distinctions

### 1. Habituation is not fatigue, sensory adaptation, or saturation

A response can decline with repetition because the bearer has learned, filtered, or reweighted the significance of the repeated stimulus, rather than because the receptor is exhausted, the channel is saturated, or the bearer is gone.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **habituation**,
- **sensory adaptation**,
- **motor fatigue**,
- **capacity saturation**,
- and **genuine loss only in stronger cases**.

Otherwise it will keep reading structured decrement as mere wear.

### 2. Sensitization is not just stronger one-off activation

A later stronger response can reflect history-shaped heightened readiness, lower trigger threshold, amplified salience, priming, or facilitation rather than simply a more intense present input.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **ordinary stronger activation**,
- **sensitization**,
- **priming / facilitated later recruitment**,
- **positive-feedback escalation**,
- and **pathological hyper-reactivity only in stronger cases**.

Otherwise it will keep flattening history-shaped gain into one present-moment push.

### 3. Desensitization and tolerance are not disappearance

A bearer can remain present and even stay structurally in place while becoming less responsive after repeated or prolonged stimulation.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **desensitization with retained bearer**,
- **downregulated responsiveness**,
- **temporary tolerance**,
- **reversible retuning**,
- and **degradation or removal only in stronger cases**.

Otherwise it will keep mistaking changed response profile for lost power.

### 4. Recalibration can be local or generalized

Repeated exposure can alter one narrow stimulus-response relation, a whole modality, a stress-response family, or a broader organizational policy.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **stimulus-specific habituation**,
- **within-modality generalization**,
- **cross-context sensitization**,
- **system-wide allostatic retuning**,
- and **mixed nested recalibration**.

Otherwise it will keep over-reading local history into global ontology.

### 5. Repetition effects can be temporary, recoverable, or enduring

Some altered response profiles wash out quickly, some spontaneously recover, some potentiate with repeated cycles, and some become long-term traits or policy shifts.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **short-term recalibration**,
- **spontaneous recovery**,
- **potentiated habituation / faster relearning**,
- **long-term habituation or tolerance**,
- and **regime change only in stronger cases**.

Otherwise it will keep treating all persistence of changed response as one kind of memory or one kind of damage.

### 6. Allostatic recalibration is not the same as homeostatic return

A system can preserve viability by changing what counts as its ordinary operating range, readiness, or mobilization pattern.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **homeostatic restoration of an old range**,
- **allostatic recalibration of response profile**,
- **adaptive accommodation to repeated demand**,
- **allostatic load / costly chronic retuning**,
- and **terminal dysregulation only in stronger cases**.

Otherwise it will keep collapsing adaptive retuning into either simple return or simple pathology.

## Compact response-recalibration profile

### A. Habituation

**Signal:** repeated exposure yields progressive decrement not best explained by mere receptor ceiling, sensory fatigue, or motor exhaustion.

**Typical pressure:** same bearer, reduced response, preserved reality.

**Examples:** repeated innocuous cue draws less orienting response; familiar alarm becomes backgrounded; repeated harmless monitoring prompt loses salience.

### B. Sensitization and priming

**Signal:** prior exposure increases later reactivity, lowers threshold, or accelerates recruitment.

**Typical pressure:** same bearer, heightened response, history-shaped readiness.

**Examples:** prior stress increases later stress reactivity; repeated threat cue increases vigilance; prior inflammatory exposure primes stronger subsequent response.

### C. Desensitization and tolerance

**Signal:** prolonged or repeated stimulation attenuates later response while the relevant receptor, pathway, or organization remains present.

**Typical pressure:** same bearer, weaker coupling from input to output.

**Examples:** receptor desensitization under agonist exposure; institutional alert tolerance; reduced analgesic response after repeated dosing.

### D. Allostatic retuning

**Signal:** repeated challenge changes the operating profile, gain setting, or default mobilization pattern of the wider system.

**Typical pressure:** adaptation through changed baseline or changed response architecture rather than simple return.

**Examples:** repeated stress with habituation to one stressor but broader vulnerability elsewhere; chronic load shifting the cost of response; organizational policy hardening after repeated shocks.

### E. Mixed or layered cases

**Signal:** decrement and increment coexist at different sites, times, or grains.

**Typical pressure:** one local channel habituates while another sensitizes; one receptor desensitizes while broader system arousal remains high; one repeated cue becomes background while related novelty becomes more salient.

**Examples:** simultaneous local habituation and generalized sensitization; short-run decrement with long-run potentiation; adaptive retuning that later hardens into costly load.

## Cross-domain pressure on the archive

Behavioral, neural, physiological, pharmacological, and institutional cases all pressure the archive here because they show that **later responsiveness is often shaped by prior exposure**. Repetition can reduce orienting or endocrine response; repeated agonist presence can desensitize receptors without erasing them; chronic challenge can recalibrate a larger system in ways that are partly adaptive and partly costly.

So the archive should stop asking only whether a response is present now. It should also ask how the target's **response history** has altered what it is ready to do next, and whether the right verdict is habituation, sensitization, desensitization, temporary tolerance, allostatic recalibration, or some mixed history-sensitive profile.

## Discriminating questions future revisions should ask

1. What is the relevant **target** — one receptor, one pathway, one behavior, one organism, one institution, one role-bearer, or one mixed organization?
2. What is the relevant **recalibration relation** — habituation, sensitization, priming, desensitization, tolerance, allostatic retuning, or mixed?
3. What is actually **doing the history-sensitive work** — repeated innocuous exposure, strong prior perturbation, threshold change, receptor uncoupling, gain modulation, negative feedback, plastic remodeling, or mixed?
4. What remains **real underneath the altered response** — same standing capacity, same bearer with new threshold, same channel with weaker coupling, same organization under higher load, or mixed?
5. What is the relevant **specificity profile** — stimulus-specific, modality-generalized, cross-context, system-wide, or mixed?
6. What is the relevant **timescale / recovery profile** — immediate, short-run with spontaneous recovery, potentiated across cycles, long-term retained, or chronically costly?
7. What is the relevant **continuity consequence** — same bearer recalibrated, same regime retuned, same system with narrowed corridor of response, successor organization only in stronger cases, or mixed?
8. And whether the best verdict is **habituation**, **sensitization**, **desensitization / tolerance**, **allostatic recalibration**, **mixed layered retuning**, or **still unsettled**.

## Compression rule for the rest of the archive

When future revisions say that something became **used to it**, **less responsive**, **tolerant**, **numbed**, **sensitized**, **primed**, **trigger-happy**, **desensitized**, **allostatically shifted**, or **recalibrated by repeated exposure**, they should state whether the case is really one of habituation, sensitization, desensitization, tolerance, allostatic retuning, or mixed history-shaped responsiveness before letting repetition-language do metaphysical work.
