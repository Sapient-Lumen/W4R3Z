# Dependence Relations Map

This file exists to stop the archive from using **grounding** as a one-word solvent for every dependence question.

## Core claim

A non-reductive metaphysics should distinguish at least five major relations:

- **composition**,
- **constitution**,
- **realization**,
- **grounding**,
- **causation**.

These relations often travel together, but they are not interchangeable.

## Compact matrix

| Relation | Guiding question | Typical contrast | Why the archive needs it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Composition | What parts make up this whole? | whole vs parts | explains material or mereological build-up |
| Constitution | What materially constitutes this thing here and now? | coincident object vs constituting matter | explains clay/statue, artifact, organism-style cases |
| Realization | What lower-level configuration realizes this type or role? | realized property/state vs realizer | explains multiple realizability and level-bridging |
| Grounding | In virtue of what does this obtain? | derivative vs more fundamental fact | explains constitutive order and layered being |
| Causation | What produced or sustained this over time? | earlier/later production and maintenance | explains temporal generation, interaction, and upkeep |

## One-line distinctions

### 1. Composition is not yet constitution

A thing can be **composed of** certain parts without being simply identical to the mereological sum under every description. Composition tells us what parts are there. It does not by itself settle what makes the result one thing of a given kind.

### 2. Constitution is not yet grounding

A statue may be constituted by clay, an organism by organized matter, or a legal office by institutional arrangements. These are intimate dependence relations, but the metaphysical work is not exhausted by saying one fact grounds another. Constitution often carries questions of coincidence, persistence conditions, and kind-specific identity.

### 3. Realization is not yet identity

A pain state, a representational state, or a social-functional role may be realized by different lower-level configurations. Realization is especially useful where the archive wants dependence **without** crude one-type/one-base identity.

### 4. Grounding is not causation

Grounding orders reality by “in virtue of” relations. Causation concerns production, maintenance, triggering, and temporal sequence. A thing's existence may be grounded in one basis while being caused, sustained, or destroyed by another chain of events.

### 5. Causation is not constitution

The maker of a statue causes it to exist. The clay constitutes it. Those are different questions.

## Archive policy

When analyzing a case, use the **narrowest adequate relation first**.

### Good order of operations

1. Ask whether the case is about **parts**. If so, start with **composition**.
2. Ask whether it is about **what makes this one thing of this kind**. If so, test **constitution**.
3. Ask whether it concerns **type-level dependence across levels**. If so, test **realization**.
4. Ask whether the issue is **ontological order or in-virtue-of explanation**. If so, use **grounding**.
5. Ask whether the issue is **production, maintenance, or change over time**. If so, use **causation**.

Only after that should the archive ask how these relations interlock.

## Four stress cases

### Statue and clay

- **composition**: atoms or molecules compose both.
- **constitution**: the clay constitutes the statue.
- **grounding**: some statue-facts are grounded in material and formal facts.
- **causation**: the sculptor causes the statue to exist.

### Living cell

- **composition**: molecules and organelles compose the cell.
- **constitution**: organized biochemical matter constitutes the cell as one living unit.
- **grounding**: some cellular facts obtain in virtue of organized lower-level facts.
- **causation**: metabolic and environmental processes sustain it.

### Corporation

- **composition**: people, records, buildings, contracts, and technical systems may be involved.
- **constitution**: those do not by themselves settle the corporation unless the right normative and institutional structure is present.
- **realization**: role and office facts may be multiply realizable across organizational setups.
- **grounding**: corporate facts are grounded in legal, social, and material arrangements.
- **causation**: decisions and enforcement mechanisms produce later effects.

### Pain or conscious state

- **realization** is often central if one wants dependence without one-to-one reduction.
- **grounding** may still be invoked, but only with care.
- **causation** matters for onset and maintenance, but does not settle the metaphysical relation by itself.

## Working archive conclusion

The archive should be **grounding-friendly but grounding-non-imperialist**.

Grounding is indispensable, but it should not erase the explanatory roles of constitution, realization, composition, and causation. A metaphysics that says only “it is grounded” too often stops exactly where real work begins.
