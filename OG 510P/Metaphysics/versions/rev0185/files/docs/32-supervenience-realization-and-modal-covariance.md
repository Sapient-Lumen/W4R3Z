# Supervenience, Realization, and Modal Covariance

The archive already used **realization**, **levels**, **dependence pluralism**, and **non-reductive higher-level reality**. What it lacked was a direct rule for a notion that often appears in those disputes without being named carefully enough: **supervenience**.

This file adds that rule.

## Core claim

The archive should treat **supervenience** first as a **no-difference-without-difference constraint**.

At minimum, a supervenience claim says:

- there cannot be a difference in the target domain
- without some difference in the base domain.

That is a powerful modal-screening tool.

But it does **not by itself** settle:

- whether the target is identical with the base,
- whether the target is grounded by the base,
- whether the target is realized by the base,
- whether the target is reducible to the base,
- whether the target is causally efficacious,
- or whether the target is metaphysically “nothing over and above” the base in any stronger sense.

The archive should therefore treat supervenience as a **constraint on covariance and modal determination**, not as a complete metaphysics.

## Why this matters

Many non-reductive views earn plausibility by saying things like:

- mental states supervene on physical states,
- social facts supervene on physical-and-practical arrangements,
- normative properties supervene on natural or institutional facts,
- higher-level patterns supervene on lower-level organization,
- macro-properties supervene on microstructure.

This vocabulary is useful because it blocks completely free-floating differences.

But it is dangerous because it is easy to move too quickly from:

- **no difference without lower-level difference**

to:

- **same thing**,
- **mere shorthand**,
- **fully explained**,
- **causally inert**,
- or **not really real**.

The archive should refuse that slide.

## Default archive stance

The archive should adopt four working claims.

### 1. Supervenience is weaker than grounding, realization, or identity

Supervenience tells us that variation in one domain depends modally on variation in another.

It does **not** yet tell us:

- what the relevant relata are,
- what kind of dependence is present,
- whether the dependence is constitutive, realizational, causal, or merely pattern-level,
- or whether the target retains explanatory autonomy.

### 2. Supervenience is often a screening test, not a final verdict

A supervenience claim is often the right **first-pass discipline** when a proposal wants to deny spooky independence but has not yet earned a stronger dependence relation.

So the archive should often proceed in this order:

1. ask whether the target plausibly **supervenes** on some base,
2. then ask whether the deeper relation is **realization**, **grounding**, **constitution**, **mechanistic organization**, **causal dependence**, or a mixed profile.

### 3. Supervenience is compatible with non-reductive realism

A target domain may supervene on a base while still being:

- explanatory,
- lawlike or pattern-stable,
- intervention-relevant,
- causally organized,
- teleologically structured,
- institutionally real,
- or ontologically derivative but serious.

The archive should therefore not infer unreality from supervenience.

### 4. Supervenience alone is too thin for the archive's hardest cases

When the archive discusses consciousness, agency, life, institutions, mathematics-in-science, or normativity, supervenience alone rarely finishes the job.

It often needs help from:

- realization,
- kind and grain discipline,
- levels and cross-scale discipline,
- naturalness and projectibility,
- truthmaker discipline,
- or teleological / institutional structure.

## Distinctions the archive should keep explicit

### A. Local vs global supervenience

A proposal may claim that:

- one individual's features supervene on another set of features,
- or that whole worlds cannot differ in target respects without differing in base respects.

Do not move silently between local and world-level claims.

### B. Weak vs strong modal strength

Some supervenience claims hold only within a world or domain.
Others are meant to hold across possible worlds.

Do not treat every supervenience thesis as equally strong.

### C. Synchronic vs diachronic use

A proposal may mean:

- same-time dependence,
- or dependence across development, history, and persistence.

Do not use supervenience to hide a temporal question.

### D. Intrinsic vs relational bases

A target may supervene on:

- intrinsic microfeatures,
- arrangement plus relation,
- institutional position,
- environmental embedding,
- or mixed structural facts.

Do not assume a purely intrinsic base unless the case earns it.

### E. Supervenience vs realization

Realization usually tells us what lower-level configuration implements or sustains a role or type.

Supervenience tells us only that no target-difference occurs without some base-difference.

So realization is often **more informative** than supervenience, even when both are true.

### F. Supervenience vs grounding

Grounding is an in-virtue-of relation.
Supervenience is a modal no-difference relation.

Grounding may explain why a supervenience pattern holds.
But a bare supervenience thesis does not yet give grounding.

## What the archive should now prefer

### 1. Use supervenience to block free-floating autonomy

When a proposal threatens to detach a domain from all lower or neighboring constraints, ask whether it supervenes on a disciplined base.

If it does not, the proposal must explain why not.

### 2. Do not use supervenience as a substitute for a dependence map

Once supervenience is established, the archive should still ask:

- is the deeper relation constitution?
- realization?
- grounding?
- causal support?
- mechanistic organization?
- institutionally structured dependence?
- or some mixed profile?

### 3. Allow multiply realizable supervenience profiles

A higher-level kind may supervene on a lower-level base even when it is realized by different lower-level structures in different cases.

This is one reason supervenience is useful in a non-reductive archive.

### 4. Treat failure of supervenience as a high-cost claim

If a proposal says that a domain does **not** supervene on an expected base, it should say whether that implies:

- stronger emergence,
- dual-aspect or neutral-basis revision,
- ontological pluralism,
- institution-relative autonomy,
- or a mistake about what the relevant base is.

## Supervenience-profile rule

When a future revision uses language like **supervenes on**, **nothing over and above**, **fixed by**, **determined by**, **duplicate**, **same base**, **no difference without**, or **metaphysically necessitated by**, it should state:

1. what the **target domain** is;
2. what the proposed **base domain** is;
3. whether the claim is **local** or **global**;
4. whether it is **within-world**, **cross-world**, **synchronic**, **diachronic**, or mixed;
5. whether the base is **intrinsic**, **relational/structural**, **institutional**, **environmental**, or mixed;
6. whether the supervenience claim is being used as a claim about **identity**, **realization**, **grounding**, **reduction**, **explanatory sufficiency**, or only **modal covariance**;
7. what stronger relation, if any, explains why the supervenience pattern holds;
8. and what would be lost if the target were treated as merely a shadow of its base.

If a proposal does not do this, its supervenience talk is under-disciplined.

## Default stress cases

This file should be used first when the archive discusses:

- consciousness and the mental,
- biological function and organization,
- social and institutional reality,
- normative properties,
- macro/micro and multiscale cases,
- realization across heterogeneous bases,
- and any claim that says a domain is “nothing over and above” another.

## Compression payoff

This file is meant to reduce slippage across at least seven existing regions of the archive:

- **dependence**, because supervenience is weaker than grounding and should not replace it;
- **levels**, because covariance and level-relation are not the same issue;
- **realization**, because implementation is richer than no-difference-without-difference;
- **emergence**, because supervenience can coexist with disciplined higher-level autonomy;
- **naturalness**, because a supervening profile may still carve at the wrong grain;
- **causation**, because supervenience does not by itself settle efficacy;
- **ontological commitment**, because supervenience neither guarantees nor eliminates derivative seriousness.

## Current archive default in one sentence

**Use supervenience as a disciplined no-free-floating-difference test, but do not mistake modal covariance for the full metaphysics of realization, grounding, reduction, or reality-status.**
