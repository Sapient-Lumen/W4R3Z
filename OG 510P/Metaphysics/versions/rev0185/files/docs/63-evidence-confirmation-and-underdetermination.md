# Evidence, Confirmation, and Underdetermination

## Why this file exists

The archive already had files on **truth**, **objectivity**, **representation**, **ontological promotion**, **measurement**, and **directness**. But it still lacked one direct file saying what the archive means when it moves from:

- observation or readout,
- data or record,
- evidential support,
- explanatory virtue,
- repeated confirmation,
- or underdetermination pressure

to a **metaphysical verdict**.

Without a dedicated file here, the archive risks six recurrent mistakes:

- treating **what is well supported** as if it were already **what is finally real**,
- treating **underdetermination** as if it automatically forced **skepticism** or suspension about every ontological claim,
- treating **theory-ladenness** as if it automatically meant **arbitrariness**,
- treating **measurement output**, **evidence**, **confirmation**, and **truth** as if they were one flat epistemic-ontological relation,
- treating **historical theory change** as if it either destroyed realism or did not matter at all,
- or treating **multiple successful models or theories** as if one must already have exhausted the space of reality-apt alternatives.

This file gives the archive a compact **evidence profile** so future revisions can say what counts as evidence in the case, what kind of support is at issue, how underdetermination is functioning, what background assumptions are doing work, and what sort of ontological promotion—if any—is actually earned.

## Working default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Evidence is world-constrained support, not a transparent ontology machine and not a mere social permission-slip.**

And more specifically:

> **Treat confirmation as graded, defeasible, theory-involving support whose metaphysical significance depends on the target, the evidential route, the contrast class of alternatives, the kind of underdetermination in play, and the difference between support for a representation and promotion into ontology.**

This fits the archive's wider commitments:

- **measurement** matters because much evidence arrives through setups, traces, readouts, and operational variables,
- **directness** matters because mediated access can still be genuine world-contact,
- **truth** matters because evidence aims at reality-answerable claims rather than mere coherence,
- **objectivity** matters because public criticism, reproducibility, and shared evidential standards can stabilize support without a view from nowhere,
- **promotion/status** matter because evidential success can create graded pressure for realism without automatically settling what kind of thing is real,
- and **representation** matters because evidence often bears first on models, hypotheses, and claims rather than immediately on raw ontology.

## Core distinctions

### 1. Evidence is not yet truth, and truth is not yet ontology

The archive should distinguish:

- **evidence**,
- **evidential support / confirmation**,
- **truth or approximate truth**,
- **ontological commitment**,
- and **final reality verdict**.

Otherwise the archive will keep sliding from what is supported to what is true, and from what is true to what must exist in the heaviest sense.

### 2. Data, measurement, and evidence are not one flat layer

The archive should distinguish:

- **data or record**,
- **measurement output or readout**,
- **interpreted empirical result**,
- **evidence for a claim or model**,
- and **support for an ontological profile**.

Otherwise the archive will keep treating whatever is registered as self-standing evidence, and whatever is evidential as already a metaphysical verdict.

### 3. Confirmation is not explanation, and explanation is not automatic realism

The archive should distinguish:

- **predictive fit**,
- **diagnostic support**,
- **explanatory unification**,
- **robust intervention-guidance**,
- **selective retention across theory change**,
- and **full ontological endorsement**.

Otherwise the archive will keep treating explanatory usefulness as if it alone promoted entities, structures, or processes into the final ontology.

### 4. Underdetermination is not one thing

The archive should distinguish:

- **local contrastive underdetermination**,
- **temporary or transient underdetermination**,
- **recurrent unconceived-alternative pressure**,
- **model plurality under shared target constraints**,
- **mere practical non-uniqueness**,
- and **radical empirical equivalence**.

Otherwise the archive will keep using one word—**underdetermined**—for importantly different epistemic and metaphysical situations.

### 5. Theory-ladenness is not arbitrariness

The archive should distinguish:

- **theory-involving interpretation**,
- **background methodological assumptions**,
- **contrast-class selection**,
- **publicly criticizable inference**,
- and **merely unconstrained projection**.

Otherwise the archive will keep oscillating between naïve empiricism and “anything goes” conventionalism.

### 6. First-order evidence is not the whole evidential story

The archive should distinguish:

- **first-order evidence** for a theory or model,
- **second-order evidence** from history of science or meta-methodology,
- **local case support**,
- **long-run retention pressure**,
- and **defeaters from past theory change**.

Otherwise the archive will keep ignoring either current evidential success or the historical record of revision and replacement.

### 7. Support for a representation is not yet promotion of its every posit

The archive should distinguish:

- **support for a model as an inferential tool**,
- **support for a target structure**,
- **support for a causal or organizational profile**,
- **support for an entity posit**,
- and **support only for empirical adequacy or practical control**.

Otherwise the archive will keep promoting every successful representational ingredient into ontology, or else refusing promotion even where the case genuinely earns it.

## Four live options

### 1. Naïve evidential realism

**Picture:** strong empirical support just is a near-direct warrant for the ontology of our best current theories.

**What it gets right:**
- evidence can genuinely constrain metaphysical theorizing,
- and inquiry should not float free of the world.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it underdescribes theory-ladenness, contrast classes, unconceived alternatives, and selective retention under theory change,
- and it reads ontology too directly off current support.

### 2. Underdetermination skepticism

**Picture:** because evidence underdetermines theory in many cases, ontology should usually be suspended or deflated.

**What it gets right:**
- evidence rarely fixes one total metaphysical story by itself,
- and historical theory change matters.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it makes it too hard to explain selective convergence, robust intervention, and why some evidential routes are better than others at tracking the world.

### 3. Instrumentalist evidential modesty

**Picture:** evidence should support predictive or practical success, but little or no ontology.

**What it gets right:**
- support is often model-relative, purpose-sensitive, and non-final,
- and not every successful posit deserves promotion.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it underdescribes why some structures, powers, entities, and organizations remain explanatorily indispensable across changing representations,
- and it risks shrinking metaphysics into bookkeeping about successful discourse.

### 4. Typed evidential realism

**Picture:** evidence is mediated, defeasible, and contrast-sensitive, but still world-constrained. Confirmation creates graded ontological pressure. Underdetermination enforces humility, selectivity, and typed verdicts rather than blanket skepticism.

**What it gets right:**
- it preserves answerability to inquiry without collapsing metaphysics into current theory,
- it preserves historical fallibilism without severing realism,
- and it keeps promotion selective and target-sensitive.

**What it risks if left alone:**
- it can become slogan-like unless each case states what the evidence bears on, which alternatives matter, and what sort of promotion is actually earned.

## Archive commitments

### 1. Treat evidence as ontologically relevant but not self-interpreting

Evidence can create real pressure on ontology, but only through a typed route that distinguishes target, vehicle, background assumptions, and contrast class.

### 2. Distinguish support for claims, structures, entities, and organizations

Do not let one success verdict do work for every ontological category at once.

### 3. Let underdetermination discipline confidence, not erase reality-answerability

Underdetermination should make the archive more selective, comparative, and explicit about alternatives; it should not force a default retreat into global anti-realism.

### 4. Use history of theory change as second-order evidence, not as a veto on inquiry

The archive should neither ignore the graveyard of past theories nor treat it as proof that present inquiry tracks nothing real.

### 5. Prefer selective promotion over wholesale endorsement or wholesale suspension

Evidence may support realism about a structure, mechanism, process, constraint, or organizational profile without equally supporting every auxiliary posit in the current representation.

### 6. Keep first-person, social, and scientific evidence typed rather than flat

Not all evidence is public measurement-data; not all private evidence is therefore epistemically illegitimate; not all institutionally mediated evidence is therefore unreal or merely semantic.

## Evidence profile rule for future revisions

Before inferring a metaphysical verdict from language like **the evidence shows**, **well confirmed**, **supported by observation**, **best theory**, **empirically successful**, **underdetermined**, **the data imply**, **strongly supported**, **unconceived alternative**, or **the model is vindicated**, future revisions should answer at least these questions:

1. **target** — object, process, structure, mechanism, power, status, field, centered fact, model-family, or mixed target;
2. **evidence type** — perception, measurement result, intervention outcome, trace, record, testimony, historical retention, explanatory success, or mixed evidence;
3. **support type** — incremental confirmation, disconfirmation, robustness, predictive success, abductive support, selective retention, empirical adequacy only, or mixed support;
4. **background load** — what theory, calibration, methodological assumption, or inferential framework is doing work in turning the raw result into evidence;
5. **contrast class** — what real alternatives are being compared, and whether unconceived alternatives matter in the case;
6. **underdetermination type** — local, transient, recurrent, empirical-equivalence, plural-model, or merely practical non-uniqueness;
7. **historical pressure** — does the case look stable across theory change, likely to fragment, selectively retained, or still too immature to promote strongly;
8. **promotion pressure** — does the case support realism about the target itself, only about a structure or process, only about predictive usefulness, or still no settled ontological promotion;
9. **final reality verdict** — strong typed promotion, selective promotion, structure-only realism, organization/process realism, empirical-adequacy-only caution, or still unsettled.

## What this file does **not** say

It does **not** say:

- that evidence is theory-free,
- that underdetermination is unreal or unimportant,
- that current best theory should be treated as final ontology,
- that all successful models are merely instrumental,
- or that the history of theory change makes realism impossible.

The point is only that **evidence needs a typed metaphysical profile rather than a flat choice between naïve current-theory realism and global skeptical suspension**.
