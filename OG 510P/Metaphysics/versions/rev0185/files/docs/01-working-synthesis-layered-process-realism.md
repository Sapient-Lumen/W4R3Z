This revision is an **adversarial stress-test revision**. It does not add a new first-order operator; it adds `145-adversarial-stress-tests-and-revision-protocol.md` so the archive can red-team candidate diagnoses after family routing and before promotion, demotion, consolidation, or synthesis-level hardening.

The most important synthetic gain is this: **operator choice must be tested after it is triaged**. Interface intimacy is not identity; path constraint is not rate control; same hardware is not same throughput; same host is not same bearer; same function is not same individual; and even a correct-looking family route can still be captured by vocabulary, proxy evidence, or the wrong grain. ## Why this synthesis exists

No single live metaphysical family seems sufficient on its own:

- pure substance views struggle with becoming,
- pure process views can thin out objecthood,
- pure grounding views risk becoming formally elegant but ontologically underdescribed,
- pure structural views can underplay concrete bearers or powers,
- pure powers views can under-specify form and level,
- and purely causal or descriptive views can under-specify normative structure where reasons, standards, roles, and correctness conditions do real explanatory work.

The synthesis is meant to preserve what is strongest in each without turning the archive into an eclectic scrapbook.

## Kernel operators

The archive now treats five operators as minimally indispensable:

- **form** — individuation and kindhood,
- **process** — persistence-through-change,
- **power** — modal and productive profile,
- **structure** — relational constitution,
- **grounding** — constitutive dependence.

Any future theoretical addition should improve one of these operators, reduce one to another, or show that the kernel is incomplete.

## Core commitments

### 1. Processes are ontologically primary enough to matter

The world is not best described as a static stock of self-identical items plus accidental motion. Change, maintenance, decay, development, and interaction belong to what many things are.

This does **not** imply that enduring things are unreal. It implies that enduring things are often best understood as **stabilized patterns of activity**.

### 2. Forms are real patterns of organization

A thing is not merely its matter, nor merely its microconstituents listed one by one. It is also the organized mode in which those constituents are configured, sustained, and made intelligible as one thing of a certain kind.

Forms here are not spooky additions. They are the real organizational principles without which individuation, function, constitution, and persistence become obscure.

### 3. Grounding orders reality without flattening it

Some facts obtain in virtue of others. This constitutive dependence is not identical to efficient causation. A living cell may be grounded in biochemical organization without being identical to a biochemical vocabulary item. A legal institution may be grounded in collective practices without being fictional.

Grounding therefore supplies order, not automatic elimination.

### 3A. Fundamentality needs a typed basis claim

The archive should now say explicitly that **fundamental**, **basic**, **ungrounded**, **independent**, and **complete basis** are not automatically interchangeable. Some disputes are about absolute fundamentality. Others are about relative priority. Others are about whether a domain supplies a complete basis for another. Others are really disputes about explanation or naturalness rather than about ontological depth.

So when a revision says that something is fundamental, it should state whether the claim is:

- independence-first,
- ungroundedness-first,
- complete-basis-first,
- primitive-by-stipulation,
- or only locally fundamental relative to a restricted dependence family.

Without this discipline the archive will keep sliding from derivative reality to unreality, and from explanatory centrality to absolute priority. The fuller treatment now lives in `48-fundamentality-grounding-and-metaphysical-explanation.md`.

### 4. Powers and dispositions are ontologically serious

Things are not only what they currently manifest; they are also what they can do, undergo, sustain, or resist. Fragility, solubility, metabolic capacity, linguistic competence, and institutional authority all point toward a world whose ontology includes capacities and tendencies, not only occurrent states.

### 4A. Power-talk must be typed

The synthesis should now say more clearly that **power** is not one flat category. Dispositions, abilities, affordances, susceptibilities, active powers, passive powers, and standing normative powers should not be collapsed together; possessing a power is not the same as presently exercising it; and a power should not be identified too quickly with one simple stimulus-manifestation conditional.

The fuller treatment now lives in `54-powers-dispositions-abilities-and-manifestation.md`.

### 5. Structure is irreducible

Relations, networks, and ordering principles are not always ontological afterthoughts. In some domains, what something is depends constitutively on the structure in which it stands.

### 5A. Structure-talk must be typed

The synthesis should now say more clearly that **structure** is not one flat category. A structure may be a role-order, network position-map, symmetry-linked arrangement, topological configuration, modal structure, or constitutive relational pattern; some structures are worldly, some idealized, and some mainly representational. Not every relation-token yields a structure, not every structure is an organization, and not every robust pattern deserves ontological promotion.

The fuller treatment now lives in `55-structure-patterns-networks-and-relational-constitution.md`.


### 4B. Facts and obtainings are real, but fact-talk must be typed

The synthesis should now say more clearly that **facts** are not automatically identical with true propositions, events, or fundamental building blocks. Sometimes fact-talk is exactly right because the issue is what **obtains**. Sometimes a broader **state-of-affairs** framework is doing the work. Sometimes an object, process, structure, or bounded totality is the leaner and better truthmaking basis.

The current synthesis therefore prefers **typed factual realism**: facts remain live where they clarify worldly obtaining, truthmaking, or constitutive explanation, but the archive should stay sparse, should resist negative-fact inflation, and should not let nominalized “the fact that ...” language settle ontology by itself.

The fuller treatment now lives in `56-facts-states-of-affairs-and-obtaining.md`.

### 4C. Truth is reality-answerable, but truth-talk must be typed

The synthesis should now say more clearly that **truth** is not one flat achievement. Some uses of “true” are largely logical, expressive, or disquotational. Some truth-ascriptions are load-bearing because they say what reality is like. Some scientific truths are approximate or idealized without therefore losing all truth-contact. Some institutional truths depend partly on enacted records and procedures. The archive should therefore preserve broad correspondence or world-answerability without demanding a one-fact-for-one-sentence picture.

The fuller treatment now lives in `57-truth-correspondence-and-reality-answerability.md`.

### 4D. Final reality verdicts should be status-typed

The synthesis should now say more clearly that **real**, **objective**, **derivative**, **thin**, **idealized**, and **merely representational** are not interchangeable verdicts. The archive should keep one ordinary notion of existence, but it should issue explicitly typed status verdicts: some candidates are **fundamental**, many are **derivative but robust**, some are **thinly dependent or aspectual**, some are **idealized but world-tracking**, and some are best treated as **fictional or merely representational**.

This matters because the archive now spans organisms, persons, institutions, centered facts, model entities, absences, and abstracta. Without one shared status discipline it will keep saying that such cases are all "real" or all "not fundamental" without enough further articulation. The fuller treatment now lives in `58-ontological-status-robustness-and-derivative-reality.md`.

### 4E. Comparative ontology is contrastive rather than winner-take-all by default

The archive should now say explicitly that not every apparently rival ontology is a dispute over **what there is** in the same sense. Some are disputes over **grain**, some over **category choice**, some over **status**, some over **target-fixation**, and some over whether one and the same domain is better handled by layered complementary articulations.

The current default is therefore:

- make the **contrast class** explicit before announcing a winner,
- distinguish **genuine incompatibility** from **recarving** or **rescaling**,
- allow **local superiority** without inflating it into global metaphysical conquest,
- and keep **layered coexistence**, **domain-splitting**, and **disciplined suspension** live where one flat package has not yet been earned.

This matters because the archive now has strong files on naturalness, grain, evidence, abduction, promotion, reference, and revision. Without one explicit comparative discipline, it would keep oscillating between premature winner-take-all monism and relaxed anything-goes pluralism. The fuller treatment now lives in `70-contrast-classes-rival-carvings-and-comparative-ontological-choice.md`.

### 4F. Idealization and approximation are world-answerable, but typed

The synthesis should now say more clearly that **idealization**, **approximation**, and **limit cases** are not one flat relation to reality. Some approximations are bounded closeness claims. Some idealizations isolate difference-makers by controlled distortion or omission. Some limiting constructions reveal stable structure or effective-regime behavior. And some representational aids are mainly heuristic. The archive should therefore preserve the epistemic and metaphysical seriousness of idealized inquiry without reading ontology straight off whatever makes calculation easiest.

This matters because the archive now leans heavily on representation, promotion, measurement, truth, revision, and contrast. Without one explicit discipline here, it would keep oscillating between exactness fetishism and a loose “only a model” retreat. The fuller treatment now lives in `71-idealization-approximation-and-limit-cases.md`.

### 4G. Metametaphysical diagnosis must be typed

The synthesis should now say more clearly that not every apparent ontology dispute is one flat disagreement about what exists. Some disagreements are about **status** rather than existence. Some are about **category or counting policy**. Some are about **scope or regime**. Some hide **target-shift**. And some really are largely **verbal** once translation preserves the same worldly, modal, explanatory, and naturalness-relevant content.

The current default is therefore:

- do **not** call a dispute merely verbal until preservation and loss are shown,
- do **not** treat ordinary-language permissibility as final metaphysics,
- do **not** infer that framework tolerance eliminates all worldly pressure,
- and do **not** inflate every paraphrase failure into deep ontological rivalry.

This matters because the archive now spans objects, processes, institutions, absences, abstracta, centered facts, effective regimes, and derivative but robust realities. Without one explicit metametaphysical discipline, it would keep oscillating between heavyweight realism about every difference and premature deflation about many real ones. The fuller treatment now lives in `74-metametaphysics-substantiveness-verbalism-and-worldly-difference.md`.

### 5B. Organization is ontologically serious but kind-sensitive

The archive should now say explicitly that **organization** is not a decorative synonym for complexity. Organized wholes often have differentiated parts, role-structure, constraints, maintenance conditions, and failure profiles that make them more than an aggregate. But organization comes in kinds: mechanism, organism, institution, network, interface, and agentive control-architecture should not be flattened into one model.

The fuller treatment now lives in `53-organization-constraints-and-mechanisms.md`.

### 6. Laws are real but not extra ontological police

Laws should not be treated as mere summaries if modality is real, but neither should they be reified into external governors. The present archive stance is that laws articulate stable modal-structural constraints in a world of power-bearing processes.

The fuller treatment now lives in `10-laws-powers-and-structure.md`.

### 6A. Chance is real where probabilistic structure earns it, but probability-talk must be typed

The archive should now say explicitly that probability is not one flat metaphysical category. It should distinguish:

- **credence**,
- **evidential probability**,
- **frequency**,
- **objective chance**,
- **effective or higher-level probability**,
- and **merely model-internal stochastic parameterization**.

The current default is therefore:

- allow **single-case chance** where a process, setup, or law genuinely earns it,
- keep **frequencies** as evidence for chances without identifying them with chance,
- keep **credence** answerable to chance where genuine chance is known,
- allow **objective higher-level probabilities** where deterministic microdescription is not the whole explanatory story,
- and refuse the shortcut from **stochastic modeling** to **automatic metaphysical indeterminism**.

This matters because the archive already leans on laws, powers, process, levels, and representation. Without an explicit chance discipline, it would keep sliding between ignorance, randomness, frequency, and objective modal structure. The fuller treatment now lives in `40-chance-probability-and-objective-uncertainty.md`.

### 7. Dependence is plural, though still ordered

The archive should not use grounding as a master label for every case. Composition, constitution, realization, grounding, and causation often cooperate in one domain, but they answer different questions.

This matters because many metaphysical confusions arise from premature unification. A cell is composed of molecules, constituted by organized biochemical matter, sustained by causal processes, and plausibly hosts grounded higher-level facts. Saying only that it is grounded is too schematic.

The fuller treatment of constitution and coincidence now lives in `35-constitution-coincidence-and-material-constitution.md`.

### 7A. Architectural direction needs its own discipline

The archive should now say explicitly that not every acceptable metaphysical structure must satisfy strong set-theoretic well-foundedness, but neither should every feedback-rich or reciprocal domain be treated as if ontological direction disappears.

The current default is to:

- keep **grounding** directional by default,
- allow that some acceptable structures may involve **bounded or otherwise anchored infinite descent**,
- distinguish **metaphysical dependence loops** from **causal, functional, and regulatory feedback**,
- and treat **local reciprocal organization** as compatible with a wider typed order of priority.

This matters because organisms, institutions, mechanisms, and structurally rich domains often exhibit real feedback without thereby collapsing all distinction between basis and derivative status.

The fuller treatment now lives in `33-well-foundedness-loops-and-architectural-direction.md`.

### 8. Emergence is real under strict conditions

Emergence should not mean miracle, ignorance, or rhetorical insistence. It should mean that when organization reaches certain forms of stability and integration, there appear higher-level properties, regularities, or necessities that are neither independent of lower-level reality nor exhaustively captured by a flat microdescription.

The stricter criteria now live in `07-real-emergence-admission-test.md`.

### 9. Unity and persistence are form-sensitive

The archive should not ask whether something simply remains identical through time in abstraction from what kind of thing it is. Organisms, artifacts, institutions, whirlpools, and persons do not all persist in the same way.

The current default is that persistence is usually governed by **organized continuity of the relevant form or process**, not by brute sameness of matter-token composition. This keeps the synthesis from collapsing either into static lump ontology or into object-dissolving flux metaphysics.

The fuller treatment now lives in `12-unity-sortals-and-persistence.md`.

### 10. Truth is world-answerable, but truthmakers should be used with restraint

The archive should be realist enough to say that truth answers to reality, and that some truths do carry ontological commitments. But it should not require a bespoke entity for every true sentence.

The current default is to prefer the **leanest adequate positive ground** for a truth: an entity, structure, power-profile, obtaining state of affairs, realized organization, or bounded totality. Negative truths and absence-talk should be handled with special care.

Where an absence is indexed to the kind of thing involved and to what it should or could sustain, the archive should often prefer **privation** over brute negative-fact ontology. Where an omission is explanatory, the archive should ask what process was blocked, interrupted, or failed to occur.

The fuller treatment now lives in `13-truthmakers-absences-and-privation.md`, and the broader truth profile now lives in `57-truth-correspondence-and-reality-answerability.md`.

### 11. Properties and kinds are real enough for explanation, but sparse enough for discipline

The archive should now say explicitly that repeatable form- and power-profiles are ontologically serious, but that it need not promote every successful predicate to a heavyweight universal. The current default is a **sparse immanent realism**:

- treat some properties or forms as genuinely repeatable across instances,
- allow particularized feature-instances where a case is best understood trope-wise,
- and treat kinds as ontologically serious when they stabilize explanation, induction, modal expectation, or persistence conditions.

This keeps the synthesis away from two opposite excesses: a plenitude of abstract universals on one side, and a nominalism too thin to explain real similarity, lawfulness, and kind-sensitive persistence on the other.

The fuller treatment now lives in `14-properties-universals-tropes-and-kinds.md`, now further sharpened by the archive's grain discipline around determinables and determinates.

### 12. Essence disciplines modality

The archive should now say more clearly that metaphysical possibility is not exhausted by bare conceivability and not fully explained by possible-worlds notation alone. What a thing **is** helps determine what it can be, become, or survive.

The current default is therefore:

- look first to **essence, form, powers, and structure** when explaining metaphysical necessity or impossibility,
- distinguish that from what is merely nomically ruled out by the actual laws,
- and treat possible worlds primarily as a representational framework unless a heavier ontology is independently earned.

This keeps the synthesis compatible with modal reasoning while preventing world-talk from silently doing ontological work it has not yet earned.

The fuller treatment now lives in `15-essence-possibility-and-necessity.md`.


### 13. Category discipline comes before many downstream disputes

The archive should now say explicitly that many metaphysical disputes are misframed because they ask first whether something is real, basic, or reducible before asking what ontological category is at issue.

The current default is to distinguish at least:

- **objects / individuals**,
- **events / processes**,
- **properties / powers**,
- **relations / structures**,
- **facts / states of affairs**,
- **kinds / forms / roles**.

This does **not** commit the archive to one timeless final table of categories. It commits the archive only to refusing category slippage. Objects are not automatically facts, events are not automatically processes of objects only, relations are not automatically reducible to intrinsic profiles, and kinds are not automatically identical with universals or predicates.

The fuller treatment now lives in `16-category-discipline-objects-events-facts-relations.md`.

### 14. Parts, wholes, and boundaries need their own discipline

The archive should now say explicitly that **composition is real but not sufficient**. Knowing what parts are present does not yet tell us whether we have:

- a mere sum,
- an integrated whole,
- a constituted individual,
- or a bounded system with its own explanatory profile.

The current default is therefore:

- treat **parthood** as ontologically serious,
- treat **integration** as an additional question beyond part-listing,
- treat **boundary type** as domain-sensitive rather than uniform,
- and refuse the shortcut from **vagueness** or **co-location** to **unreality** or **identity**.

This matters because organisms, artifacts, clouds, storms, and institutions can all survive part turnover differently, and because some are open systems with real but permeable boundaries.

The fuller treatment now lives in `17-mereology-composition-and-boundaries.md`.

### 15. Causation is real but not the whole metaphysical story

The archive should treat causation as **objective worldly difference-making realized in productive, preventive, sustaining, or interrupted processes**. That means the archive should resist both thin regularity-only views and the temptation to reduce causation to intervention-friendly modeling. Counterfactuals and interventions remain indispensable **tests** of causal structure, but they should track rather than replace a metaphysics of powers, process, and structure.

The archive should also continue to distinguish causation from:

- **grounding** — constitutive in-virtue-of order,
- **constitution** — coincidence without identity in some cases,
- **realization** — implementation/dependence by lower-level bases.

A proposal that says only that a factor “explains” an outcome is incomplete until it says whether the explanation is causal, grounding-based, constitutive, realizational, or mixed.

### 16. Time is real, but temporal questions must be kept distinct

The archive should be realist about temporal order, duration, change, and organized becoming. But it should not collapse all time-talk into one question.

At minimum it should distinguish:

- temporal order and metric,
- persistence conditions,
- tense and presentness,
- passage or becoming,
- and level-specific asymmetry or irreversibility.

The current synthesis therefore leans toward **temporal-structure-plus-local-becoming**: it treats ordered succession and processual unfolding as real, while leaving presentism, eternalism, and growing-block views as live options rather than default commitments.


### 17. Space is real, but spatial questions must be kept distinct

The archive should be realist about region, adjacency, boundary, occupancy, and situated interaction. But it should not collapse all place-talk into one question.

At minimum it should distinguish:

- region and occupant,
- exact location and loose or distributed presence,
- overlap and co-location,
- site, locale, and relational position,
- and spatial boundary from operational reach.

The current synthesis therefore leans toward **spatial-structure-plus-locational-discipline**: it treats situated organization and objective location as real, while leaving substantival, relational, structural, and processual global pictures of space as live options rather than default commitments.

The fuller treatment now lives in `20-space-place-location-and-co-location.md`.

### 18. Existence and ontological commitment need their own discipline

The archive should now say explicitly that existential language, ontological commitment, and ontological seriousness are not all the same issue. A discourse may be true, explanatory, and reality-answerable without thereby positing a new basic category of entity; conversely, a useful paraphrase does not by itself show that the target domain was unreal.

The current default is therefore:

- keep **existence** distinct from **fundamentality**,
- keep **ontological commitment** distinct from **mere surface grammar**,
- keep **truthmaker analysis** available for hard cases without making it the first move in every case,
- and classify disputed domains as **basic**, **derivative but robust**, **thinly dependent**, or **merely representational**.

This matters because the archive already wants to treat ordinary objects, social institutions, absences, roles, boundaries, and perhaps some abstracta with different grades and modes of reality rather than with one flat yes/no existence switch.

The fuller treatment now lives in `21-being-existence-and-ontological-commitment.md`.

### 19. Teleology is real, but domain-typed

The archive should now say explicitly that purposive or functional explanation is not one flat phenomenon. A living organ, a thermostat, a courtroom, a corporation, and an acting person can all be discussed teleologically, but not in the same way.

The current default is therefore:

- treat **selected-effect function** as important where evolutionary or retention history matters,
- treat **organizational function** as important where self-maintenance, regulation, repair, or control matters,
- treat **design purpose** as important where artifacts and engineered systems are at issue,
- treat **agential ends** and **institutional roles** as real where norm-governed success and failure conditions help constitute the domain,
- and refuse both teleology-eliminativism and any quick slide from local purposiveness to a cosmic design thesis.

This matters because the archive already relies on function, malfunction, role, and directedness across biology-adjacent, agential, and social cases.

## What the archive now says about the basal level

The archive remains officially open on **what is absolutely fundamental**, but it is no longer neutral on everything:

- the basal level cannot be conceived as a mere heap without structure,
- it likely cannot be wholly inert if powers are indispensable,
- it should be hospitable to process or becoming rather than treating change as wholly derivative,
- and it must be rich enough to support local forms without making medium-scale reality illusory.

So the archive remains undecided between monism, pluralism, substance-first, process-first, and structure-first baselines, but it rejects a **featureless base**.

Its current lean is modestly positive:

> **If there is a fundamental layer, it is more likely to consist in structured powers-in-process than in bare inert items.**

That claim is provisional and defeasible. It is meant to guide comparison, not to close inquiry.

## Ontological picture

The synthesis currently proposes this layered image:

1. **Basal reality** — whatever is least grounded or most ontologically basic.
2. **Deep modal-structural constraints** — the patterns, laws, powers, or structures that constrain what basal reality can sustain.
3. **Stabilized individuals** — objects, organisms, artifacts, and systems that persist through organized process.
4. **Emergent and organized levels** — biological, mental, social, normative, and perhaps mathematical domains whose reality depends on, but is not exhausted by, lower-level description.
5. **Cross-level linkages** — supervenience, realization, constitution, mechanistic decomposition, scale-bridging explanation, and domain-relative autonomy connecting those levels without forcing a single universal ladder.

## Explanatory grammar

The archive should distinguish at least eight kinds of explanation:

- **material/compositional** — what something is made from,
- **formal/constitutional** — the organization by which it is the kind of thing it is,
- **realizational** — what lower-level configuration implements or sustains a type or role,
- **efficient/causal** — what brings it about or changes it,
- **grounding/constitutive-order** — in virtue of what it obtains,
- **teleological/final** — what an organized trait, process, role, or agent is for, aims at, or counts as succeeding in,
- **temporal** — when order, duration, asymmetry, or passage is doing explanatory work,
- **spatial** — when region, boundary, adjacency, overlap, or operational siting is doing explanatory work,
- **relational / structural** — when order, direction, position in a nexus, or constitutive linkage is doing explanatory work,
- **cross-level / interlevel** — when the key issue is supervenience, constitution, realization, reduction, scale-bridging, emergence, or top-down constraint across organized strata.
- **architectural** — when the key issue is hierarchy, bounded descent, feedback, reciprocity, coherentist pressure, or the difference between vicious and non-vicious loops.

A healthy metaphysics does not force these into one slot. Supervenience claims can be useful screening devices within this grammar, but they do not by themselves decide whether the deeper linkage is realizational, grounding-based, constitutive, causal, or merely modal.

It should also resist shifting silently between ontological categories while giving those explanations. What explains an object's persistence, a process's continuation, a fact's obtaining, or a relation's holding need not be one and the same style of answer.

## Objects, laws, and social reality

### Objects

Ordinary objects are usually best treated neither as metaphysically primitive lumps nor as convenient fictions. Many are **stabilized organizations** whose persistence conditions depend on form and process, not on numerical sameness of matter.

### Laws

The archive currently favors a hybrid posture: laws are not mere accidental summaries, but neither need they be treated as free-standing commandments. They are best understood as articulations of deep modal structure, plausibly intertwined with powers and forms.

### Social reality

Institutions, offices, money, and legal statuses are real when they are grounded in organized practices, roles, recognition conditions, enforcement structures, and often records or infrastructures that generate stable normative and causal consequences. Their dependence does not make them unreal.

The archive should now say this more precisely: social reality is often **derivative but robust**, and its dependence is usually **mixed** rather than flatly one-relation-only. Some cases depend more on collective uptake; others on legal codification, documents, offices, databases, material support, or platform mediation. The fuller treatment now lives in `37-social-ontology-status-functions-and-institutional-reality.md`.

## Advantages

### Handles persistence without stasis

A river, flame, organism, or mind can persist without being composed of numerically identical matter at every moment.

### Handles dependence without elimination

Higher-level entities can be grounded yet still explanatory.

### Handles modality without mystery-lite

Powers and laws together provide a more credible modal picture than bare regularity alone.

### Handles structure without abstraction drift

Relational organization becomes constitutive without requiring that concrete entities disappear.

### Handles plural domains without ontological chaos

Biological, mental, and social reality can be treated as real under discipline rather than either dismissed or inflated.

- **pure formalism** that cannot say what is organized,
