# Powers, Dispositions, Abilities, and Manifestation

## Why this file exists

The archive has long treated **power** as one of its five kernel operators, but until now it still lacked one direct file saying what a power is supposed to be, how powers differ from mere currently manifested states, how **dispositions** relate to **abilities**, what it means for a power to be blocked, masked, or never exercised, and why power-talk is not just a compressed way of speaking about laws, conditionals, or observed regularities.

That omission matters because the archive already risks six opposite mistakes:

- treating things as nothing more than what they are doing right now,
- treating every power as a simple conditional from stimulus to manifestation,
- treating dispositions, abilities, affordances, authorities, and susceptibilities as one flat kind,
- treating powers as identical to their material bases or realizers without remainder,
- treating failure to manifest as proof that no power was there,
- or treating every appeal to powers as if it had already settled laws, causation, modality, and agency all at once.

This file gives the archive a compact **power profile** so future revisions can say whether a case is chiefly about a disposition, an ability, an affordance, a susceptibility, an authority or standing power, an active power, a passive power, or some mixed modal capacity.

## Working default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Powers are real modal features of things, agents, and organized systems: dispositions are one important subtype of power, abilities another, and possession of a power is not identical either to its present manifestation or to a simple conditional description of what would happen in one stylized circumstance.**

This fits the archive's wider commitments:

- **form** still matters because what a thing is helps determine what powers it has,
- **process** still matters because many powers are exercised, sustained, acquired, masked, exhausted, or developed through time,
- **structure** still matters because some powers depend on relational or organizational position rather than isolated intrinsic microprofile alone,
- **grounding** still matters because a power can be real while still being grounded, realized, or materially based,
- and **laws** still matter because lawfulness often articulates how powers systematically manifest, combine, or constrain one another rather than replacing powers altogether.

## Core distinctions

### 1. A power is not just an occurrent state

A glass can be fragile while unbroken. A person can know how to swim while asleep. A court can have authority while not currently issuing a ruling. A cell can have a repair capacity while not currently repairing.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **occurrent state** — what is happening now,
- **standing power** — what the bearer can do, undergo, sustain, or make possible,
- **readiness or specific opportunity** — a context in which manifestation is presently available,
- and **manifestation** — the exercise or display of the power.

Without this distinction the archive will keep collapsing the modal profile of reality into a snapshot ontology of what is presently actual.

### 2. Dispositions are not the whole family of powers

The archive should distinguish at least:

- **disposition** — a tendency or susceptibility toward characteristic manifestations,
- **ability** — an agent-linked power relating the bearer to action,
- **affordance** — an environment- or object-relative possibility for use or uptake,
- **susceptibility** — a passive openness to being affected,
- **active power** — a capacity to bring about, initiate, or sustain change,
- **standing authority or normative power** — a role- or rule-governed capacity to alter statuses, permissions, or obligations,
- and **organizational capacity** — a system-level power borne by a coordinated whole rather than a single local component considered in isolation.

Otherwise the archive will keep forcing biological capacities, skills, institutional authorities, and brittle tendencies into one model built for fragile glasses.

### 3. Possessing a power is not the same as having an immediate opportunity to manifest it

A trained pianist without a piano may retain the ability to play. A dormant seed may retain a developmental power without current exercise. A legal office may retain authority even when conditions for valid exercise are absent.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **general power** — the standing capacity retained across many contexts,
- **specific power** — the standing capacity together with enabling conditions close enough for immediate exercise,
- **latent or dormant power** — a retained capacity whose exercise currently depends on missing triggers or restored background conditions,
- and **lost power** — a capacity no longer possessed at all.

Otherwise the archive will keep sliding from lack of opportunity to lack of power.

### 4. A power is not automatically identical to its basis or realizer

Some powers may be grounded in microstructural features, some in organized form, some in relational placement, and some in social or legal structure. But the archive should not assume in advance that talk of a power is exhausted by naming one lower-level basis.

So future revisions should distinguish:

- **power-bearer** — the thing, agent, or organized system that has the power,
- **basis or realizer** — what materially, structurally, or organizationally underwrites it,
- **partner or field condition** — what it depends on in the surrounding environment,
- and **manifestation profile** — what the power is a power toward or against.

This matters because fragility, metabolism, authority, memory, and navigational competence may all have different basis stories.

### 5. Powers are not exhausted by simple stimulus-manifestation conditionals

The archive should now say explicitly that naïve conditional analyses are too thin.

Finks, maskers, mimickers, blockers, and enabling conditions show why. A thing may have a power that fails to manifest because something masks it. A thing may lack a power even though an interfering factor would make it look as if the manifestation would occur anyway. A power may disappear exactly when the triggering condition arrives. And many powers are multi-track or context-sensitive rather than tied to one simple trigger-manifestation pair.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **trigger or stimulus condition**,
- **enabling background**,
- **masking or antidoting factor**,
- **finking or power-removing factor**,
- **mimicking factor**,
- and **multi-track manifestation range**.

Otherwise the archive will keep mistaking modal structure for a too-neat counterfactual template.

### 6. Power-talk should be typed by bearer and domain

The archive should distinguish powers borne by:

- **material objects**,
- **organisms**,
- **organs or mechanisms**,
- **persons and agents**,
- **institutions or offices**,
- **artifacts and interfaces**,
- and **distributed or organized systems**.

These differ in:

- whether the power is intrinsic, relational, or organizational,
- what counts as normal exercise,
- what sustains the power through time,
- what counts as loss or impairment,
- and what sort of manifestation belongs to it.

### 7. Powers do not automatically settle laws or causation

Power-talk is indispensable in this archive, but it should not become an empire-word. A power is not yet:

- a law,
- a causal interaction token,
- a complete explanation,
- or a proof of metaphysical fundamentality.

Powers help explain why things can act, react, resist, develop, fail, or sustain patterned outcomes. But laws, causes, structures, grounds, and processes still do distinct work.

### 8. Power-realism does not force one single metaphysics of properties

The archive should keep apart:

- **categorical-base views**,
- **mixed categorical/dispositional views**,
- **dispositional essentialist or power-first views**,
- and **typed pluralist views** in which different domains may support different power-basis stories.

This matters because the archive wants powers to be ontologically serious without prematurely deciding that every sparse property is purely dispositional, or that every power is fundamental.

## Four live options

### 1. Deflationary conditionalism

**Picture:** power-talk is shorthand for suitable counterfactual or conditional facts.

**What it gets right:**
- manifestation conditions matter,
- and power ascriptions are tightly linked to modal claims.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- finks, maskers, mimickers, and context-sensitive powers make the simple model too thin,
- and it underdescribes enduring capacities that outlast missing opportunities for exercise.

### 2. Categorical-base reductionism

**Picture:** apparent powers are fully grounded in non-dispositional bases plus laws; power-talk is derivative convenience.

**What it gets right:**
- powers often do depend on realizers or bases,
- and one should not mystify dispositions.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it makes modality too external,
- it risks flattening abilities, authorities, and organizational capacities into descriptions of inert bases,
- and it underdescribes what things can do in virtue of what they are.

### 3. Global power monism

**Picture:** all real properties are powers or are dispositional through and through.

**What it gets right:**
- it takes modality seriously,
- and it resists a lawless mosaic picture.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it can overgeneralize from good anti-Humean motivations,
- and it may force every domain into one thick theory of property nature before the archive has earned that verdict.

### 4. Typed power realism

**Picture:** powers are real and indispensable, but they come in kinds; dispositions, abilities, susceptibilities, affordances, authorities, and organizational capacities should be distinguished; and powers may be intrinsic, relational, or organizationally realized depending on domain.

**What it gets right:**
- it preserves the modal seriousness of capacities,
- it fits agency, life, mechanism, and institutional cases better than fragile-object models alone,
- and it keeps power-talk from swallowing laws, causation, or grounding whole.

**What it risks if left alone:**
- if too permissive, everything useful becomes a “power”,
- if too abstract, power-talk becomes another atmosphere word,
- and it still needs discipline about basis, trigger, manifestation, masking, and loss.

## Current archive answer

The archive should adopt a **typed power realism**:

> **Powers are real modal features of bearers and organized wholes; dispositions are one subtype of power, abilities another; powers may be intrinsic, relational, or organizational; and a power should not be identified either with one simple counterfactual or with its present manifestation alone.**

In compressed form:

> **What something can do, undergo, sustain, or make possible is part of what it is.**

This does not mean:

- every predicate names a real power,
- every power is fundamental,
- every power lacks a basis,
- or every law can be read straight off one isolated bearer.

It means only that the archive should stop using power-language as if all capacities were one flat kind or as if simple conditional behavior exhausted them.

## Archive commitments

### 1. The archive should distinguish power, disposition, ability, affordance, susceptibility, and authority

These are related but not interchangeable. Future revisions should state which target they are discussing before importing conclusions about manifestation, responsibility, or law.

### 2. Power-ascriptions must separate possession from exercise

The archive should not infer that because a power is unmanifested it is unreal, and it should not infer that one lucky manifestation proves the enduring possession of a power.

### 3. Finks, maskers, blockers, and mimickers are not edge curiosities

They are among the best tests for whether a proposed account of powers is too thin. Future revisions should use them whenever a simple conditional analysis starts looking too easy.

### 4. Some powers are organizationally borne

The archive should allow that a cell, nervous system, corporation, or technical platform can bear capacities that are not well described as the intrinsic power of one isolated micro-part. This does not force strong emergence, but it does require typed organizational realism.

### 5. Active and passive powers both matter

The archive should distinguish powers to act, powers to be acted upon, powers to resist, powers to regulate, powers to authorize, and powers to maintain. Otherwise agency, causation, vulnerability, and norm-governed institutional action will be flattened together.

### 6. Power-talk should be tied to manifestation range and loss conditions

Future revisions should state at least:

- **target kind** — disposition, ability, affordance, susceptibility, authority, organizational capacity, or mixed case,
- **bearer** — object, organism, organ, artifact, person, office, institution, distributed system, or mixed bearer,
- **manifestation range** — what the power is a power to do, undergo, sustain, inhibit, permit, or make possible,
- **trigger/enabling profile** — what stimulus, opportunity, partner-condition, background support, or context is required,
- **basis/realizer profile** — what materially, structurally, relationally, or organizationally underwrites it,
- **masking/finking profile** — what blocks, removes, redirects, or mimics manifestation,
- **loss/degradation profile** — what would count as impairment, dormancy, suspension, or destruction of the power,
- **dependence relation** — whether the power is intrinsic, relational, organizational, realized, grounded, constituted, or still unsettled,
- and **reality verdict** — fundamental power, derivative but robust capacity, thin role-relative power, merely predictive gloss, or still unsettled.

### 7. Powers should constrain modality, causation, agency, and teleology without replacing them

A good revision should say what a thing can do or undergo, but also whether the relevant question is one of law, cause, organization, end-directedness, or action. Power-talk does not eliminate those distinctions.

### 8. Powers should constrain reduction debates

When a proposed reduction leaves the relevant capacities unexplained, mislocates their bearers, or confuses general abilities with immediately exercisable ones, the archive should treat the reduction as incomplete even if some lower-level basis story has been given.

## Compression rule

Future revisions should avoid using words like **power**, **capacity**, **disposition**, **ability**, **can**, **capable of**, **susceptible to**, **liable to**, **authority**, **competence**, or **tendency** unless at least three of the following are specified:

- bearer,
- manifestation range,
- trigger or enabling conditions,
- basis or realizer,
- masking or blocking conditions,
- loss conditions,
- or domain type.

Otherwise power-talk will become a metaphysical compliment word rather than a disciplined claim.

## What this file changes

This file does not add a sixth kernel operator. It sharpens one the archive already had.

It therefore changes how several existing regions should now be read:

- **laws** should not absorb powers,
- **causation** should not be reduced to mere event-sequence when capacity and inhibition matter,
- **potentiality** should not be used as if every potency had one simple trigger-condition,
- **agency** should distinguish ability from mere disposition,
- **teleology** should distinguish what a thing can do from what it is for,
- **life** should distinguish organismic capacities from artifact functions,
- **normativity** should distinguish standing authority from brute causal influence,
- and **organization** should distinguish local part-powers from whole-system capacities.

So this file should be read as another bridge-file. It says how one of the archive's original kernel operators should now be used with enough precision to support the rest of the archive rather than merely hover over it.
