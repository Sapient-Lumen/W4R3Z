# Identity, Individuality, and Haecceity

## Why this file exists

The archive already talks about:

- **unity and persistence**,
- **substances and bearers**,
- **properties, repeatables, and kinds**,
- **relations and structure**,
- **vagueness and borderline cases**,
- and **possible-worlds / essence / modality**.

But it still lacks one direct rule for a pressure point running through all of them:

- what **numerical identity** is supposed to amount to,
- how identity differs from **qualitative similarity** or indiscernibility,
- what makes something an **individual** rather than merely an instance of a type,
- whether some cases need a **haecceity** or thisness-like principle,
- and when the archive should instead explain individuality through kind, structure, relation, history, or process.

Without that discipline, the archive risks sliding among:

1. persistence talk that silently assumes individuality is already solved,
2. bundle or trope talk that underdescribes what makes one individual this individual,
3. substance talk that posits bearers without explaining individuation,
4. structural talk that makes all individuality look relationally derivative,
5. and modal talk that slides too quickly from de re possibility to transworld thisness.

A compact non-skeptical, non-reductive metaphysics needs a better rule-set than that.

## Core claim

The archive should adopt **typed individuality realism with anti-haecceity default and local exceptions**.

Compressed:

> **Identity is numerical sameness, not mere qualitative matching; individuality must usually be earned through kind, structure, relation, history, or organized continuity; and haecceity-style thisness should be treated as a pressure-test posit rather than the default explanation of individuation.**

This means:

- do **not** collapse numerical identity into qualitative similarity,
- do **not** assume every individual needs a primitive thisness,
- do **not** assume all individuality is exhausted by monadic qualitative profiles,
- and do **prefer** typed explanations of individuation that say what makes this entity one and the same across relevant contrast classes.

## Five distinctions the archive now needs

### 1. Numerical identity vs qualitative similarity

Two things can be highly alike without being one thing, and one thing can change qualitatively without ceasing to be itself.

This matters because metaphysical arguments often slide between:

- exact sameness of individual,
- close resemblance,
- indiscernibility relative to a language or theory,
- and sameness under a sortal or role.

The archive should treat **numerical identity** as its own issue.

### 2. Identity vs individuality

A theory can say what identity is formally while still leaving open what makes something an **individual** in the first place.

This matters because some disputes are really about:

- what individuates a bearer,
- what makes two candidates distinct,
- whether individuality is primitive or grounded,
- and whether the domain even contains individuals in the strong sense.

### 3. Individuality vs haecceity

A case may require an account of why there are two numerically distinct but qualitatively similar entities without requiring a heavyweight ontology of primitive thisnesses.

This matters because:

- kind,
- matter,
- place,
- relation,
- history,
- processual continuity,
- or structural position

may do enough individuating work in many domains.

The archive should therefore keep **haecceity** live, but non-default.

### 4. Sortal-relative criteria vs absolute identity talk

Many practical criteria of counting and reidentification are kind-sensitive.

This matters because organisms, artifacts, persons, waves, institutions, and particles may not all be individuated in the same way. The archive should therefore ask both:

- what makes identity possible here,
- and under what kind or sortal the identity question is being asked.

### 5. Qualitative, relational, and historical individuation

Some entities are best individuated primarily by:

- intrinsic qualitative profile,
- relational position,
- causal-historical continuity,
- spatiotemporal trajectory,
- organizational continuity,
- or some mixed basis.

The archive should not search for one flat answer before local diagnosis.

## Archive default

The current default is:

1. **treat numerical identity as distinct from qualitative similarity**;
2. **prefer kind-sensitive and domain-typed individuation stories**;
3. **allow relational, historical, and processual individuation where the domain earns it**;
4. **treat haecceity as a pressure-test posit rather than the automatic answer**;
5. **keep primitive thisness live only where leaner individuation stories fail repeatedly**.

Compressed again:

> **Individuals are usually best understood as this individual in virtue of a typed package of form, organization, relation, history, and persistence profile, not in virtue of an unexplained primitive thisness added by default.**

## What the archive should now say

### 1. Identity claims should state their contrast class

Before asking whether x at t1 is identical with y at t2, the archive should state:

- what sort of entity is at issue,
- what rivals or alternatives are being contrasted,
- and what changes are allowed by the relevant persistence profile.

### 2. Indiscernibility is evidential, not automatically decisive

Qualitative indiscernibility can create pressure for identity, but it does not by itself settle every case. Conversely, qualitative difference need not show non-identity if the difference is merely temporal, aspectual, or role-relative.

### 3. Haecceities should earn their keep

A theory may posit primitive thisnesses, transworld individuals, or irreducible identity facts, but it must say what explanatory job that posit performs that cannot be done by:

- sortal criteria,
- spatiotemporal or causal continuity,
- structural or relational position,
- organization and form,
- or a domain-typed individuality story.

If the answer is only “otherwise this individual would not be this one,” the posit is under-motivated.

### 4. Structural and relational individuality should not be overextended

Some domains may genuinely involve relational individuality. But the archive should resist the lazy inference:

- relations are real,
- therefore individuals are nothing but nodes,
- therefore all individuality is purely positional.

That inference is too fast.

### 5. Bundle-style individuality should not be overtrusted

A bundle of properties or tropes may describe an individual well while still leaving open:

- why there is one bundle,
- why the bundle persists,
- and what makes numerically distinct but similar bundles distinct.

### 6. Identity pressure differs across domains

For example:

- **organisms** may be individuated largely by organized life-process continuity,
- **artifacts** by design profile, continuity, and function-sensitive history,
- **persons** by bodily, agential, mnemonic, normative, and narrative integration together,
- **institutions** by role structure, rule-governed continuity, and recognized position,
- **fields or waves** by structured spatiotemporal profile,
- and some **fundamental physics-adjacent cases** may pressure classical individuality assumptions rather than simply confirming them.

So the archive should diagnose locally, not impose one master criterion.

### 7. Identity is not automatically fundamental

A domain can contain real individuals with robust identity conditions even if those individuals are derivative rather than fundamental.

This matters because the archive already distinguishes:

- existence,
- ontological seriousness,
- derivativeness,
- and fundamentality.

So the archive should allow **derivative but robust individuality**.

## Identity-profile rule

When a future revision uses language like **same individual**, **this very thing**, **distinct but qualitatively identical**, **haecceity**, **thisness**, **reidentification**, **indiscernibility**, **transworld identity**, **node**, **individual**, or **individuation**, it should state:

1. **what category and domain the target belongs to** — organism, artifact, person, institution, process, field, particle, event, abstractum, or something else;
2. **whether the issue is numerical identity, individuality, counting, persistence, or modal de re reference**;
3. **what is doing the individuating work** — kind, form, matter, place, relation, organization, causal history, structural position, primitive thisness, or some mixed basis;
4. **what level of qualitative similarity or indiscernibility is in play** — intrinsic, relational, theoretical, observational, or language-relative;
5. **whether haecceity is really needed** or whether a leaner typed account can do the job;
6. **what explanatory loss follows** if the case is treated as primitive thisness, mere bundle, pure structural node, or only sortal-relative bookkeeping.

If a proposal does not do this, its identity or individuality talk is under-disciplined.

## What this file is not saying

- It is **not** saying qualitative properties never individuate.
- It is **not** saying haecceities are incoherent.
- It is **not** saying all identity is merely sortal-relative.
- It is **not** saying relational individuality is unreal.
- It is **not** saying every domain must admit strong individuals.

It is only saying that the archive now needs a **typed identity-and-individuation discipline**.
