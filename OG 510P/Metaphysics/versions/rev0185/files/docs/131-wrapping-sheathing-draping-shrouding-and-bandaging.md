# Wrapping, Sheathing, Draping, Shrouding, and Bandaging

## Why this file exists

The archive now has explicit files on **attachment / anchoring / tethering / docking**, **embedding / insertion / implantation / insetting**, **enclosure / encasement / casing / housing / encapsulation**, **coating / cladding / lining / surfacing / veneering**, **hosting / lodging / carriage / guest occupancy**, **attenuation / filtering / screening / shielding**, **constitution / material constitution**, and **mereology / composition / boundaries**, but it still lacks one direct file saying what to do when a case is described as **wrapped around**, **swathed in**, **sheathed by**, **draped over**, **shrouded in**, **bandaged with**, **blanketed by**, **veiled with**, or **covered by a conformal flexible layer**.

That omission matters because the archive still risks ten opposite mistakes:

- treating every genuine **wrapping** as if it were just ordinary **coating**,
- treating every **sheath** as if it were already a rigid **casing** or full enclosure,
- treating every **drape** as if it were merely nearby rather than a real covering relation,
- treating every **bandage** or dressing as if it were numerically identical with the wound or limb it covers,
- treating every flexible or removable cover as if it were no metaphysical relation at all,
- treating every persistent wrap as if it had already become the same thing as the wrapped target,
- treating **same wrapped target** as if it meant **same wrap**,
- treating **same wrap design or material** as if it meant **same wrapped bearer**,
- ignoring the difference between **sheathing**, **draping**, **blanketing**, **shrouding**, **bandaging**, and **applied surface coating**,
- or smuggling the differences among **wrapping**, **sheathing**, **draping**, **shrouding**, **covering**, **blanketing**, and **bandaging** into neighboring files without typing them.

This file gives the archive a compact **wrapping / sheathing / draping / shrouding / bandaging profile** so future revisions can say whether a case is chiefly about a conformal protective wrap, a functional sheath, a sterile drape, a temporary shroud, a therapeutic dressing or bandage, or stronger incorporation only in harder cases.

## Compressed picture

> **Something can be genuinely covered around, over, or across by another thing without thereby becoming identical with it.**

And more carefully:

> **Wrapping, sheathing, draping, shrouding, and bandaging are typed ways in which one target covers, conforms around, lies over, or spans across another target so as to protect, conceal, isolate, support, compress, thermally regulate, keep sterile, or manage exposure without that alone settling identity, constitution, or undifferentiated fusion.**

## Why the default view should absorb this operator-family

The archive's working view already says:

- **process** matters, because wraps are applied, fitted, draped, tightened, loosened, removed, replaced, rewrapped, torn, or shifted across time,
- **form and organization** matter, because not every sheath, drape, blanket, shroud, or bandage plays the same role in the kind-profile of the larger target,
- **powers and functions** matter, because covers can insulate, shield, compress, immobilize, sterilize, conceal, or maintain interface conditions,
- **dependence pluralism** matters, because wrapping, coating, enclosure, hosting, attachment, constitution, and realization should not be collapsed,
- **sortals** matter, because a sheathed cable, a draped patient, a blanketed spacecraft, and a bandaged wound are not one flat kind of case,
- **boundary discipline** matters, because flexible coverings can preserve, obscure, soften, or temporarily regulate a boundary without erasing it,
- and **artifact-risk discipline** matters, because labels such as *wrapped*, *sheathed*, *covered*, *shrouded*, *bandaged*, or *draped* often overstate metaphysical intimacy.

So the default view should absorb covering-language without flattening it.

## What this operator-family is for

This operator-family is for cases where one target is **covered by, wrapped around, laid over, sheathed in, or draped across another target under a typed covering relation**.

The relation may involve:

- a **spacecraft wrapped in thermal blankets**,
- a **cable or conduit protected by a sheath**,
- a **patient or instrument field covered by a sterile drape**,
- a **body part secured by a bandage or dressing**,
- a **payload protected by a removable shroud**,
- or a **temporary wrap that conforms around a target without becoming its constitutive skin**.

The key question is not simply whether one thing is *on* another, but **what sort of covering relation is doing the work and whether it carries any stronger claim than conformal cover, flexible sheath, temporary drape, protective shroud, or therapeutic wrapping**.

## Why the archive needs it

### 1. Wrapping is not the same as coating

A wrap can cover a target closely and perform serious protective work without being an applied surface layer integrated into the surface itself.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **conformal cover or wrap**,
- **applied coating or film**,
- **deeper embedding into the substrate**,
- **rigid enclosure or casing**,
- and **identity only where separately argued**.

Otherwise it will keep sliding from **wrapped in** into either **coated with** or **part of** without noticing the intermediate metaphysical work done by flexible cover-relations.

### 2. Sheathing is not the same as full enclosure

A sheath often protects, insulates, or bundles a target while tracking its shape or path. That is not automatically the same as being fully cased, housed, or encapsulated.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **sheath-like protective cover**,
- **rigid or shell-like enclosure**,
- **surface-adjacent coat or cladding**,
- **mere loose proximity**,
- and **larger assemblies only where that is the real target**.

Otherwise it will keep flattening **sheathed by** into **encased in**.

### 3. Draping is not the same as hosting or occupancy

A drape can cover a field, body, or instrument area without the covered target being a guest, tenant, or hosted occupant inside an independent container.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **covering over or across**,
- **hosting or lodging within**,
- **ordinary attachment at a point or connector**,
- **shielding or screening from exposure**,
- and **mixed cases only where several relations genuinely coincide**.

Otherwise it will keep confusing *under a drape* with *inside a host*.

### 4. Same wrapped target does not mean same wrap

One wound, patient, cable, instrument table, or spacecraft can remain the same bearer while its dressing, drape, sheath, shroud, or blanket is changed, tightened, replaced, sterilized, or removed.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **same target under changed cover**,
- **same patient under changed drape**,
- **same cable under replaced sheath**,
- **same wound under renewed dressing**,
- and **same covering relation only when that relation itself is what matters**.

Otherwise it will keep letting target continuity masquerade as cover continuity.

### 5. Same wrap does not mean same wrapped target

One drape type, blanket design, dressing product, or sheath material can be used across many numerically distinct bearers.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **same cover type across many targets**,
- **same wrap design across many payloads or devices**,
- **same dressing stock across many wounds**,
- **same sheath material across many cables**,
- and **same covered target only where a single bearer actually persists**.

Otherwise it will keep letting repeatable cover design masquerade as bearer continuity.

### 6. Covering function does not automatically settle bearer identity

A wrap, sheath, or drape can be indispensable to sterile technique, thermal management, or mechanical protection without being identical with the bearer it covers.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **same bearer under an indispensable cover**,
- **same cover serving one bearer**,
- **same larger assembly containing bearer and cover**,
- **stronger constitutive inclusion only where kind and persistence criteria require it**,
- and **identity only where separately earned**.

Otherwise it will keep letting protective indispensability do more metaphysical work than it has earned.

### 7. Temporary or removable covering is still a real relation

Because wraps are often removable, flexible, or episodic, they can be mistaken for merely practical or epistemically insignificant features. That is a mistake.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **temporary but real covering relations**,
- **episodic but repeated draping practices**,
- **replaceable protective blankets**,
- **therapeutic or sterile wraps with time-limited roles**,
- and **mere accidental adjacency only where no genuine cover-relation exists**.

Otherwise it will keep undercounting one major family of real but non-identitarian dependence relations.

## Typology of wrap-like cases

### A. Protective wraps, blankets, and flexible outer covers

The key feature is that one cover conforms around or over a target to regulate temperature, exposure, contamination, or minor impact without becoming the target itself.

**Examples:** spacecraft thermal blankets; insulating wraps; protective cloth covers; temporary weather covers.

Default verdict: usually **same target under a protective wrap**.

### B. Sheaths and sleeve-like protective coverings

The key feature is a continuous or semi-continuous cover that follows the contour or route of a cable, conduit, catheter, blade, or similar target.

**Examples:** cable sheath; sleeve over tubing; protective sheath around a line or instrument.

Default verdict: usually **same target under a sheath**, not automatic casing or identity.

### C. Drapes and sterile over-covers

The key feature is a cover laid over, across, or around a person, site, or field to establish protection, isolation, or controlled exposure.

**Examples:** sterile full body drape; operative field drape; table drape; dust cover over a prepared field.

Default verdict: usually **same target under a drape**.

### D. Shrouds and concealment / transit covers

The key feature is a temporary cover used for transport, concealment, protection during passage, or controlled exposure before later removal.

**Examples:** launch shroud; temporary machine shroud; body shroud; cover removed before final operation.

Default verdict: usually **same target under a temporary shroud**.

### E. Bandages, dressings, and therapeutic wraps

The key feature is a cover applied to protect, absorb, compress, support, immobilize, or manage healing conditions around a wound, limb, or body region.

**Examples:** wound dressing; elastic bandage; compression wrap; protective postoperative wrap.

Default verdict: usually **same wound or limb under a therapeutic cover**, not identity with the cover.

### F. Mixed or harder cases

Some cases blend several relations at once: a sheath that is also bonded like a coating, a wrap that later hardens into a shell, a dressing that becomes semi-integrated with tissue, or a shroud that is part of a larger transport assembly.

Default verdict: **type the case more carefully rather than collapsing it into one flat relation**.

## Neighboring distinctions the archive should keep explicit

### Wrapping vs coating

A coating is usually an applied layer on the surface; a wrap is usually a cover placed around or over the target as a distinguishable flexible or removable item.

### Sheathing vs enclosure

A sheath may follow or protect a target without becoming a full independent casing or housing.

### Draping vs hosting

Something can be under a drape without being inside a host or container.

### Bandaging vs constitution

A therapeutic wrap can be indispensable to healing or support without being identical with the tissue or bearer it covers.

### Shrouding vs identity change

A target can be shrouded, veiled, or covered in transit while remaining the same target throughout.

### Wrapping vs mere proximity

Not every nearby cloth, film, or cover counts. The cover must genuinely span, conform around, or protect the target in the relevant way.

## Failure modes this file should prevent

- Do **not** infer identity from every case of being **wrapped in** or **bandaged with**.
- Do **not** infer coating from every close-fitting cover.
- Do **not** infer full enclosure from every **sheath** or **shroud**.
- Do **not** infer hosting from every case of being **under a drape**.
- Do **not** infer same wrap from same wrapped target.
- Do **not** infer same target from same wrap design or stock.
- Do **not** infer irrelevance from a wrap merely because it is removable.
- Do **not** infer one flat relation from **wrapped with**, **sheathed by**, **draped over**, **shrouded in**, **blanketed by**, or **bandaged with**.

## Default diagnostic questions

When a case is described as **wrapped with**, **sheathed by**, **draped over**, **shrouded in**, **blanketed by**, **bandaged with**, **covered by**, or **swathed in**, ask:

1. What exactly is the **target** — one cable, spacecraft, wound, limb, patient, instrument field, payload, or larger assembly?
2. What exactly is the **cover** — one blanket, sheath, drape, shroud, dressing, sleeve, or wrap?
3. Is the main role **protection**, **sterility**, **compression**, **concealment**, **thermal regulation**, **bundling**, **isolation**, or some mixture?
4. Is the relation chiefly **conformal wrapping**, **sleeve-like sheathing**, **laid-over draping**, **temporary shrouding**, **therapeutic bandaging**, or a mixed harder case?
5. Can the target remain the same while the cover changes?
6. Can the cover-type remain the same while the target changes?
7. Is the cover **removable**, **replaceable**, **single-use**, **reusable**, **tightened**, **loosened**, **sterile**, **therapeutic**, or **sacrificial**?
8. Is the case really about one target under one cover, or about a larger whole containing cover and target as distinguishable elements?
9. What continuity claim is actually being made — same target, same cover, same cover-target relation, same larger assembly, or only same label?
10. Which neighboring operator is most likely to be confused with this one — **coating**, **enclosure**, **hosting**, **attachment**, **shielding**, or **constitution**?

## Compressed rule of thumb

When a thing is said to be **wrapped**, **sheathed**, **draped**, **shrouded**, or **bandaged**, the archive should default to:

1. **identify the target and the covering separately**,
2. **ask whether the cover is chiefly conformal, sleeve-like, laid-over, temporary-transit, or therapeutic**,
3. **separate target continuity from cover continuity**,
4. **separate covering relations from coating, enclosure, hosting, and constitution**,
5. and **treat stronger parthood, constitution, or identity claims as further achievements, not automatic consequences of cover-language**.
