# Indispensability, Representation, and Ontological Promotion

## Why this file exists

The archive already had files on **representation**, **truth**, **structure**, **ontological status**, **objectivity**, and **ontological commitment**. But it still lacked one direct file saying what the archive means when it moves from:

- explanatory success,
- model indispensability,
- robust pattern-tracking,
- representational usefulness,
- or theoretical necessity

to a **realist ontological verdict**.

Without a dedicated file here, the archive risks six recurrent mistakes:

- treating **indispensable in inquiry** as if it automatically meant **literally part of the furniture of the world**,
- treating **model success** as if it automatically settled what kind of thing the model target is,
- treating **robust pattern** as if it automatically meant **autonomous entity**,
- treating **plural successful representations** as if they automatically forced anti-realism,
- treating **idealization** as if it automatically meant **fiction only**,
- or treating **ontological parsimony** as if it always required explanatory self-mutilation.

This file gives the archive a compact **promotion profile** so future revisions can say when explanatory indispensability is merely methodological, when it is evidence for derivative but robust reality, when it is evidence only for structure or organization, when it supports idealized but world-tracking posits, and when it does not support ontological promotion at all.

## Working default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Explanatory indispensability is ontological pressure, not a one-step ontology machine.**

And more specifically:

> **Promote a candidate into the ontology only when representational or explanatory success tracks stable worldly structure, power, organization, fact, or dependence better than the live alternatives, and when elimination would destroy more than convenience.**

This fits the archive's wider commitments:

- **representation** matters because models, maps, equations, records, and interfaces can disclose the world without being identical to it,
- **truth** matters because successful discourse should remain answerable to reality even when idealized,
- **ontological commitment** matters because what a theory quantifies over and what it ideologically relies on can come apart,
- **structure** matters because sometimes what is earned is a structural or modal profile rather than a full inventory of furniture,
- **status** matters because a posit may be fundamental, derivative but robust, thinly dependent, idealized but world-tracking, or merely representational,
- and **objectivity** matters because partial, interest-sensitive, and plural representations can still track an objective world.

## Core distinctions

### 1. Indispensability is not yet existence

The archive should distinguish:

- **practical indispensability** — useful for calculation, compression, administration, or coordination,
- **epistemic indispensability** — needed for inquiry, prediction, or surrogative reasoning,
- **explanatory indispensability** — doing genuine explanatory work we do not yet know how to replace,
- **ideological indispensability** — needed in the theory's conceptual structure,
- and **ontological indispensability** — needed because the world itself seems to require the candidate.

Otherwise the archive will keep sliding from “we need this in thought” to “this therefore exists” without enough intermediate discipline.

### 2. Ontological commitment is not identical with representational reliance

A theory may rely heavily on a concept, variable, model object, or formal device without being committed to each item in the same way.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **what the theory says exists**,
- **what the theory uses to say it**,
- **what the theory idealizes away from**,
- and **what the theory introduces only as representational scaffolding**.

Otherwise the archive will keep conflating ontological commitment, ideological commitment, and explanatory convenience.

### 3. Model vehicle is not model target

The archive should distinguish:

- **the representational vehicle** — graph, map, equation, simulation, diagram, fiction, dataset, interface,
- **the target system** — what the model is about,
- **the idealized surrogate** — a tractable stand-in with deliberate distortions,
- and **the ontological posit** — what, if anything, the archive should treat as real in virtue of the modeling success.

Otherwise the archive will keep reading off ontology from whatever happens to appear in the model.

### 4. Robust pattern is not automatically autonomous entityhood

The archive should distinguish:

- **noticed pattern**,
- **compressively useful pattern**,
- **world-tracking robust pattern**,
- **structure-fixing pattern**,
- **organization-sustaining pattern**,
- and **autonomous individual or kind**.

Sometimes what is earned is not a new object, but a real structure, organization, tendency, or higher-level profile.

### 5. Plural successful representations do not automatically force anti-realism

The archive should distinguish:

- **complementary plurality**,
- **aim-relative plurality**,
- **grain-relative plurality**,
- **inconsistent idealized models**,
- and **deep ontological underdetermination**.

Otherwise the archive will keep treating multiple successful models as either proof that reality is unreal or proof that every model component is real.

### 6. Idealization is not automatically fiction

The archive should distinguish:

- **deliberate simplification**,
- **controlled distortion**,
- **limit case**,
- **effective description**,
- **fictional prop with world-tracking role**,
- and **mere fiction with no external target**.

Some idealizations still latch onto real powers, structures, or dependencies; others are merely convenient stories.

### 7. Promotion is graded, not all-or-nothing

The archive should distinguish possible verdicts such as:

- **fundamental reality**,
- **derivative but robust reality**,
- **thinly dependent profile**,
- **idealized but world-tracking posit**,
- **representational scaffold**,
- and **still unsettled**.

Otherwise the archive will keep acting as if the only options were literal furniture or total elimination.

## Four live options

### 1. Hard indispensability promotionism

**Picture:** what our best indispensable theories or models cannot do without should generally be admitted into ontology.

**What it gets right:**
- explanatory indispensability is genuine evidence,
- and successful inquiry should not be severed from ontology.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it promotes too quickly from use to being,
- and it handles idealization, model plurality, and representational scaffolding too crudely.

### 2. Instrumentalist abstinence

**Picture:** explanatory and predictive usefulness tell us little or nothing about what exists; keep the ontology minimal and read most successful posits instrumentally.

**What it gets right:**
- models, fictions, and idealizations are often not literal mirrors of reality,
- and theory-use should not be naively reified.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it underdescribes why some higher-level or theoretical posits are harder to eliminate than mere convenience would predict,
- and it risks severing explanation, structure, and worldly answerability.

### 3. Pattern inflationism

**Picture:** any stable or information-rich pattern that supports prediction or compression deserves ontological promotion.

**What it gets right:**
- many real things are not simple little lumps of matter,
- and higher-level regularities, organizations, and roles often matter.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- not every stable pattern is a structure, not every structure is an entity, and not every predictive gain deserves a realist verdict.

### 4. Typed promotion realism

**Picture:** explanatory indispensability is serious but defeasible. Promotion depends on what kind of success is involved, what is being represented, what elimination would cost, and whether the best realist verdict is about entity, structure, organization, fact, power, or only idealized world-tracking representation.

**What it gets right:**
- it preserves contact between inquiry and ontology,
- it preserves derivative but robust reality where elimination would be too costly,
- and it stops the archive from reading ontology straight off its own explanatory tools.

**What it risks if left alone:**
- it can become vague unless each case states exactly what is being promoted, from what basis, and to what status.

## Archive commitments

### 1. Treat indispensability as evidence, not automatic verdict

The archive should neither dismiss explanatory indispensability nor treat it as ontological legislation.

### 2. Distinguish target from vehicle before promoting anything

Do not read ontology directly off equations, simulations, diagrams, narratives, or interface elements.

### 3. Let elimination tests do real work

Before demoting a candidate, ask what explanatory, modal, structural, organizational, or normative losses elimination would impose.

### 4. Allow realism about structure, organization, and profile where entity-talk overreaches

Sometimes what is earned is a real relation-pattern, mechanism, institution, field-profile, or causal-organizational structure rather than a new autonomous object.

### 5. Keep idealized but world-tracking posits separate from mere fiction

A posit may be idealized yet still answer to the world; idealization alone does not settle the status verdict.

### 6. Allow plural, partial, and interest-sensitive representations without forfeiting realism

Multiple models can track different aspects of one objective world without forcing either monist overreach or anti-realist retreat.

## Promotion profile rule for future revisions

Before inferring a metaphysical verdict from language like **indispensable**, **explanatory**, **best theory**, **model success**, **real pattern**, **tracks the world**, **only instrumental**, **just a model**, **idealized**, **fictional**, **must quantify over**, **ontological cost**, **representation**, **surrogate**, **map**, **simulation**, or **predictively indispensable**, future revisions should answer at least these questions:

1. **target candidate** — object, process, power, relation, structure, organization, fact, field-profile, institution, abstractum, model posit, or mixed case;
2. **indispensability type** — practical, epistemic, explanatory, ideological, ontological, or mixed indispensability;
3. **representation profile** — direct description, model, map, simulation, idealized surrogate, fiction-like vehicle, record, interface, or mixed representation;
4. **target/vehicle distinction** — what in the case is the world-target, what is the representational vehicle, and what is merely scaffolding;
5. **success profile** — prediction, explanation, control, compression, coordination, surrogative reasoning, structural fit, or mixed success;
6. **robustness profile** — stable across methods, grains, models, perspectives, interventions, or only local to one setup;
7. **elimination cost** — what would be lost if the candidate were eliminated: explanatory reach, modal articulation, organizational understanding, normative grip, predictive success, or little beyond convenience;
8. **promotion type** — entity-promotion, structure-promotion, organization-promotion, fact-promotion, power-promotion, status-only promotion, or no promotion;
9. **status verdict** — fundamental, derivative but robust, thinly dependent, idealized but world-tracking, merely representational scaffold, fictional, or still unsettled.

Otherwise the archive will keep sliding from successful representation to ontology, from indispensable concept to indispensable being, and from plural model-use to either inflation or abstinence with too little discipline.

A further archive lesson now made explicit by `61-measurement-detection-and-operationalization.md` is that **successful measurement practice** creates ontological pressure only after the archive distinguishes target, setup, proxy, operational criterion, and readout.

## Compression payoff

This file is meant to stabilize six already-live archive fault lines at once:

- **representation** without naïve reification,
- **structure** without pattern inflation,
- **status** without the literal/fictive binary,
- **objectivity** without requiring a single complete representation,
- **metaontology** without equating commitment and utility,
- and **scientific/social realism** without reading ontology straight off the current representational apparatus.

## Default archive verdict

The archive should now prefer a **typed promotion realism**:

> **Successful inquiry creates ontological pressure, but promotion must be earned case by case. What is real may be an entity, a structure, an organization, a power-profile, a fact, or an idealized but world-tracking posit; and the transition from indispensable representation to ontology should always be explicit, defeasible, and typed.**
