# Determinables, Determinates, and Explanatory Grain

## Why this file exists

The archive already talks about:

- **properties, repeatables, tropes, and kinds**,
- **causal profiles**,
- **naturalness and joint-carving**,
- **levels, scales, and cross-level explanation**,
- **vagueness and borderline cases**,
- and **potentiality, actuality, and manifestation**.

But it still lacks one direct rule for a pressure point running through all of them:

- when a claim should be made at a more **determinable** level rather than a maximally specific **determinate** level,
- when explanatory success depends on **grain** rather than on ontological depth,
- whether determinables are real enough to do explanatory work or are only shorthand for disjunctions of determinates,
- and how the archive should distinguish **coarse but objective structure** from mere imprecision, convenience, or underdescription.

Without that discipline, the archive risks sliding among:

1. property talk that treats every increase in specificity as automatically more metaphysically revealing,
2. reductionist pressure that mistakes finer grain for deeper ontology,
3. causal talk that forgets that explanations can fail by being too specific as well as too coarse,
4. naturalness talk that never asks whether the relevant carve is determinable, determinate, or scale-bound,
5. and vagueness talk that confuses borderline application with graded or ordered determinacy.

A compact non-skeptical, non-reductive metaphysics needs a cleaner rule-set than that.

## Core claim

The archive should adopt **typed determination realism with explanatory-grain discipline**.

Compressed:

> **Reality is often structured by determinable–determinate order. Some determinables are not mere verbal shortcuts: they track real ranges, ordered spaces, capacities, and explanatory profiles. But not every question is best answered at the finest determinate grain.**

This means:

- do **not** assume that maximum specificity is automatically maximum metaphysical adequacy,
- do **not** assume that every determinable is only a disjunction of determinates,
- do **prefer** the grain that best captures the relevant unity, modality, explanation, or causal proportionality,
- and do **ask** whether the target phenomenon is misdescribed when pushed either too coarsely or too finely.

## Six distinctions the archive now needs

### 1. Determinable vs determinate

A determinable is a more general property-space or specification range; a determinate is a more specific way of falling under it.

Examples:

- **color** / **red** / **scarlet**,
- **shape** / **triangular** / **isosceles triangular**,
- **mass** / **three kilograms**,
- **fragility** / **shatters under light impact within standard temperature range**.

The point is not merely taxonomic. The archive now needs to ask whether the metaphysical work is being done by the broader determinable, the narrower determinate, or a structured range between them.

### 2. Finer grain vs deeper ontology

A more specific description can be more detailed without being more fundamental.

This matters because the archive already distinguishes:

- fundamentality,
- naturalness,
- explanatory level,
- and ontological commitment.

It should now also distinguish **greater specificity** from **greater ontological depth**.

### 3. Coarse objectivity vs loose approximation

Some coarse-grained descriptions succeed because they genuinely track stable organization, powers, thresholds, or ordered similarity spaces.

That is different from:

- mere ignorance,
- lazy approximation,
- or convenient but reality-insensitive compression.

The archive should therefore allow that a determinable description can be objectively correct without being maximally specific.

### 4. Determination vs vagueness

A case can be determinable-structured without being vague.

For example, a thing can determinately have some determinable feature even when its more specific determination is not the point of the explanation.

Conversely, a borderline case can be vague without revealing a deep determinable structure.

The archive should therefore not collapse:

- determination order,
- imprecision,
- borderline application,
- and metaphysical indeterminacy.

### 5. Explanatory grain vs causal overfitting

Some explanations fail because they are too unspecific. Others fail because they overfit by naming a determinate more exact than the real explanatory profile.

A compact metaphysics therefore needs room for **proportionate explanation**:

- specific enough to track what made the difference,
- coarse enough not to smuggle in accidental detail,
- and typed enough to show whether the determinable or determinate level is doing the work.

### 6. Determinable structure vs flat predicate lists

If the archive says that properties, kinds, or powers come in ordered ranges, thresholds, or families, it should not treat those merely as flat lists of predicates.

Some domains seem to involve:

- ordered spectra,
- gradable qualities,
- thresholded capacities,
- or structured determination spaces.

That matters for naturalness, kindhood, manifestation, and levels.

## Archive default

The current default is:

1. **be realist about determinable–determinate structure where the domain earns it**;
2. **do not equate finer description with deeper ontology**;
3. **allow determinables to do explanatory and modal work when they track real ordered spaces, capacities, or thresholds**;
4. **prefer the grain that is proportionate to the explanatory target** rather than automatically choosing either the coarsest or finest vocabulary;
5. **treat vagueness, indeterminacy, and undermeasurement as different issues from determination order**, even when they interact.

Compressed again:

> **Some broader property-forms are real enough to matter, some narrower specifications are indispensable in the right cases, and metaphysical discipline consists partly in choosing the right grain rather than always the finest grain.**

## What the archive should now say

### 1. Determinables can be real without being fundamental

The archive should allow that some determinables are metaphysically serious because they track:

- stable powers,
- ordered similarity spaces,
- thresholds of manifestation,
- or projectible profiles across realizers.

This does **not** mean they are fundamental. It means they are often more than mere convenience.

### 2. Determinate inflation is a real danger

The archive should resist the move:

- “this more specific description is available,”
- therefore
- “this more specific description is what reality is really about.”

Sometimes the determinate matters. But sometimes the extra detail is accidental to the explanatory or constitutive issue.

### 3. Causal and constitutive explanation need grain discipline

When a case is causal, the archive should ask whether the relevant difference-maker is:

- a broad capacity,
- a thresholded power-profile,
- a medium-grain variable,
- or a narrow determinate mechanism.

When a case is constitutive, the archive should ask whether the higher-level feature is grounded in:

- a determinate microconfiguration,
- a determinable structural range,
- or an organized family of realizers.

### 4. Level-talk often smuggles in grain mistakes

Many disputes that look like disputes about “higher” and “lower” levels are really disputes about explanatory grain.

The archive should therefore ask whether a case is being redescribed at:

- finer or coarser resolution,
- more or less determinate specification,
- or genuinely different ontological or explanatory level.

### 5. Naturalness is partly about the right grain

Some classifications carve well because they are neither too coarse nor too fine for the world’s real organization.

So this file should now help the archive say why:

- some kinds are projectible at a medium grain,
- some variables are elite because they are proportionate rather than maximally specific,
- and some structured determinables deserve realism even where their determinates vary widely.

### 6. Determination order matters for powers and potentialities

A power may be real even when its manifestations admit many determinates.

The archive should therefore distinguish:

- a determinable capacity,
- determinable manifestation spaces,
- determinate triggering conditions,
- and determinate outcomes.

Otherwise it will keep confusing the reality of a capacity with one especially narrow way it can be specified.

## Grain-profile rule

When a future revision uses language like **more specific**, **same property at a higher/lower resolution**, **coarse-grained**, **fine-grained**, **broad capacity**, **determinable**, **determinate**, **threshold**, **range**, **scalar profile**, or **proportionate explanation**, it should state:

1. **what domain and category are at issue** — property, power, kind, relation, level, mechanism variable, threshold state, or some mixed case;
2. **whether the dispute is about determinable/determinate order, vagueness, measurement precision, explanatory grain, or ontological depth**;
3. **what the main rival grains are** and what is gained or lost at each;
4. **whether the determinable in question is doing real explanatory, modal, or projective work**, or is only shorthand;
5. **whether the determinate detail is essential, accidental, or overfitted** to the target explanation;
6. **what would break** if the case were redescribed at the wrong grain.

If a proposal does not do this, its grain talk is under-disciplined.

## Main payoff for the archive

This file should help the archive compress several older disputes:

- in `14-properties-universals-tropes-and-kinds.md`, it clarifies how repeatables may be ordered rather than flat;
- in `18-causation-production-counterfactuals-and-intervention.md`, it sharpens which variable or capacity is proportionate to the case;
- in `24-potentiality-actuality-and-actualization.md`, it distinguishes broad capacity from narrow manifestation;
- in `26-levels-scales-and-cross-level-explanation.md`, it separates level disputes from resolution disputes;
- in `27-vagueness-indeterminacy-and-borderline-ontology.md`, it stops the archive from confusing determinacy order with borderline application;
- and in `30-naturalness-joint-carving-and-ontic-selectivity.md`, it explains why some medium-grain classifications may carve better than both coarser and finer rivals.

## Failure modes to avoid

1. **Flat determinable reductionism** — every determinable is treated as a mere disjunction with no explanatory standing.
2. **Fine-grain fetishism** — the narrowest available description is always treated as metaphysically privileged.
3. **Coarse-grain complacency** — broad descriptions are allowed to float free of real structure.
4. **Vagueness confusion** — determination order is treated as if it were already a solution to borderlines or indeterminacy.
5. **Level confusion** — resolution or specificity disputes are mistaken for fundamentality disputes.

## Current stance

The archive should now lean toward this:

> **Many real domains are articulated by structured determinables and families of determinates. A non-reductive metaphysics should be prepared to treat some determinables as objectively explanatory and projectible, while still demanding that each case earn its preferred grain.**
