# Adversarial Stress Tests and Revision Protocol

This file adds a second-order discipline for the archive. `144-operator-family-map-and-triage-grid.md` asks where a candidate case belongs before a new operator is added. This file asks a harder question: **what would make the archive's own preferred diagnosis fail?**

The archive has become large enough that its danger is no longer only crude reduction. It is also **overconfident discrimination**: having many typed distinctions available can tempt the project to force every case into a well-named operator, to mistake a helpful analogy for an ontological discovery, or to promote every vivid failure mode into a new metaphysical kind. A mature revision process must therefore include adversarial tests.

The guiding idea is:

> **No operator, family, or synthesis claim is stable until it has survived hostile variation.**

A hostile variation is a controlled change to a case that removes, reverses, duplicates, masks, delays, relocates, or re-describes the feature that the archive initially treated as doing the metaphysical work. If the verdict stays fixed when it should change, the archive is under-discriminating. If the verdict changes when it should remain fixed, the archive is over-discriminating. If the verdict follows a word rather than the basis, the archive is being lexically captured.

## Why this file belongs after the triage grid

The triage grid in `144` improves routing. It tells a future revision to name the family, nearest files, basis, grain, continuity consequence, failure mode, and artifact risk before expanding. But triage can still go wrong in at least six ways:

1. **Nearest-file capture** — the first plausible neighboring file becomes the answer merely because it is available.
2. **Family capture** — the case is routed to the right family but the wrong operator within that family.
3. **Basis drift** — the stated basis changes midway through the analysis while the verdict pretends to remain stable.
4. **Grain laundering** — identity, continuity, or status is preserved at one grain and silently promoted to another.
5. **Analogy inflation** — a mechanical, biological, social, or informational analogy is treated as if it carried its full source-domain ontology.
6. **Artifact promotion** — a proxy, label, readout, model boundary, or administrative category is mistaken for the target itself.

This file is a safety layer against those failures. Its job is not to slow inquiry for its own sake. Its job is to make expansion, consolidation, and elimination more reliable.

## The adversarial posture

For every serious candidate claim, ask:

- What is the strongest neighboring diagnosis that would make this claim unnecessary?
- What is the strongest compression rule that would absorb this distinction?
- What is the strongest counterexample showing that the proposed basis is not sufficient?
- What is the strongest counterexample showing that the proposed basis is not necessary?
- What observation would make the archive demote the claim from ontology to model, proxy, convention, or heuristic?
- What would the archive say if the same vocabulary were used in a different family?
- What would the archive say if the same basis appeared under different vocabulary?

If the answer cannot be stated, the archive has not yet earned the claim.

## The revision protocol

Future revisions should pass through the following gates. A small editorial correction may pass through them lightly. A new operator, major synthesis change, or family consolidation should pass through them explicitly.

### Gate 1: Target isolation

State the target before stating the preferred operator. The target may be an object, process, relation, role, status, capacity, fact, representation, organization, path, stock, flow, boundary, interface, or mixed case.

Failure signal: the target changes while the answer is being developed. For example, a discussion that begins with the identity of an organism and ends with the continuity of a role has switched targets.

### Gate 2: Vocabulary quarantine

List the ordinary words that are pulling the analysis: same, real, part, supports, contains, guides, controls, represents, depends, causes, stores, transmits, remains, replaces, returns, opens, closes, fails, functions, or counts as.

Then prevent those words from deciding the case by themselves. Vocabulary may suggest a family. It cannot settle the basis.

Failure signal: the archive says that a case is sealing because someone calls it sealed, or says that a case is identity because people call it the same thing.

### Gate 3: Nearest-rival challenge

Name the three nearest files and give each one its best case. Do not use straw rivals. If a proposed valving case can be handled by gating, bottlenecking, or sealing, say so in the strongest form before choosing valving.

Failure signal: the proposed answer is compared only with obviously wrong alternatives.

### Gate 4: Basis lock

Identify what actually does the work: material continuity, form, organization, lawlike structure, causal power, control architecture, role assignment, record continuity, boundary maintenance, route constraint, surface interaction, load path, aperture setting, feedback, access route, or mixed basis.

Then hold that basis fixed while varying vocabulary and examples.

Failure signal: the basis is first described as function, then as material continuity, then as institutional status, while the verdict is treated as one continuous result.

### Gate 5: Grain lock

Specify the grain at which the verdict holds: token, type, stage, event, episode, mechanism, system, role, lineage, population, institution, model, measurement channel, regime, or world-level structure.

Then ask whether the verdict survives movement to a finer or coarser grain.

Failure signal: the archive proves continuity at the role level and concludes numerical identity at the token level.

### Gate 6: Controlled variation

Construct at least two hostile variants:

1. **Same vocabulary, changed basis.** The case is still described with the same word, but the underlying basis is altered.
2. **Same basis, changed vocabulary.** The underlying basis is preserved, but the ordinary description changes.

A robust diagnosis should follow the basis, not the word.

Failure signal: the verdict tracks the label rather than the metaphysical work.

### Gate 7: Defeater inventory

State what would defeat the diagnosis. Defeaters can include changed target, wrong grain, missing causal role, absent control architecture, proxy-only readout, hidden replacement, hidden leakage, hidden support relation, hidden institutional rule, or hidden observer-dependence.

Failure signal: the proposed operator has no imaginable defeaters.

### Gate 8: Continuity audit

If the case bears on persistence or sameness, state the preserving relation. It might be material continuity, formal continuity, organizational continuity, role continuity, record continuity, route continuity, load-path continuity, control-architecture continuity, lineage continuity, or no relevant continuity.

Failure signal: the archive says “same” without saying same in which respect and at which grain.

### Gate 9: Artifact-risk audit

Ask how the evidence could mislead: same label, same location, same external shape, same file name, same serial number, same model output, same administrative category, same route marker, same readout, same hardware token, or same design type.

Failure signal: a proxy is allowed to serve as a truthmaker without being tested.

### Gate 10: Update footprint

Before finalizing the revision, state what must be updated if the claim is accepted: README, start-here sequence, synthesis, method rules, open questions, source ledger, archive index, family map, manifest, and neighboring files.

Failure signal: the archive adds a new distinction without telling the control files how to use it.

## Red-team tests by family

The following stress tests are not meant to settle the archive. They are meant to prevent easy victories.

### 1. Status and category tests

Use these when a case may be sliding among object, process, fact, relation, role, status, kind, representation, or model.

Stress variants:

- Keep the same predicate true while changing the ontological category of the target.
- Keep the same practical role while changing whether the bearer is an object, institution, event, or record.
- Keep the same representation while changing the represented target.
- Keep the same target while changing the representational vehicle.

Red-team questions:

- Is the archive treating a status as an object?
- Is it treating a fact as a constituent?
- Is it treating a representation as the represented target?
- Is it treating a role-bearer as identical to the role?

A diagnosis fails if it cannot say whether the relevant reality is category-level, status-level, target-level, or representational.

### 2. Unity, identity, and continuity tests

Use these when a case involves sameness through replacement, movement, repair, copying, fission, merger, turnover, role succession, or support change.

Stress variants:

- Replace the material basis while preserving organization.
- Preserve material basis while changing organization.
- Preserve role while changing bearer.
- Preserve record continuity while interrupting material or organizational continuity.
- Duplicate the relevant basis and ask whether both successors inherit the same verdict.

Red-team questions:

- Does the archive distinguish numerical identity from continuity of function?
- Does it distinguish survival from replacement by an equivalent successor?
- Does it distinguish lineage from exact copying?
- Does it distinguish same system from same role within a system?

A diagnosis fails if the verdict survives fission without explaining why identity is not being duplicated.

### 3. Dependence and grounding tests

Use these when a case involves support, realization, constitution, grounding, mechanism, enabling condition, scaffold, or background field.

Stress variants:

- Remove an alleged ground and preserve the phenomenon through a substitute basis.
- Preserve the ground while removing the organization that made the phenomenon appear.
- Change an enabling condition without changing the dependent profile.
- Change the dependent profile without changing a background condition.

Red-team questions:

- Is the archive mistaking causal support for grounding?
- Is it mistaking realization for identity?
- Is it mistaking enabling background for constitutive inclusion?
- Is it mistaking mechanism for metaphysical explanation in general?

A diagnosis fails if every important condition is promoted to the same dependence relation.

### 4. Truthmaker, absence, and privation tests

Use these when the case involves lack, hole, shadow, omission, prevention, nonexistence, unrealized capacity, or negative truth.

Stress variants:

- Keep the negative truth while changing the positive basis that makes it true.
- Keep the positive basis while changing the contrast class that makes the absence salient.
- Change the normative expectation while preserving the physical arrangement.
- Preserve the physical absence while removing the relevant standard or function.

Red-team questions:

- Is the archive reifying every true negative claim?
- Is it treating a privation as a mere absence without a standard?
- Is it treating an omission as a positive event?
- Is it confusing prevention with non-occurrence?

A diagnosis fails if it cannot name the positive basis, contrast class, or norm that makes the absence metaphysically relevant.

### 5. Access, evidence, and representation tests

Use these when a case turns on measurement, model output, detection, observation, map, proxy, simulation, index, record, or sign.

Stress variants:

- Keep the target fixed while changing the detector.
- Keep the detector fixed while changing the target.
- Preserve model fit while changing the mechanism that produces the fit.
- Preserve the readout while introducing a known artifact.
- Preserve the representation while changing the user community's interpretation.

Red-team questions:

- Is the archive promoting a measurement artifact into ontology?
- Is it treating model indispensability as automatic existence?
- Is it confusing robustness across instruments with robustness of target?
- Is it treating reference-fixing as target-making?

A diagnosis fails if it cannot separate target, vehicle, access route, operationalization, and artifact risk.

### 6. Process, time, and transition tests

Use these when a case involves onset, cessation, latency, recurrence, delay, inertia, history, tipping, irreversibility, or lineage.

Stress variants:

- Preserve a periodic return while changing the bearer that returns.
- Preserve the bearer while changing the phase or activation state.
- Keep the observed abruptness while changing whether the landscape reorganized.
- Preserve the transition while changing whether it is reversible.
- Preserve a delay while changing whether it reflects inertia, masking, bottlenecking, or measurement lag.

Red-team questions:

- Is the archive treating recurrence as identity?
- Is it treating dormancy as nonexistence?
- Is it treating abrupt change as bifurcation?
- Is it treating irreversibility as impossibility rather than path-dependent cost or hysteresis?

A diagnosis fails if it cannot distinguish phase, bearer, lineage, landscape, and observation window.

### 7. Stock, flow, and availability tests

Use these when a case involves accumulation, depletion, replenishment, turnover, circulation, release, leakage, sequestration, bottlenecking, rerouting, or throughput.

Stress variants:

- Preserve stock level while changing throughput.
- Preserve throughput while changing stock level.
- Preserve route labels while changing actual traffic.
- Preserve traffic while changing route labels.
- Preserve inflow while adding hidden leakage.
- Preserve outflow while adding hidden replenishment.

Red-team questions:

- Is the archive confusing stock with flow?
- Is it treating a bottleneck as a valve?
- Is it treating leakage as release?
- Is it treating rerouting as repair?
- Is it treating turnover as replacement of the whole?

A diagnosis fails if it cannot state what is being conserved, moved, lost, replenished, redirected, or regulated.

### 8. Interface, retention, and support tests

Use these when a case involves hosting, attachment, embedding, enclosure, coating, wrapping, lamination, sealing, joining, articulation, clamping, latching, threading, meshing, gripping, support, or guiding.

Stress variants:

- Preserve spatial intimacy while changing load path.
- Preserve load path while changing attachment mechanism.
- Preserve attachment while removing support.
- Preserve enclosure while removing sealing.
- Preserve sealing while removing enclosure.
- Preserve contact while changing whether the relation is traction, clamping, latching, or meshing.
- Preserve route constraint while changing whether the guide supports the target.

Red-team questions:

- Is the archive treating contact as parthood?
- Is it treating enclosure as embedding?
- Is it treating sealing as joining?
- Is it treating support as hosting?
- Is it treating guidance as control of quantity rather than path?

A diagnosis fails if it cannot identify the interface basis: containment, contact, layer, seam, preload, capture, thread geometry, tooth engagement, traction, load path, or guide path.

### 9. Capacity, response, and control tests

Use these when a case involves masking, inhibition, gating, saturation, feedback, habituation, filtering, buffering, resistance, compliance, slack, or valving.

Stress variants:

- Preserve capacity while blocking manifestation.
- Preserve manifestation while changing capacity.
- Preserve open path while changing admissibility conditions.
- Preserve admissibility while changing rate or dose.
- Preserve rate while changing route.
- Preserve route while changing aperture setting.
- Preserve response level while changing whether the cause is saturation, fatigue, inhibition, or negative feedback.

Red-team questions:

- Is the archive confusing blocked power with absent power?
- Is it treating gating as valving?
- Is it treating saturation as depletion?
- Is it treating feedback stabilization as mere inertia?
- Is it treating filtering as shielding, screening, or selective reception without saying which?

A diagnosis fails if it cannot separate capacity, trigger, threshold, gate, aperture, load, feedback, resistance, and readout.

### 10. Social, normative, living, agential, and conscious tests

Use these when a case involves roles, offices, obligations, reasons, persons, organisms, autonomy, malfunction, self-governance, or experience.

Stress variants:

- Preserve behavior while changing agency.
- Preserve agency while changing institutional role.
- Preserve institutional status while changing material bearer.
- Preserve organismic continuity while changing social identity.
- Preserve first-person report while changing access conditions.
- Preserve norm violation while changing whether the system has the relevant function.

Red-team questions:

- Is the archive treating role continuity as person identity?
- Is it treating organismic life as social status?
- Is it treating malfunction as mere statistical deviation?
- Is it treating conscious access as reportability?
- Is it treating reasons as causes without remainder?

A diagnosis fails if it cannot say which layer is doing the work: biological, agential, personal, institutional, normative, phenomenal, or representational.

## The counterexample ledger

When a future revision proposes a new file or major change, it should include a short counterexample ledger in the working notes or in the relevant control file. The ledger should have this form:

1. **Proposed claim.** State it in one sentence.
2. **Best rival diagnosis.** Name the nearest existing file or family that might absorb it.
3. **Same-word / changed-basis variant.** Does the claim survive?
4. **Same-basis / changed-word variant.** Does the claim survive?
5. **Wrong-grain variant.** What happens if the grain shifts?
6. **Proxy-artifact variant.** What if the evidence is only a label, model, readout, or administrative category?
7. **Fission or duplication variant.** Does the verdict illegitimately duplicate identity or status?
8. **Failure-mode variant.** What breakdown would reveal the relation or expose misclassification?
9. **Compression verdict.** Existing file, paired files, family amendment, control-rule change, or new operator.
10. **Update footprint.** Which files must be updated if the claim is accepted?

The archive should prefer claims that become sharper under this ledger. A claim that becomes vaguer under challenge is not yet ready.

For reusable or precedent-setting cases, the compact ledger in this section is now expanded by `146-diagnostic-case-ledger-calibration-set-and-benchmark-protocol.md`, which adds positive controls, negative controls, hard positives, hard negatives, boundary cases, regression instructions, and calibration-family benchmark prompts.

## Positive use: not just refusal

Adversarial testing should not make the archive timid. It should make it more exact. A stress test can justify expansion when it shows that existing files fail in a stable, repeatable way.

A new operator becomes more plausible when:

- the same basis recurs across several domains,
- the nearest three existing files each fail for different reasons,
- the failure mode is specific,
- the continuity consequence is distinctive,
- the artifact risk is identifiable and controllable,
- and the compression rule can state when not to use the new operator.

In that sense, adversarial pressure is a growth condition. It decides whether growth is real or merely lexical.

## Negative use: when to demote

The archive should demote a proposed distinction when:

- it tracks ordinary vocabulary rather than basis,
- it works only at one grain while pretending to work globally,
- it duplicates an existing operator with different examples,
- it depends on a proxy readout that can be varied independently of the target,
- it has no distinct failure mode,
- it adds no new continuity consequence,
- or it cannot say which neighboring files it improves.

Demotion is not failure. It may mean the case belongs in a comparison paragraph, method rule, frontier test, footnote, or family map rather than in a new first-order file.

1. identify the target,
2. route the case by family,
3. name nearest rivals,
4. lock basis and grain,
5. run hostile variations,
6. state defeaters and artifact risks,
7. decide whether to route, amend, consolidate, demote, or promote,
8. record the result in the diagnostic case ledger when the case becomes reusable, precedential, or regression-sensitive,

## Default verdict

The default verdict of this file is stricter than the default verdict of `144`:

> **Do not merely route the case. Try to break the routing.**

If the routing survives, the archive has earned greater confidence. If it breaks, the archive has learned where to repair itself. Either outcome is progress.


If it becomes precedential, the archive should next record the positive controls, negative controls, hard positives, hard negatives, boundary cases, regression instruction, and open debt in `146-diagnostic-case-ledger-calibration-set-and-benchmark-protocol.md`. If the verdict is accepted into the archive, it should then use `147-revision-dependency-graph-update-propagation-and-drift-control.md` to prevent update-footprint drift, `148-terminology-register-synonym-control-and-crosswalk-protocol.md` to prevent vocabulary drift, `149-claim-status-register-maturity-levels-and-commitment-governance.md` to prevent commitment inflation, `150-application-dossier-decision-record-and-verdict-report-protocol.md` to prevent verdict evaporation, and `151-precedent-reuse-appeal-transfer-and-review-governance.md` to prevent precedent drift after a dossier begins to travel, `152-transmission-excerpt-compression-and-pedagogical-governance.md` to prevent summary drift after a result becomes reader-facing, and `153-reception-feedback-errata-and-correction-loop-governance.md` to prevent feedback drift after the result is received.


## Revision-integration note: stress tests and commitment status

`149-claim-status-register-maturity-levels-and-commitment-governance.md` clarifies what remains after a verdict survives or fails adversarial testing. Survival of hostile variants does not always mean promotion to doctrine. It may justify a mature diagnostic distinction, a domain-local rule, a live rival, a calibrated precedent, a narrowed working default, or only a provisional probe.

Future stress-test reports should therefore state the **status result** as well as the classification result. The archive should ask: did the hostile variants promote the item, narrow it, demote it, quarantine it, preserve it as a live rival, source-bind it, deprecate it, or mark an open debt?


## Revision-integration note: stress-test reports and application dossiers

`150-application-dossier-decision-record-and-verdict-report-protocol.md` clarifies how stress-test results should travel. A hostile-variation result is not reusable merely because it was performed; it must be recorded with the target, route, rival, basis, grain, variant outcome, defeater, status, non-verdict, and future-use permission when later revisions will rely on it.

Future stress-test reports should therefore state whether the result is **local answer only, illustration, precedent, calibration, regression warning, source-bound example, live rival, open pressure, or do-not-reuse**. Without that field, a passed stress test can be overused and a failed stress test can be forgotten.


## Revision-integration note: precedent appeals

`151-precedent-reuse-appeal-transfer-and-review-governance.md` treats failed reuse as a reason to rerun adversarial testing. A verdict that survived hostile variation in its original dossier may fail when exported to a new grain, source role, family, or teaching context. Future stress tests should therefore distinguish **original verdict survival** from **transfer survival**. If a precedent is appealed, rerun only the variants implicated by the appeal, but do not let the original dossier's confidence block renewed attack.


## Revision-integration note: hostile variants in compressed outputs

`152-transmission-excerpt-compression-and-pedagogical-governance.md` treats omitted hostile variants as a transmission risk. A short summary need not reproduce every adversarial test, but it should not imply that unrun or omitted variants were settled. Future stress-test reports should therefore mark whether an output is orientation-only, safe compact summary, technical brief, or full report, and should attach a warning whenever the summary omits a live defeater.
