# Public Reliance, Citation, Dispute, Withdrawal, and Retraction Governance

## Why this file exists

`157-review-authority-audit-certification-and-claim-warrant-governance.md` prevents review language from becoming false authority. It asks what was reviewed, by whom or what, under what authority, with what evidence, at what scope, and what wording that review permits.

That is necessary, but it leaves a further public-use problem. A result can be accurately described as self-reviewed, manifest-verified, source-reviewed, externally reviewed, or release-warranted and still be misused once it leaves the archive. It can be cited without version identity, quoted without status limits, taught without return path, relied on operationally when it was only a conceptual orientation, treated as current after supersession, disputed without a channel, corrected without notice, withdrawn without migration, or retracted as if retraction erased all historical value.

The danger is **public reliance drift**: a scoped archive claim becomes a free public warrant merely because it is published, cited, repeated, attached to a package, or described with review language.

This file adds a public-use layer after review-warrant governance: **public reliance, citation, dispute, withdrawal, and retraction governance**. Its job is not public relations. Its job is to state what a released, reviewed, transmitted, or taught result permits others to do with it, how it should be cited, how disputes are received, and what happens when the archive must clarify, narrow, hold, withdraw, retract, supersede, or recall a public claim.

## Compressed default

The archive should now say:

> **A reviewed claim is not automatically a public reliance license. Public use requires a stated reliance status, citation form, scope, warning, dispute path, update path, and withdrawal or retraction rule.**

In shorter form:

> **Publication is not permission to rely without limits.**

And more carefully:

> **Every public-facing archive claim should state what version it belongs to, what file or packet supports it, what review or certification status it has, what audience and use it permits, what it forbids, what warnings must travel with it, how it should be cited, how disputes should be lodged, what evidence would narrow or withdraw it, how corrections will be noticed, and whether later versions supersede, preserve, deprecate, or merely historically retain it.**

## Place in the control sequence

1. route the case with `144`,

The sequence matters. Public reliance should not be granted to a claim that lacks a dossier, status, release gate, lineage relation, provenance packet, or review warrant when those controls are relevant.

## Public-use statuses

Use these statuses when a claim, file, packet, example, release note, summary, derivative artifact, teaching output, or citation is meant to travel beyond private archive maintenance.

### U0 — private scratch / no public reliance

The item may guide private development but should not be cited, taught, quoted as archive doctrine, or treated as a release claim. A draft note, unreviewed experiment, or abandoned route belongs here.

### U1 — orientation-only public mention

The item may be mentioned to orient a reader, but it is not a controlled precedent or current doctrine. Required warning: "orientation only; consult the named version and file before relying."

### U2 — historical citation only

The item may be cited as something a prior version said. It should not be treated as current without a lineage and compatibility check. This is the right status for superseded but historically important claims.

### U3 — public summary with retained limits

The item may be summarized publicly if the version, file, compression level, status, and return path are retained. It is suitable for controlled summaries, teaching introductions, and high-level maps.

### U4 — controlled public citation

The item may be cited as the archive's current position on a bounded point, provided the citation includes version identity, file or packet, claim status, review status, and forbidden upgrades. This is the ordinary status for a stable governance rule in a current release.

### U5 — teaching / derivative reuse with return path

The item may be used in teaching, diagrams, rubrics, derivative notes, or paper sections if the transmission packet states audience, compression level, omitted material, and return path. It should not be mistaken for full archive-grade extraction.

### U6 — source-bound public use

The item may be used publicly only with its source scope, source date or edition, source-review status, and refresh trigger. This status is for legal, scientific, clinical, historical, textual, institutional, or empirical examples where the archive's claim depends on external material.

### U7 — advisory / decision-support use

The item may support a decision, review, classification, or editorial action, but only with explicit warning that it is not independent legal, clinical, scientific, institutional, or policy authority. The public user must preserve scope and non-verdicts.

### U8 — release-warranted public reliance

The item has passed the relevant release, lineage, provenance, review, and public-use checks for a stated audience and use. It may be relied on publicly within that scope until an expiry, dispute, supersession, source refresh, rollback, or withdrawal trigger fires. This is a strong status and should be rare.

### U9 — no public reliance / unsafe to cite

The item should not be cited or reused publicly except as an example of withdrawn, retracted, unsafe, disputed, malformed, or deprecated material. Required warning: "do not rely; consult the withdrawal or retraction note."

## Public reliance packet

A public-facing claim should carry a compact packet when it will be cited, taught, exported, or used outside the immediate archive context.

Minimum fields:

- **Public reliance ID** — stable identifier for the reliance decision.
- **Claim or artifact** — the exact claim, file, packet, output, derivative, citation, or release note.
- **Version identity** — package, file, section, and, when relevant, lineage relation.
- **Supporting controls** — dossier, precedent packet, transmission packet, reception packet, release packet, lineage packet, provenance packet, review packet, source note.
- **Public-use status** — U0 through U9.
- **Intended audience** — maintainer, reader, student, reviewer, domain expert, public user, derivative author, external citer, or institutional user.
- **Permitted use** — orientation, citation, teaching, comparison, controlled precedent, source-bound use, decision support, package verification, historical retention, or no use.
- **Forbidden use** — upgrades the packet does not allow.
- **Required warnings** — status, scope, compression, source, review, lineage, expiry, and non-verdict warnings that must travel.
- **Citation form** — required version/file/section language.
- **Dispute path** — how objections, corrections, counterexamples, source updates, citation disputes, or withdrawal requests should be routed.
- **Update / notice path** — where corrections, supersessions, holds, withdrawals, or retractions will be recorded.
- **Expiry / refresh trigger** — source change, version supersession, review expiry, failed reception, public misuse, fork import, external objection, or lineage conflict.
- **Withdrawal / retraction rule** — conditions under which the public permission narrows, pauses, is withdrawn, or is retracted.

## Citation discipline

A safe archive citation should not merely name the archive. It should name the version and the claim's status.

Recommended form:

> Metaphysics Archive, `rev0152`, `docs/158-public-reliance-citation-dispute-withdrawal-and-retraction-governance.md`, public-use status U4, source-neutral governance/control-layer rule, self-reviewed/manifest-verified but not independently certified.

For first-order operator claims, cite:

- version,
- file,
- operator or family,
- commitment status when known,
- whether the claim is a kernel/default, diagnostic distinction, calibrated precedent, source-bound example, live rival, provisional probe, or open debt,
- whether the material is being cited as current doctrine, historical position, teaching illustration, or disputed/deprecated item.

Unsafe citation forms include:

- "the archive proves that..." without file, version, or status,
- "the current archive says..." while citing an older version,
- "reviewed" without review packet or scope,
- "source-backed" without source date and source role,
- "certified" without certification status,
- "withdrawn" without saying whether the withdrawal is local, public-use-only, source-bound, conceptual, or package-level.

## Dispute classes

Public disputes should be classified before they change doctrine.

### Q0 — no dispute / accurate citation

The public use preserves version, status, scope, and warnings.

### Q1 — citation incompleteness

The citation omits version, file, section, status, warning, source date, or review scope, but the substantive claim is not yet wrong.

### Q2 — compression dispute

A summary, excerpt, diagram, table, or teaching output has flattened status, dropped rivals, removed non-verdicts, or made a local rule sound general.

### Q3 — status or review-warrant dispute

The public use overstates commitment status, maturity, review status, independence, certification, source review, or release warrant.

### Q4 — lineage / version dispute

The public use cites an old version as current, backports a later rule, imports a fork without compatibility analysis, or treats historical retention as live doctrine.

### Q5 — source-boundary dispute

The public use relies on a source-dependent example after the source changed, after the source scope was omitted, or outside the domain reviewed.

### Q6 — precedent-transfer dispute

A controlled precedent is applied to a new target without preserving basis, grain, family, rival set, term status, non-verdict, or transfer condition.

### Q7 — substantive counterexample pressure

The public objection challenges the archive's diagnosis, operator boundary, claim status, or doctrine rather than merely its citation or transmission.

### Q8 — reliance harm / operational misuse

A public user has acted as if a limited archive claim licensed legal, clinical, scientific, policy, institutional, or operational reliance that the archive did not warrant.

### Q9 — withdrawal / retraction request

A public user asks the archive to clarify, narrow, hold, withdraw, retract, recall, or supersede a claim, packet, derivative, citation, or package.

## Dispute routing

When a public dispute arrives:

1. **Preserve the disputed object.** Capture the exact quote, citation, version, derivative, context, and claimed reliance.
2. **Classify the dispute.** Use Q0-Q9 before deciding whether doctrine is threatened.
3. **Check transmission.** If the problem is compression, return to `152`.
4. **Check reception.** If the problem is uptake, misunderstanding, or repeated misuse, return to `153`.
5. **Check release and lineage.** If the problem is version identity, old-current confusion, fork import, or migration, return to `154` and `155`.
6. **Check provenance.** If the artifact may not be the claimed package, return to `156`.
7. **Check review warrant.** If review or certification language is inflated, return to `157`.
8. **Check source boundaries.** If source-dependent, return to `05` and the relevant source-refresh rules.
9. **Check doctrine.** If the dispute survives these filters, open an appeal, ledger case, adversarial stress test, or doctrine revision.
10. **Record public action.** State whether the result is no action, local clarification, correction, public warning, reliance hold, withdrawal, retraction, rollback, supersession, or package recall.

## Withdrawal and retraction classes

Do not use withdrawal and retraction as one flat act.

### W0 — no withdrawal

The public use remains allowed. A clarification or better citation may still be helpful.

### W1 — wording correction

A public sentence, title, summary, or citation form needs repair, but the underlying claim remains usable.

### W2 — public clarification note

The public use has created a predictable ambiguity; add a warning, scope note, return path, or status reminder.

### W3 — citation-warning update

The claim remains usable only if future citations include an added warning about version, source, review, status, lineage, or compression.

### W4 — temporary reliance hold

Suspend public reliance while source review, lineage analysis, review refresh, appeal, provenance audit, or reception analysis runs. Historical citation remains allowed if marked as under hold.

### W5 — withdrawal from teaching or derivative use

The claim may remain in the archive but should not be used in teaching summaries, diagrams, rubrics, prompt answers, or derivative artifacts until repaired.

### W6 — source-bound withdrawal

A source-dependent claim is withdrawn because the source changed, was misread, was out of date, or was used outside the reviewed scope. This does not automatically retract the general metaphysical distinction.

### W7 — conceptual retraction

The archive retracts the public claim because the operator boundary, diagnosis, status, or doctrine was wrong. Retain the case historically and record migration/deprecation instructions.

### W8 — package-level recall / rollback trigger

The problem affects release identity, package integrity, lineage, provenance, review warrant, or enough public uses that `154` rollback or recall procedures should be triggered.

## Public correction actions

Available actions, in increasing severity:

1. **No public action** — citation is accurate or dispute is outside scope.
2. **Private reply** — minor misunderstanding with no public reliance risk.
3. **Citation patch** — add version/file/status wording.
4. **Transmission warning** — add a warning to summaries or teaching outputs.
5. **Reception note** — log repeated misunderstanding or misuse.
6. **Source refresh** — update external source role, date, or scope.
7. **Review refresh** — re-run or narrow review warrant.
8. **Precedent freeze** — pause transfer of a case while appeal is pending.
9. **Public reliance hold** — pause reliance status U4-U8.
10. **Withdrawal note** — remove public-use permission for a defined scope.
11. **Retraction note** — state that a public claim was wrong and why.
12. **Release rollback / recall** — invoke package-level maintenance procedures.

## Anti-patterns

Watch for these recurring failures:

- **Availability laundering** — treating something as reliable because it is visible in a public ZIP.
- **Citation laundering** — repeated citation makes a claim sound more mature than its status permits.
- **Review-to-reliance jump** — a scoped review packet is used as public reliance permission.
- **Latest-version laundering** — a current package is cited as if every older public summary is current.
- **Historical undead claim** — a withdrawn or deprecated item remains alive in teaching material.
- **Orphaned correction** — a correction exists but is not connected to the public artifact it corrects.
- **Silent withdrawal** — a public claim disappears without a withdrawal class, migration note, or historical marker.
- **Retraction shame** — retraction is treated as failure rather than as responsible archive maintenance.
- **Dispute suppression** — public objections are ignored because the package passed internal checks.
- **Operational overreach** — a metaphysical diagnosis is used as legal, clinical, policy, scientific, or institutional authority.
- **Certifier drift** — an external review of one claim is cited as if it certifies all public uses.
- **Source freeze** — a source-bound claim remains public after the source, edition, legal context, scientific consensus, or institutional practice changes.

## Public-use worksheet

Before publishing, citing, teaching, exporting, or relying on an archive claim, answer:

1. What exactly is the claim or artifact?
2. Which version, file, section, packet, or source note supports it?
3. Is it current, historical, deprecated, under appeal, under hold, or retracted?
4. What is the commitment status and maturity level?
5. What is the review status and permitted claim language?
6. What public-use status applies, U0-U9?
7. Who is the intended audience?
8. What use is allowed?
9. What use is forbidden?
10. What warnings must travel with the claim?
11. What citation form is required?
12. What event would trigger refresh, hold, withdrawal, retraction, or rollback?
13. Where will corrections and disputes be recorded?
14. Does the claim depend on a source, edition, date, institution, domain, or external reviewer?
15. Would a reasonable public user misread this as stronger authority than intended?

## Source-dependent public use

When an archive claim depends on external material, public reliance requires extra care.

A source-dependent public packet should state:

- source identity,
- source date or edition,
- access date when relevant,
- whether the source was merely cited, summarized, interpreted, or reviewed,
- whether a domain expert reviewed the source use,
- whether the source is stable or update-prone,
- refresh trigger,
- public warning,
- and whether the metaphysical lesson survives if the source changes.

Source change does not always defeat a metaphysical distinction. But it can defeat a public example, teaching packet, source-bound precedent, or review warrant.

## Teaching and derivative artifacts

Teaching outputs need especially explicit public-use profiles. A good teaching use may simplify, but it should not make simplification look like unrestricted doctrine.

A teaching derivative should include:

- version,
- compression level from `152`,
- public-use status,
- intended learner or audience,
- omitted limits,
- forbidden upgrades,
- return path,
- and update notice.

If the teaching artifact cannot carry those fields, it should remain U1 or U3 rather than U5 or stronger.

## Withdrawal is not erasure

Withdrawal and retraction should preserve memory. A withdrawn claim may still be historically important, pedagogically useful as a caution, or relevant to a migration note. Retraction says the public claim should not be relied on as stated; it does not require pretending the claim was never made.

Every withdrawal or retraction should answer:

- What exactly is withdrawn or retracted?
- Is the withdrawal local, source-bound, teaching-bound, precedent-bound, public-use-only, conceptual, or package-level?
- What remains usable?
- What must be migrated?
- What public citations need warning?
- What old outputs should be marked do-not-reuse?
- What future revision should revisit?

## Relation to review governance

`157` asks what review warrants. `158` asks what public reliance follows.

The default answer is: **less than people assume**. Review may permit a sentence such as "manifest-verified," "source-reviewed for this source," or "maintainer-reviewed for integration." Public reliance still requires audience, use, citation, warning, dispute path, and withdrawal rule.

## Future expansion rule

Do not add another public-use governance file merely because more audiences or publication venues can be imagined. Add one only if actual public use reveals a recurring failure not handled by public reliance packets, citation rules, dispute classes, withdrawal/retraction classes, release gates, lineage packets, provenance packets, review packets, transmission packets, reception packets, or source notes.

Likely future additions should be concrete: machine-readable public-reliance ledger, public errata page, citation template bundle, public teaching-derivative registry, source-refresh notice log, or actual withdrawal/retraction notices.

## Closing formulation

A mature archive should not only know what it says, how it was built, how it was reviewed, or what wording review permits. It should know what the public may do with the claim, how the claim must be cited, how dispute enters the archive, and how reliance is paused, narrowed, withdrawn, retracted, or historically retained.

Public reliance governance is the archive's resistance to false authority by circulation.
