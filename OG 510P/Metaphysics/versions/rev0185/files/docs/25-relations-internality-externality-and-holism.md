# Relations, Internality, Externality, and Holism

## Why this file exists

The archive already relies on **structure**, **causation**, **space**, **social construction**, **grounding**, and **organization**, but it still lacked a direct policy for one of the oldest pressure points in metaphysics:

- when a relation is a real ontological feature rather than only a convenient way of speaking,
- when a relation is **internal** to its relata,
- when it is **external** and not fixed by their intrinsic characters,
- and when a domain is sufficiently relation-dependent that some form of **holism** is earned.

Without that discipline, the archive risks sliding among:

1. relations as mere grammar,
2. relations as automatically fundamental,
3. structure as an unexplained slogan,
4. holism as a permission slip for vagueness,
5. and reductionism that treats every relation as “no addition of being.”

A compact non-skeptical, non-reductive metaphysics needs a better rule-set than that.

## Archive default

The current default is **relation-profile realism with typed holism**.

That means:

- treat some relations as ontologically serious,
- refuse to assume that all relations are one kind,
- distinguish **internal** from **external** relations,
- distinguish **thin comparative or ordering relations** from **thicker constitutive or productive relations**,
- and allow **local or domain-specific holism** where relata are partly what they are in virtue of standing in a structured nexus, without inflating that into a global thesis that everything is equally dependent on everything else.

Compressed:

> **Relations are real where worldly order, comparison, connection, constitution, or determination cannot be recovered without them; but relational seriousness is typed, and holism should be local, structured, and earned.**

## Four live options

### 1. Eliminativist or strongly reductionist views

**Picture:** relations add no real furniture to the world; truths about relations are fully explained by monadic properties, existential profiles, or mere linguistic convenience.

**What they get right:**
- not every relational sentence requires a heavyweight relational entity,
- some comparative relations may be fixed by intrinsic features of the relata,
- and metaphysics should resist multiplying relations just because natural language is relational.

**What they miss for this archive:**
- many spatial, causal, social, normative, and structural cases are not fixed by intrinsic character alone,
- relation-talk often marks real ordering, directedness, or dependence,
- and an archive already committed to structure cannot pretend all structure is a dispensable paraphrase.

### 2. Flat relation realism

**Picture:** relations are real and irreducible across the board.

**What it gets right:**
- relations often do real ontological work,
- spatiotemporal, causal, and social domains are hard to explain without them,
- and some classical eliminativist pressures look much less plausible once science and structured systems are taken seriously.

**What it risks if left alone:**
- treating all relations as equally basic,
- ignoring the difference between thin comparative order and thick constitutive linkage,
- and overreacting to reductionism by reifying every “between.”

### 3. Internal-relation restraint

**Picture:** some relations are real, but internal relations are often derivative of the intrinsic or already-constituted profiles of their relata.

**What it gets right:**
- “taller than”, “same shape as”, and many comparative cases may depend on prior monadic or structured facts,
- not every internal relation needs its own heavyweight truthmaker,
- and relation-talk can still be explanatory without every relation being basic.

**What it risks if left alone:**
- underplaying thin but explanatory internal order,
- and encouraging the archive to over-privilege intrinsic character in cases where identity and role are partly relational.

### 4. Holist or structure-first views

**Picture:** in some domains, relata are partly constituted by the relations in which they stand.

**What they get right:**
- entities in networks, institutions, fields, ecologies, languages, and some physical theories are not always intelligible one-by-one prior to structure,
- local identity can be relation-sensitive,
- and structural explanation is often more than a summary of independent items.

**What they risk if left alone:**
- letting “holism” become a vague aura-word,
- blurring local structural constitution with global monism,
- and losing track of which relations are actually doing the work.

## Current archive answer

The archive should adopt a **typed relation profile**.

That means future revisions should distinguish at least six questions:

1. **Is the relation internal or external?**
2. **Is it thin or thick?**
3. **Is it symmetric, asymmetric, ordered, or directed?**
4. **Is it constitutive, causal, comparative, locative, normative, semantic, or mixed?**
5. **Does it merely track prior features of the relata, or partly constitute what the relata are in that domain?**
6. **Does the case support only local structural dependence, or a stronger holistic thesis?**

The archive does **not** need one universal metaphysics of relations for every domain. But it does need these distinctions.

## Archive commitments

### 1. Relations are not one flat ontological category

The archive should distinguish at least:

- **comparative relations** — taller than, earlier than, equal in charge to,
- **spatial or locative relations** — next to, inside, upstream from, field-coupled with,
- **causal or productive relations** — produces, prevents, sustains, interrupts,
- **constitutive relations** — member of, office-holder of, component in, role-defining within,
- **normative or institutional relations** — authorized by, obligated to, counts as under,
- **semantic or intentional relations** — refers to, is about, recognizes, represents.

This matters because different relations answer different metaphysical questions.

### 2. External relations are often ontologically serious

The archive should be realist enough about external relations to preserve:

- spatial organization,
- causal influence,
- ecological and network structure,
- social and institutional positioning,
- and many kinds of field-like or distributed organization.

A world of only intrinsic characters cannot easily recover these.

### 3. Internal relations need discipline, not automatic elimination or reification

Some internal relations may be derivative in the sense that once the relata and their relevant intrinsic or already-structured profiles are fixed, the relation is fixed too.

But derivative does **not** mean useless or unreal. Thin internal relations may still explain:

- comparative truth,
- order,
- proportion,
- ranking,
- and why certain prior facts suffice for certain relational claims.

So the archive should avoid both extremes:

- **do not** posit a heavyweight entity for every comparative truth,
- but **do not** pretend order and proportion add nothing worth metaphysical notice.

### 4. Some domains are relation-sensitive all the way down to identity conditions

In some cases, what a thing is cannot be specified without reference to a structured position or nexus.

This is especially plausible for:

- offices and institutional roles,
- kinship or legal status,
- nodes in some technical or infrastructural networks,
- positions in formal structures,
- and perhaps some physical systems best understood relationally rather than as isolated intrinsic bearers.

In such domains, the archive should allow that the relata are at least partly **relationally constituted**.

### 5. Holism should be typed, local, and earned

The archive should resist two bad moves:

- **global anti-holism**: pretending every entity can always be characterized independently of all larger structure,
- **global holism**: treating every identity and explanation as equally dependent on the whole universe.

The default should instead be:

> **use local or domain-specific holism where the explanatory and identity conditions of the target genuinely depend on a wider organized nexus.**

This keeps the archive hospitable to structured wholes, fields, ecosystems, languages, and institutions without collapsing into a vague everything-is-connected monism.

### 6. Relation-talk must not replace dependence analysis

A relation can hold between entities without yet telling us whether the case is about:

- grounding,
- constitution,
- realization,
- causation,
- location,
- participation in a kind,
- or merely comparison.

So this file supplements rather than replaces the archive’s dependence-pluralism.

### 7. Order and direction are metaphysically important

Some relations do more than connect relata; they impose:

- order,
- orientation,
- rank,
- precedence,
- direction,
- or asymmetry.

This matters for time, causation, authority, explanation, and many kinds of process. The archive should therefore pay attention not only to whether a relation holds, but to what kind of ordering it imposes.

## Relation-profile rule

When a future revision relies on relational language like **in relation to**, **depends on**, **is connected with**, **is ordered by**, **is constituted by**, **stands in**, **is part of a network**, **is recognized as**, or **is located relative to**, it should state:

1. **the relata**,
2. **the arity** of the relation,
3. **whether the relation is internal, external, or mixed**,
4. **whether it is thin/comparative or thick/constitutive/productive**,
5. **whether it is symmetric, asymmetric, ordered, or directed**,
6. **whether it is doing causal, constitutive, locative, semantic, normative, or merely classificatory work**,
7. **whether the relation helps constitute the relata or only links already-settled relata**,
8. **whether the case earns only local structural dependence or a stronger holistic thesis**.

If a proposal does not do this, its structure-talk is under-disciplined.

## Default archive stances by domain

### Space and physics-adjacent cases

Default stance: **external relations are often indispensable**.

Distance, adjacency, field-coupling, and many spatial or spatiotemporal structures are not plausibly recovered from intrinsic features alone.

### Organisms and ecosystems

Default stance: **mixed profile**.

Some relations are external interactions, but others are partly constitutive of what the organism or ecosystem is as a functioning system.

### Artifacts and infrastructures

Default stance: **role- and network-sensitive**.

A component can be what it is partly because of how it is connected within a designed system.

### Institutions and social reality

Default stance: **relation-sensitive and often locally holistic**.

Roles, offices, money, legal status, and authority structures are often not intelligible apart from recognition, procedure, and networked position.

### Abstracta and formal structures

Default stance: **structural relations often do the main work**.

Positions in a structure, inferential roles, and formal organization can be explanatory without forcing one heavy ontology of all abstracta.

## Working summary

The archive’s current answer is neither:

- a monadic ontology that treats relations as mostly grammatical froth,
- nor a flat relation realism that reifies every “between,”
- nor an undisciplined holism in which every thing is equally constituted by everything else.

Its current answer is:

> **Some relations are real and indispensable; some are derivative but still explanatory; some domains are relation-sensitive enough to support local holism; and the right metaphysical question is usually not whether relations exist simpliciter, but what relation-profile the case requires.**
