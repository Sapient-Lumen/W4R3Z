# Truth, Correspondence, and Reality-Answerability

## Why this file exists

The archive already had files on **truthmakers**, **facts/states of affairs**, **representation/aboutness**, **appearance/manifestation**, and **scientific-image pressure**. But it still lacked one direct file saying what truth itself is doing in the metaphysical picture, how broad **correspondence** should be understood, when deflationary insights are enough, and why the archive keeps saying that discourse must be **world-answerable**.

Without a dedicated file here, the archive risks five opposite mistakes:

- treating truth as so thin that reality-answerability disappears into mere logical convenience,
- treating truth as correspondence in so narrow a sense that every true claim must match one bespoke fact,
- treating truthbearers, truthmakers, and facts as if they were one flat ontological category,
- treating coherence, practice-success, and assertional utility as if they were competitors to reality rather than possible constraints on our access to it,
- or treating scientific realism, manifest experience, and representational success as if one truth theory automatically settled all three.

This file gives the archive a compact **truth profile** so future revisions can say whether a dispute is chiefly about truthbearers, truth conditions, truthmakers, correspondence, deflationary expressive role, coherence constraints, practical warrant, or some mixed profile.

## Working default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Truth is a real norm of answerability to how things are, but correspondence should be understood broadly and typed, not as a demand for one proprietary fact for every true sentence.**

And more specifically:

> **Prefer a sparse reality-answerable truth realism: truth is not merely applause for accepted sentences, but its worldly anchor may be an entity, process, structure, power-profile, organization, bounded totality, or obtaining state of affairs depending on the case.**

This fits the archive's wider commitments:

- **facts** still matter because some truths are about what obtains,
- **truthmakers** still matter because some truths place genuine ontological demands on the world,
- **representation** still matters because truth is borne by content-bearing vehicles or acts,
- **appearance** still matters because the world can be disclosed partially and aspectually without therefore becoming unreal,
- and **scientific realism** still matters because truth should remain answerable to a mind-independent world even when access is theory-mediated.

The fuller treatment of **objective**, **mind-independent**, **observer-sensitive**, and **response-dependent** profiles now lives in `59-objectivity-mind-independence-and-response-dependence.md`.

## Core distinctions

### 1. Truthbearers are not truthmakers

The archive should distinguish:

- **truthbearer** — what can be true or false: proposition, judgment, belief, statement, assertion, sentence-in-use, model-claim, registry entry, or mixed case,
- **truthmaker or ontological ground** — what in the world makes the truthbearer correct if a truthmaker demand is appropriate,
- **fact or obtaining** — one possible worldly item or status in virtue of which a truthbearer is true,
- and **correspondence relation** — the fit or answerability relation between truthbearer and reality, understood more broadly than a one-one picture of sentence and fact.

Otherwise the archive will keep sliding from semantics to ontology and back again.

### 2. Correspondence should be broad, not atomistically overnarrow

The archive should keep live the broad thought that truth depends on how reality is, while refusing the overnarrow thought that every true sentence must correspond to one neatly matching fact-token.

So it should distinguish:

- **broad correspondence or reality-answerability** — truth depends on how things are,
- **narrow fact-correspondence** — truth consists in relation to a matching fact,
- **structural correspondence** — truth tracks organized or relational reality rather than one compact item,
- and **domain-typed fit** — different truthbearers may answer to reality differently across ordinary object-talk, process-talk, institutional claims, scientific models, and centered truths.

Otherwise the archive will keep oscillating between baroque fact-matching and thin deflation.

### 3. Deflationary insights are real but incomplete

The archive should grant that truth has indispensable logical, expressive, and generalizing functions. Sometimes saying that a claim is true is just a device for endorsement, quantification, disquotation, or semantic ascent.

But it should not infer from this that truth has no worldly dimension. Deflationary points explain some uses of truth-talk, not automatically the whole metaphysics of truth.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **logical or expressive use of truth**,
- **semantic ascent or disquotation**,
- **substantive truth theory**,
- and **metaphysical reality-answerability**.

Otherwise the archive will keep sliding from useful logical economy to anti-realism about truth.

### 4. Coherence, inquiry-success, and realism are not one question

A coherent set of beliefs may still fail to fit reality. A practically successful representation may still idealize. A true theory may be only approximately or selectively right in what it says about unobservables.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **coherence within a body of commitments**,
- **pragmatic or inquiry success**,
- **warrant or justification**,
- **approximate truth / selective realism**,
- and **truth itself**.

Otherwise the archive will keep collapsing epistemic virtues into truth conditions.

### 5. Truth is typed across domains

The archive should not assume that all true discourse answers to reality in one flat way.

It should distinguish at least:

- **ordinary object-level truth**,
- **event and process truth**,
- **modal truth**,
- **mathematical or abstracta-adjacent truth**,
- **institutional or status truth**,
- **centered or indexical truth**,
- **model-based or idealized scientific truth**,
- and **normative truth**.

This does **not** mean “different kinds of truth” in a loose relativist sense. It means that the worldly basis, truthmaker pressure, correspondence profile, and idealization profile may differ by domain.

### 6. Approximation and idealization do not automatically break truth-contact

Especially in science, maps, models, and theories often idealize, omit, smooth, or distort some features while still getting reality importantly right.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **strict literal truth**,
- **approximate truth**,
- **idealized but world-tracking model success**,
- **merely useful fiction**,
- and **instrumental convenience with no realist load**.

Otherwise the archive will keep forcing a false choice between exact mirrorism and pure instrumentality.

### 7. Truth should not become a new empire-word

Truth is important, but it does not replace:

- grounding,
- causation,
- representation,
- justification,
- or ontological commitment.

A truth may be justified without its justification explaining its truth. A truthmaker may make a claim true without causing anyone to believe it. A sentence may be ontologically committing without settling what is fundamental.

## Four live options

### 1. Strong correspondence factualism

**Picture:** truth is a substantive relation to reality, often understood as correspondence to facts or states of affairs.

**What it gets right:**
- truth is world-answerable,
- and anti-realist flattening is resisted.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it easily becomes too one-one and fact-heavy,
- and it may make every truth look like a demand for a matching worldly item.

### 2. Deflationism / minimalist truth

**Picture:** saying that `p` is true adds no deep property beyond asserting `p`; truth's main work is logical, expressive, and generalizing.

**What it gets right:**
- many ordinary uses of “true” are thin,
- and not every truth-ascription needs heavy metaphysics.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it can make world-answerability disappear,
- and it underdescribes why some truth disputes clearly carry ontological stakes.

### 3. Coherence / pragmatist pressure

**Picture:** truth is tied closely to coherence, inquiry, assertibility, or success in practice.

**What it gets right:**
- access to truth is mediated by wider inferential and practical norms,
- and isolated correspondence imagery is often too crude.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it risks collapsing truth into warranted acceptability or usefulness,
- and it can lose the archive's non-skeptical realism about mind-independent reality.

### 4. Typed reality-answerable truth realism

**Picture:** truth is genuinely about how reality is; correspondence is retained in a broad, typed, non-mirroristic sense; deflationary insights about logical use are preserved locally; and different domains may call for different worldly bases without abolishing truth.

**What it gets right:**
- it preserves realism without fact inflation,
- it fits the archive's layered ontology,
- and it keeps truthbearers, truthmakers, facts, representations, and justifications distinct.

**What it risks if left alone:**
- it can become vague unless each case states what kind of truthbearer, what kind of worldly basis, and what kind of fit is at issue.

## Archive commitments

### 1. Truth should remain answerable to reality

The archive should reject views that make truth nothing more than coherence inside discourse, community endorsement, or practical convenience.

### 2. Correspondence is best treated broadly and sparsely

The archive should keep the relational intuition that truth depends on the world, but it should resist any automatic one-truth/one-fact template.

### 3. Deflationary uses of truth should be preserved locally

Disquotation, semantic ascent, and logical generalization are genuine functions of truth-talk and should not be denied just because the archive wants truth to stay worldly.

### 4. Truthbearer type matters

A proposition, model claim, legal filing, map, perceptual judgment, or self-locating claim may all be truth-apt, but they do not all put pressure on the world in the same way.

### 5. Truthmaker pressure should scale with metaphysical load

Ordinary semantic ascent should not force ontology, but claims about what exists, what obtains, what grounds what, and what institutional or modal order there is often do demand a more explicit worldly basis.

### 6. Scientific and manifest truth should be integrated, not played off simplistically

The archive should keep a stereoscopic posture: ordinary manifest truths can be real though partial, and scientific truths can be reality-tracking though idealized and revisable.

## Truth profile rule for future revisions

Before inferring a metaphysical verdict from language like **true**, **false**, **truth**, **corresponds**, **fits reality**, **accurate**, **correct**, **objective**, **world-answerable**, **approximately true**, **only expressive**, **mere disquotation**, **coheres**, **warranted**, or **realist**, future revisions should answer at least these questions:

1. **truthbearer type** — proposition, belief, judgment, sentence-in-use, assertion, model-claim, record-entry, map-content, centered claim, norm-claim, or mixed case;
2. **target domain** — ordinary concrete object, process, event, structure, law, institution, modal claim, mathematical/abstract target, self-locating claim, normative target, or mixed domain;
3. **fit profile** — broad correspondence, narrow fact-correspondence, structural fit, approximate fit, coherence-laden fit, expressive/disquotational use, or mixed profile;
4. **worldly basis** — entity, process, power-profile, structure, organization, obtaining state of affairs, bounded totality, institutional enactment, abstract object, no special truthmaker demand, or still unsettled;
5. **epistemic profile** — directly observed, inferentially supported, theory-mediated, model-idealized, institutionally certified, phenomenally given, or mixed access route;
6. **idealization / approximation profile** — literal, approximate, idealized-but-world-tracking, fictionalized-for-modeling, merely useful, or still unsettled;
7. **deflationary load** — whether the case mainly needs truth for disquotation, endorsement, generalization, or semantic ascent rather than metaphysical explanation;
8. **truthmaker pressure** — none, light, moderate, or heavy;
9. **reality verdict** — robustly reality-answerable truth, thin but still reality-anchored truth, mainly expressive truth-ascription, merely coherent/warranted claim not yet shown true, or still unsettled.

## Compression rules

When future revisions face truth-language, they should prefer the smallest adequate claim:

- if the issue is only semantic ascent, endorsement, or generalization, say that rather than inflating ontology;
- if the issue is what obtains, route first through facts, states of affairs, and truthmakers rather than using “truth” as a catch-all;
- if the issue is accuracy of models or maps, say how idealization and approximation work rather than forcing either exact mirrorism or anti-realism;
- if the issue is scientific realism, say what is claimed to be true: equations, structure, entities, mechanisms, selective aspects, or some mixed target;
- if the issue is institutional truth, say what role records, enactments, and valid procedures play;
- and if correspondence is invoked, say what is corresponding to what, at what grain, and with what truthmaker pressure.

## Working conclusion

The archive should now prefer a **typed reality-answerable truth realism**:

> **Truth is not merely a compliment paid to accepted sentences; it is answerability to how things are. But that answerability is domain-sensitive and need not take the form of one proprietary matching fact for every truth.**

That conclusion is strong enough to keep the archive non-skeptical and realist, but restrained enough to preserve the real insights of deflationary, coherence, and pragmatist pressure without letting any of them dissolve reality-contact.
