# Support, Bearing, Suspension, Hanging, Bracing, and Load-Path Carriage

## Why this file exists

The archive now has explicit files on **scaffolding / support / niche construction / environmental enablement**, **hosting / lodging / carriage / guest occupancy**, **attachment / anchoring / tethering / mooring / docking**, **joining / bonding / welding / brazing / soldering**, **articulation / hinging / pivoting**, **clamping / compression-locking**, and **gripping / traction / friction-drive**, but it still lacks one direct file saying what to do when a case is described as **supported by**, **bearing on**, **resting on**, **suspended from**, **hanging from**, **braced by**, **stayed by**, **propped by**, **mounted on a support**, **carried by a load path**, or **kept up by a supporting structure**.

That omission matters because the archive still risks nine opposite mistakes:

- treating every real **support relation** as if it were only generic background enablement,
- treating every **supported-by** case as if it were already **attachment**, **hosting**, or **constitution**,
- treating every **suspension** relation as if it were only location in space rather than load-carried connection,
- treating every **brace** or **stay** as if it were merely another joined part with no special stabilizing profile,
- treating every **same support surface** as if it meant **same supported bearer**,
- treating every **same supported bearer** as if it meant **same support route or same support structure**,
- treating every **load path** as if it were only engineering jargon rather than a clue to real dependence architecture,
- treating every **resting on** or **borne by** case as if it collapsed boundaries between the supported and the supporter,
- or smuggling the differences among **bearing**, **suspension**, **hanging**, **bracing**, **shoring**, **supporting**, and **load-path carriage** into neighboring files without typing them.

This file gives the archive a compact **support / bearing / suspension / hanging / bracing / load-path-carriage profile** so future revisions can say whether a case is chiefly about resting support, suspended load carriage, anti-sway or anti-tip bracing, rack-or-bracket support, distributed load path, or stronger incorporation only in harder cases.

## Compressed picture

> **One thing can genuinely support another without thereby becoming numerically identical with it.**

And more carefully:

> **Support, bearing, suspension, hanging, bracing, and load-path carriage are typed ways in which one target carries, redirects, resists, stabilizes, or transfers the weight, imposed load, inertial demand, or displacement tendency of another without that alone settling identity, parthood, hosting, or fusion.**

## Why the default view should absorb this operator-family

The archive's working view already says:

- **process** matters, because support relations are maintained, overloaded, redistributed, braced, suspended, released, and repaired across time,
- **powers and functions** matter, because supports carry load, preserve geometry, prevent tipping, arrest motion, or create a stable working platform,
- **structure** matters, because the same overall target can be supported through one column, many legs, ropes, brackets, rails, foundations, or distributed load paths,
- **dependence pluralism** matters, because support should not be collapsed into attachment, hosting, joining, constitution, or grounding,
- **boundary discipline** matters, because one thing can bear another's load while the two remain distinct,
- **failure-mode discipline** matters, because settling, sway, buckling, overload, loss of footing, broken suspension, or lost bracing reveal real support architecture,
- and **artifact-risk discipline** matters, because language like *holds up*, *supports*, *carries*, *hangs from*, or *rests on* often overstates metaphysical intimacy.

So the default view should absorb support-language without flattening it.

## What this operator-family is for

This operator-family is for cases where one target is **held up, borne, stabilized, or load-carried by another**.

The relation may involve:

- **a scaffold, platform, beam, or frame bearing on footings, columns, or a foundation**, 
- **a suspended platform, line, payload, or person hanging from ropes, harness points, or anchorages**,
- **a brace, guy, stay, outrigger, or strut preventing sway, tip, or lateral displacement**,
- **an instrument, avionics box, rack, shelf, or bracket supported by a larger structure**,
- **a spacecraft subsystem mounted on a support structure that serves as a principal load path**,
- or **one larger target continuing while the particular support route or support members change**.

The key question is not simply whether one item is near or attached to another, but **whether the explanatory center is load carriage, support routing, anti-fall stabilization, or imposed-load transfer rather than residence, path-guidance, bond-making, or identity**.

## Why the archive needs it

### 1. Support is not the same as hosting or carriage

A thing can be supported by another without being housed in it, socially hosted by it, or transported as cargo by it.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **resting on or borne by**,
- **hosted in or on**,
- **carried as cargo or passenger**,
- **attached to for some other reason**,
- and **stronger incorporation only in harder cases**.

Otherwise it will keep flattening support into hosting or carriage.

### 2. Suspension is not the same as mere attachment

A suspended or hanging relation is explanatory precisely because a load is being carried across maintained separation.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **attached but not load-carrying**,
- **suspended from and load-borne by**,
- **mere proximity below an overhead point**,
- **full enclosure or containment**,
- and **same suspended pair only where separately earned**.

Otherwise it will keep mistaking the relevant relation for generic fastening.

### 3. Bracing is not the same as joining

A brace, guy, stay, outrigger, or shore can stabilize a structure or resist displacement without thereby being the main joint-making story of the assembly.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **brace-mediated stabilization**,
- **ordinary seam or joint-making**,
- **same support geometry**,
- **same joined assembly**,
- and **fusion only in stronger cases**.

Otherwise it will keep letting joined-language swallow stability architecture.

### 4. Same support structure does not mean same supported bearer

One support point, rack, shelf, hook, anchorage, bracket, or footing can successively bear different loads or different bearers across time.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **same support site under changing supported targets**,
- **same rack or bracket under replaced equipment**,
- **same anchorage under changing suspended users**,
- **same floor or footing under changing supported structure**,
- and **same support-target pair only where that relation itself is the target**.

Otherwise it will keep letting support continuity masquerade as bearer continuity.

### 5. Same supported bearer does not mean same support route

A continuing target can remain the same while different legs, ropes, columns, braces, racks, or foundations carry it at different times.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **same bearer under changed support route**,
- **same platform under new hangers or ropes**,
- **same equipment under new rack or bracket**,
- **same structure under redistributed load path**,
- and **same support architecture only when separately earned**.

Otherwise it will keep letting support-route turnover masquerade as bearer turnover.

### 6. Load path matters

Some support cases are not just about one contact point but about a larger route through which loads are carried, distributed, or redirected.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **local contact point**,
- **principal load path**,
- **secondary or redundant support path**,
- **braced anti-tip path**,
- and **same whole only where the relevant grain is stated**.

Otherwise it will keep confusing point-contact continuity with structural-support continuity.

### 7. Support can be static, dynamic, temporary, or fail-safe

A support relation may be temporary during assembly, dynamic under motion, redundant under safety rules, or continuously maintained during use.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **temporary propping or shoring**,
- **routine working support**,
- **dynamic suspension under moving load**,
- **backup or independent support line**,
- and **support failure only in stronger collapse cases**.

Otherwise it will keep treating support as one flat state.

## Typology of support-like cases

### A. Resting and bearing cases

The key feature is that one target bears on another, and the supporting surface, frame, footing, or member carries the imposed load.

**Examples:** scaffold uprights on base plates and mud sills; a column on a finished floor or leveling plate; equipment on a rack with known load-carrying capability.

### B. Suspended and hanging cases

The key feature is that one target is held aloft or retained by ropes, harness points, hangers, or overhead members that carry the load across maintained separation.

**Examples:** suspension scaffolds, suspended workers in harness systems, hanging payloads, suspended modules.

### C. Bracing, guying, shoring, and anti-tip cases

The key feature is that auxiliary members resist sway, eccentric load effects, tipping, or displacement rather than merely making a seam.

**Examples:** guys, ties, braces, outriggers, shoring members, anti-tip supports.

### D. Rack, bracket, shelf, and mount-support cases

The key feature is that a supported device or subsystem is held in place by a support architecture designed to withstand expected loads.

**Examples:** avionics racks and shelves, brackets carrying equipment, mounted subsystems on a support structure.

### E. Primary-structure and load-path cases

The key feature is that what matters is not merely one support point but a larger structural route through which loads are carried.

**Examples:** a spacecraft support structure, a principal load path through a frame, redistributed support across multiple members.

### F. Continuity theater through support labels

The key feature is that language of *supported by*, *borne on*, *resting on*, or *held up by* hides what is really replacement, attachment-only talk, hosting, or a changing support architecture.

**Examples:** “same mounted unit” talk that tracks only one rack location across swaps; “same supported module” talk that hides changed support members; “same structure” talk that quietly trades on changed principal load paths.

## Cross-domain pressure

### Foundations, base plates, and adequate supporting surfaces

Construction cases pressure the archive because a scaffold or column can be genuinely borne by a footing, foundation, or supporting surface without that supporting base becoming the same bearer as the supported structure.

### Suspension and separate anchor points

Safety cases pressure the archive because a dropline, tieback, or harness anchorage creates a real support relation and a real suspended load path without turning the suspended worker or platform into one bearer with the structural member.

### Principal load paths and support structures

Aerospace cases pressure the archive because support structures and principal load paths are not decorative descriptions; they specify how one larger system carries subsystems and imposed loads while still preserving typed interfaces and replaceable mounted elements.

## False moves to avoid

- Do **not** infer identity from every case of being **supported by**.
- Do **not** infer generic attachment from every case of being **suspended from**.
- Do **not** infer hosting from every case of being **mounted on a support**.
- Do **not** infer one flat relation from **bearing on**, **resting on**, **hung from**, **braced by**, **guyed to**, **supported by**, or **carried through**.
- Do **not** infer same supported bearer from same support surface.
- Do **not** infer same support route from same supported bearer.
- Do **not** infer fusion from load-bearing intimacy.

## Default diagnostic questions

When a case is described as **supported by**, **bearing on**, **resting on**, **suspended from**, **hanging from**, **braced by**, **guyed to**, **mounted on a support**, or **carried through a load path**, ask:

1. What exactly is the **target** — one worker, platform, beam, structure, device, module, vehicle, or mixed organization?
2. Is the case chiefly about **resting support**, **suspension**, **hanging**, **bracing**, **rack-or-bracket support**, **principal load path**, **temporary shoring**, or a mixed architecture?
3. What exactly is the **supporting basis** — one floor, footing, structural member, rope, hanger, anchorage, bracket, rack, frame, or distributed support structure?
4. What is doing the explanatory work — **weight carriage**, **inertial load resistance**, **anti-tip stabilization**, **sway control**, **load redistribution**, **mount support**, or mixed basis?
5. Does the support remain the same under changing supported targets, does the supported target remain the same under changed supports, or is the case really about one changing **support pair**?
6. What is the relevant **grain** — one contact point, one local bracket, one hanging line, one principal load path, one whole support structure, or mixed levels?
7. What is the relevant **redundancy profile** — single support, distributed support, independent backup support line, fail-safe support, or none?
8. What is the nearest **comparison class** — scaffolding and enablement, attachment, hosting, carriage, guidance, joining, or constitution?
9. What is the relevant **artifact-risk profile** — slot-tracking confusion, support-surface inflation, hidden support-route change, or mixed?
10. Whether the best verdict is **typed bearing support**, **typed suspension**, **typed hanging support**, **typed bracing / anti-tip stabilization**, **typed rack-or-bracket support**, **typed principal load-path carriage**, **stronger incorporation only in harder cases**, or **still unsettled**.

## Compression rule

Future revisions should normally treat **support / bearing / suspension / hanging / bracing / load-path carriage** as one operator-family whenever the explanatory burden is: **one target held up, carried, stabilized, or routed through another under a typed support relation; unresolved question whether the relation is environmental enablement, generic attachment, hosting, carriage, joining, constitution, or fusion; and the need to distinguish continuing support architecture from continuing bearer identity rather than letting load-carrying language settle the ontology by itself.**

## Anchor references

- Occupational Safety and Health Administration, **29 CFR 1926.451** — states that ties, guys, braces, or outriggers shall be used to prevent tipping where eccentric load is applied, that supported scaffold poles and uprights shall bear on base plates and mud sills or other adequate firm foundation, and that suspension scaffold support devices shall rest on surfaces capable of supporting specified multiples of the imposed load, useful for distinguishing bearing support, anti-tip bracing, and suspended load carriage from mere proximity or generic attachment.  
  https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.451

- Occupational Safety and Health Administration, **29 CFR 1926.755** — states that columns shall be set on level finished floors, leveling plates, leveling nuts, or shim packs adequate to transfer construction loads, and that columns must be evaluated for needed guying or bracing, useful for distinguishing ordinary support-bearing and stabilization from generic joining talk.  
  https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.755

- Centers for Disease Control and Prevention / NIOSH, **"Preventing Worker Injuries & Deaths Caused by Falls From Suspension Scaffolds"** — states that structurally sound portions of buildings should be used to anchor droplines and tiebacks for suspension scaffold support devices and that separate anchor points on structural members matter, useful for distinguishing suspended support paths from mere attached-to language.  
  https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/docs/92-108/default.html

- National Aeronautics and Space Administration, **NASA-STD-5002A, "Load Analyses of Spacecraft and Payloads"** — defines **primary structure** as the principal load path for subsystems, components, and other structures, and states that a spacecraft can consist of a support structure onto which systems are attached, useful for distinguishing one continuing support architecture from the particular mounted elements it carries.  
  https://standards.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/standards/NASA/A/0/nasa-std-5002a.pdf

- Federal Aviation Administration, **AC 43.13-2B** — states that when manufacturer guidance is unavailable, equipment should be installed in locations of known load carrying capabilities or on support racks, brackets, or shelves attached to aircraft structure so the mounting will withstand specified inertia forces, useful for distinguishing supported equipment architecture from mere placement or generic attachment.  
  https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac%2043.13-2b.pdf
