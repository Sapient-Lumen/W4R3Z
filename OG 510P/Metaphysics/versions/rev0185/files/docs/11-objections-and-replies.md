# Main Objections to Layered Process Realism

This file forces the archive's default view to survive its strongest internal objections.

## Why this file exists

A research archive becomes useless if its working synthesis is only ever elaborated and never pressure-tested. This file records the objections that most deserve to destabilize the current default.

## Objection 1: the view is an eclectic bundle

**Charge:** the theory keeps adding primitives — form, process, power, structure, grounding — without showing why these belong together rather than merely coexisting in one slogan.

**Reply:** this objection is serious, and the archive should keep it alive. But the current answer is that the five operators answer different irreducible metaphysical questions:

- form — what makes this the kind of thing it is,
- process — how it persists or changes,
- power — what it can do or undergo,
- structure — how relations can be constitutive,
- grounding — in virtue of what it obtains.

The archive should treat the view as defeated if a leaner theory can handle those jobs equally well with fewer primitives.

## Objection 2: process talk dissolves genuine objects

**Charge:** if objects are only stabilized processes, then ordinary and medium-scale entities lose robust identity.

**Reply:** the current synthesis is not pure process metaphysics. It says that many entities are **stabilized processual organizations**, not mere flowing events. Form and organization do the work of individuation. The objection succeeds only if the archive cannot explain persistence conditions better than a substance-first rival.

## Objection 3: powers and structure double-count the same work

**Charge:** if powers already encode what things can do, and structure already encodes relations, then the theory may simply be redescribing one phenomenon with two nouns.

**Reply:** sometimes they do overlap. But the archive currently treats them as distinct because a thing's powers and the wider structure in which those powers are organized can come apart analytically. A molecule may have local dispositions while a network exhibits system-level constraints not attributable to any isolated node taken alone. Still, future revisions should try to compress this distinction where possible.

## Objection 4: grounding remains too schematic

**Charge:** even after separating dependence relations, the theory still risks saying that higher levels are "grounded" whenever they seem important but non-basic.

**Reply:** this is why the archive now insists on the narrowest adequate relation first: composition, constitution, realization, grounding, then causation. Grounding is indispensable, but the default view should be treated as weakened whenever it invokes grounding where a more specific relation would do.

## Objection 5: emergence is still a rhetoric risk

**Charge:** the archive's emergence talk may simply protect favored higher-level domains from reduction without adding real criteria.

**Reply:** the objection was strong enough to warrant a dedicated admission test. The current answer is that emergence is not a peer primitive; it is a threshold notion licensed only when dependence completeness, organization sensitivity, explanatory indispensability, and a stable new modal profile are all present. If core examples repeatedly fail that test, the archive should sharply reduce its use of emergence language.

## Objection 6: the theory is unclear about laws

**Charge:** the archive rejects both thin Humean summary views and crude governing-law pictures, but without a clear alternative it risks obscurity.

**Reply:** the present answer is that laws are derivative articulations of real modal-structural constraints in a world of power-bearing processes. That is tighter than simple Humeanism and less reified than governing-law views. But this remains a live fault line rather than a closed victory.

## What would actually defeat the current default

The archive should demote Layered Process Realism if any of the following happens:

1. a leaner theory explains persistence, dependence, modality, and higher-level reality with fewer primitives,
2. a substance-first view handles change and turnover just as well without process-first commitments,
3. a monist or neutral baseline integrates mind, structure, and lawfulness more cleanly,
4. or the distinction among form, power, structure, and process collapses into a better kernel.

## Working verdict

The current synthesis survives these objections **provisionally**, not triumphantly.

That is the right standing for an archive default: not canonized, but still best in play.
