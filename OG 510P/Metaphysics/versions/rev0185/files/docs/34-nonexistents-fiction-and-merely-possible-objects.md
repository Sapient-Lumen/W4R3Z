# Nonexistents, Fiction, and Merely Possible Objects

## Why this file exists

The archive already had files on existence, ontological commitment, modality, truthmakers, abstracta, and identity. But it still lacked a direct policy for cases where discourse seems to target **what is not concretely there**: fictional characters, mythical beings, abandoned hypotheses, design targets, merely possible individuals, and impossible descriptions.

Without a dedicated rule here, the archive risks three opposite mistakes:

- treating every meaningful singular term or quantifier as an ontological commitment,
- treating all such discourse as harmless paraphrase with no metaphysical pressure,
- or flattening fictional entities, merely possible objects, intentional objects, idealized entities, and impossible descriptions into one mixed bag.

## Core distinctions

### 1. Reference practice is not yet ontological commitment

A discourse can successfully track, compare, explain, or evaluate something without thereby introducing a new concrete occupant of reality.

The archive should therefore distinguish:

- **successful discourse**,
- **truth-conditions for that discourse**,
- **what ontological category, if any, is required**,
- and **what level of reality or dependence is being claimed**.

### 2. Fictional, merely possible, and impossible targets are different cases

The archive should not treat the following as one issue:

- **fictional entities** — Sherlock Holmes, Anna Karenina, Middle-earth,
- **mythic or culturally inherited targets** — Zeus, Pegasus,
- **failed theoretical posits** — Vulcan,
- **merely possible individuals** — an unactualized child, an unrealized invention,
- **intentional or design targets** — the house we plan, the law we draft,
- **impossible descriptions** — a round square.

These differ in origin, modal status, truth-conditions, and ontological pressure.

### 3. Default archive stance: typed realism without easy Meinongian inflation

The archive default is:

- be realist about the **truth and explanatory role** of discourse involving such targets,
- prefer **typed semantic or representational treatments** before positing a vast realm of nonexistents,
- allow that some targets may be **derivative abstracta or culturally created artifacts**,
- keep **merely possible objects** available as a live metaphysical option rather than the default ontology,
- and treat **impossible objects** primarily as diagnostic products of description unless a heavier theory is independently earned.

This keeps the archive open to Meinongian and possibilist pressure while avoiding a standing commitment to unrestricted domains full of nonexistent objects.

## The archive's default profile

### A. Fiction first: use truth-in-fiction, cultural creation, and content-structure before ontic inflation

For paradigmatic fictional cases, the archive should ask first:

- Is the target best handled by **truth in a story**?
- Is there a **created abstractum, role, or content-structure** doing explanatory work?
- Is the case primarily about a **practice-governed representational object**?

The default answer is usually **not** that a concrete detective, dragon, or city fails to exist while still literally inhabiting reality. The archive should prefer a layered treatment: authored representation, structured content, communal uptake, and derivative cultural artifacts where needed.

### B. Hypothesis cases are not fiction cases

Cases like **Vulcan** or **phlogiston** should not automatically be assimilated to fiction. Often the right treatment is:

- an intentional or theoretical posit,
- a failed explanatory commitment,
- and a historically real discourse-practice about something that was not in fact instantiated as supposed.

The archive should distinguish the **existence of the theory, model, name-use, and inferential role** from the **nonexistence of the intended concrete target**.

### C. Merely possible objects remain a live but non-default ontology

The archive should keep open that some modal metaphysics may quantify over merely possible objects. But its default is lighter:

- use **essence, powers, structure, and world-semantics** to articulate modal space,
- treat merely possible individuals as a **representational or modal-resource question** unless heavier ontology is independently earned,
- and distinguish **de re modal profiles of actual things** from commitment to a domain of nonactual objects.

### D. Impossible objects are mainly tests of descriptive discipline

Impossible-object talk is usually best treated as revealing one of three things:

- inconsistent description,
- incompatible property-ascriptions under one guise,
- or a useful intentional/representational target for reasoning.

The archive should therefore default away from a populated ontology of impossible entities.

## A decision rule for future revisions

Before adding ontology in a nonexistent-object case, state five things:

1. **Target type** — fictional, mythic, hypothetical, merely possible, intentional, idealized, impossible, or mixed.
2. **Truth regime** — truth in fiction, failed empirical hypothesis, modal description, design representation, impossible description, or some mixed profile.
3. **Best ontological treatment** — no object needed, derivative abstractum, intentional object, culturally created artifact, merely possible object, or heavier nonexistent-object ontology.
4. **Commitment level** — representational only, thinly dependent, derivative but robust, or basic.
5. **Identity basis** — text/content, authorship/history, modal role, intentional specification, social uptake, or none.

If those five items are not stated, the archive should treat the case as underdescribed.

## Default verdicts by case-type

### Fictional entities

Default: **derivative representational/cultural reality**, not concrete nonexistence-with-being.

### Mythic and legendary entities

Default: treat like fiction-plus-cultural inheritance unless a live religious or ritual ontology in the target domain earns a thicker treatment.

### Failed scientific posits

Default: the discourse and model are real; the intended concrete entity may not be.

### Merely possible individuals

Default: keep open as a modal-metaphysical option, but do not posit by default.

### Impossible objects

Default: treat as descriptive or intentional artifacts unless a stronger impossible-object ontology is explicitly defended.

## Compression payoff for the archive

This file tightens five older files at once:

- `21-being-existence-and-ontological-commitment.md` by separating discourse success from existential verdict,
- `23-abstracta-propositions-numbers-and-structures.md` by allowing created or structured cultural abstracta where appropriate,
- `15-essence-possibility-and-necessity.md` by separating modal articulation from commitment to nonactual objects,
- `13-truthmakers-absences-and-privation.md` by clarifying what makes fictional and nonexistence claims true,
- `29-identity-individuality-and-haecceity.md` by requiring a typed identity-basis for fictional and merely possible targets.

## Current archive default

The archive should therefore say:

- **not every meaningful name denotes an existent**,
- **not every apparently singular discourse is ontologically empty**,
- **fictional and merely possible cases need not receive the same verdict**,
- **created, structured, or practice-dependent abstracta may sometimes do the required work**,
- and **heavier Meinongian or possibilist ontologies remain live options, but not the archive default**.
