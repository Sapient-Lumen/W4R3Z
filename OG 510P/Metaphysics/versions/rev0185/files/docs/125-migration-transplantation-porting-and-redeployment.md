# Migration, Transplantation, Porting, and Redeployment

This file adds a compact operator-family for cases where something apparently **keeps going across a changed host, site, vehicle, platform, or local home**.

The archive needs this because it already has:

- **rerouting / bypass / shunting** for alternate paths within one larger bearer,
- **substitution / replacement / stand-ins** for changed parts, occupants, or realizers,
- **delegation / handoff** for transferred performance or control,
- **duplication / branching** for copied or fissioned continuities,
- and **succession** for later bearers inheriting an earlier line.

But it still lacks one compact place for cases described as **migrated**, **transplanted**, **ported**, **moved to another host**, **redeployed**, **relocated without stopping**, **shifted to another environment**, or **transferred into a new body / node / site / platform**.

Without that operator-family, the archive will keep risking seven compressions:

- collapsing **relocation**, **host migration**, **transplantation**, **porting**, **redeployment**, **handoff**, and **succession** into one flat story,
- inferring **same bearer** from uninterrupted service or function alone,
- inferring **new bearer** from every host or site change,
- confusing **moved whole** with **moved part**,
- confusing **ported type or design** with **persisting token**,
- treating **same organization at a new site** as either trivial motion or total refounding,
- and reading every talk of **migration** as if it already settled whether the source persisted, ceased, duplicated, or only yielded a successor.

This file gives the archive a compact **migration / transplantation / porting / redeployment profile** so future revisions can say whether a case is chiefly about same-bearer relocation, migration of an ongoing hosted process, transplantation of one part into a new bearer, porting of a design or codebase to a new platform, redeployment of one role or capacity within a larger organization, or stronger successor continuity under migration-like language.

## Compressed picture

> **Migration, transplantation, porting, and redeployment are best understood as typed ways in which one organized thing, process, service, capacity, organ, office, or design can change site, host, vehicle, platform, or local embedding without thereby making location-change, host-change, role-transfer, implementation-change, and bearer-identity all the same metaphysical question.**

## Why the default view should absorb this operator-family

The archive's working view already says:

- **process** matters, because many continuities are maintained activities rather than static lumps,
- **form and organization** matter, because what persists through transfer often depends on structured operation rather than mere matter-presence,
- **powers and functions** matter, because some capacities survive host-change while others do not,
- **history** matters, because origin, trajectory, and transfer-path can constrain identity,
- **dependence pluralism** matters, because hosting, realization, constitution, implementation, and occupancy should not be collapsed,
- **sortals** matter, because persons, organs, offices, services, institutions, and codebases do not migrate under one criterion,
- and **artifact-risk discipline** matters, because continuity labels are often operational, legal, administrative, or marketing shortcuts.

So the default view should absorb migration-language without flattening it.

## What this operator-family is for

This operator-family is for cases where continuity is claimed **across changed local embedding**.

The embedding change may involve:

- a new **place**,
- a new **host body**,
- a new **machine or node**,
- a new **implementation platform**,
- a new **team, office, or site**,
- or a new **recipient organization**.

The key question is not simply whether something moved, but **what exactly moved across what kind of support-change**.

## Why the archive needs it

### 1. Host migration is not the same as simple relocation

Something can change location while remaining the same bearer. But something can also remain functionally available while its activity is migrated from one host or node to another.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **same bearer spatially relocated**,
- **same running workload under changed host**,
- **same service with changed local implementation site**,
- **same public interface with contested token continuity underneath**,
- and **successor deployment only in stronger cases**.

Otherwise it will keep treating changed place and changed host as the same metaphysical event.

### 2. Transplantation of one part is not migration of the whole donor

A donated organ can be moved into a recipient without the donor individual migrating into the recipient. Likewise, a transplanted module or graft can preserve one capacity while changing the wider bearer that contains it.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **transfer of one organ or module**,
- **same recipient bearer under new incorporated part**,
- **continued donor nonidentity despite transferred material**,
- **restored or supported capacity in the recipient**,
- and **whole-bearer migration only in very different cases**.

Otherwise it will keep letting transferred parts masquerade as transferred wholes.

### 3. Live migration is not the same as unchanged implementation

A process, workload, or service may remain available while the host underneath it changes. That can support one continuity claim at the service grain without supporting identity at every lower grain.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **same service continuity**,
- **same running instance under host transfer**,
- **new low-level carrier with preserved high-level availability**,
- **brief overlap or staging during transfer**,
- and **mere restart or successor boot only in harder cases**.

Otherwise it will keep inferring either perfect sameness at every level or total discontinuity from host-change alone.

### 4. Porting is not the same as moving one token

A codebase, design, or recipe can be ported to a new platform while preserving much of its structure, purpose, or interface. But that often preserves **type, specification, or project continuity** more readily than **one numerically same running token**.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **same design or code lineage across platforms**,
- **same project under retargeted implementation**,
- **same interface family with changed underlying realization**,
- **new token deployment descended from an older one**,
- and **one persisting token only in the narrower cases that really earn it**.

Otherwise it will keep turning portability into identity.

### 5. Redeployment is not the same as same local bearer

A team, office, service line, or institutional function can be redeployed to a new site, jurisdiction, or subunit while the larger organization remains the same.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **same larger organization with changed local site**,
- **same role or capacity now exercised elsewhere**,
- **same office under transferred seat or jurisdiction**,
- **changed local unit beneath wider continuity**,
- and **organizational successorhood only in stronger rupture cases**.

Otherwise it will keep flattening local relocation and organizational refounding.

### 6. Source persistence matters

Sometimes the source ceases when migration completes. Sometimes the source remains while a ported or copied successor appears elsewhere. Sometimes there is staged overlap.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **move with source vacancy afterward**,
- **copy with source persistence**,
- **staged overlap during transfer**,
- **handoff after temporary dual support**,
- and **branching continuity where one line cannot be simply identified with the other**.

Otherwise it will keep using migration language where duplication or succession would be more accurate.

### 7. Vehicle dependence is not all-or-nothing

Some continuities depend deeply on a particular host or body. Others tolerate new support so long as the right organization, permissions, or interface relations are preserved.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **host-essential cases**,
- **host-flexible but organization-sensitive cases**,
- **role-continuous but occupant-variable cases**,
- **platform-flexible but implementation-sensitive cases**,
- and **apparent transportability that dissolves under closer grain-sensitive analysis**.

Otherwise it will keep oscillating between crude substrate essentialism and crude substrate indifference.

## Typology of migration-like cases

### A. Same-bearer relocation

The key feature is that one bearer changes place while remaining the same individual or organized whole.

**Examples:** a person moving houses; a relocated sculpture; a server physically moved to another rack.

### B. Hosted-process migration

The key feature is that one ongoing workload, service, or controlled process is said to continue while the supporting host changes.

**Examples:** live migration of a virtual machine between hosts; moving a running service to another node with minimal interruption; shifting an active control loop to a backup processor.

### C. Transplantation into a new bearer

The key feature is that one organ, module, graft, or structured part is transferred into a recipient bearer, changing recipient capacity without moving the donor whole.

**Examples:** kidney transplant; grafted vessel; transplanted subsystem inserted into a larger assembly.

### D. Porting or retargeted implementation

The key feature is that one design, codebase, protocol, or recipe is adapted to a new platform while preserving lineage, purpose, or interface more readily than one persisting token.

**Examples:** porting software to a new architecture; retargeting a control algorithm to new hardware; migrating an institutional procedure into a new platform stack.

### E. Redeployment within a larger organization

The key feature is that one role, function, team, or service-line is reassigned to a new site, unit, or jurisdiction while wider organizational continuity is claimed.

**Examples:** moving a call center function to another region; redeploying a response team to a new district; shifting administrative processing from one office to another.

### F. Successor migration or continuity theater

The key feature is that migration-language masks what is better described as restart, refounding, copied successorhood, or label-preserving replacement.

**Examples:** a service “migrated” but actually rebuilt from snapshots; an institution “relocated” after charter rupture; a platform “ported” under brand continuity but with new code, permissions, and bearer conditions.

## Cross-domain pressure

### Organisms, grafts, and transplantation

Biological cases pressure the archive because one living recipient can persist through transplanted support, while the transferred organ does not carry the donor person with it.

### Brains, persons, and host-sensitive identity arguments

Personal-identity cases pressure the archive because thought experiments about brain transfer, cerebrum transfer, upload, or host exchange trade heavily on whether psychology, organism, or embodied subject is doing the identity work.

### Computation, services, and host transfer

Computational cases pressure the archive because one public service can remain stable while the supporting host changes, which tempts analysts either to overstate one token's persistence or to understate real higher-level continuity.

### Institutions, offices, and jurisdictional movement

Institutional cases pressure the archive because an office, court, service desk, or administrative function can move sites or platforms while the larger institutional order persists.

## False moves to avoid

- Do **not** infer same bearer from every successful migration.
- Do **not** infer new bearer from every changed host.
- Do **not** infer whole-donor migration from transplanted parts.
- Do **not** infer one persisting token from every ported design.
- Do **not** infer same local unit from same wider organization.
- Do **not** infer duplication from every staged overlap.
- Do **not** infer harmless relocation from every “redeployment” label.
- Do **not** infer one flat relation from **migrated**, **transplanted**, **ported**, **redeployed**, **shifted to another node**, **moved to a new body**, **rehosted**, **lift-and-shifted**, **evacuated**, or **kept running elsewhere**.

## Default diagnostic questions

When a case is described as **migrated**, **transplanted**, **ported**, **redeployed**, **rehosted**, **moved to another body**, **moved to another node**, **lifted and shifted**, or **kept running elsewhere**, ask:

1. What exactly is the **target** — one person, one organism, one organ, one artifact, one workload, one service, one office, one institution, one design, one codebase, or one mixed organization?
2. Is the case chiefly about **relocation**, **host migration**, **transplantation**, **porting**, **redeployment**, **duplication**, **handoff**, **succession**, or a mixed architecture?
3. What exactly is changing — **place**, **host**, **recipient bearer**, **implementation platform**, **jurisdiction**, **operator team**, **interface layer**, or mixed embedding?
4. What is doing the continuity work — **spatiotemporal continuity**, **organizational form**, **service availability**, **legal identity**, **code lineage**, **role continuity**, **embodied subject continuity**, or mixed basis?
5. Does the **source persist**, cease, overlap temporarily, branch into another line, or remain only as archived specification or donor origin?
6. What is the relevant **grain** — whole bearer, hosted process, role, organ, module, design, token implementation, or mixed levels?
7. What is the relevant **host-dependence profile** — host-essential, host-flexible, recipient-sensitive, legally anchored, platform-sensitive, or mixed?
8. What is the nearest **comparison class** — rerouting, substitution, prosthetic support, handoff, duplication, succession, repair, or artifact?
9. What is the relevant **artifact-risk profile** — marketing continuity, administrative label carryover, snapshot restart disguised as migration, donor/recipient confusion, or mixed?
10. Whether the best verdict is **same-bearer relocation**, **typed hosted-process migration**, **typed transplantation into a new bearer**, **typed porting / lineage continuity**, **typed redeployment within a larger organization**, **successor migration**, **continuity theater / label artifact**, or **still unsettled**.

## Compression rule

Future revisions should normally treat **migration / transplantation / porting / redeployment** as one operator-family whenever the explanatory burden is: **continuity claimed across changed host, site, recipient, or platform; unresolved question whether whole, part, role, service, or design is what persists; source/destination relation not yet typed; and the need to distinguish moved bearer, moved part, ported lineage, redeployed function, and successor continuity rather than letting one migration label settle the rest.**
