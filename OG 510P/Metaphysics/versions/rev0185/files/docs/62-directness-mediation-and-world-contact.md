# Directness, Mediation, and World-Contact

## Why this file exists

The archive already had files on **appearance**, **representation**, **objectivity**, **truth**, **ontological promotion**, and **measurement**. But it still lacked one direct file saying what the archive means when it moves from:

- seeming directness,
- instrument mediation,
- theory-laden observation,
- interface-guided access,
- model-assisted disclosure,
- perspectival presentation,
- or record- and readout-dependent contact

to a **metaphysical verdict** about whether the world itself is being reached.

Without a dedicated file here, the archive risks seven recurrent mistakes:

- treating **directness** as if it meant **absence of all mediation**,
- treating **mediation** as if it meant **loss of world-contact**,
- treating **representation** as if it automatically displaced **presentation**,
- treating **perspectival or interface-shaped access** as if it were merely subjective,
- treating **instrument-mediated detection** as if it were either fully transparent or merely fictional,
- treating **social or record-mediated access** as if it could never disclose anything real,
- or treating **error-possibility** as if it automatically destroyed every stronger notion of contact with reality.

This file gives the archive a compact **directness profile** so future revisions can say when a case is direct in a phenomenological, epistemic, causal, practical, or metaphysical sense; when mediation preserves contact; when mediation partly structures what is disclosed; and when interface or readout talk is being asked to do more metaphysical work than it can carry.

## Working default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Directness is typed, not unmediated transparency. Mediation is real, but it does not automatically cancel world-contact.**

And more specifically:

> **Treat world-contact as compatible with causal, perspectival, representational, and instrument-mediated access so long as the case still earns reality-answerability, correction conditions, and an intelligible target beyond the vehicle or interface.**

This fits the archive's wider commitments:

- **appearance** matters because reality often shows up perspectivally rather than from nowhere,
- **representation** matters because interfaces, models, records, and displays often mediate access,
- **measurement** matters because detection is frequently instrument-dependent without being merely arbitrary,
- **objectivity** matters because mediation can still be publicly criticizable and corrigible,
- **truth** matters because contact should remain answerable to the world rather than to the vehicle alone,
- and **promotion/status** matter because some mediated access earns realism about the target, while other cases earn realism only about the vehicle, the pattern, or the practice.

## Core distinctions

### 1. Directness is not one thing

The archive should distinguish:

- **phenomenological directness** — how experience presents its object,
- **epistemic directness** — whether justification or warrant is inferentially mediated,
- **causal/direct-interaction profiles** — whether contact involves a continuous chain of interaction,
- **representational directness** — whether the target is presented through a vehicle or surrogate,
- **practical directness** — whether an agent engages the target itself rather than a detached description,
- and **metaphysical directness** — whether the target itself is in play rather than only something standing in for it.

Otherwise the archive will keep treating one use of “direct” as if it settled all others.

### 2. Mediation is not automatically distortion

The archive should distinguish:

- **mere enabling mediation**,
- **selective or aspectual disclosure**,
- **perspectival shaping**,
- **theory-guided interpretation**,
- **proxy substitution**,
- **distorting interface**,
- and **fiction-generating scaffold**.

Otherwise the archive will keep treating every mediation layer as if it either transparently vanished or fatally severed contact.

### 3. Presentation and representation are not identical

The archive should distinguish:

- **direct presentation of ordinary objects or properties**,
- **representation of a target through content, model, or signal**,
- **display or interface format**,
- **record or trace left by an interaction**,
- and **mere imaginative or fictional vehicle**.

Otherwise the archive will keep sliding from presentation to content, from representation to unreality, and from display to target.

### 4. Opacity of method is not opacity of target

The archive should distinguish:

- **transparent-seeming access**,
- **interpreted readout**,
- **opaque algorithmic or statistical processing**,
- **publicly criticizable mediation**,
- and **merely black-box dependence with no stable target-fixation**.

Otherwise the archive will keep assuming that if a route to a target is complicated, then no real target is being reached.

### 5. Perspective is not yet subjectivism

The archive should distinguish:

- **perspectival access to one world**,
- **centered but objective fact**,
- **response-dependent but real profile**,
- **observer-sensitive setup**,
- and **merely private or projective seeming**.

Otherwise the archive will keep treating every perspective-loaded case as if it forced anti-realism.

### 6. Instrument mediation is not mere representational detour

The archive should distinguish:

- **instrument-assisted perception**,
- **detection through traces or effects**,
- **model-mediated reconstruction**,
- **proxy-based inference**,
- and **vehicle-only manipulation with no stable target beyond it**.

Otherwise the archive will keep treating microscopes, scans, spectra, dashboards, and indirect indicators as if they all belonged to one flat ontology of “not really seeing”.

### 7. Social mediation is not unreality

The archive should distinguish:

- **world-contact through records, testimony, offices, or databases**,
- **institutionally constituted statuses**,
- **publicly stabilised interfaces and classifications**,
- and **mere coordination fictions with no robust downstream profile**.

Otherwise the archive will keep treating mediated access to social reality as though it were inferior to bare perception in every ontological respect.

## Four live options

### 1. Unmediated-access nostalgia

**Picture:** genuine directness requires something like immediate, uninterpreted, or non-mediated access.

**What it gets right:**
- contact with reality should not be dissolved into endless representation,
- and some cases do feel and function as more direct than others.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it overstates the contrast between directness and mediation,
- and it underdescribes how correction, calibration, perspective, embodiment, and instrumentation can preserve genuine access.

### 2. Total mediation constructivism

**Picture:** all access is interface-bound and theory-saturated, so talk of direct contact with reality should be abandoned.

**What it gets right:**
- access is often shaped by concepts, vehicles, practices, and perspectives,
- and transparency is rarely perfect.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it makes it too hard to explain error, correction, target stability, and why some mediations are better than others at disclosing one world.

### 3. Vehicle realism without target discipline

**Picture:** what is directly available is the signal, model, display, experience, or record; target realism should be suspended or reduced to that vehicle.

**What it gets right:**
- interfaces and representations deserve ontological and methodological attention,
- and access often arrives through vehicles rather than by bare confrontation.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it tempts the archive either to stop at the vehicle or to promote it too quickly,
- and it underdescribes the typed relations that can bind vehicles to worldly targets.

### 4. Typed mediated realism

**Picture:** access to reality is often mediated, but not therefore cut off. Directness is domain-typed. What matters is whether the mediation preserves an intelligible target, correction conditions, worldly constraint, and stable answerability.

**What it gets right:**
- it preserves contact without mythologizing transparency,
- it preserves mediation without collapsing into subjectivism or fictionalism,
- and it allows different kinds of directness in perception, action, measurement, social uptake, and self-locating access.

**What it risks if left alone:**
- it can become slogan-like unless each case states exactly what kind of directness and what kind of mediation are at issue.

## Archive commitments

### 1. Do not equate directness with absence of mediation

Some access is genuinely direct in the relevant sense even when causally, perspectivally, inferentially, or instrumentally mediated.

### 2. Do not equate mediation with loss of target

Mediation may enable, channel, reveal, or partially structure access without replacing the target with a mere fiction.

### 3. Require a target/vehicle/interface distinction before ontological verdicts

Do not read ontology straight off the vehicle, but do not assume the vehicle blocks contact either.

### 4. Keep error-possibility compatible with contact

Illusion, hallucination, noise, distortion, and misrepresentation matter because contact can fail; they do not by themselves show that every success-case lacks worldly contact.

### 5. Allow domain-typed directness

Perceptual, practical, first-personal, centered, instrument-mediated, and institutional cases need not all realize directness in the same way.

### 6. Prefer mediated world-contact over both transparency mythology and interface skepticism

The archive should default to the view that reality is often reached through forms of presentation, representation, instrumentation, and perspective that are corrigible rather than absolute.

## Directness profile rule for future revisions

Before inferring a metaphysical verdict from language like **direct**, **immediate**, **mediated**, **interface**, **through a model**, **through a readout**, **only representational**, **presented**, **given**, **transparent**, **indirect**, **openness to the world**, **contact**, **filter**, **display**, **trace**, or **only a vehicle**, future revisions should answer at least these questions:

1. **target** — ordinary object, process, property, structure, social status, centered fact, self-state, abstractum, or mixed target;
2. **access type** — perceptual, practical, first-personal, inferential, representational, instrument-mediated, institutional, or mixed access;
3. **directness type** — phenomenological, epistemic, causal, practical, representational, metaphysical, or mixed directness;
4. **mediation layer** — perspective, embodiment, concept-use, model, interface, detector, algorithmic processing, record, testimony, institution, or mixed mediation;
5. **target/vehicle relation** — presentation, representation, proxy tracking, reconstruction, constitutive procedure, or merely vehicle-internal play;
6. **error and correction profile** — what can go wrong, and what stabilizes better and worse access;
7. **dependence profile** — is the target mind-independent, response-dependent, observer-sensitive, setup-sensitive, institutionally constituted, centered, or mixed;
8. **promotion pressure** — does the case support realism about the target, only the structure, only the vehicle, or still no settled ontological promotion;
9. **final reality verdict** — robust mediated world-contact, partial but genuine disclosure, proxy-only access, mainly vehicle-level success, merely representational scaffold, or still unsettled.
