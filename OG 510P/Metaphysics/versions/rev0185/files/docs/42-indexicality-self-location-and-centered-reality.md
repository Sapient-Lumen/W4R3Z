# Indexicality, Self-Location, and Centered Reality

## Why this file exists

The archive already had files on time, space, identity, representation, social ontology, modality, and granularity. But it still lacked a direct rule for **indexicality**, **self-location**, and **centered reality**.

Without a dedicated file here, the archive risks five opposite mistakes:

- treating every indexical or perspective-marked claim as if it were merely linguistic froth rather than a clue to a real situated fact,
- treating every perspectival difference as if it forced a relativist or subjectivist metaphysics,
- treating **world-description** and **self-location within a world** as if they were the same kind of content,
- treating all centered structure as merely private first-person mentality rather than sometimes social, institutional, embodied, or interface-mediated positioning,
- or treating every “actual”, “here”, “now”, “I”, “we”, “this office”, “your turn”, or “you are here” fact as if it could be flattened without loss into one impersonal description.

This file gives the archive a compact **indexicality profile** so future revisions can say whether a case is chiefly about self-location, temporal or spatial orientation, role occupancy, centered action-guidance, communication, or only a representational convenience.

## Working default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Some truths are best understood as centered or self-locating truths: they concern how a subject, body, role-bearer, or system is situated within one world, not just which world is actual.**

This fits the archive's wider commitments:

- **form** still matters, because centered location is often relative to organized roles, bodies, institutions, and interfaces rather than to bare coordinates alone,
- **process** still matters, because many centered facts are action-guiding and update as positions, roles, and occasions change,
- **power** still matters, because what an agent can do often depends on where, when, and as whom it is situated,
- **structure** still matters, because centeredness is often defined over a structured space of agents, times, places, offices, turns, permissions, or records,
- and **grounding** still matters, because the reality of self-location should not be confused with a separate realm of private facts.

## Core distinctions

### 1. World-description and self-location are not the same

The archive should distinguish:

- **uncentered content** — how the world is, described without fixing a particular “you are here” position within it,
- **centered content** — how a subject or occupant is located within that world,
- **hybrid content** — content that mixes worldly description with centered positioning.

A person may know the whole map and still not know **where they are on it**. A theory may describe an institution accurately yet still not settle whose turn it is, who occupies an office, which node is active, or what is happening **now** from within that structure.

### 2. Indexicality is not only about language

The archive should not treat indexicals as a merely linguistic nuisance. Linguistic forms like **I**, **here**, **now**, **we**, **this**, **that**, **actual**, or **current** often mark an underlying metaphysical or cognitive difference between:

- generic description,
- self-location,
- embodied orientation,
- role occupancy,
- action-guiding relevance,
- and communication-sensitive updating.

The central issue is not the word itself, but the kind of position-sensitive fact or content the word helps express.

### 3. Centeredness is broader than first-person inwardness

The archive should distinguish several centered profiles:

- **de se** — about oneself,
- **de nunc** — about the current time,
- **de hic** — about the current place or region,
- **role-centered** — about one’s office, turn, task-position, platform-status, or institutional standing,
- **system-centered** — about a subsystem’s position within a larger organized whole.

So centered reality is not exhausted by private introspection. It can be bodily, spatial, temporal, social, institutional, or interface-mediated.

### 4. Centered facts do not imply subjectivism

That a fact is centered does **not** mean it is merely subjective or mind-dependent. “It is now noon here”, “I am the designated responder”, “this badge opens this door for me”, or “you are muted” can be objective enough while still requiring a center, position, or role for their full articulation.

The archive should therefore distinguish:

- **objective centered facts**,
- **subjective perspective reports**,
- **socially instituted position-facts**,
- and **merely context-setting representational parameters**.

### 5. Centering matters for action, not only for description

A great many metaphysical disputes quietly assume that all important content is map-like or spectator-like. But action often depends on centered facts:

- where **I** am,
- what time it is **now**,
- which option is live **for me here**,
- whether **this role** is currently occupied by me,
- whether **this interface** is addressed to my current session,
- or whether **we** are inside or outside a relevant boundary.

The archive should therefore not treat self-location as an optional afterthought. In many cases it is part of what makes a fact explanatorily relevant.

### 6. Perspective, actuality, and centered modeling must be kept distinct

The archive should distinguish:

- **actuality** — which world or situation is actual,
- **centeredness** — which agent/time/place/role within that world is at issue,
- **perspective** — the cognitive or informational route through which that centered fact is accessed,
- **representation** — how a model, sentence, record, or interface encodes the centered situation.

A metaphysics that blurs these together will keep sliding between semantics, epistemology, and ontology.

## Archive default

The current archive default is:

1. **Be realist but typed about centered facts.** Some self-locating and role-locating truths are genuinely worldly even when they cannot be flattened without loss into a purely uncentered description.
2. **Do not infer relativism from indexicality.** The need for a center does not by itself make a truth merely subjective.
3. **Do not reduce all centeredness to private mentality.** Bodies, offices, records, interfaces, and institutional roles can supply centers too.
4. **Treat action-guiding centeredness as metaphysically informative.** The difference between knowing the map and knowing where one stands in it is often explanatory, not merely verbal.
5. **Keep centeredness distinct from identity, reference, and modality.** Self-location is not automatically sameness, rigid designation, or world-selection.
6. **Allow mixed centered profiles.** A case can be at once temporal, spatial, bodily, institutional, and representationally mediated.
7. **Treat centered reality as typed, not as one global metaphysical revolution.** Different domains may earn different centered structures.

## Indexicality-profile rule for future revisions

Before inferring a metaphysical verdict from language like **I**, **here**, **now**, **actual**, **current**, **we**, **this**, **that**, **your turn**, **our office**, **present user**, **local**, **situated**, **observer-relative**, or **perspectival**, specify:

1. **center type** — agent, body, time, place, role, office, institution, interface, subsystem, or mixed case;
2. **content type** — de se, de nunc, de hic, role-relative, system-centered, uncentered, hybrid, or still unsettled;
3. **worldly basis** — embodiment, location, occupancy, turn-order, record state, access-position, legal status, coordination structure, or mixed basis;
4. **action relevance** — merely descriptive, communicative, updating-sensitive, action-guiding, normatively positioning, or mixed;
5. **representation mode** — natural-language indexical, map-marker, interface state, database flag, procedural rule, model center, or mixed vehicle;
6. **flattening cost** — whether the case can be translated into uncentered form without loss, only with practical loss, only by adding heavy machinery, or not cleanly at all;
7. **reality verdict** — objective centered fact, derivative but robust position-fact, merely representational parameter, or still unsettled.

Otherwise the archive will keep sliding from indexical language to subjectivism, from self-location to identity, and from perspective-sensitive action to mere semantics.

## Stress cases

### Time and tense

The archive should ask whether a case is really about temporal order in the world, about presentness, or about a subject’s temporal location within the world. “Now” may do different work in each case.

### Place and embodiment

The archive should ask whether “here” marks exact location, operational reach, embodied perspective, regional occupancy, or merely a discourse center.

### Institutions and roles

A fact such as “it is your turn”, “she is the current chair”, or “this badge opens this door for me” is often objective enough while still irreducibly centered around role, sequence, office, or access-position.

### Models, interfaces, and records

Maps, dashboards, user sessions, checkpoints, and database states often encode centered positions. The archive should ask whether the center is merely representational or partly constitutive of a socially or technically organized reality.

### Actuality and possibility

The archive should ask whether a case is about which world is actual, or about where a subject is located within the actual world. These are linked but not identical questions.

## What this file does **not** say

It does **not** say:

- that all truth is relative to a perspective,
- that centered facts form a separate private realm,
- that uncentered description is impossible,
- that every indexical mark forces new ontology,
- or that action-guidance always outranks impersonal description.

The point is only that **indexicality, self-location, and centered reality need a typed metaphysical profile rather than being dissolved into either semantics or subjectivism.** For the further question of how centered terms actually fix their targets across contexts and reuse, use `68-reference-designation-and-target-fixation.md`.
