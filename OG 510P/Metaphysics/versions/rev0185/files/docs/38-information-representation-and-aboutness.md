# Information, Representation, and Aboutness

## Why this file exists

The archive already had files on truthmakers, propositions, abstracta, fictional discourse, nonexistents, social institutions, and distributed records. But it still lacked a direct rule for **information**, **representation**, and **aboutness**.

Without a dedicated file here, the archive risks four opposite mistakes:

- treating every causal signal or covariance pattern as already semantic,
- treating every meaningful representation as if it required a detached realm of abstract contents,
- collapsing maps, models, records, utterances, propositions, and mental states into one flat kind of representational item,
- or reducing representation to syntax, noise, or brute correlation.

This file gives the archive a compact **representation profile** so future revisions can say what kind of content-bearing case is at issue, what vehicle carries it, what makes it about its target, how truth or accuracy enters, and what ontological verdict is really being proposed.

## Working default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Some representational and informational structures are real and explanatorily serious, but they are not all one ontological kind, and they do not all earn the same metaphysical weight.**

This fits the archive's wider commitments:

- **form** still matters, because representational vehicles are often organized in content-bearing ways,
- **process** still matters, because information uptake, storage, transmission, revision, and interpretation are temporally maintained activities,
- **power** still matters, because some representational systems can guide action, inference, coordination, or control,
- **structure** still matters, because maps, models, propositions, records, and symbol-systems often work through organized relations rather than bare marks,
- and **grounding** still matters, because contentful states do not float free of vehicles, practices, organisms, institutions, or inferential embedding.

## Core distinctions

### 1. Information is not yet semantic content

The archive should distinguish at least:

- **quantitative or signal-information** — reduction of uncertainty, coding, transmission, discriminability,
- **covariance / indication** — one state reliably varying with another,
- **semantic information** — information with meaning or truth-aptness,
- **representation / aboutness** — a state or artifact being of, about, or standing for something,
- **proposition-like content** — what can be believed, denied, or be true/false,
- **recorded inscription** — a token, trace, file, ledger, register, image, or database entry that stores or stabilizes content.

The archive should not let Shannon-style information, bare causal correlation, and full semantic content collapse into one issue.

### 2. Vehicle and content are not one flat thing

A map, sentence, drawing, registry entry, mental state, or model may function as a **vehicle** of content. What it says, represents, stores, or encodes is the **content-profile**. These should not be collapsed.

The archive should therefore distinguish:

- the **vehicle**,
- the **target**,
- the **content-type**,
- the **accuracy or truth condition**,
- and the **dependence basis** that makes the representational relation possible.

### 3. Aboutness can be mental, public, or institutional

The archive should not assume that all aboutness is the same phenomenon. At minimum it should distinguish:

- **mental intentionality** — belief, desire, perception, imagination, memory,
- **linguistic or symbolic content** — sentence, utterance, notation, diagram,
- **model-based or pictorial representation** — scientific models, maps, simulations, icons,
- **institutional or documentary representation** — passports, ledgers, titles, credentials, registries, contracts,
- **mixed cases** — where mental, linguistic, and institutional layers cooperate.

### 4. Representation is not truthmaker identity

A representation of X is not therefore the truthmaker for claims about X. A cadastral map of a boundary, a theorem statement, and a bank ledger entry can all be representationally important without being identical to the worldly state they concern.

Sometimes the representation helps **constitute** a social or institutional fact. Sometimes it merely **tracks**, **stores**, **signals**, or **models** it. The archive must keep those cases distinct.

### 5. Propositions remain one live content-profile, not the whole story

The archive already keeps proposition-talk available where sharable truth-bearing content is needed. This file adds a constraint: propositions should not silently absorb every representational role.

Some cases are better handled as:

- proposition-like contents,
- structured records,
- signal or measurement states,
- model artifacts,
- intentional episodes,
- or institutional encodings.

## Archive default

The current archive default is:

1. **Be realist but typed about representation.** Some aboutness and information-bearing organization are objective enough to matter, but not all contentful items are one kind of entity.
2. **Do not collapse signal, meaning, and truth.** Bare covariance, Shannon-information, semantic content, and proposition-like truth-bearing content are different profiles.
3. **Prefer layered ontologies.** Start with concrete vehicles, organized practices, powers, inferential roles, and social or biological embedding before positing a detached realm of semantically self-sufficient items.
4. **Keep content vehicle-sensitive.** The same target may be represented by maps, propositions, images, models, registry entries, or mental states without thereby becoming one flat ontological object.
5. **Allow institutional representation to be constitutive in special cases.** Records, ledgers, certificates, files, and databases can be partly constitutive of offices, ownership, access, status, or other social facts, rather than merely evidential.
6. **Treat accuracy, satisfaction, and truth as typed.** Pictures, maps, beliefs, models, promises, and statutes do not all succeed or fail in exactly the same way.

The fuller typed treatment of truth now lives in `57-truth-correspondence-and-reality-answerability.md`.

## Representation-profile rule for future revisions

Before inferring a metaphysical verdict from a case involving language like **information**, **representation**, **content**, **aboutness**, **map**, **model**, **record**, **symbol**, **meaning**, **intentionality**, **data**, **signal**, **encoding**, **registry**, or **tracking**, specify:

1. **target type** — object, process, event, relation, field, status, institution, possibility, fiction, or mixed target;
2. **vehicle type** — mental state, utterance, sentence, inscription, image, map, model, signal, data structure, record system, token, interface, or mixed vehicle;
3. **content type** — covariance only, semantic information, proposition-like content, directive content, model-content, pictorial content, legal/documentary content, or mixed profile;
4. **dependence basis** — biological function, interpretive practice, inferential role, institutional rule, causal tracking, design, training history, or mixed basis;
5. **success condition** — truth, accuracy, fit, satisfaction, validity, authorization, registration success, or some other typed norm;
6. **world relation** — tracks, indicates, models, stores, encodes, partly constitutes, or merely simulates;
7. **reality verdict** — basic, derivative but robust, concrete representational artifact, abstract content, practice-dependent objective, merely instrumental, or still unsettled.

Otherwise the archive will keep sliding from correlation to meaning, from content to objecthood, and from record systems to free-floating abstracta.

## Stress cases

### Maps and models

A map, simulation, or scientific model is usually not identical to its target. The archive should ask whether it is tracking scale-relations, projecting idealizations, storing actionable structure, or only giving a convenient surrogate.

### Ledgers, titles, and registries

A land title, passport database, account balance, or credential record may be partly constitutive of a social fact rather than merely describing it. The archive should ask when a record system helps make the fact and when it only reports it.

### Perception, memory, and thought

Mental content should not be forced into the same profile as public documents or diagrams. The archive should distinguish lived intentionality, cognitive representation, inference-guidance, and stored symbolic content.

### Fiction and possible-case discourse

Talk about fictional characters or merely possible designs often involves richly structured content without requiring a concrete occupant corresponding to the target. The archive should ask what is doing the representational work before escalating ontology.

### Platforms and interfaces

Menus, dashboards, permissions, identity badges, and platform states often mix representation with control. They may display a status, help constitute it, and causally route action all at once.

## What this file does **not** say

It does **not** say:

- that every informational relation is semantic,
- that every semantic item is a proposition,
- that every representation requires a detached abstract object,
- that all content is merely conventional,
- or that every record system is only evidential rather than sometimes constitutive.

The point is only that **representation needs a typed metaphysical profile rather than a flat choice between eliminativism and semantic inflation**. For the further question of how a representation actually fixes or borrows a target, use `68-reference-designation-and-target-fixation.md`. For the further question of when successful representation earns ontological promotion, use `60-indispensability-representation-and-ontological-promotion.md`.
