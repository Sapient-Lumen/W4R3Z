# Application Dossier, Decision Record, and Verdict Report Protocol

## Why this file exists

The archive now has a strong internal governance sequence:

1. `144-operator-family-map-and-triage-grid.md` routes candidate cases.
2. `145-adversarial-stress-tests-and-revision-protocol.md` attacks routed verdicts.
3. `146-diagnostic-case-ledger-calibration-set-and-benchmark-protocol.md` records reusable hard cases.
4. `147-revision-dependency-graph-update-propagation-and-drift-control.md` propagates accepted changes.
5. `148-terminology-register-synonym-control-and-crosswalk-protocol.md` controls the vocabulary.
6. `149-claim-status-register-maturity-levels-and-commitment-governance.md` assigns commitment status and maturity level.

That sequence is necessary but still leaves a practical gap. A future user can apply all six controls and still end with scattered notes: a family route in one place, a stress-test result elsewhere, a ledger hint in a third file, a terminology warning in a fourth, a commitment label in a fifth, and no single accountable statement of the case. The archive can then be internally careful while remaining externally hard to use.

This file adds the missing application layer: an **application dossier**, **decision record**, and **verdict report protocol**. Its job is not to add another metaphysical operator. Its job is to turn the archive's internal controls into a repeatable, inspectable result.

The danger addressed here is **verdict evaporation**: the tendency for a careful analysis to dissolve into unportable fragments so that later readers remember only the conclusion, not the target, basis, grain, rivals, defeaters, source limits, terminology limits, status limits, and revision consequences that made the conclusion responsible.

## Compressed default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Do not merely reach a metaphysical verdict; produce an auditable case dossier when the verdict will guide use, teaching, revision, calibration, source interpretation, or future expansion.**

In compressed form:

> **Application is part of metaphysical method.**

And more carefully:

> **A verdict is archive-ready only when a reader can see what question was asked, what target was fixed, which family route was chosen, which rivals were rejected, what basis and grain controlled the result, which hostile variants were run, which terminology and source limits apply, what status and maturity level the result has, and what future action or non-action follows.**

This file therefore shifts the archive from **status-assigned classification** to **application-auditable classification**.

## Place in the control sequence

The intended hard-case sequence is now:

1. **Route** the case or proposed addition with `144`.
2. **Stress-test** the routed verdict with `145`.
3. **Ledger** reusable or precedent-setting cases with `146`.
4. **Propagate** accepted changes with `147`.
5. **Register terminology** with `148`.
6. **Assign commitment status** with `149`.
7. **Write the application dossier** with this file when the result must be reused, communicated, compared, or revised later.
8. **Govern later reuse, transfer, appeal, and supersession** with `151-precedent-reuse-appeal-transfer-and-review-governance.md` when the dossier becomes precedent, teaching material, calibration, source-bound interpretation, live-rival pressure, or review object.
9. **Govern transmission and compression** with `152-transmission-excerpt-compression-and-pedagogical-governance.md` when the dossier is summarized, excerpted, diagrammed, taught, converted into a prompt answer, or exported into a public or derivative artifact.

This file is deliberately different from `144` through `149`. Those files govern the **validity and status** of a verdict. This file governs the **presentation, portability, and auditability** of a verdict.

The archive should therefore reject two opposite failures:

- **private rigor** — analysis is careful but not reproducible by later readers;
- **public confidence** — the final prose sounds decisive but hides the route, rivals, defeaters, and status limits.

Application-dossier discipline is the bridge between those failures.

## The basic unit: the application dossier

An **application dossier** is the smallest portable record that lets a later reader reconstruct why the archive reached a verdict.

A dossier is not always required. Minor editorial fixes, typo repairs, local phrasing improvements, or purely mechanical package repairs can close without one. But a dossier is required whenever a case will be used as:

- a **teaching example** of an operator or family,
- a **calibration case** or negative control,
- a **precedent** for future addition, demotion, consolidation, or compression,
- a **source-dependent interpretation** of an empirical, legal, engineering, clinical, textual, historical, or institutional case,
- a **synthesis-level illustration** of Layered Process Realism,
- a **front-door example** that readers may treat as representative,
- a **deprecation or quarantine record** that future revisions might otherwise forget,
- or a **decision record** for why the archive did not add a proposed file.

The core rule is:

> **The more a verdict will travel, the more dossier it needs.**

## Three levels of dossier depth

Not every use requires a full report. Use three levels.

### Level 1: compact decision record

Use this for ordinary local cases where the verdict is helpful but not yet precedential.

Minimum fields:

| Field | Required answer |
|---|---|
| Question | What is being asked? |
| Target | What exactly is under discussion? |
| Route | Which family or file is primary? |
| Rival | Which nearest alternative was rejected? |
| Basis | What makes the verdict non-accidental? |
| Grain | At what level does the verdict hold? |
| Status | What commitment status and maturity level apply? |
| Verdict | What is the bounded conclusion? |

A compact record should fit in one paragraph or one small table.

### Level 2: standard application dossier

Use this for cases that will guide later discussion, appear in synthesis, be used in teaching, or influence a method rule.

Required fields:

1. **Case ID** — stable, short, and descriptive.
2. **Prompt or question** — the question as asked, with ambiguities preserved if they matter.
3. **Rewritten diagnostic question** — the question after target, family, and grain are clarified.
4. **Target** — the bearer, process, relation, status, representation, source item, institution, organism, artifact, or mixed target.
5. **Target kind** — object, process, relation, role, fact, status, structure, power, representation, event, system, institution, or mixed.
6. **Ordinary vocabulary** — the words that tempted the diagnosis.
7. **Primary route** — the file or family selected by `144`.
8. **Nearest rivals** — at least the nearest two competing files or families.
9. **Basis** — the real feature doing the work.
10. **Grain** — token, type, role, system, lineage, model, source, institution, regime, or mixed.
11. **Evidence or source basis** — what is known, observed, cited, stipulated, modeled, or assumed.
12. **Adversarial variants** — the hostile tests from `145` that matter here.
13. **Controls** — positive control, negative control, hard positive, or hard negative when relevant.
14. **Artifact risks** — label, proxy, readout, metaphor, administrative category, model boundary, source-domain capture, or ordinary-language shortcut.
15. **Defeaters** — what would force reclassification, narrowing, demotion, or open-debt status.
16. **Continuity or status consequence** — what remains the same, changes, splits, merges, persists, depends, becomes derivative, or remains unsettled.
17. **Terminology record** — canonical term, alias, umbrella, rival, source term, deprecated term, quarantined metaphor, or unsafe shortcut under `148`.
18. **Commitment status** — status and maturity level under `149`.
19. **Verdict sentence** — the bounded conclusion.
20. **Non-verdict sentence** — the stronger conclusion explicitly not licensed.
21. **Action result** — no update, file amendment, method-rule amendment, frontier-test amendment, ledger entry, source note, status change, deprecation, quarantine, consolidation, or proposed new file.
22. **Review trigger** — what future finding, source change, example, or failure mode should reopen the dossier.

### Level 3: full archive-grade dossier

Use this when the case is precedent-setting, source-dependent, publicly important, synthesis-altering, or likely to be reused across domains.

Add to Level 2:

- a **case history** recording how the verdict changed across analysis,
- a **rival map** showing why each neighboring file does or does not apply,
- a **source ledger** if external authority matters,
- a **calibration pack** with positive controls, negative controls, hard positives, hard negatives, and regression warnings,
- a **propagation table** showing what changed and what was intentionally left unchanged,
- a **terminology crosswalk** for ordinary, source, and archive terms,
- a **status table** separating kernel commitments, working defaults, mature diagnostics, live rivals, provisional probes, source-dependent commitments, deprecations, and open debts,
- a **reader-facing summary** no more confident than the status permits,
- and a **future-use instruction** stating whether later revisions may cite the case as example, precedent, calibration, source-bound illustration, or only open pressure.

A full dossier is justified only when the cost of not preserving the reasoning is greater than the cost of writing the record.

## Intake protocol

Before routing the case, perform an intake check. Many bad verdicts begin before the archive reaches `144`.

Ask:

1. **Is there one question or several?** A prompt may combine existence, identity, dependence, evidence, status, and value.
2. **Is the target fixed?** Do not classify the word when the target is missing.
3. **Is the user's vocabulary leading the verdict?** Words such as same, part, real, support, control, open, closed, in, through, function, evidence, model, source, person, life, and cause should be treated as prompts, not verdicts.
4. **Is the case source-dependent?** A legal, technical, clinical, engineering, scientific, historical, textual, or institutional case may need source anchors before classification.
5. **Is the case time-sensitive?** Current law, current science, current standards, current institutional rules, current product states, or current social practices should not be answered from stale memory.
6. **Is the desired output analytic, editorial, comparative, or revisionary?** A user may need a diagnosis, a file amendment, a new archive version, a teaching note, or a source-grounded report.
7. **Is the grain preselected or open?** If the prompt asks about a person, organism, institution, software service, legal office, material object, or model, the relevant grain may be token, role, lineage, system, regime, or record.
8. **Would a non-verdict be more honest?** Some cases should close as underdetermined, source-bound, family-mixed, or open debt.

The intake result should rewrite the prompt into a diagnostic question:

> **Given target T, under vocabulary V, at grain G, with evidence/source basis E, is the best diagnosis file/family F, rival R, mixed treatment M, or open debt D?**

## Verdict grammar

Use a bounded verdict sentence. A good verdict has this form:

> **At grain G, target T is best treated as F rather than R because basis B does the explanatory work; this licenses consequence C but not stronger inference S; the status is K at maturity M, with review trigger X.**

Examples of the grammar:

- **At token grain, this case is best treated as attachment rather than fusion because the load-bearing connector can change while the relata remain distinct; this licenses secured connection but not identity or parthood.**
- **At role grain, this case is best treated as substitution rather than strict identity because functional equivalence preserves role performance while the bearer changes; this licenses role continuity but not numerical sameness.**
- **At institutional grain, this case is a domain-local status rule rather than archive-wide ontology because the source practice creates the office under specified recognition and enforcement conditions; this licenses source-bound institutional reality but not a general theory of personhood.**
- **At access grain, this is a proxy-artifact risk rather than robust detection because the readout can vary independently of the target; this licenses an evidential warning but not a negative ontology of the target.**

The verdict sentence should be followed by a non-verdict sentence:

> **This does not show S.**

The non-verdict sentence is not optional in hard cases. It is how the dossier prevents upgrade drift.

## Decision types

Use these decision types in the **Action result** field.

### 1. Existing-file application

The case routes cleanly to an existing file. No new archive content is needed except perhaps an illustration.

Closure condition: route, basis, grain, rival, and status are clear.

### 2. Paired-file application

Two files are jointly needed because the case has a real mixed structure: for example support plus guidance, hosting plus source-bound institutional status, substitution plus turnover, valving plus bottlenecking, or evidence plus artifact risk.

Closure condition: the dossier says which file controls which part of the verdict and blocks fusion into one vague operator.

### 3. Family-map amendment

The case does not need a new operator but reveals that `144` should route a family differently.

Closure condition: the family map, method rule, and frontier question change together.

### 4. Method-rule amendment

The case reveals a new compression, admission, demotion, or dossier rule.

Closure condition: `03` teaches the new rule and `04` gets a corresponding test if needed.

### 5. Calibration ledger entry

The case should become a positive control, negative control, hard positive, hard negative, boundary case, regression case, demotion case, quarantine case, or source-bound precedent.

Closure condition: `146` or a local ledger note preserves the contrast, controls, defeaters, and future-use permission.

### 6. Terminology registration or quarantine

The case turns on a word, alias, source term, metaphor, or unsafe shortcut.

Closure condition: `148` or the local dossier records canonical destination, forbidden inferences, aliases, rivals, and translation rule.

### 7. Status change

The case promotes, narrows, demotes, source-binds, deprecates, compresses, absorbs, or records open debt for a claim.

Closure condition: `149` status and maturity are explicit.

### 8. Source-dependent interpretation

The verdict depends on external authority.

Closure condition: `05` includes the source anchor or the dossier states that no source-dependent claim is being made.

### 9. New first-order operator candidate

The case appears to defeat existing files even after routing, hostile variation, calibration, propagation analysis, terminology audit, and status assignment.

Closure condition: the dossier records why the nearest three files fail, what new basis and failure mode are present, what continuity consequence is distinctive, and what compression rule will prevent bloat.

### 10. No-action / no-verdict

The responsible result is to leave the archive unchanged, either because the case is underdescribed, source-dependent but unsourced, mixed beyond current tools, or not metaphysical in the relevant sense.

Closure condition: the dossier states what information would reopen the case.

## Source-dependent dossier rule

When a case depends on external authority, distinguish five source roles:

| Source role | Meaning | Dossier consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Constitutive | The practice, law, standard, text, or rule helps make the status what it is | Source anchor required before archive-wide use |
| Evidential | The source is evidence for a target independent of the source | Record reliability and possible artifact risk |
| Classificatory | The source provides a domain-local taxonomy | Mark status as domain-local unless translated |
| Historical | The source records what happened or how a view developed | Do not export the historical usage as doctrine without argument |
| Illustrative | The source helps readers see the distinction | Keep example status separate from commitment status |

A source-dependent dossier should not state a stronger metaphysical verdict than the source role allows.

## Family-specific dossier prompts

Use these prompts to adapt the dossier to major archive families.

### Dependence and grounding

- Which dependence relation is at stake: composition, constitution, realization, grounding, causation, support, hosting, or scaffolding?
- What would change if the base changed?
- Does the case license ontological priority, explanatory priority, causal production, or only enabling background?
- What stronger dependence claim is not licensed?

### Unity, identity, and continuity

- What is the relevant sortal or stage-kind?
- Is continuity material, formal, organizational, role-based, record-based, institutional, or narrative?
- Does the case involve substitution, migration, succession, duplication, fusion, overlap, repair, turnover, or mere redescription?
- Which continuity consequence is licensed and which identity claim is blocked?

### Truth, modality, and abstracta

- What makes the claim true, if anything does?
- Is the target a fact, proposition, structure, kind, possibility, absence, privation, or representation?
- Does the case need a truthmaker, a bounded totality, a status profile, or a deflationary treatment?
- What ontological promotion is blocked?

### Access, evidence, and representation

- What is the target and what is the vehicle?
- Could the evidence be a proxy, readout, artifact, model boundary, administrative category, or measurement convention?
- What would count as convergence rather than single-channel indication?
- Does the verdict concern reality, access architecture, source reliability, or representation only?

### Social, normative, agential, personal, living, and conscious domains

- Which recognitional, normative, biological, agential, first-personal, or experiential basis does the verdict require?
- Is the claim domain-local, source-dependent, or archive-wide?
- Does the case involve status function, role, authority, norm, agency, personhood, life, organism, consciousness, or representation?
- What forbidden upgrade must be blocked: full agency, full personhood, consciousness, biological individuality, or social reality?

### Process, time, and transition

- Is the case about onset, cessation, recurrence, delay, inertia, attrition, irreversibility, fragility, critical transition, or history-sensitivity?
- What temporal grain matters?
- What failure or return profile would change the verdict?
- Does the case license real becoming, mere sequence, phase change, or record-based continuity?

### Stock, flow, passage, and control

- Is the case about accumulation, depletion, replenishment, turnover, circulation, bottlenecking, rerouting, leakage, release, retention, guidance, valving, or gating?
- What actually passes, stays, escapes, is retained, is admitted, or is throttled?
- Is control a rate, route, aperture, threshold, window, feedback, permission, or source-domain label?
- Does the case license regulated passage, capacity limit, mere path constraint, or artifact-risk warning?

### Interface, support, retention, and assembly

- Is the relation hosting, attachment, embedding, enclosure, coating, wrapping, lamination, sealing, joining, articulation, clamping, latching, threading, meshing, gripping, support, guiding, or valving?
- What surface, connector, load path, path constraint, retention mechanism, or closure relation does the work?
- What failure mode distinguishes it from the nearest two neighbors?
- Does the case license two distinct relata in relation, one stronger assembly, or only apparent interface language?

## Future-use permissions

Every dossier should say how later revisions may use it.

| Permission | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Illustration | May be used pedagogically but not as precedent |
| Precedent | May guide similar cases, with scope limits |
| Calibration | May serve as positive/negative/hard control |
| Regression warning | Future revisions must not accidentally reverse it |
| Source-bound example | May be reused only with its source context |
| Live rival | Preserved as pressure against the default |
| Open pressure | Useful but not settled enough for commitment |
| Do not reuse | Case is underdescribed, superseded, or unsafe |

Future-use permission prevents examples from being promoted by repetition alone.

## Dossier anti-patterns

### 1. Verdict laundering

The report gives a confident answer while hiding the route, rivals, basis, grain, defeaters, or status.

Correction: add the missing decision-record fields before using the verdict.

### 2. Form theater

The report fills out a dossier but does not actually test the case.

Correction: remove fields that are empty ornament, or run the required route, stress, terminology, and status checks.

### 3. Source smuggling

A legal, technical, clinical, engineering, scientific, historical, textual, or institutional term is treated as archive-wide doctrine.

Correction: mark source role and status, cite the source in `05` if needed, and translate before exporting.

### 4. Upgrade drift

A verdict about role continuity becomes a verdict about identity; a verdict about evidence becomes a verdict about reality; a verdict about source-local classification becomes archive-wide metaphysics.

Correction: write a non-verdict sentence and forbidden-upgrade field.

### 5. Mixed-case flattening

The case really needs paired-file handling, but the report forces it into one local operator.

Correction: split the dossier into components and assign primary/secondary route roles.

### 6. Status haze

The report says what the diagnosis is but not whether it is kernel, default, diagnostic, local, rival, provisional, source-dependent, deprecated, or open debt.

Correction: use `149` before closing.

### 7. Reuse inflation

A vivid case becomes precedent merely because it is memorable.

Correction: assign future-use permission and require calibration before precedent.

### 8. Open-debt burial

The report notes uncertainties but does not record what would resolve them.

Correction: list open debts and review triggers.

## Minimal dossier checklist

Before closing any reusable verdict, confirm:

- [ ] The target is fixed.
- [ ] The diagnostic question is rewritten.
- [ ] The primary route and nearest rivals are named.
- [ ] Basis and grain are explicit.
- [ ] At least one adversarial variant was considered.
- [ ] Artifact risks and defeaters are named.
- [ ] The verdict sentence is bounded.
- [ ] The non-verdict sentence blocks upgrade drift.
- [ ] Terminology status is clear.
- [ ] Commitment status and maturity level are assigned.
- [ ] Source role is stated if external authority matters.
- [ ] Future-use permission is assigned.
- [ ] Open debts and review triggers are recorded.

## Relation to earlier control files

### Relation to `144`

`144` routes the case. This file records the route in a portable decision record. Routing without dossiering is often enough for private analysis, but not for reusable application.

### Relation to `145`

`145` attacks the verdict. This file records which hostile variants were actually run, which were unnecessary, and which remain open debt.

### Relation to `146`

`146` turns hard cases into calibration instruments. This file decides whether the current case has future-use permission as illustration, precedent, calibration, regression warning, source-bound example, live rival, open pressure, or do-not-reuse.

### Relation to `147`

`147` propagates accepted changes through the archive. This file records the propagation result inside the dossier so that later readers know whether a local verdict changed the archive or merely applied it.

### Relation to `148`

`148` governs terms. This file records the term statuses that matter for the case and prevents ordinary vocabulary from disappearing after the verdict sentence is written.

### Relation to `149`

`149` assigns commitment status and maturity. This file puts that status into the public-facing verdict and prevents application prose from sounding stronger than the archive's actual commitment.

## Future expansion rule

Before adding another control or application file after this one, ask whether the pressure is really new. A new file is warranted only if the archive lacks a reusable answer to one of these questions:

- Where does the case route?
- Does the verdict survive attack?
- Should the case become calibration?
- Where must the change propagate?
- What terms are safe?
- What status does the claim have?
- How should the result be reported, reused, and reopened?
- How may the reported result be transferred, taught, appealed, superseded, or marked do-not-reuse?
- How is the transmitted result received, and what correction loop follows?

If the pressure is a variant of one of those questions, amend `144` through `161` rather than adding another governance or application file.

## Closing formulation

The archive's current applied sequence can now be compressed as:

> **Route the case. Attack the route. Ledger the hard result. Propagate the accepted change. Register the words. Assign the commitment level. Write the auditable verdict. Govern its reuse and appeal. Control its transmission. Audit its reception. Gate the release.**

The point is not bureaucracy. The point is memory, portability, and restraint. A metaphysical archive does not become more rigorous merely by multiplying distinctions. It becomes more rigorous when a later reader can reconstruct why a distinction applied, why its rivals failed, what it did not show, how strongly it is held, and when it should be reopened.
