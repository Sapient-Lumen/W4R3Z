# Operational Reliance, Deployment Boundaries, and Action-Use Governance

## Why semantic fidelity is not enough

`164-semantic-fidelity-generated-output-audit-and-warning-retention-governance.md` asks whether a generated, compressed, diagrammed, taught, excerpted, or derivative output preserves the meaning and warnings of its source. That layer is necessary, but it is not sufficient. An output can be semantically faithful and still be unsafe when it becomes a basis for action.

The danger is **operational-reliance laundering**. A faithful summary may be used as if it were a policy. A teaching checklist may become a real classifier. A public note may become an institutional rule. A diagnostic table may be embedded in a workflow. A source-neutral metaphysical distinction may be used as if it settled a current legal, clinical, engineering, financial, safety, or administrative question. A local validation record may be treated as operational readiness. A dashboard-like register may invite people to act as if active monitoring exists. A clean dossier may be converted into an instruction for people, machines, organizations, or public users without the domain review, notice path, rollback plan, human authority, or affected-party safeguards that action requires.

This file adds a layer after semantic-fidelity governance: **operational reliance, deployment boundaries, and action-use governance**. Its question is not merely "did the output preserve the source?" but **what may anyone do with the preserved output, and what additional authority is required before the output changes practice, classification, policy, access, rights, resources, safety, or institutional behavior?**

The rule is simple:

> **Faithful transfer is not operational authorization. Before archive material guides action, it must state deployment status, action-reliance class, domain authority, affected parties, human-review gate, source-current boundary, notice path, rollback path, monitoring duty, and forbidden use.**

## Compressed default

The archive should now say:

> **Do not treat faithful archive output as deployable guidance merely because it is accurate to the archive. Ask what action it would authorize, who could be affected, what domain authority is missing, what source currency is required, how errors would be caught, and how the action could be reversed, appealed, or stopped.**

In shorter form:

> **A faithful summary is not a safe procedure.**

And more carefully:

> **Every archive-derived object that will be used to classify, decide, recommend, route, warn, allocate, deny, approve, diagnose, rank, prioritize, teach as practice, automate, monitor, or govern should state its source version, semantic-fidelity status, public-use status, steward role, operational context, deployment status, action-reliance class, domain review status, affected parties, required warnings, human override, appeal path, rollback plan, monitoring evidence, allowed use, forbidden use, and next deployment review trigger.**

The difference between `164` and this file is crucial. `164` asks: **did the representation preserve the archive's meaning?** This file asks: **even if it did, what action is permitted, and under what additional domain, public, procedural, and accountability constraints?**

## What counts as operational use

This file applies when archive material is used, or could reasonably be used, to change practice. Operational use includes:

1. deciding, recommending, ranking, approving, denying, routing, prioritizing, or escalating cases;
2. classifying people, institutions, artifacts, sources, outputs, risks, duties, rights, obligations, or statuses;
3. creating rubrics, scoring systems, checklists, intake forms, dashboards, workflow rules, or automated prompts used in live practice;
4. instructing maintainers, teachers, reviewers, derivative authors, fork custodians, or public users what to do;
5. supporting legal, clinical, engineering, financial, safety, organizational, academic, administrative, employment, or public-facing decisions;
6. embedding archive distinctions into software, agents, schedules, validators, monitors, ticketing systems, review workflows, or derivative platforms;
7. using teaching material as practice guidance rather than as conceptual illustration;
8. using a metaphysical diagnosis to justify intervention, non-intervention, allocation, withdrawal, deprecation, notice, refusal, or enforcement.

The common feature is not publicity or automation. The common feature is **action consequence**: the archive-derived item becomes a reason for doing, not doing, permitting, forbidding, allocating, warning, escalating, or automating something.

## Deployment status classes

### OP0 — no operational claim

The artifact is not being used to guide action. It may be private reasoning, source material, or archive-local prose. No deployment language is allowed.

### OP1 — orientation only

The artifact may orient a reader conceptually. It must not be used as a classifier, policy, workflow, decision aid, or action trigger.

### OP2 — internal conceptual aid

The artifact may help archive maintainers think through cases, but it does not authorize decisions outside the archive or any automated or public action.

### OP3 — training, simulation, or teaching practice only

The artifact may be used in exercises, classroom examples, mock reviews, or simulations, provided the teaching context states that it is not live operational guidance.

### OP4 — low-stakes local workflow aid

The artifact may guide reversible local archive maintenance actions, such as routing a future revision, checking whether a register field is missing, or choosing which control file to consult. The action must be low-impact, reversible, and human-reviewed.

### OP5 — controlled decision-support use

The artifact may support a decision, but only with a named human reviewer, stated scope, source-current check where relevant, record of reasons, appeal or correction route, and prohibition on automated or sole-basis decisions.

### OP6 — source/domain-bound operational use

The artifact may guide operational use only inside a specified domain and source scope after current source review, domain authority, public-use permission, stewardship assignment, continuity registration, and monitoring duties are explicit.

### OP7 — externally governed deployment

The artifact is deployed under an external authority structure: institutional policy, professional review, legal compliance, safety protocol, audited workflow, public issue path, or domain governance. Archive governance remains only one input.

### OP8 — monitored public or institutional deployment

The artifact is part of a maintained public or institutional system with live monitoring, notice, appeal, rollback, incident handling, version migration, source refresh, and accountable stewardship. This status requires evidence beyond the local package.

### OP9 — unsafe, unauthorized, or deployment-laundered use

Operational language is being used without the authority, domain review, source currency, affected-party safeguards, monitoring, rollback, or public reliance status required. Use must be blocked, quarantined, withdrawn, or rerouted.

## Action-reliance classes

### AR0 — no action reliance

No person, process, system, or institution acts on the artifact.

### AR1 — interpretive orientation

The artifact helps someone understand a question but does not decide, classify, recommend, or trigger action.

### AR2 — archive-local routing

The artifact helps choose which archive file, control layer, or diagnostic path to consult. It remains internal and revisable.

### AR3 — reversible low-impact maintenance action

The artifact may support local edits, checklists, labels, or register updates that can be reversed without affecting external parties.

### AR4 — human-reviewed decision support

The artifact can inform a decision but may not be the sole basis. A responsible human must review the basis, limits, and alternatives.

### AR5 — process-affecting institutional support

The artifact influences a workflow, policy, notice, priority, intake rule, teaching requirement, or review process. It requires public-use status, stewardship duty, notice path, and correction/appeal route.

### AR6 — domain-sensitive action

The artifact may affect legal, medical, clinical, engineering, financial, safety, employment, education, public administration, or other consequential domains. Current source review and domain authority are mandatory.

### AR7 — automated or embedded action

The artifact directly or indirectly triggers tool actions, scheduled actions, classifier outputs, routing, recommendations, reminders, or enforcement. Automation permission under `163`, semantic fidelity under `164`, monitoring, override, and incident handling are required.

### AR8 — forbidden or unbounded action

The artifact is being used beyond authorization: as sole-basis decision, hidden classifier, policy substitute, domain authority, current-source substitute, irreversible action trigger, or high-stakes advice without review. Stop and quarantine.

## Minimum deployment packet

A deployment-boundary record should minimally include:

- deployment ID,
- artifact and version,
- governing source files,
- intended operational context,
- source version and semantic-fidelity record,
- public-use status under `158`,
- steward role and obligation class under `159`,
- continuity register entry under `160` where ongoing duty exists,
- automation status and tool-permission class under `163` where applicable,
- deployment status OP0–OP9,
- action-reliance class AR0–AR8,
- domain and source-current review status,
- affected parties or affected systems,
- permitted actions,
- forbidden actions,
- human-review gate and override path,
- notice, dispute, appeal, correction, and rollback path,
- monitoring or watch requirement,
- incident trigger,
- open operational debt,
- next deployment review trigger.

## Operational preconditions

Before moving above OP4 or AR3, the archive should be able to answer:

1. **Who acts?** A reader, teacher, maintainer, reviewer, institution, tool, automated agent, public user, or external domain actor?
2. **What action follows?** Read, teach, classify, recommend, decide, notify, deny, approve, route, escalate, migrate, deprecate, withdraw, enforce, or automate?
3. **Who is affected?** Only the archive, a local maintainer, students in a simulation, derivative users, public readers, source subjects, institutional participants, or high-stakes parties?
4. **What authority is needed?** Archive method, maintainer judgment, public-use permission, source review, domain expertise, institutional authority, legal authority, safety review, or external certification?
5. **What can go wrong?** Misclassification, source staleness, status bleaching, warning loss, unfair treatment, unsafe action, hidden automation, irreversible change, or reliance beyond review?
6. **How is it reversed or appealed?** Correction note, rollback, withdrawal, retraction, notice, public erratum, source refresh, human override, or domain-specific appeal?
7. **What is monitored?** Version drift, source changes, incidents, disputes, output degradation, warning failures, derivative misuse, or policy mismatch?

If these answers are missing, the use should remain orientation, teaching simulation, or local low-stakes workflow aid.

## Neighboring-rival distinctions

### Operational reliance is not public reliance

`158` governs what a public user may rely on. This file governs what actions may follow from that reliance. A claim may be publicly citable but still not deployable as a procedure, classifier, rule, or recommendation.

### Operational reliance is not stewardship

`159` assigns duties. This file asks whether those duties and permissions are enough to act. A steward may owe warning preservation without having authority to deploy the material.

### Operational reliance is not continuity memory

`160` records duties over time. This file asks whether the recorded duty supports operational use, what action it wakes, and what notice, rollback, or escalation follows.

### Operational reliance is not validation

`161` can show that files and registers are present and hashes match. It cannot show that a workflow is safe to deploy, source-current, domain-authorized, or fair to affected parties.

### Operational reliance is not semantic fidelity

`164` can show that a summary preserved limits. It does not authorize acting on those limits outside the scope stated in the deployment packet.

## Common failure patterns

### Faithful-to-operational jump

A faithful summary is treated as a safe procedure. Fix by assigning OP and AR classes and stating what action is permitted.

### Policy laundering

Archive method language is inserted into policy, institutional workflows, or administrative rules without public-use status, domain authority, notice, appeal, or rollback.

### Teaching-to-practice collapse

A classroom exercise, diagram, or rubric becomes a live classifier or evaluative instrument without switching from OP3 to a stronger reviewed deployment status.

### Source-neutral to source-current upgrade

A source-neutral metaphysical distinction is treated as if it settled a current empirical, legal, clinical, engineering, or institutional standard.

### Sole-basis decision creep

A file, table, model output, or generated answer becomes the sole basis for a decision, despite having only orientation, teaching, or decision-support status.

### Dashboard performativity

A tidy register, schema, or validation transcript is presented as if live monitoring, issue intake, or operational accountability exists.

### Hidden affected parties

The artifact is said to guide only internal thinking, but the action affects students, users, contributors, source subjects, public readers, or institutional participants.

### Irreversibility blindness

A reversible archive edit and an irreversible public, institutional, or high-stakes action are treated as if they require the same governance.

### Automation action creep

A tool permitted to inspect or draft becomes a tool that routes, classifies, recommends, notifies, enforces, or triggers downstream action.

### Appeal-path deletion

A deployment affects others but provides no dispute path, override, rollback, notice, or correction channel.

## When operational use should be blocked

Block, quarantine, or downgrade operational use when:

- the source version or source files cannot be identified,
- semantic-fidelity status is absent or insufficient for the intended use,
- public-use status under `158` is absent or weaker than the proposed action,
- stewardship, continuity memory, or notice path is missing where ongoing duty exists,
- a high-stakes or domain-sensitive action lacks current source review and domain authority,
- the use would be automated without `163` permission and human override,
- affected parties are hidden or cannot contest the action,
- rollback, withdrawal, correction, or incident handling is absent,
- or the artifact is being used as policy, classifier, recommendation, or decision rule despite being licensed only for orientation or teaching.

## When a lighter note is enough

A full deployment-boundary record is not required for every reader who uses the archive for private thought. A short note is enough when the use is OP0–OP2 and AR0–AR2: local, reversible, non-public, non-automated, non-source-dependent, non-high-stakes, and not likely to guide anyone's rights, safety, resources, access, or institutional treatment.

Use a full record when the output is public, automated, institutional, source-dependent, high-stakes, teaching-to-practice, derivative-platform-facing, workflow-affecting, policy-like, or likely to be reused without archive context.

## Interaction with existing governance layers

Operational-reliance governance does not replace earlier layers.

- `158` states whether public reliance is permitted.
- `159` assigns stewardship obligations.
- `160` records ongoing duties.
- `161` validates package and register invariants.
- `162` records workflow execution.
- `163` binds tool permission and automation claims.
- `164` audits semantic fidelity and warning retention.
- `165` asks whether an archive-derived artifact may guide action, classification, decision support, deployment, policy, automation, or high-stakes operational use.

## Safety and domain boundaries

No deployment-boundary packet may convert archive material into legal, medical, engineering, clinical, financial, safety-critical, public-administration, employment, educational-assessment, or institutional advice by itself. Those domains require current source review, domain authority, external governance, affected-party safeguards, and often independent review beyond the archive.

A deployment packet can responsibly say **not authorized for operational use**. In many cases, that is the correct verdict.
