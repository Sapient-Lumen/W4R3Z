# Vagueness, Indeterminacy, and Borderline Ontology

## Why this file exists

The archive already has files on **boundaries**, **mereology**, **ordinary objects**, **truthmakers**, **ontological commitment**, **persistence**, and **levels**. What it lacked was a single rule for a recurrent pressure point running through all of them:

- when a case is merely **vague in language**,
- when it reflects **epistemic limitation** or coarse representation,
- when a boundary or count is **underdescribed but still real**,
- and when there may be a case of genuine **metaphysical indeterminacy**.

Without that discipline, the archive risks sliding among:

1. crispism that treats every borderline case as merely verbal,
2. inflation that treats every vague discourse as proof of vague objects,
3. deflation that dissolves mountains, clouds, organisms, institutions, and boundaries just because they are not perfectly sharp,
4. and overreaction that lets “indeterminacy” explain everything without saying what kind.

A compact non-skeptical, non-reductive metaphysics needs a better rule-set than that.

## Core claim

The archive should adopt **typed borderline realism with restrained metaphysical indeterminacy**.

Compressed:

> **Borderline cases are often real features of inquiry and world-involving classification, but the archive should localize whether the indeterminacy is semantic, epistemic, representational, or metaphysical before drawing ontological conclusions.**

This means:

- do **not** infer unreality from vague boundaries,
- do **not** infer worldly indeterminacy merely from loose language,
- keep **metaphysical indeterminacy** as a live option where boundary, counting, future, or determinable/determinate structure earns it,
- and treat **the problem of the many** as a standing stress test rather than a curiosity.

## Four distinctions the archive now needs

### 1. Semantic vagueness

Some indeterminacy is chiefly in predicates, terms, or classification practices.

Typical signs:

- borderline application cases,
- sorites pressure,
- admissible sharpenings or precisifications,
- and no immediate need to posit a corresponding vague object.

This matters because not every vague sentence points to a vague entity.

### 2. Epistemic or evidential limitation

Sometimes the world may be perfectly determinate while our access, measurement, evidence, or operational standard is not.

Typical signs:

- noisy measurement,
- incomplete observation,
- resolution limits,
- or underdetermination by present evidence.

This matters because the archive should not convert ignorance into ontology too quickly.

### 3. Representational or model-relative indeterminacy

A representation can be coarse, idealized, scale-relative, or fit-for-purpose without the target itself being metaphysically unsettled.

Typical signs:

- scale-sensitive modeling,
- deliberately coarse categories,
- boundary conventions for practical use,
- or multiple acceptable maps of one organized domain.

This matters because levels, scale, and idealization already matter in the archive.

### 4. Metaphysical indeterminacy

Some cases may be best understood as involving genuine worldly unsettledness: not merely unknown, not merely verbally underdescribed, but underdetermined in the object, boundary, future, count, or determinate itself.

Typical pressure points:

- clouds, mountains, deserts, and organisms with vague or porous boundaries,
- the **problem of the many**,
- borderline composition cases,
- open-future cases,
- and determinable-without-unique-determinate cases.

The archive should keep this as a **live but non-default** option.

## Archive default

The current default is:

1. **start with typed diagnosis**, not with a verdict about vague objects;
2. **treat boundary vagueness as compatible with reality**;
3. **allow derivative or domain-typed indeterminacy without making everything unsettled**;
4. **treat counting and existence claims near vague margins as especially pressure-sensitive**;
5. **prefer local rather than global metaphysical indeterminacy**.

Compressed again:

> **Reality need not be perfectly sharp to be real, but metaphysical unsettledness must be earned case by case.**

## What the archive should now say

### 1. Borderline does not mean unreal

Clouds, coastlines, organisms, storms, infrastructures, and institutions may lack perfectly crisp edges while remaining ontologically serious.

The archive should therefore refuse the lazy inference:

- vague boundary,
- therefore not one thing,
- therefore not real.

That inference is too fast.

### 2. But borderline does not automatically mean metaphysically vague object

A vague term, vague standard, or coarse classificatory practice does not by itself show that reality is metaphysically indeterminate.

The archive should therefore also refuse the opposite shortcut:

- vague discourse,
- therefore vague object,
- therefore classical ontology must be abandoned.

That inference is also too fast.

### 3. The problem of the many is a core stress test

Any metaphysics that allows organized wholes with vague boundaries must face the possibility of many near-duplicate candidates:

- desk,
- desk-minus-a-molecule,
- desk-minus-two-molecules,
- and so on.

The archive should treat this as a live test for views about:

- composition,
- counting,
- persistence,
- co-location,
- and ontological commitment.

### 4. Indeterminacy can be domain-typed

Different domains may host different sorts of borderline structure.

For example:

- **material objects** may raise boundary and counting pressure,
- **institutions** may raise role and recognition pressure,
- **future-directed cases** may raise openness pressure,
- **determinable properties** may raise unique-determinate pressure,
- and **model-based sciences** may raise representation-vs-world pressure.

So the archive should not search for one flat “theory of indeterminacy” before doing local diagnosis.

### 5. Crispification has costs

Some metaphysical programs try to save determinate ontology by insisting that every borderline case has a sharp though unknowable cutoff, or by denying that the suspect objects exist.

The archive should allow these as live options in particular disputes, but it should also ask:

- what explanatory loss follows,
- whether ordinary objecthood becomes too fragile,
- whether organized wholes are being sacrificed for a taste for sharpness,
- and whether the theory handles real practice better than a typed local indeterminacy view.

### 6. Metaphysical indeterminacy should be restrained but not banned

The archive should neither:

- ban metaphysical indeterminacy by fiat,
- nor treat it as the default explanation for every hard case.

The right stance is:

> **keep metaphysical indeterminacy as a serious option where semantic, epistemic, and representational diagnoses fail and where the target domain remains ontologically serious despite borderline structure.**

### 7. Indeterminacy interacts with several existing files

This file should now constrain the archive's use of:

- **mereology and composition** — because borderline parts can pressure counting,
- **boundaries and location** — because exact demarcation may fail without unreality following,
- **sortals and persistence** — because kind-sensitive identity may tolerate some margin,
- **truthmakers and commitment** — because the truth of existential claims can be pressure-sensitive near vague cases,
- **levels and scale** — because some “indeterminacy” is really only a representational or resolution shift.

## Indeterminacy-profile rule

When a future revision uses language like **vague**, **borderline**, **unclear whether**, **indeterminate**, **unsettled**, **precisification**, **open whether**, or **there may be many equally good candidates**, it should state:

1. **what exactly is indeterminate** — predicate application, boundary, object identity, count, location, eventhood, future outcome, property-value, or something else;
2. **what kind of indeterminacy is at issue** — semantic, epistemic, representational/model-relative, metaphysical, or mixed;
3. **what the target domain is** — object, process, institution, kind, relation, region, event, future branch, or abstractum;
4. **what larger metaphysical issue is being pressured** — composition, persistence, truthmaking, commitment, causation, location, levels, or modality;
5. **whether the case supports only local indeterminacy or a stronger global thesis**;
6. **what explanatory loss follows** if the case is forced to be perfectly sharp, or if the target is eliminated altogether.

If a proposal does not do this, its vagueness or indeterminacy talk is under-disciplined.

## What this file is not saying

- It is **not** saying all objects are vague.
- It is **not** saying all vagueness is merely linguistic.
- It is **not** saying metaphysical indeterminacy is mandatory.
- It is **not** saying the archive should abandon classical logic just because borderline cases appear.
- It is **not** saying that ordinary reality depends on sharp cutoffs no one can ever find.

It is only saying that the archive now needs a **typed indeterminacy discipline**.
