# Abstracta, Propositions, Numbers, and Structures

## Why this file exists

The archive had already become disciplined about dependence, truthmaking, modality, category, and ontological commitment, but it still left a major pressure point too implicit: **abstracta**.

Numbers, sets, propositions, formal structures, models, and idealized entities keep appearing in metaphysical argument. Some theories inflate them into a second independent realm. Others try to eliminate them wholesale. The archive needs a middle policy strict enough to resist ontology blow-up and realist enough not to mutilate mathematics, truth, modality, or representation.

## Archive default

The current default is **restrained abstracta realism with domain-typed dependence**.

That means:

- do **not** assume all abstracta are one kind of thing,
- do **not** assume all abstracta are fundamental if they are real,
- do **not** assume nominalist paraphrase is always cheaper than sparse realism,
- do **not** assume that useful formal discourse is automatically merely fictional,
- and do **not** infer a fully autonomous Platonic realm unless lighter options fail.

The archive should therefore distinguish at least four abstracta profiles:

1. **mathematical abstracta** — numbers, sets, functions, spaces, structures,
2. **content abstracta** — propositions, truth-conditions, inferential contents,
3. **structural abstracta** — graphs, formal patterns, type-level organizations, ideal relations,
4. **idealized or model entities** — frictionless planes, perfectly rational agents, limiting systems, coordinate constructions.

These should not be collapsed into one dispute.

## Central compression rule

Before arguing that an abstractum exists, ask five questions:

1. **What job is it doing?**
   - truth-bearer,
   - referent,
   - structural position,
   - inferential role,
   - explanatory invariant,
   - or merely calculational convenience.

2. **What kind of abstractum is it?**
   - mathematical,
   - propositional/content-like,
   - structural,
   - idealized-model,
   - normative,
   - or mixed.

3. **What dependence profile fits it best?**
   - basic and independent,
   - derivative but robust,
   - practice-dependent but objective,
   - immanent in concrete systems,
   - or merely representational.

4. **What explanatory loss follows if we remove it?**
   - loss of mathematical truth,
   - loss of cross-linguistic content identity,
   - loss of structural invariance,
   - loss of modal articulation,
   - or no serious loss because paraphrase works.

5. **What lighter substitute is live?**
   - trope or immanent realism,
   - nominalist paraphrase,
   - fictionalism,
   - structuralism,
   - act-based or sentence-based content,
   - or some domain-specific reduction.

If those questions are not answered, abstracta-talk is too cheap.

## Working archive stance by domain

### 1. Mathematical entities

The archive should remain open between several live options:

- **full-blooded platonism**,
- **object realism without full independence**,
- **structuralism**,
- **Aristotelian or immanent realism**,
- **nominalist paraphrase or fictionalism**.

But the archive default should be **lighter than heavyweight realm-platonism and heavier than eliminativism**.

The current lean is:

> mathematical discourse tracks real structural constraints and invariants, and some of its objects may deserve realist treatment, but the archive should prefer **structure-first or dependence-sensitive realism** before positing a maximally autonomous universe of abstract mathematical individuals.

This fits the archive's larger habit of preferring structured reality over bare item-lists, and derivative seriousness over one flat existence switch.

### 2. Propositions and content

The archive should treat propositions as a **live but not mandatory posit**.

Propositions do real explanatory work when we need:

- the primary bearers of truth and falsity,
- cross-linguistic sameness of content,
- attitude objects for belief, knowledge, and assertion,
- modal bearers across possible cases,
- and the distinction between sentence tokens and what they say.

But the archive should not assume that propositions must be **unstructured, eternal, and fully independent abstract objects**.

The present lean is therefore:

> when proposition-talk is needed, prefer **structured, typed, and possibly derivative contents** over featureless content atoms.

This is continuous with the archive's preferences for form, structure, and dependence-sensitive realism.

### 3. Structures and patterns

The archive should be friendliest to **structural abstracta**.

Why? Because the archive already treats structure as ontologically serious in concrete reality. So where mathematics, logic, or science isolates a stable structural pattern, the archive need not force a choice between:

- a concrete-only ontology that cannot explain invariant form,
- and a massive second universe of detached entities.

A good intermediate policy is often:

- realism about the structure,
- openness about whether its best ontology is objectual, position-based, immanent, or type-level,
- and caution about reifying every model artifact into an entity.

### 4. Idealized models

The archive should usually classify idealized entities as **representational unless earned otherwise**.

Frictionless planes, perfectly spherical cows, infinite populations, and center-of-mass constructions may be indispensable for inquiry without thereby forcing a new heavyweight ontology.

The key question is whether the idealization is:

- tracking a real limit,
- isolating a real structure,
- approximating a real tendency,
- or merely serving computation.

The archive should not infer existence from convenience alone.

## What this file rejects

### Rejection 1: one global verdict on all abstracta

Numbers, propositions, norms, fictional characters, sets, and model-entities should not all receive one yes/no treatment.

### Rejection 2: usefulness alone implies existence

An indispensable notation, theory, or model may still be only representational.

### Rejection 3: causal inertness implies unreality

Some things may be real without entering the causal order in the same way concrete entities do.

### Rejection 4: abstract realism must mean a second detached cosmos

A metaphysics can treat some abstracta as real while still making them structured, typed, derivative, or immanently anchored.

### Rejection 5: nominalism is free

Nominalist and paraphrase strategies carry burdens too. They must explain truth, similarity, content, structure, and mathematics without covertly reintroducing what they deny.

## Interaction with existing archive files

### With `13-truthmakers-absences-and-privation.md`

Abstracta should not automatically be the truthmakers of every truth that mentions them. The archive should distinguish:

- what a statement is about,
- what makes it true,
- and what ontology is needed for that truth.

### With `14-properties-universals-tropes-and-kinds.md`

This file extends the archive's sparse realism beyond immanent repeatables. Some abstracta may be real without being universals; some universals, if admitted, may be abstract; and some kind-structures may be better treated as immanent organizational profiles rather than detached entities.

### With `15-essence-possibility-and-necessity.md`

Possible worlds should still be treated representationally by default. This file does **not** reopen the archive toward a default Lewis-style concrete pluriverse, nor toward a mandatory ontology of fully independent possible-world objects.

### With `16-category-discipline-objects-events-facts-relations.md`

Abstractum is **not** a single ontological category. An abstract entity may be a number, proposition, relation, structure, or perhaps kind. Category discipline therefore comes before “abstract vs concrete” verdicts.

### With `21-being-existence-and-ontological-commitment.md`

This file gives the archive a more exact commitment policy:

- **basic abstractum**,
- **derivative but robust abstractum**,
- **immanent structural reality**,
- **practice-dependent but objective content**,
- or **merely representational idealization**.

That is usually better than a flat “exists / does not exist” verdict.

## Practical test for future revisions

When a future proposal invokes numbers, propositions, models, formal structures, or norms, state explicitly:

1. the target abstracta category,
2. its explanatory job,
3. its dependence profile,
4. its truthmaker or truth-condition role,
5. what is lost if it is eliminated,
6. and whether the best verdict is:
   - basic,
   - derivative but robust,
   - immanent structural,
   - practice-dependent objective,
   - or merely representational.

If the proposal cannot survive that profile, it should not expand the archive.

## Working summary

The archive's current answer is neither:

- a reflex platonism that multiplies abstract entities whenever discourse seems objective,
- nor a reflex nominalism that declares victory once paraphrase is imaginable.

Its present working stance is:

> **be realist where abstraction tracks stable structure, truth, or irreducible content; be sparse about independent realms; be plural about kinds of abstracta; and force every appeal to abstract entities to earn its dependence and explanatory profile.**
