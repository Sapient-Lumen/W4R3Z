# Real Emergence Admission Test

The archive needs a disciplined way to say when higher-level reality is genuinely real without making it magically independent. This file provides that test.

## Working definition

A higher-level feature counts as **really emergent** only when it is:

- fully dependent on lower-level reality,
- essentially organization-sensitive,
- explanatorily indispensable at its own level,
- and associated with a stable new modal profile.

Emergence therefore means **dependent novelty under constraint**, not metaphysical insurrection.

## Necessary conditions

A candidate case should satisfy all or nearly all of the following.

### 1. Dependence completeness

The higher-level fact must not float free of its lower-level basis. Change the basis enough, and the higher-level fact disappears.

### 2. Organization sensitivity

Mere addition of parts is not enough. The relevant profile must depend on a specific arrangement, coordination, or form.

### 3. Novel modal profile

The organized whole must support capacities, constraints, or counterfactual truths not readable off a mere inventory of parts taken in abstraction from organization.

### 4. Explanatory indispensability

The higher-level description must explain something real that a flat lower-level description handles badly, opaquely, or not at all.

### 5. Robustness across instances

The higher-level profile should recur across a class of cases with enough stability to support disciplined explanation, not just one-off description.

### 6. No basal independence

The candidate should not be promoted to metaphysical fundamentality merely because it is explanatorily important.

## Failure modes

### Mere aggregation

A pile of grains is usually not emergent merely because it contains many grains.

### Mere shorthand

A higher-level term that adds no explanatory or modal work is a convenience label, not emergence.

### Mystery inflation

A phenomenon is not emergent just because we do not yet understand it.

### Basal rebellion

If a proposed emergent entity is treated as wholly independent of its basis, the archive should treat that as a move toward dualism or plural fundamentality, not as emergence simpliciter.

## Four-grade classification

### Grade 0: no emergence

The higher-level description is merely a summary, aggregate, or viewpoint convenience.

### Grade 1: weak/systemic emergence

Organization matters, but the case may still be fully tractable as structured lower-level dynamics.

### Grade 2: real non-fundamental emergence

The higher-level feature is dependent yet explanatorily and modally robust enough to count as genuinely real. This is the archive's target notion.

### Grade 3: suspect strong emergence

The higher-level feature is treated as introducing autonomous powers or necessities so strong that its relation to the lower-level basis becomes obscure. Keep such cases open, but do not normalize them casually.

## Stress-case sketch

### Living cell

Likely at least Grade 2. Organization, regulation, and metabolic powers appear central, and a parts-list alone does not capture the living unity.

### Whirlpool

Usually Grade 1. It is a real stabilized process, but it may not require a thick new modal profile beyond organized dynamics.

### Corporation or legal office

Often Grade 2 in a social-normative mode. These entities are grounded in practices, rules, recognition conditions, and enforcement structures, yet they alter what agents can do and owe.

### Conscious experience

Still open. It may be Grade 2, Grade 3, or evidence that the archive's basal picture is incomplete.

## Archive policy

Use the word **emergence** only after stating:

1. the lower-level basis,
2. the organization condition,
3. the new modal or explanatory profile,
4. and why the case is not merely shorthand.

If those four items are absent, prefer a more specific term: constitution, organization, realization, or dependence.
