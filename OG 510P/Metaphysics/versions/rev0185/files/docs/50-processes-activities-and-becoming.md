# Processes, Activities, and Becoming

## Why this file exists

The archive has long favored **Layered Process Realism**. It already says that being includes becoming, that many individuals are process-sustained, that powers unfold through organized activity, and that laws articulate modal-structural constraints in a world of ongoing production, maintenance, inhibition, and decay.

But until now it still lacked one direct file saying what a **process** is supposed to be, how processes differ from **events**, **activities**, **achievements**, **states**, and **process-sustained individuals**, and how far the archive really means to let **becoming** do ontological work.

That omission matters because the archive already risks six opposite mistakes:

- treating every process as just an object moving,
- treating every event as if it had the same temporal profile,
- treating becoming as if it required one strong global doctrine of temporal passage,
- treating process talk as merely verbal decoration on a static ontology,
- treating process ontology as if it had to eliminate enduring individuals,
- or sliding between **event**, **activity**, **process**, **state**, **change**, and **happening** as if one word settled them all.

This file gives the archive a compact **process profile** so future revisions can say whether a case is chiefly about an event, an activity, an accomplishment, an achievement, a state, an ongoing process, a recurrent pattern, or a process-sustained individual.

## Working default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Processes are real as temporally extended or dynamically unfolding modes of being, and many ordinary individuals are best understood either as processes, as process-sustained organizations, or as entities whose powers and persistence cannot be understood apart from processual maintenance and change.**

This fits the archive's wider commitments:

- **process** still matters because reality is not exhausted by static inventory,
- **form** still matters because many processes are typed by organized structure rather than mere succession,
- **power** still matters because processes are often the manifestation, inhibition, or modulation of capacities,
- **time** still matters because processes have direction, phase, duration, culmination, recurrence, or interruption,
- **grounding** still matters because a process may depend on materials, powers, roles, or lower-level activities without becoming unreal,
- and **substance** still matters because process realism need not eliminate every persisting bearer or organized whole.

## Core distinctions

### 1. A process is not just an event with a longer name

The archive should distinguish:

- **event** — something that happens,
- **process** — an ongoing or unfolding dynamic occurrence with some temporal articulation,
- **activity** — a process-like occurrence with no intrinsic culmination,
- **accomplishment** — a temporally extended occurrence ordered toward a completion,
- **achievement** — a culminating happening or threshold arrival,
- **state** — a condition that may endure without the same profile of unfolding or culmination,
- and **process-sustained individual** — an entity whose being depends on continued organized activity.

Without this discipline, the archive will keep calling all dynamic cases “processes” while ignoring their very different temporal logic.

### 2. A process is not merely an object in motion

Some processes are indeed changes or motions of objects. But many are not well captured that way:

- a storm,
- a conversation,
- metabolism,
- a legal proceeding,
- a learning trajectory,
- an ecosystem succession,
- a combustion,
- or a supply-chain disruption.

These are often better understood as organized unfoldings whose unity depends on patterned activity, not on one pre-given bearer simply changing accidental properties.

### 3. Becoming is broader than one theory of temporal passage

The archive should separate:

- **ordinary change**,
- **developmental becoming**,
- **happening or occurrence**,
- **local temporal succession**,
- **global passage doctrines**,
- and **mere representational updating**.

So the archive may insist that being includes becoming without thereby committing itself immediately to one heavy A-theoretic doctrine of time.

### 4. Process realism need not eliminate persisting things

The archive should distinguish:

- **everything is process and only process**,
- **many beings are stabilized processes**,
- **some beings are process-sustained composites**,
- and **some explanatory contexts focus on processes even when the ontology also includes individuals, relations, and structures**.

This matters because the archive's default view is not a blanket elimination of individuals into flux. It is a claim that many individuals are intelligible only through the processes that sustain them.

### 5. Processes are typed by phase, grain, and completion profile

The archive should ask whether a target is:

- homogeneous or heterogeneous,
- bounded or open-ended,
- repetitive or nonrecurrent,
- culminating or nonculminating,
- threshold-like or continuously graded,
- locally synchronized or distributed,
- and bearable by one organized subject or spread across many supports.

Otherwise the archive will keep confusing activity with accomplishment, recurrent maintenance with one-off happening, and temporary phase with whole process.

### 6. Processes have identity and boundary problems of their own

A process is not individuated only by where and when it occurs.

Future revisions should ask:

- what makes this process one process,
- what counts as a phase of it rather than a new process,
- what interruptions it can survive,
- what material, energetic, informational, or institutional continuity matters,
- and what kind of end, breakdown, or completion belongs to it.

### 7. Maintenance processes are metaphysically central

The archive should no longer treat maintenance as secondary to production.

Many real beings persist because of:

- repair,
- regulation,
- circulation,
- metabolic turnover,
- homeostatic control,
- information refresh,
- institutional reenactment,
- or repeated coordination.

This matters because many of the archive's favored domains — organisms, persons, institutions, and agencies — are not just caused into existence once and then left alone.

### 8. Failure, arrest, and interruption reveal process ontology

Breakdown cases often show what kind of process was present:

- Did an activity simply stop?
- Did an accomplishment fail before culmination?
- Did a state cease?
- Did the sustaining process collapse while the material basis remained?
- Did one process get replaced by another with similar outward results?

These questions are often more revealing than normal successful cases.

## Four live options

### 1. Snapshot substance primacy

**Picture:** what is most real are static or at least self-identical individuals; process language mainly tracks changes in them.

**What it gets right:**
- individuals matter,
- not every dynamic description earns a new category,
- and one should not multiply process-entities gratuitously.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it underdescribes maintenance, development, and organized becoming,
- and it makes process-sustained identity look accidental.

### 2. Event atomism

**Picture:** what really happens is best understood through discrete happenings, culminations, or event-particles; processes are derivative constructions from them.

**What it gets right:**
- some happenings are threshold-like,
- discrete events matter for causation and temporal ordering,
- and not every dynamic case is an unbroken flow.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it underdescribes extended activity,
- it treats maintenance too episodically,
- and it makes organismic or institutional continuity harder to explain.

### 3. Thin process talk

**Picture:** process vocabulary is useful but ontologically lightweight; all serious work is done by lower-level states, objects, and laws.

**What it gets right:**
- one should resist decorative metaphors of flux,
- and many process claims can be reconstrued more precisely.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it risks deflating becoming into bookkeeping,
- and it underplays the ontological role of organized temporal unfolding.

### 4. Disciplined process realism

**Picture:** processes are real, dynamically articulated modes of being; some are primary in explanation, many endure through phases and organization, and many individuals are process-sustained without thereby disappearing into pure flux.

**What it gets right:**
- becoming belongs to being,
- maintenance and development are ontologically serious,
- process and structure can be constitutively linked,
- and endurance can be compatible with processual maintenance.

**What it risks if left alone:**
- if stated too grandly, it can become a slogan of universal flux,
- if stated too vaguely, it can blur events, states, actions, and individuals,
- and it still needs typed rules across physical, biological, agential, and institutional cases.

## Current archive answer

The archive should adopt a **typed process realism** inside its wider layered process realism:

> **Processes are real dynamic occurrences or unfoldings; activities, accomplishments, achievements, and states differ in temporal logic; and many ordinary beings are best understood as process-sustained individuals rather than as static parcels that merely happen to change.**

In compressed form:

> **Being includes occurring, continuing, culminating, failing, and recurring — but not every dynamic case is the same kind of process.**

This does not mean:

- every real thing is only a process and never an individual,
- every event is temporally extended,
- every change requires a strong global doctrine of passage,
- or every explanatory success licenses a new process-entity.

It means only that the archive should stop relying on process language without typing what sort of dynamic being it is invoking.

## Archive commitments

### 1. Some individuals are best treated as process-sustained beings

Organisms, flames, storms, economies, legal procedures, and some persons or institutions are often not well modeled as fixed material parcels. Their being depends on continued organized activity.

### 2. Activities and accomplishments should not be collapsed

The archive should distinguish:

- **activity** — walking, conversing, metabolizing,
- **accomplishment** — building a house, digesting a meal, writing a contract,
- **achievement** — reaching the summit, signing the contract, the cell's death,
- **state** — resting, being solvent, being under jurisdiction,
- and **maintenance process** — continued repair, regulation, or enforcement.

Without this step, the archive will keep confusing culmination, endurance, and recurrence.

### 3. Process identity is kind-sensitive

A wildfire, a lawsuit, and an organism do not share one identity rule merely because all are dynamic. Future revisions should specify what counts as initiation, phase transition, interruption, continuation, completion, and failure for the domain in question.

### 4. Production is not the whole of process ontology

The archive should explicitly preserve:

- production,
- sustaining,
- prevention,
- inhibition,
- maintenance,
- repair,
- decomposition,
- and replacement.

Many metaphysical pictures overemphasize one-shot production and underdescribe the ontology of continued upkeep.

### 5. Becoming can be local, layered, and typed

The archive should keep apart:

- local happening,
- developmental becoming,
- agent-guided becoming,
- institutional enactment over time,
- and global theories of temporal passage.

This protects the archive from tying every process claim to one grand temporal doctrine.

### 6. Process realism and hylomorphism can reinforce each other

Many processes are not bare flows but **form-governed unfoldings**. Many hylomorphic composites are not static shapes but **processually maintained organizations**. The archive should therefore treat process and form as allied rather than rival categories in many life-, agency-, and artifact-cases.

### 7. Interruption cases are tests, not annoyances

Arrested development, interrupted combustion, suspended animation, paused institutions, dormancy, coma, litigation stays, and software or network downtime are useful tests for whether the archive is dealing with:

- one interrupted process,
- a terminated process,
- a dormant but persistence-preserving phase,
- or a merely similar replacement sequence.

## Process profile rule for future revisions

Before inferring a metaphysical verdict from words like **process**, **activity**, **event**, **happening**, **occurrence**, **becoming**, **ongoing**, **flow**, **dynamic**, **unfolding**, **culmination**, **maintenance**, **development**, **recurring**, **phase**, or **state**, specify:

1. **target kind** — event, process, activity, accomplishment, achievement, state, process-sustained individual, or mixed case;
2. **domain** — physical, chemical, biological, agential, social, institutional, informational, or mixed;
3. **temporal profile** — instantaneous, extended, recurrent, cyclical, cumulative, thresholded, open-ended, culminating, or mixed;
4. **unity basis** — continuous causal linkage, organized form, control loop, developmental trajectory, record-governed continuity, recurrence pattern, or still unsettled;
5. **phase structure** — initiation, maintenance, escalation, culmination, interruption, dormancy, breakdown, completion, or replacement;
6. **bearer profile** — object-bound, field-like, distributed, many-agent, infrastructure-bound, bearer-free occurrence, or mixed;
7. **dependence profile** — causal, constitutive, realizational, grounding-like, law/structure constrained, or mixed;
8. **persistence verdict** — one continuing process, many related processes, one process-sustained individual, mere event-sequence, state-description only, or still unsettled.

Without this step, the archive will keep sliding from happening to process, from process to individual, from continuation to identity, and from becoming to metaphysical drama without enough discipline.

## Stress cases

### Flame and wildfire

These test whether the archive is dealing with one ongoing process, a process-sustained individual, many related combustive events, or a distributed dynamic front whose identity is only partially stable.

### Organism and metabolism

These test whether the organism is itself a process, a process-sustained individual, or some composite relation between bearer and sustaining activity.

### Conversation and argument

These test whether socially and semantically organized happenings have sharper phase structure and identity conditions than mere acoustic succession.

### Lawsuit, election, and institution

These test whether some processes are record-governed, role-structured, and intermittently enacted rather than continuously material in one place.

### Sleep, coma, dormancy, and suspension

These test whether interruption, low-activity phases, or temporary inactivity terminate a process, preserve it, or only preserve the individual sustained by a larger process.

### Skill acquisition and development

These test whether the archive can distinguish ongoing process, stage-completion, temporary plateau, and enduring achieved capacity.

## Compression rules

When future revisions face process claims, they should prefer the smallest adequate claim:

1. **Do not call every dynamic case a process without typing its temporal logic.**
2. **Do not reduce every process to an object-plus-change template.**
3. **Do not infer a global doctrine of passage from local becoming.**
4. **Use process language most confidently where maintenance, development, recurrence, or organized unfolding matter.**
5. **Treat activities, accomplishments, achievements, and states as different ontological pressures, not stylistic variants.**
6. **Let interruption, dormancy, and completion cases do major diagnostic work.**
7. **Allow process-sustained individuals without forcing all ontology into pure flux.**

## Bottom line

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Processes are real, becoming is ontologically serious, and many beings persist only through organized temporal unfolding — but process realism must be typed carefully enough to distinguish events, activities, accomplishments, achievements, states, and process-sustained individuals.**

That answer sharpens a commitment the archive had already been making for a long time: reality is not adequately captured by static inventory alone, yet the cure is not to dissolve every being into undifferentiated flow.
