# Regimes, Domains, and Scope Conditions

## Why this file exists

The archive now has explicit files on **idealization**, **equivalence**, **measurement**, **artifact risk**, **levels**, **symmetry**, and **ontological promotion**. But it still lacked one compact file saying how the archive should treat **regimes**, **domains of applicability**, and **scope conditions**.

Without a dedicated file here, the archive risks eight recurrent mistakes:

- treating success in one **range, scale, or background condition** as if it automatically settled the ontology everywhere,
- treating **ceteris paribus** or domain-limited generalizations as if they were either fake laws or disguised universal laws,
- treating **effective theories** as if they were either mere instrumental crutches or globally final inventories of being,
- treating **phase changes**, threshold effects, or symmetry-breaking cases as if they automatically marked a wholly different world,
- treating failure outside a model's **scope** as if it automatically refuted the target rather than only one description or one regime,
- treating boundary conditions, environmental parameters, and maintenance conditions as if they were mere technical clutter rather than part of what makes a regime what it is,
- treating cross-regime translation as if it were either perfectly smooth or impossible in principle,
- or treating local success as if it were metaphysically trivial merely because it is not universal.

This file gives the archive a compact **regime profile** so future revisions can say what domain is at issue, what conditions stabilize the case, how wide the scope really is, where breakdown happens, and what sort of realism—global, local, effective-regime, transition-sensitive, or only heuristic—has actually been earned.

## Working default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Scope is typed: not every law, model, mechanism, or ontology claim aims at global coverage, and bounded-domain success can still reveal real structure.**

And more specifically:

> **Regime-bound success is metaphysically serious when a target, parameter range, background condition-set, transition profile, and breakdown mode are stated clearly enough to show what is real, what is local, and what does not travel.**

This fits the archive's wider commitments:

- **laws** matter because some generalizations are strict while others are ceteris paribus, stability-based, or regime-specific,
- **levels and scales** matter because many explanatory profiles are multiscale and not exhausted by one privileged grain,
- **idealization** matters because some simplifications capture real effective regimes rather than mere convenience,
- **equivalence** matters because some correspondences are global while others hold only locally, asymptotically, or in one bounded range,
- **measurement** matters because some setups detect a target only under specific parameter windows or preparation conditions,
- **artifact risk** matters because apparent regime change can be target-revealing, setup-induced, threshold-sensitive, or mixed,
- and **revision** matters because later inquiry may preserve a target while reassigning its scope or changing the conditions under which it is legitimately described.

## Core distinctions

### 1. A target domain is not the same thing as its scope conditions

The archive should distinguish:

- the **target** being studied,
- the **variables or parameters** used to characterize it,
- the **background and boundary conditions** under which a description holds,
- the **domain of applicability** of a model or generalization,
- and the **scope** of an ontological verdict drawn from that success.

Otherwise it will keep treating one good description of a target in one setting as if it were the whole metaphysics of that target.

### 2. Strict universality is not the only kind of lawlike seriousness

The archive should distinguish:

- **strict universal laws**,
- **ceteris paribus generalizations**,
- **stability- or invariance-based explanatory regularities**,
- **effective laws** that hold over a bounded range,
- and **highly local heuristic summaries**.

Otherwise it will keep forcing every successful generalization either into physics-style universality or into mere loose convenience.

### 3. Regime sensitivity is not automatically anti-realism

The archive should distinguish:

- **one target behaving differently in different regimes**,
- **different descriptive handles on one target across scales**,
- **different effective entities or kinds that are only stable in one regime**,
- **artifactual regime-selection produced by setup or record architecture**,
- and **mere notational repartitioning**.

Otherwise it will keep treating regime talk either as a retreat from reality or as a magical multiplication of worlds.

### 4. Transitions matter, but not every threshold has the same lesson

The archive should distinguish:

- **genuine phase or regime transitions**,
- **smooth crossovers**,
- **measurement thresholds**,
- **sampling or coarse-graining thresholds**,
- **institutionally imposed thresholds**,
- and **heuristic cutoffs** used only for tractability.

Otherwise it will keep letting every apparent boundary do the same metaphysical work.

### 5. Failure outside scope is not always failure of target-contact

The archive should distinguish:

- **local validity with honest breakdown outside range**,
- **proxy-only success**,
- **partial structural retention across regimes**,
- **need for bridge principles or transition models**,
- and **genuine target-mistake**.

Otherwise it will keep confusing one route's boundary with the world's boundary.

### 6. Effective descriptions can be real without being fundamental

The archive should distinguish:

- **fundamental ontology**,
- **derivative but robust effective kinds**,
- **regime-stable structures or mechanisms**,
- **parameter-bound explanatory profiles**,
- and **mere calculational scaffolding**.

Otherwise it will keep collapsing the effective into either the unreal or the basic.

### 7. Cross-regime continuity must be earned rather than assumed

The archive should distinguish:

- **smooth approximation relations**,
- **singular limits**,
- **patched but intelligible transitions**,
- **plural coexisting models with overlapping scopes**,
- and **genuine discontinuity in kind or organization**.

Otherwise it will keep mistaking continuity of vocabulary for continuity of regime, or discontinuity of equations for discontinuity of target.

## Four live options

### 1. Global uniformism

**Picture:** the best metaphysical description should hold everywhere in the same way; regime-talk is a temporary sign of incomplete science.

**What it gets right:**
- some scope restrictions do disappear under deeper unification,
- and some local descriptions are indeed placeholders.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it underdescribes effective theories, bounded but real regularities, and organized higher-level stability,
- and it encourages premature elimination of derivative but robust realities.

### 2. Patchwork instrumentalism

**Picture:** science gives us a toolbox of local models, each useful in its own patch, with no deeper metaphysical lesson.

**What it gets right:**
- scope conditions matter,
- and many descriptions are local rather than global.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it underdescribes cross-regime stability, real transition structure, and the possibility that bounded-range success reveals genuine organization or lawfulness.

### 3. Lazy regime relativism

**Picture:** every regime is its own world, so any shift in scale, parameter, or environment licenses a fresh ontology.

**What it gets right:**
- transitions and scope boundaries can matter,
- and some descriptions do fail to export cleanly.

**What it gets wrong for this archive if left alone:**
- it overmultiplies ontology from boundary-sensitive behavior,
- and it underdescribes target continuity, bridge relations, and layered dependence.

### 4. Typed regime realism

**Picture:** domains, scopes, and regimes are philosophically serious, but only in typed ways. Some generalizations are strict, some ceteris paribus, some effective, some transition-sensitive, and some merely heuristic. Some regime-bounded entities or structures are derivative but robustly real. Some regime changes matter deeply, while others mark only measurement thresholds, modeling choices, or practical cutoffs.

**What it gets right:**
- it treats scope as ontologically relevant without inflating every local success into a new world,
- it preserves reality-answerability for effective descriptions,
- and it fits the archive's layered realism and anti-flattening discipline.

**What it risks if left alone:**
- it can become vague unless each case states target, range, parameters, transition profile, and what exactly is being promoted.

## Archive commitments

### 1. State the scope before promoting the ontology

Do not let “works in this regime”, “holds at low energy”, “near equilibrium”, “for normal cases”, “at this scale”, or “under these conditions” do metaphysical work without a typed scope profile.

### 2. Keep target identity distinct from regime of manifestation

One target may exhibit different stable profiles in different regimes without becoming a different target in every case.

### 3. Allow effective-regime realism

Some bounded-range descriptions reveal genuine structures, mechanisms, powers, or kinds that are derivative but robust, even if they are not fundamental.

### 4. Keep ceteris paribus seriousness live

A non-universal generalization is not automatically empty. What matters is whether its background conditions, stability profile, and empirical content are good enough to support explanation and correction.

### 5. Treat transitions as informative but typed

Phase changes, thresholds, and breakdown points can reveal real organization, but not every apparent discontinuity is a deep metaphysical divide.

### 6. Refuse the shortcut from locality to either unreality or supremacy

A local model is not automatically “just a model”; a bounded-range success is not automatically the final ontology either.

## Regime profile rule for future revisions

Before inferring a metaphysical verdict from language like **effective theory**, **domain of validity**, **scope condition**, **ceteris paribus**, **under normal conditions**, **near equilibrium**, **low-energy**, **high-temperature**, **phase**, **regime**, **universality class**, **threshold**, **critical point**, **only locally**, **only asymptotically**, **only in this limit**, or **breaks down outside the range**, future revisions should answer at least these questions:

1. **target** — what worldly target, process, organization, quantity, or domain is at issue;
2. **regime type** — scale-bound, parameter-bound, boundary-condition-bound, maintenance-bound, institutionally bounded, transition-bound, or mixed;
3. **controlling variables** — what parameters, coordinates, environmental conditions, cutoffs, preparation procedures, or background assumptions matter;
4. **stability profile** — what remains robust across perturbation, correction, or neighboring cases;
5. **transition profile** — what changes at the boundary: quantity values, organization, lawlike profile, symmetry, kind-status, or merely measurement/readout behavior;
6. **cross-regime relation** — smooth approximation, singular limit, bridge principle, coarse-graining relation, overlap region, patched coexistence, or genuine discontinuity;
7. **failure mode** — honest scope limit, proxy breakdown, artifact-sensitive threshold, false universality claim, regime-confusion, or target-mistake;
8. **promotion pressure** — strict law realism, ceteris paribus or stability-based realism, effective-regime realism, transition-sensitive realism, derivative-status realism, heuristic-only use, or still unsettled;
9. **scope of verdict** — local, regional, multiscale, asymptotic, global, or explicitly unearned.

Otherwise the archive will keep sliding from local success to global ontology, from bounded generalization to emptiness, and from one regime shift to one untyped metaphysical rupture.

## Compression payoff

This file is meant to stabilize six already-live archive fault lines at once:

- **laws** without forcing every successful generalization into one universal mold,
- **levels and scales** without treating every scale-shift as either reduction or fragmentation,
- **idealization** without confusing effective-regime realism with mere tractability,
- **equivalence** without mistaking local or asymptotic correspondence for global sameness,
- **measurement and artifact analysis** without collapsing setup windows and target structure,
- and **ontological promotion** without reading bounded-range success either as nothing or as everything.

## Default archive verdict

The archive should now prefer a **typed realism about regimes and scope**:

> **Reality can include genuine regime structure: some laws, mechanisms, entities, and explanatory profiles are local, effective, or transition-sensitive without being merely unreal; but any ontological lesson from bounded success must state its target, range, background conditions, transition profile, and breakdown mode rather than pretending to be either globally final or metaphysically empty.**
