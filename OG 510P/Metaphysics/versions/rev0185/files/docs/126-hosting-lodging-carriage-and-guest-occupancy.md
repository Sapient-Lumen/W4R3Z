# Hosting, Lodging, Carriage, and Guest Occupancy

This file adds a compact operator-family for cases where something is said to be **in**, **on**, **within**, **aboard**, **hosted by**, **lodged in**, **carried by**, or **occupying** another thing without that alone settling whether it is **part of** it, **identical with** it, **constitutive of** it, or merely **tolerated / supported / transported** by it.

The archive needs this because it already has:

- **porous boundaries / selective exchange** for regulated traffic across an interface,
- **scaffolding / support / enablement** for environmental dependence,
- **constitution / composition / location** for different ways one thing can relate to another,
- **delegation / handoff** for performance through other channels,
- **migration / transplantation / porting / redeployment** for continuity across changed hosts, sites, or platforms,
- and **overlap / exclusion / occupancy limits** for some spatial and part-sharing pressures.

But it still lacks one compact place for cases described as **hosted on**, **living in**, **riding on**, **tenanted by**, **guest in**, **resident within**, **running on a host**, **carried aboard**, **lodged in another system**, or **temporarily occupying**.

Without that operator-family, the archive will keep risking eight compressions:

- collapsing **occupancy**, **parthood**, **constitution**, **carriage**, **support**, **hosting**, and **identity** into one flat inside/outside story,
- inferring **incorporation** from mere continued residence in or on a host,
- inferring **host continuity** from guest continuity,
- inferring **guest continuity** from host continuity,
- treating every tolerated or supported resident as if it were an organ, module, or constitutive subsystem,
- treating host-pathology cases such as **parasitism** as if they were either simple fusion or simple external contact,
- confusing **tenancy / guest status / authorized occupancy** with ownership or bearerhood,
- and reading every talk of **host** or **resident** as if it already settled whether the relation is temporary, dependent, exploitative, mutualistic, constitutive, or merely locative.

This file gives the archive a compact **hosting / lodging / carriage / guest-occupancy profile** so future revisions can say whether a case is chiefly about hosted but non-incorporated residence, vehicular or platform carriage, tenancy or authorized occupancy, parasitic or commensal host-guest relation, temporary lodging, multitenant support, or stronger incorporation only in harder cases.

## Compressed picture

> **Being in, on, within, or aboard a host is not by itself being part of it. A host can remain the same while guests come and go, and a guest can remain the same while hosts change.**

## Why the default view should absorb this operator-family

The archive's working view already says:

- **process** matters, because hosting and occupancy often unfold across time rather than at one static slice,
- **form and organization** matter, because a host relation is kind-sensitive and not every container, carrier, dwelling, or platform supports the same continuity claims,
- **powers and functions** matter, because hosts can nourish, shelter, isolate, transport, constrain, or execute without thereby constituting the guest,
- **dependence pluralism** matters, because hosting, support, location, constitution, realization, and incorporation should not be collapsed,
- **sortals** matter, because parasites, tenants, passengers, guests, organs, applications, and institutions do not count as residents or parts under one criterion,
- **boundary discipline** matters, because occupancy can cross a real boundary without erasing it,
- and **artifact-risk discipline** matters, because platforms, leases, registries, permissions, and UI labels often create continuity shortcuts that do not settle ontology.

So the default view should absorb hosting-language without flattening it.

## What this operator-family is for

This operator-family is for cases where one target is **in or on another target under a typed host relation**.

The relation may involve:

- a **guest in a dwelling or room**,
- a **tenant in a unit or building**,
- a **passenger or cargo aboard a vehicle**,
- a **parasite in or on a host organism**,
- a **virtual machine or application on a host platform**,
- a **document or account on a server**,
- or a **temporary resident within a protected or tolerated space**.

The key question is not simply whether something is located somewhere, but **what sort of host relation is doing the work and whether it carries any stronger claim than residence, support, or carriage**.

## Why the archive needs it

### 1. Occupancy is not the same as parthood

Something can be inside or on another thing without being one of its constitutive parts.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **mere residence or occupancy**,
- **temporary lodging**,
- **carried presence**,
- **nested but non-constitutive hosting**,
- and **genuine part-status only in harder cases**.

Otherwise it will keep sliding from **in** or **on** to **part of**.

### 2. Hosting is not the same as constitution

A host can support, shelter, or execute a guest while still remaining numerically distinct from it.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **hosted support**,
- **platform execution**,
- **environmental enablement**,
- **constitutive incorporation**,
- and **identity only in much narrower cases**.

Otherwise it will keep treating every successful host relation as if host and guest had fused.

### 3. Same host does not mean same guest

A building, vehicle, organism, or server can remain the same while different tenants, passengers, parasites, or workloads occupy it over time.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **same host under changing guests**,
- **same route or vehicle under changed passengers or cargo**,
- **same host platform under changed applications or VMs**,
- **same dwelling under new occupants**,
- and **same host-guest pair only when that relation itself is what matters**.

Otherwise it will keep letting host persistence masquerade as guest persistence.

### 4. Same guest does not mean same host

A guest, tenant, passenger, parasite, service, or account can continue across changed hosts or dwellings.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **same guest under changed housing**,
- **same workload under changed host platform**,
- **same passenger under changed vehicles or seats**,
- **same tenant under changed unit**,
- and **same coupled system only in stronger integrated cases**.

Otherwise it will keep letting guest continuity masquerade as host continuity.

### 5. Carriage is not the same as incorporation

A vehicle, channel, or carrier can transport something without making the passenger, payload, or routed item a constitutive part of the carrier.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **carried aboard**,
- **stored during transport**,
- **embedded as a functioning subsystem**,
- **routed through but not housed by**,
- and **mixed support / transport architectures**.

Otherwise it will keep treating every aboard-relation as componenthood.

### 6. Parasitic or tolerated residence is not the same as fusion

A parasite can live on or in a host and depend on it without becoming the same organism or one ordinary organ of the host.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **parasitic residence**,
- **commensal residence**,
- **mutualistic host-guest interaction**,
- **integrated symbiotic incorporation in harder cases**,
- and **mere external contact only in weaker cases**.

Otherwise it will keep oscillating between rigid externalism and untyped organismal holism.

### 7. Tenancy, guest status, and authorization matter

In social and institutional cases, occupancy is often permission-structured. A tenant, guest, authorized user, or unauthorized occupant may stand in importantly different relations to the same host site.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **tenant occupancy**,
- **guest occupancy**,
- **unauthorized occupancy**,
- **office or role-bearing occupancy**,
- and **ownership or bearerhood only where separately earned**.

Otherwise it will keep conflating who may occupy, who owns, and what the host itself is.

### 8. Multitenancy and layered hosting are not contradictions

A host can simultaneously support many guests, and one guest can itself host lower-level guests.

So the archive should distinguish:

- **single-guest hosting**,
- **multitenant hosting**,
- **guest-within-hosted-guest layering**,
- **carrier chains and nested lodging**,
- and **collapse into one larger bearer only where the nested structure really earns it**.

Otherwise it will keep assuming that one host implies one resident or that layering destroys individuability.

## Typology of host-like cases

### A. Temporary guest occupancy

The key feature is that one guest resides in a host site for a limited interval without thereby becoming part of the host.

**Examples:** a visitor in a room; a short-term guest in a leased unit; a temporary process in a sandbox environment.

### B. Tenancy or authorized dwelling

The key feature is that one occupant has a governed right of use or occupancy relative to a host site that remains numerically distinct from the occupant.

**Examples:** a tenant in a unit; an authorized user in an account space; one team assigned to a facility owned by a larger institution.

### C. Vehicular or platform carriage

The key feature is that one guest is carried, transported, or executed by a carrier or platform without that alone making guest and host one bearer.

**Examples:** a passenger on a train; cargo aboard a ship; a virtual machine on a host platform.

### D. Parasitic, commensal, or tolerated biological residence

The key feature is that one living thing resides on or in another under dependency, burden, tolerated coexistence, or partial mutual advantage, without automatic fusion.

**Examples:** a parasite in a host; surface-dwelling organisms on a host body; a tolerated microbiological resident that is neither just a transient contaminant nor simply one organ of the host.

### E. Nested or multitenant hosting

The key feature is that several guests occupy one host, or one guest itself supports lower-level guests, so that the metaphysical work lies in layered hosting rather than one flat pair.

**Examples:** multi-VM host platforms; an apartment building with many tenants; a cloud tenancy inside a wider datacenter stack.

### F. Continuity theater through host labels

The key feature is that language of hosting or residence masks what is really a change of bearer, ownership, implementation, or institutional role.

**Examples:** “same service” talk that merely tracks a brand label across rebuilt infrastructure; “resident team” talk that ignores charter change; “guest account” talk that hides full incorporation into a new platform identity system.

## Cross-domain pressure

### Virtualization, software hosting, and guest execution

Computational cases pressure the archive because a guest operating system or workload can run on a host platform without being identical with the host, even while the host provides real execution conditions.

### Tenancy, guests, and governed occupancy

Institutional cases pressure the archive because one dwelling or unit can remain the same while tenants, guests, or unauthorized occupants vary, and because rights of use do not by themselves settle ownership or bearerhood.

### Parasitism and host-dependent residence

Biological cases pressure the archive because one organism can live on or in a host and draw sustenance from it while remaining distinct from the host whose boundaries it crosses or exploits.

### Carriage, vehicles, and aboard-relations

Transport cases pressure the archive because passengers and cargo are genuinely carried by a vehicle without thereby becoming constitutive parts of the vehicle.

## False moves to avoid

- Do **not** infer parthood from every case of being **in**, **on**, **within**, or **aboard**.
- Do **not** infer same guest from same host.
- Do **not** infer same host from same guest.
- Do **not** infer ownership from occupancy.
- Do **not** infer incorporation from support or execution.
- Do **not** infer fusion from parasitic or tolerated residence.
- Do **not** infer one flat relation from **hosted on**, **lives in**, **aboard**, **tenant of**, **guest in**, **resident within**, **running on**, **carried by**, **lodged in**, or **occupies**.

## Default diagnostic questions

When a case is described as **hosted on**, **living in**, **tenant of**, **guest in**, **occupying**, **aboard**, **carried by**, **lodged in**, **resident on**, or **running on a host**, ask:

1. What exactly is the **target** — one organism, parasite, tenant, passenger, cargo, service, VM, account, office, or mixed organization?
2. Is the case chiefly about **temporary lodging**, **tenancy**, **carriage**, **platform hosting**, **parasitic residence**, **multitenancy**, **constitutive incorporation**, or a mixed architecture?
3. What exactly is the **host** — one body, dwelling, vehicle, platform, institution, site, or layered support stack?
4. What is doing the explanatory work — **mere location**, **permission-structured occupancy**, **support or execution**, **transport**, **resource dependence**, **regulated boundary crossing**, or mixed basis?
5. Does the host remain the same under changing guests, does the guest remain the same under changing hosts, or is the case really about a changing **host-guest pair**?
6. What is the relevant **grain** — whole bearer, occupant, role, hosted process, account, organ-scale resident, or mixed levels?
7. What is the relevant **authorization profile** — authorized, tolerated, parasitic, unauthorized, ownership-bearing, or mixed?
8. What is the nearest **comparison class** — location, constitution, support, migration, carriage, tenancy, symbiosis, or incorporation?
9. What is the relevant **artifact-risk profile** — label continuity, permission-system artifact, interface shortcut, property-language confusion, or mixed?
10. Whether the best verdict is **typed host-guest occupancy**, **typed carriage**, **typed tenancy / governed occupancy**, **typed parasitic residence**, **nested multitenant hosting**, **stronger incorporation only in harder cases**, or **still unsettled**.

## Compression rule

Future revisions should normally treat **hosting / lodging / carriage / guest occupancy** as one operator-family whenever the explanatory burden is: **one target residing in or on another under a typed host relation; unresolved question whether the relation is mere location, governed occupancy, support, carriage, parasitism, or incorporation; and the need to distinguish same host with changing guests, same guest with changing hosts, and stronger constitutive integration rather than letting one host label settle the rest.**
