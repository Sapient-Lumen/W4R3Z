# Substance, Bearers, and Substrata

## Why this file exists

The archive already talks about:

- **objects and individuals**,
- **form and matter**,
- **powers and bearerhood**,
- **persistence through change**,
- **properties, kinds, and relations**,
- and **processual organization**.

But it still lacks one direct rule for a recurrent pressure point running through all of them:

- when a metaphysics really needs a **substance**,
- what counts as a **bearer** of properties, powers, roles, or changes,
- whether a case calls for a **hylomorphic compound**, a **bundle-like unity**, a **bare particular / substratum**,
- and when the archive should instead speak of a **stabilized processual individual** without forcing a classical substance model onto everything.

Without that discipline, the archive risks sliding among:

1. substance language that silently assumes every real thing is one kind of independent bearer,
2. process language that dissolves persisting subjects too quickly,
3. bundle talk that underdescribes unity and endurance,
4. substratum talk that posits an unexplained something-beneath-properties,
5. and category drift in which objects, persons, organisms, artifacts, institutions, and events are all treated as if they needed the same bearer story.

A compact non-skeptical, non-reductive metaphysics needs a better rule-set than that.

## Core claim

The archive should adopt **typed bearer realism with hylomorphic-process priority**.

Compressed:

> **Some entities are best treated as persisting bearers or subjects of change, but bearerhood is form-sensitive, domain-typed, and often better understood through organized compounds or stabilized processes than through bare substrata.**

This means:

- do **not** eliminate substances or bearers from metaphysics altogether,
- do **not** assume every real individual needs a mysterious propertyless core,
- do **not** force one substance model onto organisms, artifacts, persons, fields, events, and institutions alike,
- and do **prefer** accounts of individual unity that explain what makes the bearer the kind of thing it is.

## Four distinctions the archive now needs

### 1. Substance as persisting subject or bearer

Sometimes a metaphysics needs a relatively unified individual that:

- persists through at least some change,
- bears properties, powers, and relations,
- and can serve as a subject of predication or alteration.

Organisms, many artifacts, and persons are the central pressure cases here.

This matters because not every ontology can do without bearerhood.

### 2. Hylomorphic or form-structured bearerhood

A bearer need not be a featureless core. It may be a **form-matter compound** or otherwise organized individual whose unity comes from structure, function, and kind-sensitive organization.

This matters because the archive already favors form, process, and powers over inert thinghood.

### 3. Bundle-like or profile-based individuality

Some views treat individuals as constituted by compresent properties, tropes, relations, or profiles rather than by an additional underlying substratum.

This matters because some domains may be adequately captured by structured profiles, especially where the explanatory burden is carried by organization rather than by an extra ontological kernel.

### 4. Bare particular / substratum theories

Some metaphysical pictures posit a thin particular or substratum underlying and instantiating properties.

This matters because such views promise a simple answer to what unifies and individuates an object across change, but they also risk positing something explanatorily inert if the substratum does no work beyond being a placeholder for “the thing itself”.

The archive should therefore keep substratum views **live but non-default**.

## Archive default

The current default is:

1. **allow substantial individuals where the domain earns them**;
2. **prefer form-structured or hylomorphic bearerhood over bare substrata**;
3. **allow process-stabilized individuals as real bearers where ongoing organization is what persists**;
4. **do not demand a substance verdict for every category**;
5. **treat substratum theories as pressure tests, not as the automatic default**.

Compressed again:

> **Substances are real where there are persisting, organized subjects of change, but the archive should usually understand them as formed, power-bearing, process-sustained individuals rather than as propertyless cores.**

## What the archive should now say

### 1. Not every real entity is a substance

Events, facts, relations, roles, absences, and some abstracta should not be forced into a substance mold merely because they are real or explanatory.

The archive should therefore refuse the lazy inference:

- real,
- therefore an independent individual,
- therefore a substance.

That inference is too fast.

### 2. But some domains do earn substantial individuality

Organisms, many persons, some artifacts, and some institutions look like more than mere bundles or momentary happenings. They exhibit:

- organized unity,
- kind-sensitive persistence,
- property and power bearing,
- and intelligible survival through at least some material or relational turnover.

The archive should therefore also refuse the opposite shortcut:

- process is real,
- therefore no enduring bearer is needed,
- therefore substance-talk is obsolete.

That inference is also too fast.

### 3. Bearerhood is domain-typed

Different domains may need different bearer stories.

For example:

- **organisms** often earn a hylomorphic or self-maintaining substantial profile,
- **artifacts** may be bearer-like but more role- and design-sensitive,
- **persons** may require bodily, agential, normative, and temporal integration together,
- **institutions** may be robustly real without being substances in the same sense as animals,
- and **events or processes** may be primary in some explanations without replacing all individuals.

So the archive should not search for one flat “theory of substance” before doing local diagnosis.

### 4. Bare substrata should earn their keep

A theory may posit a substratum or bare particular, but it must say what explanatory job that posit does that cannot be handled by:

- organized form,
- compresent tropes or properties,
- continuity of process,
- kind-sensitive persistence,
- or causal-historical continuity.

If the answer is only “there must be something underneath,” the posit is under-motivated.

### 5. Bundle-like views should also earn their keep

A theory may prefer bundle-style individuality, but it must say how the bundle is:

- one bundle rather than many,
- the same through change,
- and anchored enough to bear powers and survive turnover.

If the answer is only “the properties travel together,” the unity story is under-motivated.

### 6. Hylomorphic-process views currently fit the archive best

The archive's working synthesis already says that many real things are **stabilized processual organizations** whose form and powers matter. This file now makes explicit that such entities can still count as **substantial individuals** in a disciplined sense.

That means the archive can say of some cases:

- the thing is not merely a momentary event,
- not merely a bundle with no stronger unity,
- not merely a bare particular plus qualities,
- but a **formed bearer sustained through organized activity and capable of property-, power-, and role-bearing persistence**.

### 7. Substance is not automatically fundamental

A thing can be a substance or substantial individual in its own domain without being metaphysically basic in the strongest sense.

This matters because the archive already distinguishes:

- existence,
- ontological seriousness,
- derivativeness,
- and fundamentality.

So the archive should allow **derivative but robust substances** where a domain earns them.

## Substance-profile rule

When a future revision uses language like **substance**, **subject**, **bearer**, **substratum**, **individual in its own right**, **thin particular**, **bundle**, **hylomorphic compound**, or **what underlies the properties**, it should state:

1. **what category the target belongs to** — object, organism, artifact, person, institution, event, process, relation, or something else;
2. **whether a persisting bearer is really needed** for the explanatory job;
3. **what makes the bearer one** — form, organization, trope-bundle, substratum, processual continuity, role structure, or some mixed basis;
4. **what changes it can survive** — material turnover, property change, role change, relational change, stage change, or none;
5. **whether the case supports substance in a strict, derivative, or only analogical sense**;
6. **what explanatory loss follows** if the target is treated as a mere bundle, a bare substratum, or only a process with no bearer.

If a proposal does not do this, its substance or bearer talk is under-disciplined.

## What this file is not saying

- It is **not** saying every real thing is a substance.
- It is **not** saying substances must be fundamental simples.
- It is **not** saying a bare substratum is incoherent.
- It is **not** saying bundle theories are hopeless.
- It is **not** saying process metaphysics cannot recognize enduring individuals.

It is only saying that the archive now needs a **typed substance-and-bearer discipline**.
