# Persons, Selves, and First-Personal Subjects

## Why this file exists

The archive already has files on identity, sortals, substance, centeredness, agency, normativity, social ontology, and persistence. But it still lacked a direct rule for **persons**, **selves**, and **first-personal subjects**.

Without a dedicated file here, the archive risks six opposite mistakes:

- collapsing **persons** into mere organisms without explaining first-personal, agential, and normative structure,
- inflating the **self** into a mysterious inner substance whenever first-personal unity is hard to analyze,
- treating **subjecthood**, **personhood**, **agency**, and **social role** as if they were one flat kind,
- assuming that whatever persists biologically must settle what persists **personally**,
- assuming that minimal **self-consciousness** already settles the full metaphysics of persons,
- or treating personhood as merely ethical or legal vocabulary rather than as a real, typed organizational profile.

This file gives the archive a compact **person profile** so future revisions can say whether a case is chiefly about an organism, a conscious subject, a first-personal self, a person, a role-bearing social individual, or some mixed case.

The fuller treatment of consciousness itself now lives in `47-consciousness-experience-and-phenomenal-subjectivity.md`.

## Working default

The archive should now say explicitly:

> **Persons are real as organized, first-personally integrated subjects whose agential, normative, diachronic, and socially legible profiles are robust enough to merit a distinct metaphysical treatment without thereby becoming fundamental or detachable from all embodiment, history, and dependence.**

This fits the archive's wider commitments:

- **form** still matters, because persons are not merely heaps of episodes but organized kinds of continuing being,
- **process** still matters, because personhood is maintained across development, learning, breakdown, repair, and narrative or practical integration,
- **power** still matters, because persons have characteristic capacities for self-reference, deliberation, commitment, responsibility, and guided action,
- **structure** still matters, because first-personal, social, legal, and normative positions can help organize person-level reality,
- and **grounding** still matters, because personhood should not float free of organisms, bodies, subjects of experience, institutions, or histories of activity.

## Core distinctions

### 1. Organism, subject, self, person, and persona are not the same

The archive should distinguish at least five things:

- **organism** — a living being with a biological unity profile,
- **subject** — a locus of experience, awareness, or feeling,
- **self** — the first-personally appropriated or integrated perspective from which experiences, attitudes, and actions are mine,
- **person** — a thicker kind involving some integration of first-personality, diachronic organization, agential governance, normative addressability, and social recognizability,
- **persona / role-occupant** — a socially situated presentation or office-bearer that may partially overlap with but does not exhaust the person.

Without this distinction the archive will keep sliding from “there is experience here” to “there is a person here”, and from “there is a person here” to “this social role or legal office simply is the person”.

### 2. The term “self” is useful but dangerous

The archive should treat **self** as a compression term, not as a license for mystery.

Sometimes “self” names:

- a persisting person,
- a subject of consciousness,
- a first-personal perspective,
- a narrative or practical identity,
- or an alleged inner substance.

Future revisions should therefore say what job the term is doing rather than using it as a catch-all. In many cases the best move will be to replace “self” with the more specific notion actually needed.

### 3. First-personality is not just a semantic parameter

The archive already has a file on centeredness and self-location. But **first-personality** is thicker than a mere indexical slot.

A centered description can tell us where a perspective is located. A person-level metaphysics must also ask:

- who or what owns or appropriates the perspective,
- how that perspective is integrated with memory, anticipation, action, and responsibility,
- whether the perspective is minimally experiential or richly self-governing,
- and whether its unity is bodily, psychological, practical, normative, social, or mixed.

So the archive should not slide from “this case requires a center” straight to “this case requires a person”, nor from “this case involves self-reference” straight to “this case requires a substantial self”.

### 4. Subjecthood and personhood can come apart

The archive should keep apart:

- **minimal subjecthood** — there is something it is like, or there is a locus of awareness,
- **self-conscious subjecthood** — the subject can in some way be aware of itself as itself,
- **personhood** — there is a thicker profile involving first-personal integration, agential organization, normative standing, and diachronic unity,
- **social personhood** — there is also recognition, standing, office, or participation in normative practices.

These often travel together in paradigm adult human cases. But the archive should not assume they are always coextensive. Border cases matter: infants, impaired agents, distributed institutional actors, artificial agents, role-heavy legal constructs, and various thought experiments can pull them apart.

### 5. Personhood is a multi-strand profile rather than a single simple criterion

The archive should treat personhood as at least partly composed of several strands:

- **first-personal strand** — self-reference, ownership, or self-awareness,
- **diachronic strand** — integration across time,
- **agential strand** — guidance, control, commitment, or self-governance,
- **normative strand** — addressability by reasons, permissions, responsibilities, or standing expectations,
- **social strand** — recognizability, office, uptake, role occupancy, or institutionally supported status.

Different theories privilege different strands. The archive should resist both extremes:

- **one-strand reductionism**, where one feature settles everything,
- **flat pluralism**, where any socially salient or psychologically interesting cluster automatically earns full personhood.

### 6. Personal persistence is not automatically settled by one continuity relation

The archive already distinguishes identity, persistence, substance, constitution, and agency. Person-level cases require these to be pulled together more carefully.

Future revisions should keep live at least these rival packages:

- **organism-first views** — we are fundamentally living beings and personhood rides on animal continuity,
- **psychological-continuity views** — personal persistence is anchored primarily in psychological connectedness or continuity,
- **constitution or layered views** — persons are real but non-identical to their material or biological bases,
- **processual integration views** — persons persist through organized ongoing integration rather than one static bearer criterion alone.

The archive need not settle the rivalry yet. But it should stop talking as if biological continuity, narrative continuity, memory continuity, agential continuity, and legal-social continuity were automatically the same thing.

### 7. Personhood can be derivative but robust

The archive should resist two temptations:

- **flat reductionism**, where persons are only convenient summaries of subpersonal or biological facts,
- **flat substantivalism**, where persons are treated as metaphysically basic, detachable egos.

Many cases are better treated as **derivative but robust personal reality**. A person may be:

- embodied without being reducible to body-talk alone,
- biologically sustained without being exhausted by organismic vocabulary,
- socially recognized without being merely a legal fiction,
- normatively addressable without being a free-floating moral atom,
- and first-personally integrated without being a hidden substance behind all experience.

## Person profile rule for future revisions

When a future revision uses language like **person**, **self**, **subject**, **someone**, **agent**, **myself**, **first-personal**, **personal-level**, **personal perspective**, **owner of experience**, **center of concern**, **responsible party**, or **who someone is**, it should specify at least the following:

1. **target kind** — organism, conscious subject, self-conscious subject, person, social/legal person, role-bearer, persona, or mixed case;
2. **unity basis** — biological, experiential, psychological, agential, narrative, normative, social, institutional, or mixed;
3. **first-personal profile** — none, minimal, pre-reflective, reflective, linguistically articulated, socially scaffolded, or mixed;
4. **agential profile** — mere behavior, intentional action, organized control, self-governance, delegated/distributed agency, or mixed;
5. **normative profile** — none, thin standing, responsibility-bearing, authority-bearing, rights-bearing, role-governed, or mixed;
6. **social profile** — unrecognized, informally recognized, role-situated, institutionally codified, legally tracked, platform-mediated, or mixed;
7. **persistence basis** — organismic continuity, psychological continuity, narrative integration, practical integration, constitutional continuity, processual integration, or still unsettled;
8. **failure profile** — fragmentation, fission, radical discontinuity, depersonalization, loss of agency, loss of recognition, legal nullity, or mixed;
9. **reality verdict** — fundamental person, derivative but robust person, thinly dependent role-person, merely representational persona, or still unsettled.

## Compression guidance

The archive should prefer the **lowest-inflation personal profile** that still preserves the real differences between:

- a living organism and a first-personal subject,
- a subject and a person,
- a person and a role or office,
- a socially recognized person and a merely convenient legal or representational proxy,
- a centered perspective and an integrated self,
- a psychologically connected life and a normatively responsible one.

## Default stance for now

The archive should remain open between organism-first, psychological, constitutional, and processual accounts of persons.

But unless a future revision forces a stronger verdict, the archive should prefer:

> **typed derivative personal realism** — persons are real, organized, first-personal, agential, and normatively addressable beings or profiles, usually embodied and socially situated, whose reality is neither fundamental in every respect nor reducible to any single lower-level or thinner description.

That fits the archive's broader picture better than either a flat animal-only view, a free-floating ego ontology, or an eliminativism about persons.
