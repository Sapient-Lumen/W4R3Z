# 032 — Vagueness, borderlines, and indeterminate being

## One-line thesis

A non-reductive metaphysics should treat **vagueness and borderline status as real pressure points on ontology without assuming that every borderline case reveals a vague object**: some indeterminacy is semantic or representational, some may be worldly, and the archive should distinguish these instead of forcing either false precision or blur absolutism.

## Why this matters

The archive now has explicit positions on kinds, composition, constitution, identity, boundaries, absence, nonexistence, worldhood, contingency, and intrinsicness. But it still lacks one dedicated place to say what should happen when reality or our grip on it seems **borderline**, **soritical**, or **not fully settled at a sharp cutoff**. Without that, the datacube risks three bad slides: precision panic, where every vague case must be forcibly sharpened; blur absolutism, where every difficult boundary is treated as proof that reality itself is vague all the way down; or eliminative escape, where the price of avoiding indeterminacy is to deny ordinary objects, kinds, or boundaries altogether.

That omission matters across several domains at once. Clouds, coastlines, organisms, social statuses, heaps, species, persons, and artifacts all generate boundary pressure. Composition disputes and the problem of the many turn borderline membership into ontological stress. Identity and persistence puzzles become harder when counting and reidentification meet borderline cases. Social and normative categories may be historically real without always having razor edges. Even where one denies ontic vagueness, a non-reductive metaphysics still needs an account of why borderline discourse is stable, practically indispensable, and often answerable to real worldly structure rather than mere confusion.

The archive should therefore take a disciplined middle path: **indeterminacy without blur absolutism**. Vagueness is real as a phenomenon and often load-bearing for metaphysics. But the archive should resist the reflex that every vague predicate implies a vague object, every sorites series implies ontic indeterminacy, or every sharp metaphysical line must be available in ordinary language. Some borderline status belongs to representation, practice, or selective interests; some may belong to the world; and the task is to tell these apart case by case.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Distinguish semantic, representational, and worldly indeterminacy

The archive should not use “vagueness” as one undifferentiated label. Sometimes a term is vague because its criteria are underdetermined. Sometimes a map, model, image, or practice is imprecise. Sometimes the deeper pressure is ontological: ordinary objects, boundaries, or composition facts may resist a clean all-or-nothing inventory. A serious metaphysics should therefore ask **where** the indeterminacy lives before deciding what sort of ontology to build.

### 2. Treat borderline cases as tests, not as automatic metaphysical verdicts

Borderline cases are philosophically powerful because they reveal where our classifications, boundaries, and counting practices become inquiry-resistant. But a borderline case is not by itself proof either that the world is perfectly sharp underneath or that the world is itself vague. The archive should use soritical and boundary cases diagnostically: they test theories of kinds, composition, identity, and ordinary objects, but they do not settle those theories in advance.

### 3. Keep higher-order vagueness visible

It is not only unclear whether some cases fall under a predicate; it is often also unclear where the unclear cases begin. This matters because any metaphysics that promises one clean precision-fixing maneuver must still explain why the location of the sharp boundary is itself so resistant to articulation. The archive should therefore treat higher-order vagueness as a real design constraint on theory, not as a disposable curiosity.

### 4. Link vagueness to composition, constitution, and the problem of the many

Vagueness is not just a philosophy-of-language puzzle. When a cloud, organism, coastline, or artifact has many nearly equally good candidate boundaries, questions about what composes what become ontological. The archive should therefore connect vagueness to composition and constitution directly. Some strategies dissolve the pressure by denying ordinary composites, others by allowing plenitude, others by tolerating indeterminacy. A salient metaphysics has to say openly which cost it is willing to bear.

### 5. Resist both forced precision and vague-being inflation

Forced precision says that every acceptable metaphysics must ultimately recover a perfectly exact extension for every legitimate predicate or object. Vague-being inflation says that every fuzzy boundary licenses a full-strength vague object or indeterminate identity fact. The archive should refuse both reflexes. Some domains may support exact metaphysical structure underneath rough discourse; others may sustain derivative or ontic indeterminacy; and some may require replacing naive object-talk with more structured accounts of regions, processes, or organized wholes.

### 6. Allow exactness and vagueness at different levels

A non-reductive archive should remain open to the thought that lower-level descriptions may be exact while higher-level objecthood, kind membership, or boundary talk remains vague or interest-sensitive; but it should also remain open to the possibility that some worldly indeterminacy is not merely linguistic. The key is level-discipline. Exact micro-description does not automatically eliminate vague macro-being, and vague macro-classification does not automatically prove deep ontic vagueness.

### 7. Preserve ordinary reality without pretending it is mathematically clean

Many ordinary beings are real even if their edges, thresholds, or membership conditions are not always razor-sharp. Clouds, species, institutions, neighborhoods, and developmental stages may be real enough to support explanation, coordination, and causal discourse without satisfying a fantasy of absolute precision. The archive should therefore avoid using vagueness as a pretext either for nihilism about ordinary objects or for a lazy anything-goes pluralism.

## Guardrails

- Do not infer from every vague term that there is a corresponding vague object.
- Do not infer from every hard boundary case that reality must secretly be perfectly sharp.
- Do not collapse semantic vagueness, representational imprecision, and worldly indeterminacy into one phenomenon.
- Do not use sorites pressure as a shortcut to eliminativism about ordinary beings.
- Do not assume higher-order vagueness can be ignored without cost.
- Do not let practical usefulness alone settle whether a vague category tracks anything real.

## Failure modes

- **precision panic**: every respectable ontology is assumed to require hidden sharp lines everywhere.
- **blur absolutism**: every borderline case is treated as proof that the world is fundamentally vague.
- **semantic confinement**: all vagueness is declared linguistic before the ontological cases are examined.
- **ontic inflation**: vague objects, vague identity, or vague existence are multiplied whenever discourse gets fuzzy.
- **ordinary-object escape**: indeterminacy pressure is answered mainly by denying clouds, organisms, artifacts, or social beings.
- **level confusion**: exactness at one descriptive level is assumed automatically to settle all higher-level ontology.

## Practical tests

1. Does the theory distinguish semantic vagueness, representational imprecision, and metaphysical indeterminacy instead of using them interchangeably?
2. Can it explain borderline and higher-order borderline cases without simply stipulating a hidden sharp cutoff?
3. Can it say what vagueness does to composition, counting, and ordinary-object ontology?
4. Can it preserve real ordinary beings without demanding that every legitimate kind or boundary be mathematically exact?
5. Can it remain open to some ontic indeterminacy without turning every fuzzy case into a vague object or vague identity fact?

## Closing synthesis

The archive should adopt a posture of **indeterminacy without blur absolutism**: vagueness is metaphysically serious because borderline cases stress our theories of kinds, boundaries, composition, and ordinary objects, but the right response is disciplined differentiation, not forced precision and not unrestricted ontic blur.
