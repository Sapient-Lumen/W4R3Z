# 007 — Social and normative reality without fictionalism

## One-line thesis

A non-reductive metaphysics should treat social and normative realities as genuinely real but non-fundamental: institutions, statuses, obligations, permissions, and collective structures are constituted through practices, recognitional structures, and histories, yet they are not thereby mere fictions.

## Why this matters

Metaphysics often becomes implausible when it handles the social world badly. If it says only basic physics is real, then marriages, borders, debts, laws, offices, promises, and norms become at best convenient illusions. If it swings the other way and makes social reality free-floating, it loses the dependence of institutions on material, psychological, historical, and recognitional conditions.

The archive should take a middle route.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Treat social construction as a mode of dependence, not a synonym for unreality

To say that something is socially constructed is to say that it depends in the right way on social practices, institutions, collective intentions, statuses, classifications, or recognitional regimes. It is not to say that it is unreal.

### 2. Keep normative facts in the archive’s ontology

Promises, duties, rights, offices, and permissions alter what can be done, demanded, criticized, or enforced. Their reality is not the same as the reality of electrons, but their metaphysical standing should not be reduced to private feelings or predictive shorthand.

### 3. Emphasize historical and institutional embodiment

Social and normative entities are typically embodied in documents, habits, legal systems, practices, material infrastructures, and enforcement regimes. This embodiment prevents them from floating free while preserving their irreducibly social character.

### 4. Allow downward constraint without spooky independence

Institutions and norms can constrain individuals, shape incentives, authorize actions, and reorganize possibilities. That does not require them to be ungrounded. It requires them to be real organized structures with practical and explanatory force.

### 5. Keep social ontology connected to the rest of metaphysics

The social world is not a quarantine zone. Social entities raise the same core metaphysical questions as other entities: what are they, what grounds them, how do they persist, what powers do they have, and when do they cease to exist?

## Guardrails

- Do not infer unreality from social construction.
- Do not treat normative facts as mere emotional projection by default.
- Do not treat institutions as floating abstractions without material embodiment.
- Do not treat social dependence as identical to arbitrary convention.
- Do not isolate social ontology from broader metaphysical method.

## Failure modes

- **fictionalist deflation**: social reality is treated as make-believe.
- **free-floating normativity**: obligations are severed from institutions, practices, or forms of life that sustain them.
- **material erasure**: embodiment and enforcement disappear from institutional ontology.
- **conventionism**: all social reality is treated as effortlessly revisable.
- **dual-book metaphysics**: the archive uses one ontology for nature and an unrelated one for society.

## Practical tests

1. Can the theory explain how socially constructed entities are real?
2. Can it show what grounds particular institutions or statuses?
3. Can it explain how norms constrain action without turning them into spooky forces?
4. Can it track the persistence and dissolution conditions of institutions?
5. Can it integrate social reality with the archive’s general account of dependence and time?

## Closing synthesis

The archive should affirm **social and normative realism without fundamentality**: institutionally constituted and norm-governed realities are genuine parts of the world, even though they depend on practices, histories, recognition, and embodiment.
