# 001 — Metaphysics after skepticism and reduction

## One-line thesis

A serious metaphysics should begin from the claim that reality is knowable enough to be articulated, richer than any one reductive inventory, and structured enough that dependence, modality, time, and social reality can be studied as real features of the world rather than as mere shadows of language or physics.

## Why this matters

The archive needs an explicit starting posture because contemporary metaphysics is often pulled in two opposite directions at once. One direction says metaphysics is illegitimate, empty, or merely verbal. The other says metaphysics is legitimate only if it collapses into the most austere physical inventory available. Both pressures flatten the field.

A non-skeptical and non-reductive archive needs a cleaner starting point. Metaphysics is not a permission slip for fantasy, but neither is it just bookkeeping for current science. It is the attempt to say what kinds of things there are, how they depend on one another, what makes them the kinds of things they are, and what it would mean for a world like ours to hang together at all.

The archive therefore begins from three standing refusals:

- do not treat intelligibility as illusion;
- do not treat reality as exhausted by one favored vocabulary;
- do not confuse explanatory dependence with simple dismissal.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Keep realism without pretending to omniscience

The archive should assume that there is a world independent enough from our theorizing to constrain it. That does not mean every metaphysical question has an easy answer or that our conceptual schemes are transparent windows. It means that metaphysical inquiry can succeed or fail because reality is not merely made true by our preferences.

### 2. Distinguish metaphysical inquiry from local empirical theory without severing them

Science matters because it tells us a great deal about what exists and how it behaves. But metaphysical questions persist even when science is in excellent shape. Questions about essence, grounding, causation, laws, persistence, constitution, possibility, and social construction are not eliminated just because empirical work is successful. They often sharpen precisely when empirical work succeeds.

### 3. Refuse flat inventory metaphysics

A flat inventory asks for the one final list of what there really is and then treats everything else as second-class or unreal. The archive should resist that style. Reality may contain entities, processes, relations, structures, powers, norms, and institutions whose modes of being are not identical even when they are interdependent.

### 4. Treat dependence as more informative than mere existence claims

Questions of the form “does x exist?” matter, but they are often too coarse. A stronger archive asks:

- what makes x the thing it is;
- what x depends on;
- what x can do;
- whether x is basic, derivative, or institutionally constituted;
- and what sort of explanation properly answers to x.

### 5. Reject the equation of the non-fundamental with the unreal

Many real things are not fundamental. Storms, organisms, courts, promises, ecosystems, and currencies may be derivative in one respect while remaining perfectly real in another. The archive should treat derivative being as genuine being unless there is a positive reason to demote it.

### 6. Build from articulation, not from premature closure

A good metaphysical archive should prefer disciplined articulation over theatrical finality. It should preserve rival theories, decision criteria, and points of pressure instead of pretending that a single slogan has solved the whole field.

## Guardrails

- Do not collapse metaphysics into a fight over whether only physics is real.
- Do not inflate uncertainty into global skepticism.
- Do not treat current explanatory success as proof that every higher-level kind is unreal.
- Do not treat conceptual dependence, causal dependence, and grounding as interchangeable.
- Do not confuse a thin ontology with a deep one.

## Failure modes

- **verification panic**: only what a current empirical method directly measures is allowed to count as real.
- **inventory fetish**: the archive mistakes enumeration for explanation.
- **skeptical veto**: hard questions are treated as unintelligible by default.
- **derivative nihilism**: anything grounded, emergent, or socially constructed is dismissed as unreal.
- **category flattening**: entities with different modes of being are forced into one template.

## Practical tests

A starting posture is working when it can answer yes to most of the following:

1. Can it treat metaphysical questions as substantive without pretending they are straightforwardly empirical?
2. Can it distinguish reality from fundamentality?
3. Can it admit multiple kinds of dependence without collapsing them into one relation?
4. Can it treat socially or historically constituted phenomena as real without making them fundamental?
5. Can it keep empirical findings authoritative where they belong without letting them monopolize every metaphysical question?

## Closing synthesis

The archive’s initial stance is therefore simple: **reality is real, intelligible enough for metaphysical inquiry, and too structured to be captured either by wholesale skepticism or by one reductive final inventory**.
