# 005 — Time, persistence, and historical being

## One-line thesis

Metaphysics should treat temporality as load-bearing: reality is not fully intelligible unless it can explain change, persistence, succession, and historical constitution, and no single persistence model should be presumed to fit every kind of being.

## Why this matters

Many metaphysical pictures look stable only because time is underdescribed. But the world we inhabit includes birth, decay, development, memory, anticipation, institution-building, and irreversible history. A metaphysics that cannot say how beings persist and transform through time will either evacuate change or trivialize identity.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Keep the classic time debates open but bounded

Questions about presentism, eternalism, the growing block, and related views should remain live. The archive does not need premature settlement here. It does need a theory that can acknowledge that our temporal experience and our physical theories both constrain metaphysical options.

### 2. Treat persistence as category-sensitive

Endurance and perdurance should not be imposed universally. Some entities may be best understood as wholly present through time; others as temporally extended; still others as phase-like or historically constituted in ways that require mixed treatment.

### 3. Give history metaphysical significance

Some beings are not merely located in history but partly constituted by it. Persons, institutions, languages, traditions, and many artifacts are what they are partly because of their temporal formation. Their histories are not external annotations.

### 4. Preserve event and process ontology where change is central

A temporally serious metaphysics should make room for events and processes as primary explanatory units in many cases. This prevents the archive from treating all change as accidental modification of timeless cores.

### 5. Keep temporal metaphysics linked to identity, causation, and emergence

Persistence is not an isolated puzzle. How an entity persists affects what counts as its powers, what causal stories apply to it, and whether emergent organization can survive material turnover.

## Guardrails

- Do not talk as if time were an optional overlay on a static ontology.
- Do not insist that one persistence theory must explain every entity.
- Do not erase historical constitution from persons, institutions, or practices.
- Do not reduce events to mere changes in substances when event structure is explanatorily prior.
- Do not ignore the pressure physics places on ordinary temporal intuitions.

## Failure modes

- **timeless residue metaphysics**: change is treated as superficial compared with static being.
- **single-model persistence**: endurance or perdurance is imposed without category sensitivity.
- **history blindness**: historically constituted entities are treated as ahistorical tokens.
- **event erasure**: happenings and processes are forced into substance-only descriptions.
- **experience/physics split**: temporal theory ignores one side of the evidence base entirely.

## Practical tests

1. Can the theory explain genuine change rather than merely redescribe it?
2. Can it give a plausible account of persistence across different kinds of entities?
3. Can it treat historical constitution as metaphysically real where appropriate?
4. Can it integrate event/process ontology with object ontology?
5. Can it stay open to both phenomenological and physical constraints on temporal theory?

## Closing synthesis

The archive should adopt a **temporally serious and category-sensitive metaphysics**: time is real enough to matter, persistence may take more than one form, and history can partly constitute what certain beings are.
