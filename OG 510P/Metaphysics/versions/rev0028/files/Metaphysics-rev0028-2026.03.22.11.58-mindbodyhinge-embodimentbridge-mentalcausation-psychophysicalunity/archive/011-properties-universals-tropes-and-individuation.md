# 011 — Properties, universals, tropes, and individuation

## One-line thesis

A salient non-reductive metaphysics should treat properties and relations as real features of being, and should prefer a disciplined middle posture on which repeatable qualitative natures and particularized instances both do explanatory work in accounting for similarity, causation, and individuation.

## Why this matters

The archive already relies on essences, powers, laws, structures, and formed wholes. But those commitments remain underdescribed unless the archive can say what kinds of features things have, how qualitative recurrence is possible, why this redness and that redness are alike, and what helps make one bearer this individual rather than another.

This is where metaphysics often splits too quickly. One family of views treats properties as shareable universals. Another treats concrete reality as built from particularized property-instances or tropes. A third tries to dissolve the issue into language, sets, resemblance classes, or conceptual convenience. The archive should resist premature victory claims here while still adopting a disciplined working posture.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Require a real property story, not only a predicate story

Predicates in language are not yet properties in reality. A serious metaphysics should be able to say what in the world makes predication apt, whether by universals, tropes, structured modes, or some carefully defended alternative.

### 2. Explain qualitative recurrence without magic

Different things can really be alike. Two copper wires can share conductivity, two roses can both be red, and two legal acts can both instantiate the same status-form. The archive should therefore keep room for **repeatable qualitative structure** rather than treating resemblance as always brute or merely verbal.

### 3. Explain concrete particularity without flattening everything into shareables

Concrete reality is not exhausted by repeatable natures. This apple’s gloss, this person’s scar, and this magnet’s field-strength here and now are instance-involving features. The archive should therefore also keep room for **particularized instances**—whether called tropes, modes, or concrete property-exemplifications—where causal work, local configuration, and individuation demand them.

### 4. Keep relations first-class

Relations are not just optional decorations on independently complete individuals. Spatial order, kinship, monetary debt, causal ordering, and institutional authority all involve relational structure that cannot always be reconstructed from monadic features without loss. Property theory and relation theory must therefore stay linked.

### 5. Use property theory to discipline individuation

Individuation is not answered by bare numerical distinctness alone. The archive should ask what makes one bearer this entity rather than another: matter, form, history, relational position, trope-profile, or some combination. Different domains may individuate differently, but the archive should reject the lazy thought that individuality is metaphysically primitive in every case.

### 6. Prefer a layered property realism as the archive’s current working posture

The best working posture for the archive is a **layered property realism**:

- repeatable properties or natures help explain objective similarity, lawlike recurrence, and cross-instance intelligibility;
- particularized instances help explain local causal efficacy, situated character, and why concrete bearers are not mere placements of universals in a void;
- relations remain real and sometimes irreducible;
- sparsity and abundance questions should be handled case by case rather than by one global slogan.

This is not a final doctrine. It is a disciplined middle route between pure universalism, pure trope monism, and deflationary nominalism.

## Guardrails

- Do not reduce properties to predicates by default.
- Do not assume every explanatory role must be played by universals alone.
- Do not assume trope talk automatically solves resemblance or individuation.
- Do not reduce relations to dispensable grammar.
- Do not make property abundance so cheap that objective structure disappears into arbitrary set-membership.

## Failure modes

- **predicate deflation**: worldly features are collapsed into language alone.
- **shareable-only flattening**: local instantiation and causal particularity disappear.
- **trope monism by reflex**: tropes are invoked without an account of resemblance, kinds, or structure.
- **relation minimization**: networked structure is treated as derivative when it is load-bearing.
- **individuation laziness**: identity is asserted without any serious story about what differentiates bearers.

## Practical tests

1. Can the theory explain objective similarity among distinct things?
2. Can it explain concrete, local property-instances without treating them as mere linguistic shadows?
3. Can it say when relations are irreducible rather than monadic byproducts?
4. Can it connect property theory to causation, laws, essence, and composition?
5. Can it explain individuation without multiplying entities recklessly or appealing to brute haecceities too early?

## Closing synthesis

The archive should treat **properties, relations, and individuation as central metaphysical pressure points** and should currently favor a **layered property realism** in which repeatable natures, particularized instances, and real relations all have explanatory roles. That posture fits the archive’s broader aim: reality is articulated, non-flat, and richer than any one reductive inventory.
