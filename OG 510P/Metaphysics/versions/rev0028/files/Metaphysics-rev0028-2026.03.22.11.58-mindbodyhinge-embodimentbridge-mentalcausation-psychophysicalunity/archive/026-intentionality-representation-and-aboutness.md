# 026 — Intentionality, representation, and aboutness

## One-line thesis

A non-reductive metaphysics should treat intentionality as a **real mode of directedness**: some states, acts, symbols, and institutions are about things, possibilities, absences, values, or situations, and this aboutness cannot be reduced either to bare causal covariance, formal syntax, or free social stipulation alone.

## Why this matters

The archive now has explicit positions on truthmaking, propositions, consciousness, agency, social ontology, teleology, and absence. But it still lacks one dedicated place to say what it is for a subject, sign, representation, or collective practice to be **of**, **about**, or **directed at** something. Without that, too much of the archive risks relying on contentful language while leaving its metaphysical standing underdescribed.

The danger again runs in two directions. One extreme treats aboutness as magical: meanings and directedness are assumed but never explained, so representation becomes an occult power. The opposite extreme treats intentionality as dispensable: thoughts and signs are reduced to causal tracking, inferential role, syntax, behavior, or stipulation, leaving no robust place for error, empty reference, anticipation, or world-directed concern. The archive needs a middle path: **disciplined realism about intentionality**.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Distinguish original from derived intentionality

Some aboutness appears to belong to subjects or their states in a more basic way; other aboutness is borrowed from those subjects, practices, or systems. A belief, perception, fear, or intention may be intentionally directed in a different way from a road sign, a map, a statute, or a ledger entry. The archive should therefore distinguish **original intentionality** from **derived intentionality** instead of flattening all representation into one level.

### 2. Distinguish causal correlation from representation

A state may correlate with rain, fire, prey, inflation, or danger without thereby representing it. Mere covariance, reliable indication, or brute triggering is not yet full aboutness. A salient metaphysics should ask what makes a state or sign count as *of* something rather than merely caused by it. Functional role, teleology, embodiment, social uptake, and inferential position may all matter, but none should be treated as an automatic master key.

### 3. Keep room for error, emptiness, and non-presence

Intentionality matters partly because subjects and representations can miss their mark. Hallucination, fiction, mistaken belief, fear of what is not present, intention toward future action, and reference to absent or impossible objects all show that aboutness cannot be modeled only on successful present contact. A good metaphysics should therefore explain how directedness can reach beyond what is presently there without turning every represented object into a fully existing inner surrogate.

### 4. Treat world-involvement and embodiment as load-bearing

Actual intentional life is rarely a relation between a sealed inner item and a detached external object. Perception, practical coping, memory, anticipation, testimony, inscription, and symbol-use are embodied, temporally extended, and environmentally situated. Even where one rejects a fully externalist or enactivist theory, the archive should treat bodily organization, worldly embedding, and historical practice as metaphysically serious for intentionality as it actually occurs.

### 5. Distinguish theoretical, practical, and collective intentionality

Believing that the bridge is unsafe, intending to cross tomorrow, jointly attending to a map, and accepting a legal status are not all the same mode of directedness. The archive should distinguish **cognitive**, **practical**, and **collective or institutional** intentionality while still allowing them to interact. This matters because social reality, agency, propositions, and norms all recruit different intentional structures.

### 6. Connect intentionality to truth, normativity, and satisfaction without collapsing them

Representations can be accurate or inaccurate; intentions can be fulfilled or frustrated; desires can be satisfied or thwarted. These normative contrasts are not optional decoration. But neither truth nor satisfaction should simply be identified with intentionality itself. A robust metaphysics should treat aboutness as what makes such correctness-conditions possible, while still distinguishing representation from truthmaking, desire from fulfillment, and collective acceptance from reality itself.

### 7. Refuse both inner-picture inflation and syntax-only deflation

The archive should avoid two recurring temptations. One is **inner-picture inflation**: every contentful state is modeled as containing a little internal object that mirrors its target. The other is **syntax-only deflation**: once formal role, symbol-manipulation, or social use is in place, genuine aboutness is declared unnecessary. A better posture is that intentionality is real, structurally articulated, and often realized through embodied, inferential, teleological, and social organization.

## Guardrails

- Do not reduce intentionality to bare causal covariance or triggering.
- Do not infer that every representation must contain an inner proxy object.
- Do not collapse aboutness into truth, satisfaction, or successful reference.
- Do not treat derived sign-meaning as the whole story of original subject-level intentionality.
- Do not erase empty, mistaken, fictional, anticipatory, or absent-directed intentionality.
- Do not assume collective intentionality is either impossible by definition or metaphysically unproblematic.

## Failure modes

- **covariance deflation**: causal tracking or reliable indication is mistaken for full aboutness.
- **inner-theater inflation**: representational content is modeled through little inner objects, duplicates, or pictures by default.
- **success chauvinism**: only veridical or satisfied cases are treated as genuinely intentional.
- **syntax substitution**: formal role or symbol manipulation is allowed to replace intentionality rather than realize or support it.
- **social flattening**: institutional or linguistic meaning is treated as the only kind of aboutness, erasing subject-level intentionality.
- **collective panic**: shared or group-directed intentionality is ruled out in advance even where social ontology and joint action require it.

## Practical tests

1. Does the theory distinguish causal indication, representation, reference, and full intentional directedness?
2. Can it explain error, hallucination, fiction, anticipation, and absent-directed thought without magical inner stand-ins?
3. Does it distinguish original, derived, and collective intentionality instead of collapsing them?
4. Can it connect intentionality to embodiment, teleology, inference, and social practice without reducing it to any one of them?
5. Does it keep aboutness distinct from truthmaking, fulfillment, and mere causal success?

## Closing synthesis

The archive should adopt a **disciplined realism about intentionality**: aboutness is a real and structured feature of subjects, signs, practices, and institutions, often embodied and socially scaffolded, but not exhausted by syntax, causation, or stipulation. A salient metaphysics should therefore treat intentionality as one of the ways reality is organized, not as an embarrassing leftover or a cost-free mystery.
