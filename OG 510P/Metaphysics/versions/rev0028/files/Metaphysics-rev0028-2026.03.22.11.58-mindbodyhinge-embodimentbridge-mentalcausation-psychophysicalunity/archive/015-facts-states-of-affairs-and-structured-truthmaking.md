# 015 — Facts, states of affairs, and structured truthmaking

## One-line thesis

A salient non-reductive metaphysics should treat reality as **structured**, not as a mere list of objects: propositions are best treated as truth-bearers, facts or obtaining states of affairs as worldly truthmakers, and the archive should distinguish these roles without inflating every truth into a special entity by default.

## Why this matters

The archive already relies on truthmaker pressure, essence, grounding, properties, relations, modality, and ontological commitment. But it still lacks one explicit place to say what kind of **worldly structure** is supposed to underwrite truths such as *the statue is fragile*, *the river changed course*, or *the institution now exists*. Without that, the archive risks oscillating between two bad pictures.

One bad picture is **object-list metaphysics**: reality is treated as just a census of items, with too little attention to the structured ways those items are. The other is **sentence-driven metaphysics**: every grammatically tidy truth is assumed to demand a matching ontological item of exactly the same shape. A better archive needs a disciplined middle path. It should allow that truths are often made true by structured reality — objects exemplifying properties, things standing in relations, organized wholes having powers — while remaining cautious about bloating ontology with a distinct fact for every sentence that can be written down.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Distinguish truth-bearers from truthmakers

The archive should usually treat **propositions** as the primary bearers of truth and falsity, while treating **facts**, **obtaining states of affairs**, or other suitably structured realities as candidate truthmakers. This distinction matters because what is true and what in reality makes it true need not be the same kind of thing.

### 2. Treat states of affairs and facts as technical devices for structured reality

The archive should use *state of affairs* and *fact* as technical metaphysical notions, not as loose ordinary-language equivalents. A helpful working contrast is:

- a **state of affairs** is a possible structured arrangement of objects, properties, and relations;
- a **fact** is an obtaining structured arrangement, or at least a prime candidate for that role.

This contrast need not be treated as final dogma, but it gives the archive a way to talk about structure without collapsing everything into sentencehood.

### 3. Preserve worldly structure beyond a mere inventory of constituents

A fact is not adequately captured by listing its ingredients separately. *Socrates*, *wisdom*, and *exemplification* do not yet by themselves give us the fact that Socrates is wise unless their mode of unity is also somehow accounted for. The archive should therefore treat structured unities as metaphysically serious wherever predication, relation, or organized dependence must be explained.

### 4. Do not collapse propositions into facts by default

Some theories identify true propositions with facts, or treat structured propositions and states of affairs as the same kind of item. Those options should remain visible in the portfolio. But the archive's current bias should keep them distinct enough to preserve the difference between **something's being true or false** and **something's obtaining or failing to obtain**.

### 5. Let obtaining do metaphysical work without becoming a magical extra predicate

If the archive uses states of affairs, it should treat **obtaining** as a serious marker of actuality rather than as a decorative synonym for existence. That does not mean multiplying mysterious layers of being. It means recognizing that a merely possible structured arrangement and an actual obtaining one are not on a par, even if they are related.

### 6. Keep truthmaking plural but structured

Not every truth needs a dedicated Armstrong-style fact object. Some truths may be made true by substances instantiating properties, by events occurring, by structures holding, by powers being present, or by total configurations rather than by neat atomic units. The archive should therefore treat **truthmaker theory as a discipline of answerability to reality**, not as a rigid recipe that assigns one ontological token to every sentence.

### 7. Be especially cautious with negative, disjunctive, and higher-order facts

The pressure for facts is strongest in ordinary predication and relation cases. It becomes more controversial with negative truths, disjunctions, universal claims, modal truths, and truths about absences. The archive should keep those cases live without letting them force immediate truthmaker inflation. When needed, it should ask whether the right backing is totality structure, grounding, essence, law, or some broader worldly configuration.

### 8. Use structured truthmaking to connect ontology with explanation

A good reason to keep facts or states of affairs available is that they help explain why metaphysics cannot stop at object-counting. Laws, causes, institutions, biological functions, and historical identities are not just heaps of things; they are structured ways things are. The archive should therefore treat structured truthmaking as one bridge between ontology, explanation, and realism.

## Guardrails

- Do not assume every truth requires its own bespoke fact-object.
- Do not reduce reality to a flat list of objects with no structured ways of being.
- Do not collapse propositions, facts, and states of affairs into one notion by verbal fiat.
- Do not let truthmaker discipline replace grounding, essence, law, or causation where those are the better explanatory tools.
- Do not let worries about ontological inflation push the archive into sentence-first anti-realism.

## Failure modes

- **object-list metaphysics**: the world is treated as only an inventory of items, with no serious account of predicative or relational structure.
- **sentence mirroring**: every grammatical truth is assumed to demand a matching entity of the same shape.
- **truthmaker inflation**: controversial fact-types are posited everywhere just to satisfy a slogan.
- **role blur**: propositions, facts, and states of affairs are run together without saying what each is for.
- **structure deflation**: worldly unity is ignored and explanatory burdens are pushed back onto language alone.

## Practical tests

1. Can the theory distinguish truth-bearers from truthmakers without mystery or collapse?
2. Can it say whether facts are distinct from obtaining states of affairs, and why the distinction matters or does not?
3. Can it explain predication and relation without reducing structure to a bare list of constituents?
4. Can it use truthmaker pressure selectively, especially in hard cases like negative or modal truths?
5. Can it preserve structured reality without turning every successful sentence into a new ontological item?

## Closing synthesis

The archive should treat **facts, states of affairs, and truthmakers as part of the metaphysics of structure rather than as optional semantic decoration**. Its leading posture should remain disciplined and non-inflationary: propositions are the main bearers of truth and falsity; reality makes them true through structured being; and facts or obtaining states of affairs are useful metaphysical tools wherever the archive needs to say not just *what exists*, but *how things are*.
