# 013 — Dependence maps, building relations, and explanatory discipline

## One-line thesis

A salient non-reductive metaphysics should not speak of “dependence” as if it were one flat relation: grounding, ontological dependence, supervenience, realization, constitution, composition, and causation do different explanatory jobs, and the archive needs an explicit map of those jobs.

## Why this matters

The archive already relies on dependence language almost everywhere. Grounding appears in the core explanatory triad, emergence is described through dependence-plus-autonomy, constitution and identity are kept apart, and social reality is treated as grounded rather than fictional. But without a dedicated dependence map, those relations can blur together and the theory starts sounding more unified than it really is.

That blur is costly. Supervenience can look like explanation when it is only covariation under necessity. Realization can be mistaken for grounding. Constitution can be flattened into identity. Causation can be recruited to do the work of metaphysical building, or grounding can be stretched until it swallows every non-causal connection. A better archive needs a dependence vocabulary that is plural, comparative, and explicit about which relation is doing what.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Treat metaphysical dependence as a family, not a single magic word

The archive should assume that there are **multiple dependence-like relations** rather than one master relation hiding behind every “because.” Some concern existence, some identity, some determination, some implementation, some explanatory backing, and some productive change. A serious metaphysics needs to know which family member is in play.

### 2. Keep grounding distinct from causation

Grounding concerns constitutive or in-virtue-of structure. Causation concerns production, process, or bringing-about. The archive should continue to mark this distinction sharply. A labor strike may be grounded in certain coordinated social facts without those facts causing the strike in the ordinary temporal sense; a statue’s fragility may be caused by earlier kiln events while being grounded in its present structure.

### 3. Treat supervenience as a modal constraint, not a full explanation

Supervenience is useful because it says that no difference of one kind can occur without some difference in another base. But by itself it is often too coarse-grained to tell us **why** the higher fact obtains, what kind of dependence is involved, or whether the relation is constitutive, realizational, compositional, or merely covariance under necessity. The archive should therefore use supervenience as a check or ceiling, not as its default explanatory endpoint.

### 4. Use realization for implementation cases, especially across levels

Realization is often the right idiom when a higher-level property, capacity, or state is implemented by lower-level structures that can vary across cases. This makes realization especially important for philosophy of mind, biology, technology, and other multiple-realizability cases. But realization is not automatically identity, and it is not obviously identical with grounding either. The archive should use it when the pressure concerns **how a role or capacity is implemented**, not simply what grounds what.

### 5. Distinguish constitution and composition from more generic dependence talk

Composition asks when parts make a whole. Constitution asks how one thing can constitute another without strict identity. Those relations matter because they track formed wholes, material coincidence, and bearerhood in ways that neither grounding nor supervenience captures by themselves. The archive should therefore keep constitution and composition as first-class dependence problems rather than treating them as miscellaneous cases.

### 6. Use ontological dependence as the broader map when exact structure is unsettled

Sometimes the archive can say confidently that x depends on y without yet deciding whether the best fine-grained account is grounding, essential dependence, identity-dependence, constitution, or realization. In those cases, **ontological dependence** is the broader but still serious idiom. It should not replace more specific notions where the latter are available, but it is useful when the exact dependence profile remains contested.

### 7. Let explanatory questions choose the relation

A practical decision rule for the archive is:

- ask whether the case is about production or process; if so, causation is likely involved;
- ask whether the case is about constitutive “in virtue of” structure; if so, grounding is a leading candidate;
- ask whether the claim is only that no higher difference occurs without a base difference; if so, supervenience may be all that has been shown;
- ask whether the case concerns implementation of a role by varying lower-level structures; if so, realization may be central;
- ask whether the problem is part-whole or material coincidence; if so, composition or constitution may be the right idiom.

This rule is not algorithmic perfection. It is an anti-blur discipline.

## Guardrails

- Do not use “dependence” as if it always meant grounding.
- Do not treat supervenience as a full metaphysical explanation by default.
- Do not collapse realization into identity or into causation.
- Do not let constitution and composition disappear into generic building talk.
- Do not use one favored dependence relation to annex every domain.

## Failure modes

- **dependence blur**: distinct relations are treated as interchangeable.
- **supervenience inflation**: modal covariance is mistaken for constitutive explanation.
- **grounding imperialism**: every non-causal connection is redescribed as grounding without argument.
- **causal overreach**: production language is used where the issue is actually ontological structure.
- **building confusion**: composition, constitution, and realization are lumped together without saying what each contributes.

## Practical tests

1. Can the theory say what relation is at issue in a concrete case rather than just saying “depends”?
2. Can it explain why supervenience is evidentially useful but often explanatorily incomplete?
3. Can it distinguish implementation, constitution, and grounding in higher-level cases?
4. Can it preserve the difference between production over time and constitutive structure?
5. Can it admit broader ontological dependence when the finer relation is genuinely unsettled?

## Closing synthesis

The archive should treat **dependence as a structured family of metaphysical relations rather than a single undifferentiated glue**. Grounding, supervenience, realization, constitution, composition, ontological dependence, and causation should be kept in productive tension: close enough to illuminate one another, different enough to prevent explanatory blur. That discipline strengthens articulated layered realism because it gives the archive a clearer map of how reality can be layered without pretending every layer is connected in exactly the same way.
