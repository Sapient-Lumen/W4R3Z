# 002 — Essence, grounding, and typed fundamentality

## One-line thesis

The archive should treat essence, grounding, and fundamentality as the core explanatory triad of metaphysics, while refusing the assumption that there must be one single untyped deepest inventory from which every other aspect of reality is read off without residue.

## Why this matters

Contemporary metaphysics has increasingly shifted from asking only what exists to asking what grounds what, what is fundamental, and what things are in virtue of themselves. That shift is one of the field’s strongest advances because it replaces mere census-taking with explanatory structure.

But the shift can still go wrong. It goes wrong when grounding is confused with causation, when essence is reduced to mere necessity, or when fundamentality is assumed to require one final privileged category that explains everything else in exactly the same way.

The archive needs a more disciplined view.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Let essence do real work

The archive should treat essence as answering the question of what a thing is, not merely what it happens to be like in all possible worlds. Modal profiles matter, but the order of explanation often runs from what a thing is to what it must or can be, not the other way around.

### 2. Treat grounding as constitutive dependence, not efficient causation

Grounding should be used for “in virtue of” structure: parts and wholes, sets and members, legal facts and institutional acts, macro-patterns and their sustaining bases, truths and their truthmakers where applicable. This is not the same as one event causing another event.

### 3. Keep fundamentality distinct from mere smallness

What is fundamental is not simply what is microscopic. Fundamentality concerns ontological independence, explanatory priority, or ungroundedness. A tiny entity may be derivative; a large-scale totality might be basic on some views; and some debates are really about which explanatory task a notion of fundamentality is meant to perform.

### 4. Work with typed fundamentality

The archive’s default posture should be that fundamentality may be **typed** rather than absolutely flat. Different explanatory families may bottom out in different ways:

- material constitution may have one base profile;
- modal structure may be constrained by essences;
- normative or institutional entities may have constitutive bases in practices, statuses, or recognitional structures;
- mathematical or logical structure may not fit the same dependence grammar as concrete objects.

This is a bold synthesis, but it is preferable to pretending that one vocabulary automatically governs every domain.

### 5. Preserve the possibility of global structure without insisting on a single layer-cake

The world may still be globally ordered. Typed fundamentality is not metaphysical chaos. It says that explanatory priority may be stratified and domain-sensitive, while still preserving cross-domain constraints and bridges.

### 6. Use truthmaker discipline without overreach

Truthmaker talk is helpful because it keeps the archive answerable to being. But not every truth needs an overengineered ontology attached to it. Truthmaker discipline should be used to prevent free-floating abstraction, not to force every sentence into the same mold.

## Guardrails

- Do not reduce essence to a simple necessity test.
- Do not use grounding where causation is what is really at issue.
- Do not assume the fundamental must be particle-like.
- Do not infer unreality from groundedness.
- Do not claim a single final basis unless the archive can explain how it handles modality, normativity, and social ontology without evasion.

## Failure modes

- **modal flattening**: essence is treated as nothing but necessity.
- **ground-cause blur**: constitutive explanation is redescribed as ordinary causation.
- **micro-idolatry**: only the smallest units are allowed to count as basic.
- **single-base dogma**: one favored domain is made to explain all others by stipulation.
- **truthmaker inflation**: the archive invents unnecessary furniture for every proposition.

## Practical tests

1. Can the theory explain why essence and modality are connected without identifying them?
2. Can it mark the difference between grounding and causation in actual cases?
3. Can it say what counts as fundamental without reducing that notion to scale alone?
4. Can it allow derivative entities to be real and explanatory?
5. Can it handle social, normative, and modal facts without pretending they are just disguised microphysics?

## Closing synthesis

The archive should therefore treat **essence, grounding, and fundamentality as central metaphysical tools**, while adopting a live but disciplined hypothesis of **typed fundamentality**: reality may have real depth without being forced into one univocal lowest-level inventory.
