# 022 — Agency, action, freedom, and reasons-responsive control

## One-line thesis

A non-reductive metaphysics should treat agency as a real mode of organized power: actions are not mere bodily happenings, free action is not bare randomness, and agential control is best understood through authorship, reasons-responsiveness, and vulnerable self-governance rather than through either passive mechanism or ungrounded will-magic.

## Why this matters

The archive now has explicit positions on causation, teleology, occurrents, consciousness, and social reality. But it still lacks a dedicated account of **agents**: beings who do things, answer to reasons, initiate or guide conduct, and can sometimes be held answerable for what they do. Without that surface, the archive risks describing subjects as if they merely undergo events.

The main danger is again a forced choice between bad extremes. One extreme treats agency as nothing over and above bodily movement plus causal antecedents, leaving authorship, guidance, and control underdescribed. The opposite extreme treats any resistance to reduction as warrant for a mysterious uncaused inner will. The archive needs a middle path: **disciplined realism about agential control**.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Distinguish mere happenings, behavior, and action

Not every event involving a body is an action. Reflexes, compulsions, seizures, and externally forced motions are not well understood in the same way as intentional doings. A salient metaphysics should distinguish:

- **mere happenings** that occur in or to a being;
- **behavior** that manifests organization or response without full authorship;
- **actions** that are attributable to an agent as things the agent does.

This distinction matters because occurrent realism alone does not yet tell us which occurrents belong to agency.

### 2. Treat reasons-explanations as real without making them mere post-hoc gloss

Agents often act **for reasons**. Reasons-explanations are not mere after-the-fact stories laid over brute motions. But neither should the archive pretend that citing a reason automatically dissolves all causal questions. The better posture is dual discipline: actions are intelligible under rational descriptions while also being situated in causal, embodied, and historical processes.

### 3. Distinguish sourcehood from mere alternative-possibility talk

Freedom is not captured simply by asking whether the agent could have done otherwise in some thin modal sense. What matters at least as much is **sourcehood** or authorship: whether the action issues from the agent in the right way. Alternative possibilities still matter in many cases, especially where coercion, confinement, or inability narrow the field. But the archive should resist making “could have done otherwise” the only metaphysical key.

### 4. Treat self-governance as real, graded, and vulnerable

Agency is not an all-or-nothing perfection. Self-government admits degrees and can be undermined by compulsion, manipulation, addiction, trauma, panic, hypnosis, and structural domination. A non-reductive metaphysics should therefore treat agential control as **vulnerable organization** rather than as an invulnerable metaphysical switch that is either fully on or fully off.

### 5. Keep agent-causation as pressure, not as default victory

Some theories insist that genuine action requires a distinctive irreducible form of agent-causation. This pressure should remain live in the portfolio, because event-causal accounts can leave initiation and authorship feeling thin. But agent-causation should not become the archive’s default verdict merely because event-causal models face pressure. The burden remains to explain what this irreducible power is, how it integrates with the rest of ontology, and why it clarifies rather than mystifies action.

### 6. Connect agency to embodiment, temporality, and practical identity

Actual agents are not detached punctal wills. They are historically formed, embodied, temporally extended beings whose capacities are scaffolded by habits, attention, memory, and social recognition. Agency therefore belongs naturally with the archive’s existing themes of persistence, teleology, consciousness, and social ontology.

### 7. Connect agency to responsibility without collapsing them

Agency and responsibility are related but not identical. One can be an agent without meeting the stronger conditions for full moral responsibility. The archive should therefore distinguish:

- **agency** as the capacity to act;
- **free or self-governing agency** as a stronger form of control or authorship;
- **responsibility** as a further normative standing tied to appropriate forms of answerability.

That distinction keeps metaphysics from smuggling in ethics too quickly while still acknowledging why action theory matters beyond mere motion.

## Guardrails

- Do not reduce action to bare bodily movement by stipulation.
- Do not infer mysterious contra-causal will-powers from every failure of reduction.
- Do not treat reasons as epiphenomenal commentary on already-complete behavior.
- Do not make alternative possibilities the only test of freedom.
- Do not ignore manipulation, compulsion, addiction, or coercion as if all intentional behavior were equally self-governed.
- Do not detach agency from embodiment, history, or social scaffolding.

## Failure modes

- **agency erasure**: actions are redescribed as nothing but event-sequences or behavior outputs.
- **will-magic inflation**: agency is protected by positing an opaque inner chooser with no ontological integration.
- **reason/causation split**: reasons are treated as intelligibility only, while causal production is treated as happening elsewhere.
- **modal fixation**: freedom is reduced entirely to a crude “could have done otherwise” test.
- **responsibility collapse**: agency, autonomy, freedom, and moral liability are all run together.
- **history blindness**: manipulation, trauma, habituation, and social conditions are ignored when assessing authorship.

## Practical tests

1. Can the theory distinguish mere happenings, organized behavior, and genuine action?
2. Can it explain how actions are both causally situated and rationally intelligible?
3. Does it say something informative about sourcehood or authorship, not just alternative possibilities?
4. Can it explain why manipulation, compulsion, and coercion threaten freedom without implying that all causation does?
5. Does it connect agency to embodiment, temporality, and practical identity without dissolving the agent into those backgrounds?

## Closing synthesis

The archive should adopt a **disciplined realism about agency**: agents are real organized centers of action whose doings are intelligible through reasons, embodied powers, and self-governing control, and whose freedom should be treated neither as a magical exception to the world nor as a dispensable shadow of impersonal mechanism.
