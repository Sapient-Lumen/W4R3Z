# 004 — Powers, laws, and causation

## One-line thesis

A non-reductive metaphysics should treat causation, laws, and powers as a linked explanatory cluster: laws should not be mere brute regularities if stronger grounding is available, and causes should be understood in a way answerable both to worldly production and to the structured capacities of things.

## Why this matters

Metaphysics becomes sterile when it treats laws as accidental summaries and causation as merely a projection of human inference. But it also becomes dogmatic when it assumes one tidy model of causation fits every domain. The archive needs a middle path that preserves objective structure without pretending every causal problem is solved.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Treat laws as demanding more than bare pattern description

A law of nature is not merely any regularity. The archive should retain the pressure behind the thought that laws stand in need of stronger ground than accidental coincidence. That pressure can be answered in different ways: by universals, powers, counterfactual structure, or best-system style accounts. But the question should not be dissolved.

### 2. Let powers carry explanatory weight

Dispositions, capacities, and powers are useful because they connect what a thing is with what it can do. Powers-oriented metaphysics helps explain why causes are not just constant conjunctions and why laws may reflect the natures or capacities of things rather than merely summarize them.

### 3. Distinguish causation from grounding while linking them

Grounding and causation are different forms of dependence. Grounding is constitutive; causation is productive or generative in time, or at least tied to the structure of change. But they interact. Some causal powers may themselves be grounded in a thing’s essence or constitution.

### 4. Keep causal pluralism available

The archive should permit the possibility that causation is not a single perfectly uniform relation. Event causation, agent causation, mechanistic causation, probabilistic causation, and higher-level causal explanation may all be legitimate if disciplined by clear success conditions.

### 5. Preserve downward and cross-level causal questions without panic

If emergence is real, then higher-level properties may have genuine explanatory and possibly causal relevance. The archive should not assume in advance that all macro-level causation is either reducible residue or impossible overcounting. Those cases need argument, not veto.

## Guardrails

- Do not reduce laws to mere habit of description without argument.
- Do not reduce causation to regularity alone if productive structure is doing explanatory work.
- Do not collapse powers into occult labels with no inferential payoff.
- Do not conflate grounding, lawhood, and causation.
- Do not assume macro-level causation is automatically excluded.

## Failure modes

- **regularity deflation**: laws become just summaries of what happens to happen.
- **counterfactual isolation**: causal structure is analyzed without enough attention to powers or production.
- **power mystification**: powers are invoked instead of explained.
- **dependence blur**: law, cause, and constitution are collapsed into one relation.
- **causal exclusion panic**: higher-level explanation is dismissed too quickly.

## Practical tests

1. Can the theory distinguish a law from an accidental regularity?
2. Can it explain why some causes are productive rather than merely predictive?
3. Can it connect a thing’s powers to its nature without magical obscurity?
4. Can it keep grounding and causation apart in concrete examples?
5. Can it make room for higher-level causal relevance where explanatory practice strongly supports it?

## Closing synthesis

The archive should prefer a **powers-friendly, law-serious, causally plural realism**: causes are real, laws require stronger grounding than mere coincidence, and the capacities of things matter to what can happen.
