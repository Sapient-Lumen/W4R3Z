# 031 — Intrinsicness, duplication, and local being

## One-line thesis

A non-reductive metaphysics should treat **intrinsicness** as a real and useful distinction about what a thing is like in itself, while refusing two common overreaches: that the intrinsic is just the non-relational, and that all reality must be recoverable from a perfectly local intrinsic mosaic.

## Why this matters

The archive already has strong notes on properties, identity, dependence, space, worldhood, and chance. But it still lacks one dedicated place to say what belongs to a thing **in itself**, what belongs to it only through wider worldly placement, and why that distinction matters without becoming a total metaphysical constitution. Without such a note, the datacube risks sliding among three bad options: treating intrinsicness as too murky to matter; treating every relation as therefore extrinsic and ontologically secondary; or treating local intrinsic structure as the whole of reality.

That omission matters across several domains at once. Property theory needs a distinction between a thing's own character and its outward placement. Identity and duplication talk need some disciplined way of saying when two things are alike "from the inside" even when their surroundings differ. Internal and external relations cannot be handled well without a view about what the relata contribute on their own. Worldhood, holism, and nonseparability pressures cannot be assessed unless the archive knows what would count as a genuinely local intrinsic basis in the first place. And the archive's anti-reductive posture becomes sharper if it can say that some important structure is real even when it is not merely local intrinsic furniture.

The archive should therefore take a disciplined middle path: **intrinsicness without mosaic absolutism**. Some properties and some aspects of beings are genuinely intrinsic. Duplication and naturalness remain useful tools for articulating that claim. But not every meaningful distinction collapses into intrinsic/extrinsic, some relations are internal or externally grounded in ways that still matter metaphysically, and some worldly structure may fail to supervene on a point-by-point inventory of local intrinsic features.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Distinguish intrinsicness from non-relationality

The intrinsic is not simply the non-relational. Some relationally characterizable features may still belong to a thing in virtue of how that thing itself is organized. The archive should therefore reject the flat equation of "relational" with "extrinsic". A property can involve internal comparison, formal proportion, or constitutive organization without thereby depending on wholly distinct external companions.

### 2. Distinguish intrinsicness from mere locality

A property being intrinsic and a property being point-local or region-local are not the same idea. Some local magnitudes may already presuppose structure extending beyond a bare point, and some intrinsic features of wholes may be structurally distributed rather than atomically pinned to tiny locations. The archive should therefore keep apart talk of intrinsicness, talk of locality, and talk of separability.

### 3. Treat duplication as a test, not a tyrant

Duplication remains useful because duplicates should agree in intrinsic respects. But duplication should function as a diagnostic rather than as a metaphysical sovereign. Any duplication-based account inherits background commitments about natural properties, qualitative similarity, and what counts as the relevant grain of comparison. The archive should therefore use duplicates to sharpen intrinsicness talk without assuming that all metaphysical structure is exhausted by duplicate-preservation.

### 4. Keep internal and external relations explicit

Some relations hold in virtue of the intrinsic natures of their relata; others depend on broader worldly placement; still others may supervene on the composite whole rather than on each relata taken separately. The archive should therefore not demote all relations at once. A good metaphysics needs room for internal relations, external relations, and genuinely structure-making relations that are not reducible to two independent monadic profiles plus a verbal tie.

### 5. Resist mosaic overreach

The idea that all reality is fixed by the spatiotemporal distribution of local natural properties is powerful, elegant, and often illuminating. But the archive should not treat that picture as the default shape of all being. Composite organization, holistic physical structure, nonseparability, and world-level dependence may all pressure a purely mosaic conception. Anti-reductionism here means not that local intrinsic structure is unreal, but that it may be insufficient as the whole explanatory base.

### 6. Let naturalness guide without monopolizing

Natural properties matter because they help articulate duplication, similarity, lawhood, and explanatory joint-carving. But a non-reductive archive should resist letting naturalness alone settle every ontological dispute. Some derivative or extrinsic features may still be real and explanatorily indispensable even if they are not perfectly natural. Naturalness should discipline ontology, not flatten it.

### 7. Preserve extrinsic and holistic reality without embarrassment

To say that some feature is extrinsic is not to say that it is unreal, illusory, or philosophically second-rate. Location, kinship, institutional status, ecological role, and many world-level or system-level features may be extrinsic while still real. Likewise, if some processes or structures are nonseparable, that does not automatically make them spooky. The archive should therefore keep a layered ontology in which intrinsic, extrinsic, local, and holistic features can all be real while doing different explanatory work.

## Guardrails

- Do not equate intrinsic with merely non-relational.
- Do not equate intrinsic with point-local.
- Do not treat duplication tests as theory-neutral or self-interpreting.
- Do not assume that every real relation supervenes on the monadic intrinsic natures of its relata taken separately.
- Do not infer from extrinsicality that a feature is unreal or dispensable.
- Do not infer from holistic or nonseparable structure that local intrinsic character does no work.

## Failure modes

- **relational flattening**: every relation is treated as equally extrinsic and metaphysically secondary.
- **mosaic absolutism**: all reality is assumed to supervene on local intrinsic point-facts by default.
- **duplicate fetishism**: duplication talk is treated as settling every issue despite relying on background theories of naturalness and structure.
- **locality confusion**: intrinsicness, locality, and separability are blurred into one idea.
- **extrinsic shame**: derivative, positional, and environment-involving features are treated as ontological embarrassments rather than real aspects of being.
- **holism panic**: any failure of strict separability is treated as mystical rather than as a cue to refine the ontology of properties, regions, and wholes.

## Practical tests

1. Can the theory distinguish intrinsicness, locality, duplication-preservation, and separability rather than using them interchangeably?
2. Can it explain why some relationally characterizable features may still be intrinsic or internally grounded?
3. Can it use duplication without silently presupposing that all reality is a local intrinsic mosaic?
4. Can it allow external, positional, or holistic features to be real without turning them into second-rate pseudo-being?
5. Can it face pressures from physical holism or nonseparability without either abandoning intrinsicness or treating holistic structure as magical?

## Closing synthesis

The archive should adopt a posture of **intrinsicness without mosaic absolutism**: beings have real intrinsic character, duplication and naturalness help us say what that means, but reality is not exhausted by a pointillist stocktake of local intrinsic facts. Some relations, placements, wholes, and nonseparable structures are also real, and a non-reductive metaphysics should say so openly.
