# 016 — Abstract objects, mathematical structure, and non-spatiotemporal being

## One-line thesis

A salient non-reductive metaphysics should treat **abstracta and non-spatiotemporal being** with disciplined seriousness: neither eliminative panic nor cost-free platonist inflation is adequate; the better stance is selective realism in which propositions, mathematical structures, and some other abstract candidates earn their place by explanatory role, structural discipline, and ontological fit.

## Why this matters

The archive now speaks confidently about propositions, modality, truthmakers, properties, identity, and ontological commitment. But it still lacks one explicit place to say what to do about **abstract objects** and other non-spatiotemporal candidates. Numbers, sets, propositions, possible worlds, and certain structures keep appearing at the edges of the archive's claims. Without a dedicated note, the archive risks two opposite mistakes.

One mistake is **eliminative panic**: anything non-causal, non-spatial, or non-temporal is treated as suspect by default, as though reality had to be exhausted by what pushes and collides. The opposite mistake is **platonic over-admission**: every useful formal or semantic device is immediately reified into a realm of fully determinate abstract objects. A tighter archive needs a middle path. It should allow that some abstract or structurally characterized entities may be real, while insisting that abstract ontology answer to explanatory need, fit with the archive's broader dependence picture, and avoid gratuitous duplication.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Distinguish abstractness from unreality

The archive should not infer unreality from non-spatiotemporality or causal inertness alone. If propositions are needed as sharable truth-bearers, if mathematics is not well captured as mere inscription-shuffling, or if modal structure requires more than concrete inventory, then some abstracta may be part of the best ontology.

### 2. Distinguish abstractness from fundamentality

Even if some abstract entities exist, that does not by itself make them fundamental. The archive should keep open that some abstracta are derivative, grounded, type-level, representationally realized, or ontologically dependent on more basic structures. Abstract being and fundamental being should not be collapsed.

### 3. Prefer structural discipline over object inflation

A recurring lesson from philosophy of mathematics is that what matters is often **structure**, not a brute inventory of abstract individuals. The archive should therefore be friendlier to structuralist and role-based treatments than to the idea that every successful abstract discourse reveals a separate census of autonomous objects. When possible, it should ask whether numbers, sets, or other candidates are best understood as positions in structures, type-level patterns, or indispensable forms of organization.

### 4. Keep propositions and mathematical entities related but distinct

The archive should resist treating all abstracta as one undifferentiated realm. Propositions, numbers, sets, properties, relations, and possible worlds may pose different pressures and may deserve different treatments. A disciplined metaphysics can be realist about propositions while remaining structuralist or deflationary about some mathematical objects, or realist about mathematical structure while refusing a bloated ontology of ersatz worlds.

### 5. Use explanatory burden, not mere quantification, as the admission test

A good abstract ontology is not justified just because a favored formalism quantifies over something. The archive should ask what explanatory burden the candidate entity carries. Does it stabilize truth-bearing content, support mathematical practice, underwrite modal representation, or organize real dependence patterns? If not, quantifier appearance alone should not decide the case.

### 6. Keep nominalist and fictionalist pressure alive

Nominalism, fictionalism, and related austerity programs remain valuable rivals. They test whether abstracta can be paraphrased away, reconstructed modally, reduced to inscriptional practice, or replaced with immanent structures. The archive should keep this pressure visible because it prevents lazy reification.

### 7. But do not let anti-platonist pressure flatten the archive

The fact that abstracta raise epistemic or causal questions does not show that they are dispensable. Some domains may simply be irreducible without being fundamental in the same way as particles, fields, organisms, or institutions. The archive should therefore allow **selective abstract realism** wherever the explanatory gains are real and the ontology remains disciplined.

### 8. Treat mathematics as a major test case, not the only one

Mathematics is the clearest pressure point for abstract ontology, but it is not the only one. Propositions, properties, relations, kinds, and perhaps certain normative or formal structures may also force the archive to say what sort of being is possible beyond the concrete. A good note on abstracta should therefore connect mathematics to the archive's broader questions about structure, truth, and dependence.

## Guardrails

- Do not equate non-spatiotemporality with unreality.
- Do not treat every useful formal device as proof of a separate abstract domain.
- Do not assume that all abstracta, if real, have the same metaphysical status.
- Do not collapse abstract existence into fundamentality.
- Do not let quantifier tests do all the ontological work.

## Failure modes

- **eliminative panic**: abstract candidates are dismissed just because they are non-causal or non-spatial.
- **platonic inflation**: every successful theory is taken to reveal a new autonomous realm of abstract objects.
- **realm blur**: propositions, numbers, properties, and worlds are all treated as one amorphous category.
- **structure blindness**: structural roles are ignored in favor of brute object-census metaphysics.
- **meta-ontological shortcutting**: ontological commitment is read off a formalism without asking what explanatory work the posits actually do.

## Practical tests

1. Can the theory distinguish abstractness from both unreality and fundamentality?
2. Can it explain why some abstract candidates deserve realism while others may be better treated structurally, fictionally, or derivatively?
3. Can it say what mathematics is about without forcing one flat answer for every mathematical object-talk?
4. Can it accommodate propositions or other formal structures without turning every semantic convenience into ontology?
5. Can it keep nominalist pressure alive without collapsing into concrete-only metaphysics?

## Closing synthesis

The archive should adopt a **selective, structurally disciplined realism about abstracta**. It should allow that some non-spatiotemporal entities or structures are real, especially where truth, mathematics, or modal organization demand them; but it should also resist cost-free platonic expansion. The right posture is neither eliminativist suspicion nor abstract-object exuberance, but a calibrated ontology in which abstract candidates must earn their place by explanatory role, structural fit, and metaphysical discipline.
