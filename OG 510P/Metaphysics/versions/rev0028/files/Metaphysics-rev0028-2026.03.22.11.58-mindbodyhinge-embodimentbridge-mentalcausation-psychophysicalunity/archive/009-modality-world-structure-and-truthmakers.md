# 009 — Modality, world-structure, and truthmakers

## One-line thesis

A salient non-skeptical metaphysics should treat modal structure as real but not automatically as a separate realm of objects: possibility and necessity are features of reality’s articulation, possible-worlds talk is often a modeling device rather than an ontological verdict, and truthmaker discipline helps keep modal claims answerable to being.

## Why this matters

The archive already relies on modality whenever it speaks of essence, persistence, laws, powers, and grounding. But without a dedicated modal note, the archive risks using modal vocabulary while leaving unclear what sort of metaphysical work it is meant to do.

That gap matters because modality is one of the main places where metaphysics either overreaches or collapses. It overreaches when every useful modal model is reified into a vast ontology by default. It collapses when necessity and possibility are treated as nothing more than linguistic convenience, conceptual projection, or disguised regularity.

A non-skeptical and non-reductive archive needs a middle path: modality is real, but our best semantics for modal discourse need not dictate a single ontology of worlds.

## Pattern pack

### 1. Treat modality as load-bearing, not ornamental

A metaphysics without modality cannot say what a thing must be to count as the kind of thing it is, what changes it can survive, which dependencies are necessary rather than accidental, or what laws and powers amount to. Modal structure is therefore not optional decoration.

### 2. Let possible-worlds discourse remain a tool unless forced upward

Possible-worlds semantics is extraordinarily useful for organizing modal thought. But usefulness does not by itself settle whether worlds are concrete entities, abstract representations, maximal states of affairs, or something else. The archive should therefore treat possible worlds first as an explanatory and representational framework, not as a mandatory ontological census.

### 3. Keep essence prior to at least some modal facts

For many central cases, what a thing is helps explain what it can and must be. This keeps the archive from treating modality as a free-floating formal layer detached from the natures of things. Essence and modality are intertwined, but the order of explanation often runs from nature to modal profile.

### 4. Use truthmakers and truthmaker pressures selectively

Modal claims should remain answerable to being. Truthmaker discipline is one way to prevent modal discourse from floating free of ontology. But the archive should not demand a crude one-size-fits-all truthmaker for every modal sentence. Sometimes the relevant ontological anchor is an essence, law, structure, power, or total configuration rather than a neat standalone item.

### 5. Distinguish representational space from metaphysical depth

A space of possibilities can be used to represent dependence, counterfactuals, and laws without implying that every coordinate in that space has the same metaphysical status. The archive should resist conflating semantic convenience with ontological commitment.

### 6. Keep modal realism, abstractionism, fictionalism, and essentialism as live rivals

The archive should preserve several live options:

- robust modal realism, where worlds are fully real;
- abstract or ersatz views, where worlds are representational constructions of some kind;
- fictionalist or deflationary views, where world-talk is instrumentally useful;
- essentialist views, where at least some modality is grounded in the natures of things.

Its present bias should favor an essentialist and world-structure approach over a default inflationary ontology of worlds.

## Guardrails

- Do not treat every useful semantic model as a metaphysical inventory.
- Do not reduce modality to grammar, convention, or ignorance by default.
- Do not sever modal claims from essences, laws, powers, or structures that could anchor them.
- Do not demand a simplistic truthmaker for every modal truth.
- Do not let modal language float free of the rest of the archive.

## Failure modes

- **world-reification reflex**: every possible-worlds framework is turned into literal ontology too quickly.
- **modal deflation**: necessity and possibility are reduced to language or psychology.
- **free-floating semantics**: formal semantics replaces metaphysical explanation.
- **truthmaker maximalism without discipline**: modal ontology is bloated just to satisfy a slogan.
- **essentialist overreach**: every modal fact is forced to arise from local essence alone.

## Practical tests

1. Can the theory explain why modal discourse is indispensable to metaphysics?
2. Can it use possible-worlds modeling without automatically inflating its ontology?
3. Can it say which modal facts are explained by essences, which by laws, and which by broader world-structure?
4. Can it keep modal claims answerable to being without requiring clumsy truthmakers everywhere?
5. Can it compare modal realism, abstractionism, fictionalism, and essentialism without caricature?

## Closing synthesis

The archive should treat **modality as real and metaphysically indispensable**, while holding that possible-worlds discourse is often a **representational framework before it is an ontological verdict**; modal explanation should be anchored by essences, laws, powers, structures, and disciplined truthmaker pressure rather than by automatic world inflation.
