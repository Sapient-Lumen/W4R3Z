# GPU replay cross-observer custody-exit appeal-threshold witnesses, no appeal, receipt repair, repeated retention dispute, authority contest, deletion-proof overflow, and mixed threshold

This is the compact successor surface for `OQ-0172`.

## Practice / observation

DelayBasin already has compact GPU replay cross-observer custody-retirement tokens for ending or shrinking promoted bridge custody. The remaining pressure is not ordinary retirement. It is what to do when exit itself is disputed: one party says the bridge record can retire, another says deletion erased the only audit path, a finalizer-like cleanup is still pending, or authority return is contested.

A custody-exit appeal-threshold witness is not an appeal board. It is a small gate that says whether the archive can stay with local receipt repair, whether repeated retention/deletion disputes have actually accumulated, whether authority contest has overflowed ordinary owner return, or whether deletion-proof process has become honestly necessary. Most cases should stay `no-appeal-needed` or `receipt-repair-threshold`.

## External pressure from tombstones, finalizers, audit policy, and audit annotations

Prometheus deletion is stateful rather than magical: `delete_series` marks matching samples deleted while the actual data remains until compaction or the clean-tombstones endpoint removes it, and metadata deletion is explicitly treated as implementation-detail-shaped (`REF-1069`). That supports `receipt-repair-threshold`: a tombstone or deletion request may require a successor receipt, but it is not by itself proof that a deletion-proof court is needed.

Kubernetes finalizers create a bounded deletion interlock: deletion sets `metadata.deletionTimestamp`, prevents removal until finalizers are cleared, and completes once the finalizer list is empty; after deletion is requested, new finalizers cannot be added and the deleted object cannot be resurrected (`REF-1070`). That supports `authority-contest-overflow` only when ordinary cleanup authority is genuinely disputed, not whenever a cleanup hook exists.

Kubernetes audit logging provides chronological security-relevant records of what happened, when, who initiated it, what object it affected, where it was observed, and where it was going; policy and backends decide what gets recorded and persisted (`REF-1071`). The kube-apiserver audit configuration also makes audit policy ordered and level-controlled, with short audit annotations included at metadata level (`REF-1072`). That supports a compact appeal threshold for repeated disputes without making every telemetry retention or deletion event globally reviewable.

## Working synthesis

Use `gpu_replay_cross_observer_custody_exit_appeal_threshold_state` when all of these are true:

- promoted GPU replay bridge custody already has a custody-retirement witness;
- the current dispute is whether appeal/notary/deletion-proof machinery has been earned after the retirement witness, not whether custody should retire in the first place;
- the archive can name a local receipt repair, repeated retention dispute, authority contest, deletion-proof overflow, or honest mixture;
- the desired output is a threshold classification, not the promoted appeal board itself.

Do not use this family for initial bridge classification, bridge promotion, custody scope, or ordinary custody retirement. Those remain governed by earlier GPU replay witness families. This surface begins only after compact retirement tokens are explicit and someone is trying to escalate custody exit into appeal governance.

## No appeal vs receipt repair vs repeated retention dispute vs authority contest vs deletion-proof overflow vs mixed threshold

- `no-appeal-needed`: compact custody-retirement evidence is enough. There is no repeated retention/deletion failure, no unresolved authority contest, and no missing survivor that would force appeal machinery.
- `receipt-repair-threshold`: the exit packet is locally deficient but repairable by a successor digest, unsupported-status mark, tombstone note, retention-window citation, or authority-return receipt. The repair stays local and does not promote a board.
- `repeated-retention-dispute`: more than one public, archive-local custody-exit case has failed because the same retention/deletion boundary kept being disputed after compact retirement tokens were applied.
- `authority-contest-overflow`: ordinary authority return is not enough because the runtime/profiler/trace/metric/scheduler or archive owner boundary remains contested after the retirement witness and local receipt repair.
- `deletion-proof-overflow`: the specific failure is that deletion, tombstoning, cleanup, or carrier expiry removed or could remove the only audit path, and ordinary successor receipts cannot preserve public reviewability.
- `mixed-appeal-threshold`: several threshold reasons are material, such as repeated retention dispute plus authority contest, or deletion-proof overflow plus receipt repair. The witness names the smallest stronger process under consideration and the exit rule that would retire it.

## Countermodels / probes

- A Prometheus tombstone, clean-tombstones call, Kubernetes finalizer, deletion timestamp, or audit log exists. That does not by itself justify an appeal board.
- A single retirement packet lacks a digest. That is usually `receipt-repair-threshold`, not repeated-retention-dispute.
- A finalizer delayed deletion. That is a bounded cleanup interlock unless the authority relation itself remains contested after ordinary repair.
- An audit log records who did what. That supports reviewability, but it is not replay truth and not a standing telemetry court.
- Deletion-proof pressure is real only when the loss would remove the only public audit path and local successor support cannot repair it.

## Design consequences

A valid custody-exit appeal-threshold packet names:

1. the prior bridge, promotion-gate, custody-scope, and custody-retirement states if known;
2. the disputed exit act: retirement, retention, deletion, tombstone, finalizer, authority return, carrier expiry, or scope shrink;
3. the selected `gpu_replay_cross_observer_custody_exit_appeal_threshold_state` token;
4. the local repair attempted, if any;
5. the repeated dispute, authority contest, deletion-proof basis, or mixed threshold basis if escalation is considered;
6. the smallest stronger surface under consideration and the explicit exit condition that would retire it.

The default repair is local receipt repair or no appeal, not durable appeal governance. If a packet cannot name repeated public failures, an unresolved authority contest, or a deletion-proof overflow, keep `QWS-0254` quarantined and repair the receipt.

## Overflow test

Promote appeal-scope courts, immutable evidence vaults, deletion-dispute tribunals, or equivalent standing custody-exit governance only if later revisions show repeated, public, archive-local failures where:

- `no-appeal-needed` and `receipt-repair-threshold` cannot preserve custody-exit auditability;
- repeated retention/deletion disputes, authority contests, or deletion-proof overflows recur across more than one case;
- ordinary resolution entries, retention notes, bridge records, tombstone notes, audit citations, or authority returns cannot repair the gap;
- the stronger process names an explicit exit condition so it cannot become permanent telemetry governance.

Until then, `QWS-0254` stays quarantined.

## Transformer-facing implication

When a future continuation asks whether retention-audit appeal machinery is justified after compact custody-retirement tokens, first classify the pressure through `gpu_replay_cross_observer_custody_exit_appeal_threshold_state`. Most cases should be no appeal or receipt repair. Do not promote an appeal board, custody-exit notary, immutable evidence vault, deletion-dispute tribunal, or permanent telemetry court unless repeated public overflow has already made the compact threshold witness fail.
