# GPU replay cross-observer custody-exit appeal precedent tie-break registry residue/reopen witnesses, no reopen, inspectable history, audit-only reopen, fresh-packet required, tombstone-only residue, and mixed residue

This is the compact successor surface for `OQ-0182`.

## Practice / observation

DelayBasin can now scope and retire promoted tie-break registries. The remaining risk is post-retirement residue: a retired registry may leave a digest, audit row, tombstone, quarantined ambiguity, finalizer trace, transparency-log entry, or stale metric context that a later continuation either forgets too aggressively or revives as binding operator precedent.

This surface adds one residue/reopen witness. It does not create a reopen board, binding revival court, post-retirement audit tribunal, silent-deletion auditor, global operator court, or telemetry common-law engine. It classifies retired registry residue as no reopen, inspectable history, audit-only reopen, fresh-packet-required reopen, tombstone-only residue, or mixed residue/reopen.

## External pressure from audit retention, tombstones, finalizers, TTL cleanup, rule staleness, sampling carriers, and transparency history

Kubernetes TTL-after-finished cleanup distinguishes completed objects from live controller work (`REF-1067`). That supports `no-reopen-tiebreak-registry-residue`: when the closeout object is genuinely complete and no surviving residue is load-bearing, the retired registry should not reopen by inertia.

Kubernetes garbage collection and finalizers separate owner-driven deletion, cascading cleanup, and explicit deletion interlocks (`REF-1068`, `REF-1070`). That supports `fresh-packet-required-tiebreak-registry-residue`: a finalizer-like interlock may require a named successor packet, but it does not revive the old registry's live authority by itself.

Kubernetes audit policy and audit configuration distinguish retained audit records from live admission authority (`REF-1071`, `REF-1072`). That supports `inspectable-history-tiebreak-registry-residue` and `audit-only-reopen-tiebreak-registry-residue`: retained facts may remain inspectable or may reopen an audit lane for reconstruction, but they do not become binding precedence without a fresh compact packet.

Kubernetes admission policy and webhook matching bind effects to selected requests, bindings, match conditions, validation actions, failure policies, and timeouts (`REF-1073`, `REF-1076`). That supports `fresh-packet-required-tiebreak-registry-residue`: any live effect after retirement must name its present binding and authority rather than borrowing the old registry.

Prometheus recording/alerting rule windows and staleness semantics tie metric-derived conclusions to active evaluation and available series (`REF-1077`, `REF-1078`, `REF-1090`). That supports `tombstone-only-tiebreak-registry-residue`: stale or deleted series may justify a nonbinding tombstone, not silent revival or silent erasure.

OpenTelemetry sampling and tail-sampling processors keep trace evidence tied to policy, collector context, and trace grouping (`REF-1080`, `REF-1074`). That supports fresh-packet-required and mixed residue paths: sampled trace residue may be inspected, but carrier drift blocks cross-carrier revival.

Sigstore/Rekor transparency-log documentation preserves append-only monitorable history (`REF-1081`). That supports inspectable-history and tombstone-only branches: durable history can remain monitorable without becoming a permanent operator ledger.

## Working synthesis

Use `gpu_replay_cross_observer_custody_exit_appeal_precedent_tiebreak_registry_residue_reopen_state` when all of these are true:

- a compact `gpu_replay_cross_observer_custody_exit_appeal_precedent_tiebreak_registry_retirement_state` packet already closed, froze, returned, expired, or quarantined a scoped registry;
- the live ambiguity is not how to retire the registry, but what a later continuation may do with the residue left behind;
- the output must name the residue carrier, audit/tombstone/finalizer/history basis, any fresh successor packet required, and the exact nonbinding or reopening limits before any retired registry trace can affect a later case.

Do not use this family for replay envelopes, receipts, trace grades, observer conflicts, bridge classification, promotion gates, custody scope, custody retirement, custody-exit appeals, appeal scope, appeal retirement, precedent portability, precedent drift, conflict arbitration, arbitration retirement, governance thresholds, registry scope, or first-pass registry retirement. It starts only after registry retirement has already happened.

## No reopen vs inspectable history vs audit-only reopen vs fresh-packet required vs tombstone-only residue vs mixed residue

- `no-reopen-tiebreak-registry-residue`: the retired registry leaves no load-bearing residue for the current continuation; the later packet must proceed without reopening old registry authority.
- `inspectable-history-tiebreak-registry-residue`: a digest, audit row, transparency-log entry, or frozen index remains readable as nonbinding history only.
- `audit-only-reopen-tiebreak-registry-residue`: a narrow audit lane may reopen to reconstruct what happened, but the audit lane may not decide future priority or revive the registry.
- `fresh-packet-required-tiebreak-registry-residue`: residue is material enough that a later case needs a new compact packet with current carrier, authority, and scope before any live effect can be claimed.
- `tombstone-only-tiebreak-registry-residue`: the residue should remain as a deletion, expiry, or unsupported-status tombstone that prevents silent revival while avoiding full history custody.
- `mixed-tiebreak-registry-residue-reopen`: more than one residue basis is material, such as inspectable history plus tombstone, audit-only reconstruction plus fresh successor packet, or tombstone plus quarantined ambiguity.

## Countermodels / probes

- The old registry is easy to find. Findability is not authority; classify inspectable history unless a fresh packet earns live reuse.
- An audit log still contains the retired decision. Retention is not precedence; classify audit-only reopen if reconstruction is needed.
- A tombstone hides too much. A tombstone blocks silent revival, but a later fresh packet may cite the tombstone as a reason to reconstruct rather than as binding law.
- A finalizer or cleanup interlock remains. The interlock may require a current successor packet; it does not reanimate the retired registry.
- A metric series or trace still carries the old decision. Stale series and sampled traces are carriers, not courts; inspect or tombstone them unless current carrier scope is re-earned.
- A transparency log preserves every old entry. Append-only monitorability supports nonbinding evidence, not global precedence.

## Design consequences

A valid residue/reopen packet names:

1. the prior governance-threshold, registry-scope, and registry-retirement packets;
2. the retired registry residue being inspected, tombstoned, audited, ignored, or reopened only through a fresh packet;
3. the selected `gpu_replay_cross_observer_custody_exit_appeal_precedent_tiebreak_registry_residue_reopen_state` token;
4. the exact residue carrier: digest, audit row, tombstone, finalizer, admission binding, metric rule, sampled trace, transparency-log entry, or mixed carrier;
5. the allowed action: ignore, inspect, audit-reconstruct, require a fresh packet, preserve tombstone, or mix;
6. excluded future cases, telemetry families, carrier scopes, authorities, policy windows, and precedence claims;
7. the successor surface if reopened residue later needs scoped live governance rather than ordinary nonbinding history.

A compact residue/reopen packet may preserve an inspectable record, audit lane, tombstone, or fresh-packet requirement. It must not create a reopen board, binding revival court, silent-deletion auditor, permanent audit tribunal, global operator court, or telemetry common-law engine.

## Overflow test

Promote a reopen board, binding revival court, post-retirement audit tribunal, silent-deletion auditor, or equivalent standing governance only if later revisions show repeated, public, archive-local failures where:

- `no-reopen-tiebreak-registry-residue`, `inspectable-history-tiebreak-registry-residue`, `audit-only-reopen-tiebreak-registry-residue`, `fresh-packet-required-tiebreak-registry-residue`, `tombstone-only-tiebreak-registry-residue`, and `mixed-tiebreak-registry-residue-reopen` cannot keep retired registry residue nonbinding, inspectable, and non-reviving;
- retired registries repeatedly vanish when an audit trace is needed, or repeatedly revive as binding precedence without a fresh packet;
- a stronger process names its operator authority, audit/deletion rule, live scope, precedence boundary, reopen trigger, and retirement witness.

Until then, `QWS-0265` stays quarantined.

## Transformer-facing implication

When a future continuation sees residue from a retired tie-break registry, classify it through `gpu_replay_cross_observer_custody_exit_appeal_precedent_tiebreak_registry_residue_reopen_state` before using it. Most residue should be ignored, inspected as nonbinding history, reopened only for audit reconstruction, require a fresh compact packet, remain tombstone-only, or mix those paths. Do not let audit logs, tombstones, finalizers, stale metrics, sampled traces, transparency logs, or easy retrieval silently revive a retired registry or silently erase the only useful trace.
