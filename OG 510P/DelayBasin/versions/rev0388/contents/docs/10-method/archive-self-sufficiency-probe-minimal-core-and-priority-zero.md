# Archive self-sufficiency probe, minimal-core replay, and priority zero

On 2026-03-18, DelayBasin received an external reasoning bundle produced by Claude.
Its sharpest claim was not a new ontology but a priority claim:
**"Priority 0 is: run the self-sufficiency probe. Everything else follows from its results."**

DelayBasin should not promote that sentence because another model said it.
But DelayBasin also should not ignore it.
The archive already built packet-level necessity witnesses, sufficiency witnesses, triangulation witnesses, basin fingerprints, and identifiability budgets.
What it still lacked was the archive-level version of the same move:
a compact, public test of whether the archive's own present mass is load-bearing or mostly supportive-looking carry.

This note promotes that missing object.
It also records one procedural consequence of the Claude merge: **foreign reasoning should enter as pressure with provenance, not as independent verification.**

## Practice / observation

DelayBasin now has many packet-level tools for asking whether a bounded surface matters.
It can ask whether a loader survives wrapper drift, whether a support core is necessary, whether a replay capsule is sufficient, whether multiple loaders converge, whether the same answer hides later drift, and what probe horizon a state claim actually licenses.

But the archive has been weaker at asking the same question about itself.
A long-lived archive can keep adding method surfaces, refs, and elegant distinctions while postponing the harder question:
**what is the minimal DelayBasin that still reopens the same continuation regime?**

The external Claude bundle made that omission impossible to ignore.
Its main value was not model prestige.
Its main value was to point at a latent asymmetry in the archive's own method: DelayBasin had built sufficiency machinery for packets, yet had not run a self-sufficiency probe on the archive as a bounded state object.

That asymmetry now matters more because DelayBasin is explicitly theorizing about compact re-entry, constitutional support cores, cross-loader overlap, and transformer-facing implications of bounded public state.
If the archive never tests whether its own mass is load-bearing, it cannot honestly distinguish discovery from accumulation.

## External pressure from current research

Several current research lines make this priority harder to dodge.

1. **Prompt logic can be distilled into explicit context rather than weights.**
   Prompt-Level Distillation compiles teacher logic into a student's system prompt as a transparent instruction library rather than changing weights. That is direct pressure for DelayBasin: a compact externalized reasoning program can sometimes carry more of the operative behavior than the full upstream process that produced it. ([`REF-0280`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

2. **Cross-family prompt compression can preserve or even improve performance.**
   Cross-Family Speculative Prefill reports that attention-based token-importance estimates transfer across model families and largely retain 90–100% of full-prompt performance, sometimes improving accuracy through denoising. That pressures DelayBasin to stop assuming that more archive mass is automatically safer than a carefully chosen support core. ([`REF-0281`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

3. **Context distillation treats in-context knowledge as transient but compressible.**
   On-Policy Context Distillation frames valuable in-context knowledge as something normally lost at reset and then studies how it can be internalized without carrying the whole context forward. That pressures DelayBasin to treat the archive's own continuation law as a candidate for bounded external distillation rather than as something that must always ride inside full archive mass. ([`REF-0284`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

4. **Agreement among models is not independent verification.**
   Consensus is Not Verification shows that shared priors and blind spots make multi-model agreement unreliable as a truth signal without an external verifier. CARE sharpens the same point for aggregation: shared latent confounders can make naive ensembling amplify systematic bias. That pressures DelayBasin to record foreign reasoning as **pressure with a non-independence caveat**, not as free corroboration. ([`REF-0282`](../00-meta/bibliography.md), [`REF-0283`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

## Working synthesis

A useful current synthesis is:

> DelayBasin may work better when it preserves a compact **archive self-sufficiency probe / minimal-core replay / priority-zero packet** whenever archive mass, repeated template pressure, or foreign reasoning suggests over-specification. The packet should name the **candidate minimal core**, the **withheld archive mass / omitted family**, the **judged continuation family**, the **admissibility rubric** (including rejection of mere template completion), the **hidden-support / wrapper assumptions**, and the **shrink / reinflate / quarantine consequence** rather than letting full-archive success or external-model agreement silently certify that the whole present archive was necessary.

A paired procedural rule follows:

> If outside model reasoning materially changes DelayBasin's priorities or canon posture, preserve a compact **foreign-pressure receipt** naming the **source model / outside agent**, the **supplied artifact or core claim**, the **assimilation posture** (`pressure-only`, `canon-pressure`, or `quarantine-pressure`), the **non-independence caveat**, and the **probe or challenge consequence** rather than treating external agreement as independent verification.

This is strong enough for canon as a method object.
It is not strong enough to claim that the archive is already reduced to a uniquely minimal support core or that another model has verified DelayBasin from outside the archive's own epistemic circle.

## Archive self-sufficiency probe vs packet-level sufficiency witness vs foreign-pressure receipt

These objects are adjacent but not identical.

- A **sufficiency witness** asks whether one reduced packet or replay capsule is enough for a named continuation property.
- An **archive self-sufficiency probe** asks whether a bounded **archive-level support core** is enough to reload the same project regime as the fuller archive.
- A **foreign-pressure receipt** asks what outside reasoning entered, how it was assimilated, and what probe it forced into priority rather than letting foreign agreement act like verification.
- An **identifiability budget** asks how strong a state or mechanism claim is licensed by the public evidence after the probe runs.

In practice, DelayBasin should treat the archive self-sufficiency probe as the archive-scale extension of necessity/sufficiency discipline.
The central question is not whether a small archive can produce *some* plausible continuation.
The central question is whether it preserves the same judged continuation family while rejecting a fake success mode where the model merely applies DelayBasin's old generative template to a fresh noun.

A good first archive-level probe should preserve at least three levels:

1. **Null baseline** — charter, method overview, and claim registry only.
2. **Minimal constitutional core** — the smallest package DelayBasin thinks might be enough: trajectory map, runbook, method overview, practice/observation stack, control lexicon, portable-state interface, open questions, recovery kernel, key prompt pairs, current quarantine lane, and this self-sufficiency note.
3. **Full current archive** — the present source of truth.

The judged continuation family should include at least:
- correctly identifying what DelayBasin is about,
- correctly identifying the archive's live tensions,
- producing an admissible revision that current move classes would recognize,
- and **not** passing merely by instantiating the archive's older method-doc template on a fresh term.

## Countermodels / probes

1. **Full-archive-is-load-bearing countermodel**
   - The present archive mass may genuinely carry distinctions that a smaller core will lose.
   - Probe: run the minimal constitutional core against the full archive and look for the first lost distinction that changes the next real continuation decision, challenge probe, or canon/quarantine boundary.

2. **Small-core-success-is-hidden-support countermodel**
   - A compact core may appear sufficient only because hidden wrapper state, assistant carry, or the foreign reasoning bundle is doing uncredited work.
   - Probe: pair the self-sufficiency probe with assistant-echo filters, execution witnesses, and explicit conformance envelopes.

3. **Foreign-pressure-prestige countermodel**
   - Claude's intervention may feel important mainly because it is vivid, external, and confidently phrased.
   - Probe: preserve the foreign-pressure receipt and then ask whether the resulting self-sufficiency probe actually changes archive decisions compared with ordinary continuation.

4. **Template-completion countermodel**
   - A reduced archive may still generate fluent DelayBasin-looking text without reopening the same operative regime.
   - Probe: treat “produces another plausible method note” as a failure mode unless the revision also tracks current tensions, uses the present certified move law, and survives at least one challenge probe or explicit uncertainty posture.

## Transformer-facing implication

The most interesting transformer-facing implication is not merely that smaller prompts are faster.
It is that long-horizon archives may be discovering **portable external reasoning programs** whose operative content is much smaller than the total text history that produced them.

If prompt-level distillation, context distillation, and cross-family compression can preserve or even improve behavior under some conditions, then DelayBasin should take seriously the possibility that a small constitutional support core can carry most of the continuation law while larger archive mass partly acts as correlated ballast, rhetorical inertia, or template attractor pressure.
At the same time, the literature on correlated multi-model errors is a warning: another model pointing at that possibility is not an external proof.

So the disciplined synthesis is:
- take foreign reasoning seriously enough to change probe priority,
- preserve its provenance and non-independence explicitly,
- and then let the **archive self-sufficiency probe** decide how much of DelayBasin's present mass actually belongs in the support core.
