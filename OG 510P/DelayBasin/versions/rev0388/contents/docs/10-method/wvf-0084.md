# GPU replay cross-observer custody-exit appeal precedent tie-break governance-threshold witnesses, no registry, repeated closeout failure, cross-carrier conflict, authority split, audit retention, sunset breach, and mixed escalation

This is the compact successor surface for `OQ-0179`.

## Practice / observation

DelayBasin already has compact states for retired appeal precedent portability, drift, local conflict arbitration, and arbitration retirement. The remaining question is sharper than "should we keep a registry?" It asks what public failure would justify even considering a permanent tie-break registry, precedent-priority ledger, arbitration-closeout court, or equivalent standing closeout governance after compact arbitration-retirement tokens exist.

This surface adds one threshold witness for that escalation question. It does not promote the registry. It classifies whether no registry is needed, whether compact closeout has repeatedly failed in public, whether conflicts now span incompatible evidence carriers, whether authority is split, whether audit or retention duties require a durable closeout index, whether sunsets are repeatedly breached, or whether several escalation reasons are entangled.

## External pressure from audit policy, finalizers, rule windows, sampling state, admission boundaries, and transparency logs

Kubernetes audit policy separates request stages, recorded levels, policy rules, and backends that persist events (`REF-1071`, `REF-1072`). That supports `audit-retention-tiebreak-governance`: a durable index can become a live question only when a public audit or retention obligation needs consistent closeout facts, not merely because old arbitration packets are interesting.

Kubernetes garbage collection, owner references, and finalizers separate cleanup ownership from deletion interlocks (`REF-1068`, `REF-1070`). That supports `authority-split-tiebreak-governance`: a stronger governance surface is only plausible when multiple owners or cleanup authorities repeatedly block one another after compact authority-return and residue-quarantine tokens have been tried.

Kubernetes dynamic admission and ValidatingAdmissionPolicy keep policy decisions bound to request matching, selectors, bindings, validation actions, failure policy, and timeouts (`REF-1073`, `REF-1076`). That supports `no-registry-needed-tiebreak-governance` and `cross-carrier-conflict-tiebreak-governance`: local policy paths can usually absorb a settled tie-break, but repeated conflicts across nonmatching carriers or bindings may justify a threshold record.

Prometheus recording rules, alerting rules, `for`/`keep_firing_for` windows, and staleness behavior keep metric-derived guidance bounded by evaluation and data-availability windows (`REF-1077`, `REF-1078`, `REF-1090`). That supports `sunset-breach-tiebreak-governance`: a registry cannot be justified by one long-lived rule, but repeated resurrection after named sunsets is a real escalation signal.

OpenTelemetry sampling and tail-sampling processors separate trace-level sampling criteria, collector policy, and stateful trace grouping (`REF-1080`, `REF-1074`). That supports `cross-carrier-conflict-tiebreak-governance`: if a prior tie-break is being applied across trace carriers or collector policy windows that no longer match, the compact state must either classify the mismatch or admit threshold pressure.

Sigstore/Rekor transparency-log documentation preserves append-only monitorable records (`REF-1081`). That supports both audit retention and no-registry defaults: durable history can make evidence inspectable without making the old tie-break a live priority rule.

## Working synthesis

Use `gpu_replay_cross_observer_custody_exit_appeal_precedent_tiebreak_governance_threshold_state` when all of these are true:

- compact `gpu_replay_cross_observer_custody_exit_appeal_precedent_conflict_arbitration_retirement_state` has already been tried, cited, or explicitly considered for the local tie-break closeout problem;
- the current question is whether repeated closeout failure, carrier conflict, authority split, audit/retention duty, sunset breach, or mixed escalation justifies stronger standing governance;
- the desired output is an escalation threshold record, not the registry, ledger, court, operator authority, or binding priority ladder itself.

Do not use this family for first-pass replay envelopes, receipts, trace grades, bridge custody, custody retirement, appeal thresholding, appeal scope, appeal retirement, precedent portability, precedent drift, conflict arbitration, or ordinary arbitration retirement. It starts only after compact closeout tokens are explicit and the archive is asking what would justify stronger governance.

## No registry vs repeated closeout failure vs cross-carrier conflict vs authority split vs audit retention vs sunset breach vs mixed escalation

- `no-registry-needed-tiebreak-governance`: compact arbitration-retirement tokens still close the case; old tie-breaks stay as citations, history, counterexamples, or excluded evidence without standing governance.
- `repeated-closeout-failure-tiebreak-governance`: multiple public, archive-local attempts to use settled tiebreak, joined handoff, carrier expiry, authority return, priority sunset, residue quarantine, and mixed retirement have failed for the same class of closeout problem.
- `cross-carrier-conflict-tiebreak-governance`: the same retired-appeal tie-break is being applied across incompatible trace carriers, metric series, admission bindings, cleanup objects, transparency-log entries, or retention windows after compact carrier-match and drift checks have failed.
- `authority-split-tiebreak-governance`: two or more legitimate cleanup, policy, retention, appeal, or ownership authorities repeatedly claim final authority over the same closeout residue after ordinary authority return has failed.
- `audit-retention-tiebreak-governance`: public audit, retention, compliance, or reproducibility duties require a durable index of closeout facts, and compact citations alone repeatedly fail to keep the facts findable without granting live priority by default.
- `sunset-breach-tiebreak-governance`: named TTLs, review dates, alert windows, sampling-policy windows, policy sunsets, or retention horizons are repeatedly breached or silently extended so that retired tie-breaks revive as live priority.
- `mixed-tiebreak-governance-escalation`: more than one threshold is material, such as cross-carrier conflict plus authority split, audit retention plus sunset breach, or repeated closeout failure plus residue quarantine that compact tokens cannot keep honest.

## Countermodels / probes

- A local tie-break was cited twice. Reuse is not repeated closeout failure; require public failures after compact retirement tokens are actually attempted.
- A transparency log or audit backend preserves history. Durable history is not registry authority; require a separate audit-retention need and a rule that history remains nonbinding by default.
- An old metric rule or alert kept firing. Continuation windows are not standing priority; test whether a named sunset was breached rather than treating persistence as governance.
- A finalizer blocked deletion. A cleanup interlock is not a court; test whether multiple legitimate authorities repeatedly split after authority-return repair.
- Two carriers disagree. Carrier conflict earns threshold pressure only after carrier-match, drift, arbitration, and arbitration-retirement tokens cannot locate the smallest current carrier.
- If the threshold cannot be named, use `no-registry-needed-tiebreak-governance` or `mixed-tiebreak-governance-escalation` with explicit unresolved evidence rather than promoting the registry.

## Design consequences

A valid governance-threshold packet names:

1. the prior arbitration-retirement packet and selected closeout token;
2. the repeated closeout failures, carrier conflicts, authority splits, audit/retention duties, sunset breaches, or mixed escalation evidence;
3. the selected `gpu_replay_cross_observer_custody_exit_appeal_precedent_tiebreak_governance_threshold_state` token;
4. the compact tokens already tried and why they failed or still suffice;
5. the smallest stronger surface under consideration, if any, and its exact nonbinding-history lane;
6. future cases, carriers, authorities, policies, windows, retention duties, or appeal layers explicitly excluded;
7. the successor surface if threshold overflow later requires a scoped promoted registry rather than another compact witness.

A compact threshold packet may preserve a threshold digest, evidence list, or no-registry decision. It must not create registry operators, permanent priority, default binding precedent, or an arbitration-closeout court.

## Overflow test

Promote a standing tie-break registry, precedent-priority ledger, arbitration-closeout court, registry-operator authority, or equivalent governance only if later revisions show repeated, public, archive-local failures where:

- compact `no-registry-needed-tiebreak-governance`, `repeated-closeout-failure-tiebreak-governance`, `cross-carrier-conflict-tiebreak-governance`, `authority-split-tiebreak-governance`, `audit-retention-tiebreak-governance`, `sunset-breach-tiebreak-governance`, and `mixed-tiebreak-governance-escalation` cannot separate ordinary closeout from true standing-governance need;
- compact arbitration-retirement packets repeatedly vanish too early or persist as de facto global law despite named thresholds;
- a stronger process names its scope, operator authority, retirement rule, nonbinding-history lane, audit/retention boundary, and explicit anti-precedent rule.

Until then, `QWS-0262` stays quarantined.

## Transformer-facing implication

When a future continuation asks whether permanent tie-break governance is justified after compact arbitration-retirement tokens are explicit, first classify the case through `gpu_replay_cross_observer_custody_exit_appeal_precedent_tiebreak_governance_threshold_state`. Most cases should be `no-registry-needed-tiebreak-governance`. Escalate only for repeated closeout failure, cross-carrier conflict, authority split, audit retention, sunset breach, or a named mixed escalation. Do not let audit logs, finalizers, recording rules, sampling policies, admission bindings, transparency-log entries, or repeated reuse become a registry, priority ledger, or closeout court without a later overflow witness.
