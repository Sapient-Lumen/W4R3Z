# GPU replay reopened-residue drift governance-retirement portability-drift conflict-arbitration retirement governance-scope witnesses, packet-local, history-custody-bound, authority-lane-limited, successor-route-bound, retirement-window, and mixed scope

This is the compact successor surface for `OQ-0197`. It answers the narrow question of what a promoted post-arbitration governance surface may actually govern after compact threshold tokens overflow. It makes scope explicit before any standing surface can act, and keeps the answer bounded to named packets, history custody, authority lanes, successor routes, and retirement windows.

The surface is intentionally smaller than a post-arbitration governance court. It does not create a global conflict-history vault, successor-route authority board, retired-arbitration common law, authority-lane senate, or permanent governance operator. It only decides what a locally promoted governance surface is allowed to hold, cite, hand off, and retire.

## Practice / observation

`rev0302` established that standing post-arbitration governance is not automatic: compact threshold tokens must show no-standing sufficiency, repeated retirement failure, history loss, overbinding, authority split, successor-route loop, or mixed escalation. That solved the promotion question. It did not bound what a promoted surface may touch once a threshold has actually been earned.

Use this surface only when all five facts are public:

1. prior governance-retirement, portability, portability-drift, conflict-arbitration, arbitration-retirement, and governance-threshold tokens are named;
2. the threshold token is not merely ceremonial and the receipt explains why compact no-standing or local repair failed;
3. the affected packet, conflict-history row, authority lane, carrier, owner, or successor route is named;
4. the proposed scope is local enough to be inspected and small enough to retire; and
5. the later receipt names the selected scope token, excluded global machinery, successor surface if any, and fail-closed repair.

## External pressure from audit retention, policy binding, owner scope, stale-signal windows, trace-carrier limits, and transparency checkpoints

Kubernetes-style owner references, finalizers, admission binding, and audit retention show that a cleanup or escalation surface may need explicit ownership and scope boundaries before it can act. Prometheus staleness, recording rules, and alert windows show that signals and rules need time windows and should not govern outside the slice that made them meaningful. OpenTelemetry trace carriers and sampling policies show that context can move across boundaries only when the carrier is named and bounded. Transparency checkpoints show that retained history can preserve proof without becoming live authority everywhere.

Those analogies support a compact scope witness: packet-local, history-custody-bound, authority-lane-limited, successor-route-bound, retirement-window, or mixed scope. They do not import admission controllers, incident boards, observability stores, sampling processors, or transparency logs as DelayBasin governance machinery.

## Packet-local vs history-custody-bound vs authority-lane-limited vs successor-route-bound vs retirement-window vs mixed scope

Select `packet-local-post-arbitration-governance-scope` when the only honest authority is over the named threshold-overflow packet. Select `history-custody-bound-post-arbitration-governance-scope` when the strongest need is to retain enough conflict history for audit while keeping that history nonbinding. Select `authority-lane-limited-post-arbitration-governance-scope` when the governance surface exists only to separate incompatible authority lanes, owners, or policy bindings. Select `successor-route-bound-post-arbitration-governance-scope` when the scope is the successor route itself and not a general court. Select `retirement-window-post-arbitration-governance-scope` when the key scope limit is an explicit end condition. Select `mixed-post-arbitration-governance-scope` only when the receipt names each branch and the smallest successor question.

## Countermodels / probes

- A threshold token fired. Threshold is not scope; require the packet, lane, history row, route, and retirement boundary before acting.
- Conflict history is important. Importance is not live authority; classify history custody and keep it nonbinding outside the local packet.
- Two authority lanes disagree. Disagreement is not global jurisdiction; name the lane boundary and quarantine any wider claim.
- A successor route exists. A route is not a board; bind the route and specify when it hands off or expires.
- A scoped surface feels safer than no surface. Safety theater is not scope; if the receipt cannot name the local packet and closeout boundary, fail closed.
- The excluded synonyms `global-post-arbitration-governance-court`, `conflict-history-vault-by-scope`, `successor-route-board-by-default`, `authority-lane-senate`, and `post-arbitration-governance-scope-ish` remain outside the allowed vocabulary.

## Design consequences

Receipts that use this family must name the prior threshold token, threshold-overflow evidence, active packet, conflict-history rows, carriers or owners, authority lanes, successor route if any, selected scope token, excluded global machinery, retirement or closeout boundary, and fail-closed repair. The witness may authorize a successor question about retirement only after a scoped surface has actually been admitted. It may not silently instantiate a permanent court, vault, board, lane senate, or reusable governance operator.

## Overflow test

Promote a stronger surface only if repeated future revisions show that `packet-local-post-arbitration-governance-scope, history-custody-bound-post-arbitration-governance-scope, authority-lane-limited-post-arbitration-governance-scope, successor-route-bound-post-arbitration-governance-scope, retirement-window-post-arbitration-governance-scope, or mixed-post-arbitration-governance-scope` cannot distinguish local scope from global governance. Until then, permanent post-arbitration governance boards, conflict-history custody courts, successor-route authority registries, and equivalent standing machinery stay quarantined under `QWS-0280`.
