# GPU replay cross-observer custody-exit appeal precedent tie-break registry-retirement witnesses, settled closeout, carrier expiry, authority return, history freeze, window expiry, residue quarantine, and mixed retirement

This is the compact successor surface for `OQ-0181`.

## Practice / observation

DelayBasin now has compact states for tie-break governance thresholding and for the scope of any promoted registry-like surface. The remaining live risk is that even a scoped tie-break registry may survive after its closeout packet is settled, after its carrier expires, after authority returns, or after its retirement window ends. A scoped registry without a closeout witness can become the same permanent operator court it was designed to avoid.

This surface adds one registry-retirement witness. It does not create a registry-retirement board, operator court, immortal closeout ledger, global precedence queue, or post-registry appellate layer. It classifies how a scoped tie-break registry shrinks back as settled closeout, carrier expiry, authority return, nonbinding-history freeze, retirement-window expiry, residue quarantine, or mixed retirement.

## External pressure from TTL cleanup, finalizers, audit retention, admission bindings, rule windows, sampling carriers, and transparency history

Kubernetes TTL-after-finished cleanup distinguishes completed work from indefinitely live controller work (`REF-1067`). That supports `settled-closeout-tiebreak-registry-retirement` and `retirement-window-expiry-tiebreak-registry-retirement`: when the named closeout work is done, the registry should either retire or name why a later window remains.

Kubernetes garbage collection, owner references, and finalizers separate deletion ownership, cascading cleanup, and explicit interlocks (`REF-1068`, `REF-1070`). That supports `authority-return-tiebreak-registry-retirement`: a registry that existed to hold an authority split or cleanup interlock should return authority to the named owner rather than keep operator power by inertia.

Kubernetes audit policy and audit configuration separate durable records from live admission authority (`REF-1071`, `REF-1072`). That supports `nonbinding-history-freeze-tiebreak-registry-retirement`: a retired registry may leave an audit/history trace without retaining priority.

Kubernetes dynamic admission and ValidatingAdmissionPolicy bind policy effects to request matching, bindings, selectors, validation actions, failure policy, and timeouts (`REF-1073`, `REF-1076`). That supports `carrier-expiry-tiebreak-registry-retirement` and authority return: if the matching carrier or binding is gone, registry scope should not continue as general policy.

Prometheus recording rules, alerting rules, `for` / `keep_firing_for` windows, and staleness semantics keep metric-derived conclusions tied to active evaluation and data availability (`REF-1077`, `REF-1078`, `REF-1090`). That supports `retirement-window-expiry-tiebreak-registry-retirement`: old priority cannot quietly survive after the evaluation or staleness window that justified it.

OpenTelemetry sampling and tail-sampling processors separate sampling criteria, collector policy, and trace grouping (`REF-1080`, `REF-1074`). That supports `carrier-expiry-tiebreak-registry-retirement`: when the sampled trace carrier or collector policy changes, the registry cannot silently continue across all telemetry.

Sigstore/Rekor transparency-log documentation preserves append-only monitorable history (`REF-1081`). That supports nonbinding-history freeze and residue quarantine: durable evidence can remain inspectable without becoming an immortal precedence ledger.

## Working synthesis

Use `gpu_replay_cross_observer_custody_exit_appeal_precedent_tiebreak_registry_retirement_state` when all of these are true:

- a compact `gpu_replay_cross_observer_custody_exit_appeal_precedent_tiebreak_registry_scope_state` packet already exists or is being modeled as overflowed into scoped governance;
- the question is no longer what the registry may hold, but when and how the scoped registry closes, shrinks, freezes as history, returns authority, or quarantines residue;
- the output must name the retiring packet, carrier, authority, history lane, window, residue, and excluded future authority before any registry-like surface can continue.

Do not use this family for replay envelopes, replay receipts, trace grades, observer conflicts, bridge classification, promotion gates, custody scope, custody retirement, custody-exit appeal thresholding, appeal scope, appeal retirement, precedent portability, precedent drift, conflict arbitration, arbitration retirement, governance-threshold classification, or first-pass registry scoping. It starts only after registry scope has already been made explicit.

## Settled closeout vs carrier expiry vs authority return vs history freeze vs window expiry vs residue quarantine vs mixed retirement

- `settled-closeout-tiebreak-registry-retirement`: the scoped registry retires because the named closeout packet, conflict case, appeal residue, or replay-custody dispute is settled and no live registry function remains.
- `carrier-expiry-tiebreak-registry-retirement`: the registry retires because the evidence carrier, trace carrier, metric series family, admission binding, cleanup object lineage, or retention carrier that justified scope has expired, changed, or been replaced.
- `authority-return-tiebreak-registry-retirement`: the registry retires by returning authority to the named cleanup, retention, policy, ownership, appeal, or archive surface it temporarily mediated.
- `nonbinding-history-freeze-tiebreak-registry-retirement`: the registry is frozen as history or audit index only; future packets may inspect it, but it carries no live priority.
- `retirement-window-expiry-tiebreak-registry-retirement`: the registry retires because its TTL, review date, policy sunset, stale-data rule, closeout condition, or admission/metric/sampling window expired.
- `residue-quarantine-tiebreak-registry-retirement`: unresolved residue remains too ambiguous for deletion or reuse, so the residue is quarantined with explicit nonbinding status rather than kept as live registry authority.
- `mixed-tiebreak-registry-retirement`: more than one retirement basis is material, such as settled closeout plus history freeze, carrier expiry plus authority return, or window expiry plus residue quarantine.

## Countermodels / probes

- The registry still has entries. Entries are not live authority; test whether the active closeout job remains or whether the record should freeze as nonbinding history.
- An audit backend retains the registry record. Retention is not operator power; classify history freeze unless a separate live authority is named.
- A finalizer blocked cleanup. A finalizer may justify authority return or residue quarantine, not a permanent operator court.
- A recording rule or alert used the old tie-break. A rule window is not immortal priority; classify expiry unless a new compact packet re-earns scope.
- A sampled trace still carries the decision. Carrier continuity is not cross-carrier authority; retire on carrier expiry or keep carrier-bound scope only if the carrier is still current.
- A transparency log preserves the retired registry. Append-only history supports inspection and monitorability, not binding future cases.

## Design consequences

A valid registry-retirement packet names:

1. the prior governance-threshold and registry-scope packets;
2. the registry-like surface being closed, shrunk, frozen, returned, or quarantined;
3. the selected `gpu_replay_cross_observer_custody_exit_appeal_precedent_tiebreak_registry_retirement_state` token;
4. the closeout fact, carrier expiry, returning authority, history freeze, window expiry, residue quarantine, or mixed basis;
5. any minimal surviving record and whether it is binding, nonbinding, quarantined, or deleted;
6. excluded future cases, telemetry families, admission bindings, metric series, cleanup objects, authorities, and retention duties;
7. the successor surface if post-retirement residue later needs reopen governance rather than ordinary history.

A compact registry-retirement packet may preserve a final digest, nonbinding index row, history lane, or quarantined residue. It must not create a registry-retirement board by default, global operator authority, immortal closeout ledger, or telemetry common-law engine.

## Overflow test

Promote a registry-retirement board, global operator court, immortal closeout ledger, post-retirement audit tribunal, or equivalent standing governance only if later revisions show repeated, public, archive-local failures where:

- `settled-closeout-tiebreak-registry-retirement`, `carrier-expiry-tiebreak-registry-retirement`, `authority-return-tiebreak-registry-retirement`, `nonbinding-history-freeze-tiebreak-registry-retirement`, `retirement-window-expiry-tiebreak-registry-retirement`, `residue-quarantine-tiebreak-registry-retirement`, and `mixed-tiebreak-registry-retirement` cannot retire or shrink scoped registries;
- old registries repeatedly survive settlement, carrier expiry, authority return, history freeze, window expiry, or residue quarantine despite explicit exclusions;
- a stronger process names its operator authority, audit path, deletion/retention rule, reopen trigger, precedence rule, and retirement witness.

Until then, `QWS-0264` stays quarantined.

## Transformer-facing implication

When a future continuation asks how a scoped tie-break registry closes after settlement, first classify the case through `gpu_replay_cross_observer_custody_exit_appeal_precedent_tiebreak_registry_retirement_state`. Most registries should settle, expire with their carrier, return authority, freeze as nonbinding history, expire with their window, quarantine residue, or mix those paths. Do not let TTL cleanup, finalizers, audit records, admission bindings, rule windows, sampling carriers, transparency logs, repeated reuse, or registry scope become permanent operator governance without a later overflow witness.
