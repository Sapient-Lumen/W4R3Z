# Sham runtimes, decoy archives, and anti-self-sealing compression tests

Claude's priority-zero pressure has now been absorbed three times:
first as an archive self-sufficiency probe,
then as a template-law audit,
then as a runtime-triplet split.
A fourth recursive consequence now becomes hard to dodge.
If DelayBasin asks whether a reduced runtime is enough,
it cannot let the archive both define the candidate runtime and quietly certify that the runtime worked.
A self-sufficiency probe without a matched sham, decoy, or sequestered challenge surface is too easy to flatter.

DelayBasin therefore needs a compact canon object for **anti-self-sealing compression tests**:
a **sham runtime / decoy archive / anti-self-sealing compression-test packet**.
The weaker canon claim is procedural: when self-sufficiency, family compression, or runtime selection is doing real work, preserve a matched decoy and a sequestered challenge suite so the archive cannot certify its own minimality by taste alone.
The stronger claim — that DelayBasin may eventually need a standing foreign-family shadow archive to estimate its true compression frontier — remains speculative.

## Practice / observation

DelayBasin already has packet-level rigor surfaces: negative-control handles, blind packets, execution witnesses, assistant-echo filters, dependence-adjusted witnesses, and rewrite witnesses.
Claude's bundle also included consolidated pressure around **experimental rigor surfaces** and the warning that Priority 0 only matters if the resulting probe can actually fail.
But DelayBasin's archive-scale self-sufficiency lane still had a weak spot.
It could propose a candidate runtime triplet, choose archive-shaped challenge tasks, and then treat a plausible replay as evidence that the shrink worked.

That is a self-sealing design.
A reduced runtime can pass for several bad reasons:
- the archive picked a challenge suite whose failure modes were already described by the same runtime,
- the judge is related enough to the runtime family that it rewards familiar style,
- the challenge metric is global and flattering rather than discriminative within the actual shrink choice,
- or the archive has simply saturated on a repeated public challenge family.

Claude's pressure matters here because it was not only asking for smaller archives.
It was implicitly asking whether DelayBasin can design a probe that could honestly tell it **no**.
That recursive step is now the real continuation move.
A self-sufficiency probe that cannot lose is not a probe; it is a ceremony.

## External pressure from current research

Several current research lines sharpen this requirement.

1. **Contexts are now engineered, modular runtime objects.**
   Meta Context Engineering treats inference-time context as an explicit engineering surface whose files, code, and evaluation artifacts co-evolve. That pressures DelayBasin to treat a candidate runtime as a designed object that should be tested against alternatives rather than merely narrated. ([`REF-0294`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

2. **Prompts need tests because natural-language changes are hard to predict.**
   PromptPex treats prompts as software-like artifacts and automatically generates targeted tests to find non-compliance and model-specific regressions. That pressures DelayBasin to move from archive self-sufficiency as a prose aspiration to archive self-sufficiency as a test-bearing object. ([`REF-0295`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

3. **Related judges can reward related generations.**
   Preference Leakage shows that relatedness between generator and judge — same model, inheritance relationship, or same family — can systematically bias evaluation. That pressures DelayBasin to name the **relatedness / leakage risk** whenever the archive, the challenge suite, and the judge all arise from nearby reasoning families. ([`REF-0296`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

4. **Global evaluation can miss the real selection problem.**
   When LLM Judge Scores Look Good but Best-of-N Decisions Fail shows that decent global agreement can still produce poor within-prompt selection, and recommends reporting within-prompt signal, tie rates, and recovery metrics. That pressures DelayBasin to preserve a **within-family adjudication metric** rather than treating one global plausibility score as enough for runtime comparison. ([`REF-0297`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

5. **Fixed challenge families saturate under repeated exposure.**
   When AI Benchmarks Plateau argues that exposure dynamics and measurement resolution can make benchmarks lose discriminative power over time even without a single obvious design flaw. That pressures DelayBasin not to overtrust one static archive-shaped challenge suite: challenge banks may need refresh, retirement, or withheld slices. ([`REF-0298`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

6. **Many-shot prompting is real but selection-sensitive.**
   Test-Time Adaptation via Many-Shot Prompting shows that prompt-based updates are highly sensitive to ordering and selection policy. That pressures DelayBasin to ask whether a supposed runtime win came from the constitutional core or from an overfit exemplar/challenge ordering accident. ([`REF-0289`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

## Working synthesis

A useful current synthesis is:

> DelayBasin may work better when any serious self-sufficiency, family-compression, or runtime-selection claim preserves a compact **sham runtime / decoy archive / anti-self-sealing compression-test packet** naming the **candidate runtime**, the **matched sham or decoy runtime**, the **sequestered challenge suite**, the **within-family adjudication metric**, the main **relatedness / leakage risk**, and the **shrink / preserve / quarantine consequence** rather than letting the archive define the reduced object, define the judge, and then silently certify its own minimality.

This is strong enough for canon as an archive-scale rigor surface.
It is not strong enough to claim that DelayBasin already has the right sham family, the right sequestered challenge bank, or an independent judge.
The point is weaker and more operational: Priority 0 only becomes meaningful once the probe is designed to resist archive-shaped self-flattery.

## Sham runtime vs runtime triplet vs archive self-sufficiency probe vs negative-control handle

These objects are adjacent but not identical.

- A **runtime triplet / core-exemplars split / challenge-suite packet** says what kind of reduced runtime object is being tested.
- An **archive self-sufficiency probe** asks whether a bounded runtime seems sufficient at all.
- A **negative-control handle / sham packet** tests whether one local handle or witness is overcredited.
- A **sham runtime / decoy archive / anti-self-sealing compression test** asks whether the archive-scale shrink result survives a matched alternative plus partially sequestered evaluation rather than merely sounding plausible.

In practice, the runtime triplet tells DelayBasin what the candidate runtime contains.
The archive self-sufficiency probe asks whether that bounded object looks good enough.
The sham-runtime packet asks whether the apparent success survives an honestly losing comparison.
The negative-control handle remains a more local control surface inside that larger discipline.

## Countermodels / probes

1. **Sham bureaucracy countermodel**
   - Sham runtimes may only add ceremony and archive bulk.
   - Probe: require each sham-runtime packet to name the concrete continuation decision it gates and the smallest decoy that could overturn that decision.

2. **Decoy-too-dumb countermodel**
   - A weak sham can make any candidate runtime look good.
   - Probe: keep decoys matched on size, rhetorical fluency, and obvious DelayBasin keywords while differing in one proposed load-bearing factor.

3. **Judge-relatedness countermodel**
   - The archive may still choose judges or rubrics that favor its own family style.
   - Probe: preserve the relatedness / leakage risk explicitly and prefer adjudication surfaces that compare alternatives within the same challenge family rather than only global quality scores.

4. **Challenge-suite leakage countermodel**
   - A sequestered challenge suite can still become stale or archive-shaped through repeated reuse.
   - Probe: preserve refresh or retirement rules and keep at least one withheld challenge slice whose exact items are not explained by the candidate runtime.

5. **Full-archive-history-trap countermodel**
   - The full archive may win mainly because its history better traps the model in a familiar latent region, not because it preserves better continuation law.
   - Probe: compare candidate runtime, decoy runtime, and full archive on challenge items that require current-law discrimination rather than generic DelayBasin fluency.

## Transformer-facing implication

The strongest transformer-facing implication is that long-horizon archive reduction is not just a compression problem.
It is an **identification-under-self-reference** problem.
If contexts act like modular test-time programs, and if judges, exemplars, and challenges can all inherit family-level bias, then a reduced runtime can look successful for reasons that live in the surrounding evaluation apparatus rather than in the runtime itself.

That means DelayBasin's recursive method should not ask only, “what is the smallest runtime that still works?”
It should ask, “what is the smallest runtime that still beats a matched sham under a challenge bank designed to resist archive-family leakage?”
This is a sharper transformer-facing object.
It treats archive method as a user-space control-and-evaluation program interacting with a biased sequence model,
not as a neutral recall aid.

DelayBasin has not earned the stronger claim that it already needs an externally maintained shadow benchmark or cross-family red-team archive.
What it has earned is the weaker canon ratchet:
when Claude's pressure says Priority 0,
DelayBasin should hear **run the self-sufficiency probe in a way that can actually fail**.
