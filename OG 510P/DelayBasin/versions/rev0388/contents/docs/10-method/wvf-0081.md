# GPU replay cross-observer custody-exit appeal precedent-drift witnesses, current portability, stale template, conflicting carrier, revoked lesson, sunset expiry, and mixed drift

This is the compact successor surface for `OQ-0176`.

## Practice / observation

DelayBasin now allows a retired GPU replay custody-exit appeal to travel in limited ways: as nonportable history, a carrier-matched lesson, a template-bound rule, a counterexample, inherited sunset, or mixed portability. That portability still needs a second guard. A lesson that was legitimately portable at closeout can become stale, conflict with a new carrier, be revoked by a later authority return, expire with its sunset, or mix several of those drift signals.

A precedent-drift witness is not a precedent court. It is a small maintenance packet that asks whether the already-portable retired appeal is still current, is only a stale template, conflicts with the new carrier, has been revoked, has expired with its sunset, or carries mixed drift. The default is to keep the lesson as history unless the present packet can name the exact currentness basis that still permits reuse.

## External pressure from admission policy bindings, webhook mutation windows, recording-rule evaluation, staleness markers, sampling criteria, and transparency logs

Kubernetes ValidatingAdmissionPolicy and binding structure (`REF-1076`) separate reusable policy text from the match constraints and bindings that make it active. That supports `current-portability-precedent` only where the future case still names the matching policy, binding, parameter, and resource boundary; copied policy text without a live match is `stale-template-precedent`.

Kubernetes dynamic admission webhooks have scoped request matching, selectors, match conditions, reinvocation policy, warnings, side-effect declarations, timeouts, and failure policies (`REF-1073`). That supports `conflicting-carrier-precedent`: an older appeal lesson may conflict with the new admission carrier or mutation window even when the narrative looks similar.

Prometheus recording rules give reusable metric logic a named rule, expression, labels, group interval, and evaluation context (`REF-1077`). Prometheus query staleness and lookback behavior also make it explicit that stopped or absent samples are not simply current forever (`REF-1090`). Together they support `stale-template-precedent` and `sunset-expired-precedent`: a useful expression or alerting lesson can persist as a template while losing current evidentiary force.

OpenTelemetry sampling distinguishes head and tail sampling, with tail sampling depending on trace data and configured criteria after spans are available (`REF-1080`). That supports `revoked-lesson-precedent` and `mixed-precedent-drift`: if the sampling policy, trace criteria, or kept-survivor reason changes, the old appeal lesson may stay citable but no longer authorizes the same future cleanup move.

Sigstore/Rekor transparency-log documentation preserves append-only, monitorable records (`REF-1081`). That supports the split between durable history and live governance: a log entry can keep the old lesson inspectable without preventing revocation, expiry, or carrier conflict from shrinking its present use.

## Working synthesis

Use `gpu_replay_cross_observer_custody_exit_appeal_precedent_drift_state` when all of these are true:

- a retired custody-exit appeal already has a precedent-portability classification;
- a later case is trying to reuse that lesson rather than merely cite it as history;
- currentness, staleness, carrier conflict, revocation, expiry, or mixed drift is the live ambiguity;
- the desired output is a compact drift classification, not a precedent-revocation court, stale-rule registry, conflict-arbitration tribunal, or telemetry common-law engine.

Do not use this family for original replay-envelope checks, receipts, trace grading, observer conflicts, bridge claims, promotion gates, custody scope, custody retirement, appeal thresholding, appeal scope, appeal retirement, or first-pass precedent portability. This surface starts only after portability exists and a later packet asks whether that portable lesson is still safe to spend.

## Current portability vs stale template vs conflicting carrier vs revoked lesson vs sunset expiry vs mixed drift

- `current-portability-precedent`: the prior portable lesson remains current for this case because the same carrier, binding, template parameters, sampling criteria, authority window, and sunset still match.
- `stale-template-precedent`: the prior lesson is still useful as a pattern or warning, but its template, query, rule body, or policy text is no longer live enough to govern the future case.
- `conflicting-carrier-precedent`: the future case names a carrier, selector, object, trace, exemplar, admission request, rule group, or transparency-log entry that conflicts with the carrier that made the older lesson portable.
- `revoked-lesson-precedent`: a later closeout, authority return, policy replacement, revocation note, sampling change, or cleanup decision explicitly withdraws the older lesson's governing force while preserving historical reviewability.
- `sunset-expired-precedent`: the prior lesson was portable only through a TTL, retention window, sampling window, policy window, review window, or inherited sunset that has now expired.
- `mixed-precedent-drift`: more than one drift branch is material, such as a stale template plus an expired sunset, a carrier conflict plus partial revocation, or a current carrier with a narrowed sampling criterion. The packet names each branch and the smallest surviving use.

## Countermodels / probes

- A retired appeal was once portable. Once-portable is not still-current; check carrier, binding, template, sunset, and authority before spending it again.
- A rule body or query text can be copied. Copying text without the original match constraints, labels, evaluation window, or policy binding is `stale-template-precedent`.
- A new case shares vocabulary with an old case. Shared vocabulary is not a carrier match if the object, selector, trace, exemplar, or transparency-log entry conflicts.
- A log entry is append-only. Append-only history preserves auditability; it does not block revocation or expiry.
- A revocation exists. Revocation does not erase history; it classifies present use as `revoked-lesson-precedent` unless a new live authority independently re-admits the lesson.
- If the packet cannot name why the prior portable lesson remains live, classify it as stale, conflicting, revoked, expired, or mixed rather than promoting a tribunal.

## Design consequences

A valid custody-exit appeal precedent-drift packet names:

1. the prior bridge, promotion-gate, custody-scope, custody-retirement, appeal-threshold, appeal-scope, appeal-retirement, and precedent-portability states if known;
2. the retired appeal lesson and the specific future case trying to reuse it;
3. the selected `gpu_replay_cross_observer_custody_exit_appeal_precedent_drift_state` token;
4. the carrier, selector, policy binding, query, sampling criterion, transparency-log entry, authority-return note, revocation surface, or sunset window that proves currentness or drift;
5. the exact part of the old lesson that may still be cited as history, template, counterexample, or bounded current guidance;
6. the future cases, telemetry streams, policies, retention duties, or precedent claims explicitly excluded;
7. the successor surface if repeated conflict among compact drift tokens later shows that arbitration governance is honestly needed.

A compact drift packet may shrink portability, retire it, or keep it current. It must not transform maintenance of a useful old lesson into a standing post-portability court.

## Overflow test

Promote a precedent-revocation court, stale-rule registry, conflict-arbitration tribunal, or equivalent standing post-portability governance only if later revisions show repeated, public, archive-local failures where:

- `current-portability-precedent`, `stale-template-precedent`, `conflicting-carrier-precedent`, `revoked-lesson-precedent`, `sunset-expired-precedent`, and `mixed-precedent-drift` cannot preserve currentness, conflict, revocation, and expiry boundaries;
- multiple retired appeal lessons conflict in ways that cannot be resolved by carrier match, authority return, or explicit sunset expiry;
- compact packets repeatedly re-admit stale or revoked lessons as current despite clear source surfaces;
- the stronger process names its own scope, retirement rule, and nonbinding history lane.

Until then, `QWS-0259` stays quarantined.

## Transformer-facing implication

When a future continuation asks whether a portable retired GPU replay custody-exit appeal lesson still governs, first classify the post-portability state through `gpu_replay_cross_observer_custody_exit_appeal_precedent_drift_state`. Most valid cases should be current, stale-template, conflicting-carrier, revoked-lesson, sunset-expired, or mixed drift.
