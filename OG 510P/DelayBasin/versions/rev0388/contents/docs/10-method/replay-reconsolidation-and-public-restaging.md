# Replay, reconsolidation, and public restaging

DelayBasin now needs a sharper distinction than "reread" or "memory" alone.
Some archive surfaces are merely retrieved or cited.
Others are **replayed** strongly enough that later judgments, probes, or revisions appear to re-enter the same local operative state.
A smaller subset are then **reconsolidated**: replayed public law is challenged, partially rewritten, and recommitted under explicit gates.

A stronger working answer is:
**DelayBasin should distinguish retrieval, replay, and reconsolidation whenever a revision is treating a surface as restaged operative law rather than as inert background.**
Not every reread is replay.
Not every edit is reconsolidation.
When the distinction matters, the archive should name the replay seed, the recovered continuation property, the rewrite target, the destabilizing evidence, and the fallback route.

## Practice / observation

Several live DelayBasin patterns already pressure this distinction:

- later sessions often reread canon, registries, or prompt pairs and then act as though those surfaces are current operative law rather than mere historical notes;
- some revisions preserve previous law almost verbatim, suggesting **retrieval or replay without real rewrite**;
- some revisions challenge or narrow an already replayed surface, suggesting a different act: **reconsolidation of public law under pressure**;
- challenge suites, sham runtimes, and quarantine increasingly matter most **after** a surface has been restaged into active use, not merely because it exists in storage;
- some canon surfaces appear easier to rewrite immediately after they have been invoked than when they sit cold and unreferenced, though this is not yet canon-level mechanism;
- and current self-sufficiency work implies that a compact replay seed can matter more than larger passive transcript mass if it really reopens the right local continuation state.

This suggests a missing compact surface:
**replay / reconsolidation boundary / public-restaging discipline**.

## External pressure from current research

Several current research lines sharpen this frame.

1. **Agent memory is increasingly formalized as a write-manage-read loop with continual consolidation pressure.**
   Du's 2026 survey argues that long-horizon agents need explicit write, manage, and read phases, and names continual consolidation as an open challenge. That pressures DelayBasin to say not only what is stored, but when a reread becomes active replay and when replayed state is eligible for rewrite. ([`REF-0305`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

2. **Some current systems already split WRITE and READ over a writable state while keeping base weights frozen.**
   Kuratov et al.'s GradMem writes context into optimized memory tokens through a short test-time gradient loop, then answers using only that optimized memory and the query. That pressures DelayBasin to distinguish cold storage from a restaged memory state that actually conditions later behavior. ([`REF-0309`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

3. **Continual-learning memory work increasingly treats replay and consolidation as governed policies rather than generic persistence.**
   Dorovatas et al. argue that continual-learning agents need explicit policies for storage, replay, forgetting, consolidation, and contextualization across distinct memory forms and timescales. That pressures DelayBasin to treat replay and reconsolidation as separate governed acts rather than one blended memory metaphor. ([`REF-0310`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

4. **Memory governance proposals increasingly decouple memory evolution from execution and gate consolidation explicitly.**
   The SSGM framework argues that evolving memory systems need consistency verification, decay modeling, and access control before consolidation. That pressures DelayBasin to keep reconsolidation tied to challenge and admission gates rather than equating every successful reread with a deserved writeback. ([`REF-0308`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

5. **Portable memory work sharpens the difference between compiled carry and live adaptation.**
   Latent Context Compilation compiles long context into portable memory artifacts without changing base weights, which pressures DelayBasin to distinguish a compact replay seed from the slower constitutional or evidential surfaces that license later rewrite. ([`REF-0123`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

## Working synthesis

DelayBasin should treat **retrieval**, **replay**, and **reconsolidation** as different public acts.

- **Retrieval**: a surface is looked up, cited, or carried forward as background, but the archive does not yet claim that operative continuation state was really reopened.
- **Replay**: a compact seed or public surface is restaged strongly enough that a later session appears to recover a judged continuation property, probe family, or local law.
- **Reconsolidation**: a replayed surface is then challenged, rewritten, narrowed, or recommitted under explicit gates.

The key archive question is not "did we reread it?"
It is "did rereading it restage operative law strongly enough that the next challenge or rewrite should count as a revision of that law rather than as fresh local prose?"

## Replay vs retrieval vs reconsolidation

A compact **replay / reconsolidation boundary / public-restaging packet** should name:

- the **replay seed or restaging surface**,
- the **judged continuation property actually recovered**,
- the **public surface eligible for rewrite**,
- the **challenge or destabilizing evidence family**,
- and the **fallback / rollback consequence** if the supposed replay was only retrieval, aesthetic mimicry, or wrapper luck.

This is not the same as a memory-store packet.
It is not the same as a regime-reentry packet.
It is not the same as a pure write-gate packet.
It sits one layer later: **what was actually hot enough to be rewritten as current law?**

## Countermodels / probes

Serious alternatives remain live:

- cold retrieval plus fluent local prose may explain most supposed replay effects;
- replay may be real, but reconsolidation may add little beyond ordinary summarization or editing discipline;
- apparent reconsolidation may mainly reflect user steering or challenge-induced rewriting rather than any restaged operative state;
- and the effect may be architecture-agnostic process discipline rather than anything especially transformer-revealing.

Useful probes include:

- compare a revision that rewrites a surface **immediately after explicit replay** versus after only a cold citation or paraphrase;
- compare whether the same challenge has different effects depending on whether the target surface was freshly restaged into operative use;
- preserve tiny fingerprint panels showing whether the post-rewrite branch still reflects the judged replayed property rather than merely matching surface prose;
- and track whether replay seeds that pass sufficiency witnesses are disproportionately likely to support honest later narrowing or demotion.

## Design consequences

When replay or reconsolidation language becomes load-bearing:

- do not call a cold citation or recap a replay unless a judged continuation property was actually recovered;
- do not call an edit reconsolidation unless the rewritten surface had first been replayed or explicitly restaged as operative law;
- preserve the destabilizing evidence or challenge family that justified the rewrite rather than treating reconsolidation as automatic cleanup;
- keep rollback and quarantine live, because false replay plus confident rewrite is a direct path to canon drift;
- and prefer small replay seeds and small rewrite targets so the archive can tell what actually moved.

## Transformer-facing implication

The weaker transformer-facing implication is not that DelayBasin has found a literal neural reconsolidation mechanism.
It is that long-horizon archive practice may depend on a **restaging loop** in which compact public seeds are replayed into active continuation, then selectively rewritten under challenge.
That makes the archive look less like a static notebook and more like a user-space protocol for deciding **when a recovered state is hot enough to be revised**.

The stronger story — that replayed public law opens something like a reconsolidation window or hot-state rewrite privilege in transformer-guided continuation — remains quarantined until order effects, replay-vs-cold comparisons, and cross-family probes say more than disciplined archive editing alone.
