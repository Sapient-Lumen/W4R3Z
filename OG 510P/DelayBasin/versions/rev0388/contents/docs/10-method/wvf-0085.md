# GPU replay cross-observer custody-exit appeal precedent tie-break registry-scope witnesses, packet-local, carrier-bound, authority-limited, nonbinding-history, retirement-window, and mixed scope

This is the compact successor surface for `OQ-0180`.

## Practice / observation

DelayBasin now has compact states for appeal precedent portability, drift, conflict arbitration, arbitration retirement, and tie-break governance-threshold pressure. The live question has narrowed again: if compact thresholding ever overflows and a promoted tie-break registry or arbitration-closeout surface is honestly being considered, what is that surface allowed to hold?

This surface adds one registry-scope witness. It does not create registry operators, global priority, a standing court, or a live precedence ladder. It classifies whether a promoted registry remains packet-local, carrier-bound, authority-limited, nonbinding history, retirement-window-bound, or mixed. The point is to keep even promoted governance from inheriting universal scope by accident.

## External pressure from audit scope, owner boundaries, admission bindings, rule windows, sampling carriers, and transparency history

Kubernetes audit policy and audit configuration separate policy rules, stages, levels, and backends (`REF-1071`, `REF-1072`). That supports `nonbinding-history-tiebreak-registry-scope`: retained closeout facts can be findable without making every retained record live priority.

Kubernetes owner references, garbage collection, and finalizers separate cleanup ownership from deletion interlocks (`REF-1068`, `REF-1070`). That supports `authority-limited-tiebreak-registry-scope`: even a promoted registry must name the cleanup, retention, policy, or ownership authority it may bind and the authorities it may not override.

Kubernetes dynamic admission and ValidatingAdmissionPolicy keep decisions tied to request matching, bindings, selectors, validation actions, failure policy, and timeouts (`REF-1073`, `REF-1076`). That supports `packet-local-tiebreak-registry-scope` and `carrier-bound-tiebreak-registry-scope`: a registry inherits no authority outside the request class, binding, selector, carrier, or object family that earned promotion.

Prometheus recording rules, alerting rules, `for` and `keep_firing_for` windows, and staleness semantics bound metric-derived conclusions to evaluation and data-availability windows (`REF-1077`, `REF-1078`, `REF-1090`). That supports `retirement-window-tiebreak-registry-scope`: a registry needs a named sunset, stale-data rule, or review window before old priority can continue.

OpenTelemetry sampling and tail-sampling processors separate trace-level criteria, collector policy, and stateful trace grouping (`REF-1080`, `REF-1074`). That supports `carrier-bound-tiebreak-registry-scope`: scope cannot silently jump from one trace carrier, collector policy window, or sampling basis to all telemetry.

Sigstore/Rekor transparency-log documentation preserves append-only monitorable history (`REF-1081`). That supports the nonbinding-history lane: durable public history can be inspectable evidence without becoming a default policy rule or court decision.

## Packet-local vs carrier-bound vs authority-limited vs nonbinding-history vs retirement-window vs mixed registry scope

- `packet-local-tiebreak-registry-scope`: the promoted surface may hold only the named closeout packet, conflict instance, appeal residue, or replay-custody case and cannot answer future cases by default.
- `carrier-bound-tiebreak-registry-scope`: the promoted surface is bound to one evidence carrier, trace carrier, metric series family, admission binding, cleanup object lineage, transparency-log entry family, or retention window.
- `authority-limited-tiebreak-registry-scope`: the promoted surface may bind only named cleanup, retention, policy, appeal, or ownership authorities and explicitly cannot override others.
- `nonbinding-history-tiebreak-registry-scope`: the promoted surface is a findable history or audit index only; future packets may cite it, but it carries no live priority unless a later compact witness re-earns portability.
- `retirement-window-tiebreak-registry-scope`: the promoted surface has a named TTL, review date, policy sunset, staleness rule, or closeout condition that shrinks or retires the registry.
- `mixed-tiebreak-registry-scope`: more than one scope limiter is material, such as carrier-bound plus authority-limited, nonbinding-history plus retirement-window, or packet-local plus audit-retention history.

## Countermodels / probes

- A registry has many entries. Size is not scope; test whether the entries share one packet, carrier, authority, nonbinding-history lane, or retirement window.
- An audit backend keeps the record. Retention is not priority; classify it as nonbinding history unless a separate authority-limited rule is named.
- A finalizer or owner reference blocks deletion. An interlock is not a registry operator; name the authority boundary before allowing registry scope.
- A recording rule or alert keeps firing. A live metric window is not immortal precedence; require a retirement-window scope or fail closed.
- A sampled trace carries an old tie-break. Carrier continuity is not global telemetry authority; keep the registry carrier-bound unless mixed scope is named.
- A transparency log preserves the old decision. Append-only history is not a closeout court; it supports inspectability, not default binding precedent.

## Design consequences

A valid registry-scope packet names:

1. the prior governance-threshold packet and overflow reason;
2. the proposed registry-like surface, if any, and why a compact threshold token no longer suffices;
3. the selected `gpu_replay_cross_observer_custody_exit_appeal_precedent_tiebreak_registry_scope_state` token;
4. included packets, carriers, authorities, history lanes, and retirement windows;
5. excluded packets, future cases, telemetry families, admission bindings, metric series, cleanup objects, authorities, and retention duties;
6. the nonbinding-history rule unless live priority is explicitly and narrowly earned;
7. the successor or retirement surface if the scoped registry later needs closeout rather than continued operation.

A compact registry-scope packet may preserve an index, a scoped history lane, or a bounded registry charter. It must not create a registry operator by default, global precedence, a policy court, a telemetry common-law engine, or immortal priority.

## Overflow test

Promote a global priority registry, registry-operator court, immortal precedent ledger, universal arbitration-closeout court, or equivalent standing governance only if later revisions show repeated, public, archive-local failures where:

- `packet-local-tiebreak-registry-scope`, `carrier-bound-tiebreak-registry-scope`, `authority-limited-tiebreak-registry-scope`, `nonbinding-history-tiebreak-registry-scope`, `retirement-window-tiebreak-registry-scope`, and `mixed-tiebreak-registry-scope` cannot bound the promoted surface;
- the promoted registry repeatedly exceeds packet, carrier, authority, history, or retirement-window limits despite explicit exclusions;
- a stronger process names its operator authority, appeal path, deletion/retention rule, audit boundary, precedence rule, and retirement witness.

Until then, `QWS-0263` stays quarantined.
