## Practice / observation

DelayBasin now has compact states for post-closeout portability, portability drift, conflict arbitration, arbitration retirement, and standing-governance thresholds for closed-reopen residue. The live seam in `rev0295` was not merely whether a drift-retirement court should exist. It was that the archive named `OQ-0190` as the successor without instantiating the registry question. This surface turns that named successor into a bounded scope witness.

The tempting move is to let threshold overflow create a standing residue-drift retirement governance surface with whatever scope the next operator finds convenient. This witness does less. It classifies any honestly promoted standing governance as packet-local, carrier-bound, authority-limited, nonbinding-history-only, retirement-window-bound, or mixed. It does not create global closeout authority, revival-history custody by default, a drift-retirement operator court, or a permanent revival ledger.

## External pressure from audit scope, owner boundaries, admission bindings, rule windows, sampling carriers, and transparency history

Kubernetes audit policy and audit configuration separate policy rules, stages, levels, backends, and retained events (`REF-1071`, `REF-1072`). That supports `nonbinding-history-reopen-drift-governance-scope`: retained facts can stay inspectable without granting live governance to every old retirement trace.

Kubernetes owner references, garbage collection, and finalizers separate cleanup ownership from deletion interlocks (`REF-1068`, `REF-1070`). That supports `authority-limited-reopen-drift-governance-scope`: a scoped standing surface must name which cleanup, retention, policy, appeal, or ownership authority it may bind and which authorities it cannot override.

Kubernetes dynamic admission control and ValidatingAdmissionPolicy tie effect to match conditions, parameter binding, validation action, failure policy, request context, and timeout (`REF-1073`, `REF-1076`). That supports `packet-local-reopen-drift-governance-scope` and `carrier-bound-reopen-drift-governance-scope`: even promoted governance stays attached to the request class, binding, selector, carrier, or object family that earned overflow.

Prometheus recording rules, alerting windows, `for` and `keep_firing_for` semantics, staleness behavior, tombstone cleanup, and delete-series effects bind metric-derived conclusions to evaluation intervals, stale markers, and retention horizons (`REF-1069`, `REF-1077`, `REF-1078`, `REF-1090`). That supports `retirement-window-reopen-drift-governance-scope`: promoted governance needs a named TTL, review date, policy sunset, staleness rule, tombstone lifetime, or closeout condition.

OpenTelemetry sampling and tail-sampling processors keep trace evidence tied to collector policy, trace grouping, and sampling criteria (`REF-1080`, `REF-1074`). That supports carrier-bounded scope and mixed scope when a governance surface depends on trace carriers, collector windows, or sampling bases that cannot generalize to all telemetry.

Sigstore/Rekor transparency-log documentation preserves append-only monitorable history (`REF-1081`). That supports the nonbinding-history lane: durable visibility may prevent silent history loss while still refusing to turn every visible record into live precedent.

## Packet-local vs carrier-bound vs authority-limited vs nonbinding-history vs retirement-window vs mixed governance scope

- `packet-local-reopen-drift-governance-scope`: the promoted surface may hold only the named reopened-residue closeout, drift conflict, retirement class, or packet-local failure that earned threshold overflow.
- `carrier-bound-reopen-drift-governance-scope`: the promoted surface is bound to one evidence carrier, trace carrier, metric family, admission binding, cleanup object lineage, transparency-log family, retention lane, or tombstone family.
- `authority-limited-reopen-drift-governance-scope`: the promoted surface may bind only named cleanup, retention, policy, appeal, packet, or ownership authorities and explicitly cannot override others.
- `nonbinding-history-reopen-drift-governance-scope`: the promoted surface is an inspectable history or audit index only; future packets may cite it, but it carries no live priority unless a later compact witness re-earns current effect.
- `retirement-window-reopen-drift-governance-scope`: the promoted surface has a named TTL, review date, policy sunset, staleness rule, tombstone lifetime, sampling-policy window, or closeout condition that shrinks or retires governance.
- `mixed-reopen-drift-governance-scope`: more than one scope limiter is material, such as carrier-bound plus authority-limited, nonbinding-history plus retirement-window, or packet-local plus audit-retention history.

## Countermodels / probes

- Threshold overflow was named. Overflow is not global authority; require packet, carrier, authority, history, or retirement-window scope before allowing a standing surface.
- A revival-history board would be useful. Usefulness is not scope; classify it as nonbinding history unless current authority and excluded future cases are named.
- An audit backend or transparency log keeps all records. Retention is not governance; classify visibility as nonbinding unless a current packet earns live effect.
- A finalizer, owner reference, or policy owner blocks closeout. Interlock is not operator-court authority; name the authority-limited lane before allowing governance to bind anything.
- A recording rule, alert window, stale marker, tombstone, or trace sampler keeps a lesson visible. Carrier continuity is not universal scope; keep governance carrier-bound or retirement-window-bound.
- If scope cannot be named, fall back to the threshold witness, quarantine the standing-governance pressure, or use `mixed-reopen-drift-governance-scope` with explicit unresolved limits rather than silently promoting global governance.

## Design consequences

A valid governance-scope packet names:

1. the prior portability, drift, conflict-arbitration, arbitration-retirement, and standing-governance-threshold packets;
2. the threshold overflow reason and the compact repairs tried before considering standing governance;
3. the selected `gpu_replay_cross_observer_custody_exit_appeal_precedent_tiebreak_registry_reopened_residue_post_closeout_portability_drift_conflict_arbitration_retirement_governance_scope_state` token;
4. included packets, carriers, authorities, nonbinding-history lanes, and retirement windows;
5. excluded future packets, audit rows, transparency-log records, tombstone families, metric series, trace carriers, admission bindings, cleanup objects, policies, and authorities;
6. the nonbinding-history rule unless live priority is explicitly and narrowly earned;
7. the successor or retirement surface if scoped governance later needs closeout rather than continued operation.

A compact governance-scope packet may preserve a scoped charter, boundary digest, nonbinding-history lane, or retirement window. It must not create global drift-retirement court authority, revival-history custody by default, an operator court, deletion court, telemetry common-law engine, or immortal priority ledger.

## Overflow test

Promote global residue-drift retirement governance, a revival-history custody board, a drift-retirement operator court, an immortal closeout ledger, or equivalent standing governance only if later revisions show repeated, public, archive-local failures where:

- `packet-local-reopen-drift-governance-scope`, `carrier-bound-reopen-drift-governance-scope`, `authority-limited-reopen-drift-governance-scope`, `nonbinding-history-reopen-drift-governance-scope`, `retirement-window-reopen-drift-governance-scope`, and `mixed-reopen-drift-governance-scope` cannot bound the promoted surface;
- the promoted surface repeatedly exceeds packet, carrier, authority, history, or retirement-window limits despite explicit exclusions;
- a stronger process names its operator authority, appeal path, deletion/retention rule, audit boundary, precedence rule, and retirement witness.

Until then, `QWS-0273` stays quarantined.
