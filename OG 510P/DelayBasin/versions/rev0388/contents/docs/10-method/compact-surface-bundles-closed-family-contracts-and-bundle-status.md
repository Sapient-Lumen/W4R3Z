# Compact-surface bundles, closed-family contracts, and bundle status

## Practice / observation

DelayBasin now has a real cluster of compact operator-facing derivative surfaces:
- `context-pack.json`,
- `frontier-ticket.json`,
- `innovation-packet.json`,
- `VALIDATION-INDEX.json`,
- `REENTRY-CONTRACT.json`,
- and `REENTRY-SURFACE-CONFORMANCE.json`.

That is good news.
The archive is no longer pretending that only prose startup cues exist.
But it creates one smaller usability and honesty problem:
**the current compact family is still mostly recoverable from scattered mentions rather than one closed truthful family card.**

A future careful pass can therefore land honestly and still have to reconstruct:
- which compact surfaces actually belong to the current operator-facing family,
- what each one is for,
- what stronger underlier each one answers to,
- and when a new compact helper would need same-revision attachment rather than just being dropped beside the others.

## Pressure from neighboring datacubes

Several neighboring datacubes apply the same pressure from different angles.

`SlopOS-rev0616` keeps a closed family contract and family index when one implementable family spans enough members that later operators could lose its shape by opening only one or two docs.
`Radical-Governance-rev0535` presses bundle honesty: once several interdependent upgrades matter together, the archive should say which bundle elements are present, borrowed, or still absent instead of letting one impressive label stand in for the bundle.
`Hyperepo-rev0290` keeps porch-visible witness bundles explicit enough that later sessions can see what the visible compact layer actually contains.
`Micromax-rev0523` keeps tiny operator-facing surfaces behaviorally truthful about what they are and are not.
`Anonymity-rev0547` keeps minimal verifier cards and worked-route handoffs explicit enough that a terse later surface can still answer which owner or route really governs the compact card.

The common pressure is not “import a bundle board.”
The common pressure is:
**once a compact derivative family becomes real, make its membership and status explicit before labels, familiarity, or startup prose start carrying the load ambiently.**

## Working synthesis

DelayBasin should import only the smallest bounded take:

- keep one compact `compact-surface-bundle.json` as a **closed-family card** for the current operator-facing derivative surfaces,
- define one inclusion rule for what counts as a member,
- define one attachment rule for when a new compact derivative surface must be added in the same revision rather than left ambient,
- name the exact current members, their local roles, their stronger governing underliers, and their generator or authored status,
- and keep the whole surface explicitly derivative rather than constitutional.

The point is not to replace `START_HERE.md`, `REVISION-RECEIPT.json`, `SURFACE-STATUS.json`, or `RELEASE-MANIFEST.json`.
The point is to close one remaining recovery gap:

> a future careful pass knows that several compact derivative aids exist, but still has to reconstruct the family boundary, the member roles, and the attachment rule from scattered startup prose and recent memory.

## Bundle status vs bundle board

This note needs a sharp boundary.

- **Compact-surface bundle** means a small closed family card naming which compact derivative surfaces currently belong together.
- **Bundle status** means saying which members are present and what stronger surfaces govern them.
- **Attachment rule** means saying when a new compact derivative aid must be attached here in the same revision.
- **A bundle board** would classify many future compact surfaces, govern promotions, or turn one family card into a standing controller.

DelayBasin has earned the first three.
It has not earned the fourth.

## Design consequences

If DelayBasin keeps a compact-surface bundle, it should preserve:

- the **derivative status / explicit non-authority note**,
- the **family purpose / why this card exists at all**,
- the **inclusion rule / what counts as a member**,
- the **attachment rule / when a new compact derivative surface must be same-revision attached here**,
- the **non-members / what stronger surfaces still outrank the bundle**,
- the **bundle status / present members, absent borrowed members if any, and the next upgrade trigger**,
- the **member rows / exact surface, local role, governing underliers, and generator or authored posture**,
- and the **reentry hooks / where to reopen canon before treating the family card as stronger than a derivative discovery aid**.

This keeps the family closed and enumerable.
If the family card and its members disagree, the card should fail closed and be regenerated.

## Countermodels / probes

The obvious failure modes are:

- the family card merely recopies startup prose without clarifying the family boundary,
- the archive starts treating bundle membership itself as a new source of authority,
- a new compact helper gets added without same-revision attachment and the bundle card quietly goes stale,
- or the bundle status grows into a porch board, registry court, or standing controller for every future helper surface.

Those are reasons to narrow or retire the card.
They are not reasons to keep the family boundary ambient once the family already exists.

## Transformer-facing implication

If DelayBasin is partly functioning as a bounded continuation substrate, then continuity quality may depend not only on preserving compact derivative aids one by one, but also on preserving one tiny public answer to:
**which compact aids currently belong to the same operator-facing family, and what stronger surfaces still outrank them?**

The weaker implication is not that DelayBasin has discovered a porch operating system.
It is this:

**once a compact derivative family becomes real, one truthful family-status card may reduce reentry scan cost and same-revision attachment drift without pretending that the bundle card itself is the constitution.**
