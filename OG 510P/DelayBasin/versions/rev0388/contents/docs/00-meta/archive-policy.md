# Archive policy

## Purpose

Keep the archive small enough to survive, and structured enough to re-enter.

## Rules

1. Prefer summaries, ids, and links over long quotation.
2. Preserve seed ideas, but compress them once they are wired into stable surfaces.
3. Favor a bounded constitutional state over recap sprawl: preserve the operator grammar, active claims, open disputes, must-read surfaces, and any load-bearing control lexicon needed for re-entry.
4. Preserve portable state interface surfaces: compact state, stable ids / dereference points, and deterministic acceptance checks.
5. Preserve typed continuation surfaces when they exist: workflow requests, bounded state/resources, and deterministic checks/tools should not be collapsed into one recap blob without reason.
6. Preserve a split between certified core vocabulary and provisional/private handles when local control language becomes load-bearing.
7. New docs require:
   - a link from `docs/README.md`, and
   - attachment to a registry, trajectory map, or runbook.
8. If a concept is repeated in multiple docs, reduce duplication and point to one canonical location.
9. If a concept is unresolved, keep it in an open-question surface rather than smearing it across prose.
10. If external research matters, research online, cite it in `docs/00-meta/bibliography.md`, and summarize only the load-bearing point.
11. Do not keep PDFs or other large non-crucial artifacts in long-term releases; temporary scratch should live in a clearly quarantined lane and usually be removed before packaging.
12. Keep release-hygiene lint honest: packaged repo state should be compact enough that a future session can reopen it without hauling irrelevant binary weight.
13. Keep status labels explicit: `observed`, `inferred`, `speculative`, `adversarial-countermodel`.
14. If a canon-level claim is highly exposed to fast external drift, preserve a compact temporal-validity posture and review horizon rather than letting it persist indefinitely by inertia.
15. Preserve a small recovery kernel and explicit recovery route if the archive claims it can regain legitimate continuation after drift or corruption. Recovery should be compact and inspectable, not mystical.
16. If revision receipts exist, preserve a compact per-revision audit object rather than letting progress remain only a changelog mood.
17. If a substantial revision had a real nearby rejected alternative, preserve a tiny counterfactual shadow rather than letting the winning prose erase the local decision boundary.

## Release discipline

Each packaged release should include:
- changelog,
- docs index,
- context pack,
- release manifest,
- latest prompt-pair surface,
- `REVISION-RECEIPT.json`,
- and a predictable bundle name.

## Procedural admission

When a revision claims advancement, it should preserve the **move class** that made it admissible: research/cite, refine/compress, quarantine, promote, recertify, or package/handoff. The archive is not only preserving what it believes; it is preserving what kinds of transitions it recognizes as honest progress.


## Promotion discipline

Compactness does not excuse trust laundering. When a revision changes canon-level status, preserve a compact promotion contract or demotion reason rather than letting repeated prose imply ratification.


## Decay discipline

Promotion is only half of temporal honesty.
Where a canon-level surface is materially dependent on current literature, ecosystem state, or unstable mechanism synthesis, the archive should preserve a compact **decay watch** entry so future sessions know what deserves re-review or demotion.


## Recovery discipline

When legitimacy is in doubt, recovery comes before novelty. Reopen the recovery kernel, regenerate bounded state, run deterministic checks, and quarantine or demote ambiguous material before continuing.


## Receipt discipline

When a revision changes canon, quarantine, move-state, or release state, preserve a compact `REVISION-RECEIPT.json` object. It should be small enough to inspect in one glance and structured enough to tell later sessions why the revision counted.

## Counterfactual discipline

When a substantial revision had a real nearby rejected move, preserve one compact counterfactual shadow inside the revision receipt. Keep it small: the nearest rejected move, why it was not admitted, and whether it remains live elsewhere.
