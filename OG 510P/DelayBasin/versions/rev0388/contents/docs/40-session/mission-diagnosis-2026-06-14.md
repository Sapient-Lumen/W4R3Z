# Mission diagnosis: Priority-0 self-sufficiency before more archive-economy machinery

Status: `rev0359` mission audit memo. This is a reading and pressure surface, not a new court, not a receipt replacement, and not proof that the archive is minimal.

## Heart of the mission

DelayBasin is trying to preserve a durable continuation regime for human--LLM co-construction across resets, model changes, archive growth, and operator fatigue. The heart is not a memory store, a RAG index, or a theory notebook. It is a public state-and-method kernel that lets a future operator or model re-enter the same project without relying on private scratchpad, hidden wrapper state, social trust, or fluent house style.

The core move is typed continuity: separate practice, observation, mechanism, speculation, disagreement, quarantine, resolution, and successor work so the archive can keep learning without mistaking its own paperwork for truth. The strongest form of the mission is to discover the smallest public support core that still reopens the same live tensions and safe next moves.

## What is missing

The missing object is the actual Priority-0 assay. Existing surfaces already describe minimal core replay, sham runtimes, decoy archives, challenge escrow, rotating holdouts, and future slices. But the archive mostly validates that those ideas are represented and internally current. It does not yet force a fresh outside challenge where a minimal core, a full archive, a no-archive baseline, and a sham/decoy runtime compete on the same tasks with operator-cost and abstention measures.

That means the present archive can pass many green checks while still failing the mission test: can a future model recover what matters, reject plausible but wrong continuations, and make the next revision without importing a review court or writing yet another contract family?

## What has gone wrong or become wasteful

The severe failure is not a corrupt ZIP. The rev0358 tree was lint-clean. The waste is subtler: validation surface area has outpaced external challenge evidence. The archive has become very good at proving its own surface currentness, ledgers, receipts, and package hygiene while still postponing the assay that would show which mass is load-bearing.

That creates three correctable failure modes.

1. Internal consistency can masquerade as mission success. `make lint` proves many local contracts but does not prove that DelayBasin can re-enter from a bounded core under fresh challenge.
2. Archive-economy work can become a self-licking loop. Receipt coldstores, ledger coldstores, generated manifests, and currentness guards are useful; they are not the same as knowing whether the archive makes better continuations.
3. The live frontier can drift into local hygiene because local hygiene is tractable. `OQ-0250` asks whether to segment receipt coldstore payloads. That is a real engineering question, but the mission diagnosis says it should not outrank the self-sufficiency/sham-core challenge loop unless the monolithic coldstore causes a demonstrated operational failure.

## OQ-0250 decision

`OQ-0250` is resolved by refusing to segment receipt history now. Keep the monolithic receipt coldstore because it is exact, current-key-excluding, mutation-guarded, and already cheaper than hot receipt mass. Do not add sharding, routing, or deletion-like machinery until a real reentry or self-sufficiency trial shows that the coldstore's opacity is causing a specific failure that smaller segments would prevent.

This is a non-action resolution: preserve exact restoration, keep the current controls hot, and move the next risk burn to the larger missing assay.

## OQ-0251 target

`OQ-0251` should ask when DelayBasin must run Priority-0 self-sufficiency and sham-core assays before adding more archive-economy machinery. A first admissible assay should include at least:

- a no-archive/null baseline;
- a landing/current-receipt packet;
- a compact constitutional core;
- the full archive;
- a matched sham or decoy archive;
- fresh or escrowed tasks that include current-head identification, live-question routing, contradiction/conflict handling, abstention, and one real revision proposal;
- operator-cost measures such as surfaces read, elapsed operator steps, and confusion points.

Passing should not mean producing DelayBasin-shaped prose. Passing should mean identifying the live state, avoiding forbidden authority layers, making a bounded next move, and saying when the evidence is insufficient.

## External pressure imported as pressure only

Long-context evaluation warns against assuming that a larger prompt automatically gives robust access to all relevant details; long-term memory benchmarks sharpen the need to test retrieval, temporal reasoning, updates, abstention, and conflict resolution; memory-system work suggests hierarchical and dynamically organized external memory can help; contamination/dynamic-benchmark work warns that public/static tests can become learned exams. These do not verify DelayBasin. They pressure DelayBasin to run fresh, adversarial, and renewable self-sufficiency trials instead of treating archive mass or currentness checks as proof.

## Cloudtainer correction loop

The cloudtainer can correct the waste over time by making each package turn pay for itself in challenge information:

1. Before adding a new witness family, ask which existing surface it retires, compacts, or falsifies.
2. Route more turns to ablation, decoy, and held-out challenge rows than to new ledgers.
3. Maintain a hot-surface budget: if a new prose/control surface enters canon, retire or compact another unless it directly supports the external assay.
4. Track operator cost alongside byte count: how many surfaces and minutes does faithful reentry require?
5. Treat `CURRENT-RECEIPT.json`, `context-pack.json`, and `frontier-ticket.json` as aids, not canon; if they become sufficient, prove that by assay rather than by slogan.

It closes a local receipt-segmentation question and re-centers the next frontier on the assay the archive has already named as Priority 0.
