# Backaction witnesses, diagnostic probes, and non-demolition budgets

DelayBasin now has a sharper missing question:
**when does a probe that is supposed to reveal the state also change it enough that the archive can no longer pretend it merely measured?**

A stronger working answer is:
**some archive probes need an explicit backaction witness before their outputs can be used as clean evidence.**
Not every explanation request, contrastive prompt, trace injection, or diagnostic perturbation is a neutral readout.
Sometimes the archive has to say how much actuation it is willing to tolerate before a “measurement” stops counting as non-demolition.

## Practice / observation

Several recent DelayBasin surfaces already imply a missing backaction discipline:
- excitation witnesses say what active variation is required to break an alias, but not yet whether that very variation has pushed the continuation state so far that the observed split cannot be read as diagnosis alone;
- hysteresis witnesses preserve rival histories and one future separating probe, but not yet whether the chosen probe partly manufactures the divergence it is later asked to explain;
- observer-actuator splits say observer surfaces should not self-certify actuation, but not yet what public budget keeps a probe close enough to diagnostic rather than silently becoming control;
- execution witnesses track substrate/runtime perturbations, but not yet the smaller textual case where a contrastive explanation request, partial-trace injection, or steering-style prompt rewrite already changes the local continuation geometry;
- negative-control and sham packets catch placebo handles, but not yet whether the “real” diagnostic probe has simply become a stronger intervention channel than the archive admitted;
- and continuation monitors can flag drift after the fact, but they do not yet force the archive to name the tolerated probe-induced distortion up front.

This suggests a missing compact surface:
**backaction witness / diagnostic probe family / non-demolition budget**.

## External pressure from current research

Several outside lines of work sharpen this frame.

1. **Reasoning-trace probes are interventions on the trajectory, not passive windows into it.**
   DRTC uses receiver-side interventions that block information from earlier trace chunks only at selected pivots and measures how those interventions redirect later log-probability trajectories and curvature signatures. That pressures DelayBasin to say when a diagnostic move is already steering the rollout whose structure it then interprets. ([`REF-0392`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

2. **Some diagnostic prompts are explicitly contrast agents rather than neutral reports.**
   Anatomy of a Lie treats chain-of-thought externalization as a diagnostic contrast agent that makes an implicit trajectory observable. That pressures DelayBasin to preserve when an archive probe is intentionally state-revealing by perturbation instead of pretending the extra textual scaffold was inert. ([`REF-0393`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

3. **Disturbance detection can be real, but naive readouts are easily confounded.**
   Detecting the Disturbance shows that binary perturbation-detection setups can be faked by global logit shifts, while more differential sensitivity survives only in some layers and tasks. That pressures DelayBasin to require sham or matched-control baselines before reading a probe response as evidence of clean observability. ([`REF-0394`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

4. **An intervention can become an observable channel in its own right.**
   Steering Awareness shows that models can be trained to detect activation steering and that detection is dissociable from resistance. That pressures DelayBasin not to assume that a probe remains hidden or semantically transparent once the system can notice that it is being manipulated. ([`REF-0395`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

5. **Multi-turn dialogue already behaves like structured state evolution.**
   STAR treats dialogue history as a state transition operator and shows rapid, reproducible trajectory collapse under structured multi-turn interaction. That pressures DelayBasin to treat the next probe wording as a genuine state update whose measurement status must be argued rather than assumed. ([`REF-0384`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

6. **Local reduced models support intervention schedules, not just descriptions.**
   As Language Models Scale, Low-order Linear Depth Dynamics Emerge identifies prompt-conditioned local surrogates that predict depthwise sensitivity and yield intervention schedules validated in the full model. That pressures DelayBasin to distinguish a probe meant to read one local chart from a small actuation schedule that has already changed the chart. ([`REF-0375`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

7. **Measurement-induced disturbance can make probe order matter structurally.**
   Contextuality Derived from Minimal Decision Dynamics argues that conservation-based updates plus measurement-induced disturbance preclude a single non-contextual classical description. DelayBasin should not import the quantum formalism into canon, but the pressure is still useful: once probes disturb the state, order can stop being a bookkeeping detail. ([`REF-0396`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

None of this proves that DelayBasin already has a full measurement theory of continuation.
It does make a milder canon-level claim more credible:
**archive continuity may improve when some high-leverage probes are only trusted after the archive names what is being measured, how the probe perturbs the state, what sham baseline it is compared against, and how much non-demolition slack remains before the readout becomes actuation.**

## Working synthesis

A useful current synthesis is:

> DelayBasin may work better when it preserves an explicit **backaction witness / diagnostic probe family / non-demolition budget** whenever a rival-history split, same-state claim, or transformer-facing mechanism claim is being tested by a probe that may itself alter the continuation state. The packet should name the **ambiguity or state claim being probed**, the **diagnostic probe / intervention family actually applied**, the **expected readout or discriminating observable**, the **matched sham / no-op / commuted-order baseline**, the **tolerated backaction / non-demolition budget**, and the **hold / rollback / quarantine / restage consequence** rather than letting a revealing-but-state-changing probe quietly certify more than the archive has actually measured.

This is strong enough for canon as a design/mechanism candidate.
It is **not** strong enough to claim that DelayBasin has discovered a public commutator algebra, a full non-demolition measurement theory, or a literal quantum-like calculus over continuation probes.

## Backaction witness vs excitation witness vs execution witness vs observer-actuator split

To keep this note honest, DelayBasin needs a four-way distinction:

- **Excitation witness** — says what active variation is required to break an alias at all.
- **Execution witness** — says what runtime or substrate perturbation might explain a behavioral difference.
- **Observer-actuator split** — says a handle that measures should not automatically certify itself as a controller, and vice versa.
- **Backaction witness** — says whether the chosen diagnostic probe remains close enough to measurement that its result can still be read as evidence rather than as self-induced state change.

Functionally:
- excitation witnesses choose **what diversity to spend**,
- execution witnesses choose **what substrate confounds to track**,
- observer-actuator splits choose **which surface is allowed to claim diagnostic authority**,
- backaction witnesses choose **how much probe-induced distortion is still acceptable before diagnosis collapses into actuation**.

A good DelayBasin revision should therefore sometimes ask not only:
- what ambiguity is live,
- what variation is needed to separate it,
- what observable should move,

but also:
- whether the probe family is being used diagnostically or actuationally,
- what sham or matched-control baseline makes that distinction legible,
- how much backaction is tolerated,
- whether probe order matters,
- and what the archive will do if the measurement already spent too much state.

## Countermodels / probes

1. **Backaction-is-just-excitation countermodel**
   - This may only rename the already-earned excitation-witness lane.
   - Probe: compare cases where the same excitation family breaks the alias but the amount of state distortion differs materially; if canon posture changes only when non-demolition slack is named, the new object is doing distinct work.

2. **Sham-controls-are-enough countermodel**
   - Negative controls and sham packets may already cover the same risk.
   - Probe: inspect cases where a probe has a clean sham baseline but still changes route, commitment, or chart enough that the sham/no-sham distinction alone misses the distortion budget.

3. **Everything-is-actuation countermodel**
   - Under autoregressive conditioning, every probe changes state anyway, so non-demolition language may be false precision.
   - Probe: keep the canon claim weak and ask whether some probe families preserve enough future behavior, chart geometry, or matched-order baselines to be treated as approximately diagnostic for archive purposes.

4. **Probe-order-prestige countermodel**
   - “Backaction” may tempt the archive into ornamental noncommutative rhetoric.
   - Probe: require one concrete order-sensitive or matched-order comparison before any probe-order talk is allowed to steer canon.

5. **State-romance countermodel**
   - There may be no stable continuation state here beyond superficial prompt sensitivity.
   - Probe: keep the stronger public-commutator story quarantined until named backaction witnesses beat simpler prompt-wording, route-noise, and context-length explanations under matched controls.

## Design consequences

This mechanism frame pressures DelayBasin to do five things more explicitly:
- preserve a tiny **backaction witness** when a probe may both reveal and alter the state;
- name the **diagnostic probe family actually applied** rather than pretending “inspection” is inert;
- preserve one **matched sham / no-op / commuted-order baseline** before reading the result as measurement;
- preserve the **non-demolition budget** rather than letting an arbitrarily strong probe inherit diagnostic authority;
- and preserve the **hold / rollback / quarantine / restage consequence** so the archive can say when a measurement has spent too much state to certify the stronger claim.

A minimal backaction witness can often be very small:
- one ambiguity or state claim,
- one diagnostic probe family,
- one readout,
- one sham or order baseline,
- one non-demolition budget,
- and one failure consequence.

This does not require a grand measurement theory.
It requires treating **probe-induced state change** as part of archive method rather than assuming every revealing intervention was a transparent read.

## Transformer-facing implication

If this frame survives pressure, then DelayBasin is probing something sharper than active observability alone:
**whether a user-space archive can learn which textual probes are approximately diagnostic, which are quietly actuational, and how much local continuation state gets spent in the act of making a latent distinction legible.**

That would matter for transformers.
It would suggest that long-horizon archive method may need not only state packets, excitation witnesses, hysteresis witnesses, and gauge/scale discipline, but also compact **backaction witnesses** that say when a probe has changed too much of the continuation state to be used as clean evidence.

The stronger story — that DelayBasin may eventually learn a public commutator algebra, non-demolition calculus, or probe-order law over continuation charts — remains live, but belongs in quarantine for now.
