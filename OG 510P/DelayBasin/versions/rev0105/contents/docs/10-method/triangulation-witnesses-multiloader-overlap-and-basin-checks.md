# Triangulation witnesses, multi-loader overlap, and basin checks

DelayBasin now has several ways to ask whether a text surface matters:
conformance witnesses ask whether one loader survives a named support family,
necessity witnesses ask what inside a candidate surface is actually indispensable,
and sufficiency witnesses ask whether a reduced packet is already enough.
But one archive question still keeps appearing without a compact public object:
when do **multiple non-trivially different loaders** seem to reach the **same operative continuation basin** rather than merely producing locally acceptable prose by different accidental routes?

This note introduces a compact object for that gap:
a **triangulation witness / multi-loader overlap / basin check**.
The point is not to prove deep geometry.
It is to stop DelayBasin from silently equating one lucky wording with the basin itself.

## Practice / observation

In actual archive work, a familiar pattern keeps recurring.
One phrasing, packet layout, or prompt pair appears to work unusually well.
Then a nearby paraphrase, a differently organized loader, or a second entry surface sometimes also works — but not always.
When the alternatives agree, the archive is tempted to say it has found a “basin.”
When they do not, the archive often falls back to vague stories about prompt sensitivity, same-basin drift, or wrapper noise.

That is too blurry.
DelayBasin now has several nearby objects, but none of them cleanly serialize this specific distinction:
- **conformance witness** — one loader survives a named support family;
- **necessity witness** — some parts of a candidate surface are actually indispensable;
- **sufficiency witness** — a reduced surface is already enough on its own;
- **rewrite witness** — a recap has earned source-of-truth authority.

What is still missing is a small public object for the moment when the archive wants to say:
these two or three visibly different loaders appear to be reopening the **same judged continuation property**, so the privileged wording should no longer monopolize public authority.

That missing object matters because DelayBasin is explicitly about long-horizon continuation.
If the same operative branch can be reopened from several non-trivially different loader surfaces, that is evidence for a more stable continuation regime than “this one locally beloved phrase happened to work again.”
If not, the archive should say so and narrow the claim.

## External pressure from current research

Several current research lines make this object worth having.

1. **Semantic equivalence often fails to preserve behavior.**
   Semantic Invariance in Agentic AI argues that trustworthy reasoning agents should remain stable across paraphrase, fact reordering, expansion/contraction, and contextual reframing, while also noting that present systems are surprisingly fragile under such semantic-preserving transformations. That is direct pressure for DelayBasin: “same meaning” should not be assumed to imply “same basin.” ([`REF-0269`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

2. **Iterative rewriting can converge to recurrent sets or keep drifting.**
   Markovian Generation Chains in Large Language Models studies repeated reprocessing without explicit memory and finds that iterative rephrasing or round-trip translation can either converge to a small recurrent set or continue producing novel sentences over a finite horizon. That makes basin-style language plausible enough to test, while also warning that some apparent stability may simply be a local rewrite dynamic rather than faithful continuation. ([`REF-0267`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

3. **Context may induce shared low-rank internal updates.**
   Learning without training: The implicit dynamics of in-context learning argues that a transformer block can transform context into a low-rank effective weight update of its MLP layer. That does not prove that two different DelayBasin loaders create the same internal state, but it makes it more plausible that different visible prompts might converge onto a nearby effective operating mode. ([`REF-0268`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

4. **Transformers can behave like latent-state estimators rather than mere next-token stylists.**
   Transformers as Implicit State Estimators reports that sufficiently scaled models can emulate Kalman-like filtering, sometimes infer missing system parameters, and in some settings explicitly recover latent states. That is useful pressure for DelayBasin's weaker hypothesis: several distinct loaders might all reconstruct enough of the same latent continuation-relevant state to support the next legitimate archive move. ([`REF-0266`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

5. **Mechanistic evidence points to abstract circuits that generalize across contexts, but with caution.**
   Anthropic's attribution-graph work reports mixtures of language-specific and language-independent circuits and highlights cases where the same addition circuitry generalizes across very different contexts, while also stressing that attribution graphs are hypothesis-generating rather than final proof. That is exactly the kind of mixed pressure DelayBasin needs here: shared mechanism across prompts is possible, but any strong basin story still needs operational probes instead of prestige geometry. ([`REF-0265`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

## Why this is not already conformance or sufficiency

A triangulation witness is adjacent to several existing objects, but it is not redundant with them.

- A **conformance witness** asks whether one claimed loader survives wrapper, position, or phrasing variation inside a named support family.
- A **sufficiency witness** asks whether one reduced packet is enough on its own.
- A **necessity witness** asks what parts of one candidate surface seem indispensable.
- A **rewrite witness** asks whether a rewrite preserved source authority.

A **triangulation witness** asks a different question:
whether **at least two non-trivially different loader surfaces** appear to meet on the **same anchor continuation property / judged basin** under a **shared support envelope / fixed execution family**, and what first divergence would force DelayBasin to retreat from the broader basin claim.

That matters because the archive can otherwise fail in two opposite directions:
- it can overcredit one privileged wording and mistake a local chart for the basin itself;
- or it can overread a few flattering paraphrase successes and claim a broad basin where only loose stylistic overlap exists.

## Working synthesis

A useful current synthesis is:

> DelayBasin may work better when it preserves a compact **triangulation witness / multi-loader overlap / basin check** whenever multiple loaders, paraphrase families, packet layouts, or prompt surfaces are being treated as reopening the same operative continuation basin. The witness should name the **anchor continuation property / judged basin**, **at least two non-trivially different loader surfaces**, the **shared support envelope / fixed execution family**, the **expected overlap / divergence signature**, and the **narrowing / fallback / quarantine consequence** rather than letting one favored wording silently stand in for basin identity.

This is strong enough for canon as a design/mechanism candidate.
It is **not** strong enough to claim that DelayBasin has already identified exact continuation attractors, canonical loader manifolds, or a proven geometry of re-entry in transformer hidden state.

## Triangulation witness vs conformance witness vs sufficiency witness vs rewrite witness

To keep the object crisp, DelayBasin should preserve this four-way distinction:

- **Triangulation witness / multi-loader overlap / basin check** — says whether multiple non-trivially different loaders appear to reach the same judged continuation property under a named support family, and what first disagreement would collapse the broader basin claim.
- **Conformance witness / loader contract / ABI drift guard** — says whether one loader survives a named variation family, not whether several distinct loaders are actually reaching the same operative state.
- **Sufficiency witness / replay capsule / core-only re-entry trial** — says whether one reduced packet is enough on its own, not whether a family of visibly different loaders converges on the same judged branch.
- **Rewrite witness / round-trip packet / recap-authority test** — says whether a rewrite preserved operative distinctions from a source anchor, not whether multiple loaders genuinely overlap in continuation space.

A good DelayBasin revision should therefore sometimes ask not only:
- whether one loader survives a support family,
- or whether one reduced packet is enough,
- or whether one rewrite stayed faithful,

but also:
- whether the archive is quietly treating one privileged chart as the basin itself,
- whether alternative loaders are merely semantically similar or actually continuation-equivalent in the next decisions they support,
- what support envelope is being held fixed while that overlap claim is made,
- and what first disagreement should force narrowing instead of basin prestige.

## Countermodels / probes

1. **Paraphrase theater countermodel**
   - Several loaders may look “different” while still being too close to count as real triangulation, so the witness adds ceremony without new evidence.
   - Probe: require a small but explicit diversity rule for the compared loaders — e.g. different phrasing family, layout, evidence ordering, or packet decomposition — and preserve why that difference was non-trivial.

2. **Support-envelope confound countermodel**
   - Apparent overlap may come mainly from shared hidden support: same wrapper, same assistant carry, same runtime state, or unreduced surrounding context.
   - Probe: pair one triangulation witness with at least one assistant-echo filter or execution witness when hidden carry is plausible, and see whether the claimed overlap survives after the obvious confounds are thinned.

3. **One privileged chart countermodel**
   - Only one wording may really work, with the alternatives merely sounding acceptable while silently changing the next canonical move, admissible inference, or open-question posture.
   - Probe: preserve one small challenge panel or next-step consequence family and compare whether the candidate loaders stay aligned on those actual continuation decisions rather than on surface fluency alone.

4. **Convergence-with-loss countermodel**
   - Rewrites may converge toward a stable recurrent family only by shedding the distinctions DelayBasin still needs.
   - Probe: whenever a triangulation claim leans on paraphrase convergence, pair it with a rewrite witness or divergence panel and ask what operative distinctions remain invariant versus what was silently flattened.

## Design consequences

This mechanism frame pressures DelayBasin to do five things more explicitly:
- preserve the **anchor continuation property / judged basin** instead of leaving “same basin” implicit;
- preserve **at least two non-trivially different loader surfaces** rather than one favored wording plus vague claims about paraphrase robustness;
- name the **shared support envelope / fixed execution family** so later readers know what background conditions were held fixed while overlap was judged;
- name the **expected overlap / divergence signature** at the level of continuation decisions, challenge probes, or branch posture rather than prose similarity alone;
- and preserve the **narrowing / fallback / quarantine consequence** so the archive knows how to retreat if the multi-loader story collapses.

This does not require exhaustive paraphrase search.
It requires refusing another archive failure mode:
letting a basin claim inherit authority just because one cherished wording worked repeatedly.

## Transformer-facing implication

If this frame survives pressure, then DelayBasin is probing something slightly sharper than ordinary prompt robustness.
It is asking whether multiple visibly different text surfaces can induce enough of the same operative latent state, effective update, or abstract circuit usage to support the same next continuation decisions.

Current research makes that possibility live but not settled.
Semantic-invariance work says semantically equivalent formulations often do **not** preserve behavior, which is strong counterpressure against cheap basin talk. ([`REF-0269`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))
At the same time, work on implicit ICL dynamics, transformer state estimation, and attribution-graph case studies all suggest that models can sometimes recover abstract hidden structure or reuse more general circuits across different visible contexts. ([`REF-0265`](../00-meta/bibliography.md), [`REF-0266`](../00-meta/bibliography.md), [`REF-0268`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))
And recent generation-chain work makes it at least plausible that repeated rewriting can enter local recurrent families rather than wandering arbitrarily forever, although such recurrent sets need not coincide with faithful archive continuation. ([`REF-0267`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

That makes triangulation witnesses a useful bridge between archive method and transformer implications:
not proof that DelayBasin has found true continuation attractors,
but a disciplined way to ask whether the archive is seeing a real multi-loader basin rather than merely a locally lucky string.
