# Promotion contracts and staged ratification

## The problem

DelayBasin now has certified terms and certified moves.
That still leaves a structural gap:
**what exactly permits a risky or provisional idea to become canon at all?**

Without an explicit answer, the archive can launder trust upward.
A neat phrase, a persuasive synthesis, or a repeated local habit can drift from quarantine into canon without preserving:
- what kind of evidence supported the move,
- how strong the support actually was,
- what would expire that support,
- and what should force demotion later.

## Working claim

Archive continuity may improve when promotion into canon follows a **promotion contract** rather than vibe.
The contract should stay compact.
It does not need to be bureaucratic.
It needs to preserve enough structure that later sessions can tell the difference between:
- live conjecture,
- useful but weak canon,
- and claims that earned stronger trust under explicit conditions.

## Minimal promotion contract

A compact promotion contract should record at least:

1. **source status**
   - where the idea came from: quarantine, open question, working synthesis, external import.
2. **target status**
   - what status it is being promoted to: inferred, speculative-but-central, accepted working discipline, or another explicitly allowed layer.
3. **evidence family**
   - empirical, mechanistic, comparative, expert/position, operational, or formal.
4. **temporal validity posture**
   - durable, review-needed, or rapidly-decaying.
5. **demotion trigger**
   - what would force the claim back down: contrary evidence, failed reopening, wording drift, better countermodel, stale sources.
6. **admission route**
   - which certified moves actually carried the promotion: research/cite, refine/compress, recertify, promote, or quarantine.

The archive does not need full safety-case machinery.
It does need enough contract shape to resist semantic laundering and trust inflation.

## Why staged ratification instead of binary promotion

Binary promotion is too coarse.
A useful archive often needs at least three states:
- **quarantined** — worth keeping live, not trustworthy enough for canon,
- **candidate / weak canon** — load-bearing enough to organize work, still fragile,
- **stronger canon** — trusted enough to anchor re-entry until expiry or demotion.

This is not an argument for conservatism.
It is an argument for preserving **how** confidence was granted.

## Why this fits DelayBasin specifically

DelayBasin is not only storing conclusions.
It is storing the continuation law by which later turns decide what counts as faithful progress.
If promotion itself is under-specified, the archive can accumulate false stability:
canon that looks settled because it was repeated, not because it was ratified under explicit conditions.

That failure mode is especially dangerous here because the archive is studying transformers, archive method, and its own self-understanding.
Those are precisely the domains where elegant local language can outrun warrant.

## Practical consequence

DelayBasin should preserve a tiny promotion surface:
- one registry for promotion contract patterns,
- one certified move for ratify/demote,
- one context-pack hook so future sessions know promotion is governed rather than implied.

This is a small ratchet, not a large subsystem.
