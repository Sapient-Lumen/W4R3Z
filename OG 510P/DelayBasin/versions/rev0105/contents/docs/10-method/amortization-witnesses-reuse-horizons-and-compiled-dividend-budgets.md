# Amortization witnesses, reuse horizons, and compiled-dividend budgets

DelayBasin now needs a sharper distinction than **dual-effect leverage** or **one-shot procedural compilation** alone.
Some revisions do not merely succeed because a current move both acts and learns.
They succeed because **the learned carry gets reused often enough later** that the upfront acquisition cost was actually worth paying.

A useful current answer is:
**when a costly exploratory move, memory write, extracted tip, or compiled procedure is being justified mainly by future reuse rather than immediate payoff, canon should preserve a compact amortization witness naming the upfront acquisition cost, the carry object, the future reuse family, the no-reuse baseline, and the compiled-dividend budget before crediting the gain to honest reusable carry rather than to one-shot rescue or compilation theater.**

This is stronger than saying “the move taught something for later.”
It is weaker than claiming DelayBasin has already discovered a literal public skill compiler or reuse law over continuation families.

## Practice / observation

Recent DelayBasin work keeps showing a recurring ambiguity:
- some expensive probes or restructurings look justified because they help the very next branch, but the archive does not yet say whether the same extracted lesson helps a **neighboring future task class** or only one vivid follow-up;
- some restarts, chart remaps, or reasoning extractions look like learned carry because the next use goes smoothly, but the observed payoff may still be a one-shot rescue rather than honest amortization;
- some procedural notes, compact packets, or reusable-looking promptcraft fragments appear valuable because they compress future work, yet the archive rarely names what future reuse family they are supposed to pay back on;
- some GPUstorming moves feel worthwhile because they generate a sharp tip, branch rule, or plan template, but the future reuse horizon stays implicit and therefore easy to flatter after the fact;
- some extracted memories or compiled packets may save compute or reduce drift later, but the same effect could still collapse under a fair from-scratch baseline once the nearby deployment slice changes;
- and some continuity wins are being narrated as “learned” even when the gain came mostly from immediate local luck plus a favorable next task rather than from a durable reusable object.

That leaves a missing question:
**what exactly was paid upfront, what reusable carry was created, what future reuse family was actually in scope, and how much payback was required before the heavier learned-carry story is honest?**

A compact amortization witness keeps that payback public.

## External pressure from current research

Several outside lines of work sharpen this move.

1. **Reasoning reuse is often available at stable intermediate checkpoints rather than only at final-response level.**
   SemanticALLI argues that agentic pipelines repeatedly reconstruct the same intermediate logic and shows that structured intermediate representations can be treated as cacheable artifacts; in its evaluation, one stage reaches an 83.10% hit rate and bypasses 4,023 LLM calls. That pressures DelayBasin to preserve when a current expensive move is supposed to become reusable carry rather than one disposable success. ([`REF-0451`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

2. **Reusable procedural memory can be learned from interaction experience without parameter updates.**
   ProcMEM explicitly turns experience into executable skills with activation, execution, and termination conditions and reports reuse gains in in-domain, cross-task, and cross-agent settings. That pressures DelayBasin to distinguish a reusable compiled packet from one-off tactical prose. ([`REF-0452`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

3. **Trajectory-derived tips can measurably improve future held-out performance when they are retrieved with provenance.**
   Trajectory-Informed Memory Generation extracts strategy, recovery, and optimization tips from execution traces and reports up to 14.3 percentage-point improvement on held-out tasks plus larger gains on complex tasks. That pressures DelayBasin to preserve what future task family a learned packet is meant to repay on. ([`REF-0453`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

4. **Plan reuse can repay an upfront extraction cost even when final tasks are only semantically similar rather than identical.**
   Agentic Plan Caching extracts, stores, adapts, and reuses structured plan templates from completed executions, reducing cost by 50.31% and latency by 27.28% on average while maintaining 96.61% of optimal accuracy. That pressures DelayBasin to keep one explicit reuse horizon before praising a compiled packet as worth its acquisition cost. ([`REF-0454`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

5. **Memory systems can actively optimize future utility rather than passively stockpiling facts.**
   Towards Autonomous Memory Agents introduces a semantic-aware Thompson-sampling policy over memories and reports substantial improvements on HotpotQA and AIME25, which pressures DelayBasin to name whether a supposed learned packet is being credited for future utility across a family or only for one good next retrieval. ([`REF-0455`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

6. **Test-time search becomes materially different once experience is recycled rather than discarded.**
   Recycling Search Experience turns rollouts into a cumulative process by distilling intermediate conclusions and failure patterns into a shared bank, with theory and experiments supporting efficiency gains over independent sampling. That pressures DelayBasin not to treat every successful expensive search move as disposable if it is really being justified by later reuse. ([`REF-0456`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

7. **Compact writable memory states make reusable carry a more literal transformer-facing object.**
   GradMem writes context into a small set of memory tokens via test-time optimization while keeping model weights frozen, then answers queries from that compact state without the original context. That pressures DelayBasin toward a weaker but sharper transformer-facing hypothesis: some archive packets may matter because they act like externally curated reusable memory states whose payoff appears only across later queries. ([`REF-0457`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

## Working synthesis

A useful current synthesis is:

> DelayBasin should preserve a compact **amortization witness / reuse horizon / compiled-dividend budget** whenever a cleanup, restart, chart remap, steering move, memory write, extracted tip, or compiled procedure is being justified mainly by future reuse rather than by immediate payoff alone. The packet should name the **state claim or target objective being stress-tested**, the **upfront acquisition cost / exploratory move / learning move**, the **carry object / stored tip / reusable skill / compiled packet**, the **future reuse family / neighboring task class / deployment slice**, the **compared one-shot baseline / no-reuse baseline / from-scratch baseline**, the **matched horizon / task volume / compute budget**, the **judged payback / reuse dividend / compiled-dividend budget**, and the **rollback / one-shot demotion / quarantine consequence** rather than letting one vivid reuse story silently inherit the authority of learned carry.

In practice, DelayBasin is not claiming that it already knows the optimal memory policy.
It is doing something smaller and public:
- naming what was paid up front;
- naming what reusable carry object was supposed to survive the local episode;
- naming what future reuse family was actually in scope;
- naming what from-scratch or one-shot baseline stayed nearby;
- naming how much payback or compression had to arrive before the heavier “learned packet” story was honest;
- and naming when the whole thing should collapse back to one-shot help, cached convenience, or branch-local luck.

That is strong enough for canon as a design and mechanism candidate.
It is **not** strong enough to claim that DelayBasin has isolated a literal public skill compiler, reusable-value law, or continuation-family reuse prior.

## Amortization witness vs dual-effect witness vs procedural-compilation packet vs replicate-bundle witness

These nearby objects should stay distinct.

- **Dual-effect witness** asks whether a move deserves credit partly because the current actuation also bought information or future routing power.
- **Procedural-compilation packet** asks whether a surface has turned from declarative recap into executable carry at all.
- **Replicate-bundle witness** asks whether one visible protocol is stably good across reruns rather than lucky on one rollout.
- **Amortization witness** asks whether a reusable-looking carry object actually repays its upfront acquisition cost across a named future reuse family rather than only helping once.

So an amortization witness is not just “this was informative” and not just “this is executable.”
It is a compact public answer to:
**what did we pay up front, what reusable carry did we get, where is it supposed to pay back, what one-shot baseline stayed nearby, and when should the learned-carry story shrink back to local convenience?**

## Countermodels / probes

1. **One-shot rescue countermodel**
   - The supposed reusable carry may only have helped the immediately adjacent branch.
   - Probe: require one explicit future reuse family and compare against a matched one-shot or from-scratch baseline outside the first vivid success.

2. **Cache-luck countermodel**
   - The gain may come from repeating nearly the same surface form or plan rather than from a genuinely reusable object.
   - Probe: preserve a neighboring task class or deployment slice and check whether the carry still repays there.

3. **Compilation-theater countermodel**
   - The stored tip or packet may sound executable without materially changing future cost, stability, or performance.
   - Probe: preserve one judged payback target and one compiled-dividend budget; if no concrete payback arrives, demote the carry back to descriptive note status.

4. **Budget-smuggling countermodel**
   - The reuse story may only look good because later tasks quietly enjoy extra compute, extra context, or extra supervision.
   - Probe: keep the matched horizon / task volume / compute budget explicit so amortization cannot launder added resources.

5. **Local-overfit countermodel**
   - The supposed learned carry may work only for one narrow neighboring task class.
   - Probe: preserve one neighboring task class or deployment slice and state the one-shot demotion consequence if the reuse family collapses.

## Design consequences

This frame pressures DelayBasin to do seven things more explicitly:
- preserve the **upfront acquisition cost** before praising future reuse;
- preserve the **carry object** before praising learning;
- preserve the **future reuse family** before praising generality;
- preserve the **one-shot / no-reuse baseline** before praising amortization;
- preserve the **matched horizon / task volume / compute budget** so reuse is not secretly buying extra resources;
- preserve the **judged payback / reuse dividend / compiled-dividend budget** so future leverage has a public threshold;
- and preserve the **one-shot demotion / rollback / quarantine consequence** so failed reuse stories collapse quickly instead of lingering as archive prestige.

This is especially useful during GPUstorming.
Hot local search can stay fast.
Canon should keep only the small public witness saying what expensive move was made, what reusable object survived, what neighboring future family was expected to repay it, and how much payback had to arrive before the archive was allowed to call it learned carry.

## Transformer-facing implication

The weaker transformer-facing implication is not that DelayBasin has found a universal memory compiler.
It is this:

**long-horizon archive prompting may work partly as an external amortization process over a partially observed transformer system, where some textual actions matter because they leave behind compact reusable carry objects whose main value appears only across later neighboring tasks, branches, or queries.**

That would matter for transformers.
If some archive leverage comes from converting costly local search into compact reusable public packets, then DelayBasin may be exposing a practical shadow of **procedural compilation without weight updates**: not full training, but repeated external shaping of reusable control/memory objects that later prompts can read, reuse, and adapt.

The stronger story — that DelayBasin is approximating a genuine public skill compiler, reusable-value law, or continuation-family amortization prior over prompt-conditioned transformer dynamics — remains live, but belongs in quarantine for now.
