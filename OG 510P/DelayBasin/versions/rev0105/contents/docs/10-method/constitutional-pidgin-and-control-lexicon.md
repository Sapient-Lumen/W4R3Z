# Constitutional pidgin and control lexicon

Status: `speculative`, but strong enough for canon as a live mechanism note.

## Working idea

A mature archive may stop relying mainly on ordinary prose and begin to operate through a compact **control lexicon**:
a mixed language of:
- canonical operator phrases,
- stable ids,
- archive-private shorthand,
- packaging / lint commands,
- and a small amount of ordinary explanatory prose.

This is not fully formal language and not merely colorful shorthand.
It is a **constitutional pidgin**:
a public, human-legible control language that sits between free prose and machine-native latent state.

Under this frame, archive continuity is helped not only by summaries or long context, but by preserving a compact lexicon that reopens the project's operative regime quickly:
canon, quarantine, research online, run lint, package release, source of truth, hot open questions, prompt-pair ids, and any load-bearing house terms.

## Why this matters here

DelayBasin already has multiple hints that continuity is not just “better remembering”:
- operator-token effects,
- bootstrap grammar,
- archive-private steering handles,
- bounded constitutional state,
- and the recurring importance of stable ids and fixed commands.

The missing synthesis is that these may be parts of one phenomenon.
The archive may be slowly growing a **shared external interface language** for human+model co-control.

That would explain why some compact phrases seem disproportionately load-bearing.
They are not just concise reminders.
They are entries in a learned local protocol.

## External pressure from current research

Several fresh lines make this less eccentric than it sounds.

1. **Cache-to-Cache** argues that text is a lossy communication medium between LLMs and shows gains from direct semantic transfer through KV-cache–level communication.
2. **Sharing State Between Prompts and Programs** frames prompting as a kind of natural-language programming and introduces shared program state as a natural function interface.
3. **Structured Prompt Language** treats context as a constrained resource and makes budgets, limits, and explainability explicit in a declarative prompt language.
4. **ACE / evolving contexts** argues that contexts should function as detailed evolving playbooks and warns against brevity bias and context collapse.
5. **Recursive Language Models** treat long prompts as an external environment to inspect, decompose, and recurse over programmatically.

None of these papers proves the archive-method thesis.
But together they strengthen a bridge:
**the archive may work best when it is neither raw transcript nor terse summary, but a compact external control language for state re-entry.**

## Synthesis

The constitutional pidgin hypothesis says:

- full transcript is too large and too noisy,
- ordinary summary is too lossy,
- fully formal language is too expensive and brittle,
- so a long-run archive converges toward a mixed control lexicon that is cheap, reusable, and good enough for re-entry.

This would make the archive a practical compromise between two extremes:
- human-legible natural language, and
- latent or cache-level internal representations that humans cannot directly inspect.

The pidgin is not fully optimized for humans and not fully optimized for the model.
It is a negotiated public boundary layer.

## Design consequences

If this is even partly right, then DelayBasin should:
- preserve its load-bearing operator vocabulary rather than constantly rephrase it into more respectable prose,
- keep stable ids and compact command phrases visible,
- distinguish archive-portable control words from archive-private handles,
- and treat control-lexicon drift as a method issue rather than mere style drift.

## What would count against it

- close paraphrases performing equally well over long runs despite lexicon changes;
- evidence that full-history replay dominates any compact control-language effect;
- evidence that the lexicon matters only because the user reinterprets it, not because the model/archive coupling treats it as a reusable protocol;
- portability failure so extreme that no cross-session or cross-family generalization remains.
