# Initial turns and basin seeding

Status: `speculative`

## Working idea

The earliest turns in a new archive may carry disproportionate weight.
Not because they contain more information, but because they establish the first durable answer to questions like:

- what counts as canon,
- what is being optimized,
- what kinds of revision are admissible,
- whether prompt pairs will be preserved,
- whether speculation is welcomed or punished,
- and whether the archive is transcript-like or constitutional.

## Why this matters

If early turns are disproportionately basin-shaping, then archive method is not just a matter of steady-state hygiene. It is also a matter of **how the basin is first cut**.

This changes practical priorities:
- bootstrap prompts matter more than ordinary prompts,
- first-run runbook/trajectory surfaces matter more than they look,
- prompt-pair evolution should preserve early operator commitments rather than merely the latest wording,
- and a bad opening may force later turns to spend energy fighting the archive's own inherited geometry.

## Minimal operational consequences

1. Preserve the first serious prompt pair, not just the latest one.
2. Record initial non-negotiables explicitly.
3. Capture early misfires instead of rewriting them away; they may explain later repair costs.
4. Distinguish “good opening conditions” from “good downstream maintenance.”

## Counterpressure

This could be false. Early-turn significance may be a storytelling artifact caused by the human tendency to mythologize origins after success.

## What would support it

- cross-project evidence that successful archives have unusually explicit early canon/non-negotiable/ratchet surfaces;
- evidence that later recovery from a weak opening is costly or partial;
- sensitivity tests showing that small early framing changes alter long-run archive character more than similar late changes.
