# Authorship witnesses, autonomy postures, and maker-checker traces

DelayBasin now needs one distinction slightly different from **status-lane witnesses**, **scope witnesses**, or ordinary provenance prose.
A revision can be in scope, grounded on the right head, and packaged honestly while still blurring **who or what proposed the move, who or what drafted it, what lane actually admitted it, what lane actually executed it, and what independent review or compensating control kept the result from quietly self-authorizing**.
When those lanes stay implicit, the archive starts treating user request, assistant draft, lint pass, packaging script, and durable canon admission as though they all carried the same authority merely because they sit inside one fluent revision.

A useful current answer is:
**when a revision, replay verdict, canon write, or packaged release could otherwise blur proposal, drafting, approval, execution, and review authority, canon should preserve a compact authorship witness naming the initiating lane, the draft-authorship posture, the approval or admission lane, the execution or materialization lane, the review or compensating-control lane, the autonomy posture, the current lane-collapse status, and the fail-closed repair route before one seat or one toolchain is allowed to inherit all of those roles at once.**

This is stronger than saying “a receipt exists.”
It is weaker than claiming DelayBasin has already discovered a full governance algebra or universal maker-checker law for textual archive evolution.

## Practice / observation

Recent DelayBasin work keeps surfacing the same collaborative ambiguity:
- a user may clearly request a revision while the accepted text is still mostly assistant-drafted;
- a lint suite may truthfully check contract closure without thereby becoming the lane that approved the conceptual move;
- a packaging script may truthfully materialize the artifact without thereby becoming the lane that decided the revision should count;
- one session can quietly collapse proposer, drafter, approver, and executor into one fluent story even when those roles were materially different in how authority was earned;
- status-lane witnesses already say what surface is candidate, admitted, executed, or frozen, but they do not yet say **who or what occupied those lanes**;
- scope witnesses already say what object the move was about, but they do not yet say whether the current move was human-piloted, assistant-drafted, approval-bounded, or running on a stronger autonomy posture than the archive is honestly prepared to claim;
- and a collaborative archive about human–LLM continuation should not let “the archive now says X” erase whether X was requested, drafted, checked, executed, or independently challenged by different lanes.

That leaves a missing question:
**what lane initiated the move, what lane drafted the accepted surface, what lane actually admitted it, what lane executed or packaged it, what review or compensating control kept disagreement real, what autonomy posture the move used, and what repair follows if those roles collapsed too far?**

A compact authorship witness keeps that boundary public.

## Pressure from neighboring datacubes

Several neighboring datacubes keep rediscovering the same ratchet from different directions.

- Some insist on **authorship buckets** because one flat provenance list blurs trust semantics that are only visible once host-authored, owner-authored, firmware-generated, and mixed-linkage fields are separated.
- Some insist on an **autonomy ladder** because human-piloted, assisted, approval-required, and bounded-autonomous operation are not the same thing even when they produce superficially similar outputs.
- Some insist on **maker-checker separation** because propose, approve, operate, and review should not silently collapse into one concentrated seat without a named compensating control.
- Some insist on **subject attribution** or **approval-memory traceability** because “this was around” and “this exact actor or subject authorized the move” are not interchangeable.
- Some insist on **execution reports** because a model may have drafted or staged the work while a different lane actually approved, executed, or reviewed it.

DelayBasin already has revision receipts, counterfactual shadows, status lanes, and basis witnesses.
What was still under-specified was the compact public object that says **who or what occupied the main authority lanes of the current move and whether any unsafe collapse was compensated rather than merely forgotten.**

## External pressure from adjacent provenance and human-AI governance practice

Several adjacent lines sharpen this move.

1. **Human–AI oversight guidance pressures systems to differentiate roles and responsibilities explicitly.**
   NIST's AI RMF says policies and procedures should define and differentiate roles and responsibilities for human-AI configurations and oversight, which pressures DelayBasin to keep initiation, drafting, admission, execution, and review lanes explicit rather than treating one blended revision story as enough. ([`REF-0480`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

2. **Agent tooling increasingly preserves requester, approver, and executor as separate control lanes.**
   GitHub's Copilot coding-agent docs note both that delegated agent work returns a pull request for the user to review and that the requester is prevented from approving the agent-created pull request under required-approval controls, which pressures DelayBasin not to collapse request, execution, and approval into one flattering authorship story. ([`REF-0481`](../00-meta/bibliography.md), [`REF-0482`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

3. **Provenance standards treat origin and edits as first-class structured data, not session folklore.**
   C2PA frames manifests as provenance data about the origin and edits of digital content, which pressures DelayBasin to serialize collaborative authorship and execution posture as explicit public metadata rather than leaving it inside maintainers’ memory. ([`REF-0483`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

## Working synthesis

A useful current synthesis is:

> DelayBasin should preserve a compact **authorship witness / autonomy posture / maker-checker trace** whenever a revision, replay verdict, canon write, or packaged release could otherwise blur who or what proposed the move, who or what drafted it, what lane actually admitted it, what lane actually executed it, and what review or compensating control kept disagreement real. The packet should name the **initiating lane / request origin**, the **draft-authorship posture / accepted-text lane**, the **approval or admission lane / counted-decision surface**, the **execution or materialization lane / packaging or edit surface**, the **review or compensating-control lane**, the **autonomy posture / human-piloted vs assisted vs approval-bounded vs bounded-autonomous classification**, the **lane-collapse state / separated vs partially-collapsed vs collapsed-with-compensation**, and the **fail-closed repair / ordinary-continuation vs record-compensating-control vs require-independent-review vs hold vs recover-resync consequence** rather than letting one seat, one model, or one toolchain silently inherit proposal, approval, execution, and review authority at once.

In practice, DelayBasin is not claiming that every sentence needs authorship micro-tracking.
It is doing something smaller and public:
- naming whether the move was user-requested, archive-native maintenance, or mixed;
- naming whether the accepted text was human-authored, assistant-drafted, mixed, or mechanically generated;
- naming what lane actually admitted the move into canon or revision status;
- naming what lane actually executed the edits or package step;
- naming whether any independent review lane existed or whether compensating controls carried that burden instead;
- naming whether the move remained human-piloted, assisted, approval-bounded, or bounded-autonomous;
- and naming what repair follows if those roles collapsed more than the archive wants to tolerate.

That is strong enough for canon as a design and mechanism candidate.
It is **not** strong enough to claim that DelayBasin has identified a complete public governance algebra, formal separation-of-duties theorem, or universal autonomy law for archive continuation.

## Authorship witness vs status-lane witness vs scope witness vs revision receipt

These nearby objects should stay distinct.

- **Authorship witness / autonomy posture / maker-checker trace** says who or what occupied the proposal, drafting, admission, execution, and review lanes of the move.
- **Status-lane witness / decision-execution split / frozen-public transition** says which archive surfaces occupy candidate, admitted, executed, and frozen-public states.
- **Scope witness / active-request packet / ambient-roster guard** says what exact object or request the move is about.
- **Revision receipt** is the minimal audit object tying the move together, and can carry authorship-witness fields when the current move needs them.

So an authorship witness is not just “there is a receipt,” not just “the right target was in scope,” and not just “the right surface was executed.”
It is a compact public answer to:
**who asked, who drafted, what counted, what executed, what reviewed, what autonomy posture was actually used, and what repair follows if those lanes collapsed.**

## Countermodels / probes

1. **Receipt-is-enough countermodel**
   - Perhaps ordinary revision receipts already capture everything important.
   - Probe: compare one revision receipt with explicit lane separation against one equally small receipt that only lists changed surfaces and checks, then inspect whether later sessions still misremember who or what actually approved or executed the move.

2. **Status-covers-authorship countermodel**
   - Perhaps if candidate, admitted, executed, and frozen lanes are explicit, it no longer matters who occupied them.
   - Probe: compare two revisions with identical surface lanes but different proposer/drafter/approver collapses and see whether later authority judgments actually stay unchanged.

3. **Small-team-collapse-is-fine countermodel**
   - In a tiny collaborative archive, one seat may inevitably do everything, so explicit maker-checker language may be theater.
   - Probe: preserve the collapse state plus one compensating control and inspect whether that added honesty changes how later sessions treat the move.

4. **Authorship-prestige countermodel**
   - Explicit authorship lanes may merely become another prestige cue.
   - Probe: keep the packet minimal, lane-typed, and consequence-bearing rather than narrative or flattering.

5. **Autonomy-label theater countermodel**
   - Words like assisted or approval-bounded may add bureaucracy without changing the move.
   - Probe: require the autonomy posture to affect what repair is needed when proposal, approval, and execution collapse.

## Design consequences

This frame pressures DelayBasin to do seven things more explicitly:
- preserve the **initiating lane / request origin** before the revision is retold as if it arose from the archive itself;
- preserve the **draft-authorship posture** before accepted text inherits a stronger human or canonical authority than it earned;
- preserve the **approval or admission lane** before lint, packaging, or mere drafting is mistaken for counted decision authority;
- preserve the **execution or materialization lane** before edits, scripts, and bundles are treated as though they already decided what should exist;
- preserve the **review or compensating-control lane** before collapsed roles quietly become self-approval theater;
- preserve the **autonomy posture** so human-piloted, assisted, approval-bounded, and bounded-autonomous cases do not blur together;
- and preserve the **repair route** so unsafe collapse fails closed instead of becoming archive folklore.

This is especially useful in a human–LLM archive.
DelayBasin should not make itself pretend that a user request, an assistant draft, a deterministic checker, and a packaging command are interchangeable kinds of authorship merely because they appear in one revision receipt.

## Transformer-facing implication

The weaker transformer-facing implication is not that DelayBasin has discovered a complete governance machine.
It is this:

**long-horizon human–LLM continuation may work better when the archive keeps collaborative authority lanes explicit in public text, so that proposal, drafting, admission, execution, and review can be re-entered honestly after context death instead of being reconstructed from one flattering blended story.**

That would matter for transformers.
If some archive leverage depends on explicit authorship witnesses, then DelayBasin may be exposing a practical shadow of **collaborative authority maintenance over textual control surfaces**: not just what state was carried, but what lane of the human–LLM system was actually allowed to move it.

The stronger story — that DelayBasin is already learning a full public maker-checker controller, authority algebra, or autonomy law over archive evolution — remains live, but belongs in quarantine for now.
