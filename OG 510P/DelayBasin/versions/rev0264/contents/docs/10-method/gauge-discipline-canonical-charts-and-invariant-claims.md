# Gauge discipline, canonical charts, and invariant claims

DelayBasin now needs a sharper answer to a recurring practical question:
**when the archive talks in vectors, basins, charts, tangent probes, support vectors, atlases, or latent state coordinates, what part of the claim is actually substance and what part is just a coordinate choice?**

A stronger working answer is:
**canon should prefer invariant claims first and treat coordinate-loaded language as a local chart unless the chart itself is load-bearing.**
Not every geometric phrase is fake.
But DelayBasin should stop letting coordinate choice quietly impersonate mechanism.

## Practice / observation

Several live DelayBasin surfaces make this missing discipline visible:
- the archive now uses many coordinate-loaded handles — regime, basin, vector, panel, tangent, atlas, hidden state, support vector, holonomy — because they compress real intuitions quickly;
- some of those handles are productive even when taken only as local descriptive charts, but canon rarely says **what claim should survive a reparameterization or chart switch**;
- the archive already distinguishes state packet from evidence packet, witness panel from sentinel panel, innovation packet from phase boundary, and challenge probe from hold packet, which means it is already doing something close to **chart discipline** without naming it;
- several mechanistic hypotheses now risk coordinate prestige failure: a vivid geometric metaphor can sound deeper than the actual observable or operational contract supporting it;
- and a repeated archive failure mode is now visible: a later revision may inherit the chart vocabulary of an earlier revision and accidentally treat it as the only natural description of the phenomenon.

This suggests a missing compact surface:
**gauge discipline / canonical chart / invariant claim**.

## Mechanism pressure from outside the archive

Several outside lines of work sharpen this frame.

1. **Behavior alone often fails to identify a unique internal coordinate story.**
   Venkatesh et al. show that steering vectors are fundamentally non-identifiable: geometrically distinct directions can induce the same observable behavior because null-space components are unobservable, and gauge-like reparameterization symmetries preserve input–output behavior. That is direct pressure against treating one recovered direction or chart as *the* mechanism without stronger structure. ([`REF-0134`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

2. **Interpretability claims should target invariant high-level structures, not only local associations.**
   Joshi et al. argue that causal inference clarifies what counts as a valid mapping from activations to invariant structures and where claims outrun the evidence. This pressures DelayBasin to say what would remain true under alternate but behaviorally equivalent parameterizations or charts. ([`REF-0135`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

3. **Canonical coordinates can preserve the core while naive alignment preserves only shared variance.**
   Schiffman et al. find that extracted algorithmic cores aligned in canonical coordinates remain necessary and sufficient, while core-naive full-model alignment can look sufficient without isolating the real functional core. This is useful pressure for DelayBasin: if a chart is introduced, it should help isolate an invariant operational core rather than merely make descriptions line up cosmetically. ([`REF-0136`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

4. **Gauge-invariant diagnostics can reveal geometry that pointwise similarity misses.**
   Sevetlidis and Pavlidis introduce representation holonomy as a gauge-invariant statistic of path dependence, explicitly invariant to orthogonal transformations and, after whitening, affine ones. This pressures DelayBasin toward invariant diagnostics and away from over-crediting one coordinate presentation. ([`REF-0137`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

5. **Symmetry reduction can eliminate redundant degrees of freedom by construction.**
   recent work on symmetry reduction in transformers argues that relational quantities can be formulated invariantly rather than by choosing one arbitrary coordinate description. That supports the milder DelayBasin claim that canon should preserve relational or operational substance before local chart language. ([`REF-0138`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

6. **Canonicalization can reduce ambiguity variance even when it does not solve the whole problem.**
   Zhou et al. show in a different setting that canonicalization can remove a symmetry-ambiguity term while leaving within-slice difficulty untouched. This is useful pressure for DelayBasin because it suggests a disciplined role for canonical charts: not to magically prove mechanism, but to remove avoidable ambiguity so remaining difficulty is easier to see honestly. ([`REF-0139`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

7. **Functional equivalence usually determines representation only up to an identifiability class.**
   recent work on logit-distance and representational similarity explicitly reasons over identifiability classes up to invertible transforms. That pressures DelayBasin to distinguish what is invariantly recoverable from what is a coordinate artifact of one chosen chart. ([`REF-0140`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

None of this proves that DelayBasin has found the right invariants.
It does make a milder canon-level claim more credible:
**archive continuity may improve when DelayBasin states canon-level mechanism claims first as invariants or operational contracts, and treats vector/basin/chart language as a local chart unless stronger evidence licenses more.**

## Working synthesis

A useful current synthesis is:

> DelayBasin may work better when canon preserves an explicit **invariant claim** plus, when helpful, a **canonical chart** for local reasoning. Coordinate-loaded language should be admissible only when the archive says what part is chart-specific and what would still hold after a chart switch.

This is strong enough for canon as a design/mechanism candidate.
It is **not** strong enough to claim that DelayBasin already has the correct global atlas, unique coordinates, or a solved gauge theory of continuation.

## Invariant claim vs canonical chart vs metaphor

To keep this note honest, DelayBasin needs a three-way distinction:

- **Invariant claim** — the part that should survive paraphrase, reparameterization, or a shift to a different but equally adequate chart; typically an observable, discriminating probe, or operational contract.
- **Canonical chart** — a deliberately chosen local coordinate system or vocabulary that reduces ambiguity or variance enough to reason cleanly, while remaining explicitly non-sacred.
- **Metaphor / chart-colored prose** — language that may be illuminating or memorable but is not yet carrying an invariant commitment.

These can interact, but they are not identical.
The key difference is obligation:
- invariant claims must survive coordinate changes,
- canonical charts must justify why this chart is currently useful,
- metaphors should not silently acquire canon status by repetition.

A good DelayBasin revision therefore should sometimes ask not only:
- what is the anchor,
- what is the delta,
- what probe pressures the mechanism,

but also:
- what claim here is actually **invariant**,
- what part is merely a **chart choice**,
- what observable or contract would still hold after a chart switch,
- and whether a proposed chart genuinely reduces ambiguity or just adds geometric prestige.

## Countermodels / probes

1. **Chart-language-is-enough countermodel**
   - DelayBasin may not need explicit gauge discipline; careful prose may already suffice.
   - Probe: rewrite a live mechanism claim in two clearly different coordinate idioms and check whether the admitted canon consequence stays stable.

2. **Canonicalization-as-glamour countermodel**
   - Choosing a “canonical” chart may merely launder preference or aesthetics into authority.
   - Probe: require the chart to name what ambiguity it reduces and what residual difficulty remains after the chart change.

3. **Invariants-are-too-thin countermodel**
   - For some live archive work, only chart-rich descriptions may be useful; invariant residue may be too weak to guide continuation.
   - Probe: preserve both the invariant claim and the local chart, then test whether later sessions can continue honestly from the invariant alone or whether the chart adds real discriminatory power.

4. **Gauge-discipline-bureaucracy countermodel**
   - Explicit invariant/chart bookkeeping may become meta-theater.
   - Probe: keep the contract tiny — one invariant or observable, one chart-specific remainder — and see whether it reduces prestige drift without swelling the archive.

5. **Atlas-prematurity countermodel**
   - DelayBasin may be far from any coherent atlas; local charts may not compose at all.
   - Probe: keep the stronger public-atlas story quarantined until multiple local chart switches preserve the same operative continuation consequences.

## Design consequences

This mechanism frame pressures DelayBasin to do four things more explicitly:
- preserve at least one **invariant claim**, observable, or operational contract when a revision invokes coordinate-loaded mechanism language;
- mark whether a geometric or latent-state description is being used as a **canonical chart** or only as a local metaphor;
- use chart-switch or paraphrase probes when a mechanism story risks coordinate prestige drift;
- and reserve stronger atlas / gauge-fixing rhetoric for quarantine until chart changes repeatedly preserve the same continuation law.

This does not require a full mathematical atlas.
It requires refusing another archive failure mode: mistaking one compelling coordinate description for the phenomenon itself.

## Transformer-facing implication

If this frame survives pressure, then DelayBasin is probing something sharper than “better metaphors for internal state”:
**whether long-horizon continuation around transformers benefits from a user-space gauge discipline, where the public archive preserves small invariant claims and only secondarily chooses local charts for reasoning about latent state, basins, or control directions.**

That would matter for transformers.
It would suggest that some interpretability and continuation failures are not just missing information, but **coordinate-choice failures**: the archive may preserve enough to continue, yet still overfit to one local chart and misread what was actually invariant.

The stronger story — that DelayBasin may eventually admit a literal **textual gauge-fixing layer** or a **public atlas over continuation space** — remains live, but belongs in quarantine for now.
