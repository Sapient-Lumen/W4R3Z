# Certified core vocabulary and recertification

Status: `speculative but central`

## Core claim

DelayBasin should not treat all local archive language as equally trustworthy.
A long-run archive may work better when it separates:

- **certified core terms** — phrases stable enough to anchor faithful re-entry;
- **provisional / private handles** — useful local words that may steer continuation but are not yet trusted as archive-portable canon.

The live hypothesis is that archive continuity improves when canon is constrained by a **certified core vocabulary** that can be **recertified** when drift appears.

This is not a claim that ordinary prose becomes formal language.
It is a claim that some phrases become important enough that the archive should stop treating them as casual wording.

## Why this now belongs in canon

Several earlier lines in DelayBasin were already converging here:

1. **Control lexicon / constitutional pidgin** suggested that not all high-signal archive text is generic prose.
2. **Private steering handles** suggested that archive-grown words can become locally operative.
3. **Typed continuation protocol** suggested that continuation quality depends on preserving structured surfaces, not just more tokens.
4. Recent semantic-certification work argues that agents can reduce disagreement by certifying a core vocabulary, restricting downstream reasoning to that vocabulary, and periodically recertifying or renegotiating when drift appears.
5. Verifier-bound work argues that admission semantics must be deterministic and fail-closed rather than merely plausible.

Taken together, this suggests a stronger rule:
**the archive should preserve a small certified vocabulary for canon-level continuation and keep weirder or less-stable terms explicitly provisional until they earn promotion.**

## Archive-native mapping

### Certified core terms

These are terms whose meanings are treated as part of bounded constitutional state.
They should have:

- explicit definitions,
- stable dereference points,
- repeated archive use without obvious semantic drift,
- and enough utility that losing them would damage faithful re-entry.

### Provisional / private handles

These are archive-grown local terms that may be useful but are not yet trusted as portable canon.
They can still be valuable.
The point is to keep them from silently masquerading as certified foundations.

### Recertification

A term should be reconsidered when:

- its meaning drifts across revisions,
- it stops being load-bearing,
- close paraphrases seem equally effective,
- or model-family portability becomes important.

### Renegotiation

Some terms may fail recertification but still deserve replacement rather than deletion.
That is not failure.
That is vocabulary maintenance.

## Immediate design consequences

- DelayBasin should maintain an explicit **core lexicon registry**.
- Context pack generation should expose the current certified core and provisional handles.
- New archive-private phrases should default to provisional unless promoted explicitly.
- Canon should prefer certified terms when making claims about method, state, or required actions.

## What this does *not* claim

- not that the archive has solved semantics;
- not that every useful phrase needs bureaucratic treatment;
- not that certification proves model-internal identity;
- not that private handles are fake unless certified.

The claim is narrower:
keeping track of which phrases are core enough to trust may reduce semantic drift in long-run archive continuation.

## What would count against this

- evidence that a core/provisional split adds ceremony without improving re-entry;
- evidence that term stability is irrelevant once other archive surfaces are controlled;
- evidence that certified terms drift anyway and the registry provides no practical warning;
- evidence that archive-private handles generalize or fail in ways uncorrelated with certification status.

## Relationship to stronger speculation

A stronger live speculation is that the archive may be functioning as a **semantic treaty / recertification loop** between human and model, not merely as a vocabulary list.
That stronger ontology remains in quarantine.
