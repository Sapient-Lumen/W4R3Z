# Counterfactual shadow and nearby rejected moves

## Working claim

DelayBasin may need a compact **counterfactual shadow** for substantial revisions:
one nearby rejected alternative and why it was not admitted.

This is not branch explosion.
It is a small honesty device.
Without it, the accepted move can look inevitable after the fact,
and canon can overcredit the winning prose.

## Why this pressure appears now

DelayBasin already preserves:
- certified move classes,
- promotion contracts,
- decay watch,
- recovery kernel,
- revision receipts.

What it still weakly preserves is the **local decision boundary** around a revision.
A receipt can say what happened,
but not what nearby move was seriously live and then rejected.

That omission matters when:
- a revision could have promoted a stronger claim but chose quarantine,
- a wording variant was tempting but semantically laundered trust,
- a branch looked elegant but would have bloated bounded state,
- or a pivot in reasoning steered the archive away from a nearby alternative.

## Minimal counterfactual shadow

A minimal shadow should preserve:
- the nearby rejected move or alternative,
- the pivot surface where the decision concentrated,
- the reason the alternative was not admitted,
- and whether the rejected alternative remains live in quarantine or open questions.

## What this buys

- less winner-writing-history inside the archive,
- sharper re-entry into why a move was accepted rather than merely what was accepted,
- a smaller and more honest local decision boundary for future sessions,
- and a path toward branch comparison without turning the repo into a tree-search dump.

## What it does not buy

- exhaustive exploration of all alternatives,
- proof that the accepted move was best,
- or permission to grow branch bureaucracy.

## Design discipline

Counterfactual shadows should stay tiny.
One real nearby rejected alternative is enough.
If the shadow becomes an essay, it has failed.
If it disappears entirely, the accepted move may start laundering its own inevitability.
