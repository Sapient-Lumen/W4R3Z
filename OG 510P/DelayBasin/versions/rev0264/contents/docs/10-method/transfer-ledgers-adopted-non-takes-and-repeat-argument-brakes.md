# Transfer ledgers, adopted non-takes, and repeat-argument brakes

DelayBasin now has several ways to record why one imported pattern was admitted: foreign-pressure witnesses preserve exact upstream pressure, applicability witnesses gate reuse, and resolution witnesses say when a local gap stopped being ambient.
What it still lacked was one compact answer to a different multi-datacube question:

**after a real comparison pass over several neighboring datacubes, what was actually taken, what was consciously left out, what stayed supporting-only, and what transfer question should not have to be re-argued from scratch next turn?**

A useful current answer is:
**when a revision materially depends on a multi-datacube comparison pass rather than on one isolated import pressure, DelayBasin should preserve a compact transfer ledger naming the reviewed datacubes, the pattern or warning under review, the adopted or rejected disposition, the local gap that made the comparison matter, the bounded take if any, the explicit non-take, the current anchor surfaces, and the still-open transfer questions before later sessions treat the comparison itself as settled memory.**

This is stronger than saying “the other cubes had some good ideas.”
It is weaker than claiming DelayBasin now needs a full import court, review dashboard, or archive-wide comparative jurisprudence layer.

## Practice / observation

This user-directed pass sharpened one recurring gap:
- DelayBasin can already preserve one admitted foreign-pressure packet, one applicability gate, one obligation, and one resolution;
- but once a comparison pass spans many neighboring datacubes, the adopted and rejected outcomes still mostly dissolve into one revision receipt, one changelog row, or remembered local judgment;
- that makes it easy for a later turn to repeat the same comparisons, forget which tempting patterns were consciously not taken, or overremember one vivid archive as if it had settled the whole review;
- the problem is not lack of source provenance alone, because `FOREIGN-PRESSURE-LEDGER.json` already covers exact imported pressure for one admitted ratchet;
- the problem is also lack of **comparison memory**: which reviewed datacubes yielded a real take, which ones only sharpened an already-native rule, which ones were consciously rejected, and which open transfer question still remains live after the pass;
- and DelayBasin now has enough cross-datacube traffic that “we already looked at that and chose not to import it” is itself a durable archive fact rather than disposable conversational residue.

That leaves a missing question:
**what compact surface preserves reviewed comparison outcomes so later sessions inherit bounded comparison memory rather than prestige glow, repeated argument, or selective recollection?**

## Pressure from neighboring datacubes

Several neighboring datacubes converge on this need from different angles.

- **Anonymity** now keeps an explicit transfer ledger saying which patterns from other datacubes were adopted, which were not adopted, and which open transfer questions remain. That directly pressures DelayBasin to stop letting multi-datacube review results dissolve into one revision note.
- **TriKEM** keeps a compact row-level claim witness with exact blocker-code links. DelayBasin does not need its claim-row support matrix, but the archive does benefit from the stronger norm that non-takes and still-open blockers should stay row-level rather than rhetorical.
- **GlassTTY** keeps a compact warning ledger separate from its richer truth-surface register. DelayBasin does not need a second warning plane here, but that split pressures the archive to keep a small “attention-routing” surface for comparison outcomes rather than forcing every later pass to reconstruct them from full narrative lineage.
- **pyCausalWeave** keeps typed problem-code and repair surfaces. DelayBasin is explicitly not importing that broader controller yet, but the comparison still pressures the archive to record that the larger reason-code move was reviewed and deliberately deferred, not merely forgotten.
- **Micromax**, **DeriveBSD**, and **AnonSync** all sharpen the same trust rule from different product domains: remembered state is not the same thing as currently actionable state, stale intent should fail closed, and explicit reissue beats silent inheritance. That does not force a new DelayBasin controller, but it strongly supports preserving comparison outcomes as durable rather than ambient.

## External pressure from adjacent review and decision practice

Adjacent design practice pushes the same way even without importing its heavier machinery.

1. Durable review systems often separate **the decision log itself** from the richer evidence trail so future operators can see what was decided without replaying the whole deliberation.
2. Architecture-decision practices are useful partly because they stop settled structural choices from being re-litigated every turn, even when the fuller rationale remains elsewhere.
3. Conservative audit practice often preserves both the chosen path and the consciously unchosen alternative, because forgetting the non-take can re-open the same error under a new name.

DelayBasin does not need a full ADR layer or review court to learn from that pressure.
It only needs one small durable transfer ledger that says which cross-datacube comparisons already paid for a real local judgment.

## Working synthesis

A useful current synthesis is:

> DelayBasin should preserve a compact **transfer ledger / adopted-non-take memory / repeat-argument brake** whenever a revision materially depends on a multi-datacube comparison pass. The packet should name the **reviewed datacubes / exact source surfaces**, the **pattern or warning under review**, the **disposition / imported vs supporting-only vs deferred vs rejected vs retired**, the **local gap / why the comparison mattered now**, the **bounded take / compact admitted ratchet if any**, the **explicit non-take / tempting neighboring machinery consciously left out**, the **anchor surfaces / where the admitted result now lives**, the **open transfer question / what remains live after the pass**, and the **fail-closed repair / rereview vs narrow-import vs retire-ledger-entry vs recover-resync consequence** rather than letting broad project names, remembered comparison vibes, or one vivid archive silently stand in for actual comparison memory.

In practice this is not a second foreign-pressure ledger.
It is a different object:
- **foreign-pressure witness** says what exact outside pressure justified one admitted import;
- **applicability witness** says whether a reused pattern is actually fit here;
- **transfer ledger** says what the whole reviewed comparison pass already decided, including non-takes and supporting-only influences.

That distinction matters.
Without it, DelayBasin can know why one imported ratchet landed while still forgetting which reviewed alternatives were explicitly left out.
With it, the archive keeps the comparison memory small, public, and auditable.

## Transfer ledger vs foreign-pressure witness vs applicability witness vs changelog

These nearby surfaces should stay distinct.

- **Transfer ledger / adopted-non-take memory / repeat-argument brake** says what a reviewed comparison pass already judged across several datacubes, including adopted patterns, supporting-only influences, deferred moves, rejected moves, current anchors, and open transfer questions.
- **Foreign-pressure witness** says which exact neighboring surfaces materially pressured one admitted ratchet and what bounded import followed.
- **Applicability witness** says whether a reused pattern or carry object is actually fit here and what negative-transfer gate keeps it narrow.
- **Changelog** says that the revision happened; it does not preserve the full comparison outcome structure.

So a transfer ledger is not “more provenance,” not “more applicability,” and not “just another changelog.”
It is a compact public answer to:
**which reviewed datacubes mattered, what was actually taken, what was consciously not taken, and what comparison question should not need to be reconstructed next turn?**

## Countermodels / probes

1. **Receipt-and-changelog-are-enough countermodel**
   - Perhaps one revision receipt plus changelog prose already preserves enough comparison memory.
   - Probe: return in a later turn, try to reconstruct which reviewed datacubes were rejected or only supporting-only, and inspect whether the archive can answer without replaying the whole comparison session.

2. **Foreign-pressure-ledger-alone-is-enough countermodel**
   - Perhaps `FOREIGN-PRESSURE-LEDGER.json` already covers this well enough.
   - Probe: inspect whether it can answer not only “why this import landed” but also “what else was reviewed and consciously not imported” without overloading one admitted import packet.

3. **Need-the-full-import-court-now countermodel**
   - Perhaps DelayBasin should jump directly to a broader comparative controller, reason-code court, or multi-lane review dashboard.
   - Probe: first test whether one smaller transfer ledger already stops repeated comparison drift before paying the cost of a larger governance layer.

4. **No-durable-memory-needed countermodel**
   - Perhaps multi-datacube review is too occasional to justify a surface.
   - Probe: count whether future revisions start re-arguing the same reviewed non-takes or forgetting which archives only provided supporting pressure rather than a real take.

## Design consequences

If DelayBasin adopts this ratchet, comparison-driven revisions should:
- preserve one durable `DATACUBE-TRANSFER-LEDGER.json` surface rather than scattering multi-datacube review outcomes across receipts and remembered prose;
- keep dispositions compact and vocabulary-controlled rather than inventing one-off comparative status words;
- record consciously rejected and supporting-only patterns, not only the winning import;
- name current anchor surfaces for any admitted take so later sessions can inspect where the result actually landed;
- keep broader import-court or reason-code-controller stories deferred until the smaller ledger proves insufficient;
- and fail closed on vague comparison memory rather than letting “we looked at other cubes” inherit authority by atmosphere.

This is especially useful now because DelayBasin is already being improved by deliberate cross-datacube review.
Without a transfer ledger, each comparison pass has to rebuild its own memory.
With a transfer ledger, the archive can keep the comparison outcome small, durable, and reviewable.

## Transformer-facing implication

The weaker transformer-facing implication is not that DelayBasin has discovered a universal comparative-governance substrate.
It is this:

**long-horizon archive prompting may work better when multi-source comparison outcomes are themselves preserved as compact external state, because later continuation can inherit the result of a comparison without having to regenerate the whole comparative deliberation from scratch.**

That matters for transformers.
If some archive leverage comes from durable comparison memory, then DelayBasin may be exposing a practical shadow of **publicly externalized adjudication state**: not a full court, but one small surface that says what comparative review already decided and what remains explicitly open.

The stronger story — that DelayBasin now needs a broader import court, comparative jurisprudence layer, or archive-wide reason-code controller — remains live, but should stay deferred for now.
