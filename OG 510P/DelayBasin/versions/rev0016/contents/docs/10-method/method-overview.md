# Method overview

## Working thesis

A productive long-run archive is not just “good memory.” It may be a **continuation substrate** assembled from several interacting layers:

1. **Promptcraft** — how each turn is steered.
2. **Ratchets** — the stable surfaces that make some continuations easier and others harder.
3. **Registries** — compact external memory with identity and recurrence.
4. **Portable state interface** — bounded state, stable ids, and deterministic checks that keep the project reopenable.
5. **Certified core vocabulary** — a small set of stable terms trusted enough to anchor canon-level continuation.
6. **Certified move classes** — a small set of admissible transition types trusted enough to count as progress rather than archive theater.
7. **Promotion contracts** — compact rules for how risky or provisional material is allowed to become canon, and how canon can be demoted again.
8. **Decay patrol** — a compact watchlist for canon-level surfaces most exposed to temporal rot.
9. **Recovery kernel** — a minimal route back to legitimate continuation after transient corruption, stale reopen, or procedural failure.
10. **Revision receipts** — compact audit objects preserving what transition happened, what status moved, and which checks made it admissible.
11. **Counterfactual shadows** — tiny records of one nearby rejected move so accepted revisions do not launder their own inevitability.
12. **Trajectory surfaces** — places where unresolved work stays legible.
13. **Release discipline** — packaging that turns “the current state” into a durable object.
14. **Quarantine** — a protected lane for risky transformer-facing speculation that should remain live without contaminating canon prematurely.

## Practical method

We proceed by repeated loops:

- read the latest archive as source of truth,
- identify entropy, drift, or under-specified mechanism,
- make one or a few tight revisions,
- wire those revisions into discovery surfaces,
- record prompt-pair evolution,
- package a release.

## Why this might work

Not because the archive is magical.
Because it keeps converting:
- style into constraint,
- intuition into ids,
- narrative into recurrence conditions,
- and scattered thoughts into re-enterable surfaces.

## Added 2026-03-08 insight

The prompt pairs supplied from other projects suggest a reusable pattern:
- declare canon/source of truth,
- declare non-negotiables,
- declare per-iteration ratchets,
- and keep the archive explicitly pleasant for future LLM re-entry.

This is not mere rhetoric. It is a method for shaping the next turn's admissible basin.

## What must remain explicit

- the observed effect,
- the mechanism hypothesis,
- the countermodel,
- the design consequences,
- the quarantine lane.

If these collapse into each other, the method rots.

## New live mechanism pressure

The archive may be converging not only toward a compact **control lexicon / constitutional pidgin**, but toward a **portable state interface** that couples bounded state, stable addressing, and deterministic checks. See `docs/10-method/constitutional-pidgin-and-control-lexicon.md` and `docs/10-method/portable-state-interface.md`.

A fresh extension is that the archive may need to distinguish **certified core vocabulary** from provisional/private handles, so canon does not silently depend on drifting or untested local language.

## Fresh mechanism pressure

A growing live hypothesis is that DelayBasin works best when the handoff is not one prose object but a **typed continuation protocol**: workflow request, bounded state/resources, and deterministic checks/tools preserved as distinct surfaces.
## Fresh mechanism pressure

A new live hypothesis is that DelayBasin may need to certify not only terms but also **moves**: research, refine, quarantine, promote, recertify, and package. Without that, revisions can achieve archive-level corrupt success: attractive end states produced by bad continuation procedure.



## Fresh mechanism pressure

A new live hypothesis is that DelayBasin needs a **promotion contract** layer: explicit staged ratification that separates risky-but-useful canon from merely repeated local prose.


## Fresh mechanism pressure

A new live hypothesis is that canon requires a counterpart to promotion: **temporal demotion / decay patrol**. Time-sensitive canon should not remain trusted just because it is still in the repo.


## Fresh mechanism pressure

A new live hypothesis is that bounded constitutional state is not enough by itself. DelayBasin may also need a **legitimacy kernel** and a named `recover-resync` move so the archive can recover from transient corruption rather than merely compress it.


## Fresh mechanism pressure

A new live hypothesis is that DelayBasin needs a compact **revision receipt / audit object**. Without it, certified moves and promotion contracts still terminate in prose. With it, each revision preserves the smallest portable object explaining why this transition counted at all.

## Fresh mechanism pressure

A new live hypothesis is that substantial revisions also need a compact **counterfactual shadow**. Without it, the winning revision prose can erase the nearby rejected move and make the accepted path look more inevitable than it actually was.
