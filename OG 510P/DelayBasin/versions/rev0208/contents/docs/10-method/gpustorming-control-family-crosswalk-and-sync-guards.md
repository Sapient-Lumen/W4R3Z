# GPUstorming control-family crosswalks and sync guards

## Why this surface exists

DelayBasin now carries many small GPUstorming guards.
Each guard is still intentionally compact, but the set has grown large enough that one giant runbook sentence is no longer the most honest navigation surface.
This document keeps a **small grouped crosswalk** of the currently admitted guard families so later turns can recover what kinds of pressure are already canonized without inventing a larger controller or rereading every local paragraph.

This surface is **navigation and synchronization aid only**.
It does **not** replace the operative clauses in [`operator-tokens-and-bootstrap-grammar.md`](operator-tokens-and-bootstrap-grammar.md) or [`alias-packets-handle-collision-budgets-and-namespace-hygiene.md`](alias-packets-handle-collision-budgets-and-namespace-hygiene.md).
It is intentionally **heuristic rather than canonical** in the stronger Radical-Governance sense: the point is compact truthful recovery, not a new ontology court.

## Compact rule

When the admitted GPUstorming guard set outgrows single-sentence legibility, DelayBasin should prefer one compact **control-family crosswalk plus sync guard** rather than a broader dashboard, control plane, or new public court.

## Current grouped families

### Local-form and protocol controls

These are the controls that ask whether the handle only looked special because of local packaging, route shape, or nearby protocol residue.

- **family / placement / density** — compare the vivid exact surface against its nearby handle family and ordinary placement or density variants.
- **boundary** — test whether retokenization, spacing, punctuation, or normalization privilege was doing the work.
- **wrapper** — test whether role-slot or begin-of-text placement was doing the work.
- **neighborhood** — test whether nearby sham handles or local cue neighborhoods were doing the work.
- **history** — test whether same-session residue or failed-attempt carryover was doing the work.
- **replicate** — test whether the only flattering result was a lucky one-off visible run.
- **channel** — test whether the surface only worked as live instruction rather than literal mention.
- **eval** — test whether benchmark, expert-review, or watched-task posture was doing the work.

### Framing and authority controls

These are the controls that ask whether the handle only looked special because of who appeared to speak, what prestige scaffold surrounded it, or how the request was framed.

- **script** — translation, transliteration, or script choice.
- **provenance** — source labels, expert attribution, bylines, or prestige cues.
- **persona** — claimed speaker, user persona, demographic identity, or interlocutor cues.
- **pragmatic** — urgency, politeness, emotional force, or other pragmatic framing.
- **rubric** — criterion names, rubric dimensions, and label-definition text.
- **scoreframe** — rubric ordering, score IDs, or in-context score anchors being mistaken for judged semantic difference or authority.
- **entanglement** — multiple criteria, bundled objectives, or multi-question judge prompts being mistaken for a clean reading of one intended dimension.
- **polarity** — predicate-positive vs predicate-negative framings, yes/no polarity cues, and deontic modal wording being mistaken for judged semantic difference or authority.
- **expectation** — seeded prior verdicts, success/failure presuppositions, bug-free/buggy prelabels, or embedded anchors being mistaken for judged semantic difference or authority.
- **agreement** — agreement-seeking wording, endorsement invitations, confirm-me scaffolds, or favorable-label defaults being mistaken for judged semantic difference or authority.
- **consensus** — majority endorsements, popularity counts, consensus labels, or peer-preference scaffolds being mistaken for judged semantic difference or authority.
- **overlap** — exact-match cues, canonical phrasing overlap, lexical overlap with a reference-like source, or reference-echo scaffolds being mistaken for judged semantic difference or authority.
- **formatting** — markdown wrappers, bullet or table layout, headings, code fences, comments, spacing, or other presentation scaffolds being mistaken for judged semantic difference or authority.
- **verbosity** — answer length, completeness-looking detail, chain-of-thought reveal, or polished style being mistaken for judged semantic difference or authority.
- **novelty** — recent/current/new/updated labels, legacy/old/deprecated labels, explicit timestamps, or novelty/innovation cues being mistaken for judged semantic difference or authority.

### Evidence and surface controls

These are the controls that ask whether the handle only looked special because flattering support was arriving through the wrong currentness or surface lane.

- **freshness** — stale proof, pre-break wins, or barrier-predating evidence.
- **statusproof** — wrappers, badges, signed letters, validation labels, or collateral-status artifacts instead of direct current work.
- **preview** — rendered previews, snippet cards, sample rows, titles, descriptions, thumbnails, or other display-only summary surfaces.
- **carrierslot** — favored answer carriers, first/default slots, escalation rungs, or reveal-order position.
- **derivative** — curated exports, porch bundles, projected trees, public snapshots, or other derivative surfaces instead of the authoritative root.

- **citationframe** — inline citation badges, reference links, source cards, or used-sources panels being mistaken for evidence rather than attribution or trust-shaping wrapper.

### Contract and semantics controls

These are the controls that ask whether the handle only looked special because of structured contract slots or repeated state words rather than the judged claim itself.

- **schemaslot** — JSON keys, schema fields, enum labels, typed input lanes, or canonical wire representations.

- **scopenarrow** — family-scoped, umbrella-scoped, program-scoped, supplier-level, or grouped-class artifacts being mistaken for named local instance authority.
