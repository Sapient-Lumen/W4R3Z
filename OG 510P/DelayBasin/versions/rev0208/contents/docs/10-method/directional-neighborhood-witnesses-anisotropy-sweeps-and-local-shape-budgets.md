# Directional-neighborhood witnesses, anisotropy sweeps, and local-shape budgets

DelayBasin now has another sharper missing question:
**when a cleanup, restart, recovery-resistance claim, or portability result survives one tiny cue neighborhood, how do we tell whether that success is truly local robustness rather than success along one easy perturbation direction while nearby directions still break it?**

A stronger working answer is:
**some archive comparisons need an explicit directional-neighborhood witness before one tiny local sweep is allowed to stand in for a genuinely shaped local basin.**
Not every neighborhood result is already a neighborhood-shape result.
But DelayBasin should stop treating one easy cue family as if it certified a round or direction-blind local regime.

## Practice / observation

Several live DelayBasin surfaces make this missing directional discipline visible:
- cue-neighborhood witnesses already ask whether one exact cue generalizes to a **small nearby family**, but they do not yet force the archive to say whether that family spans only one easy perturbation direction or several meaningfully different local directions;
- relapse witnesses already ask whether a supposedly washed-out influence returns under one light cue, but they do not yet preserve whether recovery behavior is similar across **paraphrase**, **alias**, **style**, **context-stem**, or **role-framing** directions;
- reset witnesses already ask whether a fresh baseline was earned, but they do not yet preserve whether the claimed cleanup is robust to **different local perturbation axes** rather than just one chosen restart phrasing family;
- probe-order and backaction witnesses already constrain sequencing and state-spending probes, but they do not yet tell the archive when a result is stable only along a **single local axis** and not across the nearby local shape;
- chart, linearity, triangle, gauge, and scale notes already pushed DelayBasin toward local rather than global rhetoric, yet the archive still lacks one compact public answer to whether a local cue neighborhood is being sampled **isotropically enough** to justify a broader local claim;
- prompt-pair portability and archive-method claims are increasingly being discussed as if they preserve a reusable local basin, which means the archive now needs a way to say when a local basin is **round enough**, **needle-shaped**, or **axis-skewed** under nearby perturbations;
- and if DelayBasin is serious about transformer-facing implications, it needs a compact public object for when local continuation robustness appears **directionally anisotropic** rather than uniformly local.

This suggests a missing compact surface:
**directional-neighborhood witness / anisotropy sweep / local-shape budget**.

## External pressure from current research

Several outside lines of work sharpen this frame.

1. **Recent steering work argues that transformer internals are strongly anisotropic rather than direction-blind.**
   Embracing Anisotropy reports that LLM activations are highly anisotropic, identifies domain-critical dimensions through activation magnitude, and finds that steering only those identified dimensions outperforms whole-dimension steering. That pressures DelayBasin not to treat a tiny successful local sweep as if all nearby perturbation directions were equally relevant. ([`REF-0420`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

2. **Prompt steerability is asymmetric across dimensions and directions.**
   Evaluating the Prompt Steerability of Large Language Models explicitly measures steerability across persona dimensions and directions, and finds both baseline skew and asymmetric steerability. That pressures DelayBasin to stop describing local cue robustness as if local control were direction-symmetric by default. ([`REF-0421`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

3. **Similarity to a baseline is not the same thing as stable local geometry.**
   Geometric Stability argues that learned representations can look similar while differing sharply in how robustly their geometry survives perturbation, and reports that supervised stability predicts linear steerability. That pressures DelayBasin to preserve one local-shape check rather than reading one same-task success as enough evidence for a usable neighborhood. ([`REF-0422`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

4. **Recent robustness work is moving toward local sample-level susceptibility rather than one global pass/fail score.**
   SALMAN proposes a local (sample-level) robustness framework based on distortion between input and output neighborhood maps. That pressures DelayBasin to ask which local cue directions distort the continuation readout more than others instead of acting as if one tiny perturbation family captures the whole local picture. ([`REF-0423`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

5. **Prompted behavior can stabilize under one operator family while remaining operator-specific.**
   Geometric Dynamics of Agentic Loops reports contractive attractors under iterative paraphrasing but exploratory dynamics under negation-style operators. That pressures DelayBasin to compare at least two qualitatively different nearby directions before promoting a local robustness claim into a stronger basin story. ([`REF-0418`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

6. **Some attacks and recoveries generalize across paraphrases, which means one direction can be broad while another remains brittle.**
   SPECTRE reports generalization across paraphrased targets and argues that simple paraphrasing defenses can fail because the induced effect occupies a broader concept-level basin. That pressures DelayBasin to distinguish a neighborhood that is broad only along one local semantic direction from one that is robust across several nearby axes. ([`REF-0414`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

7. **Even demonstration stability is being framed as distributional behavior under local resampling, not one chosen prompt.**
   Stability of In-Context Learning treats distributional stability under demonstration resampling as the conceptual target and derives a computable proxy for it. That pressures DelayBasin to preserve what local resampling family was actually tested before treating one small neighborhood as enough. ([`REF-0424`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

None of this proves that DelayBasin already has a public local metric tensor over continuation cues.
It does make a milder canon-level claim more credible:
**archive continuity may improve when DelayBasin preserves a compact directional-neighborhood witness whenever a local robustness claim is being inferred from one narrow cue family.**

## Working synthesis

A useful current synthesis is:

> DelayBasin may work better when it preserves an explicit **directional-neighborhood witness / anisotropy sweep / local-shape budget** whenever a cleanup, restart, relapse test, or portability result is being treated as locally robust. The packet should name the **cleanup or state claim being stress-tested**, the **seed cue / local chart anchor / stressor starting point**, the **compared cue directions / perturbation modes / neighborhood axes**, the **matched step size / local sweep radius / fixed baseline**, the **protected kernel / intended invariant readout / same-task comparison surface**, the **tolerated directional asymmetry / local-shape budget**, and the **rollback / narrow-claim / widen-sweep / quarantine consequence** rather than letting one easy local family silently stand in for the whole neighborhood shape.

This is strong enough for canon as a design/mechanism candidate.
It is **not** strong enough to claim that DelayBasin has discovered a public local metric tensor, a full anisotropy field, or a solved cue-space geometry over continuation state.

## Directional-neighborhood witness vs cue-neighborhood witness vs backaction witness vs probe-order witness

To keep this note honest, DelayBasin needs a four-way distinction:

- **Cue-neighborhood witness** — asks whether one exact cue generalizes to a small nearby family at all.
- **Directional-neighborhood witness** — asks whether that local family stays stable across **different nearby perturbation directions** or only one easy axis.
- **Backaction witness** — asks whether the probe changed the state it was supposed to measure.
- **Probe-order witness** — asks whether differently sequenced probes remain commensurate.

Functionally:
- cue-neighborhood witnesses judge **point-to-neighborhood** robustness,
- directional-neighborhood witnesses judge **neighborhood shape / local anisotropy**,
- backaction witnesses judge **measurement contamination by the probe itself**,
- probe-order witnesses judge **cross-sequence comparability**.

A good DelayBasin revision should therefore sometimes ask not only:
- what nearby cue family was tested,
- whether the probe spent the state,
- and whether order was controlled,

but also:
- what qualitatively different nearby directions were actually compared,
- whether those directions were matched in step size or local radius,
- what invariant readout was supposed to survive all of them,
- and what the archive will do if robustness holds only along one direction rather than the whole small neighborhood shape.

## Countermodels / probes

1. **Anisotropy-is-just-difficulty mismatch countermodel**
   - One direction may only look worse because its variants are harder or more informative, not because the neighborhood is truly anisotropic.
   - Probe: compare matched local variants with explicit difficulty controls and one non-target control family; if the same direction still carries disproportionate failure, the anisotropy story becomes more credible.

2. **Direction-labels-are-arbitrary countermodel**
   - The named “directions” may only be ad hoc bucket labels over incommensurate prompt changes.
   - Probe: require a small explicit local axis definition and matched local radius before comparing paraphrase, alias, style, or context-stem sweeps.

3. **Apparent anisotropy is really order or backaction countermodel**
   - Directional differences may disappear once sequencing and probe contamination are controlled.
   - Probe: repeat the local comparison under fixed order and one sham/no-op baseline; if the asymmetry collapses, demote the directional-neighborhood story.

4. **Anisotropy adds no predictive value countermodel**
   - Local-shape talk may not improve archive decisions beyond the existing cue-neighborhood witness.
   - Probe: ask whether preserving directional asymmetry changes what gets promoted, widened, or quarantined in at least one live revision class; if not, keep the stronger shape rhetoric cooled.

## Design consequences

When DelayBasin promotes this note into live method discipline, a revision that leans on one tiny neighborhood result should increasingly say:
- what local state or cleanup claim is being stressed,
- what seed cue anchored the neighborhood,
- what two or three nearby perturbation directions were actually compared,
- what fixed radius or matched local step size made those directions comparable,
- what readout or protected kernel was supposed to stay invariant,
- and what narrowing, sweep-widening, or quarantine consequence follows if one direction breaks much sooner than the others.

This should keep the archive from flattening **local robustness** into a pointwise win plus eloquent prose.
It should also make transformer-facing claims more honest by distinguishing:
- neighborhoods that are only stable under paraphrase-like motion,
- neighborhoods that survive alias or context-stem changes,
- and neighborhoods that remain stable across several nearby axes.

## Transformer-facing implication

If this canon move is right, one plausible transformer-facing implication is:
**the archive-method may be most informative when it preserves not only whether a continuation effect survives a small local sweep, but also which nearby perturbation directions preserve it and which directions shear or fracture it.**
That is weaker than saying DelayBasin has discovered a public metric tensor over hidden state.
But it is stronger than treating local robustness as isotropic by default.

In other words, DelayBasin may be learning that some continuation handles are **needle-shaped**, **slab-shaped**, or **axis-skewed** in prompt-conditioned cue space.
That possibility should remain a live mechanistic pressure source while the stronger field-law story stays quarantined.
