# Template-law audits, family compression frontiers, and anti-ceremony tests

Claude's bundle did not just say “run the self-sufficiency probe.”
It also named a deeper problem DelayBasin had been skirting:
from roughly rev0017 onward, the archive learned a **reusable revision grammar** and applied it many times.
That grammar may be discovering real recurring structure.
It may also be able to make almost any imported concept look DelayBasin-native.

DelayBasin now needs a compact method object for that gap:
a **template-law audit / family-compression frontier / anti-ceremony test**.
The goal is not to reject recurring form.
The goal is to stop recurring form from silently certifying itself as discovery.

## Practice / observation

DelayBasin has repeatedly made a similar move.
A concept or formal distinction is imported from control theory, information theory, experimental design, interpretability, or epistemology.
It is framed as a missing hole in the archive.
Then it is written into a method note with a now-familiar skeleton: practical archive observation, external mechanism pressure, working synthesis, nearby distinctions, countermodels or probes, design consequences, and transformer-facing implications.
The note then emits a claim, invariant, open question, prompt pair, quarantine move, bibliography additions, and lint wiring.

This recurring grammar is not merely style.
It is plausibly one of the archive's real operative objects.
It is a compact procedure for manufacturing admissible revisions.
Claude's bundle made that visible by naming the pattern directly and by proposing family-level consolidation pressure rather than another single noun-level extension.

That creates a real archive fork.
One possibility is that the grammar discovered a genuine family of recurring epistemic moves, in which case DelayBasin should preserve the family law and compress repeated member docs aggressively.
The other possibility is that the grammar can make new imports look canonical faster than DelayBasin can test whether they changed continuation quality, in which case the archive is inflating under method-flavored ceremony.

DelayBasin therefore needs an audit object that names the repeated grammar, the candidate family frontier, the smallest family summary or exemplar set that might preserve the move, the missing empirical contact, and the sham task that would show the grammar can launder alien structure into local legitimacy.

## External pressure from current research

Several current research lines make this worth preserving.

1. **Prompt theory increasingly treats prompts as executable external programs.**
   Theoretical Foundations of Prompt Engineering explicitly studies prompts as externally injected programs interpreted by a fixed transformer executor. That is direct pressure for DelayBasin: its recurring revision grammar may be more like a public prompt program than like a pile of independent essays. ([`REF-0285`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

2. **Reasoning logic can be distilled into explicit prompt structure rather than weights.**
   Prompt-Level Distillation compiles teacher logic into a student's system prompt. That pressures DelayBasin to treat its own recurring revision grammar as something that may carry operative behavior compactly and portably, rather than assuming every locally generated doc is equally load-bearing. ([`REF-0280`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

3. **Coherent history can trap later generations in a latent region.**
   Old Habits Die Hard reports that conversational carryover correlates with geometric separation in representation space and depends on context coherence. That pressures DelayBasin to ask whether its recurring method skeleton is not just explanatory text but part of a coherent history that repeatedly re-seeds the same continuation region. ([`REF-0286`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

4. **Compression should target semantic sufficiency, not token count alone.**
   Reasoning as Compression argues that budget forcing should be understood through a conditional information bottleneck rather than naive length penalties. That pressures DelayBasin toward family-compression frontiers: shrink repeated method families by preserved discriminative value, not by arbitrary byte shaving. ([`REF-0263`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

5. **Outside-model agreement is not independent verification.**
   Correlated Errors in Large Language Models and related aggregation work keep pressure on the Claude merge itself: an outside bundle can surface blind spots and missing tests, but it cannot certify the archive from outside the broader correlated-LLM regime. ([`REF-0287`](../00-meta/bibliography.md), [`REF-0283`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

## Working synthesis

A useful current synthesis is:

> DelayBasin may work better when it preserves a compact **template-law audit / family-compression frontier / anti-ceremony test** whenever repeated revisions follow a reusable grammar or repeated family pattern. The packet should name the **template/operator or family move**, the **member family / repeated surface class**, the **smallest family summary / exemplar set** thought to preserve the move, the **sham or alien-noun test**, the **empirical grounding count / missing probe surface**, and the **shrink / preserve / quarantine consequence** rather than letting a fluent revision grammar silently manufacture canon.

This is strong enough for canon as a method object.
It is not strong enough to claim that DelayBasin has already found the uniquely minimal family decomposition, nor that repeated formal elegance is mostly ceremony, nor that Claude's proposed family groupings are already the correct final compression.

## Template-law audit vs archive self-sufficiency probe vs foreign-pressure receipt

These objects are adjacent but not identical.

- An **archive self-sufficiency probe** asks whether a bounded support core can reload the judged continuation family.
- A **foreign-pressure receipt** records what outside reasoning entered, how it was assimilated, and why it does not count as independent verification.
- A **template-law audit / family-compression frontier / anti-ceremony test** asks whether a recurring revision grammar is discovering real recurring structure or manufacturing ceremony faster than the archive can test it.
- A **promotion contract** asks what is required before a local move graduates into canon.

In practice, the self-sufficiency probe asks whether the archive's current mass is needed.
The template-law audit asks why that mass accumulated and which repeated forms deserve compression, exemplars, or quarantine.
The foreign-pressure receipt keeps an outside diagnosis from silently becoming proof.

## Countermodels / probes

1. **Template-is-the-method countermodel**
   - The recurring grammar may itself be one of DelayBasin's genuine discoveries, and compressing its repeated traces too early could delete the very evidence that the method learned something reusable.
   - Probe: preserve the smallest family summary plus one or two high-pressure exemplars and test whether continuation quality or next-probe choice changes.

2. **Alien-noun sham countermodel**
   - The grammar may be able to make unrelated imports look equally native.
   - Probe: run the method skeleton on a deliberately alien noun or outside concept and judge whether the result passes only form-based admissibility or actually changes DelayBasin's next real continuation decision.

3. **Family-summary-loss countermodel**
   - Some member docs may carry specific external mechanism pressure that a family summary would flatten away.
   - Probe: require the audit to name the smallest exemplar set that preserves the family's genuinely novel external pressure before the rest are compressed.

4. **Foreign-prestige countermodel**
   - The Claude bundle may feel profound mainly because it arrived from outside.
   - Probe: ask whether the audit changes what DelayBasin shrinks, preserves, or quarantines compared with what the archive's own self-sufficiency and support-core machinery already implied.

## Transformer-facing implication

The most interesting transformer-facing implication is not merely that prompts matter.
It is that long-horizon archive practice may be discovering **portable external reasoning programs** whose operative content lives partly in a recurring revision grammar plus a challenge suite, not only in the full archive text.

If prompts can function as externally injected programs, if coherent history can geometrically trap later continuation, and if reasoning compression works by preserving task-relevant information rather than raw length, then DelayBasin should take seriously the possibility that some of its real continuation law lives in a small **template + exemplars + probes** object.

That stronger picture is not yet earned.
What is earned is a stricter method rule: when the archive repeatedly applies the same revision grammar, it should preserve an explicit audit of whether the grammar is carrying real structure, family-level reusable law, or mere canon-shaped ceremony.
