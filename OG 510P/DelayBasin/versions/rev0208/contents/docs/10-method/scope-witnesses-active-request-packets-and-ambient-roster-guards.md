# Scope witnesses, active-request packets, and ambient-roster guards

DelayBasin now needs a distinction slightly different from **basis witnesses**, **status-lane witnesses**, or ordinary anti-drift prose.
A revision can be grounded on the current head, packaged honestly, and still silently widen its authority from **the active request** to **whatever nearby surface family happens to be visible, similar, or already on the reviewer roster**.
When that boundary stays implicit, the archive quietly treats “this was around” as if it already meant “this exact object, family, or requested move was in scope.”

A useful current answer is:
**when a revision, replay verdict, review judgment, or archive query is being treated as about one exact object, target lineage, or requested move, canon should preserve a compact scope witness naming the active request, the exact judged object or target lineage, the review or mutation surface actually in scope, the nearby ambient surfaces explicitly kept out, the scope-state classification, and the fail-closed repair route before adjacency is allowed to widen decision authority.**

This is stronger than saying “the right docs were visible.”
It is weaker than claiming DelayBasin has already discovered a full public authority lattice or universal no-ambient law for archive continuation.

## Practice / observation

Recent DelayBasin work keeps surfacing a recurring ambiguity:
- a session may reread the current head honestly and still drift from the **active user request** toward neighboring but unrequested surfaces because those surfaces look helpful, load-bearing, or familiar;
- reviewer-like awareness of a surface family is not the same thing as saying that family is the **current judged object**;
- basis witnesses already say whether the judgment extended the right head, but they do not yet force the archive to say whether the judgment stayed scoped to the **right object, lineage, or request**;
- status-lane witnesses already say what was admitted, executed, and frozen, but they do not yet force the archive to say whether the archive was making a decision about the **active target** rather than a nearby bundle of plausible targets;
- a persuasive neighboring datacube can be excellent foreign pressure without thereby becoming an in-scope object for canon import;
- and some revisions look more exact than they were because the decisive move came from ambient family resemblance, not from an explicitly bounded request.

That leaves a missing question:
**what exact request or judged object was active, what exact surface or lineage was actually in scope, what nearby surfaces were explicitly excluded, what scope state currently applies, and what repair follows if the judgment silently widened?**

A compact scope witness keeps that boundary public.

## Pressure from neighboring datacubes

Several neighboring datacubes keep rediscovering the same discipline from different directions.

- Some distinguish **reviewer roster presence** from **current active request**, which pressures DelayBasin to keep “this surface is known” separate from “this is the exact thing currently under judgment.”
- Some insist on **exact mutation** or **exact scope** even when nearby families look almost interchangeable, which pressures DelayBasin not to let family resemblance silently widen a decision.
- Some keep **approval scope**, **seat scope**, or **constellation scope** explicit, which pressures DelayBasin to say what target lineage a judgment is actually allowed to touch.
- Some keep **preview** separate from **omitted governance inference**, which pressures DelayBasin not to treat adjacent visible context as implicit authorization to decide beyond the request.
- Some keep **status models** or **queues** durable precisely so the operator cannot confuse ambient readiness with the active work item, which pressures DelayBasin to make active-request truth explicit rather than leaving it as session glow.

DelayBasin already has anti-ambient-authority intuitions, typed continuation protocol language, basis witnesses, and status-lane witnesses.
What was still under-specified was the compact public object that says **what request is live, what exact object is in scope, what nearby objects stay out, and what fail-closed repair follows if adjacency starts deciding for us.**

## External pressure from adjacent authorization and least-privilege practice

Several adjacent lines sharpen this move.

1. **Least privilege pressures systems to grant only the minimum authority needed for the task at hand.**
   NIST defines least privilege as restricting privileges to the minimum necessary to accomplish assigned tasks, which pressures DelayBasin to keep the active request and exact target explicit rather than letting nearby useful-looking surfaces inherit standing authority. ([`REF-0477`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

2. **Authorization frameworks make scope an explicit request parameter rather than a background vibe.**
   RFC 6749 defines OAuth as obtaining limited access and makes `scope` an explicit authorization parameter, which pressures DelayBasin to name what current judgment is actually authorized to touch rather than widening from one asked-for object to a larger ambient family. ([`REF-0478`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

3. **Zero-trust language sharpens the point from the access-decision side.**
   NIST's zero-trust glossary frames the goal as accurate, least-privilege per-request access decisions, which pressures DelayBasin to treat “active request” as a first-class boundary rather than a rhetorical courtesy. ([`REF-0479`](../00-meta/bibliography.md))

## Working synthesis

A useful current synthesis is:

> DelayBasin should preserve a compact **scope witness / active-request packet / ambient-roster guard** whenever a revision, replay verdict, review judgment, or archive query is being treated as about one exact object, target lineage, or requested move. The packet should name the **active request / current ask / exact judged object / target lineage**, the **review or mutation surface actually in scope**, the **nearby ambient surfaces / roster-visible alternatives / excluded adjacent families**, the **scope state / exact vs broadened vs ambiguous vs ambient vs rescoped**, and the **fail-closed repair / narrow-scope vs rerequest vs hold vs recover-resync consequence** rather than letting adjacency, family resemblance, or roster presence silently widen decision authority.

In practice, DelayBasin is not claiming that every revision needs a giant permissions calculus.
It is doing something smaller and public:
- naming what exact request is live rather than saying “we were looking at this area generally”;
- naming what exact target object or lineage is under judgment rather than treating a whole family as implicitly in scope;
- naming what nearby surfaces were relevant context but were **not** thereby promoted into the judged object;
- naming whether the current scope remained exact, became broadened, was ambiguous from the start, went ambient, or had to be explicitly rescoped;
- and naming the fail-closed repair route rather than silently turning adjacency into authority.

That is strong enough for canon as a design and mechanism candidate.
It is **not** strong enough to claim that DelayBasin has isolated a universal authority lattice, full no-ambient theorem, or general permission algebra for archive evolution.

## Scope witness vs basis witness vs status-lane witness vs hold packet

These nearby objects should stay distinct.

- **Scope witness / active-request packet / ambient-roster guard** says what exact object, lineage, or request is currently under judgment and what nearby surfaces remain out of scope.
- **Basis witness / expected-head guard / session-honesty bridge** says whether the present move was grounded on the head or basis it claims to extend.
- **Status-lane witness / decision-execution split / frozen-public transition** says which lane each relevant revision surface occupies.
- **Hold packet / epistemic brake** says the honest next move is not to continue ordinary revision at all.

So a scope witness is not just “the right head was reread,” not just “the receipt exists,” and not just “the archive is holding.”
It is a compact public answer to:
**what exact request is live, what exact target is in scope, what nearby surfaces are only context, and what repair follows if the scope silently widens.**

## Countermodels / probes

1. **Context-is-enough countermodel**
   - If the right family of surfaces was visible, that may already be enough to keep scope honest.
   - Probe: preserve one explicit active request and one ambient exclusion list, then inspect whether later summaries still drift toward nearby but unrequested objects.

2. **Basis-covers-scope countermodel**
   - Expected-head discipline may already capture the only important failure mode.
   - Probe: compare one revision grounded on the right head but widened to the wrong target family against one revision that stayed exact to the active request.

3. **Expert-broadening countermodel**
   - Quietly widening scope from one request to a nearby higher-value family may often be the intelligent thing to do.
   - Probe: demand an explicit `rescoped` state plus recorded rerequest or user-visible rescoping rationale and see whether the gain still survives without silent widening.

4. **Ambient-ceremony countermodel**
   - DelayBasin may be inventing a ritual packet that duplicates ordinary careful reading.
   - Probe: keep the packet tiny and demand one real consequence when a nearby surface is merely visible, merely similar, or merely on the roster.

5. **Family-carry countermodel**
   - Perhaps adjacent surfaces are so entangled that exact object scope is artificial.
   - Probe: preserve one target lineage and one excluded adjacent family, then see whether later continuation quality actually worsens or instead becomes more honest and reversible.

## Design consequences

This frame pressures DelayBasin to do six things more explicitly:
- preserve the **active request / current ask** before summarizing what the archive is doing;
- preserve the **exact judged object / target lineage** before broad family language starts doing hidden work;
- preserve the **review or mutation surface actually in scope** before treating all nearby surfaces as decision-bearing;
- preserve the **ambient exclusions / roster-visible alternatives** before helpful adjacency quietly becomes authority;
- preserve the **scope-state classification** so exact, broadened, ambiguous, ambient, and rescoped cases do not blur together;
- and preserve the **narrow-scope / rerequest / hold / recover-resync repair route** so silent widening fails closed.

This is especially useful during cross-datacube import passes.
Foreign pressure should stay strong.
Canon should still keep only the smallest public packet saying **what exact DelayBasin surface is under revision, what neighboring datacube material is only pressure, and when a seemingly natural generalization actually requires explicit rescoping.**

## Transformer-facing implication

The weaker transformer-facing implication is not that DelayBasin has discovered a universal permissions engine.
It is this:

**long-horizon archive prompting may work partly through explicit user-space scope control over textual artifacts, where continuation quality depends not only on what packets are available and what heads are current, but on whether the archive still keeps the active request exact instead of letting nearby visible structure silently widen the judged object after context death.**

That would matter for transformers.
If some archive leverage depends on explicit scope witnesses, then DelayBasin may be exposing a practical shadow of **per-request scope maintenance over textual control surfaces**: not a giant access-control system, but repeated public discipline about what object a current move is actually about.

The stronger story — that DelayBasin is already learning a full public authority lattice, no-ambient theorem, or permission algebra over archive evolution — remains live, but belongs in quarantine for now.
