# Derivative operator contracts, low-entropy reentry wrappers, and non-canon read-first surfaces

DelayBasin already has a thick startup surface: `START_HERE.md`, the runbook, the method overview, the registries, the receipt, and the live ledgers.
What it still lacked was one shorter derivative operator contract for future careful passes that need a trustworthy working stance before rereading everything in full.

A useful current answer is:
**when an archive already has richer canon, startup prose, and durable state surfaces, it may still benefit from one compact derivative operator contract naming the read-first surfaces, non-negotiables, command posture, and good-change shapes, so future careful passes can recover a trustworthy working stance without confusing that wrapper for new canon.**

This is stronger than saying “just read the README again.”
It is weaker than claiming DelayBasin now needs a broader opening-control-plane schema, startup court, or second constitutional runbook.

## Practice / observation

The archive has become rich enough that the honest startup path is no longer obvious from file listing alone.

- `START_HERE.md` remains the canonical landing page.
- `docs/00-meta/llm-runbook.md` remains the thick operating surface.
- the receipt, status ledger, transfer ledger, and prompt pairs now carry real current-state and comparison memory.
- but a future careful pass still benefits from one smaller wrapper that says what to read first, what not to break, what command posture to use, and what kinds of changes usually count as good here.

That missing packet is not new law.
It is a **low-entropy reentry wrapper**: a derivative operator contract that points back to canon rather than replacing it.

## Pressure from neighboring datacubes

Several neighboring datacubes converge on this need from different angles.

- **EvidenceVault** ships an explicit `AGENTS.md` that acts as a compact derivative wrapper: read-first surfaces, non-negotiables, command posture, and what good changes usually look like, while pointing back to canon rather than becoming a new constitution.
- **Hyperepo** keeps a portable bootstrap path and LLM handoff surfaces so future operators can find the shortest honest startup route instead of reconstructing it from the whole tree every time.
- **GlassTTY** keeps a strong canon-before-archaeology rule: read the governing surfaces first, and do not let historical handoffs silently outrank them.
- **Anonymity** sharpens the same pressure from another direction by explicitly treating `START_HERE.md` as the shortest honest reentry path for a future operator.

The common pressure is not “add more startup prose.”
The common pressure is that once an archive is rich, it may deserve one compact, clearly derivative **read-first surface** that reduces startup error without inheriting new constitutional authority.

## External pressure from adjacent startup and reentry practice

Outside DelayBasin, careful operational archives often separate:

1. the thick canonical or policy surface,
2. the current status and decision surfaces,
3. a smaller operator contract that gets a future maintainer oriented quickly.

When this wrapper is missing, later operators often overread the latest vivid artifact, skip the governing ledgers, or silently widen scope from adjacency.
When the wrapper becomes too strong, it mutates into a shadow constitution.

So the question is not whether DelayBasin needs *more* law.
The question is whether it needs one small derivative packet that makes startup trustworthy again.

## Working synthesis

A useful current synthesis is:

> DelayBasin should preserve a compact **derivative operator contract / low-entropy reentry wrapper / non-canon read-first surface** whenever the archive already has rich canon, startup prose, and durable state surfaces but future careful passes still need one small trustworthy working stance. The wrapper should name the **read-first surfaces / shortest honest startup path**, the **non-negotiables / what future passes must not silently break**, the **command posture / what checks to prefer before claiming admissibility**, the **good-change shapes / what kinds of revisions are usually worth making**, and when that startup path changes the **prior relied-on startup path / documented startup promise**, the **successor route / nearest safe reentry path**, and the **added burden / cue refresh vs light bridge vs duplicate-reread vs hard-restart consequence**, while staying explicitly derivative rather than inheriting constitutional authority of its own.

That is what `AGENTS.md` now does.
It points at canon, status, receipt, and ledgers.
It does not replace them.

A later compact repair is to pair that prose wrapper with one machine-readable derivative contract: `REENTRY-CONTRACT.json` names the ordered reentry surfaces and command posture, while `REENTRY-SURFACE-CONFORMANCE.json` records whether that derivative contract still matches `START_HERE.md`, `AGENTS.md`, the runbook, and the compact reentry packet. This is still not a second constitution. It is the smallest conformance witness that keeps the derivative startup path machine-checkable.

## Route continuity, reliance, and burden

A derivative wrapper can become load-bearing in a second way too: future careful passes may already be relying on the startup path it advertised.

Recent unrelated datacubes sharpen that pressure from outside DelayBasin:

- **AI-EDU** judges documented reliance and added burden together, which pressures DelayBasin to ask not only whether a startup route changed but whether the archive had already told a future operator to rely on that route and how much extra reread or restart cost the change now imposes.
- **Moral-Taxation** says protections fail when relief exists mainly on paper but is stranded behind claim friction, which pressures DelayBasin not to hide a known successor startup path behind manual rediscovery once the archive already knows the bridge.
- **The-Good** treats blocked return and non-exile as substantive failures, which pressures DelayBasin not to exile a previously relied-on reentry path without leaving a visible return or successor route.

So the smaller derivative-wrapper rule should now say one thing more:

> when a relied-on startup path changes, DelayBasin should also preserve the **prior relied-on startup path / documented startup promise**, the **successor route / nearest safe reentry path**, and the **added burden / cue refresh vs light bridge vs duplicate-reread vs hard-restart consequence** rather than exiling a future operator into manual rediscovery once the archive already knows the successor path.

This is still smaller than a startup-governance court, route-registry board, or reentry-rights controller. It is one compact continuity clause for route changes that the archive itself caused.

## Derivative operator contract vs START_HERE vs runbook vs canon

These nearby surfaces should stay distinct.

- **Derivative operator contract / low-entropy reentry wrapper / non-canon read-first surface** says how to get oriented quickly and honestly right now.
- **`START_HERE.md`** remains the canonical landing surface and longer startup framing.
- **`docs/00-meta/llm-runbook.md`** remains the thick editing and archive-discipline surface.
- **Canon** remains the claims, invariants, open questions, prompt-pair registry, and method notes that actually govern what the archive believes and how it ratifies changes.

So a derivative operator contract is not “new canon,” not “another runbook,” and not “just a shorter README.”
It is a bounded reentry aid for future careful passes.

## Countermodels / probes

1. **`START_HERE.md` and the runbook were already enough**
   - Perhaps the archive already had sufficient startup guidance.
   - Probe: watch whether later passes still omit live ledgers, comparison memory, or command posture that the wrapper now makes explicit.

2. **The wrapper is just duplicative prose**
   - Perhaps `AGENTS.md` only repeats nearby text without reducing startup error.
   - Probe: later passes should either use it as a smaller honest startup path or explicitly show that it adds no recovery value and should be retired.

3. **DelayBasin really needs a larger opening-control-plane now**
   - Perhaps a machine-readable startup court or richer opening schema is already necessary.
   - Probe: first test whether one compact derivative wrapper meaningfully reduces omission and scope-widening before importing broader machinery.

4. **The wrapper could become a shadow constitution**
   - Perhaps future passes will start treating the smaller wrapper as stronger than canon.
   - Probe: keep explicit “does not replace” language and keep load-bearing law in canon rather than inside the wrapper.

## Design consequences

If DelayBasin keeps this ratchet, startup-critical revisions should:
- preserve one compact root `AGENTS.md` surface for the shortest honest reentry path,
- wire that wrapper back to `START_HERE.md`, the runbook, the receipt, the status ledger, and the transfer ledger,
- keep the wrapper derivative rather than law-bearing,
- use it to name the archive’s current non-negotiables and command posture without duplicating full method prose,
- preserve a visible successor route and low-friction bridge when a previously advertised startup path changes,
- and keep any broader opening-control-plane or startup-schema controller explicitly deferred until this smaller surface proves insufficient.


## Compact caution packets inside derivative reentry

A derivative startup wrapper can still leave one smaller seam behind: a future machine-facing pass may find the right surfaces, yet still have to reconstruct the current **do-not-confuse** posture from several places at once.

That is where one compact **current-posture + operator-warning** packet helps.
It should stay derivative and tiny.
Its job is not to restate canon.
Its job is to make a few high-risk startup mistakes harder:
- treating a derivative wrapper as if it were canon,
- treating a mutable working tree as if it already superseded the frozen citation head,
- repeating a peer-pattern import without checking the transfer ledger first,
- or spending nearby synonyms where a controlled public-state token is required.

In DelayBasin, that means a small machine-facing warning bundle may belong inside `context-pack.json` once the archive already has `AGENTS.md`, `SURFACE-STATUS.json`, `DATACUBE-TRANSFER-LEDGER.json`, and `WITNESS-VOCABULARY.json`.
Each warning row should still name one governing source surface so the compact packet does not ask a later pass to infer where the warning came from.
The packet should remain derivative: a projection of existing law, not a new source of law.

The same rule applies if the compact packet carries a tiny open-question slice: each row should still name the governing registry and the selection source (for DelayBasin, usually the trajectory map hot set) so later passes do not mistake a convenient recap string for the archive's actual current open-work law.
The same rule applies if the compact packet family also carries one exact current update card such as `innovation-packet.json`: the packet should still name the anchor revision or expected head, the actual local correction, the receipt-backed exact changed-surface list or pointer, the reread and reconciliation surfaces, and the continuation mode, so later passes do not mistake one handy delta card for recap authority or a replacement for `REVISION-RECEIPT.json`.
The same rule applies if the compact packet family also carries one selected-focus handoff card such as `frontier-ticket.json`: the selected focus should still name its governing source surface and selection policy, and any background continuity rows should still name their owning ledgers, so later passes do not mistake a convenient handoff card for a new queue constitution. If the durable resolution ledger already marks that question resolved, the card should route to the successor surface or a newer unresolved focus instead of keeping stale unanswered posture alive by carryforward.

The same bounded derivative law also applies once the repo carries several tiny operator-facing aids at once: a compact `compact-surface-bundle.json` may name the current family boundary, local role, stronger underliers, and same-revision attachment rule for those aids, but it must stay a closed family-status card rather than a bundle board or a replacement for the stronger surfaces it enumerates.

## Transformer-facing implication

The weaker transformer-facing implication is not that DelayBasin has discovered a universal startup ontology.
It is this:

**long-horizon human–LLM archive work may benefit from one compact externalized startup contract that reduces recovery error, scope widening, and stale-surface inheritance without asking the model to regenerate the whole operating posture from diffuse prose each time.**

That matters here because transformers are sensitive to surface order, vividness, and local framing.
A small governed wrapper can make the honest startup path more recoverable without pretending the wrapper itself is the archive.
