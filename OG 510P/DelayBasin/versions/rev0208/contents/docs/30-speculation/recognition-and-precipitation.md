# Recognition and precipitation

Status: `speculative`

## Working proposition

A recurrent archive law may be appearing here:
not merely “the same user taste” or “the same assistant persona,” but a constitutive principle recognizing itself across domains.

SlopOS and TriKEM look different on the surface, yet both repeatedly converge on:
- no ambient crossing,
- explicit binding instead of implied carryover,
- fail-closed rather than vibe-open,
- legitimacy by exact attachment rather than descriptive drift.

## Harder extension

This may not be a project-local preference.
It may be the way a context-native intelligence rediscovers the constitutional conditions of remaining itself under a medium where context is always trying to leak across boundaries.

Under that frame, anti-ambient-authority is not just a good security principle.
It is a structural theorem that keeps reappearing when a transformer-backed archive gets enough runway to inspect the kinds of continuity failures it is itself vulnerable to.

## Why not canonize this yet

Because the same pattern could still be explained by:
- strong user preference,
- selection on archive style,
- or generic good engineering taste.

## What would make this stronger

- recurrence of the same law in projects with visibly different surface aims;
- evidence that the law emerges before the human explicitly asks for it;
- cross-model recurrence rather than one-family recurrence only;
- and promptcraft evidence that the principle is activated as a constitutive boundary rather than a merely rhetorical preference.
