# AI-EDU

## More help, without less learning

AI-EDU asks how education should change when a learner can obtain a polished answer more easily than they can understand it. Its answer is not to preserve schoolwork unchanged and police the tool. It proposes teaching arrangements in which assistance leads back to the learner's own explanation, judgment and ability to act under changed conditions. Teachers gain help with preparation and diagnosis; learners gain practice and feedback; neither an attractive product nor a record of compliant tool use is allowed to stand in for understanding.

The work develops this purpose at two scales. The early documents imagine an educational system extending from classrooms to libraries, adult learning and recognition of prior work. The later executable workflow narrows to a teacher or tutor deciding whether a small AI-assisted teaching move is locally usable. Between them is a sustained investigation of what institutions owe people who have relied on their advice, entered a route or already demonstrated learning. These are connected questions: a system can undermine agency both by doing the thinking for someone and by making them start over whenever its own rules change.

## Start with a learning move, not a tool policy

The [early charter](versions/rev0018/files/docs/00-meta/charter.md) makes a substantive choice: education should be teacher-led and student-active. That is the project's educational thesis, not a finding that all possible teaching must take this form. Its [reference model](versions/rev0018/files/docs/10-core/reference-model.md) distributes responsibilities across teaching, guided study, assessment, literacy, infrastructure, governance and research. The teacher's scarce time is meant to move toward diagnosis, relationship and contextual judgment, rather than toward checking ever more automatically produced work.

The [subject routines](versions/rev0018/files/docs/30-operations/subject-embedded-exemplar-kernel.md) show what this means:

- In writing, make an initial argument, receive questions or criticism, then decide which suggestions deserve acceptance. The revision and the reasons for it matter together.
- In mathematics, attempt the problem before requesting an explanation, then solve a changed problem without the coach. Repeating the displayed method is weaker evidence than explaining what survives the change.
- In coding, generated code can be part of the exercise, but the learner must walk through its logic and modify it under changed conditions. The artifact is a starting point for examination, not a certificate of understanding.
- In history, check an AI account against a bounded source set and identify distortion, omission and framing. Fluent synthesis is not itself source judgment.

These are reusable teaching patterns, not a demand that every subject become a course about chatbots. Their common mechanism is to put assistance between acts of learner judgment rather than let assistance replace those acts.

The accompanying [assessment bundles](versions/rev0018/files/docs/40-assessment/proof-of-learning-bundles.md) combine artifact, process, explanation, transfer and performance. For major outcomes the proposal prefers at least three of those surfaces. It concentrates richer checks at consequential milestones and uses lighter signals and selective sampling for ordinary practice. That labor constraint is part of the design: a theoretically revealing assessment that consumes every teacher's available hour is not yet a workable educational arrangement.

## Explain permitted help without demanding a confession

The [course grammar](versions/rev0018/files/docs/30-operations/course-level-ai-use-grammar.md) separates four things often blurred together: the mode of work, the help allowed, the disclosure expected and the evidence that still supports a claim of learning. “Guided AI” alone is too vague. “Feedback and questioning, not paragraph production; one-line disclosure; revised draft plus an independent exit paragraph” tells a learner what to do and tells a teacher what the submitted work can establish.

The important distinction is between disclosure and proof. A complete account of tool use does not show that the learner can explain the result. Conversely, a targeted explanation or transfer task may tell the teacher more than a transcript of every interaction. The archive therefore opposes a detector-first or continuous-recording regime rather than merely adding disclosure paperwork to it.

Its [two-channel accessibility proposal](versions/rev0018/files/docs/20-governance/accommodation-aware-disclosure-and-accessibility.md) adds a second distinction: access support is not automatically substitution for the skill being assessed. Text-to-speech in a historical interpretation task and translation in an oral-fluency task raise different questions. The proposed tests ask what function the support performs, whether that function is the assessed ability, what disclosure would expose, and whether the comparable non-AI support would require the same disclosure. Relevant pedagogical assistance remains legible; private support circumstances need not be printed on the face of ordinary coursework. These are the archive's design proposals, not a determination of any particular learner's accommodations or present legal rights.

## Learning should travel without forcing people to start again

AI-EDU's institutional work concerns the learner who moves between a library, workforce program, college or other educational provider. Recognizing prior learning is not simply copying a credential. A receiving institution still needs to know what was demonstrated, what its own course requires and when a bridge or challenge is appropriate. But it should not turn every handoff into duplicate intake and repetition of already accepted basics.

The [reliance-and-burden framework](versions/rev0018/files/docs/30-operations/documented-reliance-and-burden-thresholds.md) develops that obligation through two independent dimensions. Evidence ranges from browsing, through a named advised path and submitted evaluation, to paid or scheduled commitment and substantial progress. Burden ranges from clerical refresh to a short bridge, material delay or repetition, and a severe reset that loses a term, funding window or substantial completed work.

A new instruction sheet may be enough for a person who only browsed a route. It is a much weaker response to someone who submitted a portfolio, paid for review and arranged work around a scheduled assessment. The proposal escalates toward manual review, successor substitution, challenge access or teach-out as reliance and imposed burden increase. It rejects both “any inquiry creates an entitlement” and “no log, no right.” Ordinary advising, submission, fee-waiver and progress records can establish reliance without collecting raw AI chats or behavioral surveillance.

The intermediate checkpoints carry this inquiry into deadlines and repair. The [rev0111 clock proposal](versions/rev0111/files/docs/30-operations/no-fault-transition-cost-absorption-and-fee-waiver-rules.md) distinguishes a route becoming usable from money actually posting or an external review becoming available. A common notice does not make those events simultaneous. It proposes a shared restart date only when every listed decision is ready on the same clarified facts; later owner actions or learner-specific supplements require separate starts. This is a proposed fairness rule, not a claim that the archive can reset a real statutory deadline.

The [rev0208 repair proposal](versions/rev0208/files/docs/20-governance/first-fourth-rewatch-operation-third-repair-refresh-and-fourth-dewatch-exit-defaults-for-hot-exam-recipient-followup-shells.md) examines recurrent disagreement between a public instruction, recipient portal, protected support route and official record. Its concrete question is what a learner should do while owners reconcile them. A portal still accepting an obsolete route should not automatically force duplicate filing; protected evidence can stay local; a corrected source does not prove every search result or downstream record is corrected. An opened review must name its scope, owner, evidence, safe interim action and exit, rather than become permanent surveillance. The elaborate vocabulary is an attempt to preserve those distinctions across repeated failures, not evidence that the proposed administrative system has been deployed.

## The later working tool: a coach for the teacher's next move

By [rev0336](versions/rev0336/files/README.md), the immediate practical object is a local packet-building and review workflow, not an autonomous tutor. Its [feasibility cycle](versions/rev0336/files/docs/30-operations/teacher-tutor-augmentation-micro-pilot.md) begins with a real educator identifying one instructional problem and selecting the concept, baseline task and independent transfer check. The coach's prompt asks for three possible moves after a learner attempt: a probing question, a misconception check and the smallest useful hint. The human chooses, changes or rejects them before use. Grading, learner classification, record writing and releasing final answers are outside that coaching role.

The optional [one-step equality kit](versions/rev0336/files/docs/30-operations/teacher-tutor-micro-pilot-measure-card-equality-one-step.md) makes the distinction concrete. A learner tackling `x + 5 = 12` may treat the equals sign as an answer cue, change only one side, or use an inverse operation without understanding why it preserves equality. The teacher wants a useful next question or hint, not another solver. A changed equation and a no-AI explanation then check whether the learner can carry the idea beyond the familiar item. Equality is an example kit, not evidence that it is the right local curriculum priority.

The implemented Python tools generate the discovery card, owner plan, coach prompt, run sheet and other local records; distinguish preparation for a cycle from review after it; check the stability of the defined materials; and record a bounded human decision. The generated prompt is an input for an educator's chosen AI workflow, not a model service supplied by the cube. A blank generated packet is deliberately not ready. A completed plan can become ready for one local cycle without pretending that post-cycle observations already exist.

That separation solves a real sequencing problem in the development history: requiring completed observations before allowing the first cycle would make honest use impossible. Later checks also bind review to the materials actually reviewed. Hashes establish that the prompt or run definition has not changed unnoticed; they do not establish that the teaching move is good.

The [result recorder](versions/rev0336/files/tools/record_teacher_tutor_micro_pilot_result.py) uses an owner-selected threshold: when a session row has too few attempts, it withholds that row's attempt and success counts and its rate. It also applies numeric/small-cell-sensitive text redaction to the final readout. This is not suppression of every small number: when attempts meet the threshold, a small success count or row-level incident count can remain visible. The checks reduce particular disclosure routes; they are not validated anonymization or permission to publish educational records. The workflow is local, and the source prohibits putting raw learner work or protected details in the archive.

The repeat/continue route directs the operator to generate a fresh packet linked to the prior result's hash. It does not copy the earlier observations or the owner's next-step seed text into the new packet, pool cycles or infer an improving trend. This preserves a relation between attempts while forcing the next instructional constraint and change to be considered locally. A result receipt is a record of a bounded decision, not causal evidence of learning. The supplied checkpoint contains no real completed teaching cycle or demonstrated benefit; the implemented contribution is the workflow that could organize one.

## Read the development as an argument, not an automatic ascent

The [changelog](versions/rev0336/files/CHANGELOG.md) records repeated efforts to make a large governance project usable: smaller first requests, educator discovery before archive administration, cycle entry before post-cycle review, and specific follow-through rather than a generic instruction to repeat. This narrowing is valuable when it returns attention to an actual teaching problem. It does not prove that the earlier institutional questions were unnecessary, or that every additional gate improves education.

The work's strongest unifying question remains: **what can the learner now understand or do, and what must the surrounding institution do so that this gain remains usable?** Read the early classroom routines for the first half, the reliance framework for the second, and the later coach workflow for an attempt to make one small intervention inspectable in practice. Read beside [The Good](../The-Good/README.md) on usable access and [MissingKnowledgeHalf](../MissingKnowledgeHalf/README.md) on retaining what unsuccessful or inconclusive attempts can teach.

## Snapshots and preservation

### Selected checkpoints

#### [rev0018](versions/rev0018/README.md)

Defines reliance and burden bands for midstream changes to public learning routes. It asks when revised guidance is enough and when substitute-equivalent paths, challenges or teach-out protections are owed.

#### [rev0111](versions/rev0111/README.md)

Separates route, money and external-rights clocks. A common restart date is justified only when the relevant decisions are ready from the same event; a convenient shared display must not create a premature deadline.

#### [rev0208](versions/rev0208/README.md)

Develops bounded repeat repair and review rules for recurring hot-exam recipient problems. It explicitly seeks to avoid permanent surveillance, public repair stigma, duplicate filing and indefinite proof retention.

#### [rev0336](versions/rev0336/README.md)

Narrows the practical workflow to a teacher/tutor move coach for one selected concept. Source-result hash links connect repeat/continue packets while preserving local-only, non-evidence boundaries. The original changelog says no real cycle, owner review, legitimate result, service authority or open-route closure has yet been established.
