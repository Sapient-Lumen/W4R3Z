## Non-negotiables

- Keep the archive small, linked, and cumulative.
- Add evidence only when it changes design judgment.
- Preserve the distinction between what is **canon**, what is **open**, and what is **quarantined**.
- When making policy or procurement claims, name the evidence threshold.
- When making design claims, say what educational failure mode they are meant to prevent.
- Treat teacher agency, student learning, and institutional safety as co-equal constraints.
- Do not let “AI literacy” degenerate into tool tips detached from curriculum, rights, or assessment.
- Prefer mode-tagged subject routines and a light common course grammar over generic exhortation whenever implementation is the real problem.
- Preserve the distinction between pedagogical disclosure and protected accessibility/accommodation routing so integrity policy does not chill legitimate support use.
- Preserve learner-controlled handoff, small typed packets, recognition-before-duplication, sector-specific recognition gates, dated standing-equivalency lists, a bounded exception grammar, privacy-light maintenance rules for appeals/false matches, and a thin public maintenance-history profile when the design judgment concerns adult routes, public access, or cross-node transfer.

## Command posture

Run `make lint` before packaging a revision. If you add or remove a surface, update `ARCHIVE_INDEX.md`, `CHANGELOG.md`, and `REVISION_RECEIPT.json` together.

## What good changes usually do

Good revisions usually do one or more of the following:

- clarify what learning should be measured once AI assistance is common,
- tighten the procurement/evidence bar,
- refine the teacher–student–institution role split,
- turn a vague policy concern into a concrete design constraint,
- preserve a bold idea in quarantine without smuggling it into canon,
- or reduce archive size while improving future recovery.
