# Reference model

This document states the archive's current best compact blueprint for **education with AI**.

## The seven-layer model

### Layer 1. Safe institutional substrate

Every institution needs a governed AI workspace with identity, access controls, privacy protections, logging appropriate to educational use, clear data boundaries, and opt-out paths for learners and families where needed. The archive currently assumes this is a prerequisite for serious scale. The substrate also needs an accessibility and support-routing layer: some AI-mediated activity belongs in ordinary course policy, while some belongs in protected accommodation/access channels with tighter privacy controls and clearer human ownership.

### Layer 2. Teacher co-intelligence layer

Teachers should receive AI support for:

- lesson adaptation,
- formative question generation,
- rubric drafting,
- feedback drafting,
- translation and accessibility support,
- and administrative compression.

The purpose is not to script teachers into compliance, but to free scarce time for diagnosis, relationship, live teaching, and expert judgment.

### Layer 3. Student study-companion layer

Every learner should have access to a guided AI study companion that is optimized for:

- explanation,
- retrieval practice,
- questioning,
- scaffolding,
- worked-example fading,
- error diagnosis,
- and metacognitive reflection.

The archive does **not** currently endorse a default “answer engine” posture for student-facing systems. The more promising shape is guided or Socratic support, visible in recent provider moves and in tutoring research. See `B13`, `B14`, `B15`.

### Layer 4. Proof-of-learning layer

Institutions should add explicit mechanisms that preserve evidentiary value when AI use is normal, and pair them with simple course-level legibility about allowed use and disclosure:

- supervised or live checkpoints,
- oral defenses,
- in-class transfer tasks,
- process logs and drafts,
- selective teacher audits,
- performance tasks,
- and portfolio-based demonstrations tied to clear outcomes.

The archive's default posture is that these mechanisms should be assembled into **bundles** matched to age band, subject family, and stake level rather than applied indiscriminately. See [`../40-assessment/proof-of-learning-bundles.md`](../40-assessment/proof-of-learning-bundles.md).

### Layer 5. AI literacy layer

AI literacy should be a strand across grades and sectors, not a one-off assembly or optional seminar. Students need to learn safe use, critique, provenance, limitations, and appropriate delegation. Teachers need a deeper equivalent, including pedagogical use and misuse. The archive now treats age-banded progression plus a small exemplar kernel and a light course-level use/disclosure grammar as specified enough to guide local implementation. See [`minimal-ai-literacy-spine.md`](minimal-ai-literacy-spine.md), [`../30-operations/subject-embedded-exemplar-kernel.md`](../30-operations/subject-embedded-exemplar-kernel.md), and [`../30-operations/course-level-ai-use-grammar.md`](../30-operations/course-level-ai-use-grammar.md).

### Layer 6. Procurement and governance layer

Institutions need explicit thresholds for adoption, pilot design, equity review, rights review, accommodation routing, and retirement of bad tools. The archive's current posture is that educational AI should be treated more like a governed public-interest technology than like ordinary classroom software. This layer now includes a concrete accessibility floor for institutional tools and a two-channel rule separating pedagogical disclosure from protected support routing.

### Layer 7. Improvement and research layer

Every serious deployment should produce learnings about what actually improved:

- teacher workload,
- student understanding,
- participation,
- equity outcomes,
- accessibility,
- and unintended harms.

Public R&D efforts such as IES U-GAIN matter because they point toward a future in which educational AI is studied as an educational intervention rather than merely adopted as enterprise software. See `B12`.


## Cross-cutting lifelong public access mesh

The archive now treats education with AI as extending beyond ordinary course shells. Community colleges, VET and adult-education providers, public workforce systems, libraries, and learning-city/community spaces form a **public access mesh** for adults, workers in transition, older learners, and residents who are not currently enrolled anywhere. Formal institutions still carry the deepest curricular and credentialing work, but these public nodes provide first contact, bridge routes, navigation, and ongoing participation across the lifespan. The mesh is now paired with a compact public handoff standard, a typed portability rail, sector-specific recognition defaults, dated standing-equivalency lists, a tiny publication profile with family-specific recency windows, a bounded exception grammar for partial equivalencies and local overrides, a privacy-light maintenance rule for learning from appeals, disagreement, and false matches over time, a thin public history/profile that lets learners and partner systems see state changes without raw counts, and a dedicated in-flight protection grammar for learners already in motion when standing rules change, now paired with a compact evidence-and-burden rule that distinguishes browsing from documented reliance and clerical refresh from material reset: low-friction entry, short foundational learning, human navigation, warm transfer, learner-controlled portability, recognition before duplication, small machine-readable claims, short human-readable handoff notes, protected local support/evidence records, published gates for when outside learning should mean continuity only, waiver of duplicate basics, stronger review, or formal credit, narrow public tables for recurring pre-cleared cases with expiry and manual-review fallback, a small interoperable feed that says what each standing rule applies to and how long it remains trustworthy, a tiny exception layer that carries repeated public constraints without dragging whole local policy manuals into the shared layer, a maintenance layer that converts repeated appeal/reversal patterns into dated rule updates without centralizing learner dossiers, a future-use-first downstream action grammar that still prefers teach-out, successor substitution, challenge access, or construct-close equivalents over hard restart for good-faith in-flight reliance, and a small local evidence vocabulary plus burden bands for deciding when stronger midstream protection is actually owed. See [`../30-operations/lifelong-public-ai-learning-stack.md`](../30-operations/lifelong-public-ai-learning-stack.md), [`../30-operations/minimum-public-entitlement-and-handoff-standard.md`](../30-operations/minimum-public-entitlement-and-handoff-standard.md), [`../30-operations/portable-public-learning-packet-and-recognition-profile.md`](../30-operations/portable-public-learning-packet-and-recognition-profile.md), [`../30-operations/sector-defaults-for-public-ai-learning-recognition.md`](../30-operations/sector-defaults-for-public-ai-learning-recognition.md), [`../30-operations/standing-equivalency-lists-and-review-governance.md`](../30-operations/standing-equivalency-lists-and-review-governance.md), [`../30-operations/standing-list-publication-profile-and-recency-windows.md`](../30-operations/standing-list-publication-profile-and-recency-windows.md), [`../30-operations/standing-recognition-exception-profile-and-local-overrides.md`](../30-operations/standing-recognition-exception-profile-and-local-overrides.md), [`../30-operations/appeal-feedback-and-standing-list-maintenance.md`](../30-operations/appeal-feedback-and-standing-list-maintenance.md), [`../30-operations/public-maintenance-history-and-trust-signals.md`](../30-operations/public-maintenance-history-and-trust-signals.md), [`../30-operations/partner-consumption-and-grandfathering-rules.md`](../30-operations/partner-consumption-and-grandfathering-rules.md), [`../30-operations/in-flight-teach-out-and-substitute-equivalent-rules.md`](../30-operations/in-flight-teach-out-and-substitute-equivalent-rules.md), and [`../30-operations/documented-reliance-and-burden-thresholds.md`](../30-operations/documented-reliance-and-burden-thresholds.md).

## Core role split

### Teachers

Accountable for purpose, review, adaptation, escalation, care, and final judgment.

### Students

Active users of AI for practice, exploration, drafting, critique, and reflection—but within norms that preserve responsibility for learning.

### Institutions

Responsible for safe infrastructure, procurement discipline, AI literacy provision, and assessment redesign.

### AI systems

Useful when they help extend explanation, feedback, personalization, and teacher capacity; unsafe when they replace responsibility, conceal reasoning failures, or dilute the evidence of learning.

## Compact thesis

The right institutional shape is **not** “AI replaces teachers.”

It is closer to: **every teacher gets a serious co-pilot, every learner gets a serious tutor/study companion, every institution gets a serious proof-and-governance layer, the curriculum teaches people how to use all of this without surrendering judgment, and the public system offers lifelong entry routes plus a small handoff-and-recognition rail beyond formal enrolment, including dated standing lists for recurring outside-learning cases, a tiny machine-readable publication profile that keeps those lists fresh and legible across systems, a bounded exception grammar that keeps repeated local constraints public without turning interoperability into brittle policy code, a privacy-light maintenance rule that lets those standing rules learn from appeals and reversals without becoming a shared case-tracking regime, a thin public maintenance-history/profile that publishes rule state and impact without publishing operational telemetry, an in-flight teach-out/substitute-equivalent rule that protects good-faith learners from midstream restart when continuity is still lawfully and pedagogically possible, and a small evidence-and-burden grammar for deciding when that stronger protection is actually owed.**
