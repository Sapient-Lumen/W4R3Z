AI-EDU is a compact, cumulative archive for designing an education system that treats AI neither as a ban-worthy novelty nor as an excuse to automate schooling into emptiness. The archive is built around six commitments:

1. **Learning over task completion.** AI should increase understanding, transfer, agency, and access—not just help students finish work faster.
2. **Teacher-centred institutions.** Teachers remain the primary accountable adults for instruction, judgment, care, and escalation.
3. **Assessment redesign, not denial.** Once capable AI is normal, many legacy take-home tasks stop functioning as reliable proxies for learning, so institutions need explicit proof-of-learning bundles.
4. **Curricular AI literacy, not prompt fashion.** Students and teachers need staged competence in how to use, inspect, disclose, and govern AI across the lifespan. That competence should cash out in subject-embedded, mode-tagged routines and a light course-level use/disclosure grammar rather than abstract framework language alone.
5. **Evidence, equity, and safety by default.** Access, privacy, bias, disability inclusion, cultural responsiveness, and anti-manipulation safeguards are first-order design requirements, and accommodation/access support should not be collapsed into the same disclosure rail as ordinary authorship.
6. **Lifelong public routes, not school-only AI adoption.** Community colleges, VET, workforce systems, libraries, and learning-city/community spaces are part of the educational system AI will reshape; the archive therefore treats public first-contact points, adult transition routes, a small cross-node handoff standard, a typed portability/recognition rail, sector-specific recognition gates, reviewable standing-equivalency lists, and a tiny machine-readable publication profile with family-specific freshness windows, plus a bounded exception grammar for local overrides and partial equivalencies, a privacy-light maintenance rule for learning from appeals and false matches over time, and a thin public maintenance-history layer that publishes state, dates, scope, and short reasons without raw counts or case traces, plus a partner-consumption / grandfathering rule that defaults most maintenance changes to future-use effects rather than retroactive churn, as core infrastructure rather than side cases.

The archive is intentionally small. It prefers ratcheted syntheses, open questions, and explicit ledgers over large dumps of literature.
