# AI-EDU — rev0018

## When a learning route changes beneath someone

A learner submits a portfolio along a route recommended by an adviser. Before the evaluation is finished, the institution changes the rule. Must the learner start again?

This edition asks what the institution owes someone already relying on its guidance. A public route needs correction when its requirements become stale or its recognition rules prove too broad. But correcting the system should not automatically erase the learner's progress or make them absorb all the resulting delay and expense. The [reliance-and-burden framework](files/docs/30-operations/documented-reliance-and-burden-thresholds.md) makes that tension concrete: identify what the learner relied on, what the change would cost them, and which defensible continuation could preserve their learning.

This is the institutional-continuity strand of a larger educational proposal. The [charter](files/docs/00-meta/charter.md) seeks greater understanding, agency, access and teacher capacity rather than polished answers without learning. The [collection introduction](../../README.md) follows its classroom practices and later teacher-coach workflow. Here, the question is what makes learning remain usable when it crosses institutions or their rules change.

## Two questions, not one ladder

**Reliance concerns the relationship already established.** Browsing a course page differs from receiving a named advised route; submitting a portfolio differs from merely expressing interest. The framework separates generic interest, institutional guidance, a submitted or evaluated case, a paid or scheduled commitment, and meaningful progress or near-completion. A fee officially waived counts among the commitment traces: protection is not reserved for those able to pay.

**Burden concerns what the new demand would do.** Refreshing a document already in hand is different from repeating accepted basics, paying for another assessment, missing a funding window, or losing a term. The burden bands distinguish clerical refresh, a light bridge, material delay or repetition, and severe reset. A scheduling or accessibility change can alter whether participation remains feasible, even when it looks small on an administrator's checklist.

Keep the same submitted portfolio in view. If the change asks only for a clearer syllabus, with no lost place or missed cycle, the proposal routes that submitted case toward a successor or equivalent path. If instead it imposes repeated basics or pushes the learner beyond the next meaningful start window, ordinary restart is presumptively too harsh: the default moves toward an equivalent route or an opportunity to demonstrate competence without repeating the underlying learning. If substantial completed progress would be lost, the case for completion protection becomes stronger still.

These are proposed presumptions for human judgement, not an automatic entitlement calculator. Nor does protection begin only when both dimensions reach their highest band. An advised path can already justify preserving the queue and avoiding duplicate intake; a submitted case calls for manual review before denial. The document's detailed matrix is more discriminating than a single high-reliance/high-burden threshold.

## Preserve progress without freezing every rule

The preceding [in-flight protection document](files/docs/30-operations/in-flight-teach-out-and-substitute-equivalent-rules.md) supplies the practical alternatives:

- **Preserve place and review:** keep the person in the process rather than sending them back to its entrance.
- **Use a successor:** move to a replacement recognition entry where the learning and recognition limits remain materially continuous.
- **Offer an equivalent route:** use a closely matched requirement or short bridge that preserves as much prior progress as possible.
- **Allow a challenge or portfolio review:** let the learner show the competence instead of repeating the full learning process.
- **Protect completion through teach-out:** retain a path to completion through the old route or an approved equivalent, especially for near-finish cases.

“Equivalent” does not mean merely convenient. The source's *construct fit* asks whether the learning being recognized actually matches the ability or outcome required. An expired shortcut can justify fresh examination without making prior learning worthless. Equally, continuity cannot silently turn a major mismatch into formal credit.

The strongest objection is that preserving reliance might perpetuate a bad decision. The archive takes that objection seriously: fraud, failed authority or legal invalidity can break the old route, and genuine learning mismatches constrain what can be preserved. Independent demonstration of competence may still offer a way forward; where it cannot, the proposal allows what it calls a graceful restart rather than pretending the former claim remains valid. Its aim is the least-disruptive defensible continuation, not automatic credit or unconditional grandfathering.

## Evidence for fairness, not a surveillance record

The proposed evidence is ordinary institutional material: advising notes, referrals, submission timestamps, evaluation-start markers, fee or waiver records, scheduled assessments and progress records. The framework rejects both “any inquiry creates entitlement” and “no log, no right.” Its latter warning matters because institutions should not benefit from ignoring or erasing the ordinary traces of advice and progress.

Raw AI conversations, keystroke histories and broad behavioral telemetry are not required. Accommodation and other protected support details should not become public merely to prove reliance. These traces generally stay local rather than travelling by default in a portable learning packet. A record can support a fair decision without becoming a universal learner dossier.

## What this edition settles for itself, and leaves open

The [revision history](files/CHANGELOG.md) moves from public learning handoffs and recognition, through maintenance of standing rules, to what partners should do for people already in motion. rev0017 names the protections; rev0018 specifies evidence and burden bands for choosing among them.

Its next question is fiscal: when a system-caused change still requires a bridge, challenge or equivalent route, which fees and support costs should the system absorb? It has a presumption against casually imposing new costs and no universal numeric cap for delay or expense. Classification is therefore not the end of the moral argument: an offered alternative can remain unusable if the learner cannot afford it.

The documents draw analogies from the public teach-out, transfer and appeal materials recorded in their historical bibliography. These are the archive's normative proposals, not a statement of current educational law or evidence that the arrangement has been deployed.
