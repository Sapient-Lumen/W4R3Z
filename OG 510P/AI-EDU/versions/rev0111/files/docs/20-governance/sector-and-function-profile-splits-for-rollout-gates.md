# Sector and function profile splits for rollout gates

This document closes the archive's next governance gap about **which rollout-gate expectations should already split by sector and function before local evidence accumulates, because one generic `RG0-RG4` ladder is too blunt for minors, formal assessment, professional gatekeeping, and public-route services**.

The archive's current bet is:

> keep one generic rollout ladder as the floor, but publish a second tiny starter profile layer wherever developmental duty, qualification validity, supervised-practice safety, or benefits-linked public routing already makes a uniform “promising pilot → broader rollout” rule misleading.

The point is to avoid five predictable failures at once:

- **pilot glow travels too far** — a promising local result is treated as if it had already survived different cohorts, ordinary staff, and ordinary calendar pressure;
- **context-transfer fiction** — institutions treat tutoring help, assessment-critical tooling, minor-facing services, and public-route triage as if they share the same burden of proof for expansion;
- **exceptional-operator dependence** — early wins generated by unusually strong teachers, tutors, coordinators, or researchers are mistaken for ordinary-operating readiness;
- **rights-blind scale** — broad rollout happens before subgroup, accessibility, contestability, or family/public-legibility questions have caught up to the function actually being scaled;
- **quiet infrastructure by drift** — a tool that should remain a governed `RG2-RG3` service slips toward `RG4` simply because it keeps working well enough for central office convenience.

Current public signals point in the same direction. OECD's 2026 Digital Education Outlook says successful task performance with general-purpose GenAI does not automatically produce learning and argues that educational tools with clear pedagogical purpose, co-design, and rigorous research support are a different proposition from casual general-purpose use. Digital Promise's 2026 state-guidance analysis then makes the maturity problem explicit: most states remain in nascent or piloting stages, and wider adoption should follow clearer evidence that tools improve learning. SETDA's 2025 trends report adds the operational warning that AI is now a top state priority while durable funding plans and professional learning still lag. IES's 2026 school-turnaround synthesis sharpens the function split further: tutoring evidence looks strongest, teacher-support tools look promising but still emerging, and predictive or early-warning uses remain more mixed and action-path dependent. Stanford's 2026 review of the K-12 evidence base adds the final caution: the research base is growing quickly, but rigorous causal evidence is still thin and concentrated in a relatively small number of use types. Ofqual's 2026 AI-marking principles sharpen the assessment end by centering fairness, validity-linked accountability, and contestability, while the European Commission's AI Act guidance and the U.S. Department of Labor's AI Literacy Framework help separate public-route informational help from hotter public-service decision support. See `B11`, `B126`, `B127`, `B130`, `B131`, `B132`, `B133`, `B135`.

## Relationship to the generic rollout ladder

This document does not replace [`pilot-to-scale-evidence-and-rollout-gates.md`](pilot-to-scale-evidence-and-rollout-gates.md), [`evidence-and-procurement.md`](evidence-and-procurement.md), or [`general-purpose-vs-purpose-built-deployment-ladder.md`](general-purpose-vs-purpose-built-deployment-ladder.md).

It adds one thing only:

- a **starter profile layer** for places where the archive already knows the generic rollout expectations need a pre-declared split.

The generic rollout ladder still answers the first question:

- is the institution still exploring,
- running a bounded pilot,
- expanding under active review,
- approving broad routine use,
- or treating the service as ordinary infrastructure?

This document answers the second question:

- in this sector or function, does the archive already know that broad rollout should require a hotter proof package, a stronger rights review, a lower ceiling, or a presumption that the service should remain governed rather than become background infrastructure?

## The starter profile table

The archive keeps this table intentionally small. These are **starter inherited profiles**, not a full implementation-science manual.

| Profile | Scope | Ordinary examples | Default hot adjustments | What broad approval additionally requires | Default cap / remain-local warnings | Why the split exists |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `RP-K12-MINORS` | repeated learner-facing or school-managed AI for minors, including tutoring, study help, attendance/behaviour follow-up, wellbeing-adjacent support, and family-facing school AI | school homework help, revision copilots, attendance nudges, school support chat, multilingual family help | treat cross-school expansion as minimum `RG2` with subgroup/access review and visible family/staff handling instructions; treat younger-cohort expansion, new persistence, new external action, or movement into attendance/wellbeing/support routing as hotter than the original pilot claim | for `RG3`, require evidence from more than one ordinary school context or term, realistic staff training beyond enthusiasts, and proof that fallback and safeguarding routing survive ordinary use | default against `RG4` for direct student-facing copilots, attendance/wellbeing-adjacent services, or systems whose safe use still depends on visible adult mediation; a promising local tutor may scale, but a quiet child-facing background service rarely should | minors create a faster duty of care and a lower tolerance for dependency, behavioural drift, or hidden escalation, so “helpful in one school” is not yet enough reason to make the service ambient |

| `RP-PUBLIC-ROUTE` | adult basic-education, workforce, library, and public-service learning routes where AI can influence intake, queue order, referral, funded-seat access, or linked-service movement | intake chat, route triage, seat-allocation support, multilingual navigator help, referral support, benefits-adjacent learning navigation | treat expansion from informational help into triage, queue shaping, referral, or funded-route recommendation as hotter than the original claim and at least `RG2` with explicit contestability and human-route checks | for `RG3`, require evidence that people can still understand, challenge, and bypass the service without losing place or access, plus durable staffing for the human alternative during incidents and review cycles | default against `RG4` for queue-shaping, eligibility-adjacent, or cross-agency route tools; background infrastructure is especially dangerous where the user may have little practical choice but to go through the system | public-route systems can look clerical from the inside while materially shaping access to scarce seats, funded support, or linked services, so a generic “successful pilot” story is too weak here |

## Reading the profiles correctly

These profiles are **overlays**, not replacements.

The generic rollout ladder still answers the first question:

- what stage of institutional maturity is this service actually in?

This document answers the second question:

- in this sector or function, does the archive already know that some stages should begin hotter, some approvals need stronger evidence, or some services should be presumed to stay governed rather than disappear into `RG4`?

That means the same underlying model can inherit different rollout expectations in different settings:

- an ordinary teacher planning copilot may travel under the generic rollout ladder;
- the same model wrapped as a school-managed learner helper for minors belongs under `RP-K12-MINORS`;
- a similar tool used inside controlled assessment belongs under `RP-FORMAL-ASSESS`, where broad approval has a lower ceiling and a hotter validity burden;
- and a route-helper used for public intake, referral, or funded-seat movement belongs under `RP-PUBLIC-ROUTE`, where explanation and human-route continuity matter immediately.


## Starter-profile disposition rule

A starter rollout profile should never harden by drift.

At each real review point, the institution should make one explicit disposition for the inherited profile:

| Disposition | Use it when | What publication should say |
|---|---|---|
| **harden unchanged** | the same profile keeps fitting across ordinary contexts without hidden extra safeguards or hero operators | this profile may now be inherited as written for the named function and context |
| **branch** | the service remains promising, but one recurring context change predictably alters burden, ceiling, or review path | publish the child branch and stop pretending the parent profile still travels unchanged |
| **stay provisional / local** | the evidence is still too dependent on protected pilots, exceptional staff, or unresolved access/funding/governance questions | keep the profile as a starter overlay only, not a hardened inherited default |
| **cap below `RG4`** | the service is useful and stable enough for governed routine use, but not for invisible infrastructure | publish the highest gate the service may ordinarily reach and keep the human alternative visible |

The point is to block a familiar mistake: treating years of acceptable use as if they automatically prove that the same profile now travels everywhere or deserves background-infrastructure status.

## Five tests before a starter profile hardens

A starter rollout profile should harden unchanged only when all five tests are substantially met.

### 1. Claim portability

The educational claim must still be the same claim.

If the service has shifted from optional help to school-managed expectation, from tutoring help to attendance or wellbeing follow-up, from practice support to readiness signalling, or from informational route help to queue-shaping recommendation, the archive treats that as a branch trigger rather than as continued evidence for the old profile.

### 2. Ordinary-operating portability

The service must work under ordinary staffing, training, and calendar pressure.

If success still depends on unusually strong pilot champions, protected cohorts, researcher/vendor hand-holding, extra temporary funding, or bespoke monitoring that ordinary adopters will not sustain, the profile has not hardened yet even if outcomes look good.

### 3. Governance portability

The same memory, observability, failure, and change assumptions must still fit.

If later sites only succeed by adding hotter persistence, broader trace capture, tighter freezes, or more frequent manual triage than the parent profile assumed, that is evidence for a branch or cap, not for unchanged inheritance.

### 4. Human-path portability

The human alternative, complaint path, and no-penalty bypass route must still work at ordinary volume.

If people can challenge, opt around, or fall back to humans only in the pilot's protected conditions, the archive treats the profile as still provisional even when measured effectiveness looks strong.

### 5. Ceiling fit

A hardened profile still needs a separate judgment about whether the service should ever become `RG4`.

Some services can harden as stable governed `RG2-RG3` profiles while remaining permanently capped below default infrastructure, especially when safe use still depends on visible adult mediation, contestability, validity review, or route-protection duties.

## What counts as a branch trigger rather than hardening

The archive now treats seven recurring changes as presumptive branch triggers for rollout profiles:

1. **consequence stretch** — the service begins affecting progression, qualification, readiness signalling, queue order, funded-seat movement, or linked-service access in ways the parent profile did not cover;
2. **cohort stretch** — the service moves to younger minors, more vulnerable users, mandatory use, or ordinary-volume use outside the protected pilot population;
3. **modality stretch** — text-only help becomes multimodal capture, live monitoring, or background routing;
4. **persistence stretch** — the service starts remembering, joining, or reusing person-level information more durably than the parent profile assumed;
5. **workflow stretch** — optional assistive use becomes school-managed workflow, standing recommendation, or cross-office operational dependence;
6. **fallback stretch** — the human route exists in principle but no longer remains timely, legible, or non-penalizing at real scale;
7. **ceiling stretch** — people start acting as if a governed service should now disappear into ordinary infrastructure even though the public reasons for visible governance are still active.

One branch trigger does not always mean the service is bad. It means the parent profile no longer carries enough information.

## What the archive now treats as enough to cap below `RG4`

The archive now makes one tighter judgment explicit: **profile hardening and infrastructure approval are different questions**.

A service may earn repeated evidence, survive ordinary staffing, and still deserve a published ceiling below `RG4` when any of the following remain true:

- safe use still depends on visible adult mediation;
- assessment validity still depends on explicit reviewable handling rather than background automation;
- readiness, progression, queueing, referral, or access effects still require contestable human treatment;
- the human alternative would otherwise atrophy if the service were treated as background infrastructure;
- or the public reason for the service's caution is structural rather than temporary.

This matters because some of the archive's hottest contexts may mature into stable governed services without ever becoming silent institutional rails.

## The first child branches the archive now publishes

The archive keeps this split intentionally small. It does **not** try to map every educational-AI product type. It only names the first branch where the parent overlays were still hiding too much practical difference.

The repeating pattern is simple:

- one branch covers **support / augmentation** uses where the system helps a human-led educational process without directly shaping high-consequence treatment;
- the other branch covers **consequence-bearing or route-shaping** uses where the same technical family starts influencing marks, readiness, progression, queue order, referrals, or support treatment.

| Child branch | Parent profile | Covers | Default rollout posture | What broad approval additionally requires | Default ceiling signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `RP-K12-TUTOR` | `RP-K12-MINORS` | school-managed tutoring, revision help, or practice support for minors with visible adult framing and no attendance/wellbeing routing | may progress under the hotter K-12 minors floor, but still starts at least `RG2` once it becomes cross-school or routinely provisioned | evidence of learning gain or tutor/teacher augmentation under ordinary staffing, plus visible family/staff handling instructions and a live adult fallback | often governed below `RG4`; routine availability may be fine, but an ambient child-facing copilot should rarely become invisible infrastructure |
| `RP-K12-SUPPORT-ROUTE` | `RP-K12-MINORS` | attendance follow-up, behaviour/wellbeing-adjacent support, family messaging that shapes support routing, or school support chat that can trigger action | presumptively hotter than `RP-K12-TUTOR`; cross-school or cross-office rollout should begin at `RG2` and often stay visibly governed longer | safeguarding review, false-positive / false-negative handling, no-penalty human route, and proof that ordinary staff can understand and override the system's treatment logic | strong presumption against `RG4`; many such services should remain bounded `RG2-RG3` services if used at all |
| `RP-ASSESS-MARK-SUPPORT` | `RP-FORMAL-ASSESS` | assessor-facing marking support, rubric assistance, script triage, or oral-exam workflow help that does not itself impose candidate consequences | may move toward governed `RG3` only after real-condition validity and fairness review | evidence that marks, reviews, and manual recheck paths stay sound under operational pressure, not just in lab or classroom conditions | often capped below `RG4` whenever human review remains necessary for validity or public confidence |
| `RP-ASSESS-CANDIDATE-CONTROL` | `RP-FORMAL-ASSESS` | cheating-monitoring, trace capture, candidate-facing control tools, flagging logic, or any assessment AI whose output can directly shape candidate treatment | hotter than marking support by default; multi-site live use should usually begin no lower than `RG2` and may remain local or tightly governed | direct evidence that fairness, contestability, substitute/manual paths, and error-handling remain credible under real assessment conditions | strong presumption against `RG4`; many such tools should never become background infrastructure |
| `RP-PRO-COACH` | `RP-PRO-PRACTICE` | simulation feedback, practicum coaching, remediation support, or supervisor-visible learning help that augments rather than signals readiness | can progress under a governed professional-practice floor when named supervisors retain direct responsibility | evidence that the system improves coaching or remediation without displacing supervisor judgment, and that the service behaves acceptably across ordinary programmes or cohorts | often stable as governed `RG2-RG3`; `RG4` is unusual whenever safe use depends on named supervisor mediation |
| `RP-PRO-SIGNAL` | `RP-PRO-PRACTICE` | readiness signalling, placement recommendation, progression triage, or other AI that materially shapes supervised-practice treatment | presumptively hotter than `RP-PRO-COACH`; even apparently helpful pilots should not inherit the lower-consequence branch's scale path | supervisor-accountable appeal/review, evidence against proxy or hidden gatekeeping effects, and proof that direct professional oversight remains real rather than ceremonial | default against `RG4`; many services should remain bounded augmentation or not scale at all |
| `RP-ROUTE-INFO` | `RP-PUBLIC-ROUTE` | multilingual wayfinding, informational navigation, eligibility explanation, appointment preparation, or general learning-route help that does not rank or allocate people | may move toward `RG3` when the service remains informational, legible, and easy to bypass without losing place or access | durable human alternative, clear statement that the system does not prioritize people or allocate scarce opportunities, and monitoring that misinformation/error correction works at ordinary volume | may sometimes approach `RG4` only when the service remains truly informational and reversible; otherwise keep it governed |
| `RP-ROUTE-QUEUE` | `RP-PUBLIC-ROUTE` | queue shaping, referral prioritization, funded-seat recommendation, cross-agency route steering, or other AI that affects who moves first or where they are sent | presumptively hotter than `RP-ROUTE-INFO`; should begin at least at `RG2` with explicit contestability and public-service review | explanation, no-loss-of-place bypass, human override, input-quality checks, and, where applicable, public-service fundamental-rights review before first use | strong presumption against `RG4`; queue- and access-shaping systems should remain visibly governed if they are used at all |

## What these child branches change in practice

The archive now makes four tighter judgments explicit:

1. **minor-facing tutoring is not the same branch as minor-facing support routing** — helpful study support may travel further than attendance, wellbeing, or support-treatment systems even when both are technically “student help”;
2. **marking support is not the same branch as candidate control** — assessor-facing aid and candidate-affecting monitoring or flagging should not inherit one assessment rollout path;
3. **professional coaching is not the same branch as readiness signalling** — support for supervised learning and signals that shape access to live practice have materially different public consequences;
4. **informational public-route help is not the same branch as queue shaping** — a multilingual navigator or explainer may deserve a different ceiling from a tool that changes referrals, order, or funded access.

These branches do not eliminate local judgment. They simply stop the archive from pretending that one hotter parent profile already says enough once support use turns into treatment.


## What the archive now treats as knowable in advance

Local evidence still matters, but the archive now treats five rollout facts as knowable **before** local implementation evidence accumulates.

### 1. Not every successful `RG1` deserves the same `RG2` or `RG3` burden

Tutoring help, teacher planning assistance, assessment-critical tooling, and queue-shaping public-route systems should not inherit the same default expansion expectations just because each can produce a promising pilot story.

### 2. Younger cohorts and hotter consequences are not evidence-neutral context changes

A tool that looked acceptable with older students, volunteer tutors, or optional help use has not yet shown readiness for younger minors, formal progression consequences, or benefits-linked routing.

### 3. Exceptional implementers do not prove ordinary-operating readiness

If success depended on unusually strong tutors, specialist coordinators, highly protected pilots, or heavy researcher/vendor support, the archive prefers a remain-local or still-`RG2` judgment rather than scale by admiration.

### 4. Durable funding and training are part of the evidence package, not an afterthought

SETDA's current signal matters here: pilot excitement can outpace durable funding and professional learning. The archive therefore treats support capacity as part of scale readiness, not as something to sort out after approval. See `B131`.

### 5. Some services should stop at governed routine use rather than become invisible infrastructure

`RG4` is not the natural destiny of every educational-AI service. Direct student-facing child services, assessment-critical tools, and route-shaping public systems often deserve a persistent governed status even when they are useful and stable.

## What institutions should publish

Where a local rollout rule inherits one of these profiles, publish one extra line with only six additions beyond the generic rollout fields:

1. profile identifier (`RP-K12-MINORS` through `RP-PUBLIC-ROUTE` or local equivalent);
2. which expansions are presumed hotter than the generic ladder suggests;
3. what extra evidence broad approval requires in that profile;
4. whether the profile carries a presumption against `RG4` for some functions;
5. who must sign off on scale or reopen in addition to the ordinary service owner;
6. where users can find the human alternative or complaint path while the service remains at a governed gate.

This keeps the public layer small while blocking the common failure where an institution publishes one generic rollout rule but never says that minors, formal assessment, professional gatekeeping, and public-route services already require different expansion burdens or lower infrastructure ceilings.

## What counted as a real archive gain

The archive previously knew that educational-AI adoption should rise with stakes and that promising pilots should not jump straight into default infrastructure. It still lacked a compact answer to a harder operational question: **where do we already know that one generic rollout ladder is too permissive, too transferable, or too infrastructure-hungry?**

This document answers that with a bounded starter layer. It keeps the generic `RG0-RG4` grammar, but it now makes the archive more deployable across schools, assessment systems, professional education, and public-route coordination without pretending those contexts share one default path from pilot to scale.

## Current archive bet

The archive's current best guess is that a **generic rollout ladder plus a tiny sector-and-function profile layer** will outperform both extremes:

- one universal pilot-to-scale rule for all educational-AI services;
- and bespoke case-by-case rollout writing with no inherited defaults.

That claim is now canon, but still live. The next problem has moved sideways: which institution-facing starter profiles are stable enough to harden, where they should branch further by office or cross-agency context, and where they should retreat back toward human-only handling. See `OQ-0007`.
