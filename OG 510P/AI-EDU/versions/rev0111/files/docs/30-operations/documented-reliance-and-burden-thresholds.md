# Documented reliance and burden thresholds

This document closes the archive's live gap around **documented learner reliance**.

The archive already had an in-flight protection grammar (`I0`-`I3`, `T0`-`T5`). What it still lacked was a sharper answer to two linked questions:

> **what evidence is enough to show that a learner truly relied on a standing rule or advised route, and when does the burden of a midstream change become large enough that ordinary substitute-equivalent routing is no longer enough?**

The archive's answer is:

> **evidence and burden should be judged together. Mere browsing does not create a teach-out right, but documented institutional guidance, submitted review, paid/scheduled commitment, or meaningful progress can. Once a system-caused change would impose material delay, repeat already-accepted basics, or add non-trivial direct cost, stronger protection is owed.**

This move is grounded in a converging public pattern. HLC's current teach-out rules require fair and equitable treatment, reasonable opportunities to complete, disclosure of the credits a receiving institution will accept, declared time windows, and disclosure of tuition, fees, records retention, and communication plans. HLC's current provisional-plan guidance also recommends curriculum crosswalks and clear, timely communications so institutions can identify reasonably similar paths before students are stranded. SACSCOC centers minimal disruption and additional cost, requires reasonable completion options for all students regardless of progress, and explicitly permits institutional-credit exceptions for near-finish students. U.S. Department of Education closure/comparable-program guidance sharpens the continuity test further by tying acceptable continuation to minimal interruption, delay, and loss of credits. Public transfer systems add a process signal: SUNY now recommends recording all received courses or prior-learning experiences, performing automatic second review when a course or experience is not accepted, recording reasons for non-acceptance, removing transcript-fee friction, and supporting centralized appeals; CUNY and campus transfer pages give learners a documented re-evaluation path with advisor review and supplemental materials; CSU's `similar degree` logic explicitly treats aligned routes as a way to prevent students from repeating courses. See `B74`, `B76`, `B77`, `B79`, `B80`, `B81`, `B82`, and `B83`.

## Core rule

The archive now distinguishes two separate questions:

1. **Was reliance documented strongly enough to create more than an ordinary navigation duty?**
2. **Would the changed rule impose enough added burden that ordinary updated-route treatment becomes unfair?**

Stronger protection is owed when **both** conditions are met:

- the learner's reliance is documented by ordinary institutional traces rather than by a purely private claim; and
- the midstream change would impose a burden that is more than clerical refresh or a short, low-cost bridge.

The archive rejects two bad shortcuts:

- **"no log, no right"** — which encourages institutions to erase or ignore ordinary traces of advice, submission, or progress; and
- **"any inquiry creates entitlement"** — which makes mere browsing look like a settled instructional commitment.

## Evidence bands for documented reliance

These evidence bands do **not** require raw chat logs, keystroke capture, or heavy behavioral surveillance. They rely on ordinary administrative traces that public systems already create for advising, intake, evaluation, payment, and progress.

### `E0 browse_or_generic_interest`

Typical evidence:

- public browsing;
- generic inquiry email;
- attendance at an open house or general workshop;
- no named advised route, no submission, no seat, no review, no progress record.

Default consequence:

- no special in-flight right;
- provide updated guidance, reasons, and next steps.

### `E1 institutionally_guided_path`

Typical evidence:

- advisor or navigator note naming a route;
- referral ticket to a receiving node;
- saved pathway plan or appointment outcome tied to a named learner;
- published standing entry explicitly recommended to the learner by a recognized public node.

Default consequence:

- enough to preserve queue position and avoid duplicate intake;
- not yet enough, by itself, to demand full teach-out.

### `E2 submitted_or_evaluated_case`

Typical evidence:

- application, packet, syllabus, portfolio, or transcript submitted for evaluation;
- official standing-match or pre-evaluation initiated;
- CPL / PLA / review workflow opened;
- institutional request for supplemental materials under a named route.

Default consequence:

- denial should not occur without manual review;
- successor substitution or construct-close routing becomes the default rather than fresh restart.

### `E3 paid_or_scheduled_commitment`

Typical evidence:

- review fee or assessment fee paid or officially waived;
- seat reserved in a cohort or bridge;
- supervised assessment, interview, or portfolio defense scheduled;
- aid, employer sponsorship, or public-funding step approved on the basis of the route.

Default consequence:

- stronger protection against system-caused churn;
- the system should not impose new direct cost lightly after this point.

### `E4 progress_or_near_finish`

Typical evidence:

- the learner has started the recognized offering;
- meaningful progress is recorded;
- most required work is complete;
- completion is within the next short cycle or term;
- the learner has already shaped work, caregiving, travel, licensure, or aid timing around the route.

Default consequence:

- strongest ordinary presumption in favor of teach-out, successor substitution, or challenge access rather than restart.

## Burden bands

The archive now distinguishes four burden bands.

### `B0 clerical_refresh`

Typical pattern:

- resubmit or refresh a document already in hand;
- provide a clearer syllabus or artifact;
- no new instructional work;
- no new direct charge beyond ordinary document handling;
- no queue loss and no missed cycle.

Default implication:

- ordinary updated-route treatment may still be fair.

### `B1 light_bridge`

Typical pattern:

- one short bridge, attestation, or challenge step;
- no repeated basic module;
- no tuition-bearing extra course requirement;
- delay stays inside the current review or start window;
- any fee is nominal and ordinarily waived when the system caused the change.

Default implication:

- substitute-equivalent routing is usually enough.

### `B2 material_delay_or_repeat`

Typical pattern:

- repeat of already-accepted basic learning or duplicate intake;
- extra supervised assessment or bridge requirement that extends beyond the shortest ordinary bridge;
- added direct cost beyond nominal handling;
- delay that pushes the learner past the next meaningful start/review/funding window;
- new travel, scheduling, or accessibility burden that changes participation feasibility.

Default implication:

- stronger protection is owed for `E2`-`E4` cases;
- manual review plus challenge/substitute-equivalent access should replace ordinary restart.

### `B3 severe_reset`

Typical pattern:

- extra term, major cycle, or substantial restart;
- loss of aid, employer support, licensure timing, internship/clinical sequence, or job-transition window;
- large non-refundable direct cost;
- loss of substantial completed progress;
- route change that would predictably derail continuation for a learner already well inside the path.

Default implication:

- teach-out, successor protection, funded substitute-equivalent routing, or challenge access is presumptively owed unless legality, fraud, authority failure, or construct collapse blocks it.

## Default decision rule

Read evidence and burden together.

### `E0`

- `B0`-`B1` → ordinary updated route.
- `B2`-`B3` → still no teach-out right by default, but the system should provide human navigation and explain why a larger burden is occurring.

### `E1`

- `B0` → `T1 preserve_queue_and_review` may be enough.
- `B1` → `T2 successor_substitution` is the default if a successor exists.
- `B2`-`B3` → do not force duplicate intake or repeated basics; move to `T2` or `T3`.

### `E2`

- `B0`-`B1` → `T2` or `T3`.
- `B2` → `T3` or `T4` by default; restart is presumptively too harsh.
- `B3` → `T4` or `T5` unless the invalidity family blocks continuity.

### `E3`

- `B0`-`B1` → `T3` at minimum, with a bias toward no new direct cost.
- `B2` → `T4` or `T5` is usually warranted.
- `B3` → strongest ordinary teach-out / substitute-equivalent presumption short of full grandfathering.

### `E4`

- `B1` → preserve the near-finish path if lawfully possible.
- `B2`-`B3` → `T5 teach_out_commitment` is the default ordinary response; hard restart requires an explicit integrity, legality, or construct-fit justification.

## Cross-sector defaults

### Libraries and civic first-contact nodes

Enough evidence for `E1` usually includes a logged referral, pathway plan, or navigator note. Because these nodes often sit at the very start of a learner journey, they should preserve queue position and warm transfer even when stronger protection is not yet owed.

`B2` commonly appears here when a learner loses a cohort slot, a reserved workshop seat, or a public-funding window because a route changed after referral.

### Workforce systems and bridge / noncredit routes

Enough evidence for `E2` often includes an intake case, approved training plan, employer/wage-board sponsorship step, or enrollment in a bridge sequence. Loss of a funding cycle, training seat, or employer timing window should usually count at least as `B2`, often `B3`.

### Community colleges, VET, and CPL / PLA-heavy routes

Enough evidence for `E2` or `E3` often includes transcript receipt, syllabus/portfolio submission, fee payment, assessment scheduling, or formal pre-evaluation. Repeating already-aligned basics, paying a second review fee because the system changed its rule, or missing the next term because reevaluation ran long should usually count at least as `B2`.

### Universities and higher-stakes receiving nodes

Enough evidence for `E2` often includes a department-level transfer/CPL appeal, pre-approval, or entered evaluation record. Near-finish students who would otherwise lose a term or require unnecessary new residency/institutional-credit work should move quickly into `E3`/`E4` plus `B2`/`B3`, where public teach-out and exception practices already provide a policy analogy.

## What should not be required

The archive explicitly rejects several bad evidentiary demands.

Do **not** require:

- raw chat transcripts with AI systems;
- keystroke or screen surveillance;
- broad behavioral telemetry from a learner's study tools;
- public disclosure of accommodation/support-channel traces;
- a rule that only fully approved credit decisions count as reliance.

Ordinary institutional records are enough:

- advised-route notes,
- referral or queue records,
- submission timestamps,
- evaluation-start markers,
- fee/waiver records,
- assessment scheduling,
- and progress or completion markers.

These traces should usually remain **local**. They are evidence for fairness, not default payload for the portable packet.

## Compact operating test

A receiving or partner system should ask:

1. **What is the strongest evidence band present (`E0`-`E4`)?**
2. **What is the likely burden band if the rule change is applied now (`B0`-`B3`)?**
3. **Can a successor, bridge, challenge, or construct-close equivalent reduce that burden?**
4. **If not, would restart contradict the archive's continuity standard by creating avoidable delay, repeated basics, or non-trivial new direct cost?**

If the answer to (4) is yes, stronger protection is owed.
