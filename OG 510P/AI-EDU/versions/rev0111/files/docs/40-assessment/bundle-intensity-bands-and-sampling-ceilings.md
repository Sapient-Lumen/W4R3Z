# Bundle intensity bands and sampling ceilings

This document closes the archive's main labor-plausibility gap.

The archive already says to use **proof-of-learning bundles** for major outcomes. It now needs a companion rule for **where not to use them**.

The current archive bet is simple:

> richer proof should be common enough to preserve credibility, but rare enough to remain teachable, reviewable, and non-surveillant.

UNESCO's current assessment writing pushes toward process evidence, dialogue, and contextualized tasks; OECD 2026 keeps warning that stronger task performance with GenAI is not the same thing as learning; College Board's current AP guidance uses checkpoints, in-progress meetings, and interim-work review for the most exposed cases rather than for every assignment; Cambridge's current coursework guidance awards credit for student judgment and tells teachers to keep drafts under supervision and investigate further only when concern is triggered; and the IB says it will continue to rely on human examiners even where AI is used as a quality-control aid. See `B11`, `B26`, `B92`, `B95`, `B96`.

## The four intensity bands

The archive now recommends four default proof-intensity bands for planning at course, programme, and sector level.

These are mainly **design bands for staff and programme maps**, not a new public label students must see on every worksheet.

### `P0` — ordinary practice

Use for routine homework, warmups, retrieval practice, low-stakes drafts, and guided study.

Default shape:

- artifact only, or artifact plus a tiny teacher check;
- `D0` or `D1` disclosure if disclosure is needed at all;
- no required process archive;
- no oral defense requirement.

Typical proof move:

- short no-AI exit response,
- quick teacher question,
- or visible in-class attempt.

`P0` should remain the dominant band for everyday learning.

### `P1` — ordinary assessed work

Use for quizzes, short graded responses, routine problem sets, ordinary lab writeups, coding exercises, and other assessed work that matters but is not a gateway claim.

Default shape:

- artifact plus **one light corroborating surface**;
- usually `D1` disclosure;
- no standing demand for full drafts, full logs, or continuous recording.

Typical corroborating surfaces:

- one short explanation note,
- one small transfer item,
- a brief no-AI checkpoint,
- a spot walkthrough,
- or a teacher-selected follow-up question.

The point is to add enough evidentiary texture that a polished artifact does not stand alone, without turning ordinary grading into mini-vivas.

### `P2` — major outcome bundle

Use for unit culminations, substantial projects, threshold concepts, major papers, major labs, portfolios, and other claims that should survive ordinary AI use.

Default shape:

- **at least three surfaces** from the bundle grammar in [`proof-of-learning-bundles.md`](proof-of-learning-bundles.md);
- usually `D2` disclosure, occasionally `D3` where workflow accountability matters;
- planned in advance and named in the syllabus or programme map.

Default course-budget starting point:

- a standard semester or term course should usually have only a **small number** of `P2` events;
- the archive's current starting default is **two to four** `P2` tasks in a typical semester-length course, with lower counts for survey/introductory courses and higher counts only where writing, design, laboratory method, language performance, or studio critique are themselves central outcomes.

That range is a design starting point, not a law of nature.

### `P3` — gateway, credential, or safety-relevant proof

Use for capstones, clinical or practical demonstrations, licensure-like thresholds, external moderation points, live professional simulations, and other claims where the institution owes a stronger warrant.

Default shape:

- full bundle plus a live defense, performance, or supervised transfer;
- `D3` if AI enters the workflow at all;
- human review cannot be skipped.

In most ordinary courses, `P3` should be absent or appear once at most. Repeated `P3` use makes sense only where live performance is itself the course, as in studio, practicum, language conversation, clinical, or trade performance settings.

## `PX` — triggered deep review

This is **not** a standing band for planned coursework. It is an escalation state.

Use only when there is a concrete reason to look harder:

- abrupt mismatch with prior demonstrated performance,
- conflicting evidence across surfaces,
- authenticity or malpractice concern,
- appeal or dispute,
- or a low-rate random audit the programme has publicly declared.

`PX` can temporarily add:

- interim-work review,
- longer defense,
- additional live transfer,
- supervisor authentication,
- or manual second review.

The archive's rule is that `PX` should remain exceptional. A system that quietly treats everyone as `PX` has rebuilt surveillance by another name. The archive now pairs that warning with a cross-cutting observability/retention ladder so stronger review can move through selected excerpts or case files rather than defaulting to full durable trace capture for everyone.

## Sampling ceilings

The archive now makes six default ceiling rules explicit.

### 1. No universal process capture

Do not require every learner, on every task, to submit full draft trails, chat logs, screen recordings, keystroke traces, or all intermediate versions.

### 2. Deep review is targeted, not ambient

For ordinary assessed work, deep post-hoc review should be rare. If a programme insists on random audit for `P1` work, the archive's starting ceiling is **below one in ten** submissions, and never as a shadow second-marking system.

### 3. Short explanations by default

Spot explanations should usually be brief:

- around **2–5 minutes** for `P1` checks,
- around **5–10 minutes** for `P2` defenses,
- and longer only where `P3` claims justify it.

If explanation time routinely exceeds these bands, the design is probably being overused or misclassified.

### 4. Not every major task needs every teacher move

A course should not stack all proof moves at once when two or three well-chosen surfaces will do. Choose the surfaces that best fit the construct.

### 5. Most assessed work stays at `P1` or below

The archive's current planning rule is that the majority of assessed tasks in a normal course should remain in `P0` or `P1`. `P2` is for major outcomes. `P3` is for strong claims.

### 6. Detection does not set the ceiling

AI detectors, originality tools, or automated flags may contribute context, but they do not justify raising a whole course into `PX`, and they never substitute for human review. See `B26`, `B92`, `B96`.

## Subject-family shifts

Some families will legitimately run a little hotter than others. The starter inherited profiles now live in [`subject-family-proof-intensity-defaults.md`](subject-family-proof-intensity-defaults.md) and [`sector-and-age-profile-splits-for-proof-intensity-defaults.md`](sector-and-age-profile-splits-for-proof-intensity-defaults.md).

### Writing-intensive, studio, and design courses

These may use more `P2` events because iterative critique is part of the construct.

### Laboratory, clinical, trade, and performance settings

These may use more `P3`-like moments because live method, safety, and adaptation are part of the claim.

### Introductory survey or broad general-education courses

These should usually lean harder on `P0` and `P1`, reserving `P2` for only a few threshold outcomes.

### Accessibility-sensitive settings

Do not let proof-intensity planning silently override protected support defaults. Higher proof intensity does not erase the archive's protected-access rules or the need to keep `A1-A3` support use off the ordinary disclosure rail unless a published override row or trigger applies. See [`../20-governance/accommodation-aware-disclosure-and-accessibility.md`](../20-governance/accommodation-aware-disclosure-and-accessibility.md), [`../20-governance/presumptive-access-support-categories-and-override-triggers.md`](../20-governance/presumptive-access-support-categories-and-override-triggers.md), and [`../20-governance/reusable-starter-override-rows.md`](../20-governance/reusable-starter-override-rows.md).

## Minimal programme packet

A programme, department, or institution that already publishes the course-use grammar should also publish one short internal planning note stating:

- what counts locally as `P0` through `P3`;
- the default number of `P2` events expected in a standard course;
- when `PX` may be triggered;
- who may authorize deep review;
- and that universal logs, detectors-as-proof, and ambient recording are not default integrity methods.

This keeps bundle design coherent without forcing every teacher to reinvent the burden rule.

## Current archive bet

The archive's current best guess is that a **small intensity-band and sampling-ceiling grammar** plus a tiny set of **subject-family starter profiles** will preserve assessment credibility better than either of the two obvious bad responses:

- pretending polished artifacts still prove what they used to prove;
- or trying to recover credibility by turning ordinary education into a constant documentation regime.

That claim is now canon. The next live problem is narrower: which of those starter family profiles should harden, split, or cool once age-band, stakes, and sector differences become more visible.
