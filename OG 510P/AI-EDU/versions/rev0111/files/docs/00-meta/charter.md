# Charter

## North star

Design an educational order for the 21st century in which AI increases **understanding, agency, access, teacher capacity, and institutional intelligence** more than it increases shortcutting, surveillance, deskilling, or inequality.

## What this archive is trying to answer

What would an **ideal but institutionally plausible** education system look like if powerful AI is normal for students, teachers, schools, and universities?

The archive is not trying to preserve nostalgia for pre-AI schooling. Nor is it trying to excuse empty automation in the name of modernization. It is trying to determine:

- which human functions in education should become more central because AI exists;
- which routine tasks should be offloaded because they are not the heart of education;
- which assessments still count as evidence once AI assistance is common;
- what public guardrails institutions need before adopting AI at scale;
- and what kinds of AI tools are actually worth trusting in schools and universities.

## Current thesis

The best near-term shape for education with AI is **teacher-led, student-active, evidence-rich, and safety-governed**:

- **teacher-led** because pedagogy, care, judgment, and escalation remain irreducibly human and context-sensitive;
- **student-active** because AI should increase guided practice, reflection, dialogue, and transfer rather than merely produce polished outputs;
- **evidence-rich** because ordinary assignment completion is no longer enough to prove learning;
- **safety-governed** because educational settings involve minors, vulnerable populations, disability rights, civil rights, and long-run developmental effects.

## Scope

In scope:

- K-12, higher education, and lifelong learning;
- student-facing AI, teacher-facing AI, and institution-facing AI;
- curriculum, pedagogy, assessment, governance, procurement, and professional learning;
- equity, accessibility, privacy, safety, and labor implications.

Out of scope for now:

- fully autonomous “AI schools” with negligible teacher agency;
- detailed product comparison tables;
- preserving every new study or news item;
- speculative AGI futures that do not currently change educational design judgment.

## Source posture

The archive prefers:

1. public institutions and standards bodies,
2. rigorous trials or strong quasi-experimental evidence,
3. implementation-relevant guidance,
4. and only then vendor evidence for market-shape signals.

Key starting sources include UNESCO guidance and competency frameworks, OECD policy/evidence syntheses, RAND surveys of school use, U.S. Department of Education guidance, IES R&D programs, and recent tutoring trials. See [`bibliography.md`](bibliography.md).
