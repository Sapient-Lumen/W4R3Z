# Reference model

This document states the archive's current best compact blueprint for **education with AI**.

## The seven-layer model

### Layer 1. Safe institutional substrate

Every institution needs a governed AI workspace with identity, access controls, privacy protections, a published observability/retention rule, a tiny sector-and-function overlay where the generic logging floor is already too blunt, logging appropriate to educational use, clear data boundaries, and opt-out paths for learners and families where needed. The archive currently assumes this is a prerequisite for serious scale. The substrate also needs an accessibility and support-routing layer: some AI-mediated activity belongs in ordinary course policy, while some belongs in protected accommodation/access channels with tighter privacy controls and clearer human ownership. The archive now makes that layer more explicit by pre-clearing ordinary presentation, communication, and executive-function supports by default, while routing transformative or surrogate supports to local override or ordinary course policy when they begin to replace the target construct. It now also expects repeated construct-sensitive cases to be published as inherited subject- and age-sensitive override rows and to ship with a tiny starter set for the most reusable repeated families rather than being improvised anew by each teacher.

### Layer 2. Teacher co-intelligence layer

Teachers should receive AI support for:

- lesson adaptation,
- formative question generation,
- rubric drafting,
- feedback drafting,
- translation and accessibility support,
- and administrative compression.

The purpose is not to script teachers into compliance, but to free scarce time for diagnosis, relationship, live teaching, and expert judgment.

### Layer 3. Student study-companion layer

Every learner should have access to a guided AI study companion that is optimized for:

- explanation,
- retrieval practice,
- questioning,
- scaffolding,
- worked-example fading,
- error diagnosis,
- and metacognitive reflection.

The archive does **not** currently endorse a default “answer engine” posture for student-facing systems, and it no longer treats unmanaged consumer chat as sufficient institutional infrastructure once repeated student-facing use is being endorsed or provisioned. The more promising shape is guided or Socratic support in a governed workspace, and stronger claims about tutoring or developmental impact should increasingly move toward pedagogically explicit tools rather than bare general-purpose chat. Those supports should also default to cooler memory than the market story of “personalisation” suggests: most study help should remain stateless or preference-light unless a bounded course/service continuity case has been explicitly governed. The archive now adds three narrower rules too: official learning surfaces should usually move through diagnosis, hinting, bounded scaffold, named answer-release, and reconstructive follow-through rather than defaulting either to instant solutions or to endless refusal; that interaction contract should usually harden only through a small branch map — ordinary hint-first study help, critique-first feedback after learner production, and bounded example/check phases — rather than through one universal answer-release posture; and repeated answer dependence or non-transfer should now move through a small recovery ladder — bounded reset, temporary cool-down, teacher-owned recovery, then human-only handling where needed — rather than being met by faster answers or deeper learner profiling; that recovery path should use only tiny temporary packets plus de-identified or minimum-cell aggregate service signals rather than durable learner-risk files; and those starter recovery defaults should already split once minors, credit-bearing higher education, professional-practice training, or public adult-route services make one generic packet-and-signal posture misleading; the archive now adds one narrower rule too: those starter profiles may harden only when the same owner, modality, aggregate scope, and re-entry truth still travel together, and they should branch once optional study help, queue-owned recovery, and consequence-bearing treatment stop sharing one parent row; the archive now adds one narrower portability rule too: some of those child branches may carry shared review-window bands, substitute-path expectations, and publication floors, while hotter treatment branches often harden only at a no-silent-penalty or minimal-loss floor rather than through one universal interim package. It now adds one narrower packet-governance rule too: repaired owner-facing packet defaults should not keep inherited trust merely because later shell edits are individually small; once several individually non-reopening packet-field, visibility, anchor, expiry, challenge-route, or effect-boundary edits accumulate on the same repaired path, the service should treat that basket as a fresh packet reset cluster rather than harmless maintenance, while allowing recurring same-basket calendar, route, or label upkeep to batch prospectively only inside a tiny pre-declared maintenance envelope with published shell invariants and explicit breach resets, and now saying that only label/plain-language harmonisation usually travels as a branch-portable envelope default, calendar housekeeping travels only for record and route owner families, and route/contact upkeep still stays local, with only label cleanup usually carrying a shared `QC-3 + QW-14D` band and record-owner calendar cleanup carrying only a shared `QC-2` cap while its real window stays local. See `B11`, `B13`, `B14`, `B15`, `B99`, `B100`, `B151`, `B154`, `B155`, `B156` and [`../20-governance/learning-first-interaction-defaults-and-answer-release-triggers.md`](../20-governance/learning-first-interaction-defaults-and-answer-release-triggers.md), [`../20-governance/recovery-packets-and-aggregate-dependence-signals.md`](../20-governance/recovery-packets-and-aggregate-dependence-signals.md), [`../20-governance/sector-and-context-profile-splits-for-recovery-defaults.md`](../20-governance/sector-and-context-profile-splits-for-recovery-defaults.md), and [`../20-governance/hardening-branching-and-retreat-rules-for-recovery-profiles.md`](../20-governance/hardening-branching-and-retreat-rules-for-recovery-profiles.md).

### Layer 4. Proof-of-learning layer

Institutions should add explicit mechanisms that preserve evidentiary value when AI use is normal, and pair them with simple course-level legibility about allowed use and disclosure:

- supervised or live checkpoints,
- oral defenses,
- in-class transfer tasks,
- process logs and drafts,
- selective teacher audits,
- performance tasks,
- and portfolio-based demonstrations tied to clear outcomes.

The archive's default posture is that these mechanisms should be assembled into **bundles** matched to age band, subject family, and stake level rather than applied indiscriminately, rationed through a small `P0-P3` intensity grammar plus a `PX` escalation state, and then tuned through inherited family-specific starter profiles plus a tiny sector-and-age overlay so richer proof stays concentrated where claims are strongest without forcing minors, ordinary higher education, professional live-performance training, and public adult-route learning into one burden floor. See [`../40-assessment/proof-of-learning-bundles.md`](../40-assessment/proof-of-learning-bundles.md), [`../40-assessment/bundle-intensity-bands-and-sampling-ceilings.md`](../40-assessment/bundle-intensity-bands-and-sampling-ceilings.md), [`../40-assessment/subject-family-proof-intensity-defaults.md`](../40-assessment/subject-family-proof-intensity-defaults.md), and [`../40-assessment/sector-and-age-profile-splits-for-proof-intensity-defaults.md`](../40-assessment/sector-and-age-profile-splits-for-proof-intensity-defaults.md).

### Layer 5. AI literacy layer

AI literacy should be a strand across grades and sectors, not a one-off assembly or optional seminar. Students need to learn safe use, critique, provenance, limitations, and appropriate delegation. Teachers need a deeper equivalent, including pedagogical use and misuse. The archive now treats age-banded progression plus a small exemplar kernel and a light course-level use/disclosure grammar as specified enough to guide local implementation. See [`minimal-ai-literacy-spine.md`](minimal-ai-literacy-spine.md), [`../30-operations/subject-embedded-exemplar-kernel.md`](../30-operations/subject-embedded-exemplar-kernel.md), and [`../30-operations/course-level-ai-use-grammar.md`](../30-operations/course-level-ai-use-grammar.md).

- student understanding,

## Core role split

### Teachers

Accountable for purpose, review, adaptation, escalation, care, and final judgment. Teacher-facing AI may compress drafting, planning, analysis, and quality checking, but it does not displace teacher sign-off for grades, records, consequence-bearing intervention, or outward communication that the institution may later need to defend.

### Students

Active users of AI for practice, exploration, drafting, critique, and reflection—but within norms that preserve responsibility for learning.

### Institutions

Responsible for safe infrastructure, procurement discipline, AI literacy provision, assessment redesign, and explicit publication of where student-facing AI may remain generic support versus where it needs stricter inherited learner-service profiles, and where institution-facing AI may compress workflow versus where it may only advise, flag, or queue under contestable human review, including stricter inherited profile splits for minors, professional gatekeeping, and benefits-linked public routing.

### AI systems

Useful when they help extend explanation, feedback, personalization, and teacher capacity; unsafe when they replace responsibility, conceal reasoning failures, or dilute the evidence of learning.

## Compact thesis

The right institutional shape is **not** “AI replaces teachers.”

It is closer to: **every teacher gets a serious co-pilot, every learner gets a serious tutor/study companion, every institution gets a serious proof-and-governance layer, the curriculum teaches people how to use all of this without surrendering judgment, and the public system offers lifelong entry routes plus a small handoff-and-recognition rail beyond formal enrolment, including dated standing lists for recurring outside-learning cases, a tiny machine-readable publication profile that keeps those lists fresh and legible across systems, a bounded exception grammar that keeps repeated local constraints public without turning interoperability into brittle policy code, a privacy-light maintenance rule that lets those standing rules learn from appeals and reversals without becoming a shared case-tracking regime, a thin public maintenance-history/profile that publishes rule state and impact without publishing operational telemetry, an in-flight teach-out/substitute-equivalent rule that protects good-faith learners from midstream restart when continuity is still lawfully and pedagogically possible, and a small evidence-and-burden grammar for deciding when that stronger protection is actually owed.**
