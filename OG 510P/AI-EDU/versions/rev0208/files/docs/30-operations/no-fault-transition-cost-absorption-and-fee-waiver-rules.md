# No-fault transition cost absorption and fee-waiver rules

This document closes the archive's next live gap about **who should actually carry continuity costs once a system-caused route change has already triggered protection duties**.

The archive already had:

- a standing-list layer;
- a maintenance/history layer;
- a partner-consumption / grandfathering rule;
- an in-flight teach-out / substitute-equivalent grammar;
- an evidence-and-burden rule for documented reliance;
- and a front-end no-learner-charge rule for system-caused continuity work.

What it still lacked was the next narrower answer:

> **when continuity is owed, which payer default really travels across sectors, when should the changed-rule owner keep the cost, when should a receiving/public node front it, and where do elective learner choices begin again to count as learner-borne?**

The archive's tighter answer is:

> **keep the learner off the front-end invoice, use a very small back-end payer ladder, and charge the learner only for the elective margin above the continuity floor rather than for the continuity floor itself.**

Current public signals point in the same direction. HLC's current teach-out materials keep fair transition concrete by requiring clear disclosure of accepted credits, tuition, fees, additional charges, records retention, and communications, and by treating fee waivers, transcript help, support services, and comparable pricing as ordinary teach-out design questions. SACSCOC's current substantive-change policy still centers completion with minimal disruption and additional cost. The U.S. Department of Education's current closed-school comparable-program rule still anchors continuity in minimal interruption, delay, or loss of credits. The Office for Students' current student-protection guidance keeps alive a more practical detriment lens by naming refunds, additional travel costs, honoured bursaries, and compensation for maintenance costs or lost time where continuation cannot be preserved. SUNY and CUNY keep the transfer side honest by reinforcing early evaluation, no needless repetition of already completed work, and an appeal path when recognition is denied or restricted. And current U.S. Department of Labor WIOA guidance keeps public-route continuity from looking like mere benevolence by framing education, training, and support services as ordinary participation infrastructure in workforce systems. See `B110`, `B111`, `B112`, `B113`, `B114`, `B115`, `B116`, and `B157`.

## Relationship to the existing route-protection stack

This document still does **not** replace:

- [`partner-consumption-and-grandfathering-rules.md`](partner-consumption-and-grandfathering-rules.md)
- [`in-flight-teach-out-and-substitute-equivalent-rules.md`](in-flight-teach-out-and-substitute-equivalent-rules.md)
- [`documented-reliance-and-burden-thresholds.md`](documented-reliance-and-burden-thresholds.md)

Those documents answer:

- whether a standing-entry change should affect future, in-flight, or settled uses;
- what substantive continuity protection is owed;
- and when reliance and burden are strong enough that restart becomes unfair.

This document now answers two separate cost questions:

- **who fronts the continuity work without billing the learner;**
- **and where the learner may still pay only for an elective expansion above the preserved baseline.**

## Core rule

The archive now treats **no-fault continuity cost absorption** as a two-layer rule.

### Layer 1. Front-end justice rule

> **The learner is not the default float, buffer, or insurer for system-caused continuity work.**

If two institutions, agencies, or funders disagree about reimbursement, they should settle that dispute behind the scenes. The continuity floor should still move forward without a new learner invoice.

### Layer 2. Back-end payer ladder

Behind that front-end rule, the archive now distinguishes three payer postures.

| Payer code | Who carries the cost behind the scenes | When it is the archive's default |
|---|---|---|
| `P0 changed-rule owner` | the provider, recognizer, route owner, or authority that destabilized the path | when the cost exists mainly because that actor withdrew, narrowed, delayed, or reinterpreted the route after documented reliance |
| `P1 receiving continuity node` | the receiving institution or node that uniquely controls the substitute-equivalent, bridge, challenge, or intake step needed to preserve continuity | when the step is real continuity work but can only be operationalized by the receiving node's own machinery |
| `P2 public/shared backstop` | a closure reserve, public funder, hardship/continuity fund, or other shared mechanism | when the cost is too large, too cross-node, or too support-heavy to rest truthfully on one office alone, or when one actor cannot lawfully waive/absorb it by itself |

The payer ladder is intentionally small:

> **first ask who destabilized the route, then ask who uniquely controls the substitute path, then ask whether the remaining cost is really public continuity infrastructure.**

## Cost families, now with payer defaults

The archive keeps the cost grammar small but now makes the payer default sharper.

| Cost family | Typical examples | Starter payer default |
|---|---|---|
| `K0 clerical continuity` | transcript movement, records release, re-evaluation, syllabus re-review, reopening a review case, forced application refresh, replacement of already-submitted ordinary evidence | `P0` when the changed-rule owner created the paperwork; `P1` only when the receiving node's own intake semantics force the refresh; never ordinary learner-borne |
| `K1 bridge and challenge` | short bridge module, challenge exam, portfolio review, successor-equivalent check, supervised reassessment needed only because the route changed | `P1` usually fronts the work because it controls the mechanism; reimbursement may later sit with `P0` or `P2` |
| `K2 continuity supports` | additional travel, childcare increase, accessibility support, interpreting/translation, temporary device/connectivity help, scheduling support caused by route movement | `P2` when the burden is truly cross-node or public-access in character; otherwise `P0` where the changed-rule owner directly caused the move |
| `K3 cycle-loss mitigation` | deposit replacement, seat hold, emergency aid bridge, subscription/access extension, financial-aid reprocessing, deadline-extension support | `P0` when one late owner change caused the cycle loss; `P2` when several nodes or public timing systems jointly create the exposure |
| `K4 restart-scale instructional cost` | extra tuition-bearing course, repeated basic module, extra term, relocation-scale replacement, major clinical/practicum restart | presumptively avoid through teach-out, substitute-equivalent routing, or funded challenge; if unavoidable, default to `P0` or `P2`, not to an ordinary learner invoice |

## What actually hardens across sectors

The archive now answers the old live question more sharply.

### What now hardens almost everywhere

Three claims are now treated as cross-sector starter canon.

1. **`K0` should not come back to the learner.** The changed-rule owner usually pays, except where the receiving node must run its own continuity intake and can absorb that work directly.
2. **`K1` is usually receiving-node work at the front end.** The receiving node usually controls the bridge, challenge, or equivalency mechanism, so it should front the step without billing the learner and settle reimbursement later.
3. **`K4` should almost never become ordinary learner-borne cost.** If the system truly cannot avoid restart-scale burden, the cost should move to the changed-rule owner or a public/shared backstop rather than silently becoming the learner's problem.

### What hardens only as a split default

Two claims now harden only with an explicit split.

1. **`K2 continuity supports` split by route character.** In higher-education transfer they may remain partly owner-borne or local hardship decisions, but in workforce, library, adult-education, and other public-route contexts they more often behave like public continuity infrastructure and therefore escalate toward `P2`.
2. **`K3 cycle-loss mitigation` split by source of the missed window.** A late owner-side rule change points toward `P0`; a missed aid, seat, or multi-node timing window points more often toward `P2`.

The archive therefore rejects both bad shortcuts:

- **"the receiver always pays because the learner lands there"**;
- **"the public always pays because continuity sounds socially good."**

The right question is narrower:

> **what kind of cost is this, who created the instability, and who uniquely controls the continuity mechanism?**

## Elective margin rule

The archive now makes the learner-borne boundary explicit.

### `L0 preserved baseline`

The learner is only trying to reach the continuity floor already owed.

Default:

- no ordinary learner charge;
- apply the payer ladder above.

### `L1 continuity-plus elective upgrade`

The learner accepts the continuity floor but chooses something broader, faster, more prestigious, more convenient, or more expensive than that floor.

Default:

- the preserved baseline still stays non-learner-borne;
- the learner may pay only the **marginal extra cost above the baseline**.

This is the archive's key anti-slippage rule:

> **do not re-label the whole preserved path as elective just because the learner chooses an elective increment on top of it.**

### `L2 elective redirection`

The original lawful continuity floor still exists, but the learner chooses a substantially different route for preference rather than preservation.

Default:

- ordinary learner-borne costs may apply again.

## Relationship to evidence and burden bands

The archive still reads payer defaults through the existing `E0-E4` and `B0-B3` grammar, but now with a sharper back-end consequence.

### `E0`

- `B0-B1` → updated guidance may be enough; no general funded-path claim from mere browsing.
- `B2-B3` → the learner still should not be charged simply to understand the changed route, but strong `P2` funding is not automatic.

### `E1`

- `B0` → `K0` usually sits at `P0` or `P1`, never at the learner.
- `B1` → `K1` usually fronts at `P1`.
- `B2-B3` → move toward `P0/P2`, especially for queue loss or missed cycles.

### `E2-E4`

- `B0-B1` → continuity paperwork and short bridge work should ordinarily stay inside `P0/P1`.
- `B2-B3` → move decisively toward `P0/P2`; restart-scale cost should not become a fresh learner liability once documented reliance is already strong.

## Cross-sector defaults

### Public transfer / teach-out in higher education

`K0` now hardens cleanly here: transcript handling, re-evaluation, forced application refresh, and records access should not come back to the learner once the continuity problem is acknowledged. `K1` short bridges and successor-equivalent checks usually front through `P1`, because the receiving institution controls the actual mechanism. `K4` should ordinarily move to `P0` or `P2` if unavoidable. The archive's starter posture for repeated `K2` / `K3` burdens here is usually **`CSP-CASE` or `CSP-RESERVE`**, not a universal public entitlement floor.

### Workforce, adult-learning, and library-routed transitions

The archive now treats `K2` and `K3` as especially real here. Travel, childcare, device/connectivity, interpretation, and lost funding or intake windows are not side issues in public-route continuity; they are often the difference between formal continuity and real abandonment. These sectors therefore push faster toward `P2` than ordinary campus transfer does. Their starter posture is usually **`CSP-RESERVE`**, and some recurring support families may truthfully harden to **`CSP-ENTITLEMENT`** when they function as ordinary route-participation infrastructure rather than exceptional hardship relief.

### Professional and practicum gates

Because missed cycles can mean lost placements, licensure timing, employment windows, or clinical progression, `K3-K4` escalate quickly. The archive is especially hostile to a second learner-borne gatekeeping charge when the first path failed because the system changed its own recognition, route, or timing rule. The starter posture here is usually **`CSP-RESERVE` with local legal/funder splits**, because placement law, host capacity, liability, and licensure calendars still vary too much for one universal entitlement floor to travel cleanly.

## Continuity-support postures for repeated `K2` / `K3` burdens

The archive now adds one still smaller posture ladder for the burdens that most often get misfiled as "just hardship."

| Posture code | Meaning | When it is the archive's starter default |
|---|---|---|
| `CSP-CASE` | case-by-case hardship handling | the burden family is still uncommon, proof remains too local, or authority is too fragmented for a standing promise |
| `CSP-RESERVE` | predeclared continuity reserve | the same burden recurs predictably enough that a node or funder should budget a support pool, publish short trigger facts, and stop demanding a fresh hardship narrative every time |
| `CSP-ENTITLEMENT` | published public entitlement floor | the route is public/default enough, recurrence is high enough, and the support is participation-critical enough that qualifying learners should receive a named minimum without discretionary hardship gatekeeping |

This ladder does **not** replace `P0` / `P1` / `P2`. It sits downstream of them. The payer ladder says **who carries the burden behind the scenes**; the `CSP-*` ladder says **how far repeated `K2` / `K3` burdens should move from exception handling into standing continuity infrastructure.**

## When office or funder authority still forces a local split

The archive now treats four tests as decisive.

1. **lawful-waiver test** — if the changed-rule owner or receiving node cannot lawfully waive, reimburse, or pre-fund the burden on its own, the case should escalate toward `P2` rather than fall back to the learner;
2. **unique-mechanism test** — if only the receiving node can operationalize the bridge, seat-hold, challenge, or substitute path, `P1` should still front the continuity step even when later reimbursement sits elsewhere;
3. **pooled-timing-loss test** — if a missed cycle, lost seat, or lost aid window was created by interacting calendars or multiple public nodes rather than one office alone, the archive prefers `P2` or a reserve posture over a false single-owner story;
4. **urgency-and-dignity test** — if help must arrive quickly enough that discretionary hardship review would itself cause abandonment, the support should move toward `CSP-RESERVE` or `CSP-ENTITLEMENT` instead of staying trapped in ad hoc exception handling.

## When repeated `K2` / `K3` burdens should graduate out of ad hoc hardship

A burden family should usually move from `CSP-CASE` to `CSP-RESERVE` when **all** of the following have become true:

1. **recurrence** — the same burden family keeps appearing on the same route or closely related route family;
2. **bounded design** — the support can be budgeted or capped in a short, legible way rather than requiring open-ended individualized reconstruction;
3. **timing criticality** — delay itself predictably causes dropout, missed cycles, or route abandonment;
4. **low-narrative proof** — eligibility can be shown with short route facts rather than a fresh, intimate hardship narrative every time.

A burden family should move further from `CSP-RESERVE` to `CSP-ENTITLEMENT` only when **three additional conditions** are met:

1. **public-route centrality** — the route is a public/default access path rather than a narrow premium option;
2. **ordinary-need character** — the support is necessary for ordinary participation, not merely a kindness for unusually severe cases;
3. **publishable floor** — the minimum support, proof, claiming window, and coordination owner can be published in a way that is short, truthful, and contestable.

The archive's anti-slippage rule is now explicit:

> **once the same route predictably needs the same support to stay real, stop pretending each learner has arrived as a one-off hardship exception.**

## Exceptions the archive still allows

The exceptions remain narrow.

### 1. Claimant-caused defect

The learner withheld, falsified, or materially misrepresented information, or refused to provide ordinary evidence after a reasonable chance to do so.

Default:

- no-fault continuity may be displaced.

### 2. Pure elective expansion

The learner bypasses an available preserved baseline and selects a materially different route for preference rather than preservation.

Default:

- `L2` may apply.

### 3. Premium acceleration above a protected baseline

The learner is owed continuity, but chooses a faster or more premium version of it.

Default:

- preserve the baseline at `L0`;
- charge only the incremental premium at `L1` if the premium is real.

### 4. Lawful local limit with available shared fund

A single actor cannot lawfully waive or absorb the cost on its own.

Default:

- move the cost to `P2` rather than to the learner.


## Publication-and-activation profile for reserves and entitlements

The archive now closes the next narrower gap.

Once repeated `K2` / `K3` burdens stop living as ad hoc hardship handling, the question is no longer only **who pays**. It is also:

> **what must be published, what short proof package can be demanded, when must claims be accepted, and which coordination facts must stay visible so a reserve or entitlement is real rather than decorative?**

Current public signals support a small, concrete answer rather than an invisible discretionary one. HLC's current teach-out materials require a clearly stated time period, identified student populations, credit-acceptance information, tuition / fee information, records-retention information, and a communications plan written in clear language. The OfS's current student-protection regime likewise insists that plans be published, clear, and current, and that students be told about disruption-minimisation measures, continuation options, refund / compensation expectations, and communications. Current WIOA guidance keeps the public-route side honest by requiring local supportive-services policies and procedures, including referral and funding coordination rules, while still allowing local limits and exceptions. See `B110`, `B111`, `B115`, `B157`, and `B158`.

The archive's answer is therefore:

> **publish one small activation notice whenever support has graduated beyond ad hoc hardship, keep the proof minimum tied to short route facts rather than intimate personal storytelling, and make the claiming window and coordination owner visible enough that the learner does not have to discover the reserve or entitlement by rumor.**

### Shared notice shell that now travels

The archive now treats the following fields as the smallest shared publication-and-activation shell for both `CSP-RESERVE` and `CSP-ENTITLEMENT` postures.

| Field | What must be visible |
|---|---|
| `N1 route scope` | which route, programme family, campus / provider change, placement family, or public-service path the reserve / entitlement applies to |
| `N2 in-scope learners` | which learner populations are within scope (for example in-flight, admitted-but-not-yet-started, waiting-list, placement-assigned, or aid-window-affected) |
| `N3 trigger facts` | the short event facts that activate the support, stated in route language rather than policy prose |
| `N4 proof minimum` | the smallest evidence package needed to claim the support |
| `N5 claiming window` | when a learner may claim, what date starts the clock, and whether late-claim rescue exists |
| `N6 coordination owner` | the office or node that must receive the claim or triage it onward |
| `N7 response clock` | the expected acknowledgement / decision timing, especially where delay itself causes abandonment |
| `N8 continuity floor` | what baseline is protected without ordinary learner charge |
| `N9 elective margin rule` | what counts as learner-chosen upgrade above the preserved baseline |
| `N10 review path` | where appeal, manual review, or human escalation sits if the claim is denied or the reserve appears exhausted |

This is intentionally smaller than a full local fund manual.

The shared shell is **not** required to publish internal reimbursement plumbing, accounting codes, or every lawful funding source. It is required to publish enough that a learner or partner node can tell:

- whether the support exists,
- whether the learner is plausibly inside it,
- what short facts activate it,
- where to go,
- and how long the learner can wait before the route meaningfully closes.

### Trigger facts that truly travel

The archive now treats three trigger families as the portable minimum.

#### 1. `TF1 route-change fact`

A named continuity-disrupting event happened.

Examples:

- a course / campus / provider closure,
- a recognition withdrawal,
- a successor-route substitution,
- a practicum or placement collapse,
- a forced modality / location move,
- or a missed aid / intake / seat window caused by the system's own route change.

#### 2. `TF2 learner-position fact`

The learner was already truthfully on the affected path.

Examples:

- currently enrolled,
- accepted and preparing to start,
- already assigned a placement / intake slot,
- already told a transfer / substitute path would count,
- or otherwise inside the archive's `E1-E4` reliance grammar.

#### 3. `TF3 burden-family fact`

The claim belongs to a named continuity burden family that the notice covers.

Examples:

- `K2` travel / childcare / accessibility / device / interpretation support,
- `K3` deposit, access-extension, aid-reprocessing, or missed-window mitigation.

The archive rejects activation notices that hide all three behind vague language like **"hardship may be considered"**. Once a reserve or entitlement is predeclared, the learner should not have to infer the trigger from rumor or reconstruct the whole policy from scratch.

### Proof minima that now travel

The archive now takes a harder line on proof.

> **A predeclared reserve or entitlement should default to low-narrative proof.**

That means the ordinary claim package should usually stop at:

1. the route fact (`TF1`),
2. the learner-position fact (`TF2`),
3. the burden-family fact (`TF3`), and
4. one short amount / necessity fact sufficient to size or authorize the support.

Examples of proof that usually fit the archive's minimum:

- current timetable / transfer / placement / intake notice,
- ordinary receipt or estimate,
- a short travel-distance or schedule conflict fact,
- an accessibility / translation / care requirement already documented elsewhere in the route,
- or a brief attestation where the cost is too small or urgent for documentary perfection.

The archive now rejects two common failure modes.

1. **Narrative inflation.** Do not require a fresh intimate hardship essay every time the same predictable continuity burden recurs.
2. **Merits creep.** Do not turn a reserve or entitlement claim into a full re-argument about whether continuity protection was owed at all once the route has already admitted that it was.

Where a public funder or statute requires extra verification, that extra proof may remain local. But the portable default is now clear:

> **the first proof layer should be route facts plus one bounded support fact, not an open-ended reconstruction of the learner's private life.**

### Claiming-window defaults that now travel

The archive now adds a tiny claiming-window grammar.

| Window code | Default meaning |
|---|---|
| `CW-ROLL` | claims remain open on a rolling basis while the affected cycle or transition is still live |
| `CW-30D` | where a discrete claim window is used, the learner should ordinarily get at least 30 days after clear individualized notice or after the relevant cost becomes legible, whichever is later |
| `CW-EXC` | a late-claim rescue path remains available where delayed records, aid timing, provider communication failure, or similar system facts prevented timely filing |

The archive's default cross-sector answer is now:

- use `CW-ROLL` for time-critical continuity supports and cycle-loss mitigation wherever possible;
- if a discrete claim deadline is unavoidable, publish `CW-30D` as the floor rather than a hidden or same-week expiry;
- keep `CW-EXC` available whenever the system's own timing failure made timely filing unrealistic.

This does **not** mean every sector must use the same outer deadline. It means the portable shared layer should stop at one humane floor:

> **no reserve or entitlement should disappear before the learner had a clear chance to know it existed, understand the baseline, and file the short proof package.**

### Reserve-specific publication rule

For `CSP-RESERVE`, the archive now requires one additional visibility promise.

The notice should publish, in short form:

- whether the reserve is capped or case-banded,
- whether ordinary exceptions can exceed that cap,
- and where the claim goes if the reserve cannot fully absorb the burden.

The archive still allows the exact cap, budget source, and reimbursement pathway to remain local where law or funding design varies. But the learner-facing layer should still make one thing visible:

> **a reserve is not permission to hide the exhaustion path.**

If a reserve may run short, the notice must identify the escalation owner rather than letting the learner hit a silent dead end.

### Entitlement-specific publication rule

For `CSP-ENTITLEMENT`, the archive now requires one stronger publication promise.

The notice should publish, in short form:

- the minimum floor the learner can expect,
- whether the floor is automatic or claim-activated,
- any narrow proof additions above the shared minimum,
- and any residual learner-borne elective margin above that floor.

The archive now rejects any supposed entitlement that still depends mainly on discretionary hardship narration or unpublished local discretion.

### What truly stays local

The archive keeps the local remainder narrow but real.

The following may still remain local even after the shared shell hardens:

- exact funding caps or payment amounts,
- internal reimbursement routes between `P0`, `P1`, and `P2`,
- statutory eligibility overlays tied to one funder,
- procurement / host-capacity constraints,
- account codes, voucher systems, or vendor contracts,
- and any extra verification legally required for a specific public fund.

The anti-bloat rule is important here.

> **Do not drag the whole finance manual into the portable layer just to avoid publishing the few learner-facing facts that actually matter.**

### Sector starter defaults for publication and activation

The archive now sharpens the cross-sector answer one step further.

#### Higher-ed transfer / teach-out

Usually publish the full `N1-N10` shell, use `CW-30D` as the ordinary discrete-window floor where a rolling window is not possible, and make the continuity floor / elective margin split explicit because pricing and receiving-institution choice often create confusion here.

#### Workforce, adult-learning, and library-routed continuity

Usually publish the same `N1-N10` shell, but prefer `CW-ROLL` because delay itself often collapses participation. Keep proof close to route facts and short support facts, and publish the coordination owner clearly because the learner may otherwise be bounced between service nodes.

#### Professional / practicum routes

Usually publish the same shell plus any cycle-anchor facts that control placement, licensure, or host timing. These routes may keep more legal and host-capacity specifics local, but should still publish `CW-EXC` because delayed records, host responses, or licensing calendars often surface too late for an ordinary short deadline to stay fair.

### Anti-slippage rules

The archive now treats four slippages as design failures.

1. **Hidden-support failure** — the reserve or entitlement exists, but the learner must learn about it informally.
2. **narrative-gatekeeping failure** — a predeclared support still requires an intimate hardship story by default.
3. **deadline-trap failure** — the claiming window expires before the learner had clear individualized notice or before the cost became legible.
4. **silent-exhaustion failure** — the reserve reaches a local limit but the notice does not publish where escalation or manual override goes next.


## Reserve-depletion, capacity-collision, and stronger-floor escalation

The archive now closes the next narrower gap.

Once reserves and entitlement floors are visible, another problem appears immediately:

> **what happens when the reserve runs short, the receiving host has only a few real places left, or several nodes each deny sole responsibility while the learner's continuity window is still live?**

Current public signals support a small scarcity rule rather than silent collapse. HLC's current policy requires teach-out agreements to be fair and equitable, to provide reasonable opportunities to complete, and allows HLC to disqualify a receiving institution that lacks capacity to serve in that role. The OfS's current closure brief emphasizes early detailed contingency planning, multi-agency coordination, and even multiple destination agreements so pressure does not fall invisibly on one institution alone. Current WIOA guidance requires local supportive-services policies to address coordination, funding, limits, and exceptions, and later DOL guidance explicitly encourages investment in supportive services and notes that WIOA itself imposes no cap on supportive services in its programmatic or nondiscrimination rules. See `B157`, `B158`, and `B159`.

The archive's answer is therefore:

> **when a live no-fault continuity claim collides with reserve depletion, host-capacity strain, or cross-node responsibility denial, stop treating the case as ordinary reserve handling and escalate to a temporary stronger floor until a truthful landing path or a reasoned human denial exists.**

### Scarcity-escalation ladder (`ES0-ES3`)

| Code | Meaning | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `ES0 ordinary reserve` | the reserve / entitlement and host pathway are still sufficient | ordinary `CSP-RESERVE` or `CSP-ENTITLEMENT` handling continues |
| `ES1 continuity hold` | scarcity or responsibility uncertainty is real enough that ordinary processing may defeat the live window | pause adverse clocks, keep the claim live, and preserve the possibility of the baseline while manual coordination runs |
| `ES2 temporary stronger floor` | reserve depletion, host-capacity shortage, or timing collision would otherwise turn continuity into a speed race | activate a minimum protected floor that stays non-learner-borne and cannot be lost solely because someone else reached the queue first |
| `ES3 shared override` | the local node cannot restore `ES2` inside the response clock, or lawful authority is too fragmented for one node to resolve it alone | escalate to the named `P2` / leadership / regulator-facing override owner for emergency routing, funding, or next-slot protection |

This ladder does **not** abolish reserve limits or capacity reality. It changes what happens when those limits appear:

> **scarcity may narrow the floor, but it should not silently erase the floor.**

### When escalation is required

Move at least to `ES1` when **all** of the following are true:

1. the learner has already made a live continuity claim inside the archive's ordinary trigger / proof shell;
2. a real no-fault scarcity or responsibility problem now exists; and
3. waiting for ordinary reserve handling would itself create a meaningful risk of route loss, missed cycle, or abandonment.

Move to `ES2` when **any** of the following is true:

1. known eligible demand now exceeds the reserve or the ordinary per-claim cap;
2. the substitute course, host, bridge, placement, or intake path has scarce real capacity;
3. two or more nodes each deny sole liability while the learner's continuity window is still live;
4. the remaining time before transfer, placement, aid, licensure, or intake closure is too short for ordinary reserve handling to stay truthful.

Move to `ES3` when the `ES2` floor cannot be restored locally within the published response clock, or when only a shared/public override can lawfully preserve the route.

### What the temporary stronger floor must do

The archive now hardens four minimum consequences.

1. **clock-stop rule** — while `ES1-ES3` is active, the claim should not die simply because the system is resolving scarcity, liability, or host capacity.
2. **no-front-end-invoice rule** — the learner still should not be asked to float the gap just because the reserve is short or the offices are arguing.
3. **anti-race rule** — do not default to raw first-come-first-served within the same protected continuity window.
4. **named-human-escalation rule** — the learner must be able to reach a visible manual owner before the continuity window closes.

The anti-race rule is the archive's sharpest new move.

> **When several protected continuity claims arrive inside the same live window, priority should follow continuity-risk facts, not rumor, refresh-speed, or who happened to know the system best.**

### Priority facts that may govern `ES2`

The archive keeps the portable priority layer narrow.

A temporary stronger floor may give earlier attention to claims where one or more of the following is true:

1. **current-cycle fragility** — a present transfer / practicum / aid / intake / exam / licensure cycle will close before ordinary review could finish;
2. **already-assigned route fragility** — the learner already had a named seat, bridge, placement, or successor path that will disappear without immediate hold action;
3. **participation-critical support fragility** — travel, childcare, accessibility, translation, connectivity, or similar support is necessary for participation and delay itself defeats the route;
4. **no-immediate-alternative fragility** — there is no other timely non-learner-borne baseline available from another node.

If several claims remain indistinguishable after those floor facts are applied, the archive prefers **date-banded batching or a neutral tiebreak** over an opaque speed race.

### What `ES2` usually looks like by sector

#### Higher-ed transfer / teach-out

`ES2` usually means some combination of deadline extension, provisional seat or transfer-path hold, continued fee / transcript waiver handling, and active routing to more than one receiving destination where one host alone lacks capacity. The archive does **not** require one institution to promise seats it does not have. It does require the system not to let one thin-capacity destination quietly become the only visible route.

#### Workforce, adult-learning, and library-routed continuity

`ES2` usually means immediate small-support continuity handling: short transport / device / childcare / interpretation help, same-week warm transfer, and coordination across nodes without making the learner restart the whole claim story. Because delay itself often defeats participation here, `CW-ROLL` plus a visible escalation owner is especially important.

#### Professional / practicum routes

`ES2` usually means preserving a placement or next-cycle priority where possible, coordinating with host and regulator calendars quickly, and refusing to turn a system-caused collapse into a second learner-borne gatekeeping charge. These routes may keep more host-specific scarcity rules local, but they should still publish the override owner and late-claim rescue path.

### What may still stay local

The archive still allows local variation in:

- exact reserve amounts or per-claim caps;
- host-specific placement-allocation formulas;
- statutory funding restrictions and reimbursement routes;
- and the specific officer or committee that executes `ES3`.

But the archive now rejects one local move outright:

> **a local cap, queue, or host shortage is not enough by itself to justify silent first-come-first-served collapse inside a live protected window.**

## Priority, batching, scarcity-publication, and graduation defaults for oversubscribed stronger floors

The archive now closes the next narrower gap.

Once `ES2` or `ES3` is live, the question is no longer only whether first-come-first-served collapse should be rejected. It is also:

> **which priority facts may really govern the remaining queue, how should tied claims be batched or broken without a speed race, what scarcity fields must stay visible to learners, and when should repeated temporary stronger-floor handling stop masquerading as emergency override and harden into standing infrastructure instead?**

Current public signals support a very small shared answer rather than a hidden or narrative-heavy one. HLC's current teach-out toolkit says teach-out plans should prioritize students with less than one year until completion, recommends posting an institutional closure website quickly with timelines, contact points, and support information, and treats multiple transfer / teach-out options as best practice rather than optional polish. The OfS's current student-facing closure guidance likewise expects clear timescales, alternative study options, credit-transfer or recognition information, refund / compensation information, support contacts, and complaint routes. Current WIOA guidance requires local supportive-services policies to address coordination, funding, limits, and exceptions, and treats supportive services as participation-enabling rather than ornamental. See `B157`, `B158`, `B159`, and `B160`.

The archive's tighter answer is therefore:

> **when an `ES2` or `ES3` floor is itself oversubscribed, harden only a tiny shared scarcity grammar: prioritize by route-loss fragility, batch by disruption cohort or short hold window instead of click-speed, publish the scarcity state and triage rule in learner-facing form, and treat repeated use of the same stronger-floor override as evidence that the system should rebuild the support as reserve, entitlement, or predeclared capacity rather than rerun emergency triage forever.**

### Priority facts that now travel (`PF1-PF4`)

The archive now hardens only four portable priority facts.

| Priority code | What it means | Why it travels |
|---|---|---|
| `PF1 imminent cycle loss` | the current term, transfer, aid, exam, practicum, licensure, or intake cycle will close before ordinary review can finish | it tracks whether delay itself destroys continuity |
| `PF2 already-assigned route fragility` | the learner already had a named seat, bridge, placement, successor path, or intake slot that will disappear without immediate hold action | it tracks concrete route loss rather than general preference |
| `PF3 participation-critical support fragility` | travel, childcare, accessibility, translation, connectivity, or similar support is necessary for participation and delay itself defeats the route | it tracks whether the support is functionally part of continuation |
| `PF4 no near non-learner-borne alternative` | there is no other timely preserved baseline available from another node without shifting ordinary cost or route loss onto the learner | it keeps oversubscription from quietly becoming forced self-funding or abandonment |

These facts are intentionally narrow. They are not a general merits or hardship score.

The archive now rejects five priority inputs as portable scarcity canon:

1. **persuasiveness of personal narrative** once the route is already inside no-fault protection;
2. **predicted success or deservingness scores** that rebuild admissions-like ranking inside continuity handling;
3. **general academic merit comparisons** unrelated to the threatened route loss;
4. **advisor power or insider familiarity** with the institution's processes;
5. **same-minute click speed** inside one protected disruption cohort.

The anti-slippage rule is now sharper still:

> **inside an oversubscribed stronger floor, triage by near-term route-loss facts, not by eloquence, prestige, prediction, or interface speed.**

### Batching rules that now travel (`SB1-SB3`)

The archive now hardens three small batching defaults.

| Batching code | Meaning | Starter default |
|---|---|---|
| `SB1 cohort band` | claims from the same disruption notice, same route family, or same short filing band are grouped for review together | use this instead of letting every claimant race individually against rumor or refresh speed |
| `SB2 short hold` | the node may pause final allocation briefly to collect all plausibly in-scope claims in the cohort band | use when immediate piecemeal allocation would itself decide the queue unfairly |
| `SB3 neutral tiebreak` | if claims remain indistinguishable after `PF1-PF4`, break the tie by a neutral rule declared in advance | prefer lottery, neutral timestamp-at-band-close, or another non-merits-based rule over hidden staff discretion |

The archive therefore rejects two common batching failures.

1. **open-refresh failure** — leaving a scarce continuity floor on a rolling first-click interface when the affected learners were all hit by the same disruption window.
2. **hidden-merits failure** — using unpublished staff judgments to sort otherwise tied continuity claims when the only honest remaining difference is scarcity itself.

### Learner-facing scarcity fields that now travel (`SF1-SF7`)

The archive now adds a very small scarcity-publication layer on top of `N1-N10`.

| Field | What must be visible once `ES2` or `ES3` is active |
|---|---|
| `SF1 scarcity state` | whether the route is currently at `ES1`, `ES2`, or `ES3`, and whether ordinary reserve handling has been suspended |
| `SF2 protected cohort band` | which learners or filing band are being triaged together |
| `SF3 priority facts in use` | the narrow `PF1-PF4` facts that may affect order inside that cohort |
| `SF4 batching / tiebreak rule` | whether `SB1`, `SB2`, and `SB3` are in force and what neutral tiebreak applies if claims remain tied |
| `SF5 pending-floor promise` | what minimum non-learner-borne protection remains in place while triage or escalation is ongoing |
| `SF6 next review / update point` | when the learner should expect the next allocation review, update, or escalation checkpoint |
| `SF7 override owner` | the named human or office responsible if the route must move to `ES3` or beyond local handling |

This layer is intentionally thinner than a full queue dashboard. The archive does **not** require publication of exact remaining seat counts, full claimant rankings, individual financial awards, or internal reimbursement disputes. It does require enough visibility that the learner can tell:

- that scarcity handling is actually active;
- what cohort they are inside;
- which narrow facts may change order;
- what minimum protection remains while waiting;
- and who owns the next escalation if the floor cannot be restored locally.

### Sector starter defaults for oversubscribed stronger floors

#### Higher-ed transfer / teach-out

`PF1` and `PF2` usually travel strongest here: closeness to the present completion cycle and loss of an already identified teach-out or transfer path are often the most honest scarcity facts. `SB1` cohort-band review is usually truer than rolling click-speed because closures and route changes commonly hit a whole programme family at once. `SF5` should make clear what remains protected while triage runs, and repeated oversubscription on the same programme family should trigger review of whether the system needs more than one receiving destination predeclared.

#### Workforce, adult-learning, and library-routed continuity

`PF3` and `PF4` usually travel strongest here because participation-critical support and absence of a timely non-learner-borne alternative often decide whether continuity is real at all. Use `SB2` short holds sparingly and briefly, since delay itself can defeat participation. `SF6` and `SF7` are especially important because cross-node routing failures are common and the learner may otherwise be bounced between offices.

#### Professional / practicum routes

`PF1` and `PF2` again travel strongly, but host-law, licensure calendars, and placement inventory often keep more of the actual allocation formula local. The archive therefore expects stronger `SF1-SF7` publication here without pretending that all host-specific scarcity mechanics can be standardized. Where the same placement family repeatedly hits `ES2` / `ES3`, the route should review whether a standing reserve or predeclared host-sharing arrangement is now owed.

### When repeated stronger-floor scarcity should graduate out of override work

The archive now makes the graduation rule sharper.

Move repeated `ES2` / `ES3` handling toward **`CSP-RESERVE` or another predeclared scarcity-capacity layer** when **all** of the following are true:

1. the same route family has triggered materially similar stronger-floor handling across more than one cycle, term, or disruption cluster;
2. the same narrow priority facts keep doing the real sorting work;
3. the same support or slot family keeps requiring manual triage;
4. the resulting floor can be described in a short, publishable way without rebuilding a full local queue manual.

Move further toward **`CSP-ENTITLEMENT`** only when **all** of the following are also true:

1. the protected support is ordinary participation infrastructure rather than exceptional rescue;
2. the qualifying cohort can be identified mostly through route facts plus one bounded support fact;
3. the floor is public/default enough that leaving it to recurring override work would predictably recreate the same learner-facing scarcity each cycle;
4. lawful funding or authority fragmentation no longer makes truthful publication impossible.

The archive keeps one limit explicit.

> **Repeated scarcity does not automatically mean universal entitlement. Sometimes the right graduation target is a predeclared reserve, a multi-destination path, or a published cohort-band rule rather than a single guaranteed amount or seat.**

### What still remains local inside oversubscribed stronger floors

Even after `PF1-PF4`, `SB1-SB3`, and `SF1-SF7` harden, the archive still leaves the following local where law, host inventory, or fund design vary too much:

- exact remaining seat or placement counts;
- detailed host-allocation formulas;
- statutory priority groups imposed by one funder or licensing regime;
- exact per-claim amounts or voucher mechanics;
- and internal reimbursement flows between `P0`, `P1`, and `P2`.

The anti-bloat rule remains the same.

> **Do not export the whole scarcity engine just to avoid publishing the few learner-facing facts that actually decide whether continuity remains real.**

## Predeclared capacity for repeated stronger-floor scarcity

The archive now closes the next narrower gap.

Once the same route family keeps reaching `ES2` / `ES3`, the archive can no longer stop at better triage. Another question appears:

> **when is repeated emergency scarcity evidence that the system should prebuild continuity capacity before the next disruption rather than rerun emergency coordination yet again?**

Current public signals support a small predeclared-capacity answer rather than universal guarantees or endless override craft. HLC's current teach-out policy allows arrangements with one or more receiving institutions and requires those institutions to have the capacity and support services needed to serve learners in that role. The OfS's current closure guidance says providers should consider agreements in principle, potentially multiple agreements for different courses or subjects, so students are not trapped by a single receiving destination. Current DOL WIOA state-plan materials likewise require states and local areas to strengthen coordination and partnerships with local entities and supportive-service agencies instead of waiting to discover every path only after disruption has begun. See `B159`, `B160`, and `B161`.

The archive's answer is therefore:

> **when the same route repeatedly needs stronger-floor scarcity handling, graduate from pure emergency override into the smallest truthful predeclared capacity posture that can keep the next continuity shock from becoming another blind bottleneck.**

### Capacity-posture ladder (`CP0-CP3`)

| Code | Meaning | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `CP0 override-only` | scarcity is still rare enough that emergency `ES2` / `ES3` handling remains the truthful posture | keep routing through stronger-floor triage and shared override only |
| `CP1 multi-destination` | the route should predeclare more than one plausible receiving destination or continuation path | publish the destination family and handoff owner before the next disruption |
| `CP2 shadow-capacity` | one node should hold a small reserved margin or pre-cleared fallback inventory for protected continuity cases | keep the exact local capacity local, but publish that a protected reserve margin exists and who may activate it |
| `CP3 shared-slot pool` | several nodes should jointly maintain a cross-node continuity pool because one host alone is predictably too fragile | predeclare the pooled route family, intake owner, and shared escalation owner rather than rediscovering them during the next shock |

This ladder is intentionally smaller than a full market-design or seat-allocation regime.

It does **not** claim that every route needs preallocated seats or a public capacity promise. It says only:

> **when the same emergency scarcity keeps recurring on the same route family, the system owes some prebuilt continuity shape before the next cycle.**

### When repeated scarcity should trigger a capacity posture

Stay at `CP0` only when **all** of the following are still true:

1. `ES2` / `ES3` use is genuinely infrequent rather than seasonally or structurally recurrent;
2. emergency handling usually restores truthful continuity without repeated cohort loss, long holds, or opaque queue behavior;
3. no small destination map, shadow margin, or pooled-slot design could be published honestly yet.

Move at least to `CP1` when **all** of the following are true:

1. materially similar scarcity has repeated on the same route family across more than one disruption cluster, cycle, or intake window;
2. the same downstream route types keep reappearing as the realistic landing paths;
3. learners are being forced to rediscover those paths through emergency coordination instead of inheriting them from a short predeclared map.

Move from `CP1` toward `CP2` when **any** of the following is true:

1. one destination repeatedly becomes the true bottleneck even after multi-destination planning exists;
2. near-term cycle loss depends on a small margin of pre-cleared seats, placements, bridge inventory, or intake flexibility rather than on destination discovery alone;
3. the route repeatedly uses `ES2` holds to preserve continuity while waiting for the same small fallback resource to open.

Move from `CP1` / `CP2` toward `CP3` when **any** of the following is true:

1. no single receiving node can honestly carry the continuity burden alone;
2. the real continuity path repeatedly depends on cross-node swapping, mutual aid, or pooled fallback inventory;
3. legal or funding fragmentation makes a single-host promise unstable, but a shared route family can still be predeclared truthfully.

### Minimal cross-node packet (`XC1-XC7`)

Once a route graduates beyond `CP0`, the archive now says one tiny cross-node packet should travel.

| Field | What the predeclared layer must carry |
|---|---|
| `XC1 route family` | the affected programme / route / placement family for which predeclared capacity exists |
| `XC2 protected cohort` | which in-scope learners the posture is designed to preserve (for example in-flight, next-intake, placement-assigned, or aid-window-affected) |
| `XC3 destination set` | one or more plausible receiving nodes, route substitutes, or pooled capacity families |
| `XC4 activation owner` | the node that may declare the posture active when `ES2` / `ES3` conditions appear |
| `XC5 transfer minimum` | the smallest records / packet / aid / placement facts that must move with the learner rather than being renegotiated from scratch |
| `XC6 capacity form` | whether the posture is `CP1`, `CP2`, or `CP3`, and whether activation means route mapping, shadow margin release, or pooled-slot allocation |
| `XC7 refresh / expiry anchor` | when the arrangement must be rechecked, refreshed, or allowed to lapse if the route or partner conditions have materially changed |

This packet is deliberately thinner than a full contract or memorandum of understanding.

The archive does **not** require publication of exact seat counts, full partner contracts, reimbursement formulas, or every fallback rule. It requires enough that partner nodes and continuity owners do not have to rediscover the route from scratch during the next disruption.

### Sector starter defaults for predeclared capacity

#### Higher-ed transfer / teach-out

Usually graduate first to `CP1`. The archive's starter answer is that repeated programme-family scarcity should more often produce more than one plausible receiving destination before it produces a public seat guarantee. `CP2` may follow where one receiving route repeatedly needs a small protected margin, but exact counts may remain local.

#### Workforce, adult-learning, and library-routed continuity

Often jump faster toward `CP2` or `CP3`. These routes commonly depend on a thin combination of intake timing, supportive services, and local partner coordination, so repeated `ES2` / `ES3` scarcity often means one node cannot truthfully protect continuity alone. A shared-slot or shared-routing posture is therefore more plausible here than in ordinary campus transfer.

#### Professional / practicum routes

Usually move through `CP1` with a hot eye on `CP2`. Placement calendars, licensure sequencing, and host-law constraints often make exact capacity highly local, so the archive prefers predeclared route maps and shadow margins before pretending a clean cross-site pooled entitlement exists. `CP3` is still possible, but usually only where host-sharing or regional placement coordination already functions in practice.

### What still stays local

Even after `CP1-CP3` harden, the archive still leaves the following local where law, inventory, or partner terms vary too much:

- exact seat, placement, or service counts;
- commercial or inter-institutional contract terms;
- detailed reimbursement and settlement rules;
- host-specific screening or compliance mechanics;
- and any local legal overlays that constrain which node may actually release a slot or fund.

The anti-bloat rule remains the same.

> **Do not export the whole partner agreement just to avoid publishing the small continuity-planning facts that actually keep the next disruption from starting at zero.**


## Learner-visible shell, partner-facing remainder, and freshness rules for predeclared capacity

The archive now closes the next narrower gap.

Once `CP1` / `CP2` / `CP3` planning exists, the question is no longer only **whether some predeclared route should exist**. It is also:

> **which parts of that planning layer learners must be able to see before the next disruption, which parts may stay partner-facing, and when a stale arrangement stops counting as a live continuity promise?**

Current public signals support a thin visibility-and-freshness rule rather than either total secrecy or full contract publication. HLC's current teach-out policy requires agreements to state the applicable time period, in-scope programmes and students, accepted credits, tuition and fees, records-retention plans, and communications to affected students. The OfS's current Condition C3 says student protection plans should be revised regularly so they remain current, practicable, relevant, and effective, should be published clearly and accessibly, and should provide clear information about when and how measures may be triggered. Current DOL WIOA guidance keeps the local remainder honest by treating supportive-service design as an area for local policy discretion, including limits and exceptions, rather than pretending every operational funding or capacity rule can travel unchanged. See `B158`, `B160`, `B162`. Current public consumer-rights and planning-modification signals also support a narrow materiality rule rather than quiet stale carry-forward: OfS consumer-rights guidance treats delivery, location, and placement facts as material information for informed student choice; HLC's current teach-out policy allows new review when circumstances change; and current DOL WIOA planning guidance itself is framed around formal modification requirements rather than silent plan drift. See `B158`, `B160`, `B162`, `B163`.

The archive's answer is therefore:

> **publish one very small learner-visible shell for `CP1-CP3`, keep counts/contracts/settlement mechanics partner-facing, and let stale arrangements decay from live learner-facing promise to partner-only lead before they expire entirely.**

### Learner-visible planning shell (`LV1-LV8`)

The archive now treats the following as the smallest learner-visible shell that should usually travel once a route has hardened beyond `CP0`.

| Field | What should be learner-visible |
|---|---|
| `LV1 route scope` | the route / programme / placement / service family covered by the predeclared posture |
| `LV2 protected cohort` | which learners the posture is designed to protect (for example in-flight, next-intake, placement-assigned, or aid-window-affected) |
| `LV3 posture type` | whether the route is operating through `CP1`, `CP2`, or `CP3` |
| `LV4 continuity promise` | the protected baseline the learner may expect, stated as a floor rather than as a guarantee of every preferred destination or exact slot count |
| `LV5 activation owner` | the office / node that can declare the posture active and route the learner onward |
| `LV6 destination shape` | whether continuity is expected to flow through more than one destination family, a shadow margin, or a shared-slot pool, and whether exact destinations are prelisted or individualized at activation |
| `LV7 freshness state` | whether the arrangement is current, needs live confirmation, or has lapsed, plus the last refresh / next review anchor |
| `LV8 challenge / fallback path` | where the learner goes if the visible planning layer turns out unavailable, exhausted, or disputed |

This shell is intentionally thinner than a full partner directory or contingency manual. The learner-visible layer is there so an affected person can tell **that a predeclared continuity shape exists, what kind of shape it is, who owns activation, whether it is current, and what protected baseline survives even if the first listed route fails.**

### What may remain partner-facing

The archive now keeps the partner-facing remainder narrow but real.

The following may usually remain partner-facing even after `CP1-CP3` harden:

- exact seat, placement, or service counts;
- exact size of any shadow margin or pooled-slot reserve;
- unpublished partner rankings or swap logic used behind the scenes;
- reimbursement formulas, financial settlement terms, and commercial clauses;
- host-specific screening, compliance, indemnity, or licensure mechanics;
- and internal activation thresholds whose disclosure would misstate a live promise or destabilize scarce inventory.

The archive's anti-bloat rule is now sharper here too:

> **do not hide the continuity shape behind contract secrecy, but do not pretend learners need every contract term or seat formula in order to know whether a protected path is real.**

### Named-destination rule

The archive now adds one small timing distinction.

Before disruption, the learner-visible shell may sometimes stop at **destination shape** rather than a complete named partner list, especially where host terms, programme approvals, or placement inventories change frequently. But once a learner or disruption cohort is actually inside a live activated posture, the learner should ordinarily be told the real candidate destination(s) or routing owner then in play for that case.

The archive therefore rejects both opposite failures:

1. **premature-opacity failure** — an activated continuity route still refuses to name any real downstream path to the affected learner;
2. **false-specificity failure** — a public learner-facing page lists specific destinations or hosts as if they are live promises after refresh has lapsed or partner conditions have materially changed.

### Freshness states that now travel (`FS0-FS2`)

The archive now adds a tiny freshness grammar for the learner-visible shell.

| Code | Meaning | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `FS0 current` | the posture has been refreshed on schedule and no material change has been left unreviewed | keep the `LV1-LV8` shell learner-visible as a live continuity-planning promise |
| `FS1 verify-on-activation` | the posture still provides a credible partner-side lead, but one or more route, host, or timing facts now require live reconfirmation before it can be presented as a currently usable learner path | keep the shell learner-visible only if it clearly says live confirmation is required; do not present named destinations as settled promises |
| `FS2 lapsed` | the posture has missed its refresh / review anchor or has undergone material change serious enough that the prior shell is no longer a truthful live promise | remove or mark the learner-facing shell as lapsed, and treat any old arrangement only as a fresh manual lead until reconfirmed or rebuilt |

The archive's anti-slippage rule is explicit:

> **stale continuity planning may still help the system remember where to call first, but it must not remain a learner-facing promise once it has stopped being current enough to rely on.**

### Refresh anchors that now travel (`RA1-RA3`)

The archive now hardens only three small refresh anchors.

| Anchor code | Meaning | Starter default |
|---|---|---|
| `RA1 cycle review` | review before the next ordinary admissions, aid, placement, practicum, or transfer cycle that the posture is meant to protect | use where the continuity risk is tied to recurring academic or intake cycles |
| `RA2 annual review` | refresh at least annually even if no disruption has fired yet | use as the ordinary floor for publicly learner-visible continuity-planning shells |
| `RA3 material-change review` | refresh immediately when partner, route, approval, location, modality, timing, or legal conditions materially change | use whenever those changes could make the visible shell untruthful even inside the current cycle |

These anchors can stack. A route may use `RA1 + RA3`, `RA2 + RA3`, or all three. The point is not to impose one universal calendar. It is to make freshness honest in a small shared way.

### Republication rule

The archive now distinguishes between **partner-side reconfirmation** and **learner-facing republication**.

Fresh partner-side reconfirmation is usually enough when only the hidden mechanics change and the learner-visible shell remains truthful. Fresh learner-facing republication is usually required when any of the following change materially:

1. route scope or protected cohort;
2. posture type (`CP1` / `CP2` / `CP3`);
3. continuity promise or fallback / challenge path;
4. activation owner or visible routing entry point;
5. destination shape as seen by learners;
6. freshness state itself (`FS0` to `FS1` or `FS2`, or back again).

The archive therefore rejects another common failure:

> **quietly keeping the old learner-facing planning shell in place after the shell has materially changed, while updating only the partner notes behind the scenes.**

### Material-change bands that now travel (`MC0-MC3`)

The archive now adds a very small materiality ladder for deciding when a visible continuity shell may stay on partner-side notes only, when it must be republished to learners, and when the old shell has broken badly enough that it should lapse instead of drifting forward on inertia.

| Code | Meaning | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `MC0 hidden-maintenance only` | partner contracts, settlement channels, internal reservation mechanics, or backstage routing notes change while the visible learner shell (`LV1-LV8`) remains fully truthful | partner-side reconfirmation is usually enough; no fresh learner-facing republication is owed yet |
| `MC1 shell-stable refresh` | a learner-visible field such as timing, contact point, or destination wording changes, but the protected cohort, posture type, continuity promise, charge floor, and fallback path remain the same | republish the learner-facing shell promptly as an ordinary refresh; the route may remain `FS0` if no live truth gap exists |
| `MC2 learner-decision material change` | destination family, delivery/location/placement shape, timing window, activation owner, approval basis, learner charge floor, or visible eligibility/scope changes in a way a learner would need for an informed decision | fresh learner-facing republication is required before the route is presented as a current continuity promise; until then the shell should usually sit at `FS1` rather than pretending nothing changed |
| `MC3 promise fracture` | the prior continuity promise, fallback path, protected cohort, no-worse charge floor, or approval basis has broken enough that the old shell is no longer a truthful protected promise | move to `FS1` or `FS2` immediately, remove the old shell as a live promise, and rebuild or reconfirm before learner-facing reuse |

The archive's new anti-slippage rule is therefore tighter than before:

> **not every partner update is learner-facing news, but any change a learner would need to know to rely on the route again is no longer backstage maintenance.**

### Provisional-lead states that now travel (`PL0-PL2`)

The archive now answers the second half of the same problem: when a lapsed or verify-on-activation route may still surface as a provisional lead without being misrepresented as a live promise.

| Code | Meaning | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `PL0 partner-memory only` | the old arrangement is useful only as staff memory about where to call first | keep it off learner-facing shells; use it only for fresh manual rebuilding or partner outreach |
| `PL1 family-only provisional lead` | the system can still truthfully name a destination family or route class plus activation owner, but not a specific named host/path as presently usable | learners may see the route family, freshness state, and owner contact, but not a named destination as if it were already secured |
| `PL2 named verify-on-activation lead` | the same named destination may remain visible only because the core promise still holds and the remaining uncertainty is narrow, such as live capacity or intake timing reconfirmation | the shell must be clearly marked `FS1`, keep the no-worse charge / scope / approval facts intact, and avoid presenting the named route as settled or guaranteed before activation |

`PL2` is deliberately narrow. Once approval, charge floor, protected cohort, or destination shape itself is in doubt, the archive should usually fall back to `PL1` or `PL0` rather than letting a half-true named route keep circulating.

Current public signals support a narrow destination-detail rule rather than either decorative partner lists or total opacity. HLC's current teach-out policy says receiving institutions must have real capacity and that teach-out agreements must clearly state applicable time period, in-scope programmes and students, accepted credits, tuition / fee facts, records-retention, and student communications. The OfS's current closure brief pushes advance course mapping and agreements in principle, but its current student-protection and consumer-rights guidance also insists that plans stay current, fair, and clear, and treats delivery, location, and placement facts as material for informed student choice. Current WIOA planning materials likewise require coordination with local entities and supportive-service agencies through formal state-plan modification rather than quiet stale carry-forward. See `B159`, `B161`, `B163`, and `B164`.

### Destination-detail bands that now travel (`DD0-DD3`)

The archive now answers the next narrower question: how much destination detail may remain learner-visible once a continuity shell is already `FS1` or provisional.

| Code | Meaning | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `DD0 off-shell memory` | only partner-side memory remains useful; no learner-facing route detail is presently truthful | keep old route detail off learner surfaces; use it only as `PL0` staff memory or for fresh manual rebuilding |
| `DD1 owner-contact-only shell` | the continuity promise and activation owner still stand, but even a destination family would currently overstate what is live | learners may see the activation owner, freshness state, protected cohort, continuity floor, and next verification point, but not a route family or named host |
| `DD2 destination-family shell` | the route family or path class remains stable enough to publish, but the specific host/path does not | learners may see the destination family, freshness state, owner, and fallback, but not a named host as if already secured |
| `DD3 named verify-on-activation shell` | a specific named destination may remain visible because the core route still stands and only narrow live verification remains | keep it clearly marked `FS1`, avoid guarantee language, and keep the no-worse charge / scope / approval facts intact |

The anti-slippage move is now sharper again:

> **when a continuity promise weakens, route detail should usually degrade before protection degrades.**

### Destination-detail downgrade rule

Keep `DD3` only when **all** of the following still hold:

1. the same named destination or host/path remains the genuine first candidate at activation;
2. the unresolved uncertainty is narrow timing, live-capacity release, or same-cycle intake confirmation rather than route-shape drift;
3. approval basis, accepted-credit floor, learner charge floor, and protected cohort remain materially unchanged;
4. the activation owner can verify the named path inside the published window without fresh destination rediscovery.

Downgrade from `DD3` to `DD2` when the route family remains real but the named host/path no longer does. Downgrade from `DD2` to `DD1` when even the family statement would overstate what is live — for example because allocation now depends on live broker matching, pooled-slot distribution, or local partner availability across roughly equivalent route classes. Move to `DD0` / `PL0` when no truthful learner-facing continuity shell remains beyond a fresh manual callback or rebuilding lead.

In practice, the archive treats named host/provider drift, delivery / location / placement movement, unresolved approval or licensure basis, learner-facing charge-floor or accepted-credit uncertainty, and posture drift from one-node protection to pooled allocation as strong downgrade signals rather than backstage maintenance.

### Sector-and-posture starter defaults for destination detail

| Capacity posture | Higher-ed transfer / teach-out | Workforce, adult-learning, and library-routed continuity | Professional / practicum routes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `CP1 multi-destination` | usually start at `DD2`; use `DD3` only when one receiving institution already remains the real candidate and the remaining uncertainty is narrow timing or live-capacity confirmation | usually `DD2`, sometimes `DD1` where routing depends on warm-transfer brokerage more than one stable provider family | usually `DD1` or cautious `DD2`; keep named hosts partner-side until activation unless matching is already largely done |
| `CP2 shadow-capacity` | `DD3` is more plausible because one named receiver often really holds the protected margin; downgrade if campus, credit, price, or scope facts move | usually `DD2`; use `DD3` only where one site/provider truly holds the protected margin and eligibility / support facts remain stable | usually `DD2`; `DD3` only when host approval is already secured and the remaining uncertainty is merely slot release |
| `CP3 shared-slot pool` | usually `DD2`; drop to `DD1` when the pooled-allocation owner matters more than any pre-allocation host name | usually `DD1` by default; shared pools and fast-moving local inventories make named destination lists misleading early | usually `DD1` almost by default; pooled host and licensure constraints make pre-activation named publication unusually fragile |

Higher education can usually keep more detail learner-visible because teach-out agreements and course mapping can stabilise destination families in advance, but named receiving institution, accepted-credit, tuition / fee, or protected-cohort drift is already `DD2` / `DD1` terrain. Workforce, adult-learning, and library systems often have real continuity protection but more variable local provider maps, so family and owner publication usually travel better than named-site promises. Professional and practicum routes often owe the strongest continuity work yet the weakest pre-activation naming because host approval, placement calendars, and professional-body constraints can move faster than learner-facing shells.


### Activated-route outputs that now travel (`AR1-AR3`)

The archive now answers the next narrower question: once a learner actually activates a `DD1` or `DD2` shell, provisional continuity publication must resolve into one of three honest outputs rather than lingering as indefinite family / owner language.

| Code | Meaning | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `AR1 named destination` | one specific receiving host/path is now truthfully verified as the continuity route | publish the named destination, keep the no-worse charge / scope / approval facts visible, and give the learner the handoff / acceptance steps |
| `AR2 equivalent-option set` | two or more materially equivalent routes are now genuinely live and each can preserve the continuity floor without ordinary learner-borne loss | publish the bounded option set and the learner's next step rather than forcing the learner to rediscover comparable paths by rumor |
| `AR3 explicit no-route-yet notice` | no truthful named destination or equivalent option set is yet available | say so explicitly, keep the continuity claim live under the applicable `ES1-ES3` floor, and publish the next verification point, owner contact, and review / escalation path |

The archive's anti-drift rule is now explicit again:

> **`DD1` and `DD2` are allowed as pre-activation truth shells, not as indefinite post-activation waiting rooms.**

Once the learner has made a live continuity claim with the archive's ordinary `TF1-TF3` + minimum-proof package, the route should move inside one acknowledgement clock and one route-resolution clock. If no truthful `AR1` or `AR2` exists inside that clock, the archive now prefers `AR3` over letting a provisional shell drift forward without saying whether any real route is ready.

### Acknowledgement clocks that now travel (`AK1-AK3`)

The archive now hardens only three small acknowledgement bands.

| Code | Meaning | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `AK1 urgent-live-window ack` | an affected learner faces plausible near-term loss of transfer, intake, aid, placement, practicum, or licensure position if the claim sits quietly in an inbox | acknowledge by the end of the next business day |
| `AK2 ordinary activation ack` | the continuity claim is live but not in same-week forfeiture territory | acknowledge within 2 business days |
| `AK3 cohort-batch ack` | the route is being handled through a declared cohort batch and no near-term route loss is presently in view | acknowledge within 5 business days and publish the next common review point |

These are learner-facing floor clocks, not permission to wait until the last allowed minute.

### Route-resolution clocks that now travel (`RK1-RK3`)

The archive now adds one equally small verification ladder for when an activated `DD1` / `DD2` shell must become `AR1`, `AR2`, or `AR3`.

| Code | Meaning | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `RK1 fast route resolution` | one of `AR1-AR3` should be published quickly because the route is already tight, mapped, or urgency is high | resolve within 5 business days |
| `RK2 standard route resolution` | some cross-node checking is real, but the route should still be settled within one short working-cycle | resolve within 10 business days |
| `RK3 extended host/approval resolution` | professional-body, practicum-host, or multi-node approval work makes a shorter clock unrealistic | resolve within 15 business days, but do not let the shell go silent before the next visible review point |

If the system cannot truthfully reach `AR1` or `AR2` inside the applicable `RK` band, it now owes `AR3` rather than one more round of vague owner-contact language.

### When each activated output is owed

Use `AR1` when one named host/path is genuinely ready or has become the truthful first landing path after activation. Do **not** force `AR2` merely to create decorative choice where one route is already the real answer.

Use `AR2` when two or more routes are already live and the material differences between them do not push route delay, accepted-credit loss, approval risk, or ordinary added cost back onto the learner. The point is a real bounded choice set, not a family label in slightly expanded form.

Use `AR3` when neither `AR1` nor `AR2` is presently truthful. `AR3` is not a denial. It is the learner-facing statement that routing is still live but not yet concrete enough to name honestly, together with the next review point, the owner, and the continuity floor that remains protected while matching continues.

### What `AR2` must actually show (`EQ1-EQ6`)

The archive now closes the next narrower seam after activation timing. `AR2` is not a bucket for "several plausible places" or "more than one route is being explored." It is allowed only when the learner is being shown a **bounded option set whose relevant burdens stay materially inside the same continuity floor**.

A truthful `AR2` publication should therefore carry six small comparability fields:

| Code | What must be learner-visible | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| `EQ1 recognition / remaining-work floor` | accepted credit or prior-work treatment, remaining required work, and whether any option changes the protected completion floor | a route is not equivalent if one path quietly adds extra restart or weakens prior-work recognition |
| `EQ2 learner-charge floor` | tuition, fees, additional charges, and whether no-fault payer / waiver protections still keep ordinary added cost off the learner | a route is not equivalent if one option is merely the same programme name with a materially worse price floor |
| `EQ3 timing / intake floor` | next start or handoff timing, any live risk of term / aid / cycle loss, and whether a hold is already preserving place | a route is not equivalent if one option predictably loses the continuity window while another does not |
| `EQ4 delivery / location / access floor` | mode, site or geography, schedule shape, key accessibility or accommodation facts, and any major travel / attendance implications | a route is not equivalent if one option quietly depends on materially harder travel, schedule, or access conditions |
| `EQ5 approval / practicum / licensure floor` | whether any route still depends on separate approval, host acceptance, placement, or licensure-relevant fit that is not already secured | a route is not equivalent if one path is already real while another still rests on materially different approval risk |
| `EQ6 support / handoff burden floor` | named owner, next-step burden, and any continuity-critical support or referral facts needed to participate | a route is not equivalent if one option stays usable only by shifting extra coordination, documentation, or support-navigation work back onto the learner |

Higher-ed `AR2` sets will often turn first on `EQ1-EQ3`; workforce, adult-learning, and library-routed sets often turn on `EQ3`, `EQ4`, and `EQ6`; and professional / practicum sets often turn on `EQ4` and `EQ5` more than on raw provider count alone.

### When differences are too material for `AR2` (`MV1-MV4`)

The archive now hardens a still smaller material-variance rule.

| Code | Difference that breaks `AR2` | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `MV1 recognition / completion drift` | one option materially worsens accepted-credit treatment, remaining required work, or likely time-to-completion | do not call the set equivalent; prefer `AR1` if one route is already the truthful floor-preserving landing path, otherwise `AR3` |
| `MV2 learner-borne cost drift` | one option materially worsens ordinary out-of-pocket cost, travel / housing / childcare burden, or aid handling after the no-fault floor is applied | do not hide the burden inside choice language; either absorb the difference so the set becomes truthful or stop calling it `AR2` |
| `MV3 delivery / access drift` | one option materially differs on delivery mode, location, schedule, accessibility, or placement conditions in a way that predictably re-sorts who can actually use it | do not publish the set as equivalent just because several destinations exist on paper |
| `MV4 approval / certainty drift` | one option still depends on materially different approval, host matching, seat competition, or bespoke learner proof not shared across the set | do not present the set as already-live equivalence when one route is still meaningfully more contingent than the others |

The archive's test is deliberately practical: **if an ordinary learner could predictably end up with worse timing, weaker recognition, greater unreimbursed burden, or materially shakier approval by landing on one option rather than another, the set is not yet an honest `AR2`.**

### When choice stays real and when defaulting turns coercive (`CH1-CH3`)

The archive now adds one last small choice/default grammar for activated equivalent-option sets.

| Code | Meaning | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `CH1 supported bounded choice` | the learner receives the `EQ1-EQ6` fields, a named owner, and a choice window long enough to compare routes, ask questions, and use needed accessibility or support channels without losing the continuity floor | this is the ordinary truthful way to publish `AR2` |
| `CH2 protective provisional default` | the system may place a temporary seat-hold or provisional assignment inside a genuine `AR2` set only to prevent immediate forfeiture, and only if the learner can switch to another `AR2` option without losing priority, payer protection, or review position | silence may preserve protection, but it may not silently decide the learner's future on worse terms |
| `CH3 coercive choice / default` | the window is too short or unsupported, silence locks the learner into a materially worse route, switching requires new proof or loss of place, or recommendation/default language quietly steers the learner toward the owner's cheaper/easier route | the set is no longer functioning as continuity-preserving choice and must be rebuilt, individualized, or restated |

A genuine `AR2` set therefore permits only **reversible** defaulting. Any default that becomes sticky, costly to escape, slower to resolve, or materially worse on `EQ1-EQ6` is not a continuity aid; it is a disguised assignment rule.

Current public signals support this compact equivalence-and-coercion rule. HLC's current teach-out policy requires receiving institutions to be reasonably similar in content, delivery modality, and scheduling, to have the necessary support services and capacity, and to state accepted credits plus tuition and fees for affected learners. The OfS's current student-protection guidance says mitigations should be fair and reasonable for students, taking account of mobility, educational need, parity of course content, and financial consequences, while current student-facing closure guidance expects clear timescales, alternative study options, transfer arrangements, and support information. Its current consumer-information guidance also treats delivery, location, and placement facts as material information for informed choice. Current DOL guidance treats supportive services as participation-enabling, requires local policies on referral, funding, limits, and exceptions, and notes there is no cap on supportive services under WIOA's programmatic or nondiscrimination rules. See `B158`, `B160`, `B165`, and `B166`.

## First-assignment handling, hold windows, and no-loss switch rights

The archive now closes the next narrower gap.

Once a genuine `AR2` set exists, another question appears immediately:

> **may the system hold one route, recommend one route, or temporarily place the learner onto one route without turning bounded continuity choice into de facto single-route assignment?**

Current public signals support a small answer rather than either silent assignment or total first-assignment improvisation. HLC's current teach-out policy requires teach-out arrangements to be fair and equitable, to provide reasonable opportunities to complete, and to publish scope, timing, accepted-credit, tuition / fee, and communications facts. OfS's current `F2` condition requires providers to publish information about student-transfer arrangements, its current `C3` condition requires published student-protection plans and implementation when the triggering events occur, and its 2025 closure guidance says providers should map courses, plan transfer support, and communicate so students can make informed choices. Current DOL guidance keeps supportive-services routing inside local policies that must address referrals, funding, limits, and exceptions, while still requiring those supports to be necessary for participation rather than rumor-based favors. See `B160`, `B165`, `B166`, and `B167`.

The archive's answer is therefore:

> **inside a genuine `AR2` set, first-assignment handling may preserve continuity only while it stays visibly temporary, explained, reversible, and no-loss.**

### First-assignment bands that now travel (`FA1-FA4`)

| Code | Meaning | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `FA1 neutral pre-hold` | the system temporarily holds one or more `AR2` routes open while records sync, contact is made, or the learner compares options, but does not treat one route as the preferred outcome | the hold may protect capacity, but it must publish the expiry point and preserve full switch rights |
| `FA2 explained soft recommendation` | the system highlights one `AR2` route as operationally simpler, faster, or nearer because of route facts that matter to the learner, while the other `AR2` routes remain genuinely protected | the learner must see why the recommendation exists and must be able to choose another `AR2` route without losing protection |
| `FA3 protective temporary default` | the system provisionally places the learner onto one `AR2` route only to prevent immediate seat / cycle / intake / host loss while contact, advice, or comparison is still happening | the default must expire unless affirmed or replaced, and it must carry the archive's no-loss switch rights automatically |
| `FA4 disguised assignment` | the pre-hold, recommendation, or provisional default quietly makes one route sticky, penalizes switching, or hides that one route has become the only really protected path | restate the case as `AR1`, rebuild the `AR2` set, or move to individualized / `AR3` handling |

The archive's anti-slippage line is now explicit again:

> **a first assignment inside `AR2` is allowed only as continuity protection, never as a cheaper way to avoid admitting that the system has already chosen the route.**

### Hold-duration bands that now travel (`HD1-HD3`)

The archive now adds one small hold-window ladder for how long `FA1-FA3` handling may remain live after clear individualized notice.

| Code | Meaning | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `HD1 imminent-loss hold` | an immediate seat, intake, aid, placement, practicum, or licensure risk is already live | keep the protected switch right open through at least the end of the second business day after clear notice or until the immediate-loss event passes, whichever is later |
| `HD2 ordinary comparison hold` | the learner needs a short but real comparison window, and no same-day forfeiture is live | keep the protected switch right open for at least 5 business days after clear notice |
| `HD3 supported comparison hold` | translation, accessibility support, records movement, caring duties, cross-node advising, or similar facts make a shorter comparison window predictably misleading | keep the protected switch right open for at least 10 business days after clear notice unless the learner affirmatively settles sooner |

These are starter floors, not maximums. Where the archive already owes `AK1` / `RK1` urgency, the system should act quickly **and** keep the switch right usable long enough to be real.

### Explanation fields that now travel (`XF1-XF5`)

A learner-facing first assignment is now truthful only if the archive can publish five very small explanation facts.

| Field | What must be visible |
|---|---|
| `XF1 assignment reason` | whether the current state is a neutral hold, a soft recommendation, or a protective temporary default, and the short route fact that caused it |
| `XF2 expiry point` | when the hold / recommendation / provisional default will lapse, refresh, or require review |
| `XF3 live alternatives` | which other `AR2` routes remain inside the protected set at the moment of notice |
| `XF4 switch method` | how the learner switches, what channel or owner receives the switch, and whether any same-day confirmation step is needed |
| `XF5 protection carryover` | what remains preserved on switch — priority, payer protection, records/support carryover, and review / escalation path |

The archive rejects any first-assignment state that forces the learner to reverse-engineer these facts from partner notes, silent UI defaults, or call-center rumor.

### No-loss switch rights that now travel (`SW1-SW5`)

A temporary first assignment inside `AR2` is now honest only if the learner keeps five small no-loss switch rights.

| Code | Meaning | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `SW1 priority carryover` | switching among still-live `AR2` routes does not forfeit the learner's protected place, queue date, or same-window priority band | no reset to the back of the line merely because first assignment happened |
| `SW2 payer-floor carryover` | switching does not convert the preserved baseline into fresh learner-borne charge, deposit, or bridge cost | the original no-fault floor still travels with the learner |
| `SW3 proof carryover` | the learner does not have to re-prove continuity entitlement, restart hardship narration, or rebuild already-accepted route facts | only genuinely route-specific facts may be newly requested |
| `SW4 record-and-support carryover` | already-gathered records, accessibility facts, support routing, and handoff notes should travel across the `AR2` set where lawful and relevant | the system should move the packet, not force redisclosure by default |
| `SW5 review-path carryover` | the same human escalation / complaint / override path remains available after the switch | a provisional assignment may not reset accountability ownership |

The archive now treats these as the core test for whether a first assignment is still `AR2`-truthful. Once one or more of `SW1-SW5` falls away, the system is usually no longer describing bounded equivalent choice. It is describing a narrower route truth and should say so.

### When `FA2` or `FA3` remains truthful

A soft recommendation or temporary default usually remains truthful only when **all** of the following hold:

1. the `AR2` set still passes the archive's `EQ1-EQ6` and `MV1-MV4` tests;
2. the recommendation/default reason is learner-relevant route protection (timing, accessibility, proximity, host readiness, course map stability, support continuity) rather than merely owner convenience, cheaper settlement, or invisible institutional preference;
3. the learner receives `XF1-XF5` clearly enough to act on them;
4. the learner can use the applicable `HD1-HD3` window and still keep `SW1-SW5`.

The archive now rejects four common slippages.

1. **hidden-steering failure** — one route is preselected or verbally framed as the natural choice without the short route fact that justifies it.
2. **sticky-default failure** — switching loses place, payer protection, support routing, or review ownership.
3. **silent-expiry failure** — the hold or switch right disappears before clear notice plus a usable window.
4. **disguised-singularity failure** — one route is already functioning as the real outcome, but the system still calls the state `AR2` to avoid admitting that it has become `AR1`.

|---|---|---|---|

| Workforce, adult-learning, and library-routed continuity | often `FA3`, because intake, support-service timing, and local provider inventory can move quickly | when waiting for affirmative response would itself destroy participation or a public-route slot | often `HD1` or `HD2`, but with strong `SW1-SW5` carryover because routing can be fast-moving |

### When no-loss switch rights may taper without retroactive burden shift

The archive now closes the next seam after first assignment. A genuine `AR2` set cannot keep full `SW1-SW5` portability forever once one route has begun to consume real route-specific capacity, support spend, or legal aid handling. But the taper rule must be **prospective, component-specific, and anchored in actual consumption** rather than owner convenience or vague claims that onboarding has already started.

### Switch-taper bands that now travel (`TP0-TP3`)

| Code | State | What it means | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `TP0 full no-loss state` | no strong route-consumption fact has yet occurred | the learner may still use the archive's full `SW1-SW5` set across the live `AR2` routes | preserve the full switch package; do not narrow rights merely because the system began matching or packaging work |
| `TP1 started-but-still-reversible` | ordinary onboarding has started, but only weak or replaceable preparation facts exist | the route is warm, not yet consumed in a way that makes narrowing truthful | a shorter response window may be justified, but `SW1-SW5` still travel intact |
| `TP2 component taper` | one or more strong consumption facts now exist for a particular route component | some portability may narrow, but only for the actually consumed component and only prospectively | do not convert the whole case into ordinary route rules; preserve every `SW` element not truthfully exhausted |
| `TP3 ordinary-route conversion` | the learner has crossed a visible route-specific commitment point such that the old `AR2` set no longer exists as a still-live equivalent set | switching may now move onto ordinary local transfer / withdrawal / host-change rules for the forward path | keep no retroactive burden shift, preserve already-earned proof/record carryover, and keep the same accountability/review owner visible |

### Which consumption facts are strong enough (`CF1-CF5`)

| Code | Fact pattern | Taper strength | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| `CF1 admin-only movement` | account creation, routine advising, tentative schedule placement, ordinary record sync, partner inquiry, or estimated / memo-entry aid handling only | not strong enough by itself | admin motion is not yet real consumption; it should not be used to trap the learner inside the first route |
| `CF2 individualized capacity or host preparation` | a real seat, cohort place, host slot, site-specific compliance step, or supervisor preparation has been reserved or materially consumed for this learner | often enough for `TP2`, sometimes `TP3` in practicum-heavy routes | real individualized preparation can create scarce forward commitments, but it does not justify wiping the whole switch package by default |
| `CF3 attendance or participation start` | the learner has actually begun attendance, training activity, or host/practicum participation on the assigned route | often enough for `TP2`, sometimes `TP3` where the old option set is no longer materially equivalent | once the learner has genuinely started, proof, timing, and route-shape facts change in a way that generic pre-start portability cannot ignore |
| `CF4 legal aid or charge event` | actual disbursement, direct payment, credit-balance release, accepted post-withdrawal disbursement, or return/withdrawal clock has begun | often enough for `TP2`; use `TP3` where the original `AR2` set is no longer legally/economically symmetric | financial states become materially different only once the aid/charge event is real, not when rough packaging or memo entries merely predicted it |
| `CF5 consumed supportive-service spend` | transportation, childcare, application/testing/certification, housing, accessibility, or similar support has actually been used or irretrievably committed under local policy | often enough for narrow `TP2` treatment | consumed support can justify tapering portability for that spent component, but it does not by itself erase route, proof, or review protections wholesale |

### How the five switch rights taper

| Right | What usually survives longest | When narrowing is most truthful | What the archive now forbids |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SW1 priority carryover` | preserve queue date / protected cohort position through `TP2` wherever another still-live route can absorb the learner without displacing a newly committed participant | where `CF2` / `CF3` means the original reserved slot has become route-specific and non-fungible | resetting the learner to the back of the line because of `CF1` admin handling alone |
| `SW2 payer protection carryover` | all already absorbed no-fault continuity costs remain system-borne | future route-specific costs may stop traveling once `CF4` / `CF5` / strong `CF2` facts make them truly new forward costs rather than costs of the temporary first assignment | retroactive invoicing for sunk costs incurred while the system itself was still using protective default / routing help |
| `SW3 proof carryover` | started work, attendance, or already-earned recognition should keep traveling as transcript, packet, or prior-work carryover even inside `TP3` | only where the destination route lawfully cannot treat the same in-flight proof as equivalent without new review | treating ordinary route change as if prior participation simply vanished |
| `SW4 record-and-support carryover` | records, accessibility facts, and already-gathered support routing should usually survive all the way into `TP3` | only where law, safety, or route-specific relevance makes a component non-portable | forcing full redisclosure merely because the learner exercised a still-live switch right |
| `SW5 review-path carryover` | the same accountable owner and escalation path should remain visible even after taper | only the ordinary forward route rule may change; accountability ownership should not reset | using taper to launder the first assignment out of the same review / complaint path |

### When `TP2` or `TP3` is actually truthful

A narrowing or expiry of `SW1-SW5` is now truthful only when **all** of the following hold:

1. at least one strong `CF2-CF5` fact exists, rather than `CF1` admin movement alone;
2. the system narrows only the component that has actually been consumed, instead of using one spent support item or one host-prep step to erase the whole switch package;
3. the learner gets clear notice of what changed, what still travels, what no longer travels, and what ordinary local rule now governs the forward route;
4. already absorbed or system-caused sunk costs do not get pushed backward onto the learner;
5. if the original `AR2` set is no longer honestly live, the archive says so and moves to `TP3` / ordinary-route language rather than pretending reversible multi-route choice still exists.

The archive now rejects four common slippages.

1. **admin-as-consumption failure** — packaging, memo-entry aid, routine orientation, or ordinary record sync is treated as if it already made switching too costly.
2. **retro-billing failure** — once the system used `FA2` / `FA3`, later taper is used to invoice the learner for that temporary default's sunk costs.
3. **one-component-total-forfeiture failure** — one spent support item or one host-prep step is used to erase all of `SW1-SW5`.
4. **accountability-reset failure** — taper of route portability is used to reset the human review owner, complaint path, or override responsibility.

### Sector-and-posture starter defaults for switch taper

| Route type | Most ordinary taper point | What usually counts as too weak | What usually survives even after taper |
|---|---|---|---|
| Higher-ed transfer / teach-out | often `TP1` until real attendance or `CF4` aid/charge events begin; move to `TP2` for real start or disbursement, and to `TP3` only when the old `AR2` set is no longer materially live | estimated aid, memo-entry packaging, routine course mapping, or non-binding registration work alone | proof/credit carryover, record transfer, review ownership, and no retroactive fee shift for the temporary route |
| Workforce, adult-learning, and library-routed continuity | often earlier `TP2`, because support timing and local inventory move fast and `CF5` can become real quickly | generic referral activity, ordinary intake talk, or tentative slot discussion alone | review ownership, core record/support carryover, and payer protection for already approved or consumed continuity support |
| Professional / practicum routes | often `TP2` or `TP3` sooner, because `CF2` host-prep and site compliance can become genuinely scarce and route-specific | mere host outreach, speculative matching, or unconfirmed site interest | no retroactive burden shift for prep the learner did not choose, plus carryover of records, disclosed approvals, and review path where lawful |

### When post-start route deterioration must reopen continuity protection

The archive now closes the next seam after taper. A `TP2` or `TP3` state is **not** a one-way ratchet into ordinary learner risk. If the assigned route later worsens, loses promised support, or fails in a way the learner did not cause, earlier taper cannot be used as a reason to deny fresh continuity protection. The archive needs a small reopen grammar so system-caused route deterioration does not get laundered into ordinary progress failure merely because the learner already started.

### Reopen-after-taper bands that now travel (`RO0-RO3`)

| Code | State | What it means | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `RO0 no reopen` | only ordinary learner-progress or voluntary-change facts exist | the problem is not a fresh continuity event | stay on ordinary local route rules; do not relabel ordinary academic, conduct, or preference changes as no-fault continuity failure |
| `RO1 in-route repair` | a system-caused deterioration exists, but the same route can still be repaired fast enough and without extra learner-borne loss | the continuity floor can still be preserved inside the current assignment | stop harmful clocks, keep payer protection live, and repair the route in place rather than forcing a fresh route search |
| `RO2 component reopen` | one or more post-start deterioration facts break a previously tapered component, but the learner still has a realistic path to continue through rebuilt route choice or substitute support | some fuller protection must reopen again for the affected component | restore the affected parts of `SW1-SW5`, reopen route-resolution work, and treat the case as fresh continuity on the impaired component rather than as a finished ordinary-route matter |
| `RO3 fresh continuity event` | the assigned route no longer offers a reasonable opportunity to complete on the protected terms the archive had been relying on | the old route has materially failed as a continuity destination | reopen the fuller continuity stack: restate the case through fresh `AR1` / `AR2` / `AR3` handling, re-enter `ES` scarcity / stronger-floor handling if needed, and preserve proof, record, and no-retro-billing protections from the failed route |

### Which post-start deterioration facts are strong enough (`DF1-DF5`)

| Code | Fact pattern | Usual reopen strength | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| `DF1 timing or delivery drift` | start date, schedule, pace, modality, site, or access conditions move in a way that now breaks the continuity floor the learner was relying on | often `RO1`, sometimes `RO2` | some route changes can be repaired in place, but not if they now destroy the learner's practical ability to continue on the promised protected terms |
| `DF2 recognition or approval drift` | credit treatment, credential level, licensure / accreditor / validator status, or route content changes materially after assignment | often `RO2`, sometimes `RO3` | once recognition or approval facts move, the learner is no longer just facing ordinary study risk; the continuity route itself has changed shape |
| `DF3 capacity, host, or partner-performance failure` | a receiving institution, host, supervisor, site, or partner can no longer perform the material route role it had taken on | often `RO2`, often `RO3` in practicum or teach-out settings | the route the learner actually entered may cease to exist in the promised form even after start, so prior taper cannot stand as if nothing changed |
| `DF4 charge, aid, or support deterioration` | new learner-borne charges appear, a promised waiver or funding arrangement drops away, or participation-enabling support fails for reasons the learner did not cause | often `RO2`, sometimes `RO3` | when the system-side financial or support floor worsens after start, the learner is facing a fresh continuity burden rather than ordinary route cost |
| `DF5 route closure or legal shutoff` | the assigned route, branch, host site, or provider closes, loses authority, or the school responsible for the teach-out / continuation route fails the material terms of the arrangement so the learner no longer has a reasonable opportunity to complete | usually `RO3` | this is the clearest case of a fresh continuity event: the destination the learner was routed into has itself materially failed |

### When post-start deterioration is a fresh continuity event rather than ordinary learner risk

A `TP2` or `TP3` case must reopen fuller continuity protection when **all** of the following hold:

1. one or more `DF1-DF5` facts arise after the learner was assigned or started on the route;
2. the deterioration is not principally explained by the learner's own academic nonperformance, misconduct, voluntary preference change, or failure to complete clearly disclosed route obligations after reasonable support;
3. the deterioration breaks or seriously threatens one of the continuity-floor facts the archive had relied on — timing, delivery/access, recognition/approval, capacity, charges/aid, or participation-enabling support;
4. the problem cannot be cured inside the current assignment quickly enough and without extra learner-borne loss, or else the route no longer exists in a form that makes the old `TP2` / `TP3` treatment truthful; and
5. already-earned proof, already-transferred records, and already-absorbed no-fault costs continue forward instead of being erased because the destination later failed.

### What a truthful reopen must do first

| Protection element | What must usually reopen | What the archive now forbids |
|---|---|---|
| Route-resolution work | restart a visible route-resolution clock and restate the learner's live route state as fresh `AR1` / `AR2` / `AR3` or `RO1` repair-in-place work | leaving the learner inside stale `TP3` ordinary-route language after the destination itself materially worsened |
| Payer protection | keep already absorbed costs system-borne and reopen no-fault handling for new costs caused by the route deterioration | treating the failure of the assigned destination as if the learner had voluntarily created a new self-funded move |
| Proof / record carryover | preserve already-earned proof, attendance, record, accommodation, and support-routing packets into the reopened continuity step | forcing restart-from-zero because the learner had already started on the now-failed route |
| Accountability / review | keep the same accountable owner visible until a clearly named successor takes over the reopened case | using route failure to wash the earlier assignment out of the complaint, escalation, or override path |

The archive now rejects four common slippages.

1. **learner-risk laundering** — a route-side deterioration is redescribed as if the learner simply failed to persist.
2. **consumed-once-forever failure** — earlier `CF2-CF5` consumption is treated as permanently blocking any later reopen even after the route itself materially fails.
3. **reopen-with-new-charge failure** — the learner must now pay to escape a route that deteriorated for system-caused reasons.
4. **stale-ordinary-language failure** — the route has materially worsened or collapsed, but the archive still speaks as if only ordinary local withdrawal / transfer rules remain.

### Sector-and-posture starter defaults for reopen-after-taper handling

| Route type | Most ordinary truthful reopen point | What usually does **not** count | What usually must reopen fastest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Higher-ed transfer / teach-out | often `RO1` for bounded timetable or delivery repair, but `RO2` / `RO3` once receiving-route scope, approval, charges, or teach-out performance materially drift | ordinary study difficulty, voluntary major preference change, or nonresponse after clear notice alone | route-resolution clocks, payer protection for replacement costs caused by the new route failure, and proof / record carryover |
| Workforce, adult-learning, and library-routed continuity | often `RO2`, because provider availability and support failures can quickly block participation even when the learner had already started | ordinary job-search disappointment, missed appointments without good reason, or generic labour-market difficulty alone | support restoration, referral / routing rebuild, and no-fault handling for new participation-enabling costs caused by the failed route |
| Professional / practicum routes | often earlier `RO2` / `RO3`, because host, site, supervisor, and approval failures can quickly destroy the route's real viability | learner incompetence, misconduct, or failure to meet plainly disclosed professional requirements not caused by the route | host / approval replacement work, preserved record and review ownership, and no-retro-billing for preparatory work tied to the failed placement |

### How much of the old package must revive after reopen

The archive now closes the next seam after reopen. Saying that a `TP2` / `TP3` route failure can reopen continuity is still too vague if the reopened learner is simply sent back into a thinner queue, a weaker scarcity band, or a new charge stack. The archive therefore needs one still smaller revival grammar so `RO2` and `RO3` restore the old protection **to the extent that the new failure has broken it**, without pretending that literally every consumed route-specific fact can be rolled back as if it never happened.

### Revival bands that now travel (`RB0-RB3`)

| Code | State | What it means | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `RB0 repair-only carryforward` | the case sits in `RO1`, or the reopen does not break any earlier priority / payer / portability component | no old package element has actually been lost | keep the current package live, stop new harmful clocks, and repair in place rather than manufacturing a wider revival |
| `RB1 impaired-component revival` | the case truthfully sits in `RO2` | only some earlier continuity protections have been broken again | revive the affected components of priority, payer, portability, and write-off handling while leaving unaffected, truthfully consumed components settled |
| `RB2 equivalent-protection rebuild` | the case sits in `RO2` or `RO3`, but the exact old slot, host, or pooled route cannot literally be recreated without displacing already-committed third parties or pretending the old route still exists | literal reinstatement is untruthful, but back-of-line restart would under-protect the learner | rebuild the old protection through the nearest truthful equivalent: cohort-date carryover, same-or-better scarcity band, shadow-slot, next protected cycle, or equivalent no-fault cost floor |
| `RB3 full continuity revival` | the case truthfully sits in `RO3` because the assigned route has materially failed as a destination | the old destination can no longer be treated as an ordinary local route | revive the fuller continuity stack from the learner's earlier protected position, reopen fresh `AR1` / `AR2` / `AR3` work, and re-enter scarcity / stronger-floor handling where the new failure makes that necessary |

### What usually comes back under `RB1` versus `RB3`

| Package element | `RB1` / ordinary `RO2` | `RB3` / ordinary `RO3` | What the archive forbids |
|---|---|---|---|
| Priority / queue position | restore the learner's earlier queue date, protected cohort anchor, or same-window review position **for the impaired component** where that queue still governs the reopened step | restore the learner's earlier continuity seniority as the floor for the rebuilt route, using `RB2` if the old slot cannot literally return | sending the learner to the back of a new line merely because the first destination later failed |
| Scarcity band / stronger-floor status | restore the earlier `PF` / `ES` posture for the reopened component, unless current facts now justify a stronger one | re-enter scarcity / stronger-floor handling no worse than the earlier band that had already been earned, again subject to stronger current facts where truthful | treating later route failure as a reason to demote the learner below the protection already earned before the failed assignment |
| No-fault payer coverage | reopen no-fault handling for new costs caused by the route deterioration; keep already absorbed waivers, write-offs, and support spend absorbed | reopen the fuller no-fault stack for the replacement route or substitute support, while keeping the failed route's already absorbed costs out of the learner's new bill | billing the learner again for escaping a route that failed for system-caused reasons |
| Portability / packet carryover | revive proof, records, accessibility, support-routing, and review ownership for the reopened component without forcing re-proof absent a genuine route-specific need | carry the packet forward into the fresh continuity event as the starting record, not as a scrap of stale history | restart-from-zero treatment because the learner had already entered the failed route |
| Component write-offs / offsets | preserve earlier write-offs and waivers on the affected component; any unused or no-longer-useful failed-route charge should be offset, waived, or credited before new learner-borne charging begins | preserve prior write-offs, and treat failed-route nonvalue or unusable spend as part of the new system-side failure rather than as fresh learner debt | using one consumed fee, one partial support spend, or one failed partner invoice to erase the whole reopened protection package |

### The archive's revival anchors

A truthful `RB1` / `RB2` / `RB3` revival now usually needs five anchors.

1. **seniority anchor** — use the learner's earlier continuity claim date, protected cohort date, or same protected scarcity window as the reopening floor rather than the date on which the failed destination later collapsed.
2. **equivalent-band anchor** — where the exact old slot or route family no longer exists, the system owes the nearest truthful equivalent protection, not a decorative statement that the exact old promise was impossible.
3. **prospective-cost anchor** — new route-failure costs reopen no-fault coverage prospectively; earlier absorbed waivers, credits, and write-offs stay absorbed.
4. **nonvalue-offset anchor** — failed-route charges or support spend that did not deliver enduring usable value for reasonable completion should be offset, waived, refunded, or credited before new learner-borne charges are even considered.
5. **packet-survival anchor** — records, proof, accessibility/support facts, and accountability ownership survive the failed route unless a genuinely different lawful route requirement says otherwise.

### When exact reinstatement is not required but equivalent protection is

The archive now allows `RB2` because literal reinstatement is sometimes impossible or misleading. A reserved practicum host may already have been reassigned after the host withdrew; a teach-out destination may no longer exist in that form; a shared-slot pool may have converted into a different live route family. In those cases the archive does **not** require fiction. It requires an equivalent protection rebuild.

A truthful `RB2` substitute usually means at least one of the following:

- same protected cohort date or queue seniority in the rebuilt route search;
- same-or-better scarcity band or stronger-floor treatment than the learner had already earned;
- immediate placement into the next protected intake / cycle rather than ordinary future-cycle competition;
- a shadow-capacity or reserved-review position that substitutes for the vanished exact slot; or
- an offset / waiver / support package that neutralizes the extra burden created because the old destination cannot literally be recreated.

### What counts as real value and what counts only as failed-route residue

The archive now keeps the value question narrow. A failed route may have generated real educational use, real support value, or real legal/financial states, but not every invoice, onboarding step, or internal transfer counts as value the learner must now absorb.

Treat the failed-route residue as still system-borne where the spend or charge was:

- preparatory only and did not become usable learning, access, approval, or support value for reasonable completion;
- tied to a destination, host, or provider that then failed before the learner obtained the protected benefit it was meant to secure;
- duplicated only because the first destination later failed and the learner must now be rebuilt elsewhere; or
- no longer lawfully or practically portable into the reopened route.

Treat the value as already truthfully consumed only where the learner actually received route-specific instruction, support, attendance opportunity, approved host preparation, or legally operative aid/charge treatment that remains educationally or financially real even after reopen. Even then, the archive forbids retroactive burden shift: real consumption may narrow duplication, but it does not erase seniority, packet survival, or the new no-fault coverage owed for the reopened failure.

### Failed-route residue escalation bands that now travel (`ER0-ER3`)

Revived continuity is not always the whole remedy. Sometimes `RB1-RB3` plus offsets and waivers really are enough: the learner still has a reasonable opportunity to complete on the protected terms, the failed-route residue can be neutralized before any new learner-borne charging begins, and no live debt-relief or failure-notice right is being suppressed. But once those conditions fail, the archive now requires visible escalation.

| Band | When it is truthful | What it means | Minimum treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ER0 offset-contained` | the rebuilt route still offers a reasonable opportunity to complete on the protected terms, and failed-route residue can be fully neutralized through waiver, credit, offset, or carryover without leaving nonportable loss or live debt states behind | the failure is real, but it can still be fully absorbed inside reopened continuity | offset, waive, credit, or carry failed-route residue before any new learner-borne charging; do not imply that accepting the offset waives later escalation if new facts emerge |
| `ER1 refund or compensation required` | continuity can still be rebuilt, but offset / waiver alone cannot neutralize actual out-of-pocket loss, lost bursary or maintenance support, duplicated travel / relocation / living burden, or nonportable spend caused by the failed route | the learner needs money back or equivalent make-whole treatment, not just a book entry against later charges | provide refund, compensation, honoured-bursary replacement, or direct make-whole treatment alongside reopened continuity work |
| `ER2 formal continuity-failure notice` | the failed route has called the protected completion opportunity itself into doubt for the learner or cohort even if alternatives may still be rebuilt | silent case handling is no longer enough because the learner needs explicit notice that the first continuity route itself materially failed | provide a visible failure notice with route status, complaint / review / regulator path, record-access path, and current replacement-route status; do not treat internal credits as a substitute for notice |
| `ER3 debt-relief or discharge guidance` | the failed route plus real debt / aid / tuition states means the learner may have live external relief rights because the route no longer gave a reasonable opportunity to complete on the protected terms | some remedy now lies partly outside the continuity owner, so internal offset alone is untruthful | preserve all internal offset / refund / compensation duties **and** give timely debt-relief / discharge guidance plus the evidence packet needed to use it |

### The five failure facts that force the residue question open (`VF1-VF5`)

| Fact | What it means | Usual escalation direction | Why offset alone is no longer enough |
|---|---|---|---|
| `VF1 preparatory nonvalue residue` | the learner paid, traveled, prepared, or onboarded for a route benefit that never matured into durable usable value for reasonable completion | often `ER1`, sometimes `ER2` | a later replacement route cannot truthfully treat purely failed preparation as already-consumed learner value |
| `VF2 nonportable partial value` | some instruction, support, host preparation, or approval work happened, but material portions cannot now travel into the rebuilt route | often `ER1`, often `ER2` in practicum / teach-out settings | the learner cannot be left carrying duplicated spend or time loss merely because the first route delivered partial but unusable fragments |
| `VF3 protected-floor break` | after reopen, the learner still faces extra learner-borne travel, living, maintenance, timing, or duplicated participation cost that the protected floor was supposed to absorb | often `ER1`, sometimes `ER2` | offset against future tuition alone does not restore the protected terms if the actual loss is off-ledger or already spent |
| `VF4 reasonable-opportunity collapse` | the assigned route, teach-out, host, or provider no longer leaves a reasonable opportunity to complete on the protected terms | at least `ER2`, often `ER3` | once the continuity destination itself materially fails, the learner needs explicit failure notice and not just quiet internal rerouting |
| `VF5 live legal debt state` | actual loan, aid-return, tuition, fee, or other legally sticky debt treatment survives the route failure and cannot be fully neutralized internally | `ER3` | the learner may need formal debt-relief or discharge guidance because internal credits do not exhaust the live external rights or obligations |

### What lets residue stay inside `ER0`

A truthful `ER0` case now usually needs **all** of the following:

1. **reasonable-opportunity survival** — after reopen and revival, the learner still has a real path to complete on the protected terms rather than a thinner, slower, or materially dearer imitation.
2. **full residue neutralization** — failed-route residue is fully offset, waived, credited, or ported before any new learner-borne charging begins.
3. **no hidden off-ledger loss** — travel, maintenance, housing, childcare, living-support, or bursary loss created by the failed route is either neutralized or genuinely absent.
4. **no live external-rights suppression** — the case does not involve a live failure / complaint / regulator / discharge route that the learner needs to know about in order to preserve rights.

### The four residue evasions the archive now rejects

1. **offset-instead-of-refund failure** — the system offers a future credit even though the real loss was already paid in cash, time, travel, or maintenance.
2. **credit-means-keep-quiet failure** — an internal offset is used to avoid telling the learner that the first continuity route itself materially failed.
3. **reroute-means-no-compensation failure** — a replacement route is offered as if that alone eliminates lost bursaries, duplicated costs, or failed-route nonvalue.
4. **internal-fix-means-no-external-rights failure** — the system acts as if accepting an internal refund or waiver extinguishes live complaint, regulator, or debt-relief routes.

### The four revival failures the archive now rejects

1. **back-of-line reopen failure** — a learner whose destination failed is restarted as if they were a brand-new claimant.
2. **exact-or-nothing failure** — the system says the old slot cannot literally return and therefore offers no equivalent protected rebuild.
3. **double-charge restart failure** — failed-route residue is billed first and only then is replacement continuity discussed.
4. **consumed-once-erases-all failure** — one real consumed component is used to wipe out unrelated revived priority, scarcity, packet, or waiver protections.

### Sector-and-posture starter defaults for revival after reopen

| Route type | Ordinary truthful revival shape | Where `RB2` is especially important | What usually must survive even after real consumption |
|---|---|---|---|
| Higher-ed transfer / teach-out | often `RB1` for one broken component, but `RB3` once the receiving route no longer offers a reasonable opportunity to complete | where the first teach-out partner or intake cannot literally be recreated and the learner instead needs same-cohort transfer seniority, same-or-better scarcity treatment, or offset-before-new-charge handling | accepted-credit / proof carryover, record transfer, earlier waivers, and no new learner-borne escape cost from the failed destination |
| Workforce, adult-learning, and library-routed continuity | often `RB1`, because support or provider failure may reopen only the participation-enabling component; use `RB3` once the route family itself collapses | where local intake, supportive-service inventory, or provider availability has changed and the learner needs equivalent-band routing rather than an exact old appointment slot | referral / support packet survival, priority treatment for the rebuilt placement window, and absorption of new participation-enabling costs caused by the failed route |
| Professional / practicum routes | often quicker `RB2` / `RB3`, because a precise host or site may vanish even when equivalent completion options still exist elsewhere | where exact host reinstatement is impossible but equivalent protected review, shadow slot, or next-cycle priority can still be rebuilt | approval records, preparedness evidence, accountability ownership, and no retro-billing for preparatory work tied to the failed placement |

### Minimum escalation packet that now travels (`NS1-NS8`)

Once `ER1-ER3` applies, the archive now says the learner must receive one small packet that makes the remedy surface truthful without forcing every local complaint manual, regulator memo, or debt-workflow detail into portable canon. The packet is not the whole local process. It is the minimum learner-facing shell that keeps one offered remedy from silently swallowing the others.

| Field | What must be told to the learner | Why it now travels |
|---|---|---|
| `NS1 escalation state` | which escalation band currently applies (`ER1`, `ER2`, `ER3`, alone or together) and whether internal continuity rebuild is still live | the learner has to know whether the system is treating the case as make-whole, formal route failure, external-rights guidance, or some combination |
| `NS2 failed-route status` | the current route status in plain language, including the material failure facts in play (`VF1-VF5`) and whether the failure is learner-specific or cohort-wide | quiet local phrasing like “adjustment” or “transition issue” should not hide that the first continuity route itself failed |
| `NS3 remedy lanes now open` | which lanes are open right now: offset / waiver, refund, compensation, complaint / review, regulator route, record-access route, debt-relief or discharge guidance | a learner cannot make an informed next step if only the owner’s preferred lane is shown |
| `NS4 current money and support handling` | what charges are paused, waived, refunded, offset, disputed, or already legally live; what bursary, maintenance, travel, childcare, or support replacement is being handled separately | out-of-pocket and off-ledger losses are often the real harm and must not disappear behind a tuition ledger note |
| `NS5 clocks and deadlines` | the next meaningful dates for internal review, refund / compensation action, complaint or regulator escalation, external application windows, and whether any clock is paused or preserved | the learner needs live timing, not just a statement that “rights may exist” |
| `NS6 named owners` | the continuity owner, money / remedy owner, and external-rights / evidence owner, with contact path | “contact us” is too weak once the case has moved beyond ordinary offset handling |
| `NS7 evidence and records path` | where the learner can obtain the core evidence packet, academic record evidence, charge / aid ledger evidence, and prior notices or route assignments | rights that depend on evidence are not real if the learner cannot identify how to get it |
| `NS8 non-waiver statement` | a clear statement that accepting one internal remedy does not by itself waive still-live complaint, regulator, or debt-relief routes, subject only to later reconciliation against duplicate monetary recovery | the first offered internal fix must not masquerade as the only lawful path |

### Minimum evidence packet fields that now travel (`EV1-EV6`)

The archive now keeps the evidence packet thin too. The learner does not need every internal note. But they do need enough evidence to understand the route failure, use the internal remedy, and preserve external rights.

| Field | What it contains | Why it is portable |
|---|---|---|
| `EV1 chronology` | the key dates for route offer, activation, first assignment, later deterioration, reopen, revival, escalation, and any replacement-route offer | chronology is the backbone for complaints, regulator review, and discharge or refund narratives |
| `EV2 route and scope facts` | the affected program / route, original destination family or named destination, accepted-credit / recognition position, and the current replacement-route status | the learner needs one consistent statement of what was promised, what failed, and what remains on offer |
| `EV3 charge and aid facts` | actual charges, waivers, refunds, offsets, disbursement status, returned-aid or live-debt facts, and any amounts still under review | real disbursement and charge states often determine whether the case is merely internal or partly external-rights relevant |
| `EV4 support and burden facts` | the material support or burden items tied to the failed route: bursary / maintenance loss, travel, relocation, childcare, books, equipment, application fees, or host-specific spend | many continuity harms live off-ledger, so the packet must make those losses legible |
| `EV5 record and attainment facts` | how the learner obtains transcripts, progress, proof, placement or host-preparation evidence, and any formal recognition already earned | record access and attainment evidence should not depend on a separate rediscovery process |
| `EV6 notices and remedy interactions` | the notices already issued, any internal remedy already offered or accepted, and any known third-party, regulator, or discharge-relevant interaction | later review should not depend on the learner reconstructing which notices or remedies were already put in play |

### Owner split that now travels (`OW1-OW3`)

One case may still be locally coordinated by one team, but the archive now says the packet must distinguish three owner roles:

| Owner tag | Default job |
|---|---|
| `OW1 continuity owner` | owns the live route status, replacement-route work, and continuity-floor promises |
| `OW2 money owner` | owns offset, waiver, refund, compensation, bursary replacement, and other make-whole treatment |
| `OW3 external-rights / evidence owner` | owns the evidence packet, record-access path, complaint / regulator routing, and debt-relief / discharge guidance handoff |

A single office may hold more than one owner tag, but the packet may not leave those roles blurred or unnamed once `ER1-ER3` applies.

### Timing and refresh defaults that now travel (`TM1-TM3`)

1. `TM1 immediate notice floor` — issue the escalation packet when the owner truthfully reaches `ER1`, `ER2`, or `ER3`; do not wait for the money figure, replacement route, or regulator conversation to be perfectly final before telling the learner that stronger remedy lanes are live.
2. `TM2 material-change refresh` — reissue or refresh the packet whenever route status, remedy status, money status, or external-rights relevance changes in a learner-decision material way.
3. `TM3 no-deadline-ambush floor` — the archive now rejects any local process that lets an internal response window or external-rights clock silently run while the learner still lacks the basic packet needed to understand the failure, the evidence, and the open lanes.

### Non-waiver and duplicate-recovery defaults that now travel (`NW1-NW4`)

1. `NW1 no-waiver-by-acceptance` — taking an offset, waiver, temporary refund, or replacement route does not by itself waive complaint, regulator, or debt-relief / discharge routes that are still live.
2. `NW2 no-waiver-by-routine-form` — route-acceptance, transfer, refund, reimbursement, or record-request forms may not hide release language that purports to settle other remedy lanes by default.
3. `NW3 separate-settlement rule` — if a case ever truthfully reaches a real release or settlement, that act must be separate, explicit about which claims it covers, and not bundled into the basic continuity packet.
4. `NW4 duplicate-recovery reconciliation only` — overlapping monetary recovery may later be reconciled so the learner is not paid twice for the same loss, but that reconciliation does not erase disclosure, complaint, regulator, or debt-guidance duties.

### The four packet evasions the archive now rejects

1. **notice-after-settlement failure** — the learner is given money or a reroute first and only later discovers that a formal failure, complaint, or discharge lane was live all along.
2. **owner-blur failure** — the continuity office, finance office, and evidence / rights office each assume another one is telling the learner what matters.
3. **evidence-on-request-only failure** — the learner is told that rights exist but cannot identify the dates, ledgers, records, or notices needed to use them.
4. **routine-form waiver failure** — a standard transfer, refund, or route-acceptance form is used to imply closure of claims that were never separately surfaced or explained.

### When the escalation packet may stay cohort-wide (`CG0-CG3`)

The archive now closes the next seam after `NS1-NS8`, `EV1-EV6`, `OW1-OW3`, `TM1-TM3`, and `NW1-NW4`. Not every escalated failure needs a separately drafted notice from the first minute. Some facts are genuinely common across a cohort. Others are only true for a subgroup, and some become learner-specific the moment route status, money status, support loss, or external-rights relevance stops matching.

| Code | State | What may still travel together | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `CG0 learner-only packet` | there is no truthful common shell because the first meaningful facts already differ learner by learner | only the broad event announcement may be shared; the actual escalation packet starts at learner level | do not pretend a cohort packet exists when route, money, or rights status is materially individualized from the start |
| `CG1 cohort-common core` | the event, affected scope, common owner set, common review points, and common remedy lanes are materially the same across the affected cohort | one shared packet may carry the common core | the packet must stay explicitly limited to facts that are true for all affected learners, not imply identical destination, amount, or eligibility outcomes |
| `CG2 subgroup supplement` | one or more meaningful subgroups share additional facts not true of the whole cohort | keep one cohort core and add subgroup-specific supplements | use subgroup layers for things like the same destination family, same regulatory track, same licensure / practicum posture, same aid-status cohort, or the same potentially eligible discharge / refund screen |
| `CG3 learner-specific supplement` | route, money, support, timing, or external-rights facts now differ in a learner-decision material way | issue an individual supplement before asking the learner to choose, accept, waive, transfer, or let a meaningful clock run | the archive now rejects any packet that stays only cohort-wide after the facts have become materially individualized |

### Which escalation fields may stay common and which must individualize (`IS1-IS5`)

A cohort core is allowed only while it carries genuinely shared facts. The archive now hardens five supplement triggers.

| Code | What has diverged | What must now individualize |
|---|---|---|
| `IS1 route-status divergence` | `AR1-AR3`, accepted-credit treatment, remaining-work position, approval / host status, or route family no longer matches across learners | the learner's route status, route options, recognition position, and the route-specific parts of `EV2` / `EV5` |
| `IS2 money-state divergence` | actual charges, waivers, offsets, refunds, disbursements, credit balances, or disputed amounts no longer match across learners | the money-handling parts of `NS4`, `NS5`, and `EV3` |
| `IS3 support-loss divergence` | travel, childcare, relocation, accessibility support, bursary replacement, books / equipment, or other participation-enabling support losses differ materially | the support / burden parts of `NS4` and `EV4` |
| `IS4 clock or owner divergence` | the next meaningful deadline, pause, preservation rule, review point, or accountable office no longer matches across learners | the learner-specific parts of `NS5`, `NS6`, and any live review / appeal timing |
| `IS5 external-rights divergence` | complaint posture, regulator route, refund / compensation relevance, third-party recovery relevance, discharge relevance, or already accepted remedy status no longer matches | the rights-path parts of `NS3`, `NS8`, and `EV6` |

The archive's working test is now explicit: **a cohort packet may tell learners what happened; it may not force them to infer whether the same thing happened _to them_.**

### Refresh duties after the first escalation packet (`RF1-RF5`)

The first packet is no longer treated as a one-and-done disclosure. Once `ER1-ER3` is live, the archive now requires refresh when the packet's practical meaning changes.

| Code | What changed | Minimum refresh consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `RF1 route refresh` | route status moves between `AR1-AR3`, a route family narrows, a destination becomes named or falls away, or reopen / revival changes the learner's continuity posture | refresh the route, recognition, and next-step fields immediately for the affected learners or subgroup |
| `RF2 money refresh` | an estimate becomes an actual charge or disbursement, a waiver / offset / refund is approved or denied, a credit balance becomes payable, or a disputed amount is resolved materially | refresh the money-handling fields and any learner deadlines tied to them |
| `RF3 rights refresh` | complaint, regulator, refund / compensation, third-party recovery, or discharge relevance newly opens, narrows, or closes | refresh the open-lane statement, evidence needs, and non-waiver statement for the affected learners |
| `RF4 owner-or-clock refresh` | the accountable office changes, a review point moves, a pause is lifted, or a deadline is newly triggered or preserved | refresh the owner and clock fields rather than leaving stale contact paths or dates in circulation |
| `RF5 evidence refresh` | a material chronology, ledger, academic-record, or prior-notice fact is corrected or newly available | refresh the evidence-path statement and make the corrected packet accessible without forcing the learner to reconstruct the old one manually |

### What may usually remain cohort-common

A truthful `CG1` cohort core will usually be enough for:

- the event description and affected scope (`NS1`);
- the common failure posture in plain language (`NS2`) so long as it does not overclaim identical route or money effects;
- the list of remedy lanes that are open in principle for the cohort (`NS3`);
- shared owner contacts (`NS6`) and shared evidence-access routes (`NS7`);
- the non-waiver floor (`NS8`);
- and the next cohort review point where `TM1-TM3` is still common.

### What may not stay cohort-only once the facts diverge

The archive now treats the following as presumptively supplement-level once `IS1-IS5` is present:

- specific route assignment, destination, recognition, approval, or host facts;
- actual charges, disbursements, offsets, refunds, and credit-balance treatment;
- individualized support or burden losses;
- learner-specific deadlines, pauses, or preserved windows;
- and any statement that a learner is, or is not, personally inside a still-live complaint, regulator, compensation, third-party, or discharge lane.

### The four packet-splitting evasions the archive now rejects

1. **cohort-overclaim failure** — a common notice is written as if route, money, or rights outcomes are identical when they are not.
2. **subgroup-burial failure** — the owner knows a subgroup is differently situated but leaves that difference hidden inside generic cohort language.
3. **election-before-supplement failure** — a learner is asked to transfer, accept money, or let a deadline run before receiving the individualized facts that make the choice intelligible.
4. **stale-refresh failure** — a packet stays in circulation after a route, money, owner, or rights change has made the old notice materially misleading.

### How unresolved escalation fields must now be marked (`UF0-UF4`)

The archive now closes the next seam after `CG0-CG3`, `IS1-IS5`, and `RF1-RF5`. Once a packet has the right scope and refresh pattern, it still needs one more truthfulness layer: unresolved route, money, and external-rights fields may not read like quietly final answers. The archive now hardens five portable field-state tags.

| Code | State | What the packet may truthfully say | What it may not imply |
|---|---|---|---|
| `UF0 fixed for current packet` | the field is presently settled enough to state as the current answer | the packet may present the current route / money / rights fact in ordinary language | that the field can never change again; ordinary `RF1-RF5` refresh may still later alter it |
| `UF1 bounded estimate` | the owner can give a best current amount, range, destination family, or likely posture from already-known facts | the packet may show the estimate only if it is clearly labeled as estimated and bounded | that the estimate is already an actual charge, disbursement, refund, host approval, or final rights outcome |
| `UF2 pending external confirmation` | the answer depends on another office, host, partner, regulator, lender, or formal posting event that has not yet occurred | the packet may say what is being awaited and who owns the next step | that the field is denied, unavailable, or final merely because outside confirmation has not yet arrived |
| `UF3 disputed / under challenge` | the current value or posture is actively contested by the learner, owner, or third party | the packet may state the owner's present position only if it also states that the field is disputed | that the owner-preferred value is settled while the challenge is still live |
| `UF4 unknown / not yet knowable` | there is not yet a truthful estimate, route answer, or rights answer | the packet may say that the field is currently unknown and name the next review point | that silence or omission means zero, none, denied, or not relevant |

### The minimum marker shell for unresolved fields (`MK1-MK6`)

Unresolved fields are now allowed to travel only with a small context shell.

| Code | What must travel with the unresolved field | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `MK1 state tag + last-confirmed date` | the field must carry `UF0-UF4` plus the date the current status was last truthfully checked or posted | a learner should not have to guess whether a value is fixed, estimated, pending, disputed, or genuinely unknown |
| `MK2 last fixed anchor` | the packet must state the last thing that is actually settled: last accepted-credit position, last posted charge / refund / disbursement state, last named route family, or last confirmed rights posture | provisional movement should sit on top of a real anchor rather than replacing it with haze |
| `MK3 present owner / dependency` | the packet must name who currently owns movement of the field or what outside dependency is awaited | unresolved fields may not look ownerless once `ER1-ER3` is live |
| `MK4 next event / review point` | the packet must give the next meaningful check, posting, response, decision point, or refresh moment | unresolved does not mean open-ended silence |
| `MK5 interim protection while unresolved` | the packet must state the strongest truthful interim floor: hold, no-invoice, no-waiver, preserved priority, provisional destination-family shell, or rights-preservation rule | the learner should be able to see what is still being protected while the answer is not final |
| `MK6 learner-action posture` | the packet must say whether the learner is only being informed, may browse or rank options provisionally, may hold a place reversibly, or should wait for refresh before a meaningful election | provisional information may support navigation, but it may not masquerade as a final instruction |

### The four false-finality moves the archive now rejects (`GU1-GU4`)

1. `GU1 no-finality-by-format` — a field labeled `UF1-UF4` may not be bolded, row-titled, or narrated as if it were already final.
2. `GU2 estimate-is-not-ledger` — estimated aid, memo entries, projected charges, likely refunds, and likely route costs may not be presented as already posted ledger reality.
3. `GU3 pending-is-not-denial` — a pending host approval, external review, regulator step, or complaint response may not be flattened into a denial or non-eligibility statement just because the answer is not yet back.
4. `GU4 dispute-is-not-resolution` — once a field is actively disputed, the packet may not quietly suppress the dispute behind the current owner-preferred value or behind silence.

### The five learner-action postures the archive now distinguishes (`PA0-PA4`)

The archive now closes the next seam after `UF0-UF4`, `MK1-MK6`, and `GU1-GU4`. Once a packet truthfully marks what is fixed, estimated, pending, disputed, or unknown, it still needs one more operating rule: whether the learner may do anything _with_ that provisional field yet. The archive now hardens five action postures.

A field is **material to action** when it could still change route equivalence, credit / approval portability, learner-borne charge or debt, support availability, or the existence / timing of a complaint, regulator, refund, compensation, or discharge lane.

| Code | What the learner may do | When this is the strongest truthful posture | What the archive rejects |
|---|---|---|---|
| `PA0 informed-but-wait` | read the current packet, ask questions, and gather documents, but not make a meaningful election yet | a material field is still `UF2-UF4`, or a `UF1` estimate could still worsen route, money, or rights posture in a nontrivial way | presenting a live deadline, transfer choice, waiver, or commitment as if the packet were already decision-ready |
| `PA1 browse / compare` | browse destinations, compare likely burdens, rank interests, and request clarification without surrendering any current protection | the packet can show enough bounded shape for orientation, but not enough settled detail for commitment | treating curiosity, browsing, or ranking as a commitment or as consent to lose another lane |
| `PA2 reversible hold / provisional preference` | place a temporary hold, express a nonbinding preference, or begin administrative preparation that can be unwound without loss | the current field mix can support queue preservation or preparatory routing, but not a binding destination / money / rights election | letting expiry of the hold destroy priority, payer protection, or unresolved-rights access beyond what the packet already disclosed |
| `PA3 protected provisional uptake` | accept a temporary default, interim placement, or provisional money / support step that may later be corrected without learner loss | the unresolved remainder is now bounded enough that the learner can safely move forward because the downside is capped, unwind rights are clear, and unresolved components remain protected | treating a provisional uptake as if it were already the learner's final route, final ledger, or final rights position |
| `PA4 final election / ordinary running` | make a binding destination, transfer, acceptance, settlement, or other consequential election under ordinary clock rules | every field material to that election is now effectively `UF0` for the current packet, with only immaterial residue still unresolved | calling a choice final while material route / money / rights ambiguity is still live |

### How the unresolved field states now map to learner action

The archive's current starter map is intentionally conservative:

- `UF0` may support any `PA0-PA4` posture because the field is presently fixed enough for the current packet.
- `UF1` usually supports `PA1` or `PA2`, and may support `PA3` only when the estimate is bounded, the learner-facing downside is capped, and any later correction is no-loss.
- `UF2` usually supports `PA1` or `PA2`; it does **not** support `PA4` where the pending confirmation could still change route equivalence, actual charge / disbursement, host approval, transferability, or rights relevance.
- `UF3` may support `PA1` or `PA2` to preserve options, but it does **not** support waiver, settlement, or adverse deadline loss by treating the owner-preferred position as already final.
- `UF4` ordinarily supports only `PA0` or `PA1`; the packet may orient the learner, but not ask for a meaningful election while the material field is still genuinely unknown.

### The five pause / restart rules the archive now distinguishes (`PC0-PC4`)

The archive now answers the narrow clock question too. Once unresolved fields are present, clocks no longer run in one undifferentiated way.

| Code | Rule | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `PC0 ordinary running` | a choice, transfer, acceptance, or rights clock may run normally only when every field material to that action is already settled enough for `PA4` | unresolved residue that is immaterial to the action does not itself freeze the world |
| `PC1 no meaningful-election clock on material ambiguity` | while a material route / money / rights field is still too unresolved for `PA4`, the packet may not let a binding election, transfer, or acceptance deadline quietly expire | the learner may browse or hold provisionally, but not lose the protected floor by guessing |
| `PC2 reversible-hold clocks may run only if expiry is harmless` | short windows for ranking, reservation, or provisional holds may run under `PA1-PA2` only when expiry merely releases the reversible hold and does not waive rights, drop priority outside the disclosed rule, or convert silence into commitment | not every countdown is forbidden, but ambiguous countdowns may not carry hidden loss |
| `PC3 disputed-or-unresolved-rights clocks pause` | complaint, regulator, refund / compensation, discharge, or equivalent external-rights clocks may not run adversely while the field that determines their relevance is still `UF2-UF4` or actively `UF3` disputed | the owner may not use uncertainty itself as the reason a learner's external lane quietly expires |
| `PC4 refreshed-restart only` | once material ambiguity narrows enough to permit a stronger action posture, any restarted clock must be stated in a refreshed packet with the new date, owner, and consequence | a stale ambiguous date may not survive refresh as if it had always been controlling |


### The four restart-notice minima the archive now distinguishes (`RN1-RN4`)

The archive now closes the next seam after `PA0-PA4` and `PC0-PC4`. Once material ambiguity narrows enough for a paused learner clock to run again, the packet still needs one more rule: what a fair restart notice minimally owes before the learner can be said to have a real chance to act. The archive now hardens four restart-notice minima.

| Code | Minimum restart-notice duty | Starter consequence | What the archive rejects |
|---|---|---|---|
| `RN1 changed-fact restart notice` | say what actually changed since the pause, including which material field narrowed and which stronger action posture is now truthful | a restarted clock may not appear out of nowhere; it must be tied to an identifiable packet change | restarting a deadline without telling the learner which ambiguity ended |
| `RN2 lane-specific restart notice` | name the exact lane now running again — route election, transfer, acceptance, settlement, refund / compensation, complaint / regulator, discharge, or equivalent | one generic “you now have time to act” message is not enough where materially different lanes exist | one blended notice that leaves the learner guessing which decision or rights lane is actually live |
| `RN3 date-owner-consequence restart notice` | state the new start date, the operative deadline or review anchor, the named owner, and the consequence of inaction | `PC4` becomes concrete: the restart date must be new, visible, and attributable | stale carry-forward dates or ownerless restart language |
| `RN4 accessible mirrored delivery` | deliver the restart notice through the same or a safer channel than the pause-era packet, with reasonable adjustments or accessible format where already needed | a newly running adverse clock must reach the learner in a form they can actually use | burying the restart in a harder-to-find channel, inaccessible format, or learner-irrelevant broadcast |

### The four grace bands the archive now distinguishes (`GR0-GR3`)

The archive still does not need one universal day count. It does need a small banded answer about when restarted clocks may run immediately, when they owe a nonzero buffer, and when owner-caused notice failure forces another pause.

| Code | Grace rule | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `GR0 harmless-hold release only` | no extra grace is owed only where the restarted timer merely releases a reversible hold under `PC2` and does not trigger route loss, money loss, waiver, or external-rights loss | a short countdown may remain acceptable if expiry is genuinely harmless |
| `GR1 anti-surprise grace floor` | any restarted adverse clock that can now change route, money, or rights posture must include a real nonzero buffer after refreshed notice | same-day or effectively immediate adverse expiry is not a fair restart |
| `GR2 supplement-or-rights grace` | where restart depends on a learner-specific supplement, a newly live external-rights lane, or a material shift in the available options, the packet owes a larger buffer than the bare `GR1` floor | the learner must get enough time to read the individualized packet, ask questions, and act on the newly clarified lane |
| `GR3 owner-fault extension` | if the restart notice is late, contradictory, inaccessible, or sent through a broken channel, the adverse clock pauses again and restarts only after corrected notice | owner-caused notice failure cannot be converted into learner lateness |

### The four reminder duties the archive now distinguishes (`RM0-RM3`)

The archive now adds one still smaller reminder rule. A fair restart is not just a new date; for materially adverse restarted clocks of nontrivial length, the learner should not have to remember a newly clarified deadline with no further cue.

| Code | Reminder duty | Starter consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `RM0 no reminder owed for harmless release` | a timer that only releases a harmless reversible hold under `GR0` does not itself require reminder treatment | not every countdown needs a reminder layer |
| `RM1 one pre-expiry reminder for adverse restarted clocks` | a restarted route / money / rights clock that can expire adversely should ordinarily receive at least one reminder before expiry | clarification should not become a single-message trap |
| `RM2 same-safe-channel reminder` | the reminder should use the same or a safer channel and preserve the same accessible path as the restart notice | reminder treatment should reinforce, not undermine, usable notice |
| `RM3 refresh-not-reminder on material change` | if the operative date, owner, consequence, or material facts change again, the archive requires a refreshed restart notice rather than a mere reminder | reminder language may not hide a second substantive packet change |

### The four restart-synchrony bands the archive now distinguishes (`SY0-SY3`)

The archive now closes one still narrower seam after `RN1-RN4`, `GR0-GR3`, and `RM0-RM3`. A fair restart notice is not yet enough by itself, because several lanes may become clearer at different times. The archive now adds one small synchrony rule for when route, money, and external-rights clocks may truthfully share one common restart date and when they must split instead.

| Code | Synchrony rule | Starter consequence | What the archive rejects |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SY0 split-by-lane default` | absent the conditions for `SY1`, each restarted lane keeps its own restart date and owner | convenience is not enough to synchronize clocks | one generic synchronized deadline simply because the packet is being resent |
| `SY1 common-anchor synchronized restart` | one shared restart date is truthful only where the same refreshed packet narrows the same material ambiguity for every listed lane, every listed lane is already decision-ready on common facts, and no listed lane awaits a later owner act, posting, or external trigger | a common restart date may exist, but only when there is one real common anchor | pretending route, money, and rights are synchronized when one of them is still only estimated, pending, or provisional |
| `SY2 owner-or-event split restart` | if a lane depends on a different owner or a later operative event — host acceptance, actual charge posting, actual aid disbursement, final internal decision / Completion of Procedures Letter, regulator filing acceptance, discharge notice, or equivalent — that lane restarts on that later event | one packet may announce several lanes, but it may not backdate slower lanes to the earliest common notice | using a route-ready date as if it also started a later money or external-rights clock |
| `SY3 learner-specific supplement split` | if a lane becomes decision-ready only after a learner-specific supplement, individualized route set, or individualized money / rights fact, the restart date for that lane begins when that supplement reaches that learner | cohort-core notice may coexist with individualized restart dates, but it may not overwrite them | letting a cohort date silently govern a learner-specific deadline that became real only after later individualized notice |

### The five false-synchrony failures the archive now rejects

1. **cohort-date laundering failure** — one shared restart date is reused even though different lanes became decision-ready on different events.
2. **estimate-to-ledger synchronization failure** — an estimated or pending money state is treated as if it had restarted the same day as an actual posted or disbursed money lane.
3. **internal-to-external shortcut failure** — an internal route or settlement update is treated as if it also started a complaint, regulator, or discharge lane that really depends on a later final or external trigger.
4. **supplement-backdating failure** — a learner-specific supplement arrives later, but the deadline is still measured from the earlier cohort packet.
5. **owner-blur synchronization failure** — multiple owners with different control over route, money, or rights events are collapsed into one ownerless common deadline.

### The four unfair-restart failures the archive now rejects

1. **restart-without-delta failure** — a paused clock restarts, but the learner is never told which ambiguity actually narrowed or why the lane is now decision-ready.
2. **same-day surprise expiry failure** — refreshed notice arrives only when the meaningful time to act has already effectively vanished.
3. **reminder-in-place-of-refresh failure** — a later material change is smuggled through as a reminder instead of issuing a new restart notice with a new operative date.
4. **channel-mismatch failure** — a clock that previously paused for ambiguity quietly restarts in a harder-to-find, inaccessible, or otherwise weaker delivery path.


### The four ambiguous-action failures the archive now rejects

1. **estimate-to-election failure** — a bounded estimate is used as if it were already strong enough to force final route or money choice.
2. **pending-to-commitment failure** — a learner is pushed into transfer, acceptance, or settlement while a pending host / partner / regulator / posting event could still materially change the outcome.
3. **dispute-to-waiver failure** — the packet lets a waiver, limitation, or external-rights clock run by pretending the owner-preferred position is already settled while a live dispute remains.
4. **stale-date carry-forward failure** — after refresh narrows or changes the ambiguity, the old ambiguous deadline is still treated as if it had been fair and controlling all along.

### What counted as a real archive gain after that

The archive already knew what the minimum escalation packet had to contain. But it still lacked the next narrower operating answer:

- when one cohort-wide packet is actually truthful;
- when subgroup layers are enough and when the case must split into learner-specific supplements;
- which packet fields become individualized first as route, money, support, timing, or rights facts diverge;
- which later changes require refresh instead of leaving the first packet to go stale;
- how unresolved route, money, and external-rights fields must be marked while the packet is still in motion;
- what minimal owner / anchor / review / interim-protection context must travel with those unresolved fields;
- how the packet avoids false finality while still giving the learner something usable before every value is settled;
- which unresolved field states still permit browsing, ranking, or reversible holds;
- when ambiguity is still too material for binding transfer, acceptance, settlement, or waiver;
- and how clocks may pause, run harmlessly, or restart only after refreshed notice once ambiguity narrows;
- what a restarted clock must actually say once the packet becomes decision-ready again;
- when restart owes a bare nonzero buffer, when it owes a larger individualized grace window, and when owner-caused notice failure forces another pause;
- when one reminder is enough versus when a later packet change requires a fresh restart notice instead;
- and when several restarted lanes may still truthfully share one common anchor versus when they must split by lane, owner, later event, or learner-specific supplement.

This document now closes that seam with one compact action-and-restart grammar layered on top of the existing marking rule:

- five learner-action postures (`PA0-PA4`),
- five pause / restart rules (`PC0-PC4`),
- four restart-notice minima (`RN1-RN4`),
- four grace bands (`GR0-GR3`),
- four reminder duties (`RM0-RM3`),
- and four restart-synchrony bands (`SY0-SY3`).

The display consequences of that synchrony rule now live in [`split-lane-packet-display-and-dependency-rules.md`](split-lane-packet-display-and-dependency-rules.md).
