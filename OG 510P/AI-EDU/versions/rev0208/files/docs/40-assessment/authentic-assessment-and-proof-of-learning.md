# Authentic assessment and proof of learning

This document states the archive's strongest current substantive claim.

## Claim

In an environment where students have routine access to capable AI, many legacy assignments stop functioning as reliable stand-alone evidence of learning.

This does **not** mean writing is dead, projects are dead, or homework is dead. It means the institution must redesign what counts as proof.

## Why this matters

RAND reports that student and teacher AI use rose quickly while school policies and student training lagged. Many students also worry about false cheating accusations. A system that tries to preserve old assessment logic with only policing will become both less fair and less educational. See `B06`.

RAND's 2026 youth update sharpens the demand signal further: homework use keeps rising, and many students themselves think routine AI use can harm critical thinking. See `B17`.

OECD 2026 sharpens the learning point: better task performance with GenAI can coexist with weaker learning if cognitive effort is outsourced. See `B11`.

## Current archive posture

### 1. Separate practice from certification

Students should often be allowed to use AI during practice, drafting, and exploration. But certification should rely more heavily on richer evidence.

### 2. Use bundles, not single signals

Better proof comes from combinations of:

- draft history,
- oral explanation,
- in-class transfer,
- reflection on choices,
- teacher questioning,
- and selective live performance.

The archive's new default bundle patterns are in [`proof-of-learning-bundles.md`](proof-of-learning-bundles.md). Those bundles increasingly make sense when attached to recognizable subject routines rather than only to general academic-integrity rules, they now sit inside an explicit `P0-P3` intensity grammar plus `PX` escalation state so institutions do not answer the AI problem by demanding defense-heavy evidence everywhere, and they now carry a small layer of inherited hotter/cooler family defaults so programme maps do not stop at generic burden language. See [`proof-of-learning-bundles.md`](proof-of-learning-bundles.md), [`bundle-intensity-bands-and-sampling-ceilings.md`](bundle-intensity-bands-and-sampling-ceilings.md), [`subject-family-proof-intensity-defaults.md`](subject-family-proof-intensity-defaults.md), and [`../30-operations/subject-embedded-exemplar-kernel.md`](../30-operations/subject-embedded-exemplar-kernel.md).

### 3. Prefer explanation and transfer over polished surface quality

If a student can explain, adapt, critique, and extend an idea across contexts, learning is more plausible than if they submit only a polished artifact.

### 4. Normalize bounded AI use declarations

Instead of pretending AI use is absent, institutions should define what forms of help are allowed, what must be disclosed, and where the learner must still carry the cognitive load. The archive now prefers a small course-level grammar with lighter disclosure and stronger proof surfaces over universal provenance demands, and it distinguishes pedagogical disclosure from protected accessibility/accommodation routing when disclosure would reveal sensitive support information without improving the learning claim. See [`../30-operations/course-level-ai-use-grammar.md`](../30-operations/course-level-ai-use-grammar.md) and [`../20-governance/accommodation-aware-disclosure-and-accessibility.md`](../20-governance/accommodation-aware-disclosure-and-accessibility.md).

### 5. Use teacher judgment strategically, not total surveillance

The answer is not continuous monitoring of every keystroke. It is more selective, intelligent teacher sampling: short viva-style checks, targeted follow-up questions, and live mini-performances where needed. The archive is also increasingly skeptical of detector-led integrity posture. See `B26`.

### 6. Reserve the richest bundles for major outcomes

The archive does **not** recommend turning every worksheet, discussion post, or homework task into a defense-heavy ritual. Richer proof bundles should concentrate on major competencies, threshold concepts, gateway courses, and credential-bearing moments, with most everyday work staying in `P0` or `P1`, a small number of `P2` events carrying the major load, and `P3` reserved for the strongest claims. See [`bundle-intensity-bands-and-sampling-ceilings.md`](bundle-intensity-bands-and-sampling-ceilings.md).

## Practical redesign moves

- convert some essays into essay-plus-defense;
- convert some problem sets into problem-set-plus-transfer task;
- convert some take-home projects into milestone-based portfolios;
- convert some reading responses into discussion plus low-stakes notes;
- convert some coding assignments into code plus walkthrough plus adaptation challenge.

## Institutional implication

Schools and universities that refuse assessment redesign will drift toward either performative bans or collapsing evidentiary standards. Neither is stable.
