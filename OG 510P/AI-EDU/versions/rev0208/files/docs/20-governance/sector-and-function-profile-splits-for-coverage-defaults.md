# Sector-and-function profile splits for coverage defaults

This document closes the archive's next implementation gap about **where the generic human-coverage ladder already starts too low if reused unchanged**.

The archive now has a small `HC0-HC4` ladder for official educational-AI services. What it still needed was a **starter profile layer** for contexts where the same nominal service becomes more reliance-bearing because of minors, live class windows, credit or aid deadlines, professional gatekeeping, or public-route timing.

The archive's current bet is:

> a generic coverage ladder is not enough; some educational-AI services should start from a stricter coverage floor because the service window, learner dependence, or rights stakes are already knowable in advance.

Current public signals point the same way. UNESCO's 2026 teacher framework treats AI readiness as structured professional capability rather than casual familiarity. The 2026 UNESCO-UNICEF-ITU Charter for Public Digital Learning Platforms treats educational platforms as governed public infrastructure serving teachers, learners, and families. OECD's 2026 work keeps human judgment, feedback, and responsibility at the centre of effective GenAI use. U.S. DOE's 2025 guidance explicitly allows tutoring, advising, and navigation uses, but still frames AI as support for educators and institutions rather than as an ownerless replacement service. SETDA's 2025 state trends report then adds the operational warning that AI priority is rising faster than durable funding and professional-learning capacity. IES's 2026 synthesis reinforces the same point: AI may help, but it works best when paired with strong human judgment, resources, and implementation support. See `B03`, `B99`, `B100`, `B101`, `B131`, `B132`.

## Relationship to the generic coverage ladder

This document does not replace [`../30-operations/human-coverage-bands-and-no-orphan-handoffs.md`](../30-operations/human-coverage-bands-and-no-orphan-handoffs.md).

It adds one thing only:

- a **starter profile layer** for places where the archive already knows the generic `HC0-HC4` floor should begin stricter.

The generic ladder still answers the first question:

- is there no live service promise,
- delayed owner-backed coverage,
- routine service coverage,
- consequence-aware coverage,
- or human-primary duty coverage?

This document answers the second question:

- in this sector-and-function context, does the archive already know the honest starting band is hotter than the generic service description suggests?

## The starter profile table

The archive keeps this table intentionally small. These are **starter inherited profiles**, not full staffing codes.

| Profile | Scope | Function families most affected | Default coverage adjustments | Never below | Why the split exists |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `CP-TF-RUNTIME` | officially provisioned teacher-side workflows whose failure can create same-cycle learner burden | planning-to-delivery chains, accessibility-material generation, class-ready translation, outward teacher messages sent as part of live course operation | teacher-private drafting may stay at the generic `HC0-HC1` floor; move to at least `HC2` once same-day class delivery or learner communication depends on the service; move to `HC3` when service failure would strand a live class, miss an access promise, or require same-day substitute action | `HC4` for final grading, discipline, formal accommodation determination, crisis/well-being ownership, or any accountable judgment merely drafted by AI | once a teacher-side workflow becomes runtime infrastructure, "staff can sort it out later" is no longer a truthful coverage story |
| `CP-K12-MINORS` | repeated or default-path learner support in primary/secondary settings where learners are minors | school-help chat, homework study support, tutoring wrappers, attendance or timetable help, multilingual access routing, family-facing school support surfaces | low-stakes FAQ may stay at `HC1`; repeated or default-path study help should usually start at `HC2`; tutoring, advising, access routing, or attendance/welfare support used during the school week should usually start at `HC3`, with a named same-day owner and a truthful family-/learner-facing substitute path | `HC4` for safeguarding, acute distress, discipline/exclusion movement, formal support determination, or any service that can trigger child-protection duty | minors compress the gap between "optional help" and real institutional reliance because family duty, school participation, and safeguarding sit close to the service surface |
| `CP-HE-CREDIT-WINDOW` | credit-bearing postsecondary services that can affect live registration, progression, aid, or transfer status | registration help, degree-progress interpretation, aid navigation, transfer/credit exploration, course-eligibility support, practicum-readiness administration | optional study help may inherit the generic floor; services that shape live add/drop, progression, aid, transfer, or readiness windows should usually start at `HC3` with a same-cycle manual path and a named next-action owner; they may cool back toward `HC2` outside live decision windows only where delay does not change status or opportunity | `HC4` for final academic-standing action, official accommodation decisions, final aid determinations, discipline, or other formally contestable judgments | adult status does not remove timing stakes; during live credit or aid windows, delayed fallback is often not an honest substitute |
| `CP-PRO-PRACTICE` | professional, clinical, practicum, apprenticeship, and readiness-sensitive training contexts | remediation support, placement-prep dialogue, supervised-practice tutoring, readiness prompts, support around clinical/safety requirements | tutoring, feedback, or advising that can affect practice readiness, placement movement, remediation intensity, or supervised responsibility should usually start at `HC3`; off-hours AI may hold only if the accountable daytime service, review owner, and substitute path are already published | `HC4` for fitness-to-practice, placement decisions, readiness sign-off, patient/client-safety ownership, or other professional gatekeeping acts | these contexts combine learning support with real readiness and safety consequences, so a delayed generic queue is often false protection |
| `CP-PUBLIC-ROUTE` | public adult-route, workforce, library, and community-learning services where timing can affect funded access or mandated participation | first-contact navigation, funded-seat guidance, benefits-adjacent route help, multilingual public intake, public referral support | optional navigation may sometimes remain at `HC2`; once the service touches funded-seat access, mandated programme movement, benefits-adjacent timing, public referral, or scarce-slot continuity, it should usually start at `HC3` with a published service window, queue-preserving substitute path, and no-deadline-loss rule; if that promise cannot be met, the service should cool back toward optional or human-primary handling rather than pretend | `HC4` for final public eligibility, exclusion from funded pathways, sanctions, crisis/well-being ownership, or other accountable public determinations | public-route services often look administratively light until a missed response window turns into lost access, missed benefits timing, or silent queue harm |

## Window and stakes modifiers the archive now treats as knowable in advance

The archive now treats four right-shift triggers as knowable **before** local failure evidence accumulates:

1. **same-cycle dependence** — the service can fail while a live class, assignment release, access support, or learner communication still needs a truthful substitute path that day;
2. **live timing or queue integrity** — the service sits inside a registration, aid, practicum, funded-seat, attendance, or public-route window where delay can itself change the outcome;
3. **minors, safeguarding, or family-duty proximity** — the service can spill quickly from ordinary support into welfare, safety, or family-facing obligation;
4. **rights-bearing or gatekeeping consequences** — the next handoff can touch formal accommodation, professional readiness, public eligibility, or another accountable determination.

When one of those is present, the archive prefers publishing a stricter starter profile up front rather than learning the lesson only after a missed deadline, stranded class, silent queue loss, or ownerless escalation.

## Cooling back is a governance action, not a failure to scale

This document adds one small retreat rule.

When the truthful coverage promise cannot be met, the service should **cool back visibly** rather than keep the same official posture with a quietly broken human route.

Ordinary cooling moves are:

- narrow the published service window;
- return the service to clearly optional/reference-only use;
- remove default-path or required-use expectations;
- or move the function back to explicit human-primary handling.

The archive prefers visible cooling to false `HC2-HC3` promises, because an overstated service window is itself a learner burden.

## What institutions should publish

Where a recurring service inherits one of these profiles, publish one extra line with only six additions beyond the generic coverage packet:

1. profile identifier (`CP-TF-RUNTIME` through `CP-PUBLIC-ROUTE` or local equivalent);
2. whether the profile is inherited unchanged or locally right-shifted;
3. which function families are lifted above the generic `HC` floor;
4. the published service window that makes the promise truthful;
5. the substitute path and no-silent-penalty rule if that window breaks;
6. the cooling action that will occur if the institution can no longer meet the stated band.

This keeps the public layer small while blocking a common failure: one institution-wide coverage statement that never says live classroom support, repeated minor-facing support, credit-window advising, practicum readiness, and public-route navigation already need different starting assumptions.

## What counted as a real archive gain

The archive previously had a generic coverage ladder, but still left one obvious implementation question unresolved: **which recurring educational-AI services should already start hotter because the service window and consequence shape are known in advance?**

This document answers that with a bounded starter layer. It keeps the generic `HC0-HC4` ladder, but it now makes the archive more deployable across teacher-runtime, minor-facing, higher-education pathway, professional-practice, and public-route contexts without pretending that all official service promises can inherit one ordinary queue story.

## Current archive bet

The archive's current best guess is that a **generic human-coverage ladder plus a tiny sector-and-function starter profile layer** will outperform both extremes:

- one universal coverage rule for all recurring educational-AI services;
- and bespoke office-by-office staffing language with no inherited defaults.

That claim is now canon, but still live. The next problem is narrower: which of these starter profiles are stable enough to harden, where they should branch further by after-hours posture, queue-integrity exposure, or office role, and when apparent demand should instead force retreat back toward optional or human-primary handling. See `OQ-0012`.
