# First activation-cycle drift and route-choice conflict defaults for hot-exam recipient followup shells

The archive can now publish one still smaller operating layer for the hottest exam-like learner-request routes.

It can already say:

- when a successor route has been decommissioned, replaced, split, or reduced to no-active-route residue;
- when a retired route later reappears or a replacement route activates;
- which owner, proof object, surface, and learner action-now truth make that activation current; and
- how prior timely action and no-fault posture survive post-decommission reappearance without becoming a public old-proof dossier.

That is still not enough.

The first live cycle after activation is where the next fiction usually appears. The old revived route and the replacement route can both be visible but disagree. The source page can be corrected while the search result, image result, cache, custom search, archive capture, recipient portal, transcript field, score-report queue, or provider workflow still reflects the old route. A replacement route can be current for one learner cohort but not yet accepted by the recipient-of-record. A revived route can publish one clock while the replacement route publishes another. A protected accommodation route can require a manual owner even when the public route says self-service. A learner can be shown two routes and told, implicitly, that choosing the wrong one will be their fault.

This document adds one thing only:

- a **tiny activation-cycle drift / route-choice conflict field set** for hot-exam recipient followup shells after `YP0-YP4` has activated a revived old route, replacement route, proof refresh, split-surface shell, reopened stability posture, or no-active-route current path.

That means the archive now asks a different question than before. It no longer asks only **what post-decommission trigger activated a route**. It now asks **what happens in the first activated cycle if the revived path, replacement path, source/search/archive/recipient surfaces, proof objects, or clocks disagree — without making every activation a standing watch, every old-route revival a replacement-route failure, every route-choice conflict learner fault, or every conflicting copy a public failure dossier**.

Current official signals support a deliberately narrow answer. Google Search Console's Removals tool is property-owner, exact-URL, temporary-removal, status-history, current/expired-state, cancellation, Google-Search-only, and source-side-permanent-method based rather than a global route-choice authority. Google's Refresh Outdated Content route is exact page/image URL, changed/gone-content, queue/status, additional-URL, image/container-page, and 180-day-expiry based; it can refresh a search result without proving that every recipient or source route has changed. Google's personal-content and site-owner removal guidance keeps Search removal separate from source-page removal, screenshots, notifications, and source-owner persistence. WiscWeb separates local source editing, emergency edits, primary-contact support, redirection, caching, decommissioning, campus search/lookahead/directory behavior, normal-hours limits, and major-search-engine non-control. Princeton separates local deletion, document/media cleanup, cache, Google/Bing/custom-search exclusion, access control, and Wayback persistence. Rowan separates Cascade CMS, Web Services support, Siteimprove/accessibility monitoring, contributor training, support tickets, redesign/audit work, and accommodation routing. Internet Archive asks for URL, time period, control-period, and review context before exclusion and does not give an all-route disappearance certificate. Bing distinguishes temporary blocks, up-to-90-day expiry, cached/outdated-content handling, exact URL submission, extension or permanent-removal prerequisites, and source-side methods such as 404/410 or noindex. Together those signals support a tighter archive rule: **the next truthful portability gain here is one tiny first-cycle activation / route-choice layer for drift signal, affected route-choice scope, current owner/precedence proof, learner-safe action and clock posture, conflict disposition, minimization, and reopen triggers — but not one standing activation watch, one automatic replacement-failure label, one wrong-route learner-fault rule, one all-surface route-choice certificate, or one public conflict-proof vault.** See `B260`.

## Small field set for activation-cycle drift and route-choice conflict

| Code | Meaning | Default archive action |
|---|---|---|
| `YC0-NO-ACTIVATION-AS-STANDING-WATCH-OLD-REVIVAL-AS-REPLACEMENT-FAILURE-OR-ROUTE-CHOICE-AS-LEARNER-FAULT` | the first activated cycle is not automatically a watch, a replacement failure, or learner fault merely because two routes or surfaces disagree | keep the conflict tied to the affected route, surface, owner, proof object, clock, and learner dependency; do not widen it to every revived path, replacement path, search result, archive copy, recipient record, or future cycle |
| `YC1-PUBLISH-FIRST-CYCLE-DRIFT-SIGNAL-AND-ROUTE-CHOICE-SCOPE` | the shell must say what disagreed in the first activated cycle and where the learner could actually be sent | publish whether the drift is revived-vs-replacement disagreement, first replacement-cycle drift, source/search mismatch, archive recurrence, recipient-portal mismatch, official-record mismatch, provider-route mismatch, protected-route conflict, clock conflict, prerequisite conflict, or unrelated copy |
| `YC2-PUBLISH-CURRENT-OWNER-PRECEDENCE-AND-PROOF-SPLIT` | the shell must name which current owner or route controls the next action, or say that owner precedence is unresolved | label the proof as source owner, replacement owner, revived-route owner, successor owner, provider route, search-provider state, archive/platform review, recipient-of-record, official-record owner, protected/accommodation owner, local manual owner, split-owner unresolved, stale proof, owner claim without proof, or no proof yet |
| `YC3-PUBLISH-LEARNER-SAFE-ACTION-CLOCK-AND-NO-FAULT-CHOICE` | route choice must not punish the learner for owner-side conflict, stale surfaces, or unpublished precedence | publish whether the learner should wait, use the replacement route, use the revived route, use either route without adverse difference, submit once with dual recognition, use the more protective / longer-window route until resolved, contact named manual support, or take no action; clocks start only from current conflict-aware notice unless the route itself lawfully says otherwise |
| `YC4-PUBLISH-CONFLICT-DISPOSITION-MINIMIZATION-AND-REOPEN-TRIGGERS` | the shell must resolve, split, hand back, reopen proof refresh/stability, or stay residue without retaining a public conflict dossier | publish replacement controls, revived route controls, dual-route recognition, split-surface operation, owner reconciliation opened, proof refresh opened, successor-stability reopened, decommissioned-route residue, no-active-route residue, protected/local-only handling, ordinary activation restored, or minimized conflict memory with reopen only on material new route, owner, proof, clock, or learner-dependency facts |

## Field values that now travel together

When this layer is used, the shell should publish only six concrete fields:

1. `activation_cycle_drift_token` — `no first-cycle drift`, `revived route and replacement route disagree`, `replacement first cycle drifted`, `revived route proof stale`, `replacement proof stale`, `source and search disagree`, `source and archive disagree`, `source and recipient portal disagree`, `recipient portal and official record disagree`, `provider route and local route disagree`, `public route and protected route disagree`, `route prerequisite changed`, `route clock changed`, `two active routes offered`, `unrelated copy only`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
2. `route_choice_scope_token` — `revived route only`, `replacement route only`, `revived and replacement route conflict`, `one exact URL`, `one redirected or canonical family`, `one provider route`, `one recipient portal`, `one official-record field`, `one archive time slice`, `one search result or image family`, `one local source or CMS workflow`, `one protected accommodation workflow`, `split-surface shell`, `no-active-route current path only`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
3. `route_precedence_proof_token` — `replacement owner controls`, `revived-route owner controls`, `successor owner controls`, `source owner controls`, `provider route controls`, `recipient-of-record controls`, `official-record owner controls`, `protected or accommodation owner controls`, `search provider state only`, `archive or platform state only`, `dual owners agree`, `dual owners disagree`, `precedence unresolved`, `owner claim without proof`, `stale proof only`, `no proof yet`, `sensitive proof local-only`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
4. `learner_safe_action_token` — `no learner action now`, `information-only notice`, `wait for named owner`, `use replacement route`, `use revived route`, `choose either route without adverse difference`, `submit once with dual-route recognition`, `use more protective route until resolved`, `use longer current window until resolved`, `do not duplicate filing`, `do not refile into dead path`, `manual local support only`, `protected route controls`, `clock starts only after conflict-aware notice`, `prior timely action preserved`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;

6. `conflict_reopen_trigger_token` — `new owner proof`, `new route proof`, `new recipient confirmation`, `new official-record state`, `new search-provider state`, `new archive/platform state`, `new protected-route requirement`, `clock or prerequisite changed`, `old route disappeared again`, `replacement route failed again`, `learner dependency became live`, `privacy or safety owner required local handling`, `ordinary lag only`, `no reopen trigger`, `none inherited`, or `not published`.

## Practical route-choice defaults

| Situation | Minimal publication | Why |
|---|---|---|
| retired provider path reappears while replacement route is current | `revived route and replacement route disagree`, `revived and replacement route conflict`, current replacement-owner proof, `use replacement route` or `choose either route without adverse difference`, and `replacement route controls` | old-route visibility does not by itself prove replacement failure; the learner needs the current action path and clock, not a broad decommission-failure label |
| replacement route opens but first recipient portal still accepts only the old path | `source and recipient portal disagree` or `provider route and local route disagree`, `one recipient portal`, recipient-of-record proof, `submit once with dual-route recognition` or `use more protective route until resolved`, and `split-surface operation` | recipient dependency can govern the learner-facing path even when the source or provider route has migrated; do not force duplicate filings or punish wrong-route choice while owners conflict |
| source page is corrected but Google/Bing/custom search or image/snippet results still show the old route | `source and search disagree`, `one search result or image family`, source-owner plus search-provider state, `no learner action now` or `information-only notice`, and `proof refresh opened` | search reflection is exact-URL/status based and can lag; the route-choice shell should not reopen recipient obligations unless the result affects a live learner dependency |
| Wayback, mirror, screenshot, or archive capture preserves the old route after replacement activation | `source and archive disagree` or `unrelated copy only`, `one archive time slice`, archive/platform state, `information-only notice`, and `decommissioned-route residue retained` or `minimized conflict memory` | archive persistence is capture/time-slice specific; it does not make the replacement path fail unless a current owner, recipient, or learner dependency depends on that capture |
| protected accommodation or accessibility route conflicts with the public replacement route | `public route and protected route disagree`, `one protected accommodation workflow`, protected/accommodation owner proof, `protected route controls`, and `protected local-only route retained` | protected routes can control without making the public path defective; sensitive proof should stay local-only unless publication is necessary for current action |
| revived route and replacement route publish different clocks | `route clock changed`, `revived and replacement route conflict`, precedence proof or `precedence unresolved`, `use longer current window until resolved` or `clock starts only after conflict-aware notice`, and `owner reconciliation opened` | clock conflict is owner-side ambiguity; learner fault should not attach until current conflict-aware notice identifies the controlling route-native window |
| first replacement cycle changes prerequisite proof after activation notice | `route prerequisite changed`, `replacement route only`, replacement-owner proof, `wait for named owner` or `clock starts only after conflict-aware notice`, and `proof refresh opened` | the route can require current proof prospectively, but it should not treat a learner's earlier timely filing as abandoned because activation proof changed after notice |
| no-active-route residue exposes a current path but that path conflicts with an old decommissioned route still visible elsewhere | `no-active-route current path only` plus the visible old-route conflict, current-path owner proof, `use more protective route until resolved`, and `route migration reopened` or `owner reconciliation opened` | later owner discovery can make a path live prospectively without erasing the earlier residue truth or turning stale old-route visibility into a learner obligation |

## What this layer now makes portable

Hot-exam recipient followup shells may now publish one tiny `YC0-YC4` layer after `YP0-YP4` when the archive needs to say not merely that **a post-decommission trigger activated a route**, but that **the first activated cycle has route-choice drift or conflict that could misdirect a learner unless owner precedence, proof split, learner-safe action, clock posture, and minimization are named**.

That means a shell may now truthfully say things like:

- `the replacement route is current, but the old provider path still appears in search; use the replacement route, preserve prior timely action, and refresh only the affected search proof`;
- `the recipient portal still accepts the old route while the source page has migrated; submit once with dual-route recognition until the recipient-of-record names the controlling path`;
- `two active routes show different clocks; use the longer or more protective window until current conflict-aware notice resolves precedence`;
- `the protected accommodation route controls for this learner even though the public replacement route is ordinary`; or
- `a Wayback capture still shows the old route; keep archive residue time-slice-specific and do not reopen the replacement route without a live dependency`.

It still may not pretend:

- that every activation creates a standing watch;
- that every old-route revival proves replacement-route failure;
- that every route-choice conflict is learner fault;
- that a stale search result, archive capture, or screenshot creates a current action path by itself;
- that owner-side clock conflict can start an adverse learner deadline before conflict-aware notice;
- that protected or sensitive proof must become public to resolve ordinary public-route drift;
- that dual active routes automatically require duplicate learner filings; or
- that one surface's conflict proves all-surface instability.

## Why this matters for the larger archive

The archive's education-with-AI program is not only about publishing route activation. It is also about making official infrastructure legible enough that learners are not asked to guess which of two official-looking paths will preserve their rights.

This layer matters because first-cycle activation produces two opposite bad defaults. One bad default says any revived old path means the replacement route failed and broad watch/stability machinery must restart. The other says the replacement route is current, so any learner who followed the still-visible old path is at fault. Both are wrong. The portable answer is narrower: publish **what drift appeared**, **which route-choice scope is affected**, **which current owner or proof controls**, **what safe action the learner can take without adverse wrong-route risk**, **which clock applies or is suspended**, **how prior timely action remains protected**, and **how the shell resolves or minimizes the conflict once it no longer affects live action**. Everything else remains route-native casework, local support, protected handling, ordinary search/cache/archive lag, or no-active-route residue.

## Boundary conditions

This layer should not be used when only one of the following is true:

- `YP0-YP4` has not activated a revived route, replacement route, proof refresh, split-surface shell, reopened stability posture, or no-active-route current path;
- the visible difference is ordinary search/cache/crawl lag with no learner-facing route-choice, owner, proof, clock, recipient, protected-route, or live dependency effect;
- the route-native activation notice already names a single controlling path, current owner, proof requirement, and clock, and no conflicting official-looking path remains learner-facing;
- the only signal is an unrelated mirror, screenshot, archive capture, history row, or downstream copy with no current owner or learner dependency;
- a protected accommodation, privacy, safety, legal, records, appeal, or recipient-of-record process already controls route choice and proof handling;
- the learner has no action-now obligation and no current deadline, recipient dependency, official-record dependency, or proof dependency is live; or
- a current route names a specific conflict-resolution, appeal, precedence, or duplicate-filing rule that should govern instead of a portable first-cycle layer.

In those cases, publish `no first-cycle drift`, `ordinary route-native window applies`, `ordinary search or archive lag`, `unrelated copy only`, `protected local-only route`, `no learner action now`, `no active route`, `precedence already resolved`, or `none inherited` rather than treating every first-cycle difference as standing watch, replacement failure, or learner fault.

## What this closes

This closes `FT-0146`.

The archive no longer stops at "post-decommission activation occurred". It can now publish a minimal activation-cycle drift / route-choice conflict layer: the first-cycle drift signal, route-choice scope, current owner / precedence proof, learner-safe action, clock posture, conflict disposition, minimization, and reopen triggers when a revived route and replacement route disagree or when source/search/archive/recipient/protected surfaces split immediately after activation.

It also opens one narrower seam.

After a first activated cycle resolves or splits a route-choice conflict, the archive may still need a tiny repeat activation-cycle conflict / route-stability threshold layer for cases where the same revived/replacement pair repeatedly disagrees, clock conflicts recur, recipient portals repeatedly accept the wrong path, or proof split recurs after minimization — without making every first-cycle conflict a standing watch, every repeated conflict learner fault, or every route-pair history a permanent public instability label.
