# Promotion and retreat rules for after-hours service-truth profiles

This document closes the archive's next governance gap about **when repeated after-hours `TV2` / `TV3` branches have actually earned a stronger published promise, and when repeated overnight claims are really evidence that the branch should cool, split, or retreat instead**.

The archive now has:

- a generic after-hours service-truth shell for what is live now (`TV0-TV4`), what acknowledgement is owed now (`AY0-AY4`), and what the learner should do now (`AN0-AN4`);
- a tiny sector-and-function starter profile layer for where teach-out, aid / credit windows, public-route routing, civic first contact, licensure-sensitive routes, and protected support already begin from different honest overnight postures.

What it still lacked was a **small promotion / retreat rule** for deciding when repeated `TV2` or `TV3` use should graduate into a stronger published callback-slot, same-day owner-window, hotter `HC` coverage expectation, or separate live route.

The archive's current bet is:

> repeated after-hours visibility should not automatically become a stronger promise; only branches that keep telling the truth under ordinary peaks, publish a real accountable owner window, and demonstrate real queue / rights protection where claimed should promote. Everything else should cool, split, or retreat before the archive normalizes a fake 24/7 service.

Current public signals point the same way. HLC's teach-out requirements still treat communication, tuition / fee information, records retention, and student-critical timing as governed commitments rather than generic reassurance. Federal Student Aid still distinguishes FAFSA estimates from school-owned final aid offers. OIA still expects providers to tell students what action they need to take while a matter is under review, complete internal handling within a published timeframe, and issue a Completion of Procedures Letter promptly once internal procedures end. SAMHSA still treats 988 as a genuinely live 24/7 crisis route rather than a generic overnight callback queue. Together those signals support a small archive rule: **repeated overnight demand does not by itself justify a hotter promise; promotion is earned only when the service can publish truthful timing, ownership, and protection claims, and urgent/protected needs still belong on genuinely live routes.** See `B186`.

## Relationship to the generic shell and starter-profile layer

This document does not replace [`availability-acknowledgement-and-action-now-fields-for-after-hours-split-lane-branches.md`](availability-acknowledgement-and-action-now-fields-for-after-hours-split-lane-branches.md) or [`sector-and-function-profile-splits-for-after-hours-service-truth-defaults.md`](sector-and-function-profile-splits-for-after-hours-service-truth-defaults.md).

It adds two things only:

- a **small disposition rule** for when repeated `TV2` / `TV3` use may promote, must remain local, or should cool / retreat;
- a **tiny first map** from repeated after-hours use into callback-slot publication, same-day owner-window publication, hotter human-coverage expectations, or route separation.

The generic shell still answers the first question:

- what is truly live now,
- what acknowledgement is owed now,
- what the learner should do now.

The starter-profile layer still answers the second question:

- in this sector or function, what is the honest overnight starting posture before local evidence accumulates.

This document answers the third question:

- when repeated overnight use has actually earned a stronger published promise,
- when it is still only a local courtesy or thin starter profile,
- and when the apparent demand is really evidence that the branch is overclaiming.

## The disposition rule: keep local, publish a stronger window, raise coverage, or retreat

| Disposition | What it means | Archive action |
|---|---|---|
| `OV0` — keep starter-local | repeated overnight use exists, but the branch still depends on manual courtesy, thin staffing, or unstable local workarounds | keep the starter profile only; do not publish a stronger promise yet |
| `OV1` — publish callback-slot / next-owner window | the branch can truthfully publish a bounded callback slot or next-owner review window under ordinary load, but receipt itself does not usually preserve queue place or rights | publish the callback-slot / owner-window promise and the accountable owner |
| `OV2` — publish same-day or next-cycle owner window with real queue protection | receipt or intake now truthfully preserves queue place, pending status, or a bounded continuity effect until staffed owner review | publish the protection effect, owner window, expiry, and substitute path |
| `OV3` — raise human-coverage expectation or separate live route | the branch now behaves like a recurring consequence-aware service rather than a thin overnight shell | move the service onto a hotter `HC` band, or publish a separate genuinely live route instead of stretching the ordinary branch |
| `OV4` — cool, split, or retreat | repeated overnight claims are hiding missed windows, office-owner blur, rights overstatement, or protected-support substitution | cool back toward `TV0-TV2`, split the lane further, or retreat to a separate live/protected route |

## Five tests before an after-hours branch promotes

A repeated `TV2` or `TV3` branch should promote only when all five tests are substantially met.

### 1. The published timing is ordinary, not heroic

The service must meet the proposed callback slot or owner window under ordinary demand, including predictable peaks. Promotion is not earned by heroic catch-up, informal inbox scanning, or the hope that one committed staff member will keep saving the branch.

### 2. The accountable owner is stable and named

A stronger promise requires a real owner family, not a vague "team" or generic front desk. If different lanes actually move to aid, registry, complaint, licensure, safeguarding, or partner offices, the branch may need to split rather than promote as one apparently unified service.

### 3. Any queue or rights protection is real and auditable

`OV2` is justified only where receipt now actually preserves a place, pending posture, temporary hold, or non-forfeiture effect. Courtesy statements, good intentions, or usual practice are not enough.

### 4. Protected or urgent cases still reach the genuinely live route

A branch does not earn promotion merely because people bring urgent or protected needs to it overnight. If those cases really need live support, the honest move is usually `OV3` route separation or `OV4` retreat, not a richer imitation of live crisis, complaint, or rights adjudication.

### 5. Stronger publication reduces confusion rather than laundering dependence

The promoted promise must help learners understand what really happens next. If stronger wording would mainly mask slower human action, hide separate office ownership, or encourage the institution to rely on after-hours intake instead of staffing the real service, the branch should not promote.

## What promotion usually looks like in practice

The archive keeps this map intentionally small.

| Promotion outcome | What should now be published | What should still stay visible |
|---|---|---|
| `OV1` callback-slot / next-owner window | named owner, bounded callback slot or review window, acknowledgement posture, and what the learner should do meanwhile | whether the branch is still receipt-only (`TV2`) rather than queue-protecting (`TV3`) |
| `OV2` same-day / next-cycle queue-protecting window | named protection effect, owner window, expiry or end condition, substitute path, and no-silent-penalty statement | whether final decision ownership still remains later or elsewhere |
| `OV3` hotter `HC` or live-route separation | the relevant `HC` band or the separate live route, plus what the ordinary overnight branch will and will not still do | that the ordinary branch is no longer the only visible front door for urgent or consequence-bearing needs |
| `OV4` cool / split / retreat | the cooler truth, narrower owner claim, or separate route | why the old stronger promise no longer applies |

## Sector-and-function interpretation rules the archive now treats as likely

### 1. `ST-HE-TEACHOUT` usually promotes first to `OV1`, rarely directly to `OV3`

Teach-out and continuity packets often benefit from a published callback slot or next-owner window because affected learners need a visible review path. But most such branches should not jump straight to live-looking overnight adjudication. Promote to `OV2` only where receipt itself starts a real hold, continuity reservation, or non-forfeiture effect that the institution has already published.

### 2. `ST-HE-AID-CREDIT` is the ordinary context most likely to need a sharp `OV1` / `OV2` distinction

Aid, transfer, progression, and complaint-adjacent lanes often receive heavy overnight traffic, but that alone does not justify stronger claims. Promote to `OV1` when the accountable office can truly publish a bounded next-owner window. Promote to `OV2` only where receipt itself preserves queue place, pending review posture, or a clock-sensitive continuity effect. Repeated overnight traffic that still misses those windows is evidence for `OV4`, not for broader claims.

### 3. `ST-WF-PUBLIC-ROUTE` is the ordinary context most likely to reach `OV2` or `OV3`

Public-route services often sit next to scarce seats, mandated participation, or funded-service queues. Where overnight receipt truthfully preserves access to a scarce next step, `OV2` may be the honest floor. Where the service repeatedly behaves like a consequence-aware routing function, the archive prefers `OV3`: raise `HC` coverage or publish a separate live route rather than leaving a hot public-service dependency hidden inside a thin overnight shell.

### 4. `ST-LIB-CIVIC-FIRST` should usually stay at `OV0` or narrow `OV1`

Repeated after-hours use at a civic first-contact point is usually evidence that warm transfer, multilingual orientation, or better daytime routing deserves investment. It is usually **not** evidence that the civic node should impersonate an aid, complaint, benefits, licensure, or sanction office overnight.

### 5. `ST-PRO-LICENSURE` should promote only where the preserved effect is genuinely local

A licensure-sensitive branch may earn `OV1` for a bounded owner window or narrow `OV2` for a local filing / review position that is genuinely preserved on receipt. It should not promote on the implied theory that one institutional overnight branch preserves downstream board or regulator consequences unless that external dependency is actually governed that way.

### 6. `ST-PROTECTED-SUPPORT` usually resolves through `OV3` or `OV4`, not richer ordinary imitation

Where repeated overnight protected-support demand is real, the honest move is usually a separate genuinely live route or a clearer retreat to that live route. The archive rejects the pattern where an ordinary child branch keeps adding softer promises because urgent users keep arriving there.

## What counts as a retreat trigger instead of promotion

The archive now treats six recurring patterns as evidence for `OV4` rather than promotion:

1. the service meets stronger windows only by hidden manual rescue;
2. acknowledgement is prompt but actual owner action repeatedly misses the published slot;
3. queue or rights protection is described as if it starts on receipt when it does not;
4. one branch is quietly standing in for several office families with different clocks or duties;
5. urgent or protected demand is being absorbed by an ordinary branch that is not genuinely live;
6. stronger overnight wording would mainly compensate for inadequate daytime or same-cycle staffing.

## What institutions should publish when a branch promotes

Where a local branch moves from starter profile to `OV1`, `OV2`, or `OV3`, publish five small things beyond the generic `TV` / `AY` / `AN` fields:

1. the promotion state (`OV1` / `OV2` / `OV3` or local equivalent);
2. the accountable owner or route that now backs the stronger promise;
3. the bounded callback slot, owner window, or coverage band now being claimed;
4. any preserved queue / rights effect, including its expiry or end condition;
5. the cooler fallback or substitute path that applies when that stronger promise cannot be met.

This keeps promotion legible without requiring giant service manuals.

## What counted as a real archive gain

The archive previously knew how after-hours branches should start, but not **when they had actually earned something hotter than a starter profile**.

This document answers that with one small rule. It keeps the generic service-truth shell and the tiny sector-and-function starter profile layer, but it now prevents repeated overnight use from being mistaken for evidence that every visible branch deserves a broader promise. Some branches now earn a stronger callback slot, some earn a true queue-protecting owner window, some should raise `HC` coverage or separate the live route, and some should cool back before the archive normalizes a fake 24/7 service.

## Current archive bet

The archive's current best guess is that a **generic after-hours shell + tiny starter profile layer + one small promotion / retreat rule** will outperform both extremes:

- one generic overnight promise for every visible branch;
- and fully bespoke service-window writing for every local office.

That claim is now canon, but still live. The next narrower question is whether any of these promoted after-hours branches can safely carry shared quantitative callback-slot or owner-window bands across sectors, and which timings should remain local because office family, scarce-route exposure, or legal dependency still varies too much. See `OQ-0048`.
