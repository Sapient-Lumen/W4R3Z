# First post-restoration recurrence and minimized-evidence recall defaults for hot-exam recipient followup shells

The archive can now publish one still smaller recurrence layer for the hottest exam-like learner-request routes.

It can already say:

- whether a residual-audit fact still has a live learner-facing dependency;
- which proof may be redacted, compressed, moved local-only, or replaced by an as-of residue pointer;
- which affected surface has returned to ordinary status;
- what material live dependency would reactivate the shell; and
- why ordinary maintenance, local record retention, and learner-facing action are not the same thing.

That is still not enough.

A restored ordinary shell can recur. A temporary search block can expire. A source page can regress. A cached snippet can reappear. A recipient may later say it cannot confirm a record, or a learner may need proof of a past route attempt after the public evidence was minimized for privacy. A campus office may still hold a local ticket, but the learner-facing shell may no longer show the exact URL, screenshot, owner email, request ID, or name-bearing evidence. A platform history row may have aged out. A new copy may be materially similar but not identical to the old one. If the archive stops at ordinary restoration, one system may automatically unminimize everything whenever a recurrence is alleged, while another may say the minimized state is final and the learner must start over without the prior proof that the institution deliberately moved local-only.

This document adds one thing only:

- a **tiny post-restoration recurrence / minimized-evidence recall field set** for hot-exam recipient followup shells after learner-facing residue has been minimized or an affected surface has been restored to ordinary status.

That means the archive now asks a different question than before. It no longer asks only **when residue may be minimized or ordinary status restored**. It now asks **what later fact is material enough to reopen the affected surface, who may recall minimized proof, how much of that proof may become learner-facing again, and how the shell returns to ordinary/minimized status after the recurrence is handled**.

Current official signals support a deliberately narrow answer. Google Search Console exposes temporary removals, snippet clearing, current and expired histories for a limited window, about-six-month temporary removal, and expiry/reappearance eligibility rather than permanent all-surface disappearance. Google’s Refresh Outdated Content tool exposes request states and approval expiry, while still limiting the route to Google Search reflection. Google personal-content routes require concrete URLs and sometimes screenshots, distinguish Search removal from source-site or other-engine persistence, and can surface alerts or new-result handling without making the original source disappear. Princeton distinguishes local deletion, Google, Bing, custom search, and Wayback routes. WiscWeb separates local emergency edits from campus search, lookahead, directory, and major-search-engine timing/status, and says WiscWeb cannot expedite or control those major search engines. Internet Archive requires URL, time-period, and control-period facts and gives no guaranteed outcome before review. Bing exposes temporary blocks, up-to-90-day windows, cached/outdated-content handling, and permanent-removal prerequisites rather than one durable closure state. Together those signals support a tighter archive rule: **the next truthful portability gain here is one tiny recurrence-and-recall layer for affected-surface reopening and privacy-preserving proof recall, but not one permanent recall box, one automatic unminimization rule, one universal post-restoration monitoring duty, one proof-expiry-as-never-happened rule, or one all-copy recurrence certificate.** See `B250`.

## Small field set for post-restoration recurrence and minimized-evidence recall

| Code | Meaning | Default archive action |
|---|---|---|
| `XU0-NO-PERMANENT-RECALL-BOX-AUTO-UNMINIMIZATION-OR-UNIVERSAL-MONITORING-DUTY` | ordinary restoration or minimization does not create an always-open evidence drawer, automatic re-publication rule, or duty to keep scanning every source/search/archive/mirror surface | reopen only on material current proof or route-native expiry/reappearance, and keep local proof recall narrower than public proof revival |
| `XU1-PUBLISH-MATERIAL-RECURRENCE-TRIGGER-BEFORE-REOPENING` | a restored shell reopens only when a current result, source regression, route-native expiry, recipient-record effect, owner/state change, or learner/recipient proof need materially affects the same or clearly linked surface | do not reopen merely because old screenshots exist, provider history aged out, or a nonadverse maintenance reflection occurs |
| `XU2-PUBLISH-MINIMIZED-EVIDENCE-RECALL-SCOPE-AND-AUTHORITY` | local-only proof may be recalled only by a named owner, appeal/remedy reviewer, recipient-confirmation process, official-record process, or learner-access route with a current need | publish which proof class is being recalled, who may see it, and whether it stays local-only, redacted, surfaced as an as-of pointer, or partially reattached to the learner-facing shell |
| `XU3-PUBLISH-PRIVACY-PRESERVING-REACTIVATION-AND-LEARNER-NOTICE` | when action resumes, publish only the current action facts needed for the affected surface and avoid re-exposing old names, screenshots, request IDs, owner emails, or third-party identifiers unless the current route requires them | give the learner prospective action-now notice and identify what prior proof will be used without turning minimized evidence back into a public dossier |
| `XU4-PUBLISH-POST-RECURRENCE-REMINIMIZATION-OR-RECLOSURE-STATE` | once the recurrence is cured, denied, redirected, stale, or no longer material, the shell must say whether it returns to ordinary status, minimized residue, local-only proof, no-active-route residue, or a new live route | close the reopened surface back to the narrowest truthful state rather than keeping it permanently hot because evidence was once recalled |

## Field values that now travel together

When this layer is used, the shell should publish only five concrete fields:

1. `post_restoration_recurrence_trigger_token` — `same exact URL reappeared`, `same source page regressed`, `same snippet or image copy reappeared`, `route-native temporary block expired`, `provider history expired but current result visible`, `new materially linked copy`, `recipient cannot confirm record`, `official score or record effect reopened`, `learner requests prior proof for live remedy`, `owner or platform route changed`, `no material recurrence`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
2. `minimized_evidence_recall_authority_token` — `current source owner`, `current campus web owner`, `current search-provider route`, `current archive or platform reviewer`, `current recipient-confirmation owner`, `official record or score owner`, `appeal or remedy reviewer`, `learner-access request`, `privacy or safety reviewer`, `no current recall authority`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
3. `recalled_proof_scope_token` — `as-of residue pointer only`, `exact URL local-only`, `request ID local-only`, `redacted screenshot`, `surface class only`, `prior owner outcome summary`, `prior submission timestamp`, `prior closure token`, `recipient-send evidence`, `no prior proof needed`, `proof unavailable or history expired`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
4. `reactivation_publicity_token` — `learner-facing current action only`, `redacted learner-facing recurrence note`, `name suppressed`, `third-party identifiers suppressed`, `old screenshots suppressed`, `current URL visible while live`, `recipient-only proof disclosure`, `owner-only proof disclosure`, `private review only`, `no public reactivation`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
5. `post_recurrence_state_token` — `return to ordinary status`, `return to minimized residue pointer`, `retain proof local-only`, `affected surface reclosed`, `redirect to current owner`, `no-active-route residue`, `live route remains open`, `official record effect remains live`, `new recurrence pattern flagged`, `no current action path`, `none inherited`, or `not published`.

That is deliberately small. It is enough to decide whether a restored surface has actually recurred, which minimized proof may be recalled, who may view or use that proof, how much should become learner-facing again, and how the shell cools back down after the recurrence is handled. It is not enough to create a universal watchlist, one all-owner proof-recall vault, one unredacted evidence republication rule, one cross-platform recurrence certificate, or one rule that local proof must become public again whenever a learner or office needs it.

## First post-restoration recurrence and minimized-evidence recall assignments

| Route or family | Recurrence / recall truth now admitted | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Google Search Console temporary-removal or snippet-clear route after an ordinary-restored search surface reappears | `route-native temporary block expired`, `same exact URL reappeared`, `current search-provider route`, `exact URL local-only` or `current URL visible while live`, and `return to minimized residue pointer` after the current route closes | Search Console temporary removals are limited, can expire, and are Search-only unless source-side permanent-removal conditions are satisfied |
| Google Refresh Outdated Content route after an approved request, history-row expiry, or later snippet/page reappearance | `same snippet or image copy reappeared`, `provider history expired but current result visible`, `current search-provider route`, `prior submission timestamp`, `prior owner outcome summary`, and `reopen affected surface on current Search proof only` | the route updates Google Search for changed or removed content, exposes request states and approval expiry, and does not prove source-page or all-platform disappearance |
| Google personal-content / Results-about-you style route after minimized name or screenshot evidence exists | `new materially linked copy`, `learner requests prior proof for live remedy`, `appeal or remedy reviewer`, `redacted screenshot`, `name suppressed`, and `learner-facing current action only` | personal-content routes need concrete URL/screenshot proof and may involve sensitive personal data, while Search removal still leaves possible source-site, social, or other-engine persistence |
| campus source, media, file, redirect, or local-search route after source correction later regresses | `same source page regressed`, `current campus web owner`, `prior owner outcome summary`, `redacted learner-facing recurrence note`, and `affected surface reclosed` or `redirect to current owner` | campus web owners can control local source surfaces or campus-search levers but not every external index, archive, or mirror surface |
| WiscWeb-style emergency edit after a safety, harassment, directory, or sensitive-name surface recurs | `same source page regressed` or `new materially linked copy`, `privacy or safety reviewer`, `redacted screenshot`, `old screenshots suppressed`, and `private review only` unless current learner action is required | safety-sensitive local edits may need local proof recall, but major-search timing/status and public disclosure remain separate from the local emergency route |
| Princeton-style local deletion plus Google/Bing/custom-search/Wayback split after one surface recurs | `same exact URL reappeared`, `new materially linked copy`, `current source owner` or `current search-provider route`, `surface class only`, and `split post-recurrence state` | local deletion, Google, Bing, custom search, and Wayback persistence remain different route families, so recurrence on one surface does not reopen every surface |
| Bing temporary block or content-removal route after the 90-day window ends or a cached/outdated copy returns | `route-native temporary block expired`, `same exact URL reappeared`, `current search-provider route`, `request ID local-only`, and `return to ordinary status` only after current Bing/source proof is handled | Bing blocks are temporary by default and permanent removal depends on separate source-side conditions such as durable removal or noindex-style controls |
| Wayback Machine or Internet Archive exclusion review after an archived copy reappears or a new time slice is discovered | `new materially linked copy`, `current archive or platform reviewer`, `exact URL local-only`, `prior submission timestamp`, and `live route remains open` only for the affected archive/time slice | archive routes require URL/time/control-period facts and review context; a new capture or time slice may be related but is not automatically the same all-copy case |
| social, mirror, repost, or third-party platform copy after the original source/search surface is ordinary | `new materially linked copy`, `current archive or platform reviewer` or `no current recall authority`, `surface class only`, `redacted learner-facing recurrence note`, and `redirect to current owner` | outside copies have their own owners and platform routes, so prior campus/search proof may help orientation but does not itself create authority over the new copy |
| recipient record, downstream confirmation, or official score/report surface after public residue was minimized | `recipient cannot confirm record` or `official score or record effect reopened`, `current recipient-confirmation owner` or `official record or score owner`, `recipient-send evidence`, `recipient-only proof disclosure`, and `official record effect remains live` until settled | downstream records can be the live learner-facing dependency even when source/search/archive residue is ordinary, but proof disclosure can remain recipient-limited |
| learner-access or remedy route asking for prior proof after public minimization | `learner requests prior proof for live remedy`, `learner-access request` or `appeal or remedy reviewer`, `as-of residue pointer only` plus any required local-only proof, and `name suppressed` or `third-party identifiers suppressed` by default | minimization should not erase a learner's ability to prove prior action, but remedy proof can be narrower than public republication |
| ordinary maintenance reflection with no current result, owner path, score effect, recipient dependency, or learner remedy need | `no material recurrence`, `no current recall authority`, `no prior proof needed`, `no public reactivation`, and `return to ordinary status` | not every crawler reflection, closed ticket, or missing history row is live learner casework after ordinary restoration |

## What this layer now makes portable

Hot-exam recipient followup shells may now publish one tiny `XU0-XU4` layer after `XT0-XT4` when the archive needs to say not merely that **ordinary status was restored or residue was minimized**, but that **a later recurrence has reopened only an affected surface and only a narrow slice of previously minimized proof may be recalled**.

That means a shell may now truthfully say things like:

- `the Google temporary block expired and the exact URL is visible again; recall the prior local request ID, but show only the current URL and action owner to the learner`;
- `the source page regressed; reopen the campus-source surface, not the archive or recipient surface`;
- `the learner needs proof of a prior filed route for a live remedy; provide an as-of residue pointer and local-only evidence access rather than republishing screenshots`;
- `a new mirror copy is materially linked but owner authority is different; redirect to the current platform owner and keep old campus proof minimized`; or
- `there is no material recurrence, so the shell stays ordinary and the old local ticket remains ordinary record retention only`.

It still may not pretend:

- that minimized proof is gone for all purposes;
- that minimized proof must automatically become public again;
- that a restored surface creates a permanent monitoring duty;
- that every related copy is the same recurrence;
- that missing provider history means no prior action occurred;
- that local-only proof recall gives every owner or recipient a right to unredacted screenshots; or
- that one recurrent result reopens source, search, archive, mirror, recipient, and official-record surfaces together.

## Why this matters for the larger archive

The archive's education-with-AI program is not only about preventing over-collection. It is also about preventing privacy-preserving minimization from becoming institutional amnesia.

This layer matters because restored ordinary shells are prone to two opposite automation failures. One failure is over-revival: any later recurrence causes a system to unminimize names, screenshots, URLs, owner emails, and request IDs, turning a narrow proof recall into a public dossier. The other failure is under-revival: because the public shell was minimized, a learner or recipient cannot show the prior route, proof, or owner action needed to handle a live recurrence. The point here is narrower: after ordinary restoration, publish **what current fact materially recurred**, **who may recall minimized proof**, **which proof class is recalled and how it is redacted**, **what current learner-facing action is needed**, and **how the affected surface cools again after the recurrence closes**. Everything else remains ordinary maintenance, local record retention, privacy-aware proof access, or a new proof-bearing route.
