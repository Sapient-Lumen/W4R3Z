# First stakes-sensitive child branches for direct-production override rows

This document closes the archive's next narrower governance gap about **where the still-hot direct-production override rows now need their first stakes-sensitive child branches** rather than one ordinary classroom rule that quietly travels into timed, gateway, or tightly controlled settings.

The archive's current bet is:

> split direct-production rows first by whether the claim is still an ordinary classroom production surface or has become a timed, gateway, or proctored independence claim; do not let the hotter branch inherit the cooler row's ordinary defaults.

The point is to avoid five predictable mistakes at once:

- **tempo laundering** — a row that is acceptable for ordinary coursework silently travels into speed-bearing settings where pace itself is now being measured;
- **gateway flattening** — low-stakes classroom evidence gets treated as interchangeable with placement, progression, certification, or high-consequence performance evidence;
- **controlled-independence drift** — a branch that is supposed to show the learner's own unaided production is quietly governed as if supervision, tool lock-down, or exam conditions make no difference;
- **access-collapse panic** — once one hotter branch exists, institutions overcorrect by treating every related classroom task as if it were already an exam-security problem;
- **row inflation** — every timer length, platform, or local proctoring protocol becomes its own named inherited row before the archive has even made the first honest stakes split.

Current official signals point in the same direction. College Board's current AP guidance already distinguishes exploratory assistance from final learner-owned performance and explanation. Its current calculator policy and subject pages still separate calculator-permitted and calculator-prohibited parts inside the same course rather than pretending one mathematics tool posture fits all tasks. Current AP digital administration guidance also keeps exam-mode and proctoring conditions explicit instead of treating ordinary classroom production and controlled exam production as the same surface. College Board's current recording-response guidance keeps dictation, writer/scribe, and speech-to-text accommodations distinct from unapproved extra tools or composition help, while WIDA continues to keep the validity line explicit by prohibiting modifications that change what the assessment measures. Together those signals support a tighter archive rule: the first truthful split for still-hot direct-production rows is usually **ordinary classroom direct production** versus **hotter timed / gateway / controlled-independence direct production**, not a much finer-grained proliferation of local rows. See `B196`, `B197`, `B198`.

## Small hot-branch trigger grammar

The archive keeps the first stakes-sensitive split deliberately small.

| Trigger family | What changed | Default effect |
|---|---|---|
| `HS1` — **tempo-bearing** | independent production pace, speed floor, or section timing is now part of the claim | branch to the hotter child row |
| `HS2` — **gateway-bearing** | the surface now helps decide progression, placement, credentialing, licensure, or other meaningful opportunity gates | branch to the hotter child row |
| `HS3` — **controlled-independence** | proctoring, lock-down, supervised independence, or exam-room controls are now part of the proof surface | branch to the hotter child row |

The archive's starter rule is intentionally coarse: **any one of `HS1-HS3` is enough to stop inheriting the ordinary classroom direct-production row unchanged**.

## Row notes

### `SR-READ-FOUND-01A1` vs `SR-READ-FOUND-01A2`

The archive is now making one narrow claim about foundational decoding: the first honest stakes split is not by every programme brand or timer length, but by whether the row is still an **ordinary classroom direct-decoding check** or has become a **timed / gateway / controlled-independence decoding proof surface**. The hotter branch should usually preserve at least one supervised direct reading surface and should not inherit the cooler row's lighter classroom assumptions. See `B196`, `B198`.

### `SR-MATH-PROC-01A1` vs `SR-MATH-PROC-01A2`

The archive is making the same narrow move for no-executor symbolic procedure. A classroom teacher may still want one first-pass unaided procedure check before supported application work returns. But once the task is in a no-calculator exam part, a placement/gateway decision, or a controlled independence surface, the proof burden is hotter than the ordinary classroom row. The first child split should therefore separate ordinary class checking from the hotter branch instead of letting the ordinary row travel upward unchanged. See `B196`, `B198`.

### `SR-WRITE-RHET-01A` vs `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B`

The writing row now gets its own first stakes-sensitive split. `SR-WRITE-RHET-01A` keeps the archive's ordinary classroom rule: learner-owned language remains the graded claim, and a narrow recording-not-rewriting carveout can still preserve access without hiding surrogate authorship. `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B` marks the hotter branch where timing, gateway consequence, or controlled independence becomes part of the writing claim. That hotter branch should usually require a more controlled proof surface than disclosure or draft history alone. See `B197`, `B198`.

## What the archive is now making explicit

### Ordinary direct production and hot direct production are no longer the same row

The archive now treats the first honest stakes split as a **two-level child map** rather than a giant local explosion. Ordinary classroom direct-production work may still inherit a reusable row. But once pace, consequence, or controlled independence becomes part of the claim, the row moves to a hotter child branch. See `B196`, `B198`.

### The first hot branch is still not the final branch map

This document does **not** claim that all hot branches are now stable enough to harden broadly. It only says the first truthful stakes split is now named. The hotter branches still remain provisional, and later sector-, age-, disability-, and modality-sensitive refinements may still matter. See `B197`, `B198`.

### Protected access is preserved on the cool side, not laundered through the hot side

The archive is deliberately resisting a common policy mistake: once a hotter branch exists, institutions sometimes drag the whole family onto the hot rail. That is not the rule here. The ordinary classroom rows keep the cooler access logic where it remains truthful. The new hotter child rows exist to prevent quiet upward travel, not to erase protected access from ordinary teaching. See `B197`, `B198`.

## What the archive is **not** doing yet

The archive is still holding back from three wider moves:

- it is **not yet** publishing timer-specific or exam-brand-specific child rows;
- it is **not yet** hardening the new hot branches across sectors or modalities;
- it is **not yet** deciding whether some of the hot branches should later split again by age band, disability modality, or course family.

## Current archive bet

The archive's current best guess is that **the first truthful stakes-sensitive split for still-hot direct-production rows is ordinary classroom direct production versus a hotter timed / gateway / controlled-independence child branch**.

That move keeps the archive tight. It prevents ordinary classroom defaults from laundering themselves upward into exam-like or progression-bearing settings, while also preventing hot conditions from collapsing ordinary classroom access rules. The next narrower problem is no longer whether those first stakes-sensitive child branches exist or whether any of them can harden at all; those seams are now closed by [`first-hardening-and-locality-judgments-for-hot-direct-production-child-branches.md`](first-hardening-and-locality-judgments-for-hot-direct-production-child-branches.md). The live issue is where the still-local hot decoding and hot writing branches deserve their first modality-, age-, sector-, or exam-family child splits, and which residue inside hot no-executor mathematics still needs tool-family-local branching. See `OQ-0005`.
