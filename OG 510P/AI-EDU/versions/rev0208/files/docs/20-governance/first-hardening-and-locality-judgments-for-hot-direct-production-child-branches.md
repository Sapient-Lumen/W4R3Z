# First hardening and locality judgments for hot direct-production child branches

This document closes the archive's next narrower governance gap about **which of the new hot direct-production child branches can really harden at all** and which should still remain local or manual-review rows because the proof burden keeps shifting across age band, disability modality, sector, or course family.

Three failure modes matter here:

- **exam-heat laundering** — once a hot branch exists, institutions start treating every timed or gateway case as if it can inherit one generic central rule;
- **mode-blind accessibility** — approved reading or writing accommodations get treated as if they prove one stable hot branch, even though the accommodation logic still varies by disability, testing mode, and assessment family;
- **tool-family drift** — one published no-calculator or no-executor mathematics family gets over-generalized into broader symbolic-procedure settings where calculator, CAS, graphing, or modeling expectations are materially different.

Current official signals point in the same direction. College Board's current AP calculator policy and current Calculus AB / Precalculus exam pages still publish explicit no-calculator parts inside named mathematics families rather than one universal math-tool rule. Its current AP English Language exam page still publishes a timed fully digital final-writing surface, but the current AP accommodations overview plus current reading / writing and response-recording guidance also keep approved text-to-speech, dictation, writer/scribe, and mode-specific review requirements live. WIDA continues to frame accessibility support around **valid assessment results** and says state-specific policy still matters. Together those signals support a tighter archive rule: among the new hot direct-production branches, only no-executor symbolic procedure currently looks ready for **narrow course-family hardening** where a published no-calculator/no-executor claim is already explicit, while hot timed/gateway decoding and hot final writing should still remain local or manual-review rows. See `B198`, `B199`.

## Small portability grammar for hot branches

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `HP0-LOCAL` | keep the hot branch local or manual-review because age, modality, sector, or controlled-condition variance is still too high for inherited hardening |
| `HP1-FAMILY` | harden only inside a named course / exam family whose hot direct-production claim is already explicit and publicly published |
| `HP2-SECTOR` | harden across a broader sector or modality family because the hot claim and access logic now travel stably |

The archive is not yet using `HP2-SECTOR` for any of the current hot direct-production rows.

|---|---|---|---|

## `SR-MATH-PROC-01A2` — the first hot branch that can harden a little

This is the archive's first narrow hardening move inside the hot direct-production family. Where a course or exam family **already publicly declares** a no-calculator or no-executor part under controlled conditions, the archive no longer needs to pretend every such case is purely local. In those named families, the hot branch can usually travel as a **course-family child row** because the constraint is already legible to students, teachers, and proctors.

That is a narrow claim, not a universal one. It does **not** mean all symbolic mathematics can now inherit one hot direct-production rule. CAS-heavy, graphing-heavy, modeling-heavy, or proof-intensive families can still diverge too much for the same child row to travel unchanged. The archive is only hardening the thin family where the no-executor claim is already explicit and publicly published. See `B198`, `B199`.

## `SR-READ-FOUND-01A2` — hot decoding still remains local

The archive is deliberately not hardening the hot decoding branch yet. Direct first-pass decoding under timed, gateway, or controlled-independence conditions remains too sensitive to **who the learner is, what the assessment is trying to measure, and which read-aloud / screen-reader / human-reader rules are in force**. Those lines vary by age band and assessment family faster than the archive can currently tell one truthful inherited story.

So the hotter decoding branch stays local or manual-review. The archive is still better off publishing the hot branch than laundering the cooler classroom row upward, but it is **not** yet better off pretending one inherited hot decoding row travels cleanly across schools, higher-ed placements, language-proficiency tests, or professional gatekeeping surfaces. See `B198`, `B199`.

## `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B` — hot final writing still remains local

The archive is making the same cautionary move for hotter final writing. A timed or gateway writing surface is plainly different from ordinary course writing, so the hot branch needed to exist. But it still does **not** follow that one inherited hot writing row now travels unchanged across digital AP exams, paper exams, sector-specific timed writing, or professional-gate writing tasks.

Current official signals show why. Timed final writing is real, but approved dictation, writer/scribe, and digital-mode accommodations remain live too, and some through-course oral/presentation tasks need different accommodation logic than written exams. That means the hot writing branch still carries too much modality and sector residue for broad hardening now. See `B198`, `B199`.

## What the archive is **not** doing yet

The archive is still refusing three wider moves:

- it is **not** publishing one generic hot reading rule across early-literacy checks, later reading exams, language-proficiency tests, and professional gatekeeping surfaces;
- it is **not** publishing one generic hot writing rule across timed classroom essays, AP digital free responses, proctored higher-ed writing, and professional-gate written tasks;
- it is **not** pretending that explicit no-calculator families settle all hot mathematics cases once graphing, CAS, modeling, or tool-ecology differences become part of the claim.

## Current archive bet

The archive's current best guess is now slightly asymmetric again.

The new hot direct-production branches were worth naming, but only one of them currently deserves even a **thin** inherited hardening move. `SR-MATH-PROC-01A2` can now harden narrowly by **published no-calculator / no-executor course family**. `SR-READ-FOUND-01A2` and `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B` should still remain local or manual-review rows until the archive names the first narrower age-, modality-, sector-, or exam-family child splits that actually travel.

That claim is now canon, but still live. The mathematics half of that next issue is now narrowed further by [`first-tool-family-residue-splits-for-hot-no-executor-symbolic-procedure-branches.md`](first-tool-family-residue-splits-for-hot-no-executor-symbolic-procedure-branches.md): the hotter no-executor line now separates declared symbolic no-executor families from calculator-expected and tool-integrated quantitative residue. The live remaining issue is therefore **where the still-local hot decoding and hot writing branches deserve their first modality-, age-, sector-, or exam-family child splits, and whether any calculator-expected quantitative family eventually deserves a reusable hot row of its own**. See `OQ-0005`.
