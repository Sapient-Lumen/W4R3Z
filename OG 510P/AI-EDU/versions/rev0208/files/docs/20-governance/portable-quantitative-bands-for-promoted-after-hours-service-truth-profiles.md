# Portable quantitative bands for promoted after-hours service-truth profiles

This document closes the archive's next after-hours governance gap: **once a repeated `TV2` / `TV3` branch has honestly earned promotion into `OV1-OV3`, do its numeric callback-slot or owner-window bands travel too, or should the promotion state travel while the exact timing promise stays local?**

The archive's answer is intentionally narrower than the already narrow promotion rule:

> only a small class of low-consequence, non-rights-preserving `OV1` branches may usually inherit one **coarse callback-slot band** across sectors. No starter profile currently inherits one shared owner-window band, one shared queue-protection window, or one shared missed-window consequence package. Numeric timing portability is harder to earn than promotion portability.

The point is to avoid three opposite errors at once:

- **timing laundering** — a service uses one promoted state to smuggle in a stronger or more legal-significance-heavy timing promise than the accountable office can actually keep;
- **false precision** — the archive publishes one cross-sector owner window even where office family, scarce-route exposure, or legal dependency make the real operative clock materially different;
- **permanent under-specification** — a genuinely low-consequence callback pattern that really is stable enough to travel never gets one reusable coarse band and keeps re-litigating the same tiny promise locally.

Current public signals support this thinner move. HLC's teach-out policy still requires student-facing communications to specify scope, accepted credits, tuition/fees, records handling, and the applicable time period. OIA says each provider has its own timelines, treats 90 calendar days as a good-practice completion target for complaints/appeals, tells providers to explain what students must do to preserve their position while issues remain live, and expects a Completion of Procedures Letter as soon as possible and within 28 days once internal review ends. Federal Student Aid still distinguishes FAFSA estimates from school-owned final aid offers. WIOA still joins employment, education, training, and support services without collapsing them into one office rhythm. ALA still frames libraries as low-barrier public access infrastructure. SAMHSA still treats 988 as a genuinely live 24/7 route. Together those signals support a small archive rule: **a coarse callback band may sometimes travel, but owner windows, queue-protection windows, and consequence-bearing timing should usually remain local unless the archive later finds a much tighter shared regime.** See `B187`.

- **which promoted after-hours branches, if any, can also inherit one shared numeric callback-slot or owner-window band across sector profiles?**

## Three timing-portability tiers

The archive now distinguishes three after-hours timing-portability tiers.

| Tier | Meaning | What travels | What stays local |
|---|---|---|---|
| `AQT-LOCAL` | no shared numeric band travels with the promoted state | only the `OV` state and the qualitative promise | callback-slot numbers, owner windows, queue-protection expiry, and missed-window handling |
| `AQT-CALLBACK` | one shared **coarse callback-slot** band may travel | the `OV` state plus one callback band | owner windows, queue protection, rights effects, and local missed-window handling |
| `AQT-OWNER` | one shared owner-window band would travel | reserved only; **not granted in the starter canon** | everything remains local until the archive has stronger evidence |

The asymmetry is deliberate:

> a branch can honestly earn `OV1` without thereby earning one shared owner clock. The promotion state may travel while the exact time promise does not.

## Five tests before a numeric band travels

A promoted after-hours branch may harden above `AQT-LOCAL` only when all five tests substantially hold.

### 1. No-rights-dependency test

The portable band must not itself determine appeal survival, queue place, seat preservation, aid continuation, or other legally or materially consequence-bearing status. If the timing promise changes rights or scarce-route position, it stays local.

### 2. Same-practical-closure test

The band must mean the same practical thing across the reuse scope. A callback band that merely promises human contact may travel more easily than an owner window that implies review, reservation, adjudication, or protected continuity has begun.

### 3. No-scarcity-laundering test

The proposed shared band must not hide differences in scarce seats, funded-service inventory, partner release, or regulator-controlled next steps. If scarcity or external dependency is doing the real timing work, the number stays local.

### 4. Same-owner-family test

The named owner family behind the promise must be stable enough that one numeric band is not really standing in for multiple different office clocks. If aid, registry, complaint, licensure, safeguarding, or partner offices are materially different actors, exact timing should usually remain local even where the promoted state travels.

### 5. Live-route-separation test

The shared band must not blur an ordinary callback shell into a fake live route. Protected or crisis channels still belong on genuinely live routes such as `TV4`, not inside portable callback numbers.

## The starter quantitative band

The archive now names only one starter portable callback band.

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `CB-1BD` | **callback by the end of the next published business day** after complete receipt, or publication of a local reason that the callback band does not apply |

The archive does **not** yet create a richer ladder. In the starter canon:

- no promoted after-hours branch currently inherits a shared callback band slower than `CB-1BD`;
- no promoted after-hours branch currently inherits any shared owner-window band;
- any branch that cannot honestly meet `CB-1BD` should keep its timing local rather than inheriting a weaker cross-sector band by default.

## The archive's starter quantitative answers

### `ST-HE-TEACHOUT` may sometimes inherit `AQT-CALLBACK` at `OV1`

Teach-out continuity often benefits from a visible callback promise because affected learners need a human path for comparison, interpretation, and next-step orientation. Where the promoted branch still remains **callback-only** rather than queue-protecting or rights-preserving, the archive will usually allow one shared `CB-1BD` band.

What does **not** travel:

- any owner window that implies credit acceptance, aid continuity, refund treatment, or complaint-preserving status has begun;
- any queue or reservation effect;
- any same-day or rights-linked timing promise.

### `ST-LIB-CIVIC-FIRST` may sometimes inherit `AQT-CALLBACK` at `OV1`

A library or civic first-contact branch may sometimes inherit `CB-1BD` where the promise is still only navigator contact, bridge help, or public-information follow-up, and where the service stays visibly outside eligibility, benefits, complaint, or licensure decision ownership.

What does **not** travel:

- any promise that a seat, appointment, aid status, or protected position is being held;
- any owner window that would make the civic front door look like the accountable downstream office.

### `ST-HE-AID-CREDIT` stays `AQT-LOCAL`

Aid, transfer, progression, and complaint-adjacent branches may promote qualitatively into `OV1` or `OV2`, but their exact timing bands stay local in the starter canon.

Why the archive stops there:

- school-owned aid offers are the final source of truth;
- complaint/appeal timing may preserve position or external review options;
- office clocks and consequence shape vary too much for one cross-sector number to stay honest.

### `ST-WF-PUBLIC-ROUTE` stays `AQT-LOCAL`

Public-route routing may promote into real queue-protecting or consequence-aware states, but its exact timing stays local because scarce service inventory, priority handling, and partner coordination vary too much for one shared numeric promise.

### `ST-PRO-LICENSURE` stays `AQT-LOCAL`

Licensure-sensitive routes remain local because downstream decision authority, regulator calendars, and state-specific consequences vary too much for one shared owner-window or callback band to travel honestly.

### `ST-PROTECTED-SUPPORT` never uses portable callback bands in the starter canon

Protected support does not use a portable callback band at all in the starter canon. It should either remain local or separate directly onto the genuinely live protected route. A portable callback number would too easily impersonate crisis, safeguarding, or protected adjudication support.

## What portable timing bands still may not do

Even where `AQT-CALLBACK` applies, a portable callback band may not:

1. imply that owner review has already begun;
2. imply queue preservation, slot reservation, or rights protection;
3. substitute for a genuinely live urgent/protected route;
4. hide different office-family clocks behind one front-door number;
5. decide missed-window consequences, cures, or republication duties.

Numeric portability is therefore subordinate to service truth. The number travels only while the underlying meaning stays thin.

## What services must still publish locally

Even under `AQT-CALLBACK`, a service must still publish locally:

- the accountable owner family;
- whether the branch is only `OV1` callback truth or a hotter `OV2-OV3` state;
- any queue, rights, expiry, or substitute-path effect;
- what happens if the callback band is missed;
- any stricter local window or any statement that no portable band applies.

This document therefore does **not** create a right to reuse old after-hours notices. It only says when the archive is willing to let one coarse callback number travel with an already-promoted branch.

## Why this belongs in the archive now

This is a small but useful hardening step. The archive already had a service-truth shell, a sector-profile layer, and a promotion / retreat rule; without this quantitative pass, a service could still over-interpret a qualitative promotion and quietly turn it into a cross-sector timing claim stronger than the underlying office truth.

The archive can now close `FT-0064` directly:

> only a narrow class of low-consequence `OV1` branches may usually inherit one shared `CB-1BD` callback band; no starter profile currently inherits one shared owner-window band, and aid / credit, public-route, licensure, and protected-support timing stays local even after qualitative promotion.

That claim is now canon, but still live. The next narrower question is what happens when a published portable callback band is missed: **which promoted after-hours branches, if any, can safely carry one shared missed-window / breach / cure package across sectors, and which should keep slip handling fully local because rights, queue effects, or owner dependency still vary too much?** See `OQ-0049`.
