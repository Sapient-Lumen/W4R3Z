# Portable defaults for pre-declared packet maintenance envelopes

This document closes the archive's next packet-governance gap: **once a service may use a tiny pre-declared maintenance envelope for recurring packet-shell upkeep, which of those envelopes ever deserve promotion into portable branch- or authority-family defaults, and which should remain permanently local?**

The archive's current bet is deliberately narrow:

> only envelope baskets whose truth is preserved by the same packet semantics should travel. In the starter canon, label/plain-language harmonisation usually travels at the branch level, calendar housekeeping travels only for some authority families, and route/contact upkeep stays local because challengeability and first meaningful human contact are too regime-specific.

The point is to avoid three opposite mistakes at once:

- **fake portability** — a service treats one apparently familiar maintenance basket as reusable everywhere even though it changes owner reality, challengeability, or the first meaningful review point in practice;
- **permanent locality** — a basket that really is shell-preserving never hardens and therefore has to be rediscovered as owner-local housekeeping forever;
- **maintenance laundering** — a service uses a portable basket name as cover for route weakening, later review, or hidden family-conditioned field drift.

Current public signals support this thinner move. OECD's 2026 outlook keeps pressing educational AI toward pedagogically purposeful design rather than convenience-first task completion; RAND's March 2026 survey shows student AI use rising alongside concern about harm to critical thinking; OpenAI's current learning-outcomes work emphasizes longitudinal measurement rather than one-off performance snapshots; and ICO guidance keeps simple challenge routes, privacy-by-default, and child-facing design limits in view. See `B11`, `B143`, `B146`, `B149`, `B150`, `B151`.

## Relationship to the existing packet-governance canon

This document does not replace [`publication-of-local-departures-from-owner-facing-recovery-packet-defaults.md`](publication-of-local-departures-from-owner-facing-recovery-packet-defaults.md). That surface already answers:

- when recurring same-basket packet upkeep may use a tiny pre-declared maintenance envelope at all;
- which starter baskets are admissible as `PME-CALENDAR`, `PME-ROUTE`, and `PME-LABEL`;
- the shell invariants, edit cap, and breach triggers that keep batching honest.

This document answers one narrower question:

- **which of those envelope baskets actually travel once the archive asks for a portable default rather than an owner-local notice?**

## The three portability tiers

The archive now distinguishes three portability tiers.

| Tier | Meaning | When the archive uses it | What it refuses |
|---|---|---|---|
| `PMT-BRANCH` | the envelope may harden across one named recovery branch regardless of authority-family variant | the basket changes wording or presentation but does not change who owns the path, when meaningful review happens, or what the packet can do | fake portability built on one family's local calendar or routing habits |
| `PMT-FAMILY` | the envelope may harden only inside one named authority family | the basket stays truthful only when the same owner family controls the same review and expiry semantics | pretending record, safety, and route owners share one calendar truth |
| `PMT-LOCAL` | the envelope remains owner-local even if the basket repeats often | the basket too easily changes challengeability, first contact, or regime-specific shell truth | endless local residue with no publication discipline |

The archive is intentionally asymmetric here:

> a basket must earn portability by preserving the same packet truth, not merely by looking administratively familiar.

## Five tests for whether an envelope basket truly travels

A starter envelope basket may harden above owner-local notice only when all five tests hold.

1. **same-semantic-shell test** — the basket preserves the same branch tag, same owner meaning, same non-override statement, and the same field-inclusion truth rather than merely the same topic area.
2. **review/expiry stability test** — the basket keeps the same or earlier meaningful review point and the same or shorter truthful expiry across the candidate reuse set.
3. **challenge-route stability test** — the basket leaves the learner with the same or stronger explanation/challenge route, including the same first meaningful human contact where that matters.
4. **no-hidden-merits test** — the basket does not import family-conditioned field drift, queue cues, priority implications, or other material that would make the packet do more than shell upkeep.
5. **cross-regime truth test** — the basket remains truthful across the proposed reuse scope without leaning on one office's local law, safeguarding stage, partner inventory, or welfare practice.

Failing any one of these keeps the basket at `PMT-LOCAL`.

## The archive's starter portability answers

The archive now gives a first narrow answer for each starter basket.

### `PME-LABEL` usually hardens as `PMT-BRANCH`

`PME-LABEL` may usually harden one step further into a **branch-portable maintenance envelope**.

Why it travels:

- label/plain-language harmonisation of already-permitted floor or family-conditioned fields often changes wording, ordering, or public explanation without changing packet power;
- the truth of the packet usually still turns on the already-governed branch and authority family, not on the local wording habit;
- the shell invariants are comparatively easy to audit because field logic, owner, review anchor, expiry, and challenge route all stay fixed.

What must still stay out:

- new field-inclusion logic;
- family-conditioned scope change;
- queue cues, likely-outcome phrasing, or merits narratives;
- any label change that weakens learner visibility or makes the challenge route harder to find.

The archive's starter default is therefore:

> `PME-LABEL` may usually harden as `PMT-BRANCH` when it harmonises only wording, ordering, or public-facing plain-language for already-live packet fields and leaves the rest of the shell intact.

### `PME-CALENDAR` hardens only as `PMT-FAMILY` for `AF-RECORD` and `AF-ROUTE`

`PME-CALENDAR` does **not** get one cross-family portable default. It now splits.

#### `PME-CALENDAR` for `AF-RECORD`

Record-owner calendar housekeeping may harden as `PMT-FAMILY` when it preserves the same or earlier meaningful review point and the same or shorter truthful expiry for the same record-governed packet path.

Why it travels that far:

- record owners often share a stable calendar distinction between packet housekeeping and real standing/progression treatment;
- the packet's review-anchor and expiry language can often be cleaned up without changing which office decides or what the packet can decide.

What stays out:

- later real review points;
- hidden carry-forward or auto-renewal;
- calendar wording that quietly changes who owns the next record decision.

#### `PME-CALENDAR` for `AF-ROUTE`

Route-case calendar housekeeping may also harden as `PMT-FAMILY`, but only when the same owner boundary and same challenge ladder remain live and the calendar change does not imply slot, referral, or eligibility priority.

Why it travels only this far:

- route systems often do have recurring checkpoint or callback housekeeping;
- but those calendars stay truthful only inside one owner family, because partner inventory, statutory windows, and dispatch practice vary too much across regimes.

What stays out:

- changes that imply queue position, exposure entitlement, or partner commitment;
- later first meaningful review points;
- calendar drift that weakens the explanation route.

#### `PME-CALENDAR` for `AF-SAFETY` stays local

Safety/supervisor calendar housekeeping remains `PMT-LOCAL` in the starter canon.

Why it stays local:

- safety, welfare, placement, and safeguarding calendars are too tightly coupled to live exposure judgment, duty stages, or incident handling to claim one portable family rule yet;
- the same wording change can be harmless in one regime and materially delay protective review in another.

### `PME-ROUTE` stays `PMT-LOCAL`

`PME-ROUTE` remains owner-local in the starter canon.

Why it does not yet travel:

- route/contact upkeep too easily changes the first meaningful human contact even when the nominal owner stays the same;
- a changed inbox, portal, or first-contact channel can weaken challengeability, hide queue dependence, or silently move the learner into a thinner explanation environment;
- the basket looks shell-like, but its truth often depends on local queue and channel design rather than on the shared packet branch alone.

The archive therefore treats `PME-ROUTE` as a basket that may be honest for bounded local batching but has **not** yet earned portable default status.

## What a promoted portable envelope default must still publish

When a basket hardens above owner-local notice, the service should publish only six extra things:

1. the portability tier (`PMT-BRANCH` or `PMT-FAMILY`);
2. the exact branch or authority-family scope it covers;
3. the inherited shell invariants that may not weaken;
4. the inherited edit-cap and short-window rule from the starter envelope canon;
5. the breach signals that immediately collapse portability back into ordinary packet rules;
6. the residue that still remains local.

This document does **not** yet create a richer numeric portability code. The starter canon simply inherits the existing low-edit-cap / short-window discipline from the maintenance-envelope rule.

## What still remains permanently local even after this promotion pass

Three kinds of material still stop at `PMT-LOCAL` even when one starter basket hardens.

1. **family-conditioned timing calendars** whose truth depends on local law, safeguarding stage, placement duty, or partner inventory;
2. **first-contact or route-channel design** that changes the practical challenge path even when the office label stays the same;
3. **any basket whose wording change also changes field logic, packet visibility, implied outcome, or owner-boundary truth.**

Portable envelope defaults are therefore not a permission slip for broader packet redesign. They are only a statement that a very small class of shell upkeep really travels.

## Why this counted as a real archive gain

The archive already knew when a service may use a named maintenance envelope at all. But it still lacked the next tighter answer:

- which envelope baskets really deserve promotion beyond owner-local notice;
- and which only look portable because the same administrative topic recurs.

This revision now answers that narrower question with one small portability layer:

- `PME-LABEL` usually hardens as `PMT-BRANCH`;
- `PME-CALENDAR` splits, hardening only as `PMT-FAMILY` for `AF-RECORD` and `AF-ROUTE`;
- `PME-CALENDAR` for `AF-SAFETY` and all `PME-ROUTE` cases remain `PMT-LOCAL` for now.

That is a real operating gain because it blocks three opposite mistakes at once:

- keeping obviously shell-preserving label harmonisation permanently local for no reason;
- pretending all recurring calendar housekeeping means the same thing across record, safety, and route owners;
- and letting route/contact upkeep quietly inherit portable status before the archive can honestly say that challengeability still travels.

## Current archive bet

The archive's current best guess is that **portable pre-declared packet maintenance envelopes should remain extremely rare: label/plain-language harmonisation usually travels at the branch level, calendar housekeeping only travels at the authority-family level for record and route owners, and route/contact upkeep still remains owner-local until the archive has a better truth-preserving channel rule.**

That claim is now canon, but still live. The next narrower question is which of these promoted portable envelope defaults, if any, can safely carry shared numeric edit-cap and maintenance-window bands across their reuse scope, and which should keep those quantitative limits local even when the basket name itself travels. See `OQ-0035`.
