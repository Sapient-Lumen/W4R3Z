# First exam-family child splits for still-local hot decoding and final-writing branches

This document closes the next narrower governance gap for the two hot direct-production branches that were still left as one local/manual blob after the archive's first portability judgment.

The archive had already made two asymmetric calls:

- hot no-executor symbolic procedure could harden **a little**, but only inside published no-calculator / no-executor families; and
- hot decoding plus hot final writing should still remain local or manual-review rather than inheriting one portable row.

That was honest, but still incomplete. It left two recurring residue problems unsaid:

- **reading-family collapse** — foundational reading-construct checks and English-language-proficiency reading surfaces get talked about as if they were the same hot decoding branch merely because both care about direct reading performance;
- **writing-surface collapse** — fully digital timed free-response writing and checkpointed long-form paper-plus-defense work get talked about as if they were one hot writing row simply because both are consequence-bearing and student-authored;
- **calculator-row impatience** — once the archive names tool-family-local mathematics residue, institutions start asking whether calculator-expected quantitative families already deserve a reusable hot row just because they are also timed or proctored.

Current official signals support a tighter child map. NCEO says accessibility policies are state-determined and often vary by content area; WIDA says accommodations for ACCESS-family testing are selected to produce **valid assessment results** and that state-specific policy remains relevant. College Board now publishes a fully digital AP English Language exam in Bluebook with a fixed timed free-response section, while its current accommodations pages still list typing permission, human readers, and writers/scribes as distinct approved supports. College Board also publishes a different AP Capstone family where the live authorship claim is carried by academic papers, presentations, oral defenses, teacher checkpoints, and authenticity affirmations rather than one timed end-of-course written surface. Together those signals support a tighter archive rule: the first honest child split for still-local hot decoding is **general content-assessment reading construct** versus **ELP/ACCESS-family validity**, the first honest child split for hot final writing is **timed digital free-response authorship** versus **checkpointed paper-plus-defense authorship**, and no calculator-expected quantitative family yet deserves a reusable hot row of its own. See `B201`.

## Small exam-family residue grammar

| Code | Meaning | Default archive action |
|---|---|---|
| `EF1-CONTENT-READ` | hot direct reading/decoding in a content-assessment family where the reading construct itself is being directly tested and support policy varies by state and content area | keep family-local/manual; do not borrow ELP logic |
| `EF2-ELP-READ` | hot direct reading inside an English-language-proficiency family where validity and state-specific ELP policy define the support boundary | keep family-local/manual; do not borrow ordinary content-reading logic |
| `EF3-DIGITAL-WRITE` | hot final writing in a timed fully digital free-response family with explicit device, timing, and approved recording-access rules | publish a named exam-family child row; keep broader portability open |
| `EF4-CHECKPOINT-WRITE` | hot authorship in a through-course paper / presentation / oral-defense family with checkpoints and authenticity affirmations | publish a named family child row; do not treat it as the same surface as timed digital writing |
| `EF5-CALC-EXPECTED` | hotter calculator-expected quantitative family where device use is part of the published exam surface | keep local/manual; no reusable hot row yet |

The archive is deliberately not publishing age-year, state-by-state, or test-brand micro-rows here. It is only naming the first truthful family boundaries.

## Row notes

### `SR-READ-FOUND-01A2A` — content-assessment hot decoding stays separate from ELP logic

This child row covers hotter direct reading/decoding where the reading construct itself is being tested in a general content-assessment family. NCEO's current accessibility overview is enough to show why the archive should split here before hardening: accessibility policy is state-determined, often varies by content area, and may classify supports differently across assessments. That means the archive should not talk about these cases as if one generic hot decoding row already travels. See `B201`.

### `SR-READ-FOUND-01A2B` — ELP/ACCESS-family hot decoding is a different validity problem

This child row covers hotter direct reading inside English-language-proficiency assessment families. WIDA's current manual keeps the frame explicit: accommodations are selected for individual English learners to produce valid results, and state-specific policy still matters. That is close enough to a named family to deserve a child row, but not yet broad enough to justify inherited hardening across jurisdictions or across non-ELP reading families. See `B201`.

### `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B1` — timed digital rhetoric exams deserve a named child row

College Board's current AP English Language exam page now makes the family shape public: the exam is fully digital in Bluebook, the free-response section is timed, and the responses are submitted automatically. At the same time, current AP accommodations pages still keep typing permission, human-reader support, and writer/scribe support explicit rather than collapsing them into a generic keyboarding assumption. That is enough for the archive to stop treating timed digital final writing as one undifferentiated local blob, but still not enough to let the row travel outside the named family without further work. See `B201`.

### `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B2` — checkpointed paper-plus-defense authorship is not the same hot writing surface

AP Capstone now gives the archive a clear contrasting family. AP Research is assessed through a 4,000–5,000 word paper plus presentation and oral defense, and AP Capstone policy requires teacher checkpoints and authenticity affirmations while still allowing bounded AI use for exploration, grammar, and tone. That is a real consequence-bearing student-authorship family, but it is not the same proof surface as timed digital free-response writing. The archive now publishes that distinction rather than laundering both families into one hotter writing row. See `B201`.

## Calculator-expected quantitative families — explicit "not yet"

The archive is now making one tighter mathematics answer explicit too: **no calculator-expected quantitative family yet deserves a reusable hot row of its own**.

The archive already published `SR-MATH-PROC-01A2B` and `SR-MATH-PROC-01A2C` as local/manual residue outside the thin no-executor symbolic family. Nothing in the current official signals is yet strong enough to say that AP Statistics-like, AP Physics-like, or other calculator-expected quantitative families share one sufficiently stable hotter construct, access posture, and interpretation burden for inherited publication. For now, the honest answer remains: named local/manual handling, not a premature reusable row.

## What the archive is now making explicit

### Still-local does not mean still-undifferentiated

The archive is now publishing child rows even where portability has not improved yet. That matters because the real governance failure was not only over-hardening; it was also leaving unlike hot families inside one vague local bucket.

### Reading now has a first family fork before hardening

Hot reading/decoding now splits first by **content-assessment reading construct** versus **ELP/ACCESS-family validity**. That is a more truthful first fork than age band alone because the official support logic already diverges there.

### Hot writing now has a first surface-family fork before hardening

Hot final writing now splits first by **timed digital free-response** versus **checkpointed paper-plus-defense authorship**. The archive no longer lets one family masquerade as the other merely because both involve student-owned writing under consequence.

### Calculator-expected math remains a live question, but not a published reusable row

The archive now answers the pressure directly: no calculator-expected quantitative hot row is justified yet. That is a decision, not an omission.

## What the archive is **not** doing yet

The archive is still refusing four wider moves:

- it is **not** hardening `SR-READ-FOUND-01A2A` across states or across reading-content families;
- it is **not** hardening `SR-READ-FOUND-01A2B` across ELP jurisdictions or vendors;
- it is **not** treating `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B1` and `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B2` as one portable writing row;
- it is **not** publishing a reusable hot row for calculator-expected quantitative families.

## Current archive bet

The archive's current best guess is that the first truthful narrowing move for the still-local hot decoding and hot final-writing branches is an **exam-family split, not immediate portability**.

`SR-READ-FOUND-01A2A` and `SR-READ-FOUND-01A2B` now separate general content-assessment reading construct from ELP/ACCESS-family validity. `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B1` and `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B2` now separate timed digital free-response writing from checkpointed paper-plus-defense authorship. And the archive now states plainly that calculator-expected quantitative families still do **not** deserve a reusable hot row of their own.

That claim is now canon, but still live. The next narrower question it opened is now closed by [`first-shared-publication-fields-and-protected-access-defaults-for-named-hot-exam-family-children.md`](first-shared-publication-fields-and-protected-access-defaults-for-named-hot-exam-family-children.md), which adds the first tiny shared publication shell for all four named hot family children and a narrower record-only protected-access default for timed digital free-response writing. The live issue is now one step tighter again: which of those new family shells deserve their first modality-specific child split or fallback-default layer without overclaiming portability. See `OQ-0005`.
