# First authoritative-artifact continuity defaults for hot exam phase bands

This document closes the archive's next narrower governance gap about **where continuity actually lives once a hot exam route has already crossed its protected start / exposure boundary**.

Three failure modes matter here:

- **digital-state laundering** — every started digital route gets treated as if it carries the same resumable session logic, even when some continuity lives only while the exam is still active and some continuity disappears once the active session has ended;
- **paper-artifact amnesia** — hybrid or paper routes get discussed as if their continuity lived in software state, when the authoritative response artifact is actually a labeled physical booklet that must stay in secure custody and be returned for scoring;
- **portfolio-defense collapse** — checkpointed paper-plus-defense work gets treated like either a resumable timed exam or a purely local conversation, when the authoritative continuity is actually split between a submitted portfolio artifact and retained local presentation / oral-defense records.

Current official signals support a tighter answer. Bluebook now says that if a student's device fails during testing, staff should exit and restart first, then perform a device swap if needed, and the student resumes testing on the new device only after proctor approval. The current digital AP proctor guide then says fully digital AP responses are automatically submitted at the end of the exam, while the current coordinator manual adds the complementary limit: if answers remain unsubmitted after the exam, students have 3 calendar days to submit them **using the original testing device**, and device swap must not be used after the exam ends. The current hybrid-digital overview and digital AP proctor guide say hybrid digital AP students view questions in Bluebook but handwrite free-response answers in paper booklets that are returned for scoring, while the current accommodations guide says hybrid digital exams have unique accommodation considerations precisely because they use both Bluebook and paper free-response booklets. Current AP Research materials then keep a different continuity shape visible again: only the Academic Paper is uploaded to the AP Digital Portfolio, while Presentation and Oral Defense videos must be retained locally for one academic year, and authenticity / checkpoint affirmations still matter. Together those signals support a narrower archive rule: **the next truthful divergence inside the hot exam phase bands is authoritative-artifact continuity, not family-wide minute bands.** During an active fully digital Bluebook session, same-attempt continuity may travel as resumable session state; after the active exam ends, unresolved digital submission can become original-device-locked; hybrid digital continuity must publish paper-booklet custody as part of the authoritative artifact; and checkpointed paper-plus-defense continuity must publish portfolio-paper plus retained local-defense record rather than pretending the work lives in one resumable exam state. See `B206`.

## `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B1` — active digital continuity is real, but it expires when the active exam ends

The archive can now publish a narrower started-session truth for the hot timed-digital-writing family.

`AC1-RESUMABLE-SESSION` means that while the active digital exam is still live, the same attempt may remain the same attempt through platform-supported resume handling. Bluebook now says that after a device failure staff should try restart steps first, then perform a device swap if needed; the student opens Bluebook on the new device, chooses **Resume Testing**, and the proctor must approve the swap. That is enough for an active-session continuity token. See `B206`.

But the archive is making the complementary boundary explicit too.

`AC2-ORIGINAL-DEVICE-POSTEND` means that once the exam itself has ended, unresolved submission no longer inherits the looser started-session logic. The current coordinator manual says students have 3 calendar days after the exam to submit their answers using the **original testing device**, and the Device Swap feature must not be used after the exam has ended. So the archive now refuses to talk as if all digital continuity is the same thing. Active-session recovery and post-end submission continuity are different bands. See `B206`.

This does **not** settle writer/scribe substitute staffing. It settles the narrower artifact truth: the authoritative digital attempt may be resumable while live, and then become original-device-bound once the live exam has ended.

## Hybrid digital routes — digital delivery does not eliminate paper-artifact continuity

The archive now adds the comparator it needed in order not to overgeneralize from Bluebook.

`AC3-PAPER-BOOKLET-CUSTODY` means that a route may still use Bluebook and yet place its authoritative free-response continuity in a paper artifact. The current hybrid digital AP overview says students complete multiple-choice questions and view free-response questions in Bluebook, but handwrite free-response answers in paper booklets that are returned for scoring. The current proctor guide repeats that those free-response booklets are returned for scoring, and the current coordinator manual says proctors collect all free-response booklets at the end of the exam and return them to secure storage until they are sent back to the AP Program. The accommodations guide then makes the governance implication explicit: hybrid digital exams have unique accommodation considerations because students use both Bluebook and paper free-response booklets. See `B206`.

That is enough for one tighter archive rule.

The archive should now refuse the shortcut phrase **digital exam continuity** unless it also names the authoritative artifact. For some digital families the continuity token is active Bluebook state. For hybrid digital families the continuity token for free-response work is also paper-booklet custody.

The archive is **not** yet claiming one portable rule for reconstructing interrupted paper-booklet work, transcribing scratch-paper remnants, or replacing absent human recorders midstream. Those remain local or owner-specific. What now travels is the thinner truth that the booklet itself is part of the authoritative continuity path.

## `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B2` — checkpointed paper-plus-defense work lives in a split record, not a resumable exam state

The checkpointed paper-plus-defense family already had `FB2-PRESENT-DELIVERY`, `FB3-DEFENSE-NO-PREVIEW`, and `EX4-DEFENSE-SEAL`. It now gets one narrower artifact-continuity layer.

`AC4-PORTFOLIO-LOCAL-RECORD` means:

- the authoritative written artifact is the submitted portfolio paper rather than a live timed-session state;
- the authoritative live-performance continuity also depends on retained local presentation / oral-defense records where the programme requires them;
- authenticity / checkpoint affirmations remain part of whether the work keeps its official standing.

Current AP Research materials say only the Academic Paper is uploaded to the AP Digital Portfolio, while Presentation and Oral Defense videos must be kept for one academic year because College Board may request them for review; current AP Capstone policy also keeps teacher authenticity affirmations and checkpoint review explicit. That is enough for a split artifact continuity token. See `B206`.

This matters because interruption truth here should not be phrased as if the family had one resumable session state waiting to be reopened. Its continuity is already distributed across a submitted paper artifact, a bounded live defense event, and retained local records.

## What the archive is now making explicit

### Continuity attaches to the authoritative artifact, not just to the route label

A route can be called digital in more than one way. That is no longer enough. The archive now asks whether continuity lives in active platform state, in a post-end original-device submission path, in a paper booklet, or in a split portfolio-plus-local-record stack.

### Active-session continuity and post-end continuity are different truths

The archive now treats resume-on-swap during an active fully digital exam as a real continuity token, while refusing to extend that token past the end of the exam when the owner's rules revert to original-device submission.

### Hybrid digital and through-course families now block false generalization from Bluebook

A family can use Bluebook and still place the authoritative scored artifact in a paper booklet. Another family can avoid Bluebook entirely and instead bind continuity to a portfolio paper plus retained local defense record. Those differences now matter canonically.

## What the archive is **not** doing yet

The archive is still refusing three wider moves:

- it is **not** publishing one universal minute-band for any of these continuity tokens;
- it is **not** publishing one portable started-session substitute-owner rule for absent readers, writers/scribes, or interpreters;
- it is **not** claiming that every digitally delivered route inherits the same recovery or post-end submission logic.

## Current archive bet

The archive's current best guess is that the next truthful move after protected-exposure bands is **authoritative-artifact continuity before minute-band standardization**.

That means:

- hot timed digital writing now publishes active-session resumability separately from post-end original-device submission;
- hybrid digital families now block the lazy inference that all digital routes are software-state-only continuity paths;
- checkpointed paper-plus-defense authorship now publishes portfolio-plus-local-record continuity rather than one resumable session myth;
- and the archive still refuses one universal interruption-minute or substitute-owner package.

That claim is now canon. The next narrower question is no longer whether continuity lives in different authoritative artifacts at all; it is whether any of these newly named continuity tokens now justify family-specific minute bands, post-end reporting clocks, or started-session substitute-owner continuity rules without overclaiming portability. See `OQ-0005`.
