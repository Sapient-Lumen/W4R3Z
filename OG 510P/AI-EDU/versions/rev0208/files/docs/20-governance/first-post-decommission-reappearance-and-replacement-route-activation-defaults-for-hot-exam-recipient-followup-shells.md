# First post-decommission reappearance and replacement-route activation defaults for hot-exam recipient followup shells

The archive can now publish one still smaller operating layer for the hottest exam-like learner-request routes.

It can already say:

- when a migrated successor route later drifts and needs proof refresh rather than automatic proof carryover;
- when repeated successor-route drift crosses a stability threshold;
- how an opened successor-route stability posture runs, proves repair, splits, decommissions, migrates, or exits; and
- how decommissioned-route residue or no-active-route residue can remain visible without becoming permanent migration surveillance.

That is still not enough.

After a successor-route stability posture exits, the most confusing facts often arrive late. A retired provider path can reappear in search, a portal, a screenshot, an archive capture, or a recipient workflow. A replacement route can fail its first visible cycle, change prerequisites, or lose its named owner. A decommissioned path can receive new source-owner proof, redirect proof, noindex proof, provider proof, or recipient proof. A no-active-route residue can suddenly expose a current owner or action path. A temporary block or outdated-content request can expire and let the old result become eligible again. A local source can stay corrected while an external search result, image result, cache, custom search, archive time slice, mirror, or downstream record tells a different story. A learner can also be told to refile into a replacement route even though the only changed fact is owner-side route activation.

This document adds one thing only:

- a **tiny post-decommission reappearance / replacement-route activation field set** for hot-exam recipient followup shells after `YS0-YS4` has already exited a successor-route stability posture as ordinary successor status, replacement-route migration, decommissioned-route residue, no-active-route residue, split-surface operation, or minimized local memory.

That means the archive now asks a different question than before. It no longer asks only **how a threshold-opened successor-route stability posture operates or exits**. It now asks **what happens after that exit if the old route reappears, the replacement route fails, decommissioned residue gains current proof, or no-active-route residue exposes a current path — without treating decommission as permanent failure, replacement as total fresh-start amnesia, route revival as an automatic all-surface reopen, or old proof as a public transition dossier**.

Current official signals support a deliberately narrow answer. Google Search Console's Removals tool remains property-owner, exact-URL, current/expired-history, cancellation, temporary-removal, and source-side permanent-method based; it also says temporary removals can expire and pages can become eligible to appear again unless permanent site-side action is taken. Google's Refresh Outdated Content route is exact page/image URL, changed/gone-content, queue/status, and 180-day expiry based; it does not remove unchanged live pages and does not create a global source-side disappearance certificate. Google's owner-facing removal guidance separates temporary Search removal from source-side removal, login protection, noindex, or other permanent methods. WiscWeb separates source-site decommissioning, primary-contact follow-up, campus search/lookahead/directory handling, normal-hours constraints, and major-search-engine non-control. Princeton separates local content deletion, document/media cleanup, cache, Google/Bing/custom-search exclusion, access control, and Wayback persistence. Rowan separates Cascade CMS, Web Services support, Siteimprove/accessibility monitoring, content-audit/redesign work, contributor responsibility, and accommodation routes rather than one central route-activation desk. Internet Archive requires URL, time-period, control-period, and review context before exclusion and gives no guaranteed outcome before review. Bing distinguishes temporary blocks, 90-day expiry, cached/outdated-content handling, extension or permanent-removal prerequisites, and source-side methods such as 404/410 or noindex. Together those signals support a tighter archive rule: **the next truthful portability gain here is one tiny post-decommission / replacement-activation layer for trigger, affected surface, current owner/proof, learner action-now, clock continuity, disposition, minimization, and reopen triggers — but not one permanent decommission-failure label, replacement-route fresh-start fiction, all-surface revival certificate, public old-proof dossier, or automatic learner refile rule.** See `B259`.

## Small field set for post-decommission reappearance and replacement-route activation

| Code | Meaning | Default archive action |
|---|---|---|
| `YP0-NO-DECOMMISSION-PERMANENT-FAILURE-REPLACEMENT-AMNESIA-OR-AUTOMATIC-ALL-SURFACE-REOPEN` | a later post-exit fact does not make the old route permanently failed, the replacement route memoryless, or every surface reopened | keep the new fact tied to its named surface, owner, route, proof object, and learner dependency; do not widen it to every URL, archive, provider, recipient, or future copy |
| `YP1-PUBLISH-POST-DECOMMISSION-TRIGGER-AND-AFFECTED-SURFACE` | the shell must say what actually changed after decommission, replacement migration, or no-active-route residue | publish whether the trigger is old-route reappearance, temporary-block expiry, search/outdated-status expiry, replacement-route failure, replacement-owner change, new source proof, new provider proof, new recipient/record proof, archive capture, redirect/canonical drift, or no-active-route owner discovery |
| `YP2-PUBLISH-ACTIVATION-OWNER-ROUTE-AND-PROOF-BASIS` | activation needs current proof from the owner or route that can actually carry the next action | label the proof as old-owner revival, replacement-owner current proof, provider-route proof, source/CMS proof, redirect/canonical/noindex proof, search-provider state, archive/platform review, recipient confirmation, official-record correction, accessibility/remediation proof, decommission notice superseded, or no-active-route owner proof |
| `YP3-PUBLISH-LEARNER-ACTION-CLOCK-AND-MEMORY-CONTINUITY` | the shell must say whether the learner has to act now, which clock applies, and what prior timely action still protects them | make action prospective and route-native; preserve prior timely filings, proof, and no-fault posture unless the current route itself lawfully requires a new learner act; do not treat owner activation work as learner abandonment |
| `YP4-PUBLISH-ACTIVATION-DISPOSITION-MINIMIZATION-AND-REOPEN-TRIGGERS` | the shell must exit, split, reactivate, stay residue, or reopen a stability posture with a visible minimization rule | publish revived-route active, replacement-route active, replacement failed and split, replacement migration reopened, successor-stability posture reopened, proof refresh opened, decommissioned-route residue retained, no-active-route residue retained, ordinary replacement restored, or minimized local memory with reopen only on material change |

## What this layer now makes portable

Hot-exam recipient followup shells may now publish one tiny `YP0-YP4` layer after `YS0-YS4` when the archive needs to say not merely that **a successor-route stability posture exited**, but that **a post-exit fact has appeared and either activates the old route, activates a replacement route, reopens proof refresh, keeps decommission/no-active-route residue, or stays minimized**.

That means a shell may now truthfully say things like:

- `the old provider path reappeared in search after the temporary block expired; reopen only the affected provider/search proof, preserve prior timely action, and do not label the replacement route failed unless it also fails`;
- `the replacement route is current and named, so the old decommissioned path remains minimized residue; learner action is prospective on the replacement route only`;
- `no-active-route residue now has a current owner; reopen route migration or proof refresh from the current notice date, not retroactively from the abandoned-route date`;
- `a recipient portal failed while the source/search replacement stayed stable; split recipient action from public-source/search/archive proof`; or
- `a Wayback capture resurfaced after decommission; keep archive residue time-slice-specific and do not reopen the live replacement route without a current owner or learner dependency`.

It still may not pretend:

- that route decommission proves permanent route failure;
- that a replacement route begins with total amnesia about prior timely proof, no-fault status, or repair limits;
- that old-route reappearance automatically reopens every source, search, archive, recipient, and official-record surface;
- that a replacement-route owner can impose a retroactive clock before current notice;
- that unrelated mirrors, screenshots, or expired history rows prove current route activation;
- that sensitive learner proof should return to public view merely because a route revived;
- that no-active-route residue was false just because a later owner appeared; or
- that a learner must refile into a dead path to preserve rights that prior timely action already preserved.

## Why this matters for the larger archive

The archive's education-with-AI program is not only about forcing route owners to keep current proof visible. It is also about preventing route mechanics from laundering blame onto learners when official infrastructure moves.

This layer matters because decommission and replacement create two opposite fictions. One fiction says a decommissioned route is permanently failed and every later reappearance proves broad owner fault. The other fiction says a replacement route starts from zero and can ask the learner to prove again what the old route already accepted or what the owner already failed to carry. Both are wrong. The point here is narrower: publish **what post-exit trigger appeared**, **which surface it affects**, **which owner/proof now exists**, **whether the learner actually has to act**, **which clock is prospective**, **what prior timely action remains protected**, and **how the shell minimizes or reopens only the affected path**. Everything else remains route-native casework, local-only retention, privacy-aware minimization, ordinary search/cache lag, or no-active-route residue.

## Boundary conditions

This layer should not be used when only one of the following is true:

- `YS0-YS4` has not exited a stability posture, so ordinary `YD0-YD4` proof refresh, `YR0-YR4` thresholding, or `YS0-YS4` stability operation is still enough;
- the replacement route is still inside its own published review, implementation, reflection, temporary-block, or owner-response window;
- the only signal is ordinary search/cache/crawl lag, status-history aging, or a screenshot with no current owner, action path, proof object, recipient dependency, or route-native expiry;
- the fact belongs only to a different URL, owner, route, learner, recipient, archive time slice, image family, platform, or official-record field and does not affect the published shell;
- a protected accommodation, privacy, safety, records, legal, provider, or appeal process already controls activation, proof, notice, or retention;
- the old route is still formally decommissioned and no current owner or replacement path exists, in which case publish decommissioned-route or no-active-route residue rather than activation; or
- a current route names a specific restart, reactivation, appeal, replacement, or expiry rule that should govern instead of a portable activation layer.

In those cases, publish `no post-decommission trigger`, `ordinary route-native window applies`, `ordinary search or cache lag`, `unrelated copy only`, `protected local-only route`, `no active route`, `decommissioned-route residue`, `no proof yet`, `fresh proof refresh required`, or `none inherited` rather than treating every post-exit fact as all-surface revival, permanent decommission failure, or learner fault.

## What this closes

This closes `FT-0145`.

The archive no longer stops at "successor-route stability operation exited". It can now publish a minimal post-decommission / replacement-route activation layer: the trigger, affected surface, current owner/proof basis, learner action-now truth, clock and prior-proof continuity, activation disposition, minimization, and reopen triggers when a retired route reappears, a replacement route fails, a decommissioned route gains proof, or no-active-route residue exposes a current path.

It also opens one narrower seam.

After a post-decommission or replacement-route activation layer has opened a revived route, replacement route, proof refresh, split, or reopened stability posture, the archive may still need a tiny first-cycle activation drift / route-choice conflict layer for cases where the revived old route and replacement route disagree, the first replacement cycle drifts, or a learner is offered two paths with different clocks — without making every activation a new standing watch, every old-route revival a replacement failure, or every route-choice conflict learner fault.
