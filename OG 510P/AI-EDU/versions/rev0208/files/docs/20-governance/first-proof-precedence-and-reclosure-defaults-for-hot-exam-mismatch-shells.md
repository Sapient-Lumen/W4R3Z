# First proof-precedence and reclosure defaults for hot-exam mismatch shells

This document closes the archive's next narrower governance gap after **proof-mismatch and reopen defaults**.

Three overclaims matter here:

- **first-seen-proof supremacy** — whichever status page, room count, or local note appeared first gets treated as if it keeps governing even after the route's owner-of-record artifact says otherwise;
- **automatic reclosure fantasy** — once any mismatch narrows, the archive acts as if the whole shell has returned to ordinary closure without checking whether the route's actual official closure token has come back; and
- **local-support substitution** — a counted headcount, narrative reassurance, retained local video, or copies of interim work get treated as if they replace the route's official closure leg rather than merely supporting later review.

Current official signals support a tighter answer. Bluebook's current in-school testing page says that if Test Day Toolkit shows some students without **Submitted** status, coordinators should check Bluebook because the Toolkit status can be out of date; AP answers must be submitted within **3 days**; students must use the **same device** they tested on; and shared devices should be protected until submission is complete. Bluebook's current answer-resolution page says students reclose the ordinary digital shell only when, on that same device, they either reach the **Congratulations** page after clicking **Submit Answers** or see the exam on the **Past** tab with **“Your answers have been submitted”**. Its current pending-submission help page repeats that after time runs out, the device-swap feature must not be used and answers must be submitted from the device the student finished testing on. Current hybrid digital AP pages say students handwrite free responses in paper booklets, place an **AP ID label** on the booklet cover to connect that booklet to registration information, and return those booklets for scoring, while the current AP return page says all secure exam materials must be returned promptly and received by the AP Program by **June 1**. Current AP Digital Portfolio and AP Capstone policy pages keep the portfolio route split visible again: AP Capstone students submit written components as final by **April 30, 2026, 11:59 p.m. ET**; teachers submit presentation scores and checkpoint affirmations by **May 10, 2026, 11:59 p.m. ET**; students must complete checkpoints successfully to receive a score; teachers must affirm authenticity; an exam must be ordered for the work to be scored; College Board may request interim work for review; and presentation/oral-defense videos remain retained local artifacts for at least one academic year. Together those signals support a narrower archive rule: **the hot-exam mismatch shells may now share one tiny proof-precedence / reclosure layer, but they still do not justify one universal audit authority, one universal late-cure-to-ordinary rule, or one universal superseding packet.** See `B212`.

## Small grammar for proof-precedence / reclosure defaults

| Code | Meaning | Default archive action |
|---|---|---|
| `PR0-NO-UNIVERSAL-AUDIT-AUTHORITY` | no one universal precedence-decider or universal reclosure authority travels across these hot shells | keep precedence and reclosure shell-specific rather than inventing one shared reconciliation sovereign |
| `PR1-OWNER-OF-RECORD-PRECEDENCE` | when official signals conflict, the route's owner-of-record artifact or platform governs temporary shell truth | prefer the route-specific operative record over a stale summary layer or generic room narrative |
| `PR2-RE-CLOSE-ONLY-ON-ORDINARY-OFFICIAL-TOKEN` | a reopened shell returns to ordinary closure only when the route's own ordinary official closure token comes back | do not mark the shell closed merely because troubleshooting, recount, or local reassurance occurred |
| `PR3-CURE-ONLY-RESTORES-THE-CURED-LEG` | curing one failed leg does not silently cure every other open leg in a split shell | keep unresolved legs visible as partial closure or upstream review until their own required proof returns |
| `PR4-LOCAL-SUPPORT-CAN-TRIGGER-REVIEW-NOT-REPLACE-CLOSURE` | local counts, retained local media, or interim-work copies may support review but do not replace the route's ordinary official closure token | use local support to investigate, not to fabricate reclosure |

## `PR1` — precedence belongs to the route's owner-of-record signal, not the first summary you happen to see

`PR1-OWNER-OF-RECORD-PRECEDENCE` is the archive's smallest anti-staleness rule.

Its point is not that every shell has the same governing system. Its point is that each shell has an owner-of-record signal that outranks a thinner summary layer:

- for post-end digital submission, Bluebook on the same testing device outranks a stale Test Day Toolkit summary when closure truth is in conflict;
- for hybrid digital custody, the AP-ID-linked booklet outranks a generic room-level count or verbal assurance because the booklet is the scored artifact; and
- for portfolio/local-record continuity, the AP Digital Portfolio plus required teacher scoring / checkpoint / eligibility legs outrank a local belief that the work is "basically done."

So `PR1` is not a universal hierarchy. It is a route-specific precedence rule against summary-layer drift.

## `PR2` — reclosure must use the route's ordinary token, not a nearby substitute

`PR2-RE-CLOSE-ONLY-ON-ORDINARY-OFFICIAL-TOKEN` is the archive's smallest anti-fantasy reclosure rule.

Across the three named shells, the token is different:

- for digital AP submission, it is the ordinary Bluebook submitted token — the **Congratulations** page after submit or **“Your answers have been submitted”** on **Past**;
- for hybrid digital custody, it is the identified booklet's return to ordinary counted secure custody / return-for-scoring truth; and
- for checkpointed portfolio-plus-local-record continuity, it is completion of the missing official portfolio-side leg, not merely the existence of a local retained artifact.

That is why the archive still refuses one universal reclosure form.

## `PR3` — partial cure stays partial

`PR3-CURE-ONLY-RESTORES-THE-CURED-LEG` is deliberately modest.

It stops three quiet overclaims:

- a digital shell does not become globally closed merely because the submitted token appears if some other required review leg remains open;
- a hybrid shell does not become globally closed merely because a room count now balances if the identified booklet link or return-chain truth is still defective; and
- a portfolio shell does not become globally closed merely because the paper is final if teacher scoring, checkpoint affirmation, or score eligibility still remains open.

So `PR3` keeps repaired legs repaired, but it does not let one repaired leg swallow the rest of the shell.

## `PR4` — local support may trigger review, but it still does not replace official closure

`PR4-LOCAL-SUPPORT-CAN-TRIGGER-REVIEW-NOT-REPLACE-CLOSURE` is the archive's smallest anti-laundering rule after mismatch.

Local material still matters:

- device-side saved answers matter because they support later same-device submission;
- room counts and custody notes matter because they support booklet investigation; and
- interim work plus retained local presentation/oral-defense video matter because College Board may request them for review.

But the archive now makes the boundary explicit: those materials may support review, investigation, or later request handling; they do **not** become interchangeable with the route's ordinary official closure token.

## What the archive now publishes when using this layer

When an institution or assessment family publishes one of the hot exam mismatch shells, it should now add only the following where truthful:

1. the shell-specific notice minimum from `RC1-RC3`;
2. the applicable `SV`, `ER`, `HF`, and `MR` fields, if any;
3. the applicable `PR1` precedence source when official signals conflict;
4. the route-specific `PR2` reclosure token; and
5. any `PR3` / `PR4` limitation when only part of the shell has been cured or when local support exists without official reclosure.

That is enough to make mismatch resolution legible without inventing one universal audit authority.

## What counted as a real archive gain

The archive already knew that hot exam closure fields could conflict and that those conflicts sometimes reopen the shell. It still lacked the next tighter answer: **which official signal temporarily governs once mismatch is admitted, what actually re-closes the shell when conflict narrows, and when cure must stay visibly partial even after more evidence appears.**

This document answers yes, but only barely.

It now allows the archive to publish a small shared layer for:

- owner-of-record precedence during mismatch;
- route-specific ordinary reclosure tokens;
- partial-cure honesty; and
- local-support-but-not-substitute guardrails.

That is a real operating gain because it blocks four opposite mistakes at once:

- letting the first summary screen seen on test day govern indefinitely;
- treating every later narrowing of mismatch as automatic reclosure;
- letting one repaired leg silently cure every other open leg; and
- laundering local support material into a fake universal closure token.
