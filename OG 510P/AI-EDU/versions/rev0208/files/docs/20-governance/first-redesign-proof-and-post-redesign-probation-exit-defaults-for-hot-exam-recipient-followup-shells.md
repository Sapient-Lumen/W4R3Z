# First redesign-proof and post-redesign probation-exit defaults for hot-exam recipient followup shells

The archive can now publish one still smaller layer for the hottest exam-like learner-request routes.

It can already say:

- when a de-watched surface has materially relapsed;
- when minimized repair memory may be recalled;
- when a later event is a fresh case, repeat pattern, split-surface shell, or redesign failure; and
- when post-relapse proof must be reminimized rather than kept as public stigma.

That is still not enough.

Once a relapse is classified as suspected or confirmed redesign failure, a different fiction appears. One office may say the workflow has been redesigned, trained, migrated, access-controlled, corrected, or put under a new owner. Another surface may still show the old result, stale snippet, archive capture, recipient record, platform copy, or accessibility defect. A source owner may have fixed the page but not the search result. A search-provider block may have been renewed only temporarily. A recipient may have corrected its internal posting but not the public record. A campus route may have changed responsibility without proving that the affected learner-facing shell is safe again. Or the redesign may be real, but the shell may keep old failure proof in public view long after current trust has returned.

This document adds one thing only:

- a **tiny redesign-proof / post-redesign probation-exit field set** for hot-exam recipient followup shells after a post-dewatch relapse has been classified as suspected redesign failure, confirmed redesign failure, or post-redesign proof needed.

That means the archive now asks a different question than before. It no longer asks only **whether prior repair memory may be recalled to classify a relapse**. It now asks **what current proof is enough to close a redesign, what short verification state may remain visible, when that probation exits, and how old failure evidence is minimized without pretending the redesign never happened**.

Current official signals support a deliberately narrow answer. Google Search Console exposes temporary removals, current and expired histories, cancellation, expiry, and reappearance eligibility rather than permanent redesign closure. Google's Refresh Outdated Content route exposes request queues, request statuses, 180-day approved-request expiry, exact result URL handling, and separate image or page refresh needs rather than a global repair certificate. Google personal-content routes require URL and sometimes screenshot proof, expose review/status/notification signals, and distinguish Search-result removal from source-site removal, which supports current-purpose proof without public revival of all old sensitive evidence. Princeton distinguishes local deletion and access control, Google/Bing search-result lag, Princeton custom-search exclusion, and Wayback persistence, so a local redesign does not automatically close every external surface. WiscWeb distinguishes emergency local edits, primary-contact follow-up, campus search/lookahead/directory surfaces, major-search-engine limits, and normal-hours support boundaries. Rowan's current web and digital-accessibility surfaces distinguish ownership, contributor support, content-audit/redesign review, Siteimprove monitoring, accessibility support, QA-like service windows, and web-support routing rather than one universal redesign owner. Internet Archive requires URL, time-period, control-period, and review context before archive exclusion and gives no instant all-time proof. Bing exposes temporary blocks, up-to-90-day boundaries, extension/permanent-removal prerequisites, cached/outdated-content handling, and permanent-removal methods such as source-side 404 or noindex rather than a single durable all-engine redesign certificate. Together those signals support a tighter archive rule: **the next truthful portability gain here is one tiny post-redesign proof layer for current owner, current proof, narrow probation, clean exit, and memory minimization — but not one permanent redesign penalty box, automatic trust restoration rule, public archive of old failure proof, or all-surface repair certificate.** See `B254`.

## Small field set for redesign proof and post-redesign probation exit

| Code | Meaning | Default archive action |
|---|---|---|
| `XZ0-NO-PERMANENT-REDESIGN-PENALTY-AUTOMATIC-RESTORATION-OR-PUBLIC-OLD-PROOF-ARCHIVE` | redesign failure classification permits current repair work; it does not create permanent stigma, instant trust, or a public vault of old screenshots and tickets | require current surface, owner, proof, and live dependency before redesign status affects the learner-facing shell |
| `XZ1-PUBLISH-REDESIGN-FAILURE-SCOPE-AND-CURRENT-OWNER` | a redesign failure must say what actually failed and who currently owns the fix | publish whether the failure is source-page, search-refresh, recipient-record, archive/mirror, privacy/safety, accessibility, local workflow, or split-surface; name the current owner, successor owner, provider owner, or no-active-owner residue |
| `XZ2-PUBLISH-CURRENT-REDESIGN-PROOF-BEFORE-TRUST-RETURNS` | an owner claim that the workflow was redesigned is not enough | publish the strongest current proof available: source-state proof, route-status proof, recipient correction proof, accessibility/QA proof, owner handback proof, noindex/404/redirect/canonical proof, temporary-block-plus-permanent-method proof, or proof-insufficient residue |
| `XZ3-PUBLISH-POST-REDESIGN-PROBATION-RUN-STATE-AND-EXIT` | a short verification period may be truthful only when it is named, scoped, and exit-bounded | publish whether probation is none, one route-native cycle, one clean publication/search/reflection cycle, one recipient confirmation cycle, event-triggered only, or split by surface; publish exit, extension, or relapse during probation |
| `XZ4-PUBLISH-REDESIGN-MEMORY-MINIMIZATION-HANDBACK-AND-REOPEN-TRIGGERS` | once current proof and probation have served their purpose, old failure proof should not remain public by inertia | publish current status and minimal local memory, hand back to ordinary or residual route truth, redact old proof, expire public probation markers, and reopen only on material new proof, recurrence, owner change, or route-native expiry |

## Field values that now travel together

When this layer is used, the shell should publish only six concrete fields:

1. `redesign_failure_scope_token` — `source page workflow`, `search refresh workflow`, `search temporary block workflow`, `recipient record workflow`, `archive mirror or social workflow`, `privacy or safety workflow`, `accessibility workflow`, `local workflow or staffing workflow`, `split surface workflow`, `no redesign failure`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
2. `current_redesign_owner_token` — `same local owner`, `successor local owner`, `campus web owner`, `accessibility owner`, `recipient owner`, `provider or platform owner`, `archive or mirror route owner`, `split owners named`, `no active owner`, `owner not visible`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
3. `current_redesign_proof_token` — `source deleted unpublished or access controlled`, `noindex 404 redirect or canonical proof`, `route status approved or completed`, `temporary block renewed with permanent method pending`, `recipient record corrected`, `archive exclusion or review proof`, `accessibility QA or monitoring proof`, `workflow SOP training or permissions proof`, `owner handback proof`, `proof insufficient residue`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
4. `post_redesign_probation_token` — `no probation needed`, `one route-native clean cycle`, `one search reflection clean cycle`, `one recipient confirmation clean cycle`, `one accessibility monitoring clean cycle`, `event-triggered verification only`, `split-surface probation`, `probation extended affected surface only`, `probation not available`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
5. `probation_exit_token` — `clean exit to ordinary`, `clean exit to minimized residue`, `partial proof extend affected surface only`, `relapse during probation reopen redesign`, `owner changed handback`, `route closed no active owner`, `privacy or safety local hold`, `recipient or record hold remains`, `probation marker expired`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
6. `redesign_memory_minimization_token` — `publish current proof only`, `retain local redesign summary`, `redact old screenshots and tickets`, `expire public probation marker`, `hand back to ordinary shell`, `hand back to no-active-route residue`, `retain privacy or safety sealed memory`, `retain recipient dependency marker`, `reopen only on material new proof`, `none inherited`, or `not published`.

That is deliberately small. It is enough to show what failed, who owns the redesigned fix, what current proof supports restored trust, what bounded probation remains, and how old failure evidence exits learner-facing publication. It is not enough to create a permanent redesign penalty box, one automatic restoration rule, one all-copy repair certificate, one public archive of old failure proof, or one proof-by-owner-claim shortcut.

## First redesign-proof / post-redesign probation-exit assignments

| Route or family | Redesign proof truth now admitted | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Google Search Console temporary-removal route after repeat URL expiry | `search temporary block workflow`, `provider or platform owner` plus source owner where visible, `temporary block renewed with permanent method pending` or `noindex 404 redirect or canonical proof`, `one route-native clean cycle`, and `probation marker expired` or `partial proof extend affected surface only` | temporary removals, expiry, cancellation, and reappearance eligibility make renewed temporary blocking useful proof but not durable redesign proof unless the source-side permanent method is also visible |
| Google Refresh Outdated Content route after stale snippet or image recurrence | `search refresh workflow`, `provider or platform owner`, `route status approved or completed`, `one search reflection clean cycle`, and `publish current proof only` | the route is exact-result and image/container-page specific, so redesign proof should stay per visible result and should not certify every future snippet, mirror, or image copy |
| Google personal-content / Results-about-you style route after sensitive recurrence | `privacy or safety workflow`, `provider or platform owner` or `split owners named`, `route status approved or completed` plus screenshot/URL proof where needed, `event-triggered verification only`, and `retain privacy or safety sealed memory` | sensitive proof can be needed for the current route, but it should not become a learner-facing archive or a public redesign history once the current dependency closes |
| Campus source-page deletion, unpublication, access-control, or custom-search redesign | `source page workflow` or `local workflow or staffing workflow`, `campus web owner` or `successor local owner`, `source deleted unpublished or access controlled`, `one search reflection clean cycle` where external refresh remains live, and `hand back to ordinary shell` | local owners can prove source changes and local search/custom-search behavior, but they usually cannot certify Google, Bing, archive, social, or recipient-record reflection |
| Recipient or official-record correction route after repeat posting or mismatch | `recipient record workflow`, `recipient owner` or `split owners named`, `recipient record corrected`, `one recipient confirmation clean cycle`, and `retain recipient dependency marker` until the live dependency closes | recipient-side proof can close the current recipient dependency, but it should not become an all-recipient redesign certificate or a reason to publish old learner-specific proof indefinitely |
| Internet Archive / Wayback or mirror-copy exclusion route after redesign failure | `archive mirror or social workflow`, `archive or mirror route owner` or `owner not visible`, `archive exclusion or review proof` or `proof insufficient residue`, `event-triggered verification only`, and `hand back to no-active-route residue` when no route remains | archive and mirror routes are surface-specific and proof-hungry; proof of a request or exclusion can narrow the shell but cannot certify all historical captures or all mirrors |
| Accessibility or content-maintenance redesign after a recurring support defect | `accessibility workflow` or `local workflow or staffing workflow`, `accessibility owner` plus content owner where visible, `accessibility QA or monitoring proof`, `one accessibility monitoring clean cycle`, and `retain local redesign summary` | accessibility monitoring and content-audit/redesign review can prove current repair for the affected surface, but they should not keep old failure screenshots public once the live dependency ends |

## Boundary conditions

This layer should not be used when only one of the following is true:

- the owner says the workflow was redesigned but provides no current proof;
- the proof belongs to a different surface, different owner, different route, or different learner-facing dependency;
- only an expired request-history row, old screenshot, old ticket, or old support email remains;
- the surface has returned to ordinary maintenance lag with no live learner consequence;
- privacy or safety proof can be retained locally without learner-facing publication; or
- the needed proof is now inside a route-specific appeal, legal, or records hold that should not be converted into a portable public field.

In those cases, publish `proof insufficient residue`, `owner not visible`, `probation not available`, `privacy or safety local hold`, `recipient or record hold remains`, or `none inherited` rather than pretending redesign has either failed forever or been fully repaired.

## What this closes

This closes `FT-0140`.

The archive no longer stops at "post-dewatch relapse classified as redesign failure". It can now publish a minimal redesign-proof and probation-exit shell: what failed, who owns the current redesign, what proof exists, what verification remains, and how memory minimizes after current trust is restored.

It also opens one narrower seam.

After a redesigned route exits probation, the archive may still need a tiny successor-owner / route-migration continuity layer for cases where the redesigned workflow is later transferred, decommissioned, replaced by a provider route, merged into a campus platform, or split across source/search/recipient/archive owners. That later layer would need to preserve current proof and learner action truth without turning redesign memory into a permanent owner-transition dossier.
