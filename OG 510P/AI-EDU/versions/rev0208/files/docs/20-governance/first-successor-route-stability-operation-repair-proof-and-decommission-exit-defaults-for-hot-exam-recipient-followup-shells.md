# First successor-route stability operation, repair-proof, and decommission-exit defaults for hot-exam recipient followup shells

The archive can now publish one still smaller operating layer for the hottest exam-like learner-request routes.

It can already say:

- when a redesigned hot-exam followup route later migrates to a successor owner, URL, portal, provider route, archive path, or official-record owner;
- when later successor-route drift needs current proof refresh rather than automatic proof carryover;
- when repeated post-migration drift crosses a named stability threshold; and
- when that threshold should become owner repair, local redesign, route split, bounded watch, decommissioned-route posture, or no-active-route residue instead of another ordinary refresh.

That is still not enough.

Once a repeat-drift threshold has actually opened a stability posture, the operational temptation changes again. A successor owner can claim the route is stable after changing a form, redirect, prerequisite, or portal label. A provider route can be temporarily blocked, revived, retired, or replaced while the source page and recipient record tell different truths. A recipient portal can confirm one delivery cycle while the official-record field still lags. A local CMS/accessibility owner can repair a page workflow while a search result, image result, archive time slice, or third-party mirror remains unresolved. A decommissioned route can later reappear, but the reappearance may be an ordinary replacement, a revived old path, a stale cache, a mirror copy, or a new route with a different owner. A learner can also be asked to refile again even though the active problem is still owner stability rather than learner evidence.

This document adds one thing only:

- a **tiny successor-route stability operation / repair-proof / decommission-exit field set** for hot-exam recipient followup shells after `YR0-YR4` has already found repeat post-migration drift and opened a bounded stability watch, owner repair posture, local redesign, route split, decommissioned-route posture, or no-active-route residue.

That means the archive now asks a different question than before. It no longer asks only **whether repeated post-migration drift crossed a stability threshold**. It now asks **how the opened successor-route stability posture operates, what current proof can close it, what happens when drift recurs during it, and how decommission / no-active-route residue exits or minimizes without becoming permanent migration surveillance, public route-failure stigma, or an all-surface decommissioning certificate**.

Current official signals support a deliberately narrow answer. Google Search Console's Removals tool remains property-owner, exact-URL, current/expired-history, cancellation, temporary-removal, and source-side permanent-method based rather than a durable all-copy stability dashboard. Google's Refresh Outdated Content route is exact page/image URL, changed/gone-content, queue/status, and 180-day expiry based rather than a standing proof-refresh or repair-certificate route. Google personal-content routes can expose URL, screenshot, notification, status, new-result, and source-site separation signals, but they still separate Search removal from source-owner control and do not justify public reuse of sensitive proof. Princeton separates local deletion, document/media cleanup, page cache, custom search, Google/Bing reflection, access control, and Wayback persistence. WiscWeb separates emergency source edits, primary-contact support, WiscWeb hosting boundaries, campus search/lookahead/directory routes, normal-hours constraints, and no-control boundaries for major search engines. Rowan separates Cascade CMS, web contributors, Web Services support, Siteimprove/accessibility monitoring, content-audit/redesign review, training, support tickets, and accommodation routes rather than one central successor-stability desk. Internet Archive requires URL, time-period, control-period, and review context before exclusion and gives no guaranteed result before review. Bing distinguishes temporary blocks, 90-day expiry, cached/outdated handling, and source-side permanent methods such as 404/410 or noindex. Together those signals support a tighter archive rule: **the next truthful portability gain here is one tiny successor-stability operation layer for run-state, current repair or decommission proof, drift-during-posture disposition, exit/minimization, and reopen triggers — but not one permanent migration dashboard, public route-failure label, all-surface decommission certificate, automatic learner refiling duty, or proof-vault retention rule.** See `B258`.

## Small field set for successor-route stability operation, repair proof, and decommission exit

| Code | Meaning | Default archive action |
|---|---|---|
| `YS0-NO-PERMANENT-MIGRATION-DASHBOARD-PUBLIC-ROUTE-FAILURE-STIGMA-OR-ALL-SURFACE-DECOMMISSION-CERTIFICATE` | an opened stability posture is a bounded operating state, not a permanent public label, all-route shutdown, or evidence-retention license | keep the posture tied to its named successor route, affected surface, current owner, live learner dependency, and route-native clock; do not widen it to every copy, owner, or future recurrence |
| `YS1-PUBLISH-STABILITY-RUN-STATE-SCOPE-OWNER-AND-CADENCE` | the shell must say what kind of posture is actually running | publish whether the posture is watch, repair-under-review, redesign/probation, split-surface operation, decommission review, no-active-route residue, replacement-route handoff, exited ordinary, or reopened; name the checked surface, owner, cadence or event trigger, and learner action-now truth |
| `YS2-PUBLISH-CURRENT-REPAIR-PROOF-DECOMMISSION-PROOF-OR-SPLIT-HANDBACK` | exit needs current proof from the route or owner that can actually close the affected surface | label the proof as successor-route stability proof, source/CMS repair, redirect/canonical persistence, provider-route state, search-provider state, archive/platform review, recipient confirmation, official-record correction, accessibility remediation, redesign proof, decommission notice, replacement-route proof, or no-active-owner proof |
| `YS3-PUBLISH-DRIFT-DURING-STABILITY-POSTURE-DISPOSITION` | drift during the posture must be classified before the watch extends, splits, redesigns, decommissions, or closes | distinguish same successor-route drift, stale owner beyond its own window, prerequisite drift, proof expiry, redirect/canonical drift, provider decommission/revival, recipient portal drift, archive/control-period mismatch, split-owner disagreement, ordinary lag, or unrelated copy |
| `YS4-PUBLISH-EXIT-DECOMMISSION-RESIDUE-MINIMIZATION-AND-REOPEN-TRIGGERS` | the posture must exit, narrow, hand back, or become residue with a visible minimization rule | publish ordinary successor status, one bounded extension, repair-closed status, redesign handoff, route split, replacement migration, decommissioned-route residue, no-active-route residue, local-only memory, minimized public pointer, or reopen only on material drift, route revival, owner change, replacement-route failure, recipient dependency, or new proof |

## Field values that now travel together

When this layer is used, the shell should publish only six concrete fields:

1. `successor_stability_run_state_token` — `not opened`, `bounded stability watch active`, `repair proof under review`, `redesign or probation running`, `split-surface operation active`, `decommission review active`, `replacement-route handoff active`, `no-active-route residue`, `clean cycle running`, `exit eligible`, `exited ordinary`, `reopened after drift`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
2. `successor_stability_scope_token` — `one exact successor URL`, `one redirected or canonical family`, `one provider route`, `one recipient portal`, `one official-record field`, `one archive time slice`, `one image or snippet family`, `one local source or CMS workflow`, `one accessibility or QA workflow`, `split owner shell`, `decommissioned path only`, `replacement route only`, `no active route`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
3. `successor_repair_or_decommission_proof_token` — `successor owner stability proof`, `source corrected and persisted`, `redirect canonical or noindex proof`, `route prerequisite stabilized`, `provider route re-enabled`, `provider route formally retired`, `search-provider current state proof`, `archive or platform review proof`, `recipient confirmation proof`, `official-record correction proof`, `accessibility remediation proof`, `local redesign proof`, `replacement route named and current`, `decommission notice current`, `no active owner proof`, `owner claim without proof`, `no proof yet`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
4. `drift_during_successor_stability_token` — `same successor route drifted`, `successor owner stale beyond route window`, `route prerequisite changed`, `transferred proof expired`, `redirect or canonical changed`, `provider path decommissioned`, `provider path revived`, `recipient portal drifted`, `official-record field drifted`, `archive control-period mismatch`, `split-owner disagreement`, `ordinary search or cache lag`, `unrelated copy only`, `no drift during posture`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
5. `successor_stability_exit_token` — `ordinary successor status restored`, `bounded watch extended once`, `repair closed with current proof`, `redesign handoff retained`, `route split retained`, `replacement route migration opened`, `provider route decommissioned`, `decommissioned-route residue`, `no-active-route residue`, `local-only memory retained`, `minimized public pointer`, `post-exit relapse trigger published`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
6. `successor_stability_reopen_token` — `reopen on material same-route drift`, `reopen on owner change`, `reopen on route revival`, `reopen on replacement-route failure`, `reopen on route-native expiry`, `reopen on recipient dependency`, `reopen on archive control proof`, `reopen on official-record mismatch`, `reopen on privacy or safety event`, `fresh threshold required`, `no reopen without current owner`, `none inherited`, or `not published`.

That is deliberately small. It is enough to show the run-state, scope, owner, current proof, recurrence handling, exit, minimization, and reopen trigger for a successor route after a repeat-drift stability threshold. It is not enough to create a permanent migration dashboard, an all-surface decommission certificate, a public proof vault, automatic refile demands, public route-failure stigma, or a rule that decommissioned-route residue either lasts forever or is forgotten completely.

## What this layer now makes portable

Hot-exam recipient followup shells may now publish one tiny `YS0-YS4` layer after `YR0-YR4` when the archive needs to say not merely that **a repeated successor-route drift pattern crossed a threshold**, but that **the resulting watch, repair, redesign, split, decommission, or no-active-route posture is operating with bounded proof and a real exit**.

That means a shell may now truthfully say things like:

- `run a one-URL successor-route stability watch until the current route-native expiry or reflection window closes, then de-threshold unless the same URL materially drifts again`;
- `the successor owner claims repair, but no current redirect/canonical/prerequisite/proof-state has been shown; keep repair proof under review instead of closing or widening`;
- `the provider route is formally retired; open replacement-route migration or decommissioned-route residue rather than asking the learner to refile into a dead path`;
- `a recipient portal drifted during a source-page stability posture; split recipient action from source/search/archive stability instead of resetting everything`; or
- `the stability posture closed on current proof; retain only a local repair summary or minimized public pointer and reopen only on material same-route drift, route revival, owner change, replacement-route failure, recipient dependency, or new proof`.

It still may not pretend:

- that an opened successor-route stability posture is permanent migration surveillance;
- that every migrated route needs a standing dashboard;
- that one decommissioned provider path proves every source/search/archive/recipient copy is closed;
- that route decommission means learner fault;
- that a successor owner can close the shell by silence or unsupported assurance;
- that old screenshots, expired status rows, or stale tickets prove current route instability;
- that sensitive learner proof should stay publicly visible after exit; or
- that a replacement route starts from total amnesia about current proof and prior repair limits.

## Why this matters for the larger archive

The archive's education-with-AI program is not only about making successor routes accountable. It is also about preventing accountability machinery from becoming permanent learner-facing surveillance.

This layer matters because route migration creates two opposite fictions. One fiction says the successor route inherits every prior proof item and can keep asking the learner to refile until every copy everywhere disappears. The other fiction says the old route's decommission or replacement erases all repair memory, so the learner and local owners must start from zero whenever the path moves. Both are wrong. The point here is narrower: publish **what stability posture is actually open**, **who owns the current proof**, **whether drift during operation changes only this surface or another surface**, **how repair / split / replacement / decommission exits**, and **what minimal memory remains**. Everything else remains route-native casework, local-only retention, privacy-aware minimization, ordinary search/cache lag, or no-active-route residue.

## Boundary conditions

This layer should not be used when only one of the following is true:

- `YR0-YR4` has not found a repeat-drift threshold, so an ordinary `YD0-YD4` proof refresh is enough;
- the successor route is still inside its own published review, reflection, support, temporary-block, expiry, or implementation window;
- the only signal is ordinary search, cache, crawl, or history-row lag with no current owner, route, proof, recipient, archive, or learner dependency change;
- the issue belongs to a different URL, image, recipient, official record, archive time slice, owner, platform, or learner and does not form one affected-surface posture;
- a protected accommodation, privacy, safety, records, legal, provider, or appeal process already controls the run-state, proof, or exit;
- the old route has decommissioned but no current owner or replacement route exists, in which case publish no-active-route residue rather than a watch; or
- a current route names a specific repair, retry, appeal, status, decommission, or replacement rule that should govern instead of a portable operation layer.

In those cases, publish `not opened`, `ordinary refresh only`, `current route-native window applies`, `ordinary search or cache lag`, `unrelated copy only`, `no active route`, `protected local-only route`, `no proof yet`, `fresh threshold required`, or `none inherited` rather than treating successor-route instability as permanent migration surveillance, public route-failure stigma, or learner fault.

## What this closes

This closes `FT-0144`.

The archive no longer stops at "repeat drift crossed a threshold". It can now publish a minimal successor-route stability operation layer: the run-state, scope, owner, proof, drift-during-operation disposition, exit, decommission residue, minimization, and reopen triggers for a migrated hot-exam recipient followup route.

It also opens one narrower seam.

After a successor-route stability posture exits as ordinary, decommissioned-route residue, no-active-route residue, split-surface operation, or replacement-route migration, the archive may still need a tiny post-decommission reappearance / replacement-route activation layer for cases where a retired route later revives, a replacement route fails, or decommissioned residue gets new owner proof — without treating decommission as permanent failure, replacement as total fresh-start amnesia, or route revival as an automatic all-surface reopen.
