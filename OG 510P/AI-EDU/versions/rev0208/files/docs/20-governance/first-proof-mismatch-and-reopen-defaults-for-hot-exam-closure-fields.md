# First proof-mismatch and reopen defaults for hot exam closure fields

This document closes the archive's next narrower governance gap after **proof-of-transfer and closure-field minima**.

Three overclaims matter here:

- **closure-proof absolutism** — once a hot shell has any closure token at all, the archive acts as if that token can never be questioned by a conflicting official status, count failure, or missing required companion step;
- **split-stack amnesia** — when one artifact leg closes and another required leg stays open, the archive pretends the whole route is closed instead of admitting a partial-closure state; and
- **synthetic-cure laundering** — narrative reassurance, silent transcription, relabeling, or an unrelated retained local artifact gets treated as if it repairs the missing proof leg that actually failed.

Current official signals support a tighter answer. Bluebook's current in-school testing page says that if Test Day Toolkit shows some students without **Submitted** status, coordinators should also check Bluebook because the Toolkit status can be out of date; if answers were submitted, the exam appears on the **Past** tab with the message **“Your answers have been submitted!”**. Bluebook's current help page says that when students see **Answer Submission Pending**, they should not try submitting from any device on their own, and after testing time runs out they must submit from the **same device** they finished testing on. The current AP Coordinator's Manual, Part 2 then makes the reopen path explicit: if a student couldn't submit within 3 calendar days after the exam, the coordinator submits an IR for a technology / answer-submission error; hybrid digital materials must be collected, counted, and confirmed before dismissal; and paper-related incidents such as shared AP IDs, misplaced answers, or missing booklets stay inside named IR handling rather than ad hoc local repair. Current AP Digital Portfolio and AP Capstone policy pages keep the portfolio route split visible again: AP Capstone students must submit written components as final by **April 30, 2026, 11:59 p.m. ET**, teachers must submit presentation scores and checkpoint affirmations by **May 10, 2026, 11:59 p.m. ET**, checkpoints and affirmations are score-relevant, an AP Seminar or AP Research exam must be ordered for the work to be scored, and retained presentation/oral-defense videos stay local for at least one academic year because College Board may request them later. Together those signals support a narrower archive rule: **the hot-exam closure-field shells may now share one tiny proof-mismatch / reopen layer, but they still do not justify one universal audit packet, one universal reconciliation authority, or one universal synthetic cure.** See `B211`.

That means the archive now asks a different question than before. It no longer asks only **what minimal fields prove ordinary closure**. ## Small grammar for proof-mismatch / reopen defaults

| Code | Meaning | Default archive action |
|---|---|---|
| `MR0-NO-UNIVERSAL-AUDIT-PACKET` | no one universal mismatch-reconciliation packet travels across these hot shells | keep mismatch publication tiny and shell-specific rather than inventing one shared audit dossier |
| `MR1-PROOF-NONMATCH` | two official closure signals materially disagree | publish the conflict and stop pretending the ordinary closure token is uncontested |
| `MR2-PARTIAL-CLOSURE` | one required artifact leg is closed while another required leg stays open, missing, or unverified | publish split closure truth rather than collapsing the whole shell to closed |
| `MR3-REOPEN-TO-LAST-TRUTHFUL-STATE` | when proof fields fail, revert the shell to the last status word that is still honest | reopen to `SV1`, `SV2`, or `SV4` rather than preserving a false `SV3` closure |
| `MR4-NO-SYNTHETIC-CURE` | missing closure proof may not be repaired by narration, silent transcription, relabeling, or an unrelated retained artifact | require the official route-specific cure path instead of local improvisation |

## `MR1` — proof nonmatch is a real state, not noise to hide

`MR1-PROOF-NONMATCH` exists for cases where two official signals disagree strongly enough that the shell should stop pretending closure is straightforward.

The key examples are different across shells:

- in post-end digital AP submission, Test Day Toolkit may still show the student as not submitted while Bluebook already shows the exam on the `Past` tab with **“Your answers have been submitted!”**;
- in hybrid digital custody, a booklet may have been physically collected but the count, AP ID linkage, or answer placement no longer supports the counted-collected closure token; and
- in portfolio/local-record routes, one official system may show a paper as final while other official score-relevant legs remain incomplete or ineligible.

The archive's gain is just this: a nonmatch may now be published as a truth in its own right instead of being buried inside narrative reassurance.

## `MR2` — partial closure means split artifact stacks stay split

`MR2-PARTIAL-CLOSURE` is the archive's smallest way to admit that some required leg of the route is complete while another is not.

It matters because these hot shells do not close in the same way:

- a digital exam can have saved answers on the original device without yet having a completed College Board submission;
- a hybrid digital route can have a physically collected booklet while still carrying a custody, label, or misplaced-answer defect; and
- an AP Capstone route can have a final written upload while teacher scoring, checkpoint affirmation, or score eligibility is still open.

So `MR2` stops the archive from collapsing a split route into either total failure or total closure.

## `MR3` — reopen to the last truthful shared state, not to a fictional clean slate

`MR3-REOPEN-TO-LAST-TRUTHFUL-STATE` is deliberately modest.

It does **not** mean every mismatch returns to the same place. It means the shell should fall back only as far as the last state word that is still honest:

- often back to `SV1-ACTION-OPEN` when the learner or device still has an unresolved ordinary next step;
- sometimes to `SV2-NAMED-CUSTODY` when the artifact is in staff hands but ordinary closure no longer holds; or
- sometimes straight to `SV4-UPSTREAM-REVIEW-OPEN` when an official coordinator, IR, or score-eligibility path must decide the next truthful state.

That is enough to prevent a false `SV3-HANDOFF-COMPLETE` from lingering after the proof underneath it has failed.

## `MR4` — no synthetic cure means one shell cannot quietly heal another

`MR4-NO-SYNTHETIC-CURE` blocks the archive's most tempting false repair moves.

It means, for example:

- a proctor memory or local reassurance does not replace the official same-device digital submission path;
- a count problem, shared AP ID, misplaced answer, or missing booklet does not become cured merely because staff believe they know what probably happened; and
- a retained local presentation/oral-defense video does not cure a missing written-final, missing checkpoint affirmation, or missing score eligibility leg in the AP Digital Portfolio path.

This is the archive's smallest anti-laundering rule: **a surviving artifact in one layer does not silently repair a broken closure proof in another**.

## What the archive now publishes when using this layer

When an institution or assessment family publishes one of the hot exam closure shells, it should now add only the following where truthful:

1. the shell-specific receipt / handback / owner-notice minimum from `RC1-RC3`;
2. the applicable `SV` state word(s) and `ER` crosswalk code(s), if any;
3. the `HF1-HF4` proof fields already admitted for the shell;
4. `MR1-PROOF-NONMATCH` where official closure signals conflict;
5. `MR2-PARTIAL-CLOSURE` where one required leg is closed but another remains open or unverified;
6. `MR3-REOPEN-TO-LAST-TRUTHFUL-STATE` naming the reopened state; and
7. `MR4-NO-SYNTHETIC-CURE` wherever the shell would otherwise be tempted to repair itself by narrative, transcription, relabeling, or unrelated retained artifacts.

That is enough to make post-closure conflict legible without inventing one universal audit packet.

## What counted as a real archive gain

The archive already knew how to publish shell-specific notices, a tiny status language, and a very small proof-of-transfer / closure-field set. It still lacked the next tighter answer: **what should the archive say when those tiny proof fields no longer line up, or when they show that closure is only partial after all?**

This document answers yes, but only barely.

It now allows the archive to publish a tiny shared mismatch / reopen layer for:

- official proof nonmatch;
- partial closure;
- reopen-to-last-truthful-state; and
- no synthetic cure.

That is a real operating gain because it blocks four opposite mistakes at once:

- treating the first closure token as irreversible truth;
- hiding official signal conflicts as mere clerical noise;
- collapsing split routes into all-open or all-closed fiction; and
- letting one surviving artifact quietly pretend to heal another broken proof chain.
