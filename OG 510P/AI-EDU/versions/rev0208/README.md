# AI-EDU — rev0208

## Repair the institution without making the learner permanently suspect

A submission portal still accepts an old route, while the public instructions and official record point to its replacement. The institution has repaired related problems before. What should the learner do now, and what would count as finishing the repair?

This edition's [defining document](files/docs/20-governance/first-fourth-rewatch-operation-third-repair-refresh-and-fourth-dewatch-exit-defaults-for-hot-exam-recipient-followup-shells.md) pursues that problem through recurring, time-sensitive exam-like request routes. Its elaborate vocabulary has a practical concern underneath it: repeated institutional correction must not turn into repeated learner filing, public stigma or endless retention of sensitive evidence. At the same time, merely announcing another review does not establish that anything is being repaired.

The proposal makes a bounded review say what it is doing, which part of the route is affected, who is responsible, what current evidence matters, what the learner can safely do and how the review ends. It is an institutional design argument, not an operating service or a finding that the proposed procedure has worked.

## Read the portal example before the labels

In the source's example B, the recipient portal again accepts a retired route although the source and official record publish the repaired one. The learner has not filed twice and should not have to start over merely because those institutional accounts disagree.

The proposed response distinguishes **repairing the recipient's record** from **asking the learner to act**. It can recognize a single submission across the two routes or explicitly say not to duplicate filing. If a current recipient-of-record rule really requires refiling, that requirement must be express and preserve no-penalty treatment. “Never refile” would therefore be as inaccurate a summary as “repair means submit again.”

The difference matters because a successful source edit is not proof that the recipient portal or official record has changed. Nor does one unresolved record establish that every related surface has failed. The document asks for current, scoped evidence instead of one global repaired/not-repaired label.

## What “rewatch” and “dewatch” are doing here

The preceding revisions distinguish remembering a previous repair, deciding whether a material recurrence warrants another review, and actually operating that review. rev0208 addresses the last step once the specified recurrence threshold is met: whether and how a fourth review operates. The ordinals locate it within that historical sequence; they are not a recommendation that every institution needs four cycles or a universal recurrence count.

A **rewatch** is renewed, bounded attention to a recurring problem. **Dewatch** ends that special attention on stated grounds. An earlier failure can inform the new inquiry without making the learner, route or responsible office permanently suspect.

The source proposes six connected pieces of information:

- **Present operation:** reconciliation, correction, protected-route handling, evidence review or another named activity, rather than a warning label alone.
- **Affected scope, responsible party and cadence:** the particular portal, route, deadline, record or other surface, with an event trigger or bounded check rather than indefinite monitoring.
- **Current repair evidence:** distinguish a current confirmation from an old screenshot, expired status, unsupported assertion, pending answer or evidence properly held only locally.
- **Learner-safe action:** no action, a usable route, a protected local submission, manual support or another justified instruction, separate from the institution's own repair work.
- **Exit disposition:** correction, a retained split between routes, redesign, retirement, unresolved residue or a clean end to special review.
- **Retained memory and reopening conditions:** keep what remains justified, minimize the rest and identify which material new facts could warrant renewed attention.

These elements constrain each other. Evidence without a safe next action can leave the learner stranded. An active review without an exit can become surveillance. Ending review without reducing retained proof can leave a permanent dossier behind.

## Different surfaces need different answers

The [source's examples C–E](files/docs/20-governance/first-fourth-rewatch-operation-third-repair-refresh-and-fourth-dewatch-exit-defaults-for-hot-exam-recipient-followup-shells.md) prevent “one corrected page” from becoming a universal certificate.

A protected accommodation route may need to remain distinct from the public route. The relevant local owner can confirm its use without publishing the sensitive support information that explains it. A public repair record is not entitled to become an accommodation or appeal dossier.

A missing responsible party is another case. If the successor disappears and the portal is silent while a deadline remains live, the proposal retains the absence as an unresolved condition and directs attention to manual support or a protective current window. Missing institutional evidence must not be converted into learner fault for following the last safe published route.

A redesign can also be accepted before an official record reflects the change. Those are separate facts, not proof that one side is imaginary. The proposal keeps the affected dependency visible while allowing different parts of the repair to reach different outcomes.

Ordinary search, cache or archive lag without a current learner or institutional dependency is outside this special layer. Existing route-specific rules may already govern the matter, and protected evidence rules may require local handling. The document expressly rejects universal monitoring, all-surface repair certificates and reopening based only on stale screenshots or unrelated mirrors.

## Remember enough to learn, not enough to punish forever

The [recent history](files/CHANGELOG.md) moves from memory after a closed review, to a threshold for material recurrence, to this bounded operating response. The next recorded question concerns later recurrence after this fourth closure. It is open in this checkpoint; the new entrance does not turn it into a mandate for a fifth review.

The strongest design question is whether this accumulating vocabulary can remain usable for the people it is meant to protect. Its distinctions address real differences, but the archive's label “tiny” does not demonstrate low administrative burden. Read the six-field proposal against its own purpose: does it produce current evidence, a safe action and an honest exit, or merely rename unfinished work? That is a question for evaluating the proposal, not a reported deployment result.
