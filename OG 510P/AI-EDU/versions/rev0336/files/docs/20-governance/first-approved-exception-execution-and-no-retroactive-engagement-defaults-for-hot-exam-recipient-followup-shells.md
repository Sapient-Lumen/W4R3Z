# First approved-exception execution and no-retroactive-engagement defaults for hot-exam recipient followup shells

The archive can now publish a very small post-send and post-confirmation grammar for the hottest
exam-like learner-request routes.

It can already say:

- what downstream transfer evidence the owner system exposes;
- how old that transfer residue is and when direct recipient confirmation becomes the truthful next
  step;
- what the recipient side says once contacted — confirmed receipt, still processing, wait,
  resend/correct, temporary bridge, or no new state;
- what bounded interim bridge or no-penalty protection a named office admits without pretending that
  unofficial evidence already equals official receipt, posted credit, or official evaluation;
- what event ends that bridge, what later official proof supersedes it, and what ordinary correction
  truth follows if verification fails;
- what named correction window still protects the learner if official verification lands late enough
  to change the route; and
- what fault-based post-window exception path may still exist once that protected correction window
  has already closed.

That is still not enough.

The archive still lacked the next tighter answer: **once a named fault-based exception is approved,
what has to be true for that exception to be executable rather than merely sympathetic — what
instructor or registrar proof shows that the learner can still join honestly, what work or
participation boundaries remain non-fictional, when record repair truthfully substitutes for
course-entry repair, and when approval still cannot fabricate attendance, participation, or
completed coursework that never occurred?**

This document adds one thing only:

- a **tiny approved-exception execution / no-retroactive-engagement field set** for those already
  named hot-exam recipient followup shells.

That means the archive now asks a different question than before. It no longer asks only **whether a
post-window fault-based exception path exists**. It now asks **what current offices require before
that exception can actually be carried out honestly, and what those same offices still refuse to
treat as executable even after sympathy, fault, or approval language appears**.

Current official signals support a deliberately narrow answer. UMass says students cannot be
penalized for work missed before official enrollment begins and instructors must give them a chance
to start on the first official day of enrollment with full graded-work and participation points
available plus reasonable make-up without penalty. Notre Dame says faculty must give a student the
opportunity to make up without penalty work missed before registration when the student registers
during the regular add period, and later schedule alterations require dean approval. UCSB
Engineering says late-add requests are only approved in exceptional circumstances, the instructor is
asked about participation, and requests made after an exam or other assignments/projects have
already been graded are typically not approved. Kent says students waiting on a possible late add or
re-add should not be attending if they are not officially registered and will not receive credit or
a grade for a course if they miss the registration boundary. Columbia says neither attendance nor
willingness to pay a late fee entitles registration after the deadline. George Mason's catalog says
students will not receive credit unless their names are on the official class roster and final grade
sheets, and retroactive credits will not be awarded merely because a student says they attended
while off-roster. George Mason CHSS says retroactive add requires instructor verification of the
dates and grades for all graded work and the final grade, plus an explanation for why the student
remained in the course while not on the official roster. Ohio State says retroactive add requires a
grade-assignment form together with the retroactive-add change ticket. Colorado Boulder says
retroactive class add is for university error, but where the real truth is that the student never
attended or never self-enrolled, current late-action routes instead expose no-record drop or
cancellation rather than fake late-entry repair. Together those signals support a tighter archive
rule: **the next truthful portability gain here is one tiny approved-exception execution /
no-retroactive-engagement layer, but not one universal catch-up amnesty, one universal
retroactive-attendance fiction, or one universal guaranteed late-add completion plan.** See `B229`.

## Small field set for approved-exception execution / no-retroactive-engagement defaults

| Code | Meaning | Default archive action |
|---|---|---|
| `EX0-NO-UNIVERSAL-CATCH-UP-AMNESTY-OR-RETROACTIVE-ENGAGEMENT-FICTION` | no one universal catch-up contract, retroactive-attendance fiction, or guaranteed late-entry completion plan governs every hot recipient followup shell even after a fault-based exception is approved | keep approved-exception execution route-bounded instead of implying `approval means the class can now be reconstructed the same way everywhere` |
| `EX1-PUBLISH-WHAT-MAKES-LATE-ENTRY-OR-RETROACTIVE-REPAIR-HONESTLY-EXECUTABLE` | publish only the named proof that makes the exception executable — no-penalty make-up during an official add window, instructor participation review, dates-and-grades verification, or grade-assignment certification | distinguish real execution proof from generic `approved in principle` language |
| `EX2-PUBLISH-THE-NO-RETROACTIVE-ENGAGEMENT-BOUNDARY-WHEN-OFFICIAL-ROSTER-OR-ENROLLMENT-NEVER-EXISTED` | publish the line current offices draw against fictional retroactive participation — off-roster attendance alone, willingness to pay a late fee, or post hoc claims of having participated do not themselves create credit | distinguish honest late-entry repair from fake backdated enrollment |
| `EX3-WHEN-HONEST-COURSE-ENTRY-REPAIR-FAILS-PUBLISH-THE-RECORD-REPAIR-SUBSTITUTE-INSTEAD` | if current materials show that no-record drop, cancellation, withdrawal, or refund/removal is the truthful repair once participation cannot be certified honestly, publish that substitute explicitly | distinguish executable late-entry repair from record-side correction |
| `EX4-PUBLISH-WHO-CERTIFIES-THE-EXECUTION-PATH-AND-WHAT-TIMING-STILL-GOVERNS` | publish which instructor, dean, registrar, or college office certifies the remaining-work truth, and whether execution is still bounded by add-period, petition, or end-of-term timing | distinguish a real executable exception path from vague `someone approved this` language |

## Field values that now travel together

When this layer is used, the shell should publish only five concrete fields:

1. `execution_readiness_token` — `no-penalty make-up during official add window`, `instructor
   participation feasibility review`, `dates-and-grades retroactive-add certification`,
   `grade-assignment form required`, `not executable as late entry`, `none inherited`, or `not
   published`;
2. `execution_proof_bundle` — `official enrollment date controls no-penalty make-up`, `instructor
   says missed instructional time can still be made up`, `instructor verifies dates and grades for
   all graded work plus final grade`, `registrar form plus grade-assignment attachment`, `college or
   dean approval after ordinary add period`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
3. `retroactive_engagement_boundary` — `attendance before official enrollment cannot be penalized
   but also cannot be backdated as official participation`, `waiting-for-approval attendance does
   not earn credit`, `attendance alone does not entitle registration`, `off-roster participation
   does not itself earn retroactive credit`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
4. `substitute_record_repair_path` — `late entry remains executable`, `late add usually not approved
   after graded work already exists`, `use no-record drop or cancellation instead`, `drop /
   withdrawal / refund route instead`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
5. `execution_owner_and_clock` — `instructor inside published add window`, `dean/designee after
   ordinary add window`, `associate dean after instructor participation review`, `registrar plus
   course instructor certification`, `college or registrar change-of-record route`, `none
   inherited`, or `not published`.

That is deliberately small. It is enough to distinguish a real executable exception from a merely
sympathetic one, to distinguish no-penalty make-up from retroactive attendance fiction, to
distinguish course-entry repair from record repair, and to distinguish instructor-certified
remaining-work truth from vague `you can probably catch up` language.

## `EX1` — approval becomes real only when execution proof is named

`EX1-PUBLISH-WHAT-MAKES-LATE-ENTRY-OR-RETROACTIVE-REPAIR-HONESTLY-EXECUTABLE` is the archive's
smallest anti-fake-approval rule.

It blocks two opposite mistakes at once:

- acting as if any sympathetic exception path automatically means the learner can still join and
  complete the course honestly; and
- acting as if current institutions never publish any execution proof at all once a late add or
  retroactive repair is actually being carried out.

Current official pages now make several execution proofs visible enough to publish:

- UMass and Notre Dame make official-enrollment-date no-penalty make-up obligations visible during a
  named add period;
- UCSB makes instructor participation review visible and warns that late adds after graded exams or
  assignments are typically not approved;
- George Mason CHSS requires dates-and-grades verification plus an explanation for off-roster
  participation; and
- Ohio State requires a grade-assignment form for retroactive add.

That is enough for one tiny execution-proof field. It is not enough for one universal `the learner
can just catch up later` rule.

## `EX2` — approved exception cannot fabricate retroactive engagement

`EX2-PUBLISH-THE-NO-RETROACTIVE-ENGAGEMENT-BOUNDARY-WHEN-OFFICIAL-ROSTER-OR-ENROLLMENT-NEVER-EXISTED`
is the archive's smallest anti-fiction rule.

Current official pages now make several boundary statements visible enough to harden:

- Kent says students waiting for possible late-add approval should not attend unregistered and will
  not receive credit or a grade if they are not officially registered by the deadline;
- Columbia says attendance and willingness to pay a late fee do not entitle registration after the
  deadline; and
- George Mason says students will not receive credit unless they are on the official roster and
  final grade sheets, and retroactive credits will not be awarded merely because they say they
  attended off-roster.

That is enough for one tiny no-retroactive-engagement field. It is not enough for one universal
backdated-attendance fiction.

## `EX3` — when honest course-entry repair fails, record repair should stay visible instead of disappearing

`EX3-WHEN-HONEST-COURSE-ENTRY-REPAIR-FAILS-PUBLISH-THE-RECORD-REPAIR-SUBSTITUTE-INSTEAD` is the
archive's smallest anti-forced-late-add rule.

Current official pages now make it visible that sometimes the honest answer is **not** `late add
anyway`:

- UCSB says late-add requests after exams or other assignments/projects have already been graded are
  typically not approved; and
- Colorado Boulder publishes no-record drop or cancellation routes where the real truth is
  university error, no self-enrollment, or no attendance, instead of forcing those cases through a
  fake successful late-entry narrative.

That is enough for one tiny substitute-repair field. It is not enough for one universal rule that
every fault-based exception still ends in course-entry repair.

## `EX4` — execution still has an owner and a clock

`EX4-PUBLISH-WHO-CERTIFIES-THE-EXECUTION-PATH-AND-WHAT-TIMING-STILL-GOVERNS` is the archive's
smallest anti-owner-blur rule.

Current official pages now make it visible that approved-exception execution is still ownered and
timed:

- UMass and Notre Dame tie no-penalty late-entry execution to the student's official enrollment date
  and the published add period, with later schedule change requiring dean-level approval;
- UCSB puts the final decision with the Associate Dean after instructor participation review;
- George Mason CHSS requires instructor certification inside a retroactive-add request packet; and
- Colorado Boulder routes substitute record repair through the college or registrar change-of-record
  machinery rather than a generic late-add promise.

That is enough for one tiny owner-and-clock field. It is not enough for one universal `approved
somewhere` placeholder.

## What the archive now publishes when using this layer

When an institution or assessment family publishes one of these hot recipient followup shells, it
should now add only the following where truthful:

1. what concrete proof makes late entry or retroactive repair executable rather than merely
   sympathetic;
2. what boundary still blocks fictional retroactive attendance, participation, or completed
   coursework;
3. when record repair or withdrawal truthfully substitutes for late-entry repair; and
4. which owner and timing still govern the execution path.

That is enough to make approved-exception execution legible without inventing one universal catch-up
amnesty.

## What counted as a real archive gain

The archive already knew when a named office still admitted post-window fault-based relief. It still
lacked the next tighter answer: **what current institutions require before that relief can be
executed honestly, and what they still refuse to fictionalize even after the institution itself is
partly at fault**.

This document answers yes, but only barely.

It now allows the archive to publish one thin field set that distinguishes:

- a real execution-proof path from a merely sympathetic exception approval;
- no-penalty make-up from retroactive-attendance fiction;
- course-entry repair from record correction or withdrawal; and
- instructor/dean/registrar certification from vague `someone approved this` language.

That is a real operating gain because it blocks four opposite mistakes at once:

- treating every approved exception as if honest late participation were automatically possible;
- treating no-penalty make-up during official enrollment windows as if it justified backdating
  attendance or participation;
- treating off-roster attendance or willingness to pay fees as if they could themselves create
  credit; and
- treating record-repair truth as if it were a failed version of late-entry repair instead of its
  own honest resolution path.
