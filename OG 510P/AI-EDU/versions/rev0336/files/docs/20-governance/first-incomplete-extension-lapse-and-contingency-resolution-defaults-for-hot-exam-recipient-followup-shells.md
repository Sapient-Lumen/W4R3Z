# First incomplete-extension, lapse-relief, and contingency-resolution defaults for hot-exam recipient followup shells

The archive can now publish a very small post-send and post-confirmation grammar for the hottest
exam-like learner-request routes.

It can already say:

- what downstream transfer evidence the owner system exposes;
- how old that transfer residue is and when direct recipient confirmation becomes the truthful next
  step;
- what the recipient side says once contacted — confirmed receipt, still processing, wait,
  resend/correct, temporary bridge, or no new state;
- what bounded interim bridge or no-penalty protection a named office admits without pretending that
  unofficial evidence already equals official receipt, posted credit, or official evaluation;
- what event ends that bridge, what later official proof supersedes it, and what ordinary correction
  truth follows if verification fails;
- what named correction window still protects the learner if official verification lands late enough
  to change the route;
- what fault-based post-window exception path may still exist once that protected correction window
  has already closed;
- what current offices require before an approved exception becomes honestly executable rather than
  a vague gesture of sympathy; and
- when honest late entry remains ordinary make-up, when it becomes a written incomplete or
  remaining-work contract, and what floor or lapse rule keeps that substitution from turning into
  generic catch-up fiction.

That is still not enough.

The archive still lacked the next tighter answer: **once an incomplete or remaining-work plan
already exists, when is an extension still honest, how does lapse actually resolve if the clock is
missed, when does the route harden to contingency grade versus default failing grade, reversion
grade, no credit, or a still-permanent incomplete, and what downstream sequence, registration, or
degree-conferral effects must stay visible instead of being hidden inside generic `work in progress`
language?**

This document adds one thing only:

- a **tiny incomplete-extension / lapse-relief / contingency-resolution field set** for those
  already named hot-exam recipient followup shells.

That means the archive now asks a different question than before. It no longer asks only **whether
an incomplete was honest at the moment it was granted**. It now asks **what current offices say
happens after that — who can still approve more time, what exact academic-state result follows if
the deadline is missed, and when unresolved temporary grades begin to block next-term enrollment,
prerequisite use, assistantship funding, or degree conferral**.

Current official signals support a deliberately narrow answer. Washington says undergraduates may
receive one additional quarter only through an instructor-led extension request, otherwise the
incomplete converts on the next-quarter deadline to the submitted default grade or `0.0`, and an
incomplete on the record at degree grant cannot later be changed. UConn says a limited extension may
be granted on instructor recommendation, but if work is not submitted within twelve months or the
instructor's shorter period no credit is allowed, and more than three viable incompletes may force
resolution before additional registration. Columbia Business says each incomplete is paired with a
contingency grade, must be resolved by the earlier of degree conferral or the following term's end
date, reverts to the contingency grade if no final grade is posted, can trigger registration hold
and course drop, and bars graduation certification while it remains unresolved. Illinois says an
incomplete used as a prerequisite must be resolved before enrolling in the next course unless a
passing outcome is already certain and the department makes an exception, and unresolved incompletes
automatically become `F` at the published deadline. Idaho says incompletes carry a reversion grade,
automatically revert if unfinished, and cannot remain on the record for graduation because they
revert and count in the graduation GPA at the end of the final term. UNC says temporary grades
unresolved within sixty days of the degree award date force a subsequent graduation and the degree
is not awarded retroactively. Together those signals support a tighter archive rule: **the next
truthful portability gain here is one tiny incomplete-extension / lapse-relief /
contingency-resolution layer, but not one universal extension rule, one universal contingency-grade
consequence, one universal registration hold, or one universal graduation-delay script.** See
`B231`.

## Small field set for incomplete-extension / lapse-relief / contingency-resolution defaults

| Code | Meaning | Default archive action |
|---|---|---|
| `IL0-NO-UNIVERSAL-EXTENSION-LAPSE-OR-CONFERRAL-BLOCK-RULE` | no one universal extension owner, lapse consequence, registration-hold effect, or degree-conferral rule governs every hot recipient followup shell once an incomplete or remaining-work contract already exists | keep post-contract resolution route-bounded instead of implying `incomplete handling resolves the same way everywhere once the clock starts` |
| `IL1-PUBLISH-WHEN-EXTENSION-IS-STILL-HONEST-AND-WHO-CAN-APPROVE-IT` | publish extension only when a current office names a still-honest additional period plus the approving owner such as instructor, dean, registrar, or graduate school | distinguish bounded relief from indefinite grace |
| `IL2-PUBLISH-WHAT-LAPSE-RESOLVES-TO` | publish whether the missed completion clock resolves to contingency grade, reversion grade, `0.0` / failing grade, no credit, or a still-permanent incomplete unless and until converted | distinguish missed-deadline outcomes instead of hiding them inside `temporary grade` language |
| `IL3-PUBLISH-DOWNSTREAM-REGISTRATION-PREREQUISITE-AND-SEQUENCING-EFFECTS` | publish only the next-step academic-state effects current offices actually name — registration hold, drop from next-term schedule, prerequisite block unless exception, assistantship/funding risk, or resolve-before-more-registration | distinguish a missed completion clock from an otherwise consequence-free clerical delay |
| `IL4-PUBLISH-DEGREE-CONFERRAL-AND-POST-LAPSE-FINALITY` | publish whether unresolved incompletes block or defer degree conferral, auto-revert in the graduation term, require subsequent graduation, or cannot be changed after conferral | distinguish later-term repair truth from retroactive degree fiction |

## Field values that now travel together

When this layer is used, the shell should publish only five concrete fields:

1. `extension_route_token` — `none published`, `one additional quarter by instructor-led request`,
   `limited extension on instructor recommendation`, `exceptional further term with dean/college
   approval`, `appeal only after conversion`, or `not published`;
2. `extension_owner_and_clock` — `next quarter deadline`, `twelve months or shorter instructor
   period`, `earlier of conferral date or following term end`, `published next-semester week
   cutoff`, `final term / final conferral window`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
3. `lapse_resolution_token` — `contingency grade`, `reversion grade`, `0.0 / failing grade`, `no
   credit`, `permanent incomplete until passing conversion`, `subsequent graduation required`, `none
   inherited`, or `not published`;
4. `downstream_state_effect` — `registration hold / scheduled courses dropped`, `must resolve before
   prerequisite enrollment unless exception`, `complete viable incompletes before more
   registration`, `assistantship or funding risk`, `graduation certification blocked`, `none
   inherited`, or `not published`;
5. `post_lapse_or_post_conferral_finality` — `cannot change after degree granted`, `reverts in
   graduation term and counts in GPA`, `later degree award requires subsequent graduation`, `after
   conversion appeal/petition required`, `no further completion opportunity after NC`, `none
   inherited`, or `not published`.

That is deliberately small. It is enough to distinguish bounded extension relief from indefinite
grace, to distinguish contingency-grade or reversion-grade lapse from no-credit lapse or permanent
temporary-grade residue, to distinguish a missed clock that changes downstream registration or
sequencing from one that does not, and to distinguish later-term repair from retroactive conferral
fiction.

## `IL1` — extension is a second bounded contract, not a mood

`IL1-PUBLISH-WHEN-EXTENSION-IS-STILL-HONEST-AND-WHO-CAN-APPROVE-IT` is the archive's smallest
anti-drift rule after an incomplete already exists.

It blocks the archive from treating every missed incomplete deadline as if the same generic `ask for
more time` route were still available.

Current official pages show something sharper:

- some institutions allow only one more quarter or one limited additional period;
- some require instructor initiation or recommendation rather than student self-service alone;
- some add dean, registrar, or graduate-school approval; and
- some move the learner onto a petition or appeal path only after the original conversion has
  already happened.

So the archive now publishes extension only when the office names a still-honest owner and clock.

## `IL2` — lapse must resolve to a named academic state

`IL2-PUBLISH-WHAT-LAPSE-RESOLVES-TO` is the archive's smallest anti-ambiguity rule.

It prevents the archive from saying only `deadline missed` while concealing the real academic
result.

Current official pages make at least five distinct outcomes visible:

- contingency grade;
- reversion grade;
- `0.0` or failing grade;
- no credit; and
- a still-permanent incomplete that remains on the record unless and until it is converted under a
  narrower rule.

That is enough for a tiny cross-shell lapse field. It is not enough for one universal
missed-deadline consequence.

## `IL3` — some incompletes change next-term movement before they change the transcript

`IL3-PUBLISH-DOWNSTREAM-REGISTRATION-PREREQUISITE-AND-SEQUENCING-EFFECTS` is the archive's smallest
anti-clerical-fiction rule.

It blocks the archive from treating an unresolved incomplete as if it were merely back-office
residue when current offices say it already changes what the learner may do next.

Current official pages show at least four recurring downstream effects:

- registration hold or even drop from an already built schedule;
- prerequisite block unless a department explicitly makes a certainty-of-pass exception;
- resolve-before-more-registration pressure once too many viable incompletes accumulate; and
- funding or assistantship risk where incomplete accumulation itself becomes an academic-progress
  problem.

That is enough to justify one tiny downstream-state field.

## `IL4` — unresolved temporary grades do not silently coexist with truthful conferral

`IL4-PUBLISH-DEGREE-CONFERRAL-AND-POST-LAPSE-FINALITY` is the archive's smallest anti-retroactivity
rule.

It blocks two opposite mistakes:

- pretending every institution simply waits to award the degree later without changing anything
  else; and
- pretending every institution resolves the issue the same way at graduation.

Current official pages make several different but still portable endings visible:

- no change after a degree has already been granted;
- automatic graduation-term reversion into the GPA;
- graduation certification blocked while the incomplete remains unresolved; and
- later degree award only through a subsequent graduation rather than retroactive conferral.

That is enough for one tiny finality field. It is not enough for one universal graduation-delay
script.

## What the archive now publishes when using this layer

When an institution or assessment family publishes one of these hot recipient followup shells, it
should now add only the following where truthful:

1. whether an extension still exists and who must approve it;
2. what the live clock actually is;
3. what academic state follows if that clock is missed;
4. what next-term registration, prerequisite, or sequencing effect appears if any; and
5. what degree-conferral or post-conferral finality boundary applies.

That is enough to make post-incomplete resolution legible without pretending the archive already has
one universal rescue script after the contract clock is missed.

## What counted as a real archive gain

The archive already knew when an incomplete or remaining-work contract was honest to create. It
still lacked the next tighter answer: **what happens after that contract starts to age — whether a
still-honest extension exists, how lapse resolves, which downstream academic-state effects appear,
and when unresolved temporary grades stop coexisting with truthful conferral**.

This document answers yes, but only barely.

It now allows the archive to publish one thin field set that distinguishes:

- bounded extension from indefinite grace;
- contingency-grade, reversion-grade, default-fail, no-credit, and permanent-incomplete outcomes;
- clerical-looking delay from real registration, prerequisite, or funding effects; and
- later-term graduation truth from retroactive conferral fiction.

That is a real operating gain because it blocks four opposite mistakes at once:

- treating every missed incomplete deadline as if the same extension path still existed;
- hiding the actual lapse consequence behind generic `temporary grade` language;
- overlooking the way unresolved incompletes already alter next-term movement; and
- pretending degree-conferral truth is uniform once a temporary grade survives into the final term.
