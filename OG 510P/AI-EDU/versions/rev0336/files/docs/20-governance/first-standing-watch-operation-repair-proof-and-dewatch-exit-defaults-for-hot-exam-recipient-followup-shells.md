# First standing-watch operation, repair-proof, and de-watch exit defaults for hot-exam recipient followup shells

The archive can now publish one still smaller operating layer for the hottest exam-like
learner-request routes.

It can already say:

- whether a restored or minimized surface has materially recurred;
- whether repeated recurrence crossed a named threshold;
- who owns a bounded standing watch;
- what surface and cadence are in scope; and
- what exit/minimization state prevents the watch from becoming permanent.

That is still not enough.

Once a standing watch exists, the operational temptation changes. A source owner can claim a page
workflow has been repaired without showing what changed. A search-provider route can pass one clean
expiry cycle while a mirror or archive slice remains unresolved. A recipient-confirmation office can
fix one report queue while a different official-record field remains wrong. A privacy/safety surface
can require event-triggered attention without turning the learner's name, URL, screenshots, ticket
numbers, or owner emails into a permanent public dashboard. A recurrence can also appear during the
watch itself, forcing the shell to decide whether to extend the watch, split the affected surface,
require owner repair, hand the matter to local redesign, or close back to ordinary status.

This document adds one thing only:

- a **tiny standing-watch operation / repair-proof / de-watch exit field set** for hot-exam
  recipient followup shells after a repeat-recurrence threshold has already justified a bounded
  watch, owner repair, or local redesign posture.

That means the archive now asks a different question than before. It no longer asks only **whether
recurrence has crossed a standing-watch threshold**. It now asks **how an active watch runs, what
counts as owner repair proof, what happens when recurrence occurs during the watch, and what lets
the shell exit without either permanent monitoring or false fresh-start amnesia**.

Current official signals support a deliberately narrow answer. Google Search Console exposes
temporary removals, request histories, owner/non-owner rows, exact-URL handling, expired requests,
about-six-month temporary removal, and reappearance eligibility rather than a permanent all-surface
watch. Google's Refresh Outdated Content route exposes request queues, `Pending` / `Approved` /
`Denied` / `Expired` / `Cancelled` states, 180-day approval expiry, exact result-URL requirements,
and additional per-URL refresh needs rather than a source-page repair certificate. Google
personal-content routes can involve URL, screenshot, status, alerts, and Search-removal/source-site
separation, which supports sensitive event-triggered proof recall but not public proof retention.
Princeton distinguishes local deletion, documents/media, caching, Google/Bing, custom search, access
control, and Wayback persistence. WiscWeb distinguishes emergency local edits, primary-contact
follow-up, campus search, directory/search-engine routes, normal-hours availability, and
no-control/no-expedite boundaries for major search engines. Rowan names website ownership,
contributor obligations, access maintenance, Web Services support boundaries, project/service
request phases, QA windows, post-implementation service requests, and accessibility monitoring
rather than one central all-surface repair owner. Internet Archive requires URL, time-period,
control-period, and review context and does not turn review into guaranteed global deletion. Bing
exposes temporary blocks, up-to-90-day expiry, cached/outdated-content handling, and
permanent-removal prerequisites rather than one durable all-engine watch state. Together those
signals support a tighter archive rule: **the next truthful portability gain here is one tiny
active-watch operation layer for proving repair, handling recurrence during watch, and de-watching
cleanly — but not one permanent dashboard, all-surface scan, watch-status stigma, indefinite
evidence vault, or fresh-start rule that erases the repair history needed for later relapse.** See
`B252`.

## Small field set for standing-watch operation, repair proof, and de-watch exit

| Code | Meaning | Default archive action |
|---|---|---|
| `XW0-NO-PERMANENT-DASHBOARD-ALL-SURFACE-SCAN-INDEFINITE-VAULT-OR-WATCH-STIGMA` | an active standing watch is a bounded operating state, not a permanent public label, universal scan, or evidence-retention license | require the watch to remain tied to its named surface, owner, cadence/event trigger, and live learner dependency |
| `XW1-PUBLISH-WATCH-RUN-STATE-SCOPE-AND-CADENCE` | the shell must say whether the watch is active, paused, clean-cycle running, recurrence-observed, repair-under-review, de-watch-eligible, de-watched, redesign-handoff, or no-active-watch | publish the checked surface, cadence or event trigger, current owner, and whether the learner has any action now |
| `XW2-PUBLISH-OWNER-REPAIR-PROOF-BEFORE-DE-WATCH` | owner repair must be evidenced by the current route or owner, not inferred from silence, expired history, or old screenshots | label the proof as source repair, search-provider state, archive/platform review, recipient confirmation, official-record correction, privacy/safety review, accessibility remediation, or local workflow redesign; do not treat one repaired surface as all-copy cure |
| `XW3-PUBLISH-RECURRENCE-DURING-WATCH-DISPOSITION` | a recurrence during watch must be classified before it extends, splits, escalates, or closes the watch | distinguish same-surface recurrence, route-native expiry, different-surface recurrence, linked mirror/archive/social recurrence, recipient/record recurrence, safety/privacy recurrence, and non-material lag |
| `XW4-PUBLISH-DE-WATCH-EXIT-HANDBACK-AND-MINIMIZATION` | the watch must have a visible exit/handoff state and evidence-minimization rule | after the watch closes, publish ordinary return, minimized residue, local-only proof, repaired-owner status, no-active-route residue, local redesign handoff, or post-dewatch relapse trigger; do not keep public proof alive without a live dependency |

## Field values that now travel together

When this layer is used, the shell should publish only six concrete fields:

1. `standing_watch_run_state_token` — `active watch`, `event-triggered watch only`, `paused pending
   owner proof`, `clean cycle in progress`, `repair proof under review`, `recurrence observed during
   watch`, `de-watch eligible`, `de-watch completed`, `redesign handoff opened`, `no active watch`,
   `none inherited`, or `not published`;
2. `standing_watch_scope_and_cadence_token` — `one exact URL route-native expiry cycle`, `one source
   page after repair`, `one search-result family`, `one image or snippet family`, `one archive time
   slice`, `one recipient cycle`, `one official-record field`, `local-only event trigger`, `privacy
   or safety event trigger only`, `no active scan`, `none inherited`, or `not published`;
3. `owner_repair_proof_token` — `source corrected and persisted`, `source removed or
   access-controlled`, `noindex 404 redirect or canonical proof`, `search-provider request state
   proof`, `route-native expiry passed cleanly`, `archive or platform review proof`, `recipient
   confirmation proof`, `official record correction proof`, `local workflow repair proof`,
   `accessibility remediation proof`, `owner repair claimed no proof`, `no repair proof yet`, `none
   inherited`, or `not published`;
4. `recurrence_during_watch_token` — `same exact surface recurred`, `same owner workflow regressed`,
   `route-native temporary block expired during watch`, `different surface recurred split shell`,
   `linked mirror or archive recurrence`, `recipient or record recurrence`, `privacy or safety
   recurrence`, `crawler lag only`, `ordinary maintenance reflection`, `no recurrence during watch`,
   `none inherited`, or `not published`;
5. `dewatch_exit_state_token` — `ordinary return after clean cycle`, `minimized residue after clean
   cycle`, `local-only proof retained`, `repaired-owner status`, `no-active-route residue`,
   `split-surface watch remains`, `local redesign handoff`, `watch extended once`, `watch reclosed
   as unrepairable route`, `post-dewatch relapse trigger published`, `none inherited`, or `not
   published`;
6. `post_dewatch_memory_token` — `retain repair summary local-only`, `retain as-of public pointer
   only`, `retain privacy redacted marker`, `retain recipient confirmation marker`, `retain
   official-record correction marker`, `retain redesign handoff marker`, `purge learner-facing watch
   label`, `reopen only on material relapse`, `fresh threshold required`, `none inherited`, or `not
   published`.

That is deliberately small. It is enough to show how the watch is operating, what proof is needed
before it can close, what happens if recurrence occurs during the watch, how the watch exits, and
what minimal memory remains for a later relapse. It is not enough to create a global monitoring
dashboard, one permanent evidence vault, one public watchlist, one all-copy repair certificate, one
automatic extension rule, or one rule that a clean cycle erases the fact that repair was required.

## First standing-watch operation / repair-proof / de-watch assignments

| Route or family | Watch-operation truth now admitted | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Google Search Console temporary-removal or snippet-clear watch after the same exact URL reappears | `active watch`, `one exact URL route-native expiry cycle`, `search-provider request state proof` or `route-native expiry passed cleanly`, `route-native temporary block expired during watch`, and `ordinary return after clean cycle` or `watch extended once` | Search Console exposes temporary and expired removal states, current/expired histories, exact URL handling, and reappearance eligibility after expiry, so the watch should be exact-URL and route-native rather than all-Google or all-engine |
| Google Refresh Outdated Content watch after repeated old-snippet or image-result recurrence | `clean cycle in progress`, `one image or snippet family`, `search-provider request state proof`, `same exact surface recurred` or `crawler lag only`, and `minimized residue after clean cycle` | the route updates Google Search for changed or removed content, uses queues/statuses and exact result URLs, and can require additional refresh requests for additional URLs showing the same content |
| Google personal-content / Results-about-you style watch for sensitive personal-result recurrence | `event-triggered watch only`, `privacy or safety event trigger only`, `privacy or safety recurrence`, `retain privacy redacted marker`, and `purge learner-facing watch label` unless current action is needed | personal-content routes can involve notifications, URLs, screenshots, and review status, but removal from Search remains different from source-site removal and sensitive proof should not stay public by default |
| campus source page, media, file, redirect, or local-search watch after source regression | `repair proof under review`, `one source page after repair`, `source corrected and persisted`, `same owner workflow regressed`, and `repaired-owner status` or `local redesign handoff` | local source owners can show direct page/file/access-control/caching repair; repeated regression is an owner-workflow issue, not proof that all search/archive/social surfaces are defective |
| WiscWeb-style emergency edit watch after local content recurs | `event-triggered watch only`, `privacy or safety event trigger only`, `source removed or access-controlled`, `privacy or safety recurrence`, and `local-only proof retained` | WiscWeb supports emergency local removal but separates local site action from campus directory, campus search, and major search-engine status/timing, with normal-hours availability limits |
| Rowan-style website ownership, contributor, accessibility, or QA watch after repeated source/content recurrence | `repair proof under review`, `one source page after repair`, `local workflow repair proof` or `accessibility remediation proof`, `same owner workflow regressed`, and `local redesign handoff` where repair cannot stabilize | Rowan separates content contributor obligations, Web Services support boundaries, service/project request phases, QA, post-implementation requests, and accessibility monitoring; a repair proof should name the affected owner/workflow rather than imply one central public dashboard |
| Princeton-style local deletion / cache / Google / Bing / custom-search / Wayback split watch | `split-surface watch remains`, `one search-result family` or `one archive time slice`, `source corrected and persisted`, `different surface recurred split shell`, and `retain as-of public pointer only` | Princeton distinguishes local files, cache, access control, Google/Bing, custom search, and Wayback persistence, so a clean campus surface does not close archive or search surfaces |
| Bing temporary block or permanent-removal preparation watch | `active watch`, `one search-result family`, `route-native expiry passed cleanly`, `route-native temporary block expired during watch`, and `ordinary return after clean cycle` or `watch extended once` | Bing exposes temporary block / 90-day expiry boundaries and separate permanent-removal prerequisites, supporting a narrow Bing-result watch but not an all-engine watch |
| Wayback Machine / Internet Archive review watch after multiple captures or time slices recur | `repair proof under review`, `one archive time slice`, `archive or platform review proof`, `linked mirror or archive recurrence`, and `split-surface watch remains` or `no-active-route residue` | archive review requires URL, time-period, and control-period context and does not guarantee global outcome; each affected capture or time slice needs scoped proof |
| mirror, social, repost, or third-party platform watch after a materially linked cluster recurs | `event-triggered watch only`, `linked mirror or archive recurrence`, `archive or platform review proof` where available, `different surface recurred split shell`, and `watch reclosed as unrepairable route` where no owner path exists | repeated third-party copies can require orientation and proof preservation, but the campus/source/search owner usually cannot repair every external platform |
| recipient confirmation, downstream record, or official score/report watch after repeated confirmation failure | `active watch`, `one recipient cycle` or `one official-record field`, `recipient confirmation proof` or `official record correction proof`, `recipient or record recurrence`, and `retain recipient confirmation marker` or `retain official-record correction marker` | the live learner dependency may be a recipient or record effect, not a public search/source surface; watch proof belongs to the confirmation or official-record owner |
| local remedy packet or appeal pathway where minimized proof repeatedly has to be recalled | `event-triggered watch only`, `local-only event trigger`, `local workflow repair proof`, `recipient or record recurrence`, and `retain repair summary local-only` | repeated proof recall can show that the local remedy packet or learner notice template needs repair, but it does not justify public screenshots, ticket IDs, or watch labels |
| ordinary maintenance reflection, crawler lag, history-row expiry, or unrelated copy while a watch is active | `clean cycle in progress` or `no active watch`, `no active scan`, `crawler lag only` or `ordinary maintenance reflection`, and `no recurrence during watch` | an active watch should not be extended merely because an old row expired, a crawler is delayed, or an unrelated copy exists without current owner path or learner dependency |

## What this layer now makes portable

Hot-exam recipient followup shells may now publish one tiny `XW0-XW4` layer after `XV0-XV4` when the
archive needs to say not merely that **a repeat pattern crossed a standing-watch threshold**, but
that **the watch has a bounded operating state, repair-proof requirement, recurrence-during-watch
disposition, and de-watch exit**.

That means a shell may now truthfully say things like:

- `watch the same Google-visible URL for one route-native expiry cycle; de-watch after clean expiry
  unless the same URL reappears`;
- `the source owner claims repair, but no current source correction or workflow proof has been
  shown; keep repair proof under review rather than closing or widening`;
- `a different archive time slice recurred during the source-page watch; split the archive surface
  instead of resetting all surfaces`;
- `recipient confirmation failed during the watch; move the live watch to the recipient cycle, not
  the public source page`; or
- `one clean cycle passed; retain a local-only repair summary and remove the learner-facing watch
  label unless material relapse occurs`.

It still may not pretend:

- that an active watch is a permanent public dashboard;
- that a standing-watch owner must scan every source, search, archive, social, mirror, recipient,
  and official-record surface;
- that expired platform history proves repair;
- that owner silence proves repair;
- that one repaired surface cures all related copies;
- that recurrence during watch always means learner fault or watch extension;
- that public proof must remain visible after de-watch; or
- that a clean cycle erases all local memory needed to evaluate later relapse.

## Why this matters for the larger archive

The archive's education-with-AI program is not only about catching repeated failures. It is also
about refusing to let a useful repair posture become permanent surveillance.

This layer matters because standing-watch states are sticky. Once a dashboard, label, or proof vault
exists, automation tends to keep it alive: every expired request row becomes a warning, every
crawler delay becomes a recurrence, and every old screenshot becomes public proof of current risk.
The opposite error is also common: one clean cycle or one owner assurance can erase the repair
history needed to understand a later relapse. The point here is narrower: publish **how the watch is
running**, **what repair proof is required**, **how recurrence during the watch is classified**,
**when the watch exits or hands off to redesign**, and **what minimal memory remains after
de-watch**. Everything else remains route-native casework, local-only retention, privacy-aware
minimization, ordinary maintenance, or no-active-route residue.
