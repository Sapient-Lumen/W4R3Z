# First proof-of-transfer and closure-field minima for hot exam status shells

This document closes the archive's next narrower governance gap after **shared status vocabulary and
escalation crosswalks**.

Three overclaims matter here:

- **chain-of-custody inflation** — once hot exam notice shells publish status words and escalation
  paths, the archive acts as if they now deserve one universal custody packet with the same evidence
  fields everywhere;
- **closure-token laundering** — any reassuring screen, collected stack, or local teacher note gets
  treated as if it proves the same kind of closure across fully digital, hybrid booklet, and
  portfolio-plus-local-record routes; and
- **transfer-proof collapse** — artifact identity, moment of transfer, closure proof, and
  next-holder proof get blurred together until nobody can tell which field actually closes the shell
  and which field only says who holds the artifact now.

Current official signals support a tighter answer. Bluebook's current answer-submission page says
students with unresolved digital AP answers must open Bluebook on the **same device** they tested
on, find the recent AP Exam under **Your Tests**, and treat either the **Congratulations** page or
the message **“Your answers have been submitted”** as proof that submission is complete. Test Day
Toolkit guidance keeps the operational handoff boundary visible too: proctors dismiss students with
submitted answers before helping students with unsubmitted answers and hand AP incidents to the AP
coordinator. Current hybrid digital AP materials keep a different proof stack visible: students
write free-response answers in paper booklets, fill in brief identification information, and place
an **AP ID label** on the front cover to connect the booklet to their registration information.
Current AP Digital Portfolio pages keep a third split stack visible again: AP Capstone students
submit their written components as final by **April 30, 2026, 11:59 p.m. ET**, teachers submit
presentation scores and checkpoint affirmations by **May 10, 2026, 11:59 p.m. ET**, and AP Capstone
policy still requires teachers to keep presentation / oral-defense videos for at least one academic
year because College Board may later request them. Together those signals support a narrower archive
rule: **the new hot-exam status shells may now share one tiny proof-of-transfer / closure-field
minimum, but they still do not justify one universal chain-of-custody packet, one universal closure
token, or one universal substitute owner.** See `B210`.

- a **tiny proof-of-transfer / closure-field minimum** for those already named hot status shells.

That means the archive now asks a different question than before. It no longer asks only **what
state the shell is in**. It now asks **what minimal field set proves which artifact moved, when it
moved, what counts as ordinary shell closure, and who holds the next operational duty when closure
is still partial or split**.

## Small grammar for proof-of-transfer / closure-field minima

| Code | Meaning | Default archive action |
|---|---|---|
| `HF0-NO-UNIVERSAL-CHAIN` | no one universal chain-of-custody packet travels across these hot shells | keep proof fields minimal and shell-specific rather than inventing a full shared packet |
| `HF1-ARTIFACT-HANDLE` | publish the smallest field that ties the shell to the authoritative artifact | name the exam record, booklet identity handle, or portfolio/local-record pair |
| `HF2-TRANSFER-MOMENT` | publish the moment the ordinary shell handoff happened or was last truthfully attempted | name the submission / collection / affirmation / retention handoff moment |
| `HF3-CLOSURE-TOKEN` | publish the smallest token that truthfully marks shell closure without pretending scoring is final | name the submitted message, counted-collected booklet closure, or completed final-submit-plus-affirmation state |
| `HF4-NEXT-HOLDER-PROOF` | when closure remains open or split, publish who now holds the next operational duty or retained artifact | name the coordinator, proctor/coordinator custody chain, or teacher/local-record holder |

## `HF1` — artifact handle is the smallest identity field, not a full custody dossier

`HF1-ARTIFACT-HANDLE` is the archive's smallest anti-ambiguity field.

The key restraint is that `HF1` names only enough to keep the shell attached to the right
authoritative artifact:

- in post-end digital submission, the handle is the specific AP Exam record reachable on the same
  testing device under `Your Tests` or `Past`;
- in hybrid digital custody, the handle is the booklet carrying student identification information
  plus the AP ID label on the front cover; and
- in checkpointed portfolio-plus-local-record continuity, the handle is the uploaded AP Capstone
  written component together with the local retained presentation / oral-defense record.

That is enough to stop shell confusion. It is not a universal chain-of-custody packet.

## `HF2` — transfer moment is the handoff moment that matters for this shell

`HF2-TRANSFER-MOMENT` is the archive's smallest time-anchor for proof.

It does **not** force one universal timestamp shape. It just requires the shell to publish the
handoff moment that matters most:

- the most recent status check or submit attempt on the same testing device for unresolved digital
  submission;
- the collection-before-dismissal moment for the hybrid booklet; or
- the final student submission and teacher score / checkpoint handoff moments for AP Capstone
  portfolio work.

The common gain is simply that later review can tell **when ordinary handoff was said to occur**,
rather than inferring from vague narrative.

## `HF3` — closure token is stronger than notice, weaker than final score

`HF3-CLOSURE-TOKEN` is the archive's smallest closure field.

Across the three shells it names different truths:

- in Bluebook, the shell closes operationally when the exam shows the **Congratulations** page or
  the message **“Your answers have been submitted”**;
- in hybrid digital custody, the shell closes operationally when the identified booklet has been
  collected and counted into the staff return chain before dismissal; and
- in checkpointed portfolio-plus-local-record continuity, the ordinary platform shell closes when
  the written component is submitted as final and the required teacher scoring / checkpoint
  affirmations are complete, while the retained local record remains a distinct artifact.

That is why `HF3` is deliberately weaker than score finality and stronger than generic reassurance.

## `HF4` — next-holder proof travels only when the shell is open or split

`HF4-NEXT-HOLDER-PROOF` exists so the archive can say who now holds the next real duty when closure
is incomplete or split.

The key restraint is that `HF4` is not always required in the same way:

- for unresolved digital submission it names the still-protected same device and the
  coordinator-owned next step if ordinary self-submission is not yet complete;
- for hybrid digital custody it usually stays quiet once the booklet is collected and counted, but
  it becomes visible again when custody mismatch, missing materials, or defect review remains open;
  and
- for checkpointed portfolio-plus-local-record continuity it names the teacher or local course owner
  who must retain the presentation/oral-defense video for at least one academic year because College
  Board may later request it.

So `HF4` travels narrowly: it proves the next holder or retained-record owner only where that fact
remains operationally live.

## What the archive now publishes when using this layer

When an institution or assessment family publishes one of the hot exam notice shells, it should now
add only the following where truthful:

1. the shell-specific receipt / handback / owner-notice minimum from `RC1-RC3`;
2. the applicable `SV` state word(s) and `ER` crosswalk code(s), if any;
3. the `HF1` artifact handle;
4. the `HF2` transfer moment;
5. the `HF3` closure token; and
6. the `HF4` next-holder proof only where the shell remains open or split.

That is enough to make hot shell closure legible without inventing one universal chain-of-custody
packet.

## What counted as a real archive gain

The archive already knew that hot exam timing shells needed shell-specific notice minima and a tiny
shared state language. It still lacked the next tighter answer: **what smallest fields prove that a
handoff or closure actually happened without flattening digital, hybrid, and checkpointed portfolio
routes into one custody form?**

This document answers yes, but only barely.

It now allows the archive to publish a small shared field set for:

- artifact handle;
- transfer moment;
- closure token; and
- next-holder proof where closure is still open or split.

That is a real operating gain because it blocks three opposite mistakes at once:

- pretending status words alone are enough evidence of handoff;
- pretending minimal proof fields authorize one universal chain-of-custody dossier; and
- pretending all closure tokens mean the same thing across same-device digital submission, hybrid
  booklet collection, and portfolio-plus-local-record continuity.
