# Sector and context profile splits for recovery defaults

This document closes the archive's next governance gap about **which recovery-packet and
aggregate-signal defaults should already split by sector and support context before local evidence
accumulates, because one generic packet-and-signal rule is too blunt for minors, credit-bearing
higher education, professional-practice training, and public adult-route services**.

The archive's current bet is:

> keep one generic recovery rule as the floor, but publish a second tiny starter profile layer
wherever developmental duty, record-bearing academic consequences, supervised-practice safety, or
benefits-linked public routing already makes a uniform recovery default misleading.

The point is to avoid four predictable failures at once:

- **child-facing recovery drift** — repeated dependence handling for minors quietly becomes
  background profiling, emotional steering, or cross-course tagging rather than a short-lived reset
  owned by a named adult;
- **course-help to standing-file creep** — a credit-bearing higher-education study surface starts
  with modest recovery packets but quietly joins academic-standing, conduct, aid, or transfer
  interpretation without a sharper handoff;
- **coaching-to-readiness conversion** — recovery in clinical, practicum, apprenticeship, or other
  supervised-practice settings begins as support but quietly becomes readiness, remediation, or
  placement memory;
- **route-help to treatment-memory creep** — public adult-route recovery notes move from re-entry
  support into eligibility, queue order, mandated participation, or cross-agency routing.

Current public signals point in the same direction. OECD's 2026 outlook says generative AI supports
learning when tied to clear teaching goals rather than generic output outsourcing, and RAND's 2026
youth survey sharpens the demand-side risk that rising student AI use can also erode critical
thinking if the service shape is wrong. The UK Department for Education's current school and college
materials, together with its updated 2026 product-safety standards, make pupil-facing safeguards,
anti-manipulation design, and clear learner-facing boundaries more explicit rather than less.
UNESCO's 2025 higher-education survey shows that governance pressure is already operational in
credit-bearing institutions, not merely theoretical. OpenAI's 2026 learning-outcomes work then
sharpens the measurement side by pushing institutions toward longitudinal learning evaluation
without assuming they need durable learner dossiers to do it. ICO guidance keeps the data floor
clear: minimise what is personal, limit retention and accessibility by default, and keep profiling
for children off by default unless there is a compelling reason. Profession-specific and
public-route signals point the same way: AAMC keeps human judgment, privacy, transparency, and
evaluation central where training also shades into gatekeeping, while DOE and UNESCO UIL both treat
AI-supported public learning and pathway help as governed educational infrastructure rather than
casual consumer chat. See `B11`, `B17`, `B30`, `B101`, `B102`, `B106`, `B109`, `B151`, `B154`,
`B155`, `B156`.

It adds one thing only:

- a **starter profile layer** for places where the archive already knows the generic recovery rule
  needs a pre-declared split.

The generic recovery document still answers the first question:

- when ordinary interaction should stay at `IR0-IR2`,
- when `IR3` teacher-owned recovery is justified,
- what a temporary `RP1` packet may contain,
- and which `AG1-AG5` service-improvement signals may remain aggregate-only.

This document answers the second question:

- in this sector or support context, does the archive already know that packets must start cooler,
  aggregate signals must stay narrower, or recovery must move earlier onto a human-owned rail even
  before local evidence accumulates?

## The starter profile table

The archive keeps this table intentionally small. These are **starter inherited profiles**, not a
full recovery-governance manual.

| Profile | Scope | Ordinary packet posture | Ordinary aggregate posture | Mandatory branch / retreat triggers | Presumptive ceiling or required rail | Why the split exists |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `RD-K12-MINORS` | repeated school-managed study help, tutoring, feedback, or school-help chat for minors | prefer `IR0-IR2` without learner-bound packet; allow `RP1` only for a named adult-owned reset, one clear construct/support reason, and short expiry | keep to the coolest truthful `AG1-AG4`, minimum-cell or de-identified, usually at service/site/time-window level rather than learner- or class-rank views | required/default-path use; emotional stickiness; attendance/behaviour/wellbeing linkage; guardian/safeguarding involvement; repeated cross-course reuse pressure; any move toward inferred motivation, vulnerability, or relational state | many minor-facing uses should stay at `RP0` or the coolest `RP1`; anything touching welfare, safeguarding, discipline, or durable learner treatment moves onto a separate human-owned rail or human-only handling | minors create a faster duty of care and a lower tolerance for hidden profiling, manipulative dependence handling, or pseudo-relational recovery |
| `RD-HE-CREDIT` | ordinary credit-bearing higher-education study help, tutoring, drafting feedback, or course-support AI | course-bound `RP1` may be ordinary where one named teacher/tutor/service owner is resetting a support path with short expiry | `AG1-AG5` may be used at course, service, or programme level with minimum-cell or de-identified reporting and no learner ranking | academic standing, misconduct, aid, transfer, progression interpretation, formal accommodation handling, official record use, or cross-course patterning that materially changes treatment | course/service recovery may remain at `RP1`; record-bearing academic action should move to named teacher/programme-owned rails and should not inherit tutoring packets by default | adult status does not remove the risk that modest-seeming recovery support quietly becomes standing, conduct, or aid treatment memory |
| `RD-PRO-PRACTICE` | clinical, practicum, apprenticeship, teacher-training, and other supervised-practice settings where support also shades toward readiness or safety | bounded `RP1` may support coaching resets, simulation re-entry, or supervisor-visible remediation setup while the matter remains support-shaped and short-lived | keep aggregates at construct, service, site, or supervisor-group level; use them for branch repair and staffing, not for unreviewed readiness scoring | readiness sign-off, remediation intensity, placement, client/patient-facing exposure, fitness-to-practice concern, or progression to supervised responsibility | support recovery may remain at governed `RP1`; readiness, remediation, or safety-bearing uses should move onto supervisor-owned `M3` rails or human-only handling; AI-owned readiness memory should not harden by drift | these contexts mix learning support with safety-bearing human judgment, so coaching recovery and gatekeeping memory cannot share one ceiling |
| `RD-PUBLIC-ROUTE` | adult basic education, workforce, library, community-college/VET, and other public-route study or navigation supports | informational or bridge-support recovery may sit at `RP0-RP1` with one named owner, short expiry, and no quiet cross-agency travel | `AG1-AG5` may be used at route/service level for staffing, branch repair, language/access mismatch, and path-pressure diagnosis, but only in de-identified or minimum-cell form | funded-seat access, eligibility shaping, queue order, mandated participation, benefits-linked referral, cross-agency reuse, or route treatment that materially changes who is prioritized or denied a simpler path | informational recovery may remain at `RP0-RP1`; route-treatment memory should move onto a named route-case rail or human-only handling; cross-agency learner-risk files are presumptively out of bounds | public-route systems can look clerical while materially shaping access to funded learning, support, or public programmes |

## Read the profiles correctly

These profiles are **overlays**, not replacements.

The generic recovery document still answers the first question:

- should ordinary interaction stay inside AI-owned reset or cool-down,
- should a temporary packet exist at all,
- and which aggregate service-improvement signals are allowed?

This document answers the second question:

- in this sector or support context, does the archive already know that even the generic recovery
  floor needs a stricter starting point?

That means the same base study tool can inherit different recovery defaults in different places:

- an optional adult study helper may still live mostly under the generic packet-and-signal rule;
- the same base model normalized for minors belongs under `RD-K12-MINORS`, where repeated use,
  developmental duty, and anti-profiling concerns keep recovery cooler;
- the same model used in a credit-bearing university course may sit under `RD-HE-CREDIT`, where
  course-bound recovery is more plausible but joins to standing or aid still trigger a sharper
  handoff;
- the same model used in clinical, practicum, or apprenticeship coaching belongs under
  `RD-PRO-PRACTICE`, where readiness and safety keep human-owned rails closer;
- and the same family of model used in public adult-route learning or navigation belongs under
  `RD-PUBLIC-ROUTE`, where funded-seat and cross-agency effects make one generic recovery default
  misleading.

## Retreat triggers the archive now treats as knowable in advance

Local evidence still matters, but the archive now treats five retreat triggers as knowable
**before** local pilots accumulate:

1. **live proof or controlled assessment context** rather than ordinary study support;
2. **record-bearing academic or pathway consequence** rather than course-bound pedagogical reset;
3. **readiness, remediation, or safety-bearing supervised-practice judgment** rather than ordinary
   coaching;
4. **benefits-linked, funded-seat, queue-order, or cross-agency route treatment** rather than
   informational help;
5. **minor-facing repeated use plus relational, behavioural, or welfare linkage** rather than
   bounded study support.

When one of those is present, the archive prefers an earlier human-owned rail, a cooler `RP0`
default, or direct human handback rather than “slightly hotter packeting.”

## The disposition rule: harden, branch, stay provisional, or retreat

These are starter profiles, so the archive now adds one small disposition rule.

### Harden unchanged when...

A profile may harden unchanged only when all four conditions are true:

1. the same short-lived packet shape works across several comparable deployments;
2. aggregates remain genuinely de-identified or minimum-cell and are used only for branch repair or
   staffing;
3. the same context is not quietly producing learner-treatment joins, welfare escalation, readiness
   scoring, or route-allocation effects;
4. local operators are not rebuilding hotter casework through side channels because the published
   profile is too cool to describe reality.

### Branch when...

A profile should branch further when one row is hiding a recurring split, for example:

- optional minor-facing use versus required/default-path use;
- course-bound higher-ed recovery versus programme-standing or aid-linked support;
- coaching reset versus readiness/remediation handling in supervised-practice settings;
- informational public-route help versus funded-seat or mandated-participation routing.

### Stay provisional when...

Keep the profile provisional when implementation is still thin, when the same vendor/service is
operating under visibly different staffing or notice conditions, or when the archive cannot yet tell
whether a repeated hot case is structural or merely local variance.

### Retreat when...

Retreat below even the profile floor when packet creation is mostly serving risk memory rather than
re-entry help, when the aggregate view is too granular to avoid effectively ranking named learners,
or when the service repeatedly routes the same cases into safeguarding, readiness,
academic-standing, or public-route treatment even though the published recovery profile still claims
to be support-only.

## What institutions should publish

Where a recurring study companion, tutoring surface, or feedback assistant inherits one of these
profiles, publish only six additions beyond the generic recovery rule:

1. profile identifier (`RD-K12-MINORS` through `RD-PUBLIC-ROUTE` or local equivalent);
2. whether `RP1` is ever used in that context and the maximum ordinary expiry;
3. which aggregate signal families (`AG1-AG5`) are actually used there;
4. which joins or downstream uses are explicitly prohibited;
5. which triggers move the case onto a named human-owned rail or direct human handback;
6. whether the profile is inherited unchanged or locally cooled further.

That is enough to keep recovery legible without publishing a telemetry manual or a giant
safeguarding annex.

## What counted as a real archive gain

The archive previously had a generic packet-and-signal rule but still left one of the largest
implementation questions unresolved: **which support contexts are already different enough that they
should not wait for bespoke local policy before becoming cooler, narrower, or earlier-human-owned?**

This document answers that with a bounded starter layer. It keeps the generic rule, but it now makes
the archive more deployable across schools, higher education, professional-practice settings, and
public adult-route learning without pretending those contexts have the same dependence,
accountability, or consequence shape.

## Current archive bet

The archive's current best guess is that a **generic recovery rule plus a tiny sector-and-context
profile layer** will outperform both extremes:

- one universal packet-and-signal default for all learning support;
- and bespoke institution-by-institution recovery writing with no inherited starter profiles.

That claim is now canon, but still live. The next problem has now moved one step further again:
which of the partially portable treatment branches deserve one further split or mini-code around
non-worsening, minimal-loss continuity, or authority-sensitive handback, and which should remain
permanently local residue. See

[`portable-review-window-substitute-path-and-publication-defaults-for-recovery-branches.md`](portable-review-window-substitute-path-and-publication-defaults-for-recovery-branches.md),
and `OQ-0028`.
