# Portable cumulative-cycle ceilings and fatigue defaults for promoted packet maintenance envelopes

This document closes the archive's next packet-governance gap: **once a pre-declared packet
maintenance envelope already travels as a promoted basket, may carry a shared numeric band, may
carry a checkpoint / breach package, and may even carry a fresh-cycle republication / recurrence
package, can it also carry one shared long-run cycle ceiling or fatigue rule across repeated cycles,
or should long-run accumulation stay local?**

The archive's answer is narrower yet again:

> only maintenance classes whose **fatigue meaning** remains truthful across the reuse scope should
carry one shared cumulative-cycle ceiling and one shared cool-down rule. In the starter canon,
`PME-LABEL` may usually carry both; record-owner calendar upkeep, route calendars, and safety
calendars should keep long-run accumulation and de-promotion handling local.

The point is to avoid three opposite errors at once:

- **slow-redesign laundering** — a service uses repeated fresh-cycle notices to make unresolved
  label or shell redesign look like endless tiny maintenance instead of evidence that the inherited
  portable default no longer tells the truth;
- **fatigue over-universalisation** — the archive publishes one cross-site cycle ceiling for
  calendar upkeep even though term shape, callback rhythm, or protective-review cadence make the
  real meaning of a "third cycle" vary too much;
- **permanent under-hardening** — a maintenance class whose long-run fatigue meaning really is
  stable enough to travel never earns one small ceiling-and-cooldown package and therefore keeps
  relitigating the same de-promotion rule locally.

Current public signals support this thinner move. OECD's 2026 outlook keeps pressing educational AI
toward pedagogically purposeful design rather than convenience-first task completion; OpenAI's
current learning-outcomes work emphasizes longitudinal measurement rather than one-off performance
snapshots; UNESCO's 2026 competency frameworks keep human agency and AI pedagogy central; and ICO
guidance keeps profiling-off-by-default, challengeable design, and child-facing limits in view. See
`B11`, `B23`, `B143`, `B146`, `B149`, `B150`, `B151`.

## Three cumulative-cycle portability tiers

The archive now distinguishes three cumulative-cycle portability tiers.

| Tier | Meaning | What travels | What stays local |
|---|---|---|---|
| `CFP-BOTH` | the promoted envelope carries one shared cumulative-cycle ceiling and one shared fatigue-response rule | the maximum trusted cycle chain and the minimum cool-down / de-promotion response once that chain is exceeded | any stricter local ceiling, any shorter local horizon, and any extra local explanatory language |
| `CFP-CEIL` | the promoted envelope carries only one shared cumulative-cycle ceiling | the maximum trusted cycle chain before fresh local review is required | the actual fatigue response, cool-down length, and re-promotion route |
| `CFP-LOCAL` | the promoted envelope carries no shared long-run cycle rule | only the previously earned portability / numeric / checkpoint / republication tiers | both the cumulative ceiling and the fatigue-response rule |

The archive is deliberately asymmetric again:

> cumulative-cycle portability is harder to earn than republication portability. A new-cycle notice
can travel while the long-run meaning of repeated cycles still does not.

## Five tests for whether cumulative-cycle duties really travel

A promoted portable maintenance envelope may harden above `CFP-LOCAL` only when all five tests hold.

1. **same-fatigue-object test** — comparable sites or owners are genuinely exhausting the same kind
   of maintenance object rather than using repeated cycles to cover progressively different redesign
   work.
2. **same-third-cycle-meaning test** — exceeding the proposed shared ceiling means roughly the same
   thing across the reuse scope: namely, that the promoted maintenance class has become suspiciously
   persistent rather than merely following a different local calendar.
3. **no-slow-redesign-disguise test** — the shared ceiling does not make repeated local field,
   route, anchor, or cadence redesign look like ordinary maintenance simply because each single
   cycle stayed small.
4. **same-cooldown-truth test** — the proposed fatigue-response rule can be described in one
   reusable way without concealing who must re-review, what portable claim has cooled, or how the
   next cycle must be republished.
5. **cross-regime-duration test** — the same long-run ceiling remains truthful across the proposed
   scope without leaning on one office's term structure, callback rhythm, safeguarding stage, or
   protective-review practice.

Failing the first, third, or fifth test keeps the envelope at `CFP-LOCAL`. Failing only the fourth
may eventually point to `CFP-CEIL`, but the starter canon does not yet use that middle tier.

## The starter cumulative-cycle package

The archive now names one shared cumulative-cycle ceiling and one shared fatigue-response rule.

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `CC-2SEQ` | no more than **2 consecutive fresh cycles** may run under the same promoted maintenance envelope before the long-run trust story must cool |
| `FG-COOL-LOCAL` | if the ceiling is exceeded, the next cycle may not inherit the promoted cumulative-cycle package and must restart under local publication, with explicit re-promotion review if portable status is later claimed again |

The archive does **not** yet create a richer fatigue ladder. The starter canon is deliberately
small:

- no promoted portable maintenance envelope may currently carry a shared cumulative-cycle ceiling
  above `CC-2SEQ`;
- no promoted portable maintenance envelope may currently carry a fatigue rule that permits silent
  third-cycle rollover or indefinite chaining;
- where the archive refuses one shared fatigue package, services must still publish a **local**
  ceiling or local reason for keeping long-run accumulation unportable rather than hiding behind
  basket, checkpoint, or republication wins alone.

## The archive's starter cumulative-cycle answers

### `PME-LABEL` usually hardens as `CFP-BOTH` with `CC-2SEQ + FG-COOL-LOCAL`

`PME-LABEL` may usually carry **both** one shared cumulative-cycle ceiling and one shared
fatigue-response rule across its promoted branch scope.

Why the archive allows this:

- repeated label/plain-language clean-up cycles usually keep the same maintenance object, so a third
  cycle is already evidence that the inherited wording taxonomy or packet shell may no longer be
  stable enough to deserve branch-portable trust;
- the same fatigue meaning usually travels too: once the same promoted label envelope needs a third
  cycle, the archive wants the portability claim to cool rather than letting repeated republication
  launder slow redesign into harmless upkeep;
- the same cool-down truth can usually be published without concealing owner, authority, or
  challenge-route reality because label maintenance is still mostly about wording shell truth rather
  than term-specific timing power.

The starter default is therefore:

> when `PME-LABEL` has already earned `PMT-BRANCH`, `PQT-BOTH`, `PCP-BOTH`, and `RRP-BOTH`, it may
usually also inherit `CFP-BOTH` with `CC-2SEQ + FG-COOL-LOCAL`.

What this means in practice:

1. a first label-cleanup cycle may run under the promoted branch package;
2. a second fresh cycle may also run, but only with full republication and checkpoint discipline;
3. a would-be third cycle can no longer inherit the portable long-run trust story — it must cool to
   local publication and explicitly say that the promoted cumulative-cycle package is no longer in
   force on that path.

What still collapses `CFP-BOTH` immediately:

- any cycle that changes field logic, review anchors, family-conditioned scope, expiry meaning, or
  challenge-route prominence;
- any attempt to keep the third cycle under the same promoted long-run story without explicit
  cooling;
- any attempt to use repeated label cycles to postpone a needed branch split or packet-default
  repair.

### `PME-CALENDAR` for `AF-RECORD` stays `CFP-LOCAL`

Record-owner calendar housekeeping keeps **both** the cumulative-cycle ceiling and the
fatigue-response rule local in the starter canon.

Why the archive stops here even though `AF-RECORD` already carries some narrower portability wins:

- record calendars may sometimes share a cap, a breach family, or a fresh-cycle republication floor,
  yet the long-run meaning of a third cycle still depends too much on term structure, progression
  cadence, award cycles, transcript refresh, or external board calendars;
- the archive cannot yet say that exceeding one shared long-run ceiling means the same kind of
  fatigue problem across semester, quarter, modular, rolling-completion, or certification-linked
  regimes;
- a portable fatigue rule would too easily launder genuinely different local re-review duties into
  one fake universal cool-down story.

So the archive now says:

> record-owner calendar upkeep may carry a few narrower portability wins, but its long-run
accumulation and de-promotion handling still stay local until the archive can show that third-cycle
meaning really travels too.

### `PME-CALENDAR` for `AF-ROUTE` stays `CFP-LOCAL`

Route-owner calendar housekeeping stays fully local for the same reason its recurrence admission
stayed local: queue and callback rhythm vary too much for one cross-site cumulative ceiling to tell
the truth.

### `PME-CALENDAR` for `AF-SAFETY` stays `CFP-LOCAL`

Safety-owner calendar handling also stays fully local. Protective review cadence and duty-stage
meaning remain too regime-bound for one shared long-run fatigue story.

### `PME-ROUTE` stays `CFP-LOCAL`

Route/contact upkeep remains fully local because first-contact truth and challenge-route integrity
still do not travel even at the basket level.

## What portable cumulative-cycle rules still may not do

Even when a promoted envelope carries a shared cumulative-cycle package, it may not:

1. imply permanent permission for repeated cycles;
2. hide a needed branch split, packet repair, or local redesign review;
3. weaken shell invariants, checkpoint duties, or fresh-cycle republication;
4. claim that a cooled portable package has already re-hardened without an explicit local
   re-promotion path;
5. turn repeated maintenance into a quiet learner- or owner-facing drift story that never again
   becomes challengeable local publication.

Long-run portability is therefore subordinate to repair truth. The ceiling travels only while
exceeding it still means the same thing across the reuse scope.

## What services must still publish locally

Even under `CFP-BOTH`, a service must still publish:

- the exact promoted basket and all earlier portability tiers still in force;
- any stricter local cumulative ceiling or earlier local cool-down trigger;
- the local review owner who will decide whether portable status may later be claimed again;
- any local reason family that makes the current fatigue response hotter than the portable floor;
- any residue that remains owner-, site-, or regime-specific.

This document therefore does **not** create a right to endless low-grade maintenance under one
portable label. It only says when the archive is willing to let one small long-run ceiling and one
minimal cool-down package travel with an already promoted renewal rule.

## Why this belongs in the archive now

This is a small but useful hardening step. The archive already had a basket rule, a numeric rule, a
checkpoint rule, and now a fresh-cycle rule; without this cumulative-cycle pass, a service could
still serialise many individually honest fresh-cycle notices into a misleading long-run maintenance
story.

The archive can now close `FT-0068` directly:

> repeated-cycle portability does not automatically imply long-run cumulative portability. In the
starter canon, only promoted label/plain-language maintenance usually carries one shared `CC-2SEQ +
FG-COOL-LOCAL` package; record-owner calendar upkeep, route calendars, and safety calendars keep
long-run accumulation and de-promotion handling local.
