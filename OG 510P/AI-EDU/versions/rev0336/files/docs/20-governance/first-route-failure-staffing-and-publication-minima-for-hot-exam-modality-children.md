# First route-failure, staffing, and publication minima for hot exam-modality children

This document closes the archive's next narrower governance gap about **which of the new hot
exam-modality children and fallback shells now deserve a tiny inheritable operational layer for
route failure, staffing truth, and learner-facing publication**.

Four failure modes matter here:

- **silent cross-mode improvisation** — a software route fails and the institution quietly
  substitutes a human route, or vice versa, as if the two were automatically interchangeable;
- **test-day software fragility laundering** — student-operated dictation / speech-to-text is
  treated as though device compatibility and practice are someone else's problem until the exam is
  already live;
- **ad hoc human-route substitution** — a reader, writer/scribe, interpreter, or similar assistant
  is supplied informally without the named approval, script, or role boundary the owning authority
  expects;
- **authentic-assistance drift** — a permitted assistant for presentation, defense, or response
  delivery slides into altering the student's work or pre-briefing the defense.

Current official signals point to a deliberately thin answer. NCEO still says accessibility policy
is state-determined, often content-area specific, and may rely on embedded technology or a human
provider. WIDA still centers valid assessment results, says state-specific policy remains live, and
forbids modifications that change what the test measures; its current ACCESS materials also continue
to treat human-reader-type routes as ordered / scripted accommodations rather than casual test-room
improvisation. College Board's current digital AP accommodations pages still distinguish embedded
TTS / screen-reader routes from a human reader, distinguish approved voice recognition from
writer/scribe, and require explicit approval for writer/scribe on digital tests. College Board's
current recording-response guidance now adds a narrower operational signal too: students using
dictation / speech-to-text should practice a digital test in Bluebook to make sure there are no
issues on test day. Current AP Capstone / AP Research accommodation and policy materials then keep
the staffing boundary explicit: assistants must deliver work as dictated by the student, must not
alter authentic work, and may not reveal defense questions before the presentation. Together those
signals support a tighter archive rule: **the first inheritable operational layer here is not
substitute scoring logic, but route truth** — preflight where the student operates software, named
approved human routes where a human assistant is part of the accommodation, no silent cross-mode
swapping when the ordinary route fails, and a tiny learner-facing failure shell that tells the truth
about who decides continue / pause / reschedule. See `B204`.

## Small grammar for route failure, staffing, and publication minima

| Code | Meaning | Default archive action |
|---|---|---|
| `RM1-PREFLIGHT` | student-operated software route must be checked in advance for compatibility / practice on the real testing stack | require pretest readiness confirmation; do not wait for first live use |
| `HM1-NAMED-HUMAN-ROUTE` | human reader / writer-scribe / interpreter route must be the named approved human route with whatever script, training, or formal approval the owner requires | no ad hoc human substitution |
| `FM1-NO-SILENT-SWAP` | failure of the ordinary approved mode does not authorize automatic cross-mode substitution | escalate to the named route owner |
| `PS2-FAILURE-SHELL` | publish the ordinary approved route, the owner who decides substitution or pause, and whether unresolved failure means continue, pause, or reschedule / manual handling | keep failure truth visible even while local procedure stays local |
| `HM2-AUTHENTIC-ASSIST` | where presentation / defense assistance is allowed, the assistant may deliver or relay only as dictated, may assist within the published role, and may not alter authentic work or pre-reveal defense questions | publish assistant boundary rather than invent a new scoring child row |

## `SR-READ-FOUND-01A2A` / `SR-READ-FOUND-01A2B` — no silent cross-mode substitution

For the hot reading shells, the archive is still **not** naming embedded-TTS versus human-reader
child rows. But the archive can now say something operationally sharper when the published route
fails.

`FM1-NO-SILENT-SWAP` means:

- if the approved route is embedded / software reading access, staff may not silently switch to a
  human reader merely because the device route has failed;
- if the approved route is human, staff may not silently move the learner onto a software route
  merely because a human reader is unavailable;
- the failure must move to the owner who can truthfully decide whether the learner continues,
  pauses, or reschedules under the governing assessment policy.

That claim follows from the same asymmetry already visible in NCEO and WIDA: policies vary by
assessment owner, content area, and validity frame. The stable inherited layer is therefore route
truth, not a portable substitute script. See `B204`.

`PS2-FAILURE-SHELL` for hot reading means the shell should now publish at least:

- the ordinary approved route;
- the owner of substitute-path authority;
- the action-now truth if that route fails (`continue`, `pause`, or `manual / reschedule decision
  pending`);
- and, where a human route is part of the approval, whether that route requires a named script /
  ordered material rather than ordinary classroom read-aloud behavior.

For `SR-READ-FOUND-01A2B`, the archive now also allows one thinner staffing claim: when a human
route is the approved route, it should be the named human route the assessment owner recognizes, not
a casual room-level improvisation. WIDA's current materials do not justify one portable cross-state
human-reader procedure, but they do justify refusing ad hoc substitution. See `B204`.

## `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B1A` — preflight is now part of the route

`SR-WRITE-RHET-01B1A` already captured the software-versus-human modality split. The next stable
operational layer is now `RM1-PREFLIGHT`.

That is a narrow but real change. College Board's current recording-response guidance now explicitly
tells students using dictation / speech-to-text to try a digital practice test in Bluebook to make
sure there are no issues on test day. The archive therefore treats readiness-checking as part of the
honest published route, not as optional local helpfulness. See `B204`.

`RM1-PREFLIGHT` means:

- the learner should not meet the software route for the first time in the live assessment window;
- compatibility and practice belong to the published route, not to post-failure blame;
- if the route still fails live, the institution must use `FM1-NO-SILENT-SWAP` and the named `PS2`
  failure owner rather than silently converting the learner to writer/scribe.

The archive is **not** yet hardening a portable same-day technical troubleshooting script.
Device-specific remediation, local network contingencies, and test-center timing still remain local.

## `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B1B` — approved writer/scribe is now a named human route, not generic backup labor

College Board's current digital AP accommodations page keeps writer/scribe explicit: the school must
submit a request, the student must be approved for writer/scribe for digital tests, and that route
governs the full exam rather than acting as an invisible synonym for voice recognition. That is
enough for `HM1-NAMED-HUMAN-ROUTE`. See `B204`.

`HM1-NAMED-HUMAN-ROUTE` means:

- the human recording route must be the formally approved human route for that test family;
- staff cannot quietly treat any available adult as equivalent to the approved writer/scribe path;
- failure of human availability still does **not** authorize an automatic move into dictation /
  speech-to-text.

That is why `HM1` pairs with `FM1-NO-SILENT-SWAP` and `PS2-FAILURE-SHELL` rather than with a
portable substitute procedure.

## `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B2` — assistant boundaries are now inheritable, but same-day rescue remains local

For checkpointed paper-plus-defense authorship, the new stable operational layer is
`HM2-AUTHENTIC-ASSIST`.

Current AP Capstone / AP Research policy keeps three things explicit at once:

- assistants must deliver performance-based work as dictated by the student;
- assistants must not alter the student's authentic work; and
- sign language interpreters or scribes may not reveal defense questions before the presentation.

That is enough to publish one small inherited assistant boundary across this family. See `B204`.

`HM2-AUTHENTIC-ASSIST` means:

- an allowed assistant may relay, interpret, or record only within the published role;
- the assistant may not become a covert editor, strategist, or pre-brief channel;
- the shell should state that assistant help is bounded by authenticity and no-preview rules.

The archive is **not** yet hardening a shared same-day rescue rule if the approved assistant is
absent, late, or interrupted. Continue / delay / reschedule handling still remains owner- and
programme-local even though the assistant boundary now travels.

## What the archive is now making explicit

### The route is part of the accommodation, not just the outcome

For these hotter exam children, the published accommodation is no longer only “student receives
help.” The route itself matters: software versus human, named approved mode, preflight duty, and
failure owner.

### Human assistance is now bounded more sharply

Where the owner publishes a human route, the archive now requires that route to be the named
approved human route, not a room-level improvisation. Where presentation / defense assistance is
allowed, the archive now also publishes the authenticity and no-preview boundary.

### Failure truth now travels farther than failure procedure

The archive can now honestly inherit the truth about who decides, whether unresolved failure pauses
the session, and whether cross-mode swapping is automatic. It still cannot honestly inherit the full
local troubleshooting or rescheduling workflow.

## What the archive is **not** doing yet

The archive is still refusing four wider moves:

- it is **not** creating embedded-versus-human scoring child rows for hot reading;
- it is **not** claiming one shared same-day technical-remediation script for student-operated voice
  recognition;
- it is **not** claiming one shared staffing replacement or delay window for absent readers,
  scribes, or interpreters;
- it is **not** turning assistant-boundary rules for checkpointed presentation / defense into a
  portable scoring logic.

## Current archive bet

The archive's current best guess is that the new hot exam-modality layer now deserves **route truth
before rescue standardization**.

That means:

- hot reading now inherits no-silent-swap plus a learner-facing failure shell;
- voice-recognition writing now adds preflight as part of the published route;
- writer/scribe writing now adds a named-human-route minimum;
- checkpointed paper-plus-defense authorship now adds a bounded authentic-assistance minimum;
- and all of them now publish who owns the continue / pause / reschedule decision when the ordinary
  route fails.

That claim is now canon. The next narrower question is no longer whether any route-failure or
staffing minimum can inherit at all; it is which of these new minima now deserve shared same-day
timing, substitute-owner, or rescheduling-default bands, and which should stay owner-local because
exam security, authority, or staffing ecology still dominates the truth. See `OQ-0005`.
