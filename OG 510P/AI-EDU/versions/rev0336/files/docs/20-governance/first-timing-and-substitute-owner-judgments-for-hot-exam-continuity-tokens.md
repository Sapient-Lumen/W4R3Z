# First timing and substitute-owner judgments for hot exam continuity tokens

This document closes the archive's next narrower governance gap after **authoritative-artifact
continuity**.

Three overclaims matter here:

- **minute-band laundering** — once continuity tokens exist, every started exam route gets treated
  as if it now deserves one family-wide portable rescue timer;
- **clock/custody collapse** — owner-visible deadlines, collection duties, and retention duties get
  discussed as if they were the same kind of timing claim when they actually attach to different
  authoritative artifacts; and
- **substitute-owner fantasy** — proctors, help-room staff, interpreters, scribes, or other bounded
  assistants get treated as if they inherit one portable started-session continuity authority across
  digital, hybrid, and checkpointed defense routes.

Current official signals support a tighter answer. Bluebook's current answer-submission guidance
tells students that unresolved AP responses must be submitted within **3 calendar days after exam
day** and on the **same device they tested on**. The current AP Coordinator's Manual adds the
complementary post-end limit: submissions after the exam must be made on the original testing
device, schools should keep shared devices available until answers are submitted, and the Device
Swap feature must not be used after the exam has ended. The current Bluebook device-swap and
device-failure pages then keep active-session recovery owner-specific: device swap is a last resort
only after the start code and before testing time runs out, proctor approval is required, and the
proctor must explicitly let the student continue testing. Current hybrid digital AP materials keep a
different timing truth visible: students handwrite free-response answers in paper booklets returned
for scoring, proctors collect all booklets before students are dismissed, and coordinators return
the booklets after the administration. Current AP Research / AP Capstone materials keep a third
truth visible again: only the Academic Paper is uploaded to the AP Digital Portfolio, while
presentation/oral-defense videos must be retained locally for at least one academic year; current
accommodations guidance still treats interpreters and scribes as bounded authentic-delivery
assistants, not substitute owners of the attempt. Together those signals support a narrower archive
rule: **artifact-attached post-end clocks and custody timings now harden one layer further, but
shared minute bands and started-session substitute-owner continuity still do not.** See `B207`.

That means the archive now asks a different question than before. It no longer asks only **what
artifact keeps the attempt continuous**. It now asks **what clocks or custody timings attach to that
artifact, and whether any substitute actor truthfully inherits continuity ownership once the attempt
is already live**.

## Active fully digital continuity does not yet justify a portable minute band

The archive can now publish a negative judgment more confidently.

`MB0-NO-SHARED-MINUTE-BAND` means the existence of `AC1-RESUMABLE-SESSION` is **not** enough to
publish one shared started-session rescue timer across hot digital writing routes. Bluebook still
says device swap is a last resort only after the start code and before testing time runs out, and
the student must obtain proctor approval to continue testing. Its device-failure guidance likewise
says the proctor must explicitly let the student continue testing, and the paused-time window stays
platform-managed rather than archive-portable. That is enough to reject one family-wide minute band
for now. See `B207`.

The archive should therefore stop asking whether every resumable active session now deserves a
clock. The truer question is whether any owner/platform family later yields a reusable **receipt /
notice shell** without claiming one portable rescue timer.

## `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B1 + AC2` — the first true post-end reporting clock is digital and same-device locked

The archive can now harden its first real post-end clock.

`TC1-3DAY-SAME-DEVICE` means that once a fully digital AP attempt has already ended but answers
remain unresolved, the portable truth is a **3 calendar day** submission window on the **same device
used for testing**. Bluebook tells students exactly that, and the coordinator manual repeats the
deadline, says shared devices must be kept available until submission completes, and explicitly
forbids post-end device swap.

This is narrower than a generic incident clock.

The archive is not saying every digital interruption gets three days. It is saying only that the
**post-end unresolved-submission artifact** now carries a truthful portable notice: deadline,
same-device requirement, and no-post-end-swap boundary. That is a clock attached to `AC2`, not to
digital testing in general.

## Hybrid digital routes — custody timing is real, but it is not a learner self-cure window

The archive can now publish a second timing truth that is not numeric.

`TC2-COLLECT-BEFORE-DISMISSAL` means that once the authoritative artifact is a hybrid digital
free-response booklet, the timing truth lives in **secure same-day collection before dismissal**
plus return-for-scoring custody, not in a portable learner repair window. Current hybrid digital
materials say students handwrite free-response answers in paper booklets returned for scoring,
proctors collect all booklets before students are dismissed, and coordinators return the booklets
after the administration.

That is enough for a custody timing token.

But it is not enough for substitute-owner continuity. The booklet chain names custody, not a new
portable actor who can improvise continuity rules once the attempt is live.

## `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B2 + AC4` — portfolio-plus-local-record continuity gets a long retention floor, not a substitute owner

Checkpointed paper-plus-defense authorship now gets its own distinct timing truth too.

`TC3-LOCAL-RETAIN-1AY` means that once continuity lives in the uploaded paper plus local
presentation/oral-defense record, the archive can publish at least one stable retention floor: AP
Seminar and AP Research teachers must keep those videos for at least one academic year because
College Board may request them for review. AP Research materials also keep only the Academic Paper
in the AP Digital Portfolio, which means the defense record remains a separate local artifact rather
than collapsing into one platform session.

The archive is pairing that with `SO0-NO-SHARED-SUBSTITUTE-OWNER`.

Current accommodations materials do allow bounded assistant roles — interpreter or scribe delivery,
no revealing defense questions before the presentation, and Q&A assistance in AP Research — but
those are bounded authentic-delivery roles, not a portable substitute-owner continuity package. The
attempt remains the student's, the owner remains local, and assistant authority does not harden into
one shared restart / continue / replacement-owner grammar. See `B207`.

## First portable negative judgment for substitute-owner continuity

The archive can now say something stronger by refusing a tempting false portability claim.

`SO0-NO-SHARED-SUBSTITUTE-OWNER` means that across these hot continuity tokens, the archive should
**not** yet publish one started-session substitute-owner package. Proctors approve digital
continuation inside platform rules. Human readers, scribes, and interpreters remain named approved
routes or bounded assistants. Help-room staff may troubleshoot. None of that is yet enough to say
that once the original path is stressed, one substitute actor inherits the attempt in a portable way
across digital, hybrid, and checkpointed defense routes.

This negative judgment is progress, not hesitation. It prevents the archive from laundering
owner-specific staffing and security practice into fake portability.

## What becomes canon now

The archive's current best compact rule is now:

1. **keep minute bands local** unless an owner/platform family later yields a genuinely reusable
   timer;
2. **attach post-end clocks or custody timings to the authoritative artifact** rather than to the
   route label in general; and
3. **keep substitute-owner continuity local and role-bounded** unless the official regime later
   makes a portable inheritance path explicit.

That means the archive now carries three truthful post-continuity timing shells:

- a digital unresolved-submission shell (`TC1-3DAY-SAME-DEVICE`),
- a hybrid secure-collection shell (`TC2-COLLECT-BEFORE-DISMISSAL`), and
- a portfolio/local-record retention shell (`TC3-LOCAL-RETAIN-1AY`).

It still carries **no** shared started-session rescue timer and **no** shared substitute-owner
continuity package.

## Consequence for future revisions

The archive's next narrower question is no longer whether any timing truth travels at all. It is
whether these newly named artifact-attached timing shells now justify a tiny **incident-receipt /
handback / owner-notice** layer without overclaiming minute-band or substitute-owner portability.
See `OQ-0005`.
