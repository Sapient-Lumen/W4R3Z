# First shared publication fields and protected-access defaults for named hot exam-family children

This document closes the archive's next narrower governance gap about **what, if anything, the newly
named hot exam-family child rows can now share without pretending they have become broadly portable
scoring rules**.

Three failure modes matter here:

- **opaque localism** — a row stays officially local, but institutions stop publishing the minimum
  facts a learner actually needs in order to understand the construct, the mode, the approval owner,
  and the fallback path;
- **accommodation laundering** — the existence of approved reading or writing supports gets
  over-read as proof that one whole hot family has hardened, when the underlying scoring or validity
  logic is still family- and authority-specific;
- **authorship blur** — timed digital response-recording support and checkpointed paper-plus-defense
  authenticity controls get collapsed into one generic access story even though they solve different
  problems.

Current official signals point to a narrower answer. NCEO still says accessibility policies are
state-determined and often vary by content area. WIDA still says its accessibility manual exists to
help produce **valid assessment results**, that state-specific ELP policy still matters, and that
modifications are not allowed because they change what the test measures. College Board now
publishes a fully digital AP English free-response surface in Bluebook, keeps approved dictation /
speech-to-text / writer-scribe routes explicit for digital AP writing accommodations, and separately
keeps AP Capstone checkpoints, authenticity affirmations, digital-portfolio submission, paper
authorship, presentation, and oral defense explicit. Together those signals support a tighter
archive rule: **none of the new hot family children yet harden further as shared scoring logic, but
all four now deserve a tiny inherited publication shell, and only timed digital free-response
writing currently earns a stronger protected-access default — limited to approved response recording
rather than surrogate composition.** See `B202`.

## Small grammar for thin family shells

| Code | Meaning | Default archive action |
|---|---|---|
| `PS0-LOCAL-ONLY` | no shared publication shell yet because even the minimum learner-facing or teacher-facing fields still vary too much | keep fully local/manual |
| `PS1-SHELL` | publish a tiny shared family shell naming construct, mode, approval owner, authenticity or validity guardrail, and fallback/review path without claiming that the underlying scoring logic has hardened | publish inherited shell; keep scoring logic local |
| `PA0-NO-NEW-ACCESS` | no stronger protected-access default beyond existing local review | keep access handling local |
| `PA1-RECORD-ONLY` | approved response-recording support may inherit as protected access only while the student still owns wording, sequence, and substantive claims | publish narrow protected-access default |

## Tiny shared publication fields

Every `PS1-SHELL` family should now publish five facts even while the scoring logic stays local:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `PF1-CONSTRUCT` | what is being directly measured or what must remain student-owned |
| `PF2-MODE` | the operative test or performance surface (for example fully digital Bluebook, ELP family, or through-course paper-plus-defense) |
| `PF3-OWNER` | who actually owns the approval or interpretation rule (state policy, SSD route, course-family authority, or named local office) |
| `PF4-GUARDRAIL` | the validity or authenticity rule the support must not cross |
| `PF5-FALLBACK` | what review, substitute path, or failure route exists if the ordinary support path cannot be used |

The archive is deliberately keeping this shell tiny. It is not publishing state-by-state policy
tables, full accommodation manuals, or vendor-specific implementation scripts here.

## `SR-READ-FOUND-01A2A` — publish the owner and construct, not fake portability

Content-assessment hot decoding is still too variable to harden beyond local/manual handling. NCEO
still says accessibility policies are state-determined and often vary by content area, and it keeps
universal features, designated features, and accommodations distinct rather than pretending one
reading-support story fits every content assessment. That means the archive should now publish the
**minimum shell** for these hotter cases — what construct is at issue, which content area and
authority own the policy, which support category is being considered, and what review/fallback route
exists — without pretending the underlying reading rule itself has become portable. See `B202`.

## `SR-READ-FOUND-01A2B` — publish validity and state authority, not just "local"

ELP / ACCESS-family hot decoding has a different minimum shell. WIDA's current materials keep three
things explicit at once: the purpose is valid assessment results, state-specific ELP policies still
matter, and modifications are not allowed because they change what the test measures. That is enough
for the archive to stop saying only "local/manual" and instead require publication of the validity
frame, state authority, support category, and no-modification guardrail. It is **not** enough to
claim one reusable ELP scoring rule across states. See `B202`.

## `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B1` — timed digital free-response now gets a record-only protected-access default

This is the archive's only stronger protected-access move in this revision. College Board's current
AP English Language page and exam-mode page make the hot family surface public: the exam is fully
digital, students complete free-response in Bluebook, and responses are automatically submitted at
the end of the exam. College Board's current recording-response and digital writing-accommodations
pages then make the approved writing-support routes public too: students with qualifying
disabilities may be approved for dictation / speech-to-text or writer/scribe in digital AP exams.
That is enough for the archive to publish a stronger default: where the student still owns the
wording, sequence, and substantive claims, those approved routes count as **recording access**, not
surrogate authorship. See `B202`.

That claim remains narrow. The archive is **not** inferring from those routes that ordinary
drafting, predictive rewriting, or generic editing help belongs inside the same protected-access
shell. The only thing inheriting here is the clearly published response-recording path.

## `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B2` — checkpointed authorship now gets a stronger shell, not a stronger access default

Checkpointed paper-plus-defense authorship now also deserves a shared publication shell. College
Board's current AP Capstone and AP Research materials keep the family shape public: the academic
paper remains student-owned, the assessment also includes a presentation and oral defense, bounded
AI use for exploration/search/comprehension/grammar-and-tone is allowed, checkpoints are required,
teachers must affirm authenticity, and the submission / scoring route is the AP Digital Portfolio.
Those are now stable enough to become shared publication fields. See `B202`.

But the archive is still refusing a broader access-default claim here. Presentation delivery,
oral-defense support, and checkpoint implementation still vary too much by modality and local
practice to let one stronger inherited protected-access rule travel honestly across the whole
family.

## What the archive is now making explicit

### Local scoring logic can still carry a shared shell

A row no longer has to choose between full portability and total opacity. `PS1-SHELL` means the
archive can publish the minimum facts a learner, teacher, or proctor needs even while the underlying
scoring rule remains local.

### Timed digital writing is now asymmetric again

The archive now makes one tighter asymmetry explicit. `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B1` still does **not** harden
as one portable scoring rule, but it now does carry a stronger protected-access default for clearly
published response-recording routes.

### Checkpointed authorship needs authenticity publication more than generic access abstraction

For `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B2`, the archive now treats checkpoint timing, teacher affirmation, and
portfolio route as more reusable than any cross-family accessibility script.

## What the archive is **not** doing yet

The archive is still refusing four wider moves:

- it is **not** hardening any of these four hot family children as shared scoring logic beyond their
  current local/manual posture;
- it is **not** treating `PS1-SHELL` as if it were a national or sector-wide policy table rather
  than a tiny inherited publication minimum;
- it is **not** extending `PA1-RECORD-ONLY` from approved response-recording routes into generic
  rewriting, drafting, or predictive editing help;
- it is **not** claiming one stronger inherited accessibility rule for checkpointed paper /
  presentation / oral-defense families.

## Current archive bet

The archive's current best guess is that the newly named hot exam-family children now deserve
**publication before portability**.

`SR-READ-FOUND-01A2A`, `SR-READ-FOUND-01A2B`, `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B1`, and `SR-WRITE-RHET-01B2` now all
carry a tiny `PS1-SHELL` so they no longer disappear into opaque localism. But only
`SR-WRITE-RHET-01B1` currently earns a stronger `PA1-RECORD-ONLY` protected-access default, and even
that move is limited to clearly published response-recording routes. The rest of the family logic
stays local/manual.

That claim is now canon, but its immediate next question is now closed by
[`first-modality-child-splits-and-fallback-defaults-for-hot-exam-family-shells.md`](first-modality-child-splits-and-fallback-defaults-for-hot-exam-family-shells.md),
which adds one deeper digital-writing child split plus fallback defaults for hot reading and
checkpointed presentation/oral-defense shells. The live question is now narrower again: which of
those new modality children or fallback shells deserve shared route-failure, staffing, or
publication minima without overclaiming portability. See `OQ-0005`.
