# Proof-of-learning bundles

## Current overlay

Proof bundles should not be assigned before the construct is named. Use
[`construct-map-and-ai-use-disclosure-matrix.md`](construct-map-and-ai-use-disclosure-matrix.md) to
decide whether the bundle needs independent attempt evidence, process trace, oral defense, live
transfer, source trail, protected support record, or official assessment-body rule.

This document turns the archive's assessment claim into a compact default design grammar.

It is **not** a demand for constant defenses, constant surveillance, or maximal friction. It is a
guide for where richer proof should concentrate once routine AI use is common. The companion burden
rule now lives in
[`bundle-intensity-bands-and-sampling-ceilings.md`](bundle-intensity-bands-and-sampling-ceilings.md),
and the cross-cutting visibility/retention rule now lives in
[`../20-governance/minimum-observability-and-retention-without-surveillance.md`](../20-governance/minimum-observability-and-retention-without-surveillance.md).

## Bundle grammar

For major learning outcomes, prefer a bundle that combines at least **three** of the following five
surfaces:

1. **artifact** — the essay, solution set, code, design, performance, or project itself;
2. **process** — drafts, checkpoints, notes, or revision rationale;
3. **explanation** — oral defense, walkthrough, annotation, or teacher questioning;
4. **transfer** — a new prompt, variant, application, or live adaptation;
5. **performance** — an in-class, supervised, lab, studio, clinical, or practical demonstration.

The point is not to capture everything. The point is to prevent a polished artifact from carrying
the entire evidentiary load by itself.

## Age-band defaults

### Early primary

AI should be mostly teacher-mediated. Proof should lean on:

- reading aloud or retelling,
- manipulatives or worked demonstration,
- short teacher questioning,
- and observable performance in class.

At this stage, the archive strongly disfavors independent answer-engine use as a default.

### Upper elementary and middle grades

Allow bounded study use, but keep certification anchored in:

- short written or created artifacts,
- in-class transfer,
- verbal explanation,
- and periodic live checks.

Students should begin learning disclosure norms and appropriate delegation, not just tool operation.
See `B04`, `B18`.

### Secondary

Use fuller bundles for major outcomes:

- take-home artifact,
- process evidence,
- oral or written defense,
- and at least one transfer or performance check.

This is the first stage where “AI-assisted but still mine” must become operational rather than
rhetorical.

### Higher education and adult learning

Allow broader workflow use, but do not force ordinary credit-bearing higher education and public
adult-route learning into the same proof floor. Raise expectations for:

- method explanation,
- source and tool disclosure,
- live adaptation,
- and discipline-specific demonstration.

The archive's posture is that more powerful tools require stronger evidence, not nostalgic bans.

## Subject-family defaults

### Writing and humanities

Default major-outcome bundle:

- essay or argument artifact,
- source or reasoning memo,
- short oral defense or annotation,
- and a timed or in-class transfer paragraph.

This keeps polished prose from masquerading as sufficient proof.

### Mathematics

Default major-outcome bundle:

- worked solutions,
- error analysis or explanation of method,
- short no-AI checkpoint,
- and a transfer problem with altered conditions.

The key question is whether the learner can reconstruct and adapt the method, not only produce a
final answer.

### Science

Default major-outcome bundle:

- prediction or plan,
- experimental/lab or data artifact,
- explanation of method and uncertainty,
- and follow-up transfer to a new case.

For lab sciences, method and interpretation should carry more evidentiary weight than polished
reporting alone.

### Coding and data work

Default major-outcome bundle:

- code or analysis artifact,
- walkthrough of architecture or logic,
- modification challenge,
- and reflection on tool use, debugging, and tradeoffs.

This bundle is designed for a world where AI can generate plausible code quickly but cannot reliably
prove that the learner understands the system.

### Languages

Default major-outcome bundle:

- comprehension artifact,
- live or recorded speaking/listening performance,
- explanation of choices or corrections,
- and bounded writing support disclosure.

AI can help practice, but communicative competence still requires direct evidence.

### Career, vocational, and technical learning

Default major-outcome bundle:

- practical performance or simulation,
- rationale for the chosen procedure,
- safety or standards check,
- and transfer to a nearby real-world task.

OECD's 2026 work on neurodivergent learners in VET is a reminder that accessibility and teacher
support must be designed in, not bolted on. See `B19`.

## Labor discipline

The archive's current best guess is now specified more sharply in
[`bundle-intensity-bands-and-sampling-ceilings.md`](bundle-intensity-bands-and-sampling-ceilings.md):

- keep most work in `P0` or `P1`;
- reserve full three-surface bundles for `P2` major outcomes;
- reserve `P3` for gateway, credential, live-performance, or safety-relevant claims;
- and treat `PX` as triggered escalation rather than as the hidden default.

The generic bundle grammar now has a companion layer of hotter/cooler inherited profiles in
[`subject-family-proof-intensity-defaults.md`](subject-family-proof-intensity-defaults.md) plus a
tiny sector-and-age overlay in
[`sector-and-age-profile-splits-for-proof-intensity-defaults.md`](sector-and-age-profile-splits-for-proof-intensity-defaults.md).
The remaining live question is narrower: which of those starter profiles are actually stable across
age bands, stakes, families, and sectors once they meet real implementation. See `OQ-0003`.
