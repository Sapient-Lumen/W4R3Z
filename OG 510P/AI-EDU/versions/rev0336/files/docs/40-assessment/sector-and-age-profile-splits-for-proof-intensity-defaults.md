# Sector and age profile splits for proof-intensity defaults

This document closes the archive's next assessment gap about **which proof-of-learning defaults
should already split by sector and age band before local evidence accumulates, because one generic
burden grammar plus one subject-family table is still too blunt for minors, ordinary credit-bearing
higher education, live professional-practice training, and public adult-route learning**.

The archive's current bet is:

> keep one shared `P0-P3` / `PX` grammar and one subject-family starter layer, but publish a second
tiny sector-and-age profile layer wherever developmental stage, credential-bearing judgment,
live-practice readiness, or public-route accessibility already makes a uniform proof default
misleading.

The point is to avoid five predictable failures at once:

- **minor-homework mimicry** — schools copy adult asynchronous proof habits and quietly turn
  children's learning into after-hours explanation queues, home-environment inequality, and routine
  process capture;
- **adult-route infantilization** — adult basic, workforce, library, and community-learning routes
  inherit school-style compliance and defense burdens even when the real public goal is low-friction
  entry, confidence, and next-step mobility;
- **credit-shell collapse** — higher-education courses treat one polished artifact as enough proof
  even when transcript-bearing judgments should survive ordinary AI use;
- **practice-gate understatement** — clinical, practicum, apprenticeship, and other live-performance
  settings under-prove readiness because they inherit ordinary academic-course defaults;
- **universal-heat panic** — once one family or sector fails, institutions answer by making every
  course, every learner, and every major task run at the hottest proof setting.

Current public signals point in the same direction. College Board's current AP guidance
differentiates among AP Art and Design, AP Capstone, AP world languages, and computing rather than
publishing one universal school-age AI rule. Ofqual's current marking principles keep high-stakes
school and college assessment tied to human judgment rather than sole or primary AI marking. The IB
likewise says final assessment still relies on human examiners, even where AI may assist quality
control. AAMC's current medical-education principles treat learner assessment, simulation, advising,
and program evaluation as human-centred, privacy-relevant, and continuously monitored. UNESCO's
assessment writing pushes toward higher-order, contextualized tasks rather than polished output
alone. And OECD's current VET work plus UNESCO UIL's DELTA framework reinforce that adult,
work-based, and non-formal learning contexts need accessible, practice-aligned, non-schoolish AI
integration rather than simply importing school assessment habits. See `B19`, `B26`, `B92`, `B94`,
`B96`, `B103`, `B118`.

## Relationship to the generic proof grammar and family profiles

This document does not replace
[`bundle-intensity-bands-and-sampling-ceilings.md`](bundle-intensity-bands-and-sampling-ceilings.md)
or [`subject-family-proof-intensity-defaults.md`](subject-family-proof-intensity-defaults.md).

It adds one thing only:

- a **starter profile layer** for places where the archive already knows the generic proof grammar
  plus subject-family defaults need a pre-declared split.

The generic grammar still answers the first question:

- is this `P0`, `P1`, `P2`, `P3`, or `PX`?

The subject-family table still answers the second question:

- is this survey work,
- writing,
- studio,
- quantitative,
- laboratory,
- language,
- coding,
- or live professional performance?

This document answers the third question:

- in this sector or age-band context, should that family default already start a little hotter,
  cooler, or differently surfaced?

## The starter profile table

The archive keeps this table intentionally small. These are **starter inherited profiles**, not full
policy code.

| Profile | Scope | Default floor adjustments | Prefer these proof surfaces first | Avoid by default | Why the split exists |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `PP-K12-MINORS` | primary and secondary settings serving minors | keep most ordinary assessed work in `P0-P1`; for major claims keep `P2` but prefer teacher-witnessed or lesson-time corroboration over home-only process evidence; allow `P3` mainly for real gateways, performances, or externally moderated thresholds | in-class transfer, short teacher questioning, supervised writing/problem-solving, live reading/speaking/performance, checkpointed process inside normal class time | universal chat-log or draft-trace collection, routine after-hours viva queues, sole reliance on unsupervised take-home artifacts for major claims | developmental stage, unequal home conditions, and school duty of care make ambient documentation and home-burdened proof a poor default even when stronger corroboration is needed |
| `PP-HE-CREDIT` | ordinary postsecondary, transcript-bearing coursework | usually inherit family defaults unchanged; keep `P2` for major outcomes and reserve `P3` for capstones, gateways, and external/progression claims; tolerate somewhat more asynchronous process evidence than `PP-K12-MINORS`, but still keep most assessed work in `P1` or below | artifact plus brief explanation, altered-condition transfer, bounded method or source memo, selective process/version review where the construct warrants it | converting every major paper or project into a viva regime, detector-led blanket escalation, full provenance capture as the routine price of credit-bearing study | adult learners have more autonomy, but credit-bearing judgments still need reviewable evidence that survives ordinary AI use without collapsing into surveillance |
| `PP-PRO-PRACTICE` | clinical, practicum, apprenticeship, teacher-training, trade, and other settings where readiness for supervised practice or public safety is part of the claim | move relevant family defaults one step hotter when live adaptation, standards compliance, or readiness-to-practice is central; `P3` becomes more common than in ordinary academic courses; human observation remains primary even when AI participates in the workflow | live performance or simulation, supervisor questioning, standards/safety checks, nearby-case transfer, brief rationale tied to professional judgment | polished artifacts or reflective prose as the main warrant for readiness, remote-only proof for safety-relevant claims, AI-generated summary as substitute for supervisor observation | here the educational claim is partly a readiness claim, so the proof layer must stay closer to what a learner can actually do under live or supervised conditions |
| `PP-PUBLIC-ADULT-ROUTE` | adult basic education, workforce, library, community-learning, and other public-route settings where low-friction participation and onward mobility matter | keep entry and ordinary participation mostly in `P0-P1`; use `P2` selectively for portable recognition, bridge completion, challenge access, or funded next-step routing; use `P3` sparingly and only where a route genuinely makes a stronger public claim | short demonstration tasks, challenge checks, portfolio excerpts, contextualized transfer, workplace or community simulations, brief human review at route boundaries | school-like compliance burdens, routine long defenses, restart-scale proof for low-stakes participation, making adult learners reproduce school-age process archives to access next steps | adult-route systems need accessible, confidence-preserving proof that is strong enough for recognition when needed but light enough not to recreate school as the price of re-entry |

## Reading the profiles correctly

These profiles are **overlays**, not replacements.

The archive still wants institutions to classify the task through the shared proof grammar and
family defaults first.

Then ask the sector-and-age question:

- is this a minors-serving school context,
- ordinary transcript-bearing higher education,
- professional-practice preparation,
- or a public adult-route setting where proof must coexist with low-friction access and re-entry?

That means the same family default may land differently in different settings:

- a writing-intensive course in higher education may keep a standard `P2` bundle with a short
  defense under `PP-HE-CREDIT`;
- the comparable school-age course may still use `P2`, but under `PP-K12-MINORS` it should lean more
  on lesson-time checkpoints and less on home-captured process evidence;
- the same underlying communication or writing ability in a professional-practice programme may
  right-shift toward `PP-PRO-PRACTICE` if the writing or explanation now carries readiness, safety,
  or supervised-placement consequences;
- and a public adult-route bridge course may sit under `PP-PUBLIC-ADULT-ROUTE`, where proof should
  stay lighter until a portable or gate-moving claim is actually being made.

## Split triggers the archive now treats as knowable in advance

Local pilots still matter, but the archive now treats five split triggers as knowable **before**
local evidence accumulates:

1. **minors plus ordinary school-home inequality and duty-of-care constraints**;
2. **transcript-bearing or appealable credit judgments in postsecondary settings**;
3. **readiness for supervised practice, safety, patient/client contact, or public-risk work**;
4. **public adult-route participation where low-friction re-entry is itself a design requirement**;
5. **portable recognition or next-step routing where proof should intensify only at the boundary,
   not throughout the whole route**.

When one of those is present, the archive prefers publishing a stricter or cooler starter profile up
front rather than letting burden drift until staff either over-prove everything or trust polished
output too cheaply.

## What institutions should publish

Where a local proof table inherits one of these profiles, publish one extra line with only five
additions beyond the generic fields:

1. profile identifier (`PP-K12-MINORS` through `PP-PUBLIC-ADULT-ROUTE` or local equivalent);
2. whether the profile is inherited unchanged, right-shifted, or cooled relative to the family
   default;
3. which proof surfaces the institution prefers first in that context;
4. which anti-patterns are prohibited by default (for example, universal process capture or
   remote-only readiness proof);
5. whether stronger proof activates only at route, credit, or readiness boundaries rather than
   throughout ordinary participation.

This keeps the public layer small while blocking the common failure where an institution publishes
one proof grammar and one family table but never says that children, adult returners, ordinary
university learners, and practice-ready trainees do not start from the same burden floor.

## What counted as a real archive gain

The archive previously had a generic proof grammar plus subject-family starter profiles, but still
left the next deployment question unresolved: **which learning contexts are already different enough
that they should not wait for bespoke local policy to become hotter or cooler?**

This document answers that with a bounded starter layer. It keeps the shared grammar and the family
table, but it now makes the archive more deployable across schools, higher education, professional
education, and public adult-learning systems without pretending those settings have the same
consequence shape or burden tolerance.

## Current archive bet

The archive's current best guess is that a **generic proof grammar plus subject-family starter
profiles plus a tiny sector-and-age overlay** will outperform both extremes:

- one universal proof default for all educational settings;
- and bespoke programme-by-programme proof policy with no inherited starter layer.

That claim is now canon, but still live. The next problem is narrower: which of these starter
profiles are stable enough to harden, where they should branch further by stakes or family, and
where real implementation evidence should force retreat back toward the generic family defaults. See
`OQ-0003`.
