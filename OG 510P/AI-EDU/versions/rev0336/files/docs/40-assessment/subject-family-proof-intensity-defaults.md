# Subject-family proof-intensity defaults

This document closes the next deployment gap after the generic `P0-P3` grammar.

Institutions now need a **small set of starting profiles** for common course families so staff do
not either:

- apply the same proof budget everywhere;
- or reinvent proof intensity teacher by teacher.

The archive's current bet is:

> keep one shared burden grammar, but publish hotter and cooler starting profiles for recurring
construct families.

These are **planning defaults** for programmes, departments, and inherited course shells. They are
not laws of nature, and they do not override accessibility protections, published override rows, or
local legal duties.

UNESCO's current assessment writing argues for higher-order thinking, creativity, and ethical
reasoning rather than mere task completion; College Board's current AP guidance already
differentiates among Capstone, world languages, computer science, art/design, and
calculator-permitted quantitative settings; Cambridge's current coursework guidance treats
AI-supported planning differently from the student's own judged work and triggers deeper review only
when concern appears; ACTFL's current proficiency guidance still centers spontaneous, non-rehearsed
communicative performance; AAMC's current medical-education principles keep professional AI use
human-centred, transparent, equitable, and continuously evaluated; and the IB still relies on human
examiners even where AI may support quality control. See `B26`, `B92`, `B93`, `B94`, `B95`, `B96`,
`B97`, `B98`.

## How to read the profiles

Each family gets five default fields:

- **default major-task band** — where major outcomes usually land;
- **typical `P2` budget** — how many true bundle events a standard semester-length course should
  usually carry;
- **default defense length** — normal teacher-time target for corroborating explanation;
- **default audit posture** — whether random audit is usually unnecessary, occasional, or more
  common;
- **must-carry surfaces** — the surfaces that most often preserve the construct in that family.

These are **starting defaults**, not entitlements. Courses may run hotter or cooler when stakes, age
band, accreditation, disability law, or construct change requires it. The archive now adds a
companion sector-and-age overlay in
[`sector-and-age-profile-splits-for-proof-intensity-defaults.md`](sector-and-age-profile-splits-for-proof-intensity-defaults.md)
for places where the same family default should already shift before local evidence accumulates.

## Family profiles

### 1. Survey and general-education courses

Default profile:

- major tasks usually sit at `P1`, with only a small number rising to `P2`;
- typical `P2` budget: **1-2** in a semester-length course;
- default defense length: **2-5 minutes**;
- default audit posture: **concern-triggered only** unless a programme publicly declares a very
  low-rate audit;
- must-carry surfaces for `P2`: **artifact + short transfer + one explanation or checkpoint**.

Why this runs cooler:

- breadth is often part of the course purpose;
- staff load can explode quickly in large-enrollment settings;
- and not every threshold concept needs a full bundle if a few well-placed ones protect the main
  claim.

The archive's default warning is simple: do not answer common AI use in large survey courses by
converting every unit into an oral-defense queue.

### 2. Writing-intensive humanities and social inquiry

Default profile:

- major tasks usually sit at `P2`;
- typical `P2` budget: **2-4** in a semester-length course;
- default defense length: **5-8 minutes**;
- default audit posture: **occasional targeted audit** for selected major tasks, not routine
  post-hoc suspicion on every paper;
- must-carry surfaces: **artifact + reasoning/source memo + timed/in-class transfer or short
  defense**.

Why this runs hotter:

- polished prose is especially easy for AI to counterfeit as evidence of understanding;
- but the construct is often still reasoning, interpretation, and judgment rather than raw unaided
  sentence production alone.

College Board's current AP Capstone policy is the cleanest public example here: students may use AI
for bounded support, but must still complete checkpoints, make thinking visible, and submit work
that remains their own. See `B92`.

### 3. Studio, art, and design courses

Default profile:

- major tasks usually sit at `P2`, with some capstone or portfolio moments at `P3`;
- typical `P2` budget: **3-5**, because critique is often part of the ordinary pedagogy rather than
  an added burden;
- default defense length: **5-10 minutes**, often as critique rather than a formal viva;
- default audit posture: **little random audit**, because critique/checkpoint culture should do most
  of the evidentiary work;
- must-carry surfaces: **artifact/performance + critique/explanation + process checkpoint or live
  modification**.

Why this can run hotter without becoming bureaucracy:

- critique is already native to the pedagogy;
- a short live explanation often tells more than provenance metadata;
- but where original creation itself is the construct, AI may need to be tightly bounded or
  prohibited.

College Board's current AP Art and Design posture is the sharp public boundary case: AI is
prohibited at any stage of the creative process. The archive does **not** generalize that rule to
all studio work, but it does treat it as evidence that some creative-performance constructs really
do justify hotter or stricter profiles. See `B92`.

### 4. Mathematics and adjacent quantitative courses

Default profile:

- major tasks usually sit at `P1` or `P2`, with only gateway moments rising to `P3`;
- typical `P2` budget: **2-3** in a semester-length course;
- default defense length: **3-6 minutes**;
- default audit posture: **rare random audit**, with emphasis on first unaided attempt plus targeted
  support rules instead;
- must-carry surfaces: **worked solution + method explanation + altered-condition transfer**.

Why this profile stays moderate:

- the construct often includes reconstruction and adaptation of method, not only final-answer
  production;
- many quantitative domains already know how to govern tool use by part, condition, or phase rather
  than by blanket ban or blanket permission.

College Board's current AP calculator policy remains the archive's clearest public analogue: tools
may be allowed on some parts and not others, with the construct preserved by part-sensitive design.
See `B93`.

### 5. Laboratory and empirical inquiry

Default profile:

- major tasks usually sit at `P2`;
- typical `P2` budget: **2-4**;
- default defense length: **5-8 minutes**;
- default audit posture: **concern-triggered plus occasional notebook/checkpoint sampling**;
- must-carry surfaces: **plan/prediction + method/data artifact + explanation of uncertainty +
  nearby-case transfer**.

Why this runs hotter than broad survey work:

- method choice, data interpretation, and uncertainty handling are often the real construct;
- polished lab reports by themselves have lost too much evidentiary value under ordinary AI use.

The archive's default here is to sample method understanding and live adaptation, not to require
full continuous recording of every lab action.

### 6. Languages and communicative proficiency courses

Default profile:

- major tasks usually sit at `P2`, with some speaking/performance thresholds near `P3`;
- typical `P2` budget: **2-4**;
- default defense length: **4-8 minutes**;
- default audit posture: **little random audit**, because direct performance should carry most of
  the warrant;
- must-carry surfaces: **direct speaking/listening or spontaneous writing + comprehension artifact +
  explanation/correction of choices**.

Why this profile is distinct:

- AI can assist vocabulary, translation, and rehearsal;
- but communicative competence still needs direct evidence in spontaneous or lightly prepared
  conditions.

College Board's current AP world language guidance allows bounded AI support for exploration and
source-finding while still requiring the student's own presentation and target-language engagement.
ACTFL's current proficiency framing keeps the centre of gravity on what learners can do in
real-world, spontaneous, non-rehearsed settings. See `B92`, `B97`.

### 7. Coding and data work

Default profile:

- major tasks usually sit at `P2`;
- typical `P2` budget: **2-4**;
- default defense length: **5-10 minutes**;
- default audit posture: **occasional targeted live modification check**, not routine log
  collection;
- must-carry surfaces: **artifact + architecture walkthrough + modification/debug challenge +
  tool-use reflection where relevant**.

Why this profile runs hotter than ordinary quantitative homework:

- plausible working code is now especially cheap;
- but architecture understanding, debugging, verification, and adaptation still reveal the learner's
  actual competence.

College Board's current AP CSP policy allows AI for supplementary support and debugging but still
requires students to explain their code. AP Computer Science A goes further by making clear that AI
may support learning while assessments remain AI-free and independent mastery stays essential. See
`B92`, `B98`.

### 8. Clinical, practicum, trade, and live professional-performance settings

Default profile:

- major tasks often sit at `P2`, with repeated `P3` moments where safety, live adaptation, or
  professional responsibility are central;
- typical `P2` budget: **2-4**, but `P3` frequency may be higher than in ordinary academic courses
  because the live performance is often the construct;
- default defense length: **5-10 minutes** for `P2`, longer where `P3` warrants it;
- default audit posture: **human observation and second review where stakes justify it**, not
  detector-led suspicion;
- must-carry surfaces: **live performance/simulation + rationale + standards/safety check +
  supervised transfer**.

Why this family runs hottest:

- the claim being made is often readiness for work with patients, clients, equipment, or public
  risk;
- AI may still belong in the workflow, but final judgment and safe adaptation remain human duties.

AAMC's current medical-education principles keep AI human-centred, transparent, equitable,
privacy-protective, and continuously evaluated. The IB's current assessment note similarly keeps
final marking human even where AI quality control may assist. See `B94`, `B96`.

## Shared ceilings that still apply

These profiles do **not** cancel the generic anti-surveillance rule.

Even hotter families should still avoid:

- universal process capture;
- routine submission of full chat logs;
- ambient recording as the price of participation;
- detector-led escalation as a hidden default;
- and teacher-by-teacher inflation of `P2` or `P3` without programme review.

The archive's posture remains: **make the proof hotter where the construct requires it, not where
anxiety is loudest**.

## Minimal programme publication rule

A department or programme that adopts these defaults should publish a one-page local profile note
that states:

- which family each course primarily inherits;
- where a course departs from the inherited default and why;
- the standard `P2` budget for that family locally;
- the usual defense-length target;
- and which `P3` events are expected because of gateway, licensure, safety, or live-performance
  claims.

This keeps the burden grammar public and reviewable without pushing every teacher into local
improvisation.

## Current archive bet

The archive's current best guess is that **family-specific proof-intensity defaults** will
outperform both extremes:

- a single generic `P0-P3` rule with no family tuning;
- and totally local burden setting by individual teachers or small teams.

That claim is now canon. The next live problem is narrower: which of these starting profiles are
stable enough across age bands, stakes, and sectors to become stronger defaults, and which need to
split further once real implementation evidence arrives.
