# Sector defaults for public AI-learning recognition

This document closes the archive's next recognition gap.

`portable-public-learning-packet-and-recognition-profile.md` already says what should travel, what
should stay local, and which recognition outcomes exist. But it still left too much ambiguity at the
receiving edge. A workforce office, a library, a community-college noncredit program, a
credit-bearing college department, and a university should not all treat the same outside
AI-learning completion in the same way.

The archive's current rule is:

> **default to the weakest recognition outcome that avoids obvious duplication, then move upward
only when construct fit and assurance justify it.**

That rule is grounded in a converging set of current public signals. DOL's 2026 workforce guidance
encourages portable, learner-controlled skills and learning assertions. Credential Engine's
prior-learning-recognition model treats recognition as a published policy question with explicit
evidence, methods, and outcomes. California Community Colleges now treat credit for prior learning
as a system-building priority and explicitly name methods such as examination, portfolio review,
standardized exams, and certification evaluation. ACE's National Guide and CPL materials reinforce
that stronger academic recognition should be anchored in faculty evaluation, explicit equivalency,
and recurring review rather than improvised handoff decisions. CEDEFOP's long-running validation
work keeps the European side honest: learning outside formal classrooms matters, but it still needs
identification, documentation, assessment, and certification/validation pathways if it is to count
for further education and training. See `B40`, `B41`, `B45`, `B46`, `B47`, `B48`, `B49`, `B50`.

## The archive's three recognition tests

A receiving node should ask three questions, in order.

### 1. Construct fit

How closely does the outside learning match the outcome the receiving node actually cares about?

A short public AI-literacy module may fit very well with a repeated introductory orientation
requirement, while fitting poorly with a credit-bearing disciplinary course that asks for deeper
theory, domain transfer, or supervised performance.

### 2. Assurance level

How strong is the claim?

The archive now distinguishes:

- thin participation signals;
- bounded foundational completions with named outcomes;
- demonstrated task performance with criteria and evidence pointers;
- and stronger quality-assured credentials or assessed courses.

Published verification, clear criteria, and known review processes should move a claim upward.
Opaque vendor badges, screenshots, or unverifiable transcripts should not.

### 3. Stakes asymmetry

What happens if the receiving node gets the recognition decision wrong?

The higher the consequence, the more the archive prefers:

- challenge or placement,
- prior-learning review,
- or published equivalency logic,

instead of automatic exemption or credit.

## Default rule by receiving sector

The archive now treats sector defaults as asymmetric on purpose.

## S1. Libraries and civic first-contact points

These nodes should be generous about continuity and non-duplication, but they are not the main
academic recognition layer.

### Default mapping

- `A0` -> `R1`
- `A1` -> `R1`
- `A2` -> `R1` or occasional `R2` for skipping repeated basics inside the same civic programme
  family
- `A3` -> usually pass through to a stronger receiving node rather than decide formal recognition
  locally

### What this means

Libraries, learning-city hubs, and similar first-contact points should not ask adults to repeat
orientation, introductory safety material, or the same basic navigation workshop. But they also
should not impersonate registrar offices.

## S2. Public workforce systems

These nodes should recognize enough to shorten route time, avoid repeated onboarding, and trigger
stronger training-entry conversations.

### Default mapping

- `A0` -> `R1`
- `A1` -> `R1` or `R2`
- `A2` -> `R2` or `R3`
- `A3` -> `R3`, and occasionally `R4` only where an explicit training-provider or state policy
  already says so

### What this means

Workforce systems should use outside AI-learning claims to route people faster into the right next
training tier, not to improvise college-credit judgments. Their comparative advantage is navigation,
funding alignment, and bridge entry.

## S3. Community-college noncredit, adult education, bridge programmes, and VET transition routes

This is the archive's most recognition-friendly receiving layer.

### Default mapping

- `A0` -> `R1`
- `A1` -> `R2`
- `A2` -> `R3`
- `A3` -> `R3` or `R4` when local CPL, articulation, or quality-assurance policy already exists

### What this means

These nodes should be willing to waive duplicated foundational AI-literacy content, especially where
the outside learning is public, recent, verifiable, and clearly aligned to the local bridge outcome.
They are also the right place for challenge exams, portfolio review, short demonstrations, and
noncredit-to-credit bridge logic.

The archive's current bias is that if any public node should be operationally generous, it is this
one.

## S4. Community-college or college credit-bearing programme

This layer should still be recognition-positive, but with more construct discipline than noncredit
bridge routes.

### Default mapping

- `A0` -> `R1`
- `A1` -> `R1` or targeted `R2` for duplicated introductory or college-success content only
- `A2` -> `R3`
- `A3` -> `R3` by default, `R4` where CPL policy, credit-by-exam, portfolio review,
  industry-certification evaluation, ACE recommendation, or articulated equivalency is already
  published

### What this means

Credit-bearing programmes should prefer **reviewable pathways** over spontaneous exceptions. The
archive now treats this as the right middle posture: respect prior learning, skip obvious
duplication, but preserve faculty-governed boundary lines for transcripted credit.

## S5. Universities and high-theory/high-selectivity programmes

This layer should be the most conservative about direct exemption or credit, without collapsing back
into dismissal of outside learning.

### Default mapping

- `A0` -> `R1`
- `A1` -> `R1`
- `A2` -> `R3`
- `A3` -> `R3` by default, `R4` only when there is standing equivalency, ACE-style evaluated
  learning, credit-by-exam, or a published prior-learning/transfer rule

### What this means

Universities should not treat generic outside AI-literacy completions as automatic substitutes for
disciplinary courses. But they also should not force students through repeated basics when a narrow
introductory requirement is already satisfied. The archive's preferred university posture is:
**review and challenge before blanket denial; published equivalency before improvised credit.**

## Completion-family defaults

Receiving-sector defaults become clearer when the outside learning is grouped by family.

### F1. Orientation / first-contact workshop

Examples:

- library intro to AI and media literacy;
- public workforce orientation;
- short awareness session.

Default recognition:

- continuity and non-duplication only (`R1`),
- never direct academic credit.

### F2. Bounded foundational AI-literacy module

Examples:

- short course on safe use, verification, privacy, disclosure, and ordinary work/study use;
- public bridge module aligned to a framework with named outcomes.

Default recognition:

- `R2` is plausible in workforce and bridge/noncredit settings;
- in credit-bearing settings, `R2` should be reserved for genuinely duplicated basics rather than
  broader subject substitution.

### F3. Supervised applied demonstration

Examples:

- proctored or supervised AI-assisted research-verification task;
- portfolio-backed practical demonstration;
- evidence-linked bridge project.

Default recognition:

- `R3` is the preferred middle route;
- direct credit requires a separate policy-backed method.

### F4. Evaluated non-collegiate course, credential, or assessment

Examples:

- ACE-style evaluated learning;
- formally quality-assured VET or micro-credential with published equivalency logic;
- articulated community-college bridge completion.

Default recognition:

- `R3` by default,
- `R4` where a receiving node already publishes acceptance rules, transfer logic, challenge rules,
  or credit recommendations.

### F5. Tool- or vendor-specific workflow badge without durable policy standing

Examples:

- named-platform prompt badge;
- model-family workflow completion;
- vendor-specific “AI productivity” microbadge tied to a current product surface.

Default recognition:

- `R0` or `R1` only.

The archive explicitly rejects direct credit for opaque market badges just because they are
convenient to issue or visually credential-like. Their digital-credential form may help with
authenticity, but it does not itself raise the recognition ceiling or shelf life. See
[`standing-list-publication-profile-and-recency-windows.md`](standing-list-publication-profile-and-recency-windows.md).

## Recognition matrix

| Receiving node | Likely generous outcome | Preferred middle route | Direct credit / exemption threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Library / civic first contact | `R1` | occasional `R2` inside same programme family | almost never local |
| Workforce system | `R1`-`R2` | `R3` for stronger training-entry decisions | only under published partner/state rule |
| Bridge / noncredit / adult ed / VET transition | `R2` | `R3` | `R4` where CPL/articulation/QA policy exists |
| Credit-bearing community college / college programme | targeted `R2` | `R3` | `R4` only under published CPL / exam / portfolio / ACE / equivalency policy |
| University | `R1` | `R3` | `R4` only under standing equivalency or formal review pathway |

## What this document is trying to prevent

- **wallet theater** — every outside completion looks transferable, but almost nothing is actually
  usable;
- **shadow registrar behavior** — low-stakes public nodes start making transcript-like promises they
  cannot uphold;
- **universal dismissal** — institutions ignore prior learning because not all outside learning
  deserves credit;
- **auto-credit inflation** — generic badges substitute for real equivalency work;
- **case-by-case opacity** — recognition depends on who happens to read the handoff packet that day;
- **raw-evidence creep** — institutions demand full logs and dossiers because they never published
  recognition thresholds.

## Current archive bet

The archive's current best guess is that **sector-specific recognition defaults plus published
review paths** will outperform both universal auto-credit and purely ad hoc local judgment.

That means:

- low-stakes public nodes should recognize for continuity and non-duplication;
- bridge nodes should be willing to waive duplicated basics and run practical review paths;
- credit-bearing programmes should rely on published CPL, challenge, and equivalency rules;
- and universities should prefer formal review and standing equivalency lists over either blanket
  refusal or improvised generosity.

This remains paired to the archive's packet rule: portable claims, small handoff context, protected
local support records, and no raw telemetry portability by default. The archive now also adds a
standing-list governance rule plus a tiny publication profile so recurring cases can become public,
dated recognition tables without turning omission into denial or stale trust. See
[`portable-public-learning-packet-and-recognition-profile.md`](portable-public-learning-packet-and-recognition-profile.md),
[`standing-equivalency-lists-and-review-governance.md`](standing-equivalency-lists-and-review-governance.md),
and
[`standing-list-publication-profile-and-recency-windows.md`](standing-list-publication-profile-and-recency-windows.md).
The archive now answers how local overrides and partial-equivalency rules should be expressed:
through a bounded exception profile attached only to repeated public constraints, not through hidden
discretion or giant shared policy code. See
[`standing-recognition-exception-profile-and-local-overrides.md`](standing-recognition-exception-profile-and-local-overrides.md).
The next live question is narrower still: how disagreement, appeals, and repeated false matches
should feed back into published standing rules.
