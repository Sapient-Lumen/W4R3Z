# Minimum public entitlement and handoff standard

This document closes the main operational gap left open by the archive's public-stack move.

`lifelong-public-ai-learning-stack.md` named the nodes. This document specifies the **minimum
standard** they should share so adults and other residents do not have to restart from zero at each
doorway.

The archive's current rule is:

> treat public AI learning as a **navigable route**, not merely a collection of local offers.

That means a resident should be able to enter through a library, community college, workforce
office, or formal provider and still receive a recognisable public service: a low-friction start, a
human guide when needed, a bounded foundational offer, a warm transfer to the next node, and a
learner-controlled record of what has already been done.

This move is grounded in a converging set of signals. OECD's adult-learning work says access is
still uneven and that much adult training remains too short and compliance-shaped. U.S. workforce
guidance for 2026 pushes states toward unified, navigable systems with streamlined intake, digital
tools that guide people to the right services, and portable learning assertions. DOL's AI Literacy
Framework gives public agencies, educators, and providers a common foundation and explicitly places
AI literacy inside WIOA services, orientations, and career navigation. Public-library guidance and
infrastructure show that libraries already run low-barrier digital-literacy programmes,
digital-navigator support, and privacy-sensitive access services rather than serving only as content
shelves. See `B29`, `B32`, `B35`, `B36`, `B37`, `B38`, `B39`.

## What every node must guarantee

The archive now treats the following as the **minimum public entitlement**.

Every resident should be able to obtain, at low or no cost:

1. a **clear first-contact route** without needing current enrolment, employer sponsorship, or
   vendor account creation;
2. a **short foundational offer** covering ordinary AI use, critical evaluation, verification,
   privacy, disclosure, and basic work/study applications;
3. a **human navigation path** for cases where self-service is not enough;
4. an **accessible, multilingual, and privacy-respecting support path**;
5. a **warm handoff** to a deeper route when the next step is not local;
6. and a **learner-controlled record** of what has already been completed or demonstrated.

This is still a modest guarantee. It does **not** promise that every node provides every service. It
promises that every node participates in a public route instead of acting like an isolated pilot.

## The four shared operating rules

### 1. Low-friction entry

The first interaction should ask only for the information needed to help the learner move. Public
nodes should not demand a full institutional dossier when the immediate task is simply orientation,
diagnosis, or referral.

The archive's default intake floor is:

- goal or use case;
- current role or context;
- starting confidence level;
- obvious access constraints such as language, device, connectivity, or scheduling;
- and whether the learner wants human follow-up.

Anything beyond that needs a specific local reason.

### 2. Warm transfer, not cold referral

If the next best step sits elsewhere, the sending node should do more than post a link or name a
phone number. The public default should be a **warm handoff**: a booked introduction, a named
receiving contact, or a referral message that spares the learner from re-explaining the whole
situation.

The archive does **not** require perfect technical integration here. A human email introduction or
booked appointment is often enough. The important part is reducing abandonment at the transfer
point.

### 3. Learner-controlled portability

The route should leave behind a small portable record, but that record should be **controlled by the
learner**, not silently accumulated into a surveillance dossier.

The default handoff packet is intentionally small:

- learner name or identifier, if needed and consented;
- node of origin and date;
- stated goal or next-step target;
- foundational offer completed, if any;
- demonstrated tasks or assertions already earned;
- known practical constraints relevant to service delivery, such as language, schedule, or device
  access;
- receiving node or recommended next step;
- and a brief human note, if the learner wants one.

The packet should **not** normally include raw chat logs, private support/accommodation details,
behavioral telemetry, or broad personal profiling.

### 4. Recognition before re-intake

Receiving nodes should presume that public learning already completed somewhere else counts for
something.

That does not mean automatic credit for every activity. It means the receiving node should:

- recognise the foundational offer where appropriate;
- use the packet to skip redundant orientation;
- ask only for the additional information needed for the new stage;
- and state clearly when higher-stakes assessment, formal admission, or local verification is still
  required.

The archive prefers **recognition before duplication**.

## Node-specific obligations

The nodes still have different jobs.

### Libraries, learning cities, and community spaces

Their minimum obligation is to provide:

- trusted first contact;
- low-stakes orientation and basic workshops;
- device/connectivity help or referral;
- privacy and rights guidance for public use;
- and warm transfer into deeper study or navigation.

They should not be forced into high-stakes credentialing or complex eligibility adjudication.

### Workforce agencies

Their minimum obligation is to provide:

- pathway navigation tied to labour-market and funding reality;
- help interpreting which routes are short, stackable, or credit-bearing;
- warm transfer into providers or programmes;
- and portable records or learning assertions that make the route more navigable next time.

They should not become opaque scoring systems or chatbot-only gatekeepers.

### Community colleges, VET, and adult-education providers

Their minimum obligation is to provide:

- deeper instruction;
- bridge routes from basic AI literacy to applied programmes;
- recognition of prior public foundational work where plausible;
- stronger assessment where credentials or progression are at stake;
- and outward handoff into employment routes or further study.

This node remains the archive's preferred bridge institution.

### Formal institutions

Their minimum obligation is to provide:

- durable curriculum and credentials;
- stronger proof surfaces;
- recognition rules for external foundational learning where sensible;
- and teacher/trainer development that can flow back into the public stack.

They remain the deepest curricular node, but not the only legitimate entry point.

## What must stay protected or local

The archive does **not** recommend one giant shared learner record. The packet is now further
specified in
[`portable-public-learning-packet-and-recognition-profile.md`](portable-public-learning-packet-and-recognition-profile.md).

Some information should usually remain protected or local:

- disability or accommodation details beyond what the learner chooses to disclose;
- detailed case notes not needed for the next service step;
- high-risk personal data gathered for formal institutional compliance;
- and any vendor-specific telemetry that exists mainly because a tool can collect it.

The handoff standard is a **public-service convenience rule**, not a pretext for public-sector data
maximalism.

## Minimum technical posture

The archive's technical rule is deliberately plain:

- use structured, open, and interoperable formats where possible;
- permit a human-readable fallback when technical integration is weak;
- avoid vendor lock-in for records that describe publicly funded learning;
- and make the learner's consent, download, and sharing options obvious.

This is why the archive presently prefers a **small packet** over a giant platform promise.

## Failure modes this is meant to prevent

- **repeat-intake fatigue** — the learner tells the same story at every node;
- **dead-end first contact** — workshops without onward routes;
- **cold referrals** — links or phone numbers with no actual bridge;
- **stealth dossiers** — portability achieved by over-collecting private data;
- **node absolutism** — each institution behaves as if it owns the whole journey;
- **vendor capture** — public learning records trapped inside one product stack.

## Current archive bet

The archive's current best guess is that a **small entitlement plus a small handoff standard** will
do more practical good than either a loose patchwork of local offers or a fantasy of total platform
unification.

The required common core is now simple:

- low-friction entry,
- short foundational learning,
- human navigation,
- warm transfer,
- learner-controlled portability,
- and recognition before duplication.

That is now canon, and the packet boundary is now sharper: small machine-readable claims, a short
human handoff note, protected local support/evidence records, sector-specific recognition defaults,
a reviewable standing-list layer for recurring outside completions, and a bounded exception grammar
for partial equivalencies and local overrides. See

## Publication corollary

Where recurring outside AI-learning cases become common enough for standing recognition, public
systems should publish those rules with explicit dates, identifiers, and manual-review fallback. The
handoff standard is for learners; the standing-list publication profile is for institutions and
partners. See
[`standing-equivalency-lists-and-review-governance.md`](standing-equivalency-lists-and-review-governance.md)
and
