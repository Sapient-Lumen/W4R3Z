# Quarantine: speculations (2026-03-22)

These claims are deliberately **not canon**. They are live because they may become structurally
important, but they need stronger evidence and sharper disconfirmation hooks.

## Q-0001 — School may shift from an assignment economy to an evidence economy

### Speculation

The deepest long-run effect of AI on education may not be “students use tutors” or “teachers use
copilots.” It may be that institutions move away from counting completed assignments as the main
public currency of schooling and toward richer, lower-volume, more explicit proof-of-learning
bundles.

### Why it seems plausible

- capable AI weakens the link between polished output and learner understanding;
- institutions already feel this pressure through cheating anxiety and assessment redesign;
- tutoring, guided modes, and oral/live defenses all point toward more process-aware evidence.

### What would raise confidence

- widespread subject-specific examples of stable assessment bundles that remain valid under common
  AI use;
- evidence that these bundles improve authenticity without intolerable labor costs;
- institutional policy shifts that explicitly redefine what counts as evidence.

### What would weaken it

- strong evidence that ordinary assignment formats remain robust after modest rule changes;
- or evidence that the labor cost of evidence-rich assessment makes the shift institutionally
  unrealistic.

## Q-0002 — The best educational AI systems may look more like public infrastructure than ordinary apps

### Speculation

If AI becomes as foundational to learning as libraries, LMSs, or broadband, the long-run governance
model may need public-interest infrastructure logic: interoperability, rights protections, durable
procurement standards, and public oversight.

### Why it seems plausible

Educational AI touches minors, credentials, disability accommodations, family trust, and labor.
Those domains often cannot be left entirely to fast product churn.

### What would raise confidence

- stronger public standards,
- interoperable identity/data layers,
- and clearer evidence that market dynamics alone underprovide safety, access, and accountability.
