# Charter

## North star

Design an educational order for the 21st century in which AI increases **understanding, agency,
access, teacher capacity, and institutional intelligence** more than it increases shortcutting,
surveillance, deskilling, or inequality.

## What this archive is trying to answer

What would an **ideal but institutionally plausible** education system look like if powerful AI is
normal for students, teachers, schools, and universities?

The archive is not trying to preserve nostalgia for pre-AI schooling. Nor is it trying to excuse
empty automation in the name of modernization. It is trying to determine:

- which human functions in education should become more central because AI exists;
- which routine tasks should be offloaded because they are not the heart of education;
- which assessments still count as evidence once AI assistance is common;
- what public guardrails institutions need before adopting AI at scale;
- and what kinds of AI tools are actually worth trusting in schools and universities.

## Current thesis

The best near-term shape for education with AI is **teacher-led, student-active, evidence-rich, and
safety-governed**:

- **teacher-led** because pedagogy, care, judgment, and escalation remain irreducibly human and
  context-sensitive;
- **student-active** because AI should increase guided practice, reflection, dialogue, and transfer
  rather than merely produce polished outputs;
- **evidence-rich** because ordinary assignment completion is no longer enough to prove learning;
- **safety-governed** because educational settings involve minors, vulnerable populations,
  disability rights, civil rights, and long-run developmental effects.

## Scope

In scope:

- K-12, higher education, and lifelong learning;
- student-facing AI, teacher-facing AI, and institution-facing AI;
- curriculum, pedagogy, assessment, governance, procurement, and professional learning;
- equity, accessibility, privacy, safety, and labor implications.

Out of scope for now:

- fully autonomous “AI schools” with negligible teacher agency;
- detailed product comparison tables;
- preserving every new study or news item;
- speculative AGI futures that do not currently change educational design judgment.

## Source posture

The archive prefers:

1. public institutions and standards bodies,
2. rigorous trials or strong quasi-experimental evidence,
3. implementation-relevant guidance,
4. and only then vendor evidence for market-shape signals.

Key starting sources include UNESCO guidance and competency frameworks, OECD policy/evidence
syntheses, RAND surveys of school use, U.S. Department of Education guidance, IES R&D programs, and
recent tutoring trials. See [`bibliography.md`](bibliography.md).

## Operating boundary

`FT-0181` is the current evidence rail, not the mission itself. Provenance, privacy, acceptance, and
claim controls exist to make educational experimentation trustworthy; they must not displace the
work of defining pedagogy, teacher practice, learner activity, assessment, access, and outcomes.

While control saturation is `SAT4`:

- add no new schema, branch family, or pre-import gate unless a concrete uncovered failure cannot be
  represented or blocked by an existing control;
- prefer direct owner contact, bounded learning studies, consolidation, and deletion;
- require each revision to advance real field evidence, pedagogical knowledge, or lower operator
  burden; and
- treat a control that has not changed a decision after a real cycle as a candidate for merger,
  archival demotion, or retirement.

The archive should remain asymmetric: a small, legible mission and operating path in front; a quiet,
searchable assurance history behind it.
