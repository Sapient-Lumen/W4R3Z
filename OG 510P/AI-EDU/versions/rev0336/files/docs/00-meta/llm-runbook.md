# LLM runbook

## Purpose

This archive should remain small, cumulative, and legible across sessions.

## Rules

- Prefer revising an existing surface over adding a new one.
- Add a new document only when the distinction is durable and load-bearing.
- Cite via the bibliography and short relevance notes; do not dump long excerpts.
- Keep policy claims tied to concrete guardrails or thresholds.
- Keep speculative claims in quarantine until they have either evidence, a usable design
  consequence, or a clear disconfirmation hook.
- When operationalizing AI literacy, prefer a few reusable, subject-embedded routines plus a light
  common course grammar over sprawling banks of sample lessons or policy prose.
- Preserve a two-channel distinction between pedagogical disclosure and protected support channels
  when accommodation, accessibility, translation, or assistive use are at issue.
- Do not let the archive imply that AI education ends with enrolled students; when the design
  judgment is really about public access, workforce transition, or adult learning, preserve those
  nodes explicitly.
- When working on public routes, prefer a small learner-controlled handoff packet, typed portable
  claims, warm transfer, and sector-specific recognition defaults over bigger data-sharing
  fantasies, universal auto-credit, or pure local fragmentation.
- When recognition becomes repetitive, prefer published standing-equivalency lists with scope,
  expiry, manual-review fallback, a small machine-readable publication profile, a bounded exception
  grammar, and privacy-light maintenance signals over hidden provider whitelists, brittle local
  policy code, denial by omission, or centralized case tracking.
- If a claim depends on a live assumption, update `ASSUMPTION_LEDGER.json`.
- If work remains live after the revision, update `FOLLOWTHROUGH_QUEUE.json`.
- If a design move was imported from adjacent reference archives, note it in
  `FOREIGN_PRESSURE_LEDGER.json` rather than implying it emerged locally.

## Archive style

Aim for:

- explicit tradeoffs,
- crisp failure modes,
- bounded recommendations,
- and stable surfaces that can survive future rewrites.

Avoid:

- hype language,
- universal prescriptions without stage or context,
- product-specific lock-in when the governing principle is vendor-neutral,
- and oversized literature reviews.
