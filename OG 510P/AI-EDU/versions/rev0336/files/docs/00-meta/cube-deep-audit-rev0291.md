# rev0291 cube deep audit

## Read of the cube

The cube is internally coherent and lint-clean. `make owner-field-next OUT=scratch/ft0181-field-next-action/aiedu-sr-003` emits the intended clean-start route: `PREPARE-FIRST-CONTACT-PACKET`, with the recommended command `make owner-request-packet OUT=scratch/owner-request-packets/aiedu-sr-003-first-contact`. `make lint-full` passes across 74 lint tools. The archive now contains 597 files: 392 Markdown surfaces, 102 JSON files, 100 Python files, 2 CSV files, and a Makefile. Its Markdown layer contains roughly 613k whitespace-counted words, while exactly one followthrough item remains live: `FT-0181`.

That scale is both strength and warning. The cube has become excellent at preventing fake closure. It is less excellent at making the first real field move feel small enough that a maintainer actually does it.

## Heart of the mission

The heart of the mission is not “deploy AI in schools.” It is to keep education answerable to learning after powerful AI becomes normal. The cube tries to preserve five things at once:

- learner agency rather than answer outsourcing;
- teacher capacity rather than teacher replacement or deskilling;
- access and protected routes rather than hidden exclusion;
- institutional truth rather than dashboard or vendor theater;
- evidence-grade humility rather than public claims ahead of proof.

The most important sentence in the archive is still the boundary sentence: local controls are not owner evidence, not SRC2+, not custody, not public support, and not closure. That rule is the cube's conscience.

## What is missing

The missing center is still a real owner-returned packet. The cube has standards for what would count, tooling to protect source lineage, and a live rail for a bounded owner request. It does not yet have the external event that would make the machine prove itself.

The second missing piece is a five-move front door. The first-read path should feel like this:

1. run `make owner-field-next ...`;
2. execute exactly the emitted packet-prep command;
3. have a human send/adapt the bounded request or record a route block;
4. record the send/reask/contact clock only from real action;
5. route a returned owner CSV through `owner-field-next CSV=...`.

Everything else should remain reachable but quiet. The current archive exposes too much of its own care to the operator.

The third missing piece is deletion/compression governance. The cube already names branch-tail freeze, no-new-control rules, saturation, and maintenance mode. What it lacks is an equally visible habit of retiring or compressing surfaces that no longer change the next owner action. A deletion ledger may be more valuable than a new schema.

The fourth missing piece is an evidence-refresh lane that is small enough to run. The bibliography is strong but enormous. External AI-in-education evidence is moving quickly; the cube needs a small periodic “what changed enough to alter the live rail?” digest rather than a growing library that competes with field execution.

## What should change

The next change should be interface compression, not control expansion. Keep the router as the single source of next action. Put a five-command operator spine in front of the archive. Treat branch surfaces, long governance families, and historical audits as retrieval context, not first-read material.

The cube should also add a recurring “control value review” question: did this surface/tool prevent a real error in owner contact, intake, evidence acceptance, public claims, protected-route leakage, or closure? If not, it should be summarized, indexed, or frozen.

No new schema, registry family, or branch surface should be added until a real owner packet fails an existing gate or exposes an uncovered risk. The current `SAT4` no-new-control posture is correct.

## What has gone severely wrong or wasteful

The severe wrongness is not a broken validator today. It is a cultural failure mode: validated local artifacts can become a substitute for field contact. A lint-clean archive with no real owner packet can still be mission-poor if it keeps generating perfect non-events.

The largest waste pattern is control recursion. There are 74 lint tools, 36 schemas, 85 contracted priority surfaces, 100 branch surfaces across 12 families, 279 assumptions, 92 open questions, 597 tracked files, and one live followthrough. Those numbers are not automatically bad; they show careful work. They become bad if the operator is more likely to add the 101st branch surface than to send the bounded owner packet.

The second waste pattern is re-entry drag. Historical rev surfaces are valuable for auditability, but the startup path should not feel like an archaeological dig. Rev0279 already found a concrete stale-truth failure: the live queue pointed to older first-packet decision work after the cube had advanced. Rev0290 fixed a later executable seam. Those were real repairs. The lesson is that every additional surface increases the chance of stale truth unless the first-read path stays narrow.

The third waste pattern is evidence hunger without evidence contact. The cube has a refined doctrine for claim strength, custody, acceptance, closure, public summaries, and lifecycle movement. It now needs the humility to stop refining until real evidence arrives.

## Speculation boundary

The likely next external shift is from broad AI permissions to proof of learning integrity. Institutions will be asked to show that AI use did not outsource the construct being assessed, hide decision authority, contaminate evidence, weaken protected routes, or burden teachers with invisible governance labor.

If that is right, this cube is pointed in the right direction but over-built at the front door. Its best future shape is a small live rail backed by a large quiet archive: one command, one next action, real owner evidence, and only then deeper controls.

A stronger speculation: the most valuable next artifact is not a new policy. It is a deletion/compression ledger that records which surfaces were frozen because they stopped changing field decisions. That would convert the cube from a growing archive into a calibrated instrument.
