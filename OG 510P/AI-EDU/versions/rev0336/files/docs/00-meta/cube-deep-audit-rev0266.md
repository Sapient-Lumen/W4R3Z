# Cube deep audit rev0266

Date: 2026-06-16
Revision: rev0266
Status: mission deep-read, online research check, and anti-waste field posture review; `FT-0181` remains live and unclosed.

## Heart of the mission

The archive's north star is not AI adoption for its own sake. The heart is a human-centered
education order in which AI increases understanding, agency, access, teacher capacity, and
institutional intelligence more than it increases shortcutting, surveillance, deskilling, or
inequality.

Operationally, that mission has become a discipline of claim control. AI may help students,
teachers, and institutions, but a surface, schema, router, receipt, or example must not launder a
learning, safety, access, workload, compliance, or scale claim. The archive's best impulse is to
make every consequential educational claim name its owner, authority ceiling, source-truth class,
proof surface, protected-route boundary, fallback, and public-language ceiling.

The practical heart for this release is therefore smaller than the archive's total surface area:
prove that one real AI-education service can be handled without over-collecting learner data,
over-claiming impact, hiding action authority, or losing human contestability. That is why
`FT-0181` is still the live gate.

## What is missing

The missing center is still real owner evidence. The cube has realistic examples, validators,
release controls, public-claim boundaries, and a routed owner-request path, but it does not yet have
an `SRC2+` owner-reviewed packet, a returned eight-row owner CSV, accepted workbench content, a
live-window readout, or closure minutes. No internal refinement substitutes for that.

A second missing layer is lived implementation evidence: teacher time, student experience, parent or
learner concern, accessibility friction, procurement cost, support load, false-accusation risk, and
what actually changes after a human owner sees the eight-row request. The cube is strong on
control logic and weak on field texture.

A third missing layer is a small public outcome kernel. The archive contains many surfaces that
protect against false claims, but it still needs an operator-facing answer to: what would count as a
small, credible win; what would count as harm; what would trigger rollback; what should be shown to
teachers, students, families, procurement, and public readers after the first packet is accepted?

## Online research check

Recent public sources broadly support the archive's thesis. UNESCO's teacher and student AI
competency frameworks emphasize human agency, ethics, AI foundations, pedagogy, and responsible
student co-creation. UNESCO's generative-AI guidance stresses privacy, human-centered validation,
age-appropriate design, and institutional readiness. OECD's 2026 Digital Education Outlook warns
that GenAI can improve task performance without learning gains when used without pedagogical
guidance. RAND's 2025 survey work shows AI use in schools rising faster than student guidance and
policy coverage. The EU AI Act treats multiple education uses as high-risk, including admission,
learning-outcome evaluation, level assignment, and prohibited-behavior monitoring during tests.
NIST's Generative AI Profile gives a cross-sector risk-management frame that fits the cube's
map-measure-manage instinct.

The evidence picture is not anti-AI. Tutor CoPilot and other structured tutoring studies suggest AI
can improve learning when it is pedagogically shaped, bounded, and paired with human tutoring or
careful scaffolding. The lesson is not "ban AI" and not "let generic output engines take over". The
lesson is that the archive's distinction between learning, output, authority, and evidence is the
right center.

## What should change next

First, stop rewarding internal control growth while `FT-0181` remains owner-evidence blocked. The
next real action is still to run the router, prepare/send or adapt the owner packet externally, record
the routed sent clock, and then intake a real returned CSV or record a bounded `NO_OWNER_PACKET`
outcome. The cube should treat a true external block as progress, not as an invitation to invent more
pre-import doctrine.

Second, define an active kernel that a new maintainer can read in one sitting. A good kernel would be
about fifteen to twenty surfaces: charter, design principles, assessment construct map, risk
crosswalk, claim/evidence ladder, service intake, minimum real-data request, owner packet prep,
eight-row reply sheet, owner-reply intake, workbench seed, closure checklist, release assurance,
public claim lexicon, and current deep audit. Everything else should be reachable but not
first-read governance.

Third, put branch-history surfaces under a stronger freeze. The branch-family index already admits
that the hot-exam tail is archive history. The next correction is cultural: no new branch sibling
unless a live field artifact exposes a materially uncovered failure. The default repair should be a
compression note or a pointer into an existing family.

Fourth, add field-texture capture after the first external action. Without collecting protected data,
the cube should record whether the owner understood the request, how long it took, which rows were
confusing, which fields were refused, and whether the ask felt safer than a broad export request.
That would turn the archive from a proof machine into an implementation learner.

Fifth, make the public outcome kernel concrete. Before scale claims, define small observed outcomes:
learning/process signal, teacher workload signal, access/support signal, incident/fallback signal,
and student/teacher trust signal. Each signal should have an allowed claim, a forbidden claim, and a
minimum source-truth class.

## Where something has gone wasteful

The waste is not that the validators are broken; `make lint-full` passes. The waste is that the cube
has become very good at proving it has not yet done the one external thing. In the rev0265 package,
there are 475 tracked files, 294 markdown surfaces, 102 JSON files, 76 Python tools, 62 lint checks,
36 schemas, 198 followthrough records with only one live item, and 522,000+ words of markdown. The
largest branch family has 50 hot-exam recipient-followup surfaces. There are dozens of `first-*` and
`portable-*` branch documents that are now mostly retrieval history rather than current mission
center.

This is a severe but correctable failure mode: governance recursion. The archive anticipated it with
control-saturation, maintenance-mode, re-entry navigation, branch-family indexing, and anti-waste
notes. The next step is to obey those rules more aggressively. The cube should spend revisions on
field execution, evidence intake, public-summary safety, and compression of old tails, not on new
pre-evidence registries.

## Speculative correction path

A useful correction over several cloudtainer passes would be:

1. Keep `FT-0181` as the only live followthrough until the owner path resolves.
2. Create and enforce a `mission-kernel` bundle with no more than twenty first-read surfaces.
3. Add a `branch-freeze` note to the branch-family index: new branch siblings require a live artifact
   failure and a named uncovered risk.
4. Add a `field-texture` scratch template for owner-contact friction, not as evidence and not as a
   public claim.
5. After the first real packet, trim fields that did not affect decisions and promote only fields
   that prevented overclaiming, protected-route leakage, hidden authority, or learner harm.
6. Retire or quarantine surfaces that cannot point to a current decision, a live risk, or a reusable
   compression family.

## Still missing

`FT-0181` still lacks the only thing that changes the evidence posture: a real `SRC2+` owner-reviewed
packet and the downstream acceptance, review, readout, signoff, and closeout path. Rev0266 adds a
mission and anti-waste reading; it does not import real pilot evidence, close `FT-0181`, or prove
that any AI education service improves learning.

## Anti-waste note

Do not answer this audit by adding a new registry. The correction is not another map of the map. The
correction is one bounded external owner move, one small field-texture record, and then ruthless
compression of anything that does not help the next maintainer make or verify a real decision.
