# Mission, waste, and repair audit rev0279

## Heart of the mission

The mission is to make AI in education answerable to learning rather than to
novelty. The cube tries to preserve five things at once: learner agency, teacher
capacity, access, institutional truth, and evidence-grade humility. Its deepest
operating rule is that an AI service may help, but it must not silently become a
grade, diagnosis, placement, record mutation, public claim, or substitute for
human judgment without explicit authority and evidence gates.

In plainer terms: the archive is a brake and a compass. It should let a school
or public learning program move faster only when the work stays reversible,
contestable, source-traced, teacher-legible, and honest about what has not been
proven.

## What is missing

The missing center is a real owner-returned packet. Almost everything else is
scaffolding around that absence. The archive has become highly competent at
saying what would count, what would not count, and which local artifacts are
non-evidence. It has not yet crossed the field boundary.

The second missing piece is a very short operator path. The first-read path
should feel like five moves: prepare packet, send/adapt, record send log, wait or
re-ask, intake real returned CSV. The rest should stay available but quiet until
the router asks for it.

The third missing piece is an explicit compression/deletion rhythm. A mature
archive needs pruning rituals as much as new controls. The branch-tail audit
already names 100 branch surfaces across 12 families; that plane should now be
frozen unless real field evidence exposes a gap.

## What should change

1. Treat field contact as the scarce resource. The next useful action is not a
   larger theory; it is `make owner-field-next ...`, packet send/adaptation, and
   a local send log.
2. Convert live-state freshness into a required invariant. Rev0279 begins this by
   extending `check_followthrough_receipt.py`.
3. Push most doctrine behind indexes. Keep `README.md`, `START_HERE.md`, the
   current mission kernel, the current deep audit, and the active FT-0181 docs as
   the main path.
4. Make readout humility non-negotiable. A live-window card can only lead to a
   bounded end-of-window readout, not to outcome claims or closure.
5. Add no new schema or branch family until a real packet fails an existing gate.

## What went wrong or wasteful

The serious wrongness was stale truth inside a valid release. The archive had
moved from first-packet decision gating to post-decision ticket and live-window
card gating, but the live queue still told the operator that rev0276 first-packet
decision work was current. This made the generated context pack stale while all
existing lints passed.

The waste pattern is broader: internal controls can become a proxy for external
evidence. The cube has 67 lint tools, 36 schemas, 339 markdown surfaces before
this pass, 279 assumptions, 92 open questions, and only one live followthrough.
Those numbers are not bad by themselves, but they are a warning. A control plane
that cannot produce the next owner send is no longer serving the mission.

## Online research anchors

- UNESCO's 2024 AI competency frameworks, updated online in 2026, emphasize
  human-centered AI, ethics, AI foundations, pedagogy, and professional learning
  for teachers, plus student competencies for responsible use and co-creation.
- OECD's 2026 Digital Education Outlook says generative AI can support learning
  when guided by teaching principles, but task outsourcing can improve apparent
  performance without real learning gains.
- Stanford SCALE's 2026 K-12 evidence review found a large paper repository but
  only a small causal evidence base, which supports this cube's claim-strength
  ladder and refusal to overclaim.
- The U.S. Department of Education's 2025 AI guidance allows AI uses under
  existing programs only when aligned with statutory and regulatory requirements,
  reinforcing the need for local authority and evidence gates.
- EU AI Act Annex III treats several education uses as high-risk, including access
  or admission decisions, learning-outcome evaluation, education-level assessment,
  and test-behavior monitoring. This supports the cube's action-authority ceilings.
- NIST's AI RMF and the NSA/CISA/FBI AI data-security guidance reinforce the
  provenance, drift, integrity, and lifecycle controls that the cube already
  applies to owner packets and source artifacts.

## Speculation boundary

The likely next external shift is from "may we use AI?" to "prove this use did
not outsource learning, hide authority, contaminate evidence, or weaken protected
routes." If that is right, the cube's mission is well aimed, but its interface is
too heavy. The winning shape is probably a small live rail backed by a large quiet
archive: five operator commands in front, rigorous provenance behind, and public
claims held down until evidence earns them.

A second speculation: the most valuable future surface may not be a new policy.
It may be a deletion ledger showing which controls were compressed because they
no longer changed decisions. That would turn the cube from a growing archive into
an evidence-calibrated instrument.
