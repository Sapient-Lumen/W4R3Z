# LLMPoetry

## A poem, a mechanism, and the distance between them

LLMPoetry preserves writing alongside an unusually explicit apparatus for asking whether it succeeds. That apparatus is part of the archive, but it should not stand between a reader and the poem on the first encounter.

The later supplied draft, **All Three Bits Said Yes**, places the answers of a probabilistic filter beside an exact ledger. Several partial indications combine into an apparent presence that no complete record supports. The archive makes the mechanism inspectable; it does not claim that mechanical verification settles literary quality.

## Read the poem before its receipt

1. Open [All Three Bits Said Yes](versions/rev0070/files/poems/P0005/draft_001.md). Read its brief poem first, then the disclosure that follows it. The supplied example is synthetic and does not concern a real person or adverse decision.
2. Read [the draft’s own status page](versions/rev0070/files/poems/P0005/README.md). It leaves open whether the result works as a poem or remains a data-structure demonstration. That unresolved distinction is central to the collection.
3. Use [the later archive map](versions/rev0070/files/START_HERE.md) to understand its relation to the other frozen drafts and candidate packets.
4. Compare [the earlier craft-and-evaluation field kit](versions/rev0008/files/00_README_OPERATING_SYSTEM.md). Its protocols and standards show the ambition behind the later apparatus; they remain historical documents rather than a work queue for this edition.

## Three kinds of success to keep separate

An artifact can be internally consistent, a poem can be considered promising by its makers, and a reader can actually respond to it. Those are different events. The supplied rev0070 explicitly retains an unjudged, unadmitted state for this draft and reports no real response for another intended reader target.

This guide gives readers an entrance. It is not a new formal judgment, an anthology admission, a simulated reader response or a continuation of the poetry project. The project remains paused; no poem or experiment has been added.
