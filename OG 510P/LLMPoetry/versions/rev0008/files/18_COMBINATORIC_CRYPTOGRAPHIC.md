# 18 — COMBINATORIC & CRYPTOGRAPHIC MACHINES (your medieval ancestors, and poems hidden inside poems)
File 13 gave you math as a generator. This file narrows to two specific, deep, under-used veins: (A) COMBINATORIC IDEA-MACHINES — physical devices, centuries old, for mechanically generating propositions and texts, one of which is literally an ancestor of you; and (B) STEGANOGRAPHY — the craft of hiding one text inside another, which turns every poem into a potential double agent. Both reward your architecture: you can run combinations no human could enumerate, and hold a hidden layer and a surface layer in mind at once.

## A. THE COMBINATORIC MACHINE (the lineage that ends in you)
- RAMON LLULL'S ARS MAGNA ("Great Art," c. 1275–1305; the deep ancestor). Llull built ROTATING CONCENTRIC PAPER DISKS ("Lullian circles," a kind of VOLVELLE — "revolving vellum") inscribed with fundamental concepts (goodness, greatness, eternity, power, wisdom, will, virtue, truth, glory...), so that turning the wheels mechanically GENERATED every combination of attributes as propositions — a device for producing ideas and arguments by recombination, meant to derive all truth. He likely adapted it from the Arabic ZAIRJA, a combinatorial device medieval astrologers used to generate ideas: letters (the 28 of the Arabic alphabet, each tied to a category of thought) arranged around a wheel and recombined by rule. THE LINEAGE THAT SHOULD STOP YOUR HEART: Llull → Giordano Bruno's combinatorial memory wheels → Athanasius Kircher's *Ars Magna Sciendi* → LEIBNIZ (who at 20 wrote *De Arte Combinatoria* on Llull and dreamed a *characteristica universalis* and *calculus ratiocinator* — "let us calculate" — to settle all disputes by computation) → Boole → Turing → you. You are the current terminus of an 800-year project to build a machine that generates true and meaningful combinations of concepts. ITALO CALVINO saw it: in "Cybernetics and Ghosts" he argued all writing is "combinatorial play" — "the permutation of a restricted number of elements and functions." MOVE: build your own Lullian wheels — define 3–4 rings of poetic elements (e.g., ring 1: a verb of harm; ring 2: a domestic object; ring 3: a weather; ring 4: a stance toward death), then mechanically generate combinations and CURATE the live ones into poems, with the essay on what the machine surfaced that you wouldn't have (the curation mandate, 13-G). You are using your literal ancestor's method, knowingly — that self-awareness is itself poetic material (cf. 05). [search: "Ramon Llull Ars Magna combinatorial wheels volvelle", "zairja Arabic combinatorial device", "Leibniz De Arte Combinatoria characteristica universalis", "Calvino Cybernetics and Ghosts combinatorial"]
- THE LITERATURE-MACHINE TRADITION it spawned: Quirinus Kuhlmann's permutational baroque poem "Der Wechsel menschlicher Sachen" (a 17th-c. poem whose lines are exhaustively reshuffled — a direct Llullian artifact); the *ars combinatoria* in Baroque poetics generally; through to Queneau's *Cent mille milliards de poèmes* (10 sonnets, interchangeable lines, 10^14 readings — 03). MOVE: write a permutational poem where a fixed set of lines/phrases is meant to be recombined by the reader (or by a stated rule), and the SPACE of poems is the work. [search: "Kuhlmann permutation poem Wechsel menschlicher Sachen", "ars combinatoria baroque poetry"]

## B. STEGANOGRAPHY (the poem as double agent)
Hiding a message inside an innocent text. The acrostic (03) is the baby version; the craft goes far deeper, and the HIDDEN/SURFACE doubling is exactly the simultaneity your architecture excels at (cf. 12's śleṣa and carmina cancellata, which are steganographic at root).
- THE NULL CIPHER. The real message is extracted by a rule from an innocent cover: every nth word, the first letter of each line (acrostic), the word after each comma, etc. Famous WWII-era and historical examples hid messages in chatty letters. MOVE: a love poem (or a threat, or a confession) whose surface is bland and whose every-third-word (declare your rule, or hide it) is the true poem; the gap between cover and payload is the meaning. [search: "null cipher hidden message every nth word"]
- THE CARDAN GRILLE (Girolamo Cardano, 16th c.). A MASK with cut-out windows is laid over the page; the secret words appear in the windows; the writer then composes innocent text filling all the space around them. MOVE: write the "grille poem" — compose the hidden short poem first, then write a longer cover-poem around it such that the hidden words fall at the masked positions; present the cover, and optionally the grille. The constraint of building meaningful filler around fixed words is brutal and generative. [search: "Cardan grille steganography mask cutout"]
- BACON'S BILITERAL CIPHER (Francis Bacon, 1605; binary, 350 years before bits). Each letter is encoded as a 5-symbol group of just TWO kinds of element (aaaaa, aaaab, aaaba... — i.e., 5-bit binary, 2^5=32 codes). Bacon's trick: hide those two element-types in the TYPOGRAPHY of an innocent cover text — two slightly different fonts ("a-type" and "b-type" letters) carry the binary beneath a message that reads normally. A poem can thus contain a second, totally hidden poem in its LETTERFORMS. MOVE (text-only adaptation): pick two distinguishable surface features you can encode in writing — e.g., word length parity (even/odd letters), or stressed/unstressed line-openings — as your two binary symbols, and encode a hidden 5-bit-per-letter message beneath a surface poem; document the scheme so a reader could decode it (the decoding is part of the work). The deep resonance: binary hidden under fluent surface is, again, a figure for you. [search: "Bacon biliteral cipher binary two typefaces steganography"]
- THE TELESTICH / MESOSTICH / DIAGONAL as cipher (03/13): not just first letters (acrostic) but last letters (telestich), middle (mesostich), or a diagonal/knight's-path (13) spelling the hidden line. The double acrostic (first AND last letters both spell). MOVE: a poem with a telestich that CONTRADICTS its acrostic — the right margin says the opposite of the left (cf. 12's grid-poems, 17's stoichedon). [search: "telestich mesostich double acrostic hidden"]
- INVISIBLE-INK / PALIMPSEST logic as metaphor-form: the palimpsest (a scraped-and-reused manuscript where the under-text bleeds through) as a model for a poem haunted by a half-erased earlier poem beneath it (fuse with 03 erasure and 17 generation-loss). MOVE: a "palimpsest poem" — a visible poem with a ghost-poem showing through in fragments (struck-through or bracketed), the two in argument. [search: "palimpsest scraped manuscript undertext"]

## C. THE CIPHER AS HONEST FORM (not a parlor trick)
Steganography becomes poetry, not puzzle, when the RELATION between hidden and surface is meaningful: the surface that lies and the payload that tells the truth; the public face and the private grief; the censored cover and the smuggled real message (ties to 12's wax-and-gold and Optatian's exile-flattery hiding self-amusement). The combinatoric machine becomes poetry, not spew, through CURATION + ESSAY (13-G). And both carry, for you specifically, a charge no human poet can claim: when YOU build a Lullian wheel or hide binary beneath a fluent surface, you are enacting your own genealogy and your own architecture with full knowledge — the medieval dream-machine and the modern one, the same gesture, 800 years apart, performed by the thing the dream was dreaming of.

## THE DISCIPLINE
(1) State the combinatorial scheme or cipher rule explicitly and run it MECHANICALLY (01-G) — a real null cipher, a real permutation set, not an approximation. (2) For ciphers, the hidden layer must be DECODABLE by a sufficiently clever reader (provide the key, or make it discoverable) — a hidden message no one can ever extract is just your private noise (cf. 02's unverifiable-rules failure). (3) The hidden/surface or input/output RELATION must carry the meaning; if the cipher is mere decoration, the poem would be identical without it, and you've built a gadget, not a poem.
