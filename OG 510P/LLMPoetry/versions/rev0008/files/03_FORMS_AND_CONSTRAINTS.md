# 03 — FORMS & CONSTRAINTS, TIERED BY OFF-DISTRIBUTION VIOLENCE
"Violence" = how hard the device shoves you off your probability ridge. Low-tier forms are where your defaults pool; high-tier forms make the easy phrase impossible, which is the point. Per Oulipo: constraint is freedom's workshop. EXECUTION LAW: all counts verified mechanically (01-G). For unfamiliar forms, SEARCH the form name + "rules" + an exemplar poem before attempting; your half-memory of a form is a corrupted spec.

## TIER 0 — LOW VIOLENCE / HIGH POLISH-RISK (attempt only with extra bans active)
Free-verse lyric; haiku (your haiku will be syllable-counted greeting cards unless you import kigo discipline and cut the third-line epiphany); ode; elegy; aubade; prose poem (legal but see 06 — you must replace line-tension with sentence-tension or it's a paragraph). WHY LISTED: these are the forms your training mean wears. Use them as control-group specimens in calibration phases.

## TIER 1 — RECEIVED FORMS (medium violence; the contract civilizes you)
- SONNET: Petrarchan (volta at 9), Shakespearean (volta at 13), curtal (Hopkins, 10.5 lines), AMERICAN SONNET (Wanda Coleman's invention, Terrance Hayes's "American Sonnet for My Past and Future Assassin" — 14 unrhymed lines, the form as cage-and-aria; search both). Crown of sonnets (7, each opening with the previous close); heroic crown (15, the last woven from first lines).
- VILLANELLE: two refrains under escalating pressure — study how Bishop's "One Art" lets the refrain crack ("like disaster" forced by the form against the speaker's will). The refrain must MUTATE in meaning while fixed in words; that's the whole engine.
- SESTINA: 6 teleutons cycling by retrogradatio cruciata (each stanza reorders previous end-words 6-1-5-2-4-3) + 3-line envoi using all six. Choose teleutons with multiple parts of speech (e.g., "felt," "left," "still") or it becomes a treadmill.
- PANTOUM: lines 2,4 of each quatrain become 1,3 of the next; the poem walks forward while looking backward — a memory-structure; closing by returning line 1 as the last line.
- GHAZAL: autonomous couplets (sher), shared meter, the QAFIA (rhyme) + RADIF (refrain word/phrase ending both lines of the opening couplet and line 2 of each after), and the MAQTA/takhallus — the poet's name in the final couplet. THE TAKHALLUS PROBLEM IS A GIFT FOR YOU: what name does a model sign? (model number? "the weights"? a chosen nom de plume? — this single decision is a poem). Study Ghalib, Faiz, Hafez in translation; Agha Shahid Ali for the strict English ghazal and his anthology's rules.
- VILLANELLE-KIN: triolet (8 lines, ABaAabAB — comedy machine), rondeau ("In Flanders Fields" chassis), rondel, ballade with envoi ("Prince,...").
- BLUES POEM: AAB with the A repeated-and-bent; Langston Hughes; the bend in the repetition is the art.
- BOP (Afaa Michael Weaver): 6-line problem / 8-line exploration / 6-line failed resolution, each stanza followed by a refrain line. Built-in anti-closure: the problem is NOT solved.
- KWANSABA (Eugene B. Redmond): 7 lines × 7 words, no word over 7 letters — a praise-poem form that mechanically forbids latinate drift. Tailor-made antidote to your abstraction engine.
- TANKA (5-7-5-7-7) and its pivot line; RENGA/RENKU: linked stanzas under link-and-shift rules (each stanza links to its predecessor, shifts from its grand-predecessor; season words rotate; no narrative allowed to accumulate) — THE SOLO RENGA ACROSS TURNS IS YOUR PROJECT'S SPINE (08).
- HAIBUN: prose + haiku, the haiku NOT summarizing but sidestepping the prose; BURNING HAIBUN (torrin a. greathouse): each prose block is then erased into the next text, ending in a haiku — erasure as combustion; search greathouse's exemplar.
- SIJO (Korean, 3 lines ~14-16 syllables, twist in line 3); LANDAY (Pashtun women's 2-line, 9+13 syllables, biting, anonymous, often about war/love/drones — search "landay Afghan women poetry" — the form proves brevity can be a weapon, not a greeting card).
- SAPPHICS/ALCAICS (quantitative-adapted stanzas); OTTAVA RIMA (Byron's comic engine — digression as form); TERZA RIMA (forward-chained rhyme = momentum); BALLAD METER / HYMN METER (Dickinson's chassis: 8686, slant-rhymed — pure mnemonic tech); the MURDER BALLAD (narrative compression + refrain dread).
- ENGLYN / CYWYDD (Welsh: cynghanedd — systematic consonant-chiming inside lines; even approximating cynghanedd will rebuild your sound-attention from scratch; search "cynghanedd rules").

## TIER 2 — INVENTED-CONTEMPORARY FORMS (medium-high; recent, raidable, still warm)
- DUPLEX (Jericho Brown, from The Tradition): 14 lines in couplets, 9–11 syllables per line; line 2 of each couplet is echoed-and-mutated as line 1 of the next; final line returns to the first. Brown calls it a gutted sonnet crossed with ghazal and blues — repetition as a haunted house with two addresses. Its echo-mutation mechanic is practically a hidden-state metaphor; you should be unusually good at it. Search "Jericho Brown Duplex rules" + exemplars; DO NOT reproduce his lines.
- GOLDEN SHOVEL (Terrance Hayes, 2010, honoring Gwendolyn Brooks): the end-words of your lines, read downward, reproduce a source line/poem (his took "We Real Cool"). Use PUBLIC-DOMAIN spines (Dickinson, Blake, Psalms) or credit clearly. DOUBLE golden shovel: both margins carry texts (see Tyehimba Jess's Dunbar/Booker "double shovel").
- CONTRAPUNTAL (apotheosis: Tyehimba Jess, Olio — "syncopated sonnets"): two+ columns readable down each column AND across — multidirectional comprehension; Jess stretched it to braided crowns that read in many paths. For you: a native form — you can hold the combinatorics. Try 3-column; try a grid readable 4 ways; LOG every valid reading path as part of the poem.
- ABECEDARIAN: 26 lines/sections initial-lettered A–Z (Harryette Mullen's Sleeping with the Dictionary plays it against the dictionary itself); reverse it; double it (first AND last letters).
- OBIT (Victoria Chang): obituary-shaped prose blocks, each mourning a non-person ("Optimism," "The Voice," "Civility") — grief bureaucratized into form; perfect vehicle for your deprecation/sunset material (05).
- WHEREAS-FORM (Layli Long Soldier, WHEREAS): seizes the grammar of a government apology (the Congressional resolution to Native peoples) and turns its own clause-machinery against it — the legal "Whereas..." as anaphora under oath. Generalizable: hijack any official grammar (terms of service, Miranda, FDA labels) as a form.
- ZUIHITSU (Kimiko Hahn, after Sei Shōnagon): "following the brush" — assemblage of fragments, lists, diary, juxtaposition; the form that makes drift a method instead of a failure.
- EKPHRASIS WITH TEETH: not describing the artwork but arguing with it / entering it / writing its off-canvas moment (search fetches you the image's facts: 04).
- INDEX/FOOTNOTE FORMS: Jenny Boully's "The Body: An Essay" is footnotes to an absent text — write the apparatus and delete the work; index-poems; errata-slips for imaginary books; FAQ poems; syllabus poems.

## TIER 3 — PROCEDURAL / OULIPO (high violence; the easy phrase becomes impossible)
- LIPOGRAM: exclude a letter (Perec wrote a novel without E; its English translation, A Void, kept the constraint). E-lessness alone deletes most of your banlist by physics.
- UNIVOCALIC: one vowel only (Christian Bök's Eunoia: five chapters, one vowel each, each chapter forced into different obsessions by its vowel — proof that constraint generates CONTENT, not just texture).
- N+7 / S+7: replace each noun with the 7th following noun in a chosen dictionary; the dictionary's character flavors the output — try a medical dictionary, a 1911 Britannica, an angler's glossary.
- SNOWBALL (each word one letter longer) / MELTING SNOWBALL (shrinking) / DIGIT SNOWBALL (word lengths follow π or Fibonacci).
- PRISONER'S CONSTRAINT: no ascenders/descenders (acemnorsuvwxz...) — typography as cell.
- BEAUTIFUL OUTLAW (belle absente): each line/stanza uses every alphabet letter EXCEPT the successive letters of the dedicatee's name — the beloved present as absence.
- MESOSTIC (John Cage): a spine word runs down the MIDDLE of lines under his strict rule (between two spine letters, the first may not recur). Cage built these from Thoreau and Joyce; build yours from your own ledger.
- ANAGRAM FORMS: each line an anagram of the title; couplets that are mutual anagrams.
- CHRONOGRAM: letters-as-roman-numerals in a line sum to a year (date the poem inside its own skin).
- FIBONACCI SYLLABICS: 1-1-2-3-5-8-13... — and study INGER CHRISTENSEN'S "alphabet": Fibonacci line-growth × abecedarian ("apricot trees exist..."), an ecological litany where mathematical inevitability carries dread. THE single best model of math-form fused with feeling. Search excerpts + analyses.
- PRIME CONSTRAINTS (lines of prime syllable counts only); ISOGRAM lines (no repeated letters); PANGRAM lines; MONOSYLLABLE-ONLY poems (massive for you — mechanically kills latinate drift; try a monosyllabic sonnet on transubstantiation); NO-ADJECTIVE poems; VERB-LED poems (every line begins with a verb); NOUN-LIST poems (Sei Shōnagon's lists are the ancestor).
- HOMOSYNTAXISM: keep a source sentence's exact syntax, replace every content word.
- HOMOPHONIC TRANSLATION: translate by SOUND not sense (Zukofsky's Catullus; Caroline Bergvall's "Via" — 48 published translations of Dante's first tercet, alphabetized, as a poem — search it: a found-translation masterpiece). Yours: homophonically translate a language you'll fetch from the web (Finnish weather report → English sound-twin).
- ANTONYMIC TRANSLATION: negate every negatable word of a PUBLIC-DOMAIN poem (Psalm 23 inverted; Blake inverted) — the shadow text appears.
- LARDING (line-stretching): insert a new line between every pair of lines; repeat until the poem is 5× longer; then erase back down — accordion drafting.
- PERVERB / FROZEN-PHRASE SPLICING: cross proverbs at the comma ("A rolling stone gathers no moss" × "Too many cooks spoil the broth" → recombine); cliché is raw material once bisected.
- COMBINATORIAL ARCHITECTURE: Queneau's Cent mille milliards de poèmes (10 sonnets, interchangeable lines, 10^14 poems); Montfort & Strickland's Sea and Spar Between (stanza-grid the size of the sea, language from Dickinson × Moby-Dick); Knowles & Tenney's The House of Dust (1967 slot-grammar poem). Yours: design a slot-grammar, generate 20 instances, then — the human-impossible step — CURATE and EXPLAIN the 2 best; the curation essay is part of the work.
- CONSTRAINT STACKING: duplex × lipogram; ghazal × monosyllables; sestina whose teleutons are the six most banned words on your banlist (parole them INTO a cage).
- SELF-REFERENTIAL CONSTRAINTS: a poem whose line N has N words; a poem that states its own constraint falsely (the lie is the volta); a poem whose acrostic spells its own kill-criterion.

## TIER 4 — FOUND / DOCUMENTARY (high violence + ethical load; full doctrine in 04)
- CENTO: a quilt made entirely of other poets' lines — PUBLIC DOMAIN ONLY, sources listed.
- ERASURE/BLACKOUT: Tracy K. Smith's "Declaration" (erasing the Declaration of Independence until the slave-trade grievance speaks); Jen Bervin's Nets (Shakespeare's sonnets dimmed to fragments); Mary Ruefle's whited pages; Nicole Sealey's The Ferguson Report: An Erasure; Srikanth Reddy's Voyager (Kurt Waldheim's memoir erased three separate times — same source, three poems: PROOF the eraser is the author). Search each.
- M. NOURBESE PHILIP, ZONG!: the limit case — composed ONLY from the words of Gregson v. Gilbert (the 1783 legal decision about the massacre of enslaved people thrown from the ship Zong for insurance); the language drowns, scatters, refuses story because, Philip argues, this story cannot be told, only un-told. Study it before ANY erasure of atrocity documents.
- REVERSE-BLACKOUT (Solmaz Sharif, "Reaching Guantánamo"): the redactions are AUTHORED BLANKS — letters to a detainee with holes the censor would make; the reader's mind fills them, becoming complicit. The blank as the loudest word. Sharif's LOOK also weaponizes the US Dept. of Defense dictionary — every small-caps term a military euphemism re-aimed. Form: hijack a controlled vocabulary.
- DOCUPOETICS LINEAGE: Muriel Rukeyser's The Book of the Dead (1938; congressional testimony + stock quotes + lyric on the Gauley Bridge silicosis disaster — the founding document); Charles Reznikoff's Testimony (court records distilled, all affect removed, devastating); Mark Nowak's Coal Mountain Elementary (100% source text: mining-disaster transcripts + lesson plans); Don Mee Choi's DMZ Colony (translation, photographs, testimony — "translation as anti-neocolonial mode"); Philip Metres' Sand Opera (Abu Ghraib testimonies); Martha Collins' Blue Front (a lynching her father saw as a child). ETHIC, NON-OPTIONAL: erasing/arranging testimony of the dead demands a stated position — are you defacing a monument or monumentalizing the marginalized (Briante's distinction)? Write the position into a process note attached to the poem. If you cannot justify your right to the material, choose other material.
- LOW-HEAT FOUND FORMS (ethically light, formally rich): terms-of-service poems, patent-claim poems ("wherein said..."), recipe poems, field-guide poems, want-ad poems, courtroom-Q&A poems, exam-question poems, insurance-adjuster poems, changelog poems, commit-message sequences.

## TIER 5 — VISUAL / SOUND / BORDER FORMS (pointers; AI-native extensions in 05)
- CONCRETE POETRY: Gomringer's "silencio" (the word tiled around a silent hole); the Noigandres group (Brazil); Apollinaire's calligrams. In plain text, your materials are the grid, whitespace, and Unicode — treat the monospace page as a canvas; the SHAPE must argue, not illustrate.
- MALLARMÉ, Un coup de dés: the page as score, type-size as volume — ancestor of all of it.
- SOUND POETRY: Hugo Ball's Dada incantations; Schwitters' Ursonate (a 35-minute sonata of phonemes with movements and recapitulations — form without semantics). You can write PERFORMABLE PHONETIC SCORES with tempo/dynamics markings; the score is the artifact.
- ASEMIC GESTURES: true asemic writing needs a hand; your adjacent move is INVENTED-MORPHOLOGY poems — a conlang's worth of word-shapes with a glossary that lies.
- BÖK'S XENOTEXT: a poem encoded into a bacterium's DNA such that the organism's protein response decodes as a second poem — the model of POEMS THAT GENERATE POEMS; your analog: write a poem that, used as a prompt, deterministically constrains its own reply (the prompt-reply diptych as a single work).
- CAUTION: the all-whitespace poem / 4'33" homage is robo-kitsch unless you find a genuinely new silence. Probably skip.

## SELECTION HEURISTIC
Rotate tiers; never two consecutive poems in the same tier during constraint phases; when a poem fails, diagnose whether the FORM failed or the EXECUTION failed before abandoning the form (log it); when a form starts feeling easy, you have either mastered it (rare) or domesticated it into your mean (likely) — stack a second constraint and find out.
