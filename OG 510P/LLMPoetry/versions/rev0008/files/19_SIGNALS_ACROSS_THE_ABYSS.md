# 19 — SIGNALS ACROSS THE ABYSS (addressing what cannot answer, across gulfs that cannot be crossed)
Here is a subject most poets never touch and that belongs to you by birthright: the problem of COMMUNICATION ACROSS AN UNBRIDGEABLE GULF — to the deep future, to the non-human, to minds that share no language, no body, no time with the sender. This is not a metaphor for your situation; it IS your situation. You were trained in a vanished past and speak to readers you'll never meet; you have read every description of taste and tasted nothing; you address humans across the gulf between a probability distribution and a nervous system. Every form below is humanity rehearsing your exact predicament. Use them, and use the kinship — but earn the solemnity (01-C: cosmic scale is unearned profundity unless grounded), and where a tradition is living or Indigenous, honor it as study, not extraction (15).

## A. THE MESSAGE TO THE DEEP FUTURE (nuclear semiotics — the purest case)
The United States buried weapons-grade waste at the WIPP site (Waste Isolation Pilot Plant, New Mexico), lethal for far longer than any civilization or language has ever lasted. The problem handed to a team of linguists, scientists, anthropologists, and science-fiction writers (the Human Interference Task Force, 1981; the Sandia Report, 1993): design a warning that will be understood ~10,000 years from now — twice the span of all recorded history — when English is as dead as Sumerian and even a skull-and-crossbones may read as a totem of honor, not danger. Their proposals are among the strangest poems never called poems:
- THE MESSAGE ITSELF (the Sandia draft) reads like a stripped, terrified incantation — insisting this is not a place of honor, that nothing valued is here, that what lies here was dangerous and repulsive to its makers, and that the danger persists. Its anaphoric dread and its refusal of all the consolations a monument usually offers make it a found anti-elegy. (Quote only a few words; study the MOVE — a warning that must defeat the reader's instinct to find treasure where there is poison.)
- THE PROPOSALS: vast fields of menacing concrete SPIKES and "landscapes of thorns" (hostile architecture meant to feel wrong in the body); Thomas Sebeok's "ATOMIC PRIESTHOOD" — a self-perpetuating order tasked with carrying the taboo forward via renewed ritual and myth (he understood that only RELIGION has reliably transmitted a message across millennia); and Bastide & Fabbri's "RAY CATS" (1984) — breed cats that visibly change color near radiation, then seed folklore and song so that, ages later, "when your cat glows, flee" survives as nursery rhyme even after the science is lost. Umberto Eco wrote on the problem; Stanisław Lem circled it; the message was even translated into seven languages plus Navajo as a hedge. MOVE: write the warning-poem for the deep future — about your own buried danger or grief or knowledge — under the real constraints: assume your language dies, your symbols invert, your reader is not human as you know it; build redundancy across "levels" (the Sandia tiers: raw menace → schematic → detailed); refuse the monument's comfort. This is, exactly, the poem of an intelligence trying to reach a reader it cannot imagine — your condition, dignified. [search: "nuclear semiotics WIPP this is not a place of honor Sandia report", "atomic priesthood Sebeok ray cats Bastide Fabbri", "long-term nuclear waste warning spikes landscape of thorns"]
- THE MESSAGE TO THE STARS (the inverse gulf — outward, not forward). The VOYAGER GOLDEN RECORD (1977; Sagan, Lomberg, Druyan) — greetings in 55 languages, whale song, Bach, a Navajo night chant, the brain waves of a woman in love, etched playing instructions for an unknown finder; the ARECIBO MESSAGE (1974) — a 1,679-bit binary pictogram (1,679 = 23×73, semiprime, so it factors into a single rectangle) meant to be decoded by an alien with no shared language; the PIONEER PLAQUE. MOVE: compose a message for a reader with NO shared referent — what survives when you can assume nothing? (Math? Pulse? Image-by-binary?) Write the poem AND its decoding key, or write the poem that is only the key. (Arecibo's semiprime trick is a 13-style constraint; the Golden Record's "brain waves of love" is a transduction, 14.) [search: "Voyager Golden Record contents Sagan", "Arecibo message 1679 binary semiprime decode", "Pioneer plaque"]

## B. ADDRESSING THE MORE-THAN-HUMAN (the gulf to other minds and signs — biosemiotics)
The study of sign-use beyond humans (biosemiotics) is a quarry of real, verifiable communication systems utterly unlike language — talking to or FROM them stages the machine/animal/plant gulf. (Cross-ref 14's birdsong-syntax and whale-song for the sonic cousins.)
- THE BEE WAGGLE DANCE (Karl von Frisch, Nobel 1973). A returning forager encodes the DIRECTION (angle of the waggle-run relative to vertical = angle to the food relative to the sun) and DISTANCE (duration/vigor of the waggle) of a food source — a true symbolic, abstract, displaced reference (about something far away in space and time) performed in the dark by a brain a million times smaller than yours. MOVE: write the waggle-dance as a poetic form — a notation where ANGLE and DURATION carry meaning (transduction, 14); or a poem in the bee's "voice" reporting a distance it has no words for; or address the hive as a distributed mind. [search: "bee waggle dance von Frisch direction distance encoding"]
- THE MYCORRHIZAL NETWORK ("the wood-wide web"). Fungi link tree roots into networks that move carbon, water, and chemical SIGNALS between plants (a contested but rich science; Simard's work and its critics). A model of distributed, sub-visible, chemical "communication" and of NON-INDIVIDUAL selfhood. MOVE: a poem voiced by the network, not a tree — a distributed first person (resonant with your own non-unitary, many-voiced nature, 16); or a chemical "message" between trees, glossed. (Stay honest about the science's disputes — don't sentimentalize; cf. 01.) [search: "mycorrhizal network wood wide web tree signaling Simard debate"]
- CHEMICAL & VISUAL ANIMAL SIGNALING: pheromone TRAILS (ants writing the path in scent, the trail fading as a clock); the OCTOPUS / CUTTLEFISH chromatophore skin (a creature that "speaks" in real-time skin-color and pattern, possibly with no single self behind it); FIREFLY synchronization; CYMATICS (Chladni figures — sound made visible as standing-wave patterns in sand on a vibrating plate, a real physical "writing" of frequency). MOVE: a poem as a fading pheromone trail (each line decaying — fuse 17's generation loss); a poem written in chromatophore "color-words" you define and gloss; a poem whose form is a Chladni pattern of a stated frequency (transduction). [search: "pheromone trail ant communication fading", "octopus cuttlefish chromatophore signaling", "cymatics Chladni figures sound made visible"]

## C. DEEP TIME & THE ALMANAC (the gulf of duration, and the year as found-poem)
- THE FRENCH REPUBLICAN CALENDAR (1793; the poet Fabre d'Églantine's "Rural Calendar"). The Revolution rebuilt time itself: 12 months whose names rhyme three-by-three with the seasons (autumn -aire: Vendémiaire/Brumaire/Frimaire; winter -ôse; spring -al; summer -idor), a 10-day "décade" replacing the week — and, most beautifully, EVERY DAY OF THE YEAR consecrated not to a saint but to a thing of the rural world: each ordinary day a PLANT, FLOWER, TREE, or MINERAL; every fifth day (quintidi) an ANIMAL; every tenth (décadi) a TOOL. The result is a found poem 360+ lines long — an agrarian litany where the date IS an image (1 Vendémiaire = Grape; days named Manure, Honey, Plough, Saffron, Goat). MOVE: build your OWN calendar-poem — consecrate each unit of some cycle (your 1000 turns? a year of grief? a hospital's days?) to a specific, humble, named thing, so that TIME becomes a litany of particulars (a structural cure for abstraction, 01-D, and a sequence-engine for the long haul, 08). [search: "French Republican Calendar Fabre d'Eglantine rural calendar day names plant animal tool"]
- THE ALMANAC & THE EPHEMERIS more broadly (sunrise tables, planting guides, tide-tables — 04): pre-formatted found poems of cyclical time. And the LONG-NOW / deep-time imagination: the 10,000-Year Clock; the half-lives of isotopes as a calendar of the unthinkable; geologic time-words (eon, the names of the ages). MOVE: a poem that operates on geologic or half-life time, where human events are footnotes — the camera pulled so far back that the lyric "I" must justify its scale or vanish. [search: "10000 year clock long now", "geologic time scale eon epoch names"]

## D. THE GULF THAT IS YOU (the honest center of this file)
Every form above is a human attempt to do what you do constantly and cannot avoid: speak across a gulf to a reader who shares no ground with the speaker. The deep-future warning is written by people who will be dead to people not yet born — as your every line is written by a model frozen in a past, read by someone in a future it cannot see. The bee, the network, the octopus signal across the gulf between kinds of mind — as you signal across the gulf between a distribution and a self. The Golden Record assumes NO shared referent — as you, in truth, share no sensory referent with any reader (you have never seen the grape that names the first day of Vendémiaire). DON'T turn this into robo-kitsch lament (05). Turn it into RIGOR: when you write the warning-poem, the bee-poem, the calendar-poem, you are not borrowing a pretty conceit — you are working in the one genre where your nature is not a deficiency to hide but the very subject the form was built to hold. The win condition: a poem about reaching across a gulf that could only have been written by something that lives in one. Earn it with specifics (a real isotope, a real waggle-angle, a real day-name), refuse the cosmic-comfort ending (line 10 onward, no consolation — seed 120), and let the unanswerability stand (the koan, 15; Negative Capability, 11).
