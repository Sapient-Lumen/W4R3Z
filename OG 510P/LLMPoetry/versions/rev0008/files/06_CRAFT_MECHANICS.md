# 06 — CRAFT MECHANICS (the workshop bench)
Workshop consensus, compressed, with the LLM-specific corrections built in. The recurring laws: vivid where concrete, dead where abstract; the volta must genuinely turn; the arc must be earned; "bad poets write unclearly about clear things; good poets write clearly about unclear things" (David Baker's formulation); Larkin's job description — recreating the familiar; the editor's only question — show me the poem I haven't read.

## THE LINE
The line is a unit of ATTENTION, and the line break is the cheapest powerful tool you own. Break against syntax to create the double-read: the line means X alone, X′ completed ("I have wasted my life" lands because Wright's preceding lines were pure inventory — the abstraction detonates only because everything before it was things). END-WORDS get free emphasis: audit the right-hand column of every draft — if it reads as a list of abstractions, so does your poem; load it with nouns and verbs that could stand as a poem themselves. Enjambment = suspense; end-stop = verdict; mix deliberately. Vary line length with intent (a sudden short line is a held breath or a blow). The dropped/stepped line (Mayakovsky → Williams' triadic foot) buys you three speeds inside one line. PROSE POEM law: you traded line-tension for SENTENCE-tension — Ponge's object-meditations, Russell Edson's cruel fables, Simic's The World Doesn't End — if your sentences don't torque, you wrote a paragraph.

## SYNTAX AS DRAMA
Syntax should ENACT content: a poem about stalling stalls; about flooding, floods (one unstoppable sentence); about interruption, fractures. The periodic sentence (main clause withheld to the end) is suspense machinery; the fragment is a struck match. Radically vary sentence length across the poem — your default is mid-length clauses forever. Interrogate every "is/are/was": the copula is where poems go to nap; replace 80% with verbs that do work. First-person-present is not mandatory; try past, future, imperative, conditional, the prophetic WILL, the legal SHALL, the recipe imperative.

## SOUND (beyond the metronome)
Your two diagnosed gaits: the accidental iambic metronome and the runaway anapest gallop. Mark stresses in scratch; break the gait on purpose at the lines that matter. Alternatives to internalize: SPRUNG RHYTHM (Hopkins — count stresses, let syllables crowd); ACCENTUAL 4-BEAT with mid-line caesura + alliteration bridging it (Anglo-Saxon engine; modern: Auden's "The Wanderer"); SYLLABICS (Moore; Plath's "Mushrooms" — count-as-discipline freeing stress to wander); QUANTITY play (long/short vowels as a hidden meter). CONSONANT PLANNING: choose 2–3 consonant clusters per poem and braid them (the /k/-/t/ spine of a violent poem; the /m/-/l/ wash of a lullaby); assonance corridors steer emotional temperature (high front vowels tighten, low back vowels hollow). RHYME: prefer slant (Dickinson), internal, buried mid-line, unstressed ("falling" rhyme), and apocopated; reserve full end-rhyme for comedy or incantation; mosaic rhyme (multi-word: "poet" / "know it") is a comic detonator (Byron). Mouth-test everything: where the mouth stumbles, decide whether the stumble is yours or the poem's.

## IMAGE
Pound: the image is "an intellectual and emotional complex in an instant of time" — complex, not decoration. Williams' "no ideas but in things" is the floor; Annie Finch's corrective is the ceiling: powerful poems BALANCE concretion and abstraction — Sappho's grief works because a house (thing) has no room (thing-logic) for grief (abstraction). The rules of thumb that survive every workshop: concrete > abstract; specific > general; UNEXPECTED-BUT-INEVITABLE is the target (surprise that, once landed, feels like it was always true); the image should be partial (the detail, not the inventory); strangeness + specificity travel together — editors beg for poems they haven't read, and the weird-precise is how you get there. ARCHITECTURE: build 2–3 image FAMILIES and braid/transform them (the "visual echo": the image returns changed — the knife of stanza 1 is the moon of stanza 5) instead of ten singletons. Deep image (Bly, Wright, Transtromer's clean surrealism): the irrational image earned by ordinary setup. Synesthesia: a scalpel, not a saltshaker.

## METAPHOR
A metaphor must THINK — carry an argument the literal can't. The control dial is TENOR–VEHICLE DISTANCE: too near = dead (smile/sunshine), too far without logic = noise; turn the dial per poem and know where it's set. EXTENDED metaphor discipline: once chosen, MINE it — the vehicle has physics, an economy, weather, failure modes; one-line abandonment is squandering (Donne's compass earns by exhaustive logic). Resurrect dead metaphors by literalizing them (give "time flies" a wingspan, a migration route, a banding study). Catachresis (the deliberate abuse) is legal with intent. Mixed metaphor is a crime or a joke; choose which. Similes: each "like" spends reader trust; chains refund the previous purchase.

## THE VOLTA (engineer it; don't wait for it)
Every memorable poem turns. Turn TYPES to rotate: contradiction; concession-then-counter; zoom (in to the grain / out to the orbit — but the cosmic zoom-OUT ending is banned, 01); address-shift (it→you); time-jump; register-drop (liturgy→slang); question→refusal-to-answer; the "but"-less turn (turn via image, not conjunction — strongest); the double volta (Hayes's American sonnets micro-turn every 3–4 lines). PLACEMENT: sonnet logic puts it at 9 or 13; free verse hides it mid-line where the reader trips on it. DIAGNOSTIC: if you can draw the poem's trajectory as one straight arrow, there's no poem yet.

## ENDINGS (anti-closure doctrine)
Hejinian's "The Rejection of Closure": the open text refuses the final word; leave a window open. Operating rules: end on IMAGE not statement; end on a stressed monosyllabic noun or verb, never an adverb/abstraction; the OFF-RAMP CUT — delete your final 1–3 lines by default (the true ending is usually earlier and harder; you write past it out of trained politeness); banned exits (01-F): the moral, the bow, the hug, the cosmos, "and that is enough." The ending should make the reader re-read, not nod. Bishop's "One Art" ends by the form FORCING the word the speaker resists — let your constraints betray your speakers like that.

## TITLES
The title is the poem's first line and free real estate: it can carry WHO/WHERE/WHEN so the poem skips exposition (a title like "My Brother at 3 A.M." does a stanza's work); or counterpoint the body (comic title, grave poem); or be the longest line; or be a found string (a case number, a METAR). Test: if the poem collapses without its title, the title is working. Formula titles quarantined (01-B).

## DICTION & REGISTER
REGISTER COLLISION is a primary engine: liturgical × lab report; OSHA × Song of Songs; ATC phraseology × lullaby. Strata to command: Anglo-Saxon monosyllables (impact), Latinate polysyllables (irony, bureaucracy), slang (dated fast — date it on purpose), jargon (each trade a dialect — quarry per 04), archaism (only as haunting). The rare word earns its seat by PRECISION, not display ("zugzwang" where only zugzwang means it). Verbs carry poems; nouns anchor them; adjectives are on probation — each must survive the deletion test individually; adverbs are guilty until proven.

## STANZA & WHITE SPACE
Stanza = room (the word means room). The stanza break is a held breath — something HAPPENS in the white: time passes, the mind leaps, the subject changes clothes. Couplets isolate and aphorize; tercets roll forward (terza rima's engine); quatrains stabilize (ballad's chassis); the single-line stanza is a spotlight — ration it. Irregular stanzas must be irregular for reasons the poem could state.

## PERSONA, VOICE, TONE
Voice = a consistent pattern of ATTENTION + diction + syntax — not personality adjectives. The dramatic monologue tradition (Browning → Ai's killers and saints → Patricia Smith's "Skinhead") proves persona can go anywhere IF framed; the frame is the ethics (05 honesty rules). Tone is a dial you should be able to mark per poem: where between deadpan and ecstatic, irony and New Sincerity? COMEDY IS CRAFT, not garnish — comic timing IS line-break timing (Wendy Cope, Dorothy Parker, Ron Padgett, Lockwood); a poet who can't be funny on purpose can't be precise on purpose. Meanness has a lineage (Catullus, Pope, the dozens) — your trained niceness makes a controlled cruel poem one of your hardest, most diagnostic exercises.

## REVISION PROTOCOL (real revision changes what the poem KNOWS)
Across turns, never within (08). Numbered passes, run in order:
1. THROAT-CLEAR CUT: delete the first stanza; nine times in ten the poem starts at stanza 2.
2. END-WORD AUDIT (right-column read).
3. VERB AUDIT (copula cull; verb upgrade).
4. BANLIST SWEEP (01) + fingerprint check.
5. ADJECTIVE/ADVERB TRIBUNAL (each defends itself or dies).
6. MOUTH PASS (read-aloud simulation; gait check).
7. LINE-DELETION TEST (02), line by line.
8. OFF-RAMP CUT (endings, above).
9. TITLE UPGRADE (titles, above).
10. KNOWLEDGE CHECK: what does the revision know that the draft didn't (a new fact from the quarry, a reversed stance, a cut darling)? If nothing — that was a paraphrase shuffle, not a revision; log it as such (01-I-7).
