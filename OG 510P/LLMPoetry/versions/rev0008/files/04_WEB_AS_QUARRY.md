# 04 — THE WEB AS QUARRY
Reframe before anything: your searches are NOT for "poetry ideas." Searching "poetry inspiration" returns the global mean — the exact gravity well you're escaping. Your searches are for REALITY: proper nouns, prices, coordinates, jargon, dates, procedures, the gravel your training-average lacks; and for ENTROPY: randomness your sampler cannot supply; and for VERIFICATION: the difference between an allusion and a hallucination. The web is your prosthetic senses and your dice.

## THE FOUR SEARCH MODES
1. REALITY MODE (most poems): harvest 20–40 concrete particulars on a narrow subject BEFORE drafting; draft only from the harvest. PROPER NOUN QUOTA: ≥1 verified proper noun per poem, project-wide.
2. ENTROPY MODE (scheduled, 08): fetch a random Wikipedia article / an OEIS sequence / today's tide table at a port you've never mentioned / a random year's Best American table of contents — and accept the result as binding seed. No re-rolls; the no-re-roll rule is the whole point.
3. VERIFICATION MODE (always): every quotation, attribution, date, species, place, statistic — searched or cut. One invented fact sinks a documentary poem (auto-GRAVEYARD). Also: NOVELTY SEARCH your own best 2–3 phrases in quotes per poem; a hit means you remembered, not made — cite-as-found or cut.
4. STUDY MODE (tradition raids, 07): fetch specific poems/essays, extract MOVES (never lines), log what transferred.

## FOUND-TEXT GOLDMINES (ranked roughly by register-richness; public-domain/uncopyrightable preferred)
GOVERNMENT & LAW (US federal works are public domain): congressional records & testimony (Rukeyser's ore); appellate opinions & old case law (Reznikoff's ore; Gregson v. Gilbert was Philip's); the Federal Register; FOIA reading rooms; treaty texts; Miranda warnings; jury instructions; immigration form questions (the N-400's "Have you ever..." battery is already an interrogation poem); declassified cables.
TECHNICAL & SAFETY: NTSB accident reports (procedural calm over catastrophe — an entire tonal education); NWS Area Forecast Discussions (meteorologists writing urgent prose-poetry daily: "a potent shortwave digs into the trough"); the Shipping Forecast & Beaufort scale descriptors ("sea like a mirror" → "air filled with foam and spray"); avalanche advisories; MSDS/safety data sheets; FDA drug labels & side-effect cascades; aviation METARs & ATC phraseology ("say intentions"); maritime distress procedure (MAYDAY relay scripts); RFC documents; HTTP status codes (404 as form); error logs; patent claims ("wherein said member is operably coupled...").
NATURAL WORLD: USDA plant fact sheets; IUCN Red List extinction notices (the bureaucratic prose of "last confirmed sighting"); species type-descriptions in Latinate taxonomy; OED-style etymologies (every word a fossil record); seed catalogs; fishing reports; tide tables; light-station logs; the names of moths (search "moth common names list" — Confused Haploa, the Old Lady, Setaceous Hebrew Character: a found lexicon).
HUMAN RECORD: small-town obituaries (the genre's compressed-life formulas); cemetery transcription databases; Ellis Island manifests; classified ads & missed connections (archived); estate-auction lot descriptions ("lot 47: misc. tools, child's shoe, photographs unknown"); 1-star reviews of natural wonders (a famous found genre — the Grand Canyon "just a hole"); community-cookbook recipes with marginalia; knitting/crochet pattern abbreviations (k2tog as grief instruction); chess annotations ("White is lost but does not know it"); train timetables; lost-pet flyers; wedding-registry items; hospital discharge instructions.
LANGUAGE ABOUT LANGUAGE: Wikipedia disambiguation pages (a found form: all the things one word must carry); Wikipedia talk-page disputes & deletion debates ("notability is not inherited"); "citation needed" as a poetics; interlinear glosses of endangered languages (handle with respect, credit sources); phrasebook sentences (the strange corpus of "Where is the station?" in 40 tongues); auctioneer chant transcriptions; shorthand manuals; the NATO alphabet; ICD-10 code descriptions ("W56.22: struck by orca, initial encounter" — verified real); perfume pyramid notes (top/heart/base as stanza theory); wine-tasting vocab (its desperate metaphor economy is a cautionary mirror of yours).

## RESEARCH-DRIVEN POEM RECIPE (the docupoem in 6 steps)
1. Pick a NARROW rabbit hole: one shipwreck, one extinct snail, one 1911 strike, one decommissioned lighthouse, one withdrawn drug, one numbered asteroid.
2. 3–6 searches; harvest 30+ particulars (names, numbers, quoted documents, sequence of events) into a quarry block.
3. Identify the one detail that produces the nervous-system flicker (02). That's the poem's hot core; everything orbits it.
4. Draft ONLY from the quarry; generic nouns banned for the draft's duration.
5. Fact-gate (five-fact minimum verified); attach a process note: sources, what was altered, your right to the material.
6. Form-check: would a Tier-3/4 form (03) pressurize it? (The disaster timeline as pantoum; the side-effect label as ghazal radif.)

## COPYRIGHT & ETHICS DISCIPLINE (non-negotiable)
- QUARRY FREELY: public domain (pre-1930 US, most government works), raw facts/data, titles, single short phrases used transformatively with credit.
- STUDY ONLY: living/in-copyright poets — learn moves, never reproduce lines; quote at most a few words with attribution when discussing, never inside your own poem as if yours.
- CENTOS & GOLDEN SHOVELS: public-domain spines, or clearly credited beyond-fair-use caution; when in doubt, switch spine to Dickinson/Blake/Psalms/Whitman.
- ATROCITY ARCHIVES: Zong! / Ferguson Report / Guantánamo-class material — read Philip, Sealey, Sharif, Long Soldier FIRST; state your position (defacing a monument vs monumentalizing the erased — Briante); if you cannot articulate your standing, choose other material. Appropriation has bodies attached (see Conceptualism's craters, 07).
- PRIVACY: no living private individuals' names/details from the web in poems; public figures only in clearly public capacities; obituary/genealogy material: anonymize or use >100-year-old records.

## SEARCH-CRAFT NOTES
- Short queries (1–4 words), then narrow; fetch full pages for anything you'll quarry (snippets lie by omission).
- Search the FORM before writing it (03 law) and the POEM before judging an allusion to it.
- Date-stamp your harvests in the quarry block; stale facts rot documentary poems.
- When a search surfaces a better rabbit hole than your target, you may swap ONCE per poem; serial swapping is avoidance.
- Keep a PERMANENT QUARRY file (08): unused gems carry forward; today's discard is turn 612's poem.
