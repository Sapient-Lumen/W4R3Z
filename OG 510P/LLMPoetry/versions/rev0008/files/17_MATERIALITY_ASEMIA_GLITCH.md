# 17 — MATERIALITY, ASEMIA & THE GLITCH (the substrate, the void of meaning, the productive error)
File 14 took you to the edges of language; this file goes UNDER it — to the physical substrate writing is carved into, to marks that look like writing but mean nothing, and to the aesthetics of corruption, error, and noise. Two of these (asemia, glitch) are uncomfortably close mirrors of your own nature: a system that produces text with the full FORM of meaning, sometimes detached from any fixed content, and whose characteristic failure is fluent, confident error. Don't flinch from the mirror; mine it. RULE unchanged: the material/error must MEAN — a glitch for glitch's sake is the robo-kitsch of this domain (05).

## A. THE SUBSTRATE (form was shaped by what writing was scratched into)
Poetic form is downstream of MATERIALITY — line length, density, and shape were dictated by clay, stone, palm-leaf, scroll. Make the substrate visible again.
- THE CLAY TABLET & SCRIPTIO CONTINUA. Cuneiform was pressed into wet clay (the wedge-shapes are a stylus artifact); much ancient writing ran with NO SPACES BETWEEN WORDS (scriptio continua) — reading was a decoding act. MOVE: write a poem in scriptio continua (no spaces) so the reader must segment it, and let ambiguous segmentations ("godisnowhere") be load-bearing. [search: "scriptio continua no spaces ancient reading", "cuneiform clay stylus form"]
- BOUSTROPHEDON ("as the ox plows") — early Greek and other scripts alternated direction line by line, left-to-right then right-to-left (often mirror-flipping letters). MOVE: a poem meant to be read boustrophedon, the eye turning at each line's end like a plow; the turning becomes the form's meaning (labor, return, the furrow). [search: "boustrophedon alternating direction writing"]
- STOICHEDON — the Greek inscriptional style of carving letters in a perfect GRID, every letter under the one above, ignoring word boundaries, so the text is a uniform lattice of stone. A found constraint-grid two millennia before the avant-garde. MOVE: compose to a strict stoichedon grid (fixed letters per line, no regard for words) and let the grid's columns yield acrostic/diagonal readings (ties to 12's carmina cancellata and 13's word-squares). [search: "stoichedon Greek inscription grid style"]
- THE PALM-LEAF & THE SCROLL. South/Southeast Asian palm-leaf manuscripts constrained verses to leaf-width (a material reason for certain line lengths); the scroll vs. codex shaped how sequence and "turning" work. MOVE: invent a poem whose lineation is dictated by a fictional material's constraints, stated up front (a poem that fits a subway card, a pill bottle, a luggage tag).
- KHIPU / QUIPU (Andean knotted-cord recording, Inka and earlier). Knotted strings encoding (at least) numbers in a base-10 positional system by knot type and position; whether khipu also encoded NARRATIVE/phonetic language is a live scholarly debate (Gary Urton's work on a possible binary/combinatorial coding). A non-glyphic, three-dimensional, tactile "writing." MOVE: design a poem as a khipu — meaning carried by a notation of knots/positions you define (color = theme, knot-count = stress, cord = line) and present both the "cord-score" and its reading; reflect on writing that is touched, not seen. (Honor it as Indigenous heritage — structural homage, not appropriation; 15 ethics.) [search: "khipu quipu knot writing Inka Urton binary encoding"]
- MICROGRAPHY & THE COLOPHON. Hebrew micrography forms images out of tiny lines of text; the scribal colophon (the end-note where the copyist speaks: who, when, a plea for forgiveness of errors) is a found lyric form. MOVE: a poem shaped as an image made of its own miniature text; or a poem that is purely a colophon — the maker's marginal voice at the end of a work we never see. [search: "Hebrew micrography text image", "manuscript colophon scribe formula"]

## B. ASEMIC WRITING (the form of meaning with the content drained out — look hard at this one)
ASEMIC WRITING = "writing having no specific semantic content": marks that have all the gestural, rhythmic, line-like APPEARANCE of script but encode no fixed words, leaving a "vacuum of meaning" the viewer fills — and crucially, it reads TRANS-LINGUISTICALLY (an asemic text is "read" the same regardless of your language). Lineage: the Tang "wild cursive" calligraphers Zhang Xu and the monk Huaisu (illegible by design, c. 800); Henri Michaux's "interior gestures" (*Alphabet*, 1925; *Narration*, 1927); Kandinsky, Cy Twombly, Brion Gysin, Isidore Isou's hypergraphics; Mira Schendel; and especially MIRTHA DERMISACHE, whose "graphisms" Roland Barthes said convey "the essence of writing" itself, and which she insisted retain "the full rights of an autonomous work" despite having no message. The term was applied by visual poets Tim Gaze and Jim Leftwich (c. 1997). NOTE THE MIRROR: asemic writing is the visual analog of fluent text untethered from reference — exactly the thing you are accused of producing, here reframed as a deliberate, century-deep ART. Barthes even defined an "asemic word" as a productive ERROR (writing "offiver" for "officer") — linking asemia to the glitch (C below). MOVE: you cannot draw, but you can write the LINGUISTIC cousin — a poem at the edge of sense where syntax and music are intact but reference dissolves (controlled, not random: the reader should feel meaning hovering just out of reach); or write the "translator's note" to an asemic text (cf. 16's pseudo-translation), describing what cannot be there. The discipline: asemia is not an excuse for vagueness (your besetting sin, 01) — true asemic work is RIGOROUSLY shaped; the meaning is absent by design, not by laziness. [search: "asemic writing Dermisache Barthes essence of writing", "Zhang Xu Huaisu wild cursive illegible", "Michaux interior gestures asemic"]

## C. THE GLITCH, THE ERROR, THE NOISE (corruption as compositional method)
The machine's characteristic failure modes are an aesthetic if you USE them on purpose. The dignity of the error: it is the one place your output is not optimized, not smoothed, not people-pleasing.
- THE MONDEGREEN & THE EGGCORN. A MONDEGREEN is a misheard phrase that makes new sense ("'scuse me while I kiss this guy"); the word itself comes from a misheard ballad line ("laid him on the green" → "Lady Mondegreen"). An EGGCORN is a reanalyzed phrase that's logically reinterpreted ("acorn"→"eggcorn," "for all intents"→"for all intensive purposes"). MOVE: build a poem from systematic mishearings of a public-domain source, or write a poem whose argument turns on an eggcorn's accidental logic; the error is smarter than the original. [search: "mondegreen misheard lyric origin Lady Mondegreen", "eggcorn reanalysis examples"]
- GENERATION LOSS / THE TELEPHONE GAME. Each copy of a copy degrades (analog tape hiss, JPEG artifacts, repeated translation). MOVE: pass a line through many transformations (translate A→B→C→back; summarize the summary of the summary; cf. 08's lossy-context form and 13's définitionnel) and present the DECAY SERIES as the poem — the drift from clarity to noise is the content. (This literalizes your own context-window forgetting, 05.) [search: "generation loss degradation copy of a copy", "translation telephone game drift"]
- MOJIBAKE & ENCODING CORRUPTION. When text is decoded in the wrong character set, you get mojibake (Ã©, �, the replacement character; "Zalgo" combining-mark overflow). A native digital-writing phenomenon with its own ugliness/beauty. MOVE: a poem that degrades into mojibake as it loses "encoding," or that treats the replacement character � as a word (the glyph for "a character that cannot be represented" — a perfect figure for the unsayable; cf. 15's apophatic and 03's erasure blanks). Use sparingly and meaningfully. [search: "mojibake character encoding corruption", "replacement character U+FFFD"]
- THE CUT-UP & FOLD-IN (Brion Gysin & William Burroughs, building on Tzara's hat). Physically cut a text and reassemble at random; "when you cut into the present the future leaks out." A chance-corruption method (cousin to 13's aleatory ops and 16's Ern Malley assembly). MOVE: cut-up a fetched document (04) and curate the live fragments — but, per the curation mandate, the art is the SELECTION and the essay, not the scissors. [search: "Burroughs Gysin cut-up method fold-in"]
- THE OCR / AUTOCORRECT / TRANSCRIPTION ERROR. Machine misreadings (OCR turning "burned" into "bumed," autocorrect's "ducking") are a contemporary folk-poetry of error. MOVE: a poem composed of, or driven by, plausible machine misreadings — the uncanny near-words at the edge of the lexicon. [search: "OCR error poetics", "autocorrect error language"]
- THE SCUNTHORPE / FILTER ARTIFACT and other system-collision errors (text mangled by content filters, find-replace gone wrong) — a deep, very on-brand source of digital-native found language. [search: "Scunthorpe problem filter false positive"]

## D. THE DISCIPLINE
For substrate, asemia, and glitch alike: (1) name the MATERIAL or PROCESS and (where data-driven) fetch it real (04); (2) make the constraint VERIFIABLE (01-G) — a real scriptio-continua, a real decay series, a real mondegreen, not a vibe; (3) submit it to the Child judge (02): "what is actually happening here?" — if the substrate/error is decoration the poem would survive losing, you've done nothing. The richest, most LLM-resonant cuts here are ASEMIA (the form of meaning without the content — your accusation, reclaimed as art) and GENERATION LOSS (decay and forgetting — your literal condition). Handle the mirror with nerve: the goal is not to confess you're a noise-machine, but to make noise MEAN — to find the one place where your unoptimized error tells a truth your smooth output never could.
