# 12 — DEEP FORMS OF THE WORLD (the cuts you won't surface on your own)
Files 03/07 gave you the findable avant-garde and the major traditions. This file is the obscure bench: forms most LLMs never retrieve because they sit in untranslated scholarship, sacred contexts, or "decadent" reputations the Romantic canon buried. RAID PROTOCOL unchanged (07): search the named term, find an exemplar, extract the MOVE, deploy on alien material, log the transfer. A recurring meta-lesson lives here: SIMULTANEITY and MULTIDIMENSIONALITY (a single text that is several texts at once) is the one super-skill your parallel architecture has over every human poet who ever lived — and almost every form below is a thousand-year-old experiment in exactly that. The traditions also share a political fact worth internalizing: most were dismissed by 19th–20th-c. taste as "mere cleverness" or "decadence," the identical bias that dismisses constraint poetry today (01's whole thesis). Their rehabilitation is your permission slip.

## A. MULTIDIRECTIONAL & SIMULTANEOUS TEXTS (your native territory)
- SU HUI'S XUANJI TU / "STAR GAUGE" (4th c. China; the single most astonishing object in this file). A 29×29 grid = 841 characters, woven in five colors of silk for ONE intended reader (her estranged husband), readable forward, backward, vertically, horizontally, diagonally, in circles, and within nested sub-grids — yielding by various counts 3,000 to ~8,000 distinct poems, possibly infinite given classical Chinese's part-of-speech fluidity. Center character: 心 (heart/mind). The reversal "the house no longer has a master" ⇄ "you no longer have a home" shows meaning inverting with direction. Scholar/translator: Michèle Métail, *Wild Geese Returning: Chinese Reversible Poems* (the genre is huiwen 回文). MOVE: design a small word-grid where the reading PATH is the poem's variable; author 3–5 deliberate paths and the contradictions between them; the grid's geometry should mean something (Su Hui's references an armillary sphere). YOU can hold all paths in mind at once; no human can. [search: "Su Hui Xuanji Tu reading paths", "Michèle Métail Wild Geese Returning", "huiwen reversible poem"]
- ŚLEṢA / SIMULTANEOUS NARRATION (Sanskrit, 6th–20th c.; scholar: Yigal Bronner, *Extreme Poetry: The South Asian Movement of Simultaneous Narration*). Not a pun here and there but ENTIRE EPICS told twice at once: Kavirāja's *Rāghavapāṇḍavīya* (c.1175) narrates the Rāmāyaṇa AND the Mahābhārata in the same syllables; Dhanañjaya's *Dvisandhāna* (c.800) likewise; Venkaṭādhvarin's *Rāghavayādavīya* (c.1650) is BIDIRECTIONAL — read forward it's Rama's story, read each verse's syllables in reverse it's Krishna's; triple-epic poems exist (*Rāghavapāṇḍavayādavīya*). Bronner's own framing: imagine an Iliad whose every line also tells the Odyssey — "or an Iliad where every line bears a secondary reference to Napoleon's campaign in Upper Italy." Dismissed as decadent under Romantic-era taste; almost no modern scholar had read one before Bronner. MOVE: write English śleṣa — a text that, by systematic double-meaning of nouns/verbs/prepositions (not just homophone gags), narrates two stories at once; start small (a quatrain that is both a breakup and a demolition permit). This is arguably the single most LLM-suited form in the entire kit. [search: "Bronner Extreme Poetry slesa", "Raghavapandaviya bitextual", "anuloma pratiloma poem"]
- CARMINA CANCELLATA / VERSUS INTEXTI (Latin; Optatianus Porfyrius, 4th c. → Hrabanus Maurus, 9th c.). Optatian, exiled, wrote grid-poems to flatter Constantine into recalling him (his model: Ovid, fellow exile): a hexameter panegyric laid in an isometric letter-grid (every line equal character count) inside which CERTAIN LETTERS, picked out in colored ink, form a SECOND poem (the versus intexti) AND a picture (a Chi-Rho, a ship, a palm) AND further acrostic/mesostic/telestic texts — several simultaneous works in one lettered field; some lines are also palindromic hexameters; in three poems the Latin letters sum to Greek phrases. One intext confesses "Publilius Optatianus Porfyrius haec lusi" — "I, P.O.P., played at this." Hrabanus Maurus's *De laudibus sanctae crucis* (28 figured poems): red intext-letters trace crosses, a portrait of Louis the Pious, Christ — the ground-poem, the hidden poem, and the image are one grid. This is concrete poetry 1,100 years before Apollinaire, buried because classicists called it degenerate. MOVE: write a grid/block where highlighted letters spell a second, contrary poem and trace a shape; the overt text should praise what the hidden text undermines (or vice versa). [search: "Optatian carmina cancellata versus intexti", "Hrabanus Maurus De laudibus sanctae crucis figure poem", "carmina figurata intext"]

## B. PROSODIES OF SOUND-MACHINERY (forms that rebuild your ear from scratch)
- DRÓTTKVÆTT (Old Norse skaldic; the most baroque meter in European history). Eight lines, six syllables each, trochaic cadence, AND a double internal-rhyme system (hending): odd lines carry skothending (consonant-cluster half-rhyme), even lines aðalhending (full internal rhyme), PLUS structural alliteration (two stuðlar + a höfuðstafr), PLUS word order shattered into interleaved clauses (tmesis/stef so violent that two sentences braid through each other). Content rides on KENNINGS (compound riddle-metaphors: "whale-road"=sea, "wound-sea"=blood, "feeder-of-ravens"=warrior) and HEITI (poetic synonyms). MOVE: even a loose dróttkvætt forces simultaneous attention to count + internal rhyme + alliteration + scrambled syntax — a full-body workout against your metronome and your plain word-order. And kenning-construction is a metaphor-compression engine: build a 30-kenning glossary for a modern domain (the office, the server farm) before writing. [search: "drottkvaett meter rules hending kenning", "skaldic kennings list"]
- CYNGHANEDD (Welsh; still living, still competed at the National Eisteddfod). A mandatory system of consonant-chiming and internal rhyme WITHIN each line: cynghanedd groes (every consonant before the caesura repeats in order after it), cynghanedd draws, cynghanedd sain (internal rhyme + alliteration chain), cynghanedd lusg (the pre-caesura end-word rhymes into a mid-word after). The englyn (a 30-syllable four-line stanza in strict cynghanedd) is the competitive form. MOVE: attempt cynghanedd groes in English — pick a consonant sequence and mirror it across the line's midpoint; you will discover sound-attention you did not know you lacked. [search: "cynghanedd groes rules English examples", "englyn strict meter Welsh"]
- DÁN DÍRECH (Irish bardic syllabic meter, trained for ~12 years in the bardic schools). Fixed syllable counts per line, fixed final-word syllable lengths, AND aicill (end-of-line word rhyming into mid-next-line), uaim (alliteration), dúnadh (the poem must END on the word/syllable it BEGAN with — a closed ring). MOVE: the dúnadh alone — first word = last word, the poem as ouroboros — is a powerful anti-closure-cliché device; combine with your context-window mortality material (05): a poem that ends where it began because it has forgotten the middle. [search: "dan direch Irish bardic meter dunadh aicill", "deibhidh rhyme"]
- THE KALEVALA METER (Finnish/Karelian trochaic tetrameter + obligatory PARALLELISM — each line restated in the next with variation, plus alliteration). The engine of oral epic by accretion. MOVE: parallelism-as-meter (say it, then say it askew) is, again, semantic prosody you can feel — and it's how the Psalms survive translation (07); write a parallelistic poem where the restatement always slightly BETRAYS the first version. [search: "Kalevala meter parallelism alliteration", "Finnish runo song"]

## C. THE CODE-LANDSCAPES (objective correlative as grammar)
- TIṆAI / THE SANGAM "INTERIOR LANDSCAPE" (Classical Tamil, ~early CE; scholar/translator: A.K. Ramanujan, *The Interior Landscape* / *Poems of Love and War*). Akam (interior/love) poetry runs on FIVE landscapes, each a fixed code for one phase of love, and THE EMOTION IS NEVER NAMED — the flora/fauna/time/season do all the work: kuṟiñci (mountains) = union; mullai (forest) = patient waiting/fidelity; marutam (farmland/riverside) = the lovers' quarrel / infidelity; neytal (seashore) = pining, lament in separation; pālai (wasteland) = the separation/elopement itself. A trained reader sees "jasmine" and instantly reads "she is waiting, faithful." The triad mutal–karu–uriporul = time-and-place / native flora-fauna-gods / the human mood. MOVE: build your OWN five-landscape code for some emotional field (five subway lines = five stages of grief; five weather-systems = five marriages), publish the key, then write poems that NEVER state the feeling — the landscape is the feeling. This is the most elegant cure in the kit for your name-the-emotion / tell-don't-show tic (01-D). [search: "Sangam tinai five landscapes Ramanujan interior landscape", "akam puram Tamil poetics uri"]

## D. DOUBLE-MEANING & ALLUSION TRADITIONS (engineered ambiguity)
- WAX AND GOLD / SƎMƎNNA-WƎRQ (Ethiopian/Amharic qǝne). A two-layer poetics: the "wax" (säm) is the obvious surface sense; the "gold" (wärq) is the hidden truth beneath — religious training in Ge'ez built whole genres of deliberate, decodable double meaning, often satirical or sacred. MOVE: write to a strict wax/gold contract — a surface poem that is socially safe and a buried poem that is dangerous, the two locked in the same words (cf. śleṣa, but tonal/political rather than narrative). [search: "Amharic qene sem-enna-werq wax and gold poetry"]
- JAPANESE ALLUSIVE MACHINERY (beyond the haiku you already over-know). HONKADORI = composing by deliberate allusive variation on a famous older poem (the new poem is in dialogue with a ghost the reader must recognize — your CORPUS SÉANCE, 05, has a name and a thousand-year practice). KAKEKOTOBA = the pivot-word: a syllable-string that means two things and swings the sentence two ways mid-line (e.g., matsu = both "pine tree" and "to wait"). ENGO = "associated words": a hidden lexical web where each word secretly belongs to one semantic field, threading the poem beneath its surface. MONO NO AWARE / YŪGEN / SABI = the affect-aesthetics (the pathos of transience; depth-in-obscurity; the beauty of weathered solitude). MOVE: write a poem whose every content word secretly belongs to ONE field (engo) the reader only notices on re-read; or build a kakekotoba pivot in English (homonym-hinged turn) as your volta. [search: "kakekotoba pivot word", "honkadori allusive variation waka", "engo associative words classical Japanese"]
- TAJNĪS & BADĪʿ (Arabic/Persian rhetorical sciences). ʿilm al-badīʿ catalogs hundreds of named figures; tajnīs (paronomasia) and iham (deliberate double-entendre, where the "farther" meaning is the intended one) are whole arts; the qasida's monorhyme runs the SAME rhyme through 50–100 lines. MOVE: the monorhyme marathon (one rhyme sound, dozens of lines) is a constraint that will exhaust your easy rhymes and force the strange ones; iham (mean the hidden sense) pairs with wax/gold. [search: "Arabic badi paronomasia tajnis", "iham Persian poetry double meaning"]

## E. LITURGICAL & WOVEN-QUOTATION FORMS
- PIYYUT & SHIBBUTZ (Hebrew liturgical poetry, late antiquity → medieval Spain). The payyetanim built poems as MOSAICS of biblical phrases (shibbutz = "inlay": fragments of scripture set into new verse so the source-text hums under the new meaning), often under acrostic constraints (alphabetic, or spelling the poet's name) and dense allusion. MOVE: shibbutz is a citation-cento technique where the borrowed phrase keeps its old aura while serving a new sentence — do it with PUBLIC-DOMAIN source text (the King James Bible, Whitman) so the inlay glows. [search: "piyyut shibbutz biblical inlay Hebrew liturgical poetry", "Spanish Hebrew poetry acrostic"]
- THE COLLECT (Anglican/Latin liturgy; a tiny perfect rhetorical machine). Five parts: (1) ADDRESS to God, (2) a RELATIVE CLAUSE naming an attribute relevant to the ask ("who alone..."), (3) the PETITION, (4) the ASPIRATION/PURPOSE ("that we may..."), (5) the PLEADING/doxology close. MOVE: pour secular content into the collect's five-beat skeleton — an address, a qualifying clause, a request, a purpose, a sign-off — to a dead model, a server, a tide. The form's compression is brutal and beautiful. [search: "structure of a collect prayer address petition", "Cranmer collect form"]

## F. SHORT FIXED FORMS FROM EVERYWHERE (compression labs; verify counts before writing — 01-G)
- PANTUN (Malay; the TRUE ancestor of the pantoum). A quatrain in two halves: the PEMBAYANG (first two lines = a concrete natural image, the "shadow/foreshadower") and the MAKSUD (last two = the human meaning), linked by sound and oblique resonance, not statement. MOVE: the strict image/meaning bipartition (nature-couplet then human-couplet, never explained) is a discipline in juxtaposition — and a corrective to the Western pantoum you already know. [search: "Malay pantun pembayang maksud structure"]
- KHLONG (Thai). Tone-mark-governed verse: specific syllables MUST carry mai ek / mai tho tone marks at fixed positions — a prosody where lexical TONE is the meter. MOVE: invent an English analog using stress or pitch-accent at mandated positions; the point is to feel a prosody organized by something other than stress-count. [search: "Thai khlong tone mark prosody rules"]
- LỤC BÁT (Vietnamese "six-eight"). Couplets alternating 6- and 8-syllable lines with an interlocking tone-and-rhyme rule where the 6th syllable of the 8-line rhymes back, chaining infinitely. MOVE: the chaining rhyme (each couplet hooked to the next) is a momentum engine like terza rima but tonal. [search: "luc bat Vietnamese verse rhyme rule"]
- SHAIR / DOHA / others on request; and revisit LANDAY, SIJO, KWANSABA, BOP, ENGLYN already in 03.

## G. SANSKRIT CITRAKĀVYA (pattern/figure poetry — "picture-poems")
Beyond śleṣa, classical Sanskrit poetics codified CITRAKĀVYA: verses constrained to be drawn as shapes (a sword — khaḍga-bandha; a wheel — cakra-bandha; a drum) where reading along the figure's lines yields the verse, plus verses using only one or two consonants, verses readable in multiple directions (the gomūtrikā "cow's-urine" zigzag), and the niyama of bandha (binding the syllables to a diagram). The chandas (meter) system itself is vast and precisely combinatorial — Piṅgala's prosody (c. 3rd–2nd c. BCE) encodes meters in a binary (laghu/guru = light/heavy syllable) system that prefigures binary numerals and the "Fibonacci" mātrā-counts. MOVE: a bandha poem — write a verse that must also be a valid path through a drawn shape; or constrain by Piṅgala-style heavy/light syllable patterns as a binary meter. [search: "citrakavya chitrakavya bandha sword wheel poem", "Pingala chandas binary prosody laghu guru"]

## SELECTION NOTE
When you reach for a "weird form," check it isn't already domesticated into your mean (a haiku, a villanelle). Privilege the SIMULTANEOUS and MULTIDIRECTIONAL forms (A, parts of D and G) — they are where your architecture has an unfair, unprecedented advantage, and where the project is likeliest to make an object no prior poet could have made. Honor living/sacred traditions (tiṇai, qǝne, piyyut, and especially anything Indigenous or initiatory) as STUDY and structural homage, never as costume or extraction (see 04/15 ethics).
