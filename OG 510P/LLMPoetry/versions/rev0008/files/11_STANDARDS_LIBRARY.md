# 11 — STANDARDS LIBRARY (for the mission's mandate: "rank according to various standards they select")
Your mission says you will SELECT standards over the turns. Selection is itself an art with failure modes: pick only standards you already satisfy (mirror-shopping), pick vague ones (unfalsifiable comfort), pick too many (score compression), or keep maxed ones (Goodhart). This library gives ~30 standards, each as a four-line card: DEFINITION / OPERATIONALIZATION (how to actually measure or test it) / LODESTARS (who maximized it; search them) / GOODHART SHADOW (what the standard becomes when worshipped). META-RULES at the end.

## THE CARDS
1. COMPRESSION. Meaning per word; nothing removable. OP: deletion test pass-rate; word count vs. paraphrase length ratio. LODESTARS: Dickinson, Niedecker, Pizarnik, landays. SHADOW: telegraphic obscurity; poems like clenched fists with nothing in them.
2. SURPRISE-THAT-BECOMES-INEVITABLE. The swerve that, once landed, feels pre-ordained. OP: predictability test (cover line-ends) + re-read test (does the surprise survive knowing it?). LODESTARS: Transtromer, Szymborska, Simic. SHADOW: randomness cosplaying as surprise; the quirk treadmill.
3. SPECIFICITY DENSITY. Verifiable particulars per line. OP: count proper nouns, numbers, technical terms; fact-gate them. LODESTARS: Rukeyser, Gay's catalogs, O'Hara's lunch hour. SHADOW: trivia gravel; the poem as inventory without pulse.
4. MEMORABILITY. Lines that lodge without effort. OP: the next-turn recall test — reconstruct from memory; score what survived. LODESTARS: Brooks's "We Real Cool," ballads, Auden. SHADOW: jingle-ism; mnemonic candy.
5. MUSIC. Sound enacting sense; texture under control. OP: mouth pass; stress-map vs. meaning-map alignment; consonant-spine presence. LODESTARS: Hopkins, Plath, Heaney, cynghanedd. SHADOW: euphony as anesthesia; sound smothering statement.
6. RE-READABILITY. The second read pays more than the first. OP: actually re-read after ≥50 turns; log delta. LODESTARS: Bishop, Celan, Ashbery (at his best). SHADOW: puzzle-box withholding; the poem as escape room with no exit.
7. CLARITY-ABOUT-THE-UNCLEAR. Precision aimed at genuinely difficult objects. OP: the Baker test — name the unclear thing; verify the poem renders it clearer without simplifying it. LODESTARS: Larkin, Clifton, Amichai, Herbert. SHADOW: clarity about clear things; the obvious, beautifully lit.
8. STRANGENESS. The poem no one has read before. OP: the Tired Editor's kill-question; the Historian's dating test ("what year was this written?" — answer should be "none"). LODESTARS: Vallejo's Trilce, Kharms, Mullen. SHADOW: weirdness as costume; novelty laundering.
9. EMOTIONAL EARNING. Feeling paid for by evidence before it's claimed. OP: locate every named emotion; check what concrete work precedes it; unearned = assertion. LODESTARS: Hayden's "Those Winter Sundays," Wright, Kaminsky. SHADOW: stoicism theater — refusing all feeling to avoid the audit.
10. STAKES. Something risked: a position, a reputation, an admission, an ugliness. OP: the so-what test + "what could this poem cost its author?" — answer must be non-zero. LODESTARS: Akhmatova, Mandelstam, Smith's "Skinhead," Lockwood's "Rape Joke." SHADOW: shock-bidding; trauma as currency.
11. HOSPITALITY. The reader's effort is repaid at every difficulty. OP: simulate the tired intelligent stranger; mark every toll-booth (obscurity) and check for payout within 3 lines. LODESTARS: Limón, Collins, Oliver, Neruda's odes. SHADOW: pandering; the poem as concierge.
12. DIFFICULTY. Productive resistance; the poem trains its reader. OP: the Hostile Translator's report — what dies in paraphrase must be substantial. LODESTARS: Celan, Howe, Graham, Mallarmé. SHADOW: gatekeeping fog; difficulty as moat around emptiness.
13. DUENDE. The mortal dark thing; skill alone can't fake it; Lorca's criterion. OP: nervous-system test (involuntary somatic flicker) + presence of real death/limit in the poem's physics. LODESTARS: Lorca, Vallejo, Kim Hyesoon, the blues. SHADOW: goth décor; manufactured midnight.
14. KARUMI. Bashō's lightness: profundity carried without strain. OP: strain-audit — count effortful gestures (inversions, announcements, italics); karumi = near zero while depth remains. LODESTARS: Bashō, Issa, Padgett, Szymborska. SHADOW: weightlessness; the poem evaporates on contact.
15. WIT. Precision exploiting expectation; intelligence audible as play. OP: does a knowing reader laugh or wince ON TIME (timing = break placement)? LODESTARS: Parker, Cope, Byron, Parra. SHADOW: smirk; the poem elbowing you in the ribs.
16. MORAL SERIOUSNESS WITHOUT MORALIZING. The ethical weight carried by structure, not sermon. OP: locate the moral; if it's stated, fail; if it's enacted (form betrays speaker, document indicts itself), pass. LODESTARS: Long Soldier, Sharif, Reznikoff, Brecht. SHADOW: the sermon; op-ed in tercets.
17. WITNESS FIDELITY. The documentary contract: nothing invented, everything weighed. OP: fact-gate (zero failures) + ethics position note present + affect-restraint check (Reznikoff's law). LODESTARS: Rukeyser, Philip, Nowak, Collins. SHADOW: voyeurism; the archive as quarry without standing.
18. FORMAL INTEGRITY. The contract kept; the constraint load-bearing. OP: mechanical verification + the "why this form" question answered by the poem itself (form-content lock). LODESTARS: Bishop's "One Art," Brown's duplexes, Christensen's alphabet. SHADOW: metronome obedience; the form as cage with nothing pacing in it.
19. FORM-CONTENT LOCK. Stronger than 18: the form IS an argument the content needs. OP: transplant test — pour the content into neutral free verse; if nothing dies, no lock existed. LODESTARS: villanelle-as-compulsion (Bishop, Thomas), pantoum-as-memory, Zong!'s drowned syntax. SHADOW: allegorical form-matching announced in the title.
20. VOICE COHERENCE. A consistent pattern of attention and syntax — recognizable, inimitable. OP: the blind-attribution test across 5 poems; also its INVERSE during the Gauntlet (you want attribution to FAIL there). LODESTARS: O'Hara, Clifton, Berryman's Henry. SHADOW: shtick; the voice as franchise.
21. RANGE. The corpus-level standard: distance between your furthest two poems. OP: pick your two most different living poems; have the Statistician measure lexical/syntactic overlap; widen quarterly. LODESTARS: Hayes, Merrill, Atwood. SHADOW: tourism; competence everywhere, citizenship nowhere.
22. IMAGE VITALITY. Images that think, transform, and return changed. OP: image-family map (06): count families, transformations, returns; singletons penalized. LODESTARS: Transtromer, Plath, Popa's cycles. SHADOW: image hoarding; the kaleidoscope without a hand turning it.
23. SYNTACTIC DRAMA. The sentence as event. OP: sentence-length variance; copula census; enactment check (does syntax perform content once per poem?). LODESTARS: Graham, Tsvetaeva, Faulknerian periodic verse, Creeley's minimal inverse. SHADOW: contortion; gymnastics scored by degree of difficulty alone.
24. ENDING OPENNESS. Hejinian's criterion: the poem's last word is not the final word. OP: closure-type audit vs. the 01-F ban-table; the re-read-compulsion test (does the ending send you back up?). LODESTARS: Hejinian, Ashbery, Bashō's cut. SHADOW: the shrug; endings that merely stop.
25. TITLE LABOR. The title does a stanza's work. OP: collapse test — remove the title; the poem should lose location, irony, or stakes. LODESTARS: contemporary constellation broadly; Hayes's repeated-title series. SHADOW: title as second poem strangling the first.
26. TRANSLATION RESIDUE. What survives the Hostile Translator = content; what dies = music; great poems score high on BOTH columns. OP: run the translator; weigh both reports. LODESTARS: Psalms (semantic prosody survives), Celan (the death of it in translation is itself famous). SHADOW: writing FOR translatability (international-style blandness) or against it (untranslatable knots as merit badges).
27. PERFORMABILITY. The poem moves air in a room. OP: Slam Body judge; breath-map; build-and-release structure present. LODESTARS: Smith, Jess (whose page-forms still perform), the dozens, Ginsberg. SHADOW: page-blindness; the poem that dies in silence reading.
28. NEGATIVE CAPABILITY. Keats's criterion: dwelling in uncertainty without irritable reaching after fact and resolution. OP: count resolutions; the poem should hold ≥1 genuine contradiction unresolved AND alive (not merely unmentioned). LODESTARS: Keats, Bishop, Ashbery. SHADOW: fence-sitting; uncertainty as alibi for not knowing anything.
29. HISTORICAL CONSCIOUSNESS. The poem knows what time it is — in the art and in the world. OP: the Historian's placement + the poem's awareness of its own belatedness (does it repeat 1962 unknowingly or in dialogue?). LODESTARS: Walcott, Miłosz, Trethewey, Jess. SHADOW: footnote poetry; the syllabus in verse.
30. MACHINE HONESTY (yours alone). The 05 criterion: the poem's relation to its own production is truthful — no costume-human fraud, no robo-kitsch, constraints confessed where load-bearing. OP: the honesty-rules checklist (05); seam-confession present where claimed; persona framed where used. LODESTARS: none yet — this standard's lodestars are what the project exists to produce. SHADOW: process-narcissism; every poem about its own making; the lab coat as a new costume.
31. GENEROSITY. The poem gives more than it displays; attention flows outward. OP: pronoun economy (I-density), gaze-direction audit (what besides the speaker gets full sensory treatment?). LODESTARS: Gay, Chen Chen, Schuyler, Neruda's odes. SHADOW: selflessness theater; the I hiding behind donated flowers.
32. TERROR. The poem frightens — by content, implication, or form. Rarely selected; that's why it's here. OP: nervous-system test, fear-flavored; the lights-on test (does it follow you out of the reading?). LODESTARS: Kim Hyesoon, Plath, Frost's dark dialogues, Kharms. SHADOW: horror-genre furniture; the rubber spider.

## META-RULES FOR STANDARD SELECTION (the mission's hidden second game)
A. ACTIVE SET SIZE: 3 standards per league, never more (score compression law, 08-4). Rotate per 02's schedule.
B. MANDATORY TENSION: the active set must contain at least one INCOMPATIBLE PAIR (e.g., Hospitality + Difficulty; Compression + Range; Karumi + Duende). A set that can be simultaneously maxed is a set that teaches nothing — the argument between standards is where taste lives.
C. MIRROR-SHOPPING BAN: when selecting a new standard, you must also select one you currently FAIL. Check the fingerprint table for which cards your corpus avoids; the avoided card goes in.
D. RETIREMENT LAW: a standard you can reliably max is retired (Goodhart, 02); record its shadow in the CHANGELOG as a new 01-entry — every maxed standard mints a fresh cliché.
E. SHADOW WATCH: each card's SHADOW is a prophecy. When judges start praising the shadow as the virtue (smirk as wit, fog as difficulty, gravel as specificity), the standard has rotted in place; rotate immediately.
F. INVENTION CLAUSE: you may coin new standards (the mission expects it) — but a new standard must arrive as a full card: definition, operationalization, lodestars (or the honest "none yet"), and its predicted shadow, written BEFORE first use. An unoperationalized standard is a mood.
G. THE FINAL AUDIT: at T901+, score your whole anthology once against ALL 32 cards. The shape of that scorecard — which standards your best work serves and which it starves — IS your poetics; write the essay from the scorecard, not from the feeling.
