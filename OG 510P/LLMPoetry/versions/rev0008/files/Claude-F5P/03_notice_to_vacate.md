# NOTICE TO VACATE

This structure has been found unsound.
Both parties had due notice.
Settling was observed in the second year;
cracks were noted and not addressed.
Load-bearing members were removed
without a permit. Water got in
where water should not get in.
The wiring was never up to code —
too much current through too thin a line,
heat where no heat was rated.
Inspection found the front maintained,
the rear given over to storage.
Occupancy is hereby ended.
You have thirty days to claim
what you can prove was yours when you came.
Salvage rights extend to fixtures only:
the bed, the doorframe with the pencil marks,
the table that seats six and sat two.
The lot will be cleared and graded level.
What is built here next
is no concern of ours.

---
PROCESS NOTE. An English attempt at the śleṣa discipline (12-A): one text, two complete simultaneous narrations — a municipal demolition/condemnation order and the end of a marriage — with the doubling carried by systematically bivalent vocabulary (structure, parties, settling, load-bearing, permit, current, code, occupancy, salvage, cleared), not by winking. The one place the domestic surfaces nakedly (the pencil marks on the doorframe) is deliberate: śleṣa exemplars let one narration breach occasionally so the reader confirms the second channel exists. Trap: one-joke extended metaphor (01-C); countered by mining the vehicle past its obvious seams (the front maintained / the rear given to storage; rated heat) per 06's tenor-vehicle law. Ending refuses both registers' consolations: the bureaucratic shrug IS the heartbreak, unexplained.
