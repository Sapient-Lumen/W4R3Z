# BEAUFORT, FOR TWO

Force 0. Sea like a mirror. Smoke goes straight up.
We thought this was the permanent condition.

Force 1. Ripples with the look of scales. You said a thing
at your sister's table I decided not to hear.

Force 2. Leaves rustle. The vane begins to move.
Small adjustments of the chairs at dinner.

Force 3. A flag extends. Paper lifts from the desk
and neither of us bends to pick it up.

Force 4. Dust and loose papers raised. Small branches move.
The kind of weather people call good weather.

Force 5. Small trees in leaf begin to sway.
You took the long way home and told me so.

Force 6. Large branches in motion. Umbrellas used with difficulty.
We stopped taking each other anywhere open.

Force 7. Whole trees in motion. Effort needed to walk.
I practiced sentences at the bathroom sink.

Force 8. Twigs break off. Progress generally impeded.
Your mother called it a rough patch. Patch of what.

Force 9. Slight structural damage occurs. Slates blown away.
We kept the damage to the upper rooms.

Force 10. Trees uprooted. Considerable structural damage.
Seldom experienced inland, the handbook says. It is.

Force 11. Widespread damage. Very rarely observed.
The neighbors brought their dog in off the line.

Force 12. The air is filled with foam and spray.
Sea entirely white. Visibility almost nothing.
No craft of our size should have been out in it.

---
PROCESS NOTE. Seed 3 executed: the Beaufort wind scale (Force 0–12) as the stages of a marriage, with the two governing words — the weather one and the feeling one — banned from the text. The left-hand clauses track the real scale's canonical observational language (mirror sea, scales, vanes, umbrellas with difficulty, twigs break, slates, "seldom experienced inland," white sea at 12); the right-hand clauses are the household readings, never explanatory, never naming the tenor. Trap: chart-explains-itself (seed 3's warning) — countered by letting several forces carry only behavior (chairs, the long way home, the dog off the line). The found-language ending ("no craft of our size...") is the scale's own small-craft logic bent one degree; consolation withheld. Structure deliberately violates the uniform-couplet wallpaper (01-B) by letting Force 12 run three lines: the storm breaks the stanza pattern — that overflow is this folder's clinamen candidate; check it against the others.
