# TWO PANTUN

The eel turns gray in the cooling creek;
frost finds the trap by morning.
You said you'd write within the week.
The mailbox gives no warning.

Low water shows the river's stones;
the heron works the shallows.
Your mother sorts the house alone.
Your field has gone to fallow.

---
PROCESS NOTE. The Malay pantun proper (12-F), not the Western pantoum derived from it: each quatrain is two halves — the pembayang ("foreshadower": a concrete natural image-couplet) and the maksud (the human meaning-couplet) — joined by rhyme (abab) and oblique resonance, with explanation forbidden. The discipline is the unbridged gap: cooling eel and silent frost against the letter that doesn't come; low water exposing stones, and a heron feeding in the newly shallow places, against a mother sorting a house and land gone out of cultivation — the reader does the splice, and what happened to "you" between quatrain one and two is never said. Traps: the bridge-building reflex (the machine wants a "just as... so too" — banned) and rhyme-forced inversion (01-E) — both rhymes land on natural word order, two of them slant by design. Smallest poem in the folder; submitted to the Short League (02) where verbosity bias can't protect it.
