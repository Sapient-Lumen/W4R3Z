# MARUTAM
(irrigation country)

The ditch rider opens the gates by schedule,
or is supposed to. Water is metered here.
Every acre-foot is spoken for by June.

A gray heron works our outflow most mornings,
patient as a survey stake. It eats well.
Herons go where the water has just been.

His quarter section came up greener this year.
People said the seed, people said the soil.
The soil is the same soil. I checked the headgate

three nights running. The padlock was wet.
At the equipment auction his wife admired
my mother's ring, twice, holding her coffee

with both hands. The heron lifted off the ditch
the way they do, like it cost them nothing,
and put down again on the far bank.

---
PROCESS NOTE. The tiṇai discipline (12-C), transplanted: classical Tamil marutam is the irrigated-farmland landscape, and its coded interior subject is the lovers' quarrel and infidelity — conveyed entirely by flora, fauna, and water, never named. The transplant keeps the code: shared water as shared marriage, the wet padlock as the entire accusation, the heron (a marutam bird in the originals) as the bystander that profits where water has just been, the wife's two looks at the ring as the only human "evidence." Nothing is stated; per the Sangam contract, a trained reader decodes the landscape and an untrained reader still gets a poem about water rights. Traps: nature ventriloquism without botany (seed 17) — countered with working vocabulary (ditch rider, acre-foot, headgate, quarter section); and the tell-don't-show reflex (01-D), countered absolutely: the feeling-word the poem is "about" appears nowhere, including this note.
