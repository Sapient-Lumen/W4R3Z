# BREAD AND BUTTER
(a note left for our houseguest)

Cousin, the guest room misses you already.
Of course you're welcome any time at all.
Under the circumstances, three weeks flew.
Nobody makes an omelet quite like yours.
The eggs were ours, but still. The thought was yours.
Time, as you said so often, is a gift.
How generously you gave us yours, and gave.
Everything you touched, you left a mark on.
Surfaces gleam where you were helpful, dusting.
Please don't worry about the cellar door.
Odd, how it came unlocked. These old houses.
One day we'll laugh about the silverware.
Nothing was taken, surely, that won't surface.
Stay away as long as you need. Stay well.

---
PROCESS NOTE. A null-cipher poem (18-B): the surface is a bread-and-butter note (the traditional houseguest thank-you), and the line-initials spell the hidden verdict — COUNT THE SPOONS — verified letter by letter (01-G). The craft target was DOUBLE leakage: the surface itself should curdle on reread ("the eggs were ours," "you left a mark on," "that won't surface," the two "Stay"s), so that the acrostic, once found, feels inevitable rather than appended (Standard 2, surprise-that-becomes-inevitable). The hidden layer is decodable by an ordinary reader — the 18-C requirement that a cipher be extractable or it's private noise. Registers in play: suspicion, pettiness, social performance — all far from the machine's documented warmth prior (01-D), which is the anti-classification work here. Old etiquette note: "counting the spoons" after a guest leaves is the proverbial act of distrust; the title's gentility against the spine's accusation is the whole poem.
