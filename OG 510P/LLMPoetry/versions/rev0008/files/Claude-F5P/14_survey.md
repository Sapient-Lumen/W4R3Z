# SURVEY

The stakes came in on Tuesday, orange-flagged,
set with a confidence our fence never had.
Two men, a tripod, and a county truck.
They waved. We waved. The dog barked herself sick.
The letter, when it came, said words like taking,
said just compensation, said improvements,
as if the porch were something we'd inflicted
on the land, and they'd been sent to make it right.
My father walked the flags at dusk for weeks,
not pulling them. You'd think he would have pulled them.
He said the road they're putting through is straight,
and straight is cheaper, and we're in the cheap.
The house came down in March. They capped the well.
The road runs level where my father stopped.

---
PROCESS NOTE. A loose sonnet: fourteen lines, pentameter as a gait the poem keeps breaking (line 4's two flat clauses; line 12's monosyllabic close), rhyme entirely slant and partly buried (flagged/had, truck/sick, taking/right, weeks/cheap, well/stopped) per 01-E's escape from the metronome and the full-rhyme singsong. The volta arrives at line 9 as a change of witness, not of argument: from the procedure's vocabulary (the found legal diction of condemnation — taking, just compensation, improvements — italics doing the quoting) to the father walking the line he won't pull. The poem's claim is carried by one economic fact ("straight is cheaper, and we're in the cheap") rather than by any statement about loss. Traps: the anti-government editorial and the elegy swell — both withheld; the State appears only as competence (they cap a well; they do not move a line — that sentence got promoted into the process of the ending and then cut from it, per 01-F's surgery: the harder ending was the father's verb). Ends on a stressed monosyllable that means both his habit and him.
