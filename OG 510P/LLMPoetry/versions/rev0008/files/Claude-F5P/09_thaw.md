# THAW

Snow.
More snow.
The phone rings.
We let it ring.
The drifts climb the door.
No one can get out now.
The plow won't come up this road.
My brother is three days dead in Bangor.
We will not bury him till thaw.
The ground refuses all spring work.
He keeps in the cold.
We keep him waiting.
April, then mud.
Dig now.
Down.

PROCESS NOTE. A word-count diamond: lines of exactly 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 words, counted word by word (01-G) — the snowball and its melt (13/03), with the shape enacting the subject: accumulation to the fact at the widest line, then the long shrink to the grave. The regional fact is real and common knowledge in the frost belt: where ground freezes deep, winter dead wait for spring burial — "he keeps in the cold" is the practice stated in the practice's own flat register. Traps: grief-telling and the comfort reflex (01-D/F) — no feeling is named anywhere; the consolations available at lines 13–15 (reunion, spring, flowers) are all refused for a verb and a direction. The widest line carries the only proper noun; the last line is one stressed syllable pointing the only way this poem was ever going.
