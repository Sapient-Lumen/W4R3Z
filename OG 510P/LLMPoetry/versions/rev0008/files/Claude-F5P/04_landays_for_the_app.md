# LANDAYS FOR THE APP

My rating fell to four point six stars.
Smile more, the tips said. I was smiling at the dark road.

The map shows I am one of nine cars
circling the same wet block for the same cold plate of fries.

Five stars for the man who tipped in change
and stood and watched me count it on the step in the rain.

My car smells like a thousand dinners,
none of them mine; my son eats at six with just his phone.

When I am late they thumb me one star.
God rates the city all night and never gets shut off.

---
PROCESS NOTE. Form (03/12-F): the landay — anonymous Pashtun women's couplet, 9 syllables then 13, prized for bite without commentary. Counts verified syllable-by-syllable per line (01-G): 9/13 ×5. Trap named in seed 24: editorializing — the landay never explains; each couplet ends on the image or the fact and trusts the sting. The traditional landay's bitter address to God is honored in the last couplet, transposed to the platform without naming the platform. Banlist check: no abstraction is mourned; the grievances are a wage, a wait, a meal, a child. The anonymity is structural: nothing in these five couplets identifies a poet, a brand, or a city — the form's old survival trick, intact in the gig economy.
