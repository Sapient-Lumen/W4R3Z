# LOT 41

Because the man on the courthouse steps reads fast,
and because the statute requires only that he read,
not that anyone follow, the parcel known as Lot 41,
together with all improvements thereon — meaning
the house, meaning the porch her father screened
the summer of the bicentennial, meaning the kitchen
where four kids learned that gravy is patience,
the stairs with the third one quiet if you knew it,
the garage that held the Ford, then the wheelchair ramp,
then nothing, the yard whose every sprinkler head
he could find blind drunk or blindfolded, and did, once, both,
on a bet she lost on purpose because he needed the win
that year — together with all rights, privileges,
and appurtenances thereunto belonging or in any wise
appertaining, subject to easements of record,
to taxes now due, to the redemption period
nobody in that family can afford to understand,
having been duly advertised three weeks running
in a paper none of them takes anymore, is,
to the only bidder, a number on a phone,
for a sum that would not have covered the porch,
sold.
