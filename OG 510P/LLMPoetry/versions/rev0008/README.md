# LLMPoetry — rev0008

## A workshop that can put its own rules aside

This edition pairs fifteen worked poems with an extensive craft-and-research library. Its aim is not only to produce fluent verse, but to find forms, facts and voices that interrupt an easy default and then survive demanding revision. The most useful way in is to read a poem, inspect the craft decision that makes it move, and only afterward ask what the workshop's rules contribute.

Begin with [**Harold**](files/Claude-F5P/10_harold.md) and [**Notice to Vacate**](files/Claude-F5P/03_notice_to_vacate.md). One turns a child's misheard prayer into a familiar figure of small maintenance; the other lets an official notice describe both a building and a marriage. Neither needs to be reduced to an example of a successful rule. The poems are credited in the [specimen manifest](files/Claude-F5P/00_MANIFEST.md) to Claude (Fable 5), and each carries a process note that a reader can compare with the actual effect of the lines.

## From a procedure to an event in language

In **Harold**, the correction of a name changes the scene. The child learns the sanctioned wording while keeping the old name privately. The process note identifies that turn and the risk of cuteness, but the poem's unresolved large requests matter as much as the small ones that appear to land. The correction does not simply erase the mishearing or prove the child's theology.

In **Notice to Vacate**, the administrative register gives the poem its authority and its inadequacy. A directive can inventory fixtures and demand proof of prior ownership; it cannot neatly account for the shared life suggested by a marked doorframe or a table. The process note describes an attempt at two simultaneous narrations. Read whether both stay active, rather than treating that description as a certificate that the form worked.

These poems make a useful companion to [**Craft Mechanics**](files/06_CRAFT_MECHANICS.md), especially **Syntax as Drama**, **Metaphor**, **The Volta**, and **Diction & Register**. Its proposals concern actions words can perform: delaying a clause, making a register collide with another, carrying an extended metaphor beyond its first obvious resemblance, or turning the reader's account of what came before. Its final revision question asks what the new poem knows that the old one did not. A changed adjective is not necessarily a changed discovery.

## The kit revises its own ambition

The [opening operating document](files/00_README_OPERATING_SYSTEM.md) proposes a thousand-turn enterprise with research, constraints, judges, rankings, recurring disruptions and extensive records. Read it beside the later [self-critique](files/24_RED_TEAM_REPORT.md), not as the edition's uncontested last instruction.

That critique finds that the archive can become too large to use and its ceremonies can consume the art. RT-1 changes the model from reading everything repeatedly to consulting a library by need. RT-2 makes much of the larger protocol advisory and asks what it costs the poems. The compact [boot card](files/25_BOOT_CARD.md) carries that revised arrangement. This is a meaningful design change: continuity does not require every stored rule to occupy every working turn.

RT-6 then challenges the project's own pursuit of strangeness. Avoiding familiar language can become another repetitive style. Its alternative question is what *this poem needs*, not whether it is unlike the maker's usual output. That lets clarity remain an artistic achievement rather than a failure to be strange. RT-8 also permits an honest early ending with a surviving collection, however small, accompanied by a failure record and a short account of what the work established. Even zero surviving poems can become a documented result; the planned turn count is not itself the measure of success.

These are the workshop's proposals for correcting itself. The archive's broader empirical claims about models and judging remain claims to examine through their cited sources, not facts established by the existence of the kit.

## Specimens, criticism and evidence have different jobs

The fifteen-poem folder is substantial work to read, not a scorecard announcing that the experiment succeeded. Its manifest labels the poems first-draft specimens, records that phrase-search checks have not yet been run, and distinguishes its design intentions from later judgment. The later self-critique's RT-10 also separates study objects from independent evidence-set members and comparators: a model that studies these poems and imitates their moves has not thereby demonstrated an independent achievement.

That boundary does not diminish the poems' literary availability. A reader can find a poem moving, predictable, funny or overexplained without first admitting it into an experiment. Conversely, a mechanically satisfied constraint does not settle its artistic force. The self-critique's verification ladder concerns what formal claims can honestly be made, not a replacement for encountering the writing.

- **Craft and revision:** [Craft Mechanics](files/06_CRAFT_MECHANICS.md) → the same two poems → RT-6 in the [self-critique](files/24_RED_TEAM_REPORT.md). Ask where a rule helps and where it would flatten a particular voice.
- **How the workshop changed its mind:** [opening design](files/00_README_OPERATING_SYSTEM.md) → RT-1, RT-2 and RT-8 in the [self-critique](files/24_RED_TEAM_REPORT.md) → [boot card](files/25_BOOT_CARD.md).
