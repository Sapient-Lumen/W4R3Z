# The Lower Boards

## Poem

Behind the Marine Inspection Office,
the pier kept a ruler
for water.

Not because water asked.
Not because wood could hold it.

A staff once stood there
with its first zero
cut low
into the weather.

The harbor crossed it
all day.

Ferry wash slapped the piles.
Salt lifted
through old bolt holes.
Green weed combed
the lower boards.

Someone changed the metal.
The beginning stayed
underfoot.

Gulls walked over it.

No value came back.

Water did.

No minute.
No borrowed height.

Only wind
taking the rail's edge,
only brine
darkening the wood.

The staff is gone enough
to be floor,
not gone enough
to be past.

When the tide pulls out,
the boards keep shining
below the shoe line.

When it returns,
it does not read.

It knocks.
It leaves a narrow sound
inside the old holes.
