## Finding

D027 rightly escaped D026's clean horizontal/vertical paradox. `North / of the north curb / of Bridge Street` has useful dry-source friction, and the poem body avoids dimensions, tables, and NOAA prose.

But the draft still behaves like an improved address lyric. `ready for a height / that had to arrive / from somewhere else` explains the relation it should force the reader to feel. The curb, sidewalk, copper, staff, and absent live value remain accurate, but the poem's last move turns them into a neat summary of remote measurement.

The next revision should not add more curb syntax. It should move the pressure down to NOAA's Station Datum definition: a fixed base elevation established lower than water is ever expected to reach, held constant despite gauge or staff changes. That source fact is stranger than another address. Use it as a floor under the dry north-curb mark.

## What holds

- The dry address phrase is more resistant than D026's geometry lesson.
- The poem body keeps exact offsets and dimensions out of view.
- `No value came back.` remains a useful boundary line.

## What fails

- The ending explains height arriving from elsewhere.
- The source address is still more interesting than the poem's transformation of it.
- The draft does not yet make the fixed-zero pressure bodily enough.

## Revision instruction

Make one bounded reanchor to the Station Datum clause: lower than water is ever expected to reach. Preserve north-curb/copper/sidewalk, staff, first-zero, and no-live-value pressure. Do not return to D027's `ready for a height` closure or D026's horizontal/vertical thesis.
