## Finding

D026 found a more resistant object than the fifth-step drafts: the mark is not a disk, not a square, not a stamped name, but a line on a bolt. The geometry is genuinely strange: a horizontal line on a bolt set vertically, in the north face of a south buttress.

But the draft makes that strangeness too legible. `The mark is not the bolt`, `held level / on something / standing up`, and `without water / showing why` explain the paradox before the reader can live in it. The poem turns the NOAA phrase into a tidy geometry lesson.

The next pressure should not amplify the paradox. It should move to the dry address language around it: north of the north curb of Bridge Street, above the sidewalk, with water and the staff elsewhere. The mark becomes more disturbing if the poem lets the curb, sidewalk, buttress, and absent water remain ordinary.

## What holds

- The line/bolt object is stronger than the underfoot counting thesis.
- The poem body avoids visible NOAA dimensions and source-table display.
- The no-value boundary remains concise and honest.

## What fails

- The title and opening over-name the paradox.
- The closing explains the line as the place height begins.
- The poem still depends on a clever opposition: horizontal on vertical.

## Revision instruction

Make one bounded reanchor to the north-curb / sidewalk / buttress address language on the same NOAA benchmark sheet. Keep the staff, first-zero, and no-live-value boundary. Do not return to D026's horizontal/vertical thesis or D025's shoe/counting ending.
