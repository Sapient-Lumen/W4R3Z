# Righteousness

## What is still owed

A defensible decision can leave a real claim unanswered. An apology can begin a repair without making the earlier act innocent. Caring for someone close can be part of what is due, while favoring that same person in public office can betray it. Righteousness takes these differences seriously.

This sequence of philosophical essays asks how people and institutions remain faithful to what others may rightly claim from them, especially when gain, fear, status or the appearance of virtue pulls the other way. Its central concern is the connection between judgment, conduct and answerability: seeing what is owed, acting on it, and remaining responsible for what the action leaves behind.

**Start with the [earlier Working Theory](versions/rev0023/files/docs/10-canon/110-working-theory.md).** It gives the clearest compact account of the project's vocabulary and its movement from personal fidelity to public power. Then follow the questions below into the individual essays.

## A moral vocabulary with consequences

[Righteousness vs. Neighboring Concepts](versions/rev0127/files/docs/10-canon/130-righteousness-vs-neighbors.md) explains why the project needs this particular word. Goodness can describe a desirable result without settling how it was obtained. Integrity can hold a corrupt outlook together. Compliance can leave a person formally blameless while something genuinely owed goes unprovided. Righteousness brings fidelity under pressure and answerability after breach into the judgment.

That demand has limits as well as force. [Thresholds, Minima, and Excess](versions/rev0127/files/docs/10-canon/155-thresholds-minima-and-excess.md) places it between evasive minimal compliance and a requirement to be heroic in every case. What counts as ordinary duty can nevertheless grow with entrusted power, dependence or responsibility.

The comparative ambition is equally important. [Portable Core and Sacred Thickening](versions/rev0127/files/docs/10-canon/145-portable-core-and-sacred-thickening.md) asks what can be recognized across moral traditions without treating God, covenant, repentance or grace as removable decoration. Its companion, [Recognition Status and Translation Burden](versions/rev0127/files/docs/10-canon/150-recognition-status-and-translation-burden.md), allows a proposed translation to count as identity, incomplete identity, a neighboring analogue, or a failure. Agreement on a public rule need not mean agreement about what righteousness ultimately is.

## A right choice can leave a remainder

One of the strongest passages is [Conflicting Claims, Priority, and Moral Remainder](versions/rev0127/files/docs/10-canon/180-conflicting-claims-priority-and-moral-remainder.md). A promise and an urgent protective duty can both be real even when they cannot both be fulfilled. Choosing the stronger claim does not make the other disappear. Explanation, mitigation or later repair may still be owed.

[Portable Priority Cues](versions/rev0127/files/docs/10-canon/200-portable-priority-cues-and-burden-placement.md) gives this judgment some substance: grave harm, dependency, induced reliance and irreversible loss normally press harder than convenience or prestige. It also asks who created the predicament and who should carry its costs.

Read this beside [Breach-Response and Reparative Standing](versions/rev0127/files/docs/10-canon/160-breach-response-and-reparative-standing.md) and [Avowal, Requalification, and No Instant Purity](versions/rev0127/files/docs/10-canon/170-avowal-requalification-and-no-instant-purity.md). Repair must reach beyond a statement of regret, but it cannot demand forgiveness from the wronged as proof of completion. Changed conduct may rebuild standing over time without rewriting the earlier wrong. These distinctions make room for moral recovery without turning recovery into exoneration.

## From personal conduct to the conditions of ordinary life

The earlier snapshot's [Special Ties, Loyalty, and Impartial Bounds](versions/rev0023/files/docs/10-canon/240-special-ties-loyalty-and-impartial-bounds.md) makes the transition concrete. Relationships can deepen obligations; public roles can instead require restraint or recusal. The issue is which claim the relationship creates in that particular setting.

The later [Anti-Domination and Benevolent Mastery](versions/rev0127/files/docs/10-canon/245-anti-domination-and-benevolent-mastery.md) asks whether someone's security depends too much on a superior continuing to be kind. [Public Proof, Contestability, and Secrecy Burdens](versions/rev0127/files/docs/10-canon/175-public-proof-contestability-and-secrecy-burdens.md) develops the institutional answer: reasons, records, challenge and review give people something firmer than reassurance.

The expanded essays follow that concern into familiar arrangements:

- [Background files](versions/rev0127/files/docs/10-canon/735-consumer-reporting-background-files-and-no-future-by-file.md): when a dossier shapes a person's opportunities, can they see and correct what is deciding their future?
- [Digital-only administration](versions/rev0127/files/docs/10-canon/740-digital-only-administration-portal-services-and-no-rights-by-portal.md): when a portal fails, does the underlying claim still have a human, assisted or offline route?
- [Consent or pay](versions/rev0127/files/docs/10-canon/760-consent-or-pay-tracking-walls-and-no-privacy-by-tribute.md): when meaningful participation requires tracking consent or a fee to escape it, how real is the freedom to refuse?

Their repeated question is what actually determines the outcome. A formally available right can be defeated by the arrangement through which someone must exercise it. The essays distinguish bounded record-keeping, useful digitization and legitimate charging from arrangements that make opacity, exclusion or dependence do the decisive work.

## Questions to carry through the reading

The hardest question is how the account establishes which claims are valid when traditions or institutions disagree. The essays offer distinctions and priority cues, not a universal ranking procedure. Their later applications also invite a test: does each example deepen the theory, or chiefly redescribe an objection already available through justice, rights or non-domination? And how far do the regulatory examples support a general moral conclusion? The project's [open questions](versions/rev0127/files/OPEN_QUESTIONS.json) keep these difficulties visible. Its legal and regulatory references are historical source claims, not an update on present law.
