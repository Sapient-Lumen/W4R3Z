# Addictive design, compulsion engineering, and no-attention-by-hook

## Core claim

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when public or private actors design digital services so that streaks, autoplay, endless scroll, rabbit-hole recommenders, rewards for engagement, read-receipt pressure, friction-heavy exit, or comparable compulsion loops do the real work of capturing attention, repeated return, disclosure, spending, attachment, or dependence rather than leaving use meaningfully user-directed, intelligible, interruptible, and refusal-safe.**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S456][S457][S458][S459]

## 1. The missing moral question is not only whether a choice was distorted once, but whether a person was being habit-installed

The archive already rejected nondeception failures, burdened-consent, no-agency-by-stealth, and no-relation-by-simulation. But it still lacked a direct rule for a different temptation: **building interfaces so that the practical engine of value extraction is not one lie or one isolated manipulation, but the installation of a return-loop that keeps drawing the person back, stretching time, deepening dependence, and making disengagement harder than the surface story suggests.**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S456][S457][S458][S459]

That gap matters because many systems do not need to compel overtly or even to deceive in a narrow verbal sense. They can use variable rewards, recommender spirals, social pressure cues, points, streaks, infinite scroll, autoplay, or delayed-friction exit so that the user's own repeated return becomes the visible source of the relationship while the stronger actor hides in the architecture that made stopping, pausing, or redirecting attention predictably harder.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S456][S457][S458][S459]

## 2. This is distinct from both ordinary attractiveness and the archive's manipulation surface

The archive needs this as its own canon surface because the wrong is not exhausted by the no-agency-by-stealth rule. That rule asks when informed and resistible choice is appreciably impaired so that distorted decision does the work. **No-attention-by-hook asks a narrower and more temporal question: when is the practical lever not only a distorted choice but an engineered loop of compulsion, habitual return, or time capture?**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S456][S457][S458][S459]

That distinction matters morally because a service may later point to metrics that look voluntary: the user came back, kept scrolling, maintained the streak, opened the notification, accepted the recommendation, kept spending, or stayed up late on the app. But a righteousness inquiry has to ask what authored that pattern in practice. If the system's practical force came mainly from hooks designed to make stopping costly or unlikely, then repeated use does not by itself vindicate the arrangement.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S456][S457][S458][S459]

## 3. Current governance materials already treat addictive design as a live safety and rights problem

This is no longer only a cultural complaint about screens or attention. The Commission's current Digital Services Act materials say online platforms accessible to minors must protect their mental and physical well-being, privacy, and security, and they state that the 2025 protection-of-minors guidelines address addictive design by disabling features such as read receipts, by giving minors more control over recommendations, and by promoting private-by-default accounts.[S456] The Commission's current DSA guidelines page likewise lists addictive design among the key risks those guidelines address.[S457]

Current enforcement posture sharpens the same point. In February 2026, the Commission opened formal proceedings against Shein under the DSA that include risks linked to addictive design, specifically mentioning points or rewards for engagement and stating that such features could negatively affect users' well-being and consumer protection online.[S458] Ofcom's 2025 child-safety measures similarly require a safety-first approach, safer feeds, stronger user controls, and protection against harmful recommendation pathways for children on regulated services in the UK.[S459]

These materials matter because they are not merely complaining that some interfaces are sticky. They identify a class of systems in which retention, engagement, or repeated return can itself become the method by which harm, disclosure, spend, attachment, or exposure is produced. The archive should learn from that: some systems are burdened not only because of the content they show, but because of the hooks through which they keep persons available to those contents, pressures, and incentives.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S456][S457][S458][S459]

## 4. Attention is not a trivial residue; it is a standing-bearing condition of agency

Righteousness should not treat attention, time, wakefulness, and return capacity as negligible. They are part of how persons pray, study, work, rest, parent, protest, heal, and deliberate. So a system that gains by exhausting those capacities or by converting them into a standing loop of engineered availability is not merely competing for leisure. It may be reorganising agency itself through time capture, especially where children, lonely users, people in crisis, or other dependency-burdened users are in view.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S456][S458][S459]

OECD materials help make this broader than one statute. They describe dark commercial patterns as digital practices that subvert consumers' decision-making through interface design, including practices that extract more money or personal data or spur increased attention, and they note disproportionate harms for groups such as children.[S459] The archive should therefore not ask only whether the design increased engagement. It should ask whether the engagement was being pursued in a way that made the person's finite attention and return habits into the usable substrate of governance or extraction.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S459]

## 5. Righteousness should distinguish bounded continuity support from compulsion engineering

The deeper point is not that every reminder, continuation cue, recommendation, or streak-like structure is unrighteous. Some continuity design genuinely supports medication adherence, exercise, study, language learning, community formation, accessibility, or crisis follow-up. The narrower question is what the stronger actor is allowed to do to keep a person returning. Where the design preserves user control, meaningful pause, clear stop signals, easy exit, bounded notifications, age-sensitive defaults, and no penalty for disengagement, continuity support may remain bounded. But where the service gains force by making interruption harder, by penalising stopping, by routing people into rabbit holes, by rewarding compulsive return, or by disguising extraction as care, encouragement, or ordinary social expectation, the actor is no longer mainly offering a useful practice. It is governing through no-attention-by-hook.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S456][S457][S458][S459]

## 6. Working rule

When an actor claims righteousness while relying on engagement-heavy digital design, ask seven questions:
1. **What loop was being installed or sustained?** Repeated return, prolonged session length, night-time checking, compulsive purchase, escalating disclosure, dependency on one counterpart, or another standing-bearing pattern of availability?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S456][S457][S458][S459]
2. **What features did the practical work?** Streaks, points or rewards, variable reward schedules, autoplay, infinite scroll, rabbit-hole recommenders, read receipts, social-pressure notifications, manipulative defaults, or friction-heavy exit?[S456][S458][S459]
3. **Who bore the special burden?** Children, users in crisis, the lonely, the indebted, the sleep-deprived, the cognitively overloaded, or others whose circumstances made interruption or resistance predictably harder?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S456][S459]
4. **What made stopping, pausing, or redirecting use genuinely live?** Easy exit, disabling cues, non-punitive disengagement, transparent controls, time limits, safer defaults, non-profiled feed options, or another route that left agency intact?[S456][S458][S459]
5. **What harm profile sat downstream of the hook?** Excessive use, harmful-content spirals, unsafe spending, privacy compromise, emotional dependence, self-harm exposure, sleep loss, or another serious predictable burden?[S456][S457][S458][S459]
6. **What narrower design route was available?** User-invoked reminders, bounded notifications, chronological or non-profiled feeds, finite-session design, non-gamified participation, or another way of supporting continuity without compulsion engineering?[S456][S457][S458][S459]
7. **Were hooks doing the real work?** Did the actor's practical advantage depend mainly on having made repeated return, prolonged attention, or difficult disengagement the route to value extraction or control?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S456][S457][S458][S459]

## Compression sentence

> **Do not let repeated return masquerade as freely offered standing: where digital systems get attention, disclosure, spending, attachment, or dependence mainly by installing hooks that make interruption, reflection, or exit predictably harder, righteousness fails unless the continuity support remains user-directed, bounded, age-sensitive, easy to stop, and meaningfully contestable.**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S456][S457][S458][S459]
