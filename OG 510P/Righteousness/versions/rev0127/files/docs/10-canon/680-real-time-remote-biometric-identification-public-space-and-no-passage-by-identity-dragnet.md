# Real-time remote biometric identification, public space, and no-passage-by-identity-dragnet

## Core claim

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when public or private actors make ordinary presence or passage through publicly accessible space depend on live machine-recognisability — especially through real-time remote biometric identification — so that a person may be located, matched, intercepted, excluded, or chilled in movement, assembly, or everyday presence without a narrowly bounded, specifically targeted, lawfully authorised, necessary, proportionate, and reviewable justification.**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S438][S446][S449][S452]

## 1. The missing moral question is not only who stores faces, but who must remain instantly machine-findable in order to move through ordinary life

The archive already rejected no-identity-by-extraction, no-rights-by-list, no-face-library-by-hoovering, and no-status-by-body-signal. But it still lacked a direct rule for a different temptation: **treating ordinary passage through public or quasi-public space as something authorities or powerful operators may condition on the standing possibility of instantly matching a person against a target set while the person is still moving through the world**.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S438][S446][S452]

That gap matters because the moral defect is not exhausted either by database manufacture upstream or by later one-off identification downstream. A public square, street, station, school entrance, mall concourse, stadium perimeter, or transit network becomes different in kind when mere presence can trigger live biometric searchability. The person is no longer just visible to others in the ordinary social sense. They are rendered **continuously recognisable for institutional action**.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S438][S449]

## 2. This is distinct from face-library manufacture, general surveillance, and retrospective identification

The archive needs this as its own canon surface because several nearby wrongs should not be collapsed. No-face-library-by-hoovering names the upstream wrong of building or expanding searchable face stock from ambient life. Privacy and surveillance name the broader discipline against disproportionate legibility. Biometrics and no-identity-by-extraction name the burden of making the body into an administrative key. **No-passage-by-identity-dragnet asks a narrower question: when does live remote biometric identification make movement, assembly, access, and everyday public presence themselves contingent on being continuously machine-locatable?**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S438][S449][S452]

That distinction matters morally because a system can be defended by saying there was no permanent arrest decision, no universal blacklist, no explicit command, or no huge scraped database built locally. But righteousness still has to ask what changed about public life. If the practical effect is that people move, assemble, travel, shop, study, worship, or protest under conditions where institutional power can match them in real time at distance and act on that match, then public passage itself has been burdened.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S438][S446][S449][S452]

## 3. Current governance materials already treat real-time remote biometric identification in public space as a red-line or near-red-line practice

This is no longer merely an abstract civil-liberties concern. The Commission's current AI Act materials state that real-time remote biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces for law enforcement purposes is prohibited, subject only to narrow exceptions.[S446] The same current materials explain that any exceptional use must be necessary and proportionate, targeted, limited, and subject to prior authorisation by a judicial or independent administrative authority whose decision is binding, with deletion duties if approval is denied.[S446] The AI Act Service Desk's current Article 5 materials go further into the structure: they treat real-time remote biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces as prohibited unless strictly necessary for tightly specified objectives such as searching for specific victims or missing persons, addressing a specific substantial and imminent threat, or localising or identifying a suspect in certain serious offences; they also require fundamental-rights impact assessment, registration, notification, temporal, geographic, and personal limitation, and they forbid taking an adverse legal decision based solely on the system's output.[S438]

That matters because the line being drawn is not simply about technical accuracy. The Commission's own public explanation warns that remote biometric identification in public space can significantly impact privacy, and that even apparently small error rates can become serious when applied to large populations such as train stations or crowds.[S446] Council of Europe facial-recognition guidance adds the broader human-rights lesson by insisting that these technologies be governed so they do not adversely affect dignity, human rights, fundamental freedoms, and data protection.[S449] The archive should therefore learn from the official materials: **public passage cannot lightly be converted into a biometric checkpoint condition**.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S438][S446][S449][S452]

## 4. The deeper problem is not only misidentification but the conversion of public space into conditional recognisability

Accuracy matters, but righteousness should not reduce the problem to false positives. A perfectly accurate live-biometric system could still be standing-defective if it changes the terms on which persons occupy public space. The question is whether the street, station, protest, plaza, or venue remains a place people may traverse without first becoming a live machine-search problem for stronger parties.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S446][S449]

That deeper problem helps explain why this surface belongs near movement, assembly, thought, and association rather than only near privacy or police procurement. Where people know that ordinary presence can trigger instant matching, some will avoid marches, meetings, clinics, worship, shelters, labor actions, or public controversy. Others will pass through but as persons already pre-disciplined by the possibility of being located, classified, or intercepted. The harm is therefore not just downstream arrest or error. It is the upstream reshaping of public life into a space of conditional passage under identity dragnet conditions.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S438][S446][S449][S452]

## 5. Righteousness should distinguish tightly bounded emergency identification from ordinary governance by live biometric searchability

The deeper point is not that every use of remote biometric identification is identical. Current law itself preserves narrow, highly conditioned exceptions for certain emergency or specifically targeted cases.[S438][S446] The archive should therefore not pretend the moral task is solved by a simple slogan. It should ask what exactly made the use targeted, what emergency or victim-search ground was real, what temporal and geographic limit contained it, what authority reviewed it beforehand, what independent record and deletion discipline existed, and what prevented the system from becoming normalized infrastructure for routine patrol, access control, crowd management, migration control, protest monitoring, or convenience policing.[S437][S438][S446][S449]

So righteousness should distinguish between a genuinely exceptional, sharply bounded, lawfully authorised search for a specifically identified victim or imminent threat and a standing arrangement in which public life is quietly reorganised around the institutional ability to know who is present now. The first may still face severe burden; the second is much closer to governance through no-passage-by-identity-dragnet.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S438][S446][S449][S452]

## 6. Working rule

When an actor claims righteousness while relying on live or near-live biometric identification in publicly accessible space, ask seven questions:
1. **What public or quasi-public space was being governed?** Street, square, transit system, school entrance, shopping area, venue perimeter, border approach, protest route, workplace campus, or another setting where ordinary presence and movement were at stake.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93]
2. **What biometric process was actually in play?** Real-time remote facial recognition, gait or voice matching, watchlist alerting, continuous camera-linked identification, or another system making persons machine-findable at distance while they were present.[S437][S438][S446][S449][S452]
3. **What specifically targeted ground justified the use?** Search for a named victim or missing person, a specific imminent threat, or a tightly bounded suspect-identification case — or was the real ground convenience, generalized patrol efficiency, crowd control, access management, or deterrence?[S438][S446]
4. **What made the use genuinely exceptional and bounded?** Prior judicial or independent administrative authorisation, fundamental-rights assessment, registration, notification, temporal and geographic limits, deletion discipline, narrow person-scope, and a prohibition on adverse legal decisions based solely on system output.[S438][S446]
5. **What rights-bearing practices were chilled or burdened?** Movement, assembly, association, worship, clinic access, schooling, shopping, commuting, labor organising, protest, or ordinary presence among others.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S449]
6. **What narrower route could have served the legitimate aim?** Non-biometric search, targeted warrant work, on-scene observation, person-specific evidence, post-event review under tighter controls, or another route that did not make ordinary public passage depend on live recognisability.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S438][S446][S449]
7. **Was public passage itself being turned into an identity dragnet condition?** Did the actor's practical advantage come mainly from having made presence in public space a standing opportunity for remote machine matching rather than from a sharply bounded and reviewable exception?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S438][S446][S449][S452]

## Compression sentence

> **Do not let ordinary public passage become conditionally machine-recognisable: where authorities or powerful operators rely on live or near-live remote biometric identification so that movement, assembly, access, or public presence is governed through continuous matchability rather than narrowly bounded, specifically targeted, necessary, proportionate, and reviewable exception, righteousness fails unless the burden remains truly exceptional, time-limited, independently authorised, deletion-disciplined, and not decisive on its own.**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S438][S446][S449][S452]
