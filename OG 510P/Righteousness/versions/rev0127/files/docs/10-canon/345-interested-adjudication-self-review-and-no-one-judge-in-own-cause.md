# Interested Adjudication, Self-Review, and No One Judging Their Own Cause

## Why this surface matters

The archive already had hearing, contestability, recusal, anti-capture, and scattered warnings about compromised standing. What it still lacked was a compact rule for **what happens when the accuser, beneficiary, implicated office, or materially interested superior also controls the verdict, recommended decision, or appeal**.[S32][S34][S68][S93][S114][S150][S151][S152]

That gap matters because two opposite mistakes are common:
- **inner-sincerity laundering** — treating a decision as righteous because the decision-maker says they can be fair, even though the structure fuses accusation, interest, and adjudication; and
- **recusal fetishism** — treating every prior contact, institutional connection, or earlier procedural role as automatically disqualifying, as though impartial adjudication were impossible unless the decision-maker were socially unconnected to the world.[S32][S34][S68][S93][S114][S150][S151][S152]

## Working-canon thesis

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when the accuser, beneficiary, implicated office, or materially interested superior also controls the verdict or the appeal without real recusal, structural separation, or independent review. The point is not that every decision-maker must be psychologically pure, but that no one should be asked to trust a forum whose design makes bias too likely, too hidden, or too self-protective. Internal review can still count, but only where it is insulated enough to test the first decision rather than merely ratify it.**[S32][S34][S68][S93][S114][S150][S151][S152]

## 1. The defect is structural, not just motivational

Fair-hearing materials are useful because they state the point at a portable level. Article 14 of the ICCPR and Article 6 of the European Convention both require a competent, independent, and impartial tribunal.[S93][S114] Rule-of-law materials help because they explain why this matters morally: hearing is not just an occasion to speak, but a way of subjecting power to reasons, evidence, and an institution not already committed to one side's vindication.[S34]

So the new surface does not wait for proof that the decision-maker confessed actual bias. That demand is often too strong and too late. The deeper question is whether the forum was set up so that a reasonable person could trust it to hear, test, and correct rather than to defend itself.[S34][S68][S150]

## 2. "No one should judge their own cause" is a portable cue

Due-process materials sharpen the point in a particularly usable form. Constitution Annotated materials summarize a line of cases holding that due process is offended not only by proved bias, but also by a serious or impermissible risk of bias, including situations where the same actor effectively appears as both accuser and adjudicator.[S150] Caperton is especially helpful because it states the test objectively: the question is whether the probability of actual bias is too high to be constitutionally tolerable, not whether subjective corruption has been admitted.[S150]

That matters for righteousness because self-protective structures are common outside courts too. A supervisor investigating their own retaliation claim, a board reviewing the benefits of its own secrecy, a school adjudicating the misconduct of a donor's child through a donor-dependent chain, or a ministry auditing the legality of its own off-record directives all exhibit the same moral pattern: **the forum is too entangled with the vindication of one side to carry clean standing without heavier safeguards**.[S32][S34][S150][S152]

## 3. Self-review is not automatically void, but it is presumptively burdened

This surface is not a fantasy of frictionless external purity. Institutions often need some internal review. Administrative-law materials are useful here because they do not ban all agency adjudication; instead, they demand separations that keep investigative or prosecutorial personnel from also participating in the decisional or review role in the same or factually related case.[S152] Public-office ethics materials likewise rely on recusal, disqualification, and impartiality management rather than pretending conflict never arises.[S32]

So the archive should not say that every institution must always import a stranger to decide everything. It should say something narrower: **once a forum is materially implicated, it acquires heavier burdens of separation, record preservation, disclosure, appeal, and sometimes outside review.** Internal review may still count when the first decision-maker is not grading their own paper, when the reviewers are not subordinate to the implicated actor for the matter at hand, and when the record is preserved well enough for contradiction to survive.[S32][S34][S150][S152]

## 4. Office faces stricter anti-self-judging burdens

Office is the clearest stress test because public institutions can hide self-judging behind ordinary hierarchy. Independence-of-the-judiciary materials warn against inappropriate interference and displacement of ordinary adjudicative process, while fair-trial materials repeatedly treat independence and impartiality as public guarantees rather than as private virtues.[S93][S114][S151] That makes the office-facing lesson sharp: **public power loses standing when it treats internal loyalty or chain-of-command deference as a substitute for an impartial forum.**[S32][S34][S93][S114][S151]

So righteous office should expect stronger use of disclosure, recusal, screened investigators, separated recommendation chains, preserved record, external appeal, and where necessary independent panels or judicial review. What it may not do is say, in effect, "trust us to decide whether our own conduct was proper because we already hold the office." That is precisely the kind of standing claim that office, at its best, is supposed to discipline rather than indulge.[S18][S32][S34][S35][S93][S114][S150][S152]

## 5. Working rule

When a righteousness claim depends on whether a forum, board, office, committee, or reviewer decided fairly, ask six questions:
1. **Who had a stake in the outcome?** Personal, financial, reputational, institutional, factional, or career stake?[S150][S152]
2. **Who accused, investigated, recommended, decided, and reviewed?** Were those roles fused, insulated, or only cosmetically separated?[S32][S114][S152]
3. **What appearance of bias would a reasonable outsider see?** The archive should not demand mind-reading where structure already makes distrust intelligible.[S68][S150]
4. **What recusal or separation safeguards existed?** Disclosure, screened investigators, disqualification, outside reviewer, separate appeal, or none?[S32][S150][S152]
5. **What record was preserved for later contradiction?** Self-review gets more dangerous when evidence, reasons, or comparator cases are thin or inaccessible.[S34][S150][S152]
6. **What remedy follows if the forum was too interested?** Rehearing, vacatur, reconstitution of the panel, outside review, restored process, or structural reform may be due because the original verdict lacked clean standing.[S93][S114][S150]

## Compression sentence

> **Do not let sincerity, status, or ordinary hierarchy do the work of righteous judgment when the same actor or implicated institution is effectively sitting in judgment on its own cause; once accusation, interest, and adjudication collapse together, righteousness needs recusal, separation, or independent review rather than self-trust.**[S32][S34][S68][S93][S114][S150][S151][S152]
