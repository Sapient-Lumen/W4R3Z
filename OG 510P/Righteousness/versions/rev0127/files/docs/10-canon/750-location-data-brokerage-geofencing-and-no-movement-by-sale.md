# Location-data brokerage, geofencing, and no-movement-by-sale

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when a person’s movements, visits, routines, or presence at sensitive places are turned into saleable, transferable, or quietly reusable location exhaust so that buying and combining movement data does the real work of targeting, exposure, profiling, or exclusion rather than leaving movement legible only under bounded, necessity-tied, and contestable grounds.**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S511][S512][S513][S514]

## 1. The missing moral question is not only whether location was collected, but whether movement was converted into a marketable reservoir

The archive already rejected disproportionate legibility, no-face-library-by-hoovering, no-status-by-body-signal, no-standing-by-score, and no-child-safety-by-compulsory-identification. But it still lacked a direct rule for a different defect: **apps, SDKs, ad-tech systems, and brokers collecting precise location traces and then selling, licensing, sharing, or segmenting those traces so that other actors can buy lists of who visited clinics, shelters, churches, protests, schools, military sites, labour offices, or other sensitive places.**[S511][S512][S513][S514]

That gap matters because the moral wrong is not exhausted by generic privacy language. A person can be made newly governable when ordinary movement becomes commercially available. Once precise visits and routines are bought and sold, third parties can identify likely patients, worshippers, protesters, servicemembers, union participants, migrants, or people seeking refuge; they can infer habits and home locations; and they can target, exclude, stigmatize, or surveil without ever having had an accountable relationship with the person whose movements they now hold.[S511][S512][S513][S514]

So the archive now needs a canon surface that is not reducible either to privacy in general or to biometric identification in particular. No-movement-by-sale asks when movement data stops being a bounded operational input and starts becoming a transferable reservoir through which others can buy practical access to a person’s life.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S511][S512][S513][S514]

## 2. Current official materials now name this family clearly enough to justify canon admission

Current FTC materials are unusually revealing because they repeatedly describe precise location sales as exposing visits to sensitive locations and as creating downstream risks far beyond ordinary advertising. The FTC’s final Mobilewalla order bans the company from selling sensitive location data and says the company sold data about visits to healthcare facilities and places of worship; it also bars use, transfer, sale, or disclosure of location data from sensitive locations such as health clinics, religious organizations, correctional facilities, labour union offices, LGBTQ+-related locations, political gatherings, and military installations.[S511] The FTC’s Gravy Analytics and Venntel action says the companies unlawfully tracked and sold sensitive location data, used geofencing to identify and sell lists of consumers who attended events related to medical conditions and places of worship, and exposed consumers to risks including disclosure of health decisions, political activity, and religious practices.[S512]

The FTC’s X-Mode / Outlogic action sharpens the same point from another direction. It says the company sold precise location data that could be used to track visits to medical and reproductive health clinics, places of religious worship, and domestic-violence shelters, and says geolocation data can reveal where a person lives, with whom they spend time, what medical treatments they seek, and where they worship.[S513] California’s Attorney General now describes location data as capable of tracking people’s precise movements without them realizing it, identifying sensitive visits and home location, and becoming especially salient given threats around immigration and reproductive and gender-affirming healthcare.[S514]

Together these materials show that location-data brokerage is not a speculative concern but a live governance seam around movement, exposure, and the sale of practical knowledge about persons.[S511][S512][S513][S514]

## 3. The righteousness issue is not location use as such; it is letting bought movement traces do the real work of governing persons

Righteousness should not treat every use of location as automatically unrighteous. Emergency response, navigation, some fraud controls, device-finding, or a genuinely local operational task may require bounded place information. The narrower defect appears where a system creates or taps a market in precise movement traces and lets saleability, transferability, or quiet downstream reuse do the real work.[S511][S512][S513][S514]

That defect is clearest where an app gathers more location than the service truly needs; where SDK or ad-exchange collection outruns the user-facing task; where location tied to mobile advertising identifiers or similar device signals can be matched back to actual people; where geofences are used to create audience segments for clinics, protests, religious services, schools, shelters, or political events; where a company sells “sensitive” location-derived characteristics as if they were ordinary market inputs; or where consumers are expected to bear the risk because some disclosure was buried in a privacy notice they never realistically understood.[S511][S512][S513][S514]

In those cases the wrong is not simply that movement was observable. It is that ordinary passage through the world has been turned into no-movement-by-sale. The system has made a person’s route, visit, or routine market-readable first and answerable only later, if at all.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S511][S512][S513][S514]

## 4. Questions righteous judgment should now ask

When an actor claims righteousness while collecting, sharing, or buying location data, ask seven questions:

1. **What exact task required location at all?** Navigation, emergency assistance, anti-fraud, device-finding, operational logistics, contextual notice, advertising, analytics, or something else?[S511][S512][S513][S514]
2. **What precision and duration were actually needed?** Continuous tracking, precise coordinates, repeated background collection, one-time city-level location, or only an ephemeral local check?[S511][S512][S513][S514]
3. **Was the data sold, licensed, transferred, or otherwise made available beyond the immediate service relation?** Did the system create a marketable trail rather than a bounded service function?[S511][S512][S513][S514]
4. **Could the resulting trail reveal sensitive visits or traits?** Health care, religion, politics, union activity, shelter use, school attendance, military presence, protest participation, reproductive or gender-affirming care, immigration vulnerability, or home location?[S511][S512][S513][S514]
5. **What identifiers or joins made reidentification practical?** Mobile advertising IDs, device graphs, home/work inference, customer matching, audience segmentation, or downstream enrichment?[S512][S513][S514]
6. **What real control or correction path existed?** Was consent informed and revocable, could collection be limited or deleted, could sale be stopped, and could affected people discover where their movement data had gone?[S511][S512][S513][S514]
7. **Was the system really serving the user, or monetizing the user’s path through the world?** If the sale, segment, or resale layer disappeared, would the service still need this data in this form?[S511][S512][S513][S514]

## 5. Canonical compression

> **Do not let ordinary movement become a saleable title over the person.** Bounded location use may sometimes be justified, but righteousness is heavily burdened when precise visits, routines, or presence at sensitive locations are turned into commercially transferable traces so that bought movement data, geofenced audience lists, or location-derived traits do the real work of exposure, targeting, profiling, or exclusion.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S511][S512][S513][S514]
