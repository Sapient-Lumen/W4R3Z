# Freedom of Association, Unions, and No-Association-by-Sufferance

## Why this surface matters

The archive already rejects guilt by mere association, compelled creed, forced labour, reprisal, protest suppression, and silencing by power. What it still lacked was a compact rule for **what happens when power treats durable collective organization itself as something to be licensed, domesticated, or selectively strangled rather than as a claim-bearing practice people may use to pursue common interests together**: forced registration designed to block disfavored groups, anti-union firing, employer-dominated worker organizations, selective deregistration, frozen bank accounts, factional capture of associations, criminalization of civic groups, or administrative systems that let only the compliant organize effectively.[S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S93][S218][S222][S223]

That missing rule matters because article 22 does not describe association as a ruler-granted indulgence. It recognizes the right to freedom of association with others, including the right to form and join trade unions for the protection of one's interests, and permits restrictions only when they are prescribed by law and necessary in a democratic society for the specified protected interests.[S93] OHCHR's current assembly-and-association guidance sharpens the point by defining association as the right to interact and organize collectively in order to express, promote, pursue, and defend common interests, explicitly including trade unions, and by tying an enabling legal framework to monitoring against violence, intimidation, persecution, and reprisals directed at those exercising the right.[S218] ILO Convention No. 87 adds the decisive anti-sufferance move: workers and employers, without distinction, have the right to establish and join organizations of their own choosing without previous authorization, to draw up their own rules, elect representatives, organize activities, and formulate programmes, while public authorities must refrain from interference that restricts these rights or impedes lawful exercise; Convention No. 98 adds protection against anti-union discrimination, protection against employer or employer-organization interference, and promotion of voluntary collective bargaining.[S222][S223] So the archive should now treat association as a distinct standing surface: **people may be regulated at the margins, but they may not be left to organize only on sufferance, only through obedient bodies, or only at the cost of retaliation, capture, or civic disablement**.[S93][S218][S222][S223]

## Working-canon thesis

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when a person, office, or institution makes collective organization contingent on prior indulgence, ideological loyalty, employer domination, or retaliatory risk. Freedom of association includes the freedom to create, join, sustain, and act through organizations and trade unions pursuing common interests; regulation remains possible only under steep burdens of legality, necessity, proportionality, and independence-preservation. Systems that permit organization only for the friendly, the harmless, the apolitical, or the easily captured are not protecting civic order. They are governing by association-through-sufferance.**[S93][S218][S222][S223]

## 1. Association is more than speech plus gathering; it is organized common life

The archive already knew that persons can be wronged by censorship and by suppression of assembly. Association materials sharpen a further point: people are also wronged when they cannot build durable bodies through which claims, loyalties, bargaining, mutual aid, professional standards, worship, advocacy, and minority survival are carried across time. Article 22 protects freedom of association with others, including trade unions.[S93] OHCHR's current topic page is useful because it makes the material scope explicit: association involves interacting and organizing collectively to express, promote, pursue, and defend common interests.[S218]

That matters because power often permits atomized speech and occasional gathering while still disabling the organized forms that make resistance durable. A government may allow individuals to complain but criminalize the group; an employer may tolerate grumbling but break the union; a university may allow events but not independent student organization; a prison may allow petitions but punish collective representation; a platform may allow posts while disabling civil-society coordination. The archive should therefore now ask a sharper question whenever a collective body is burdened: **was the relevant claim merely about one message or one gathering, or about the ongoing ability to organize together at all?** Where durable organization is what is really being cut, righteousness is already under heavier strain.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S93][S218]

## 2. Prior authorization and selective registration are standing burdens, not neutral paperwork

Convention No. 87 matters because it rejects the idea that organizations come into moral existence only after prior permission. Workers and employers have the right to establish and join organizations of their own choosing without previous authorization.[S222] Article 22 of the ICCPR permits restrictions only under the familiar steep limits of legality and democratic necessity.[S93]

That helps the archive resist a familiar laundering move. Institutions often redescribe association control as mere administration: registration, reporting, beneficial-ownership disclosure, foreign-funding approval, campus recognition, strike notice, or licensing compliance. Some of that can be justified. But once registration systems become selective gates through which only aligned, tame, well-connected, or ideologically acceptable bodies can survive, the defect is not cured by the fact that a form exists. The portable point is narrower: **ordinary formalities may help public order, but the right to associate may not depend on discretionary indulgence, impossible compliance, or selective permission disguised as neutral administration**.[S19][S32][S34][S35][S93][S218][S222]

## 3. Associations must be independent of state and employer capture

Convention No. 87 is morally revealing because it protects not only the existence of organizations but also their independence: organizations have the right to draw up their constitutions and rules, elect representatives, organize administration and activities, and formulate programmes, while public authorities must refrain from interference that restricts these rights or impedes lawful exercise.[S222] Convention No. 98 sharpens the same point for workplace power by requiring protection against acts of interference between workers' and employers' organizations, especially measures aimed at placing workers' organizations under employer domination or support so as to control them.[S223]

That matters because capture is one of the easiest ways to destroy association while pretending to allow it. A state can require umbrella affiliation, install trustees, control leadership selection, dictate agendas, criminalize independent funding, or reward compliant associations until the only functioning bodies are quasi-official. Employers can sponsor company unions, threaten independent organizers, or use wages, scheduling, visas, or contractor status to make the officially available organization path practically useless. The archive should therefore now reject a shallow freedom test that asks only whether some organization exists on paper. The morally sharper question is whether the association is free enough to speak, bargain, organize, and criticize without being structurally ventriloquized by the stronger party.[S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S93][S222][S223]

## 4. Anti-union retaliation and discrimination are not side issues; they are part of whether association is real

Convention No. 98 matters because it names a familiar practical defect directly: workers must enjoy adequate protection against anti-union discrimination in employment, including conditions that employment be given only if union membership is refused or relinquished, and including dismissal or prejudice because of union membership or participation in union activities.[S223] It also calls for measures appropriate to national conditions to encourage and promote voluntary collective bargaining.[S223]

This matters because institutions often say association remains free even though the costs of using it are loaded onto the vulnerable. A worker may be free to join a union in theory but lose shifts, promotion, immigration sponsorship, or future employability in practice. A tenant association may be legal while organizers are evicted. A student group may be recognized while leaders face selective discipline. A migrant or prisoner representative may be nominally allowed while transfer, segregation, or permit threat makes the role suicidal. The archive should therefore now treat retaliation against organizing as part of the associational wrong itself rather than as a separate afterthought. Where the price of association is predictable livelihood loss, status loss, or targeted discipline, power may be allowing association in name while defeating it in practice.[S34][S35][S41][S104][S105][S106][S107][S218][S223]

## 5. Some restrictions may remain lawful, but they stay under a steep burden

Article 22 itself allows certain restrictions, and it specifically notes that lawful restrictions may be imposed on members of the armed forces and of the police in their exercise of the right.[S93] That means the archive should not treat every rule about association, funding, strikes, organizational form, conflict-of-interest, or essential-service continuity as self-evidently unrighteous. Some restrictions may protect others' rights, preserve institutional impartiality, or guard against violence or corruption.

But article 22 is useful precisely because it keeps the burden steep: legality is not enough, and necessity in a democratic society remains the touchstone.[S93] Convention No. 87 is equally useful because it requires public authorities to refrain from interference and obliges states to take all necessary and appropriate measures to ensure workers and employers may exercise the right freely.[S222] So the archive should now ask a sharper question whenever association is burdened: **what concrete protected interest required this burden, what narrower route was tried first, what independence was preserved, and who predictably lost the practical ability to organize because of the way the rule was designed or enforced?** Where emergency rhetoric, anti-extremism language, managerial order, or depoliticization talk is doing the real work, righteousness is usually not safeguarding plural order but deciding which organized life may count.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S93][S218][S222][S223]

## 6. Working rule

When a union, civic group, religious body, student organization, tenant association, party-adjacent group, advocacy network, professional body, or other association is burdened under pressure, ask six questions:
1. **What concrete protected interest justified the burden?** Was it a real rights-protective concern, or mainly discomfort with collective bargaining, criticism, minority solidarity, or organized dissent?[S93][S218][S222][S223]
2. **Was organization conditioned on permission or impossible formality?** Did the system require prior authorization, discriminatory registration, selective recognition, or compliance that only the favored could realistically satisfy?[S93][S218][S222]
3. **What happened to independence?** Could the body choose leaders, rules, activities, and programmes without state or employer domination, forced umbrellaing, or covert capture?[S222][S223]
4. **Who bore retaliatory risk?** Did organizers, members, shop stewards, student leaders, migrant representatives, or minority associations face firing, transfer, exclusion, violence, or legal threat for using the right?[S34][S35][S41][S104][S105][S106][S107][S218][S223]
5. **What organizing capacity remained in practice?** Could the association meet, recruit, fund itself lawfully, bargain, communicate, and seek remedy, or did nominal legality mask practical unusability?[S34][S35][S41][S93][S218][S222][S223]
6. **What review and repair stayed live afterward?** Did challenge, reinstatement, restoration of registration, anti-retaliation remedy, compensation, or reopening remain realistically available?[S26][S27][S34][S35][S93][S223]

## Compact maxim

> **Do not make organized life depend on indulgence.** Righteous regulation may burden associations narrowly for real protective reasons, but righteousness is heavily burdened when people may organize, unionize, or sustain common life only on sufferance, only through captured bodies, or only at punishing personal cost.[S93][S218][S222][S223]
