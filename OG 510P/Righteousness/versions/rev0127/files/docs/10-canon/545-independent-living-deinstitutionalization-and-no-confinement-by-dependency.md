# Independent Living, Deinstitutionalization, and No-Confinement-by-Dependency

## Why this surface matters

The archive already rejects benevolent mastery, inaccessible compliance, punitive noncare, homelessness by clearance, civil death by guardianship, no-body-by-compulsion, and no-rights-by-outsourcing. What it still lacked was a compact rule for **what happens when disabled people are made to live under institutional, segregated, or dependency-structured arrangements that treat support as title over residence, schedule, association, intimacy, privacy, or exit**.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S66][S67][S93][S145][S146][S147][S164][S232][S233][S256][S257][S259][S330][S331][S332][S333][S334][S335]

That gap matters because dependency is one of the cleanest laundering surfaces in modern care and welfare governance. A person is said to be "placed," "looked after," "protected," "not ready," "too complex," or "best served" in a setting where other people control the door, the timetable, the staff, the visitors, the spending, the risk rules, or the conditions of leaving. The building may be called residential support, congregate care, supported accommodation, a psychiatric or social-care facility, a group home, a nursing home, a treatment placement, or another service name. But if support is being organized so that the person must live under ongoing segregation, imposed routines, and practically thinned choice mainly because community life was not made real, righteousness is not preserved by calling the arrangement care.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S66][S67][S93][S145][S146][S147][S164][S232][S233][S256][S257][S259][S330][S331][S332][S333][S334][S335]

## Working-canon thesis

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when disability, ageing, psychosocial difference, intellectual disability, support need, or crisis is governed through institutionalization, segregation, or dependency-structured support that makes people live where they did not really choose, with whom they did not really choose, and under conditions they cannot realistically revise or exit. Congregate or high-support settings are not automatically unrighteous. But once support is doing the practical work of reducing residence choice, community inclusion, privacy, association, mobility, legal agency, or everyday self-direction because ordinary community life was not made available, righteousness fails unless unusually strong necessity, time-boundedness, review, will-and-preferences discipline, and a real path to community living remain live.**[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S66][S67][S93][S145][S146][S147][S164][S232][S233][S256][S257][S259][S330][S331][S332][S333][S334][S335]

## 1. Article 19 makes community living an equal-standing right, not a service luxury

The treaty text matters because it is direct rather than merely aspirational. Article 19 of the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities recognizes the equal right of persons with disabilities to live in the community, with choices equal to others, and requires States to ensure that people can choose where and with whom they live, are not obliged to live in a particular living arrangement, have access to a range of in-home and other community support services including personal assistance, and can use community services and facilities on an equal basis with others.[S67] General Comment No. 5 sharpens the moral meaning of that commitment by treating article 19 as rooted in dignity, autonomy, independence, participation, and inclusion, and by stressing that persons with disabilities are subjects and holders of rights rather than objects of care.[S330]

That is a decisive clarification for this archive. It means community living is not a bonus once care has been delivered. It is part of what the due already is. If a system supplies food, medicine, supervision, or nominal shelter while still deciding that the person must live under imposed arrangements because no rights-bearing community alternative was built, the archive should not describe the case as support first and freedom second. The due already included both.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S47][S49][S66][S67][S93][S164][S232][S233][S330]

## 2. Institutionalization is not neutral care

The deinstitutionalization guidelines matter because they name the structural wrong without romanticizing it away. They say the guidelines are meant to support realization of the right to live independently and be included in the community, to plan deinstitutionalization, and to prevent institutionalization; they also say the pandemic exposed widespread institutionalization together with violence, neglect, abuse, ill-treatment, torture, and restraint in institutions.[S331] General Comment No. 5 is morally revealing for the same reason: independent living and inclusion in the community are not simply about a building type but about not losing personal choice and autonomy because particular living arrangements are imposed.[S330]

That helps the archive name a recurring laundering move. Institutions are often defended by pointing to their caregiving function, staff effort, or the fact that some residents have complex needs. None of those facts settles the question. The sharper question is whether support is being arranged in a way that preserves equal-standing life in the community, or whether dependency has become the justification for living under other people's standing. Once doors, schedules, relationships, spending, movement, sexuality, privacy, or residence are governed mainly by the service because the person needs support, care has become a vehicle of confinement by dependency.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S66][S67][S93][S164][S232][S233][S256][S257][S330][S331]

## 3. Support systems have to preserve inclusion rather than produce managed dependency

Current OHCHR disability materials are useful here because they no longer treat support as a narrow welfare add-on. The 2023 OHCHR report on support systems examines the elements needed to ensure community inclusion of persons with disabilities.[S332] The 2023 report on good practices identifies key policy areas enabling persons with disabilities to live independently in their communities.[S333] The 2025 care-and-support report broadens the point by analyzing the human-rights standards relevant to care and support systems themselves.[S335]

That matters because the archive needed a way to distinguish **support that carries standing** from support that reorganizes life around managed dependency. A personal-assistance system, accessible housing pipeline, income support design, communication support, peer support network, or family support package may genuinely enlarge choice. But a residential setting, provider-controlled budget, or protective arrangement may instead decide that the person can receive support only by surrendering residence choice, ordinary privacy, intimate life, child-rearing credibility, community presence, or credible exit. Righteousness is not measured by whether some service existed. It is measured by whether support preserved life as the person's own.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S66][S67][S93][S145][S146][S147][S164][S232][S233][S256][S257][S259][S332][S333][S335]

## 4. Transformation and deinstitutionalization are active duties, not optional reform aesthetics

The 2023 report on transformation of services is useful because it says traditional service and support models often perpetuate dependency and lack of agency by focusing on impairments and treating persons with disabilities as passive recipients of care, which is at odds with the Convention's grounding in personhood, autonomy, and community inclusion.[S334] The report argues that a new philosophy of service and support is emerging and needs clearer articulation in law and policy.[S334] The 2022 deinstitutionalization guidelines make the planning duty explicit by treating deinstitutionalization and prevention of institutionalization as state tasks, not as charitable modernization projects.[S331]

This is morally clarifying for the archive because it blocks another innocence story: that institutions remain acceptable simply because better community systems would take time or money. Of course transformation does take time and resources. But righteousness is not preserved when underinvestment, inaccessible housing, lack of personal assistance, thin respite for families, or fragmented support budgets are treated as reasons why segregated arrangements must remain ordinary. Those are better read as signs that the institution was being used to manage a support deficit the stronger party chose not to repair.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S43][S45][S47][S49][S66][S67][S93][S164][S232][S233][S330][S331][S332][S333][S334][S335]

## 5. This is not a ban on all shared or high-support living

The archive needed a narrow rule here. Not every shared residence, crisis setting, high-support arrangement, or temporary placement defeats righteousness. Some people positively choose shared living. Some emergency interventions are genuinely necessary. Some support needs are intensive. Article 19 itself contemplates a range of in-home, residential, and other community support services, while the larger disability framework ties support to voluntariness, inclusion, proximity to the person's own community, and equal basis with others rather than to one rigid service form.[S67][S330][S332]

So the archive does **not** say that every staffed setting or every family-managed arrangement is automatically unrighteous. It says the justificatory burden becomes steep once disability or support need is doing the practical work of making imposed living arrangement, provider control, or segregated life seem normal. The relevant question is not whether care was present in the abstract. It is whether community inclusion, self-direction, privacy, and meaningful exit remained real enough that support was serving standing rather than replacing it.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S66][S67][S93][S145][S146][S147][S164][S232][S233][S256][S257][S259][S330][S331][S332][S333][S334][S335]

## 6. Working rule

When an actor claims righteousness while disability support, elder support, psychosocial support, or other dependency-shaped care is at issue, ask seven questions:
1. **What standing-bearing thing was actually at stake?** Residence choice, privacy, association, bodily autonomy, parenting, communication, work, education, mobility, safety, or ordinary community presence?[S67][S330][S331][S332][S333][S334][S335]
2. **What arrangement did the real burden work?** Group home, care institution, psychiatric facility, nursing home, supported accommodation, family overcontrol, provider-managed budget, guardianship-linked placement, or crisis detention-like setting?[S330][S331][S334]
3. **Who chose, and how revisably?** Was the person's own will and preference effective about where to live, with whom, on what terms, and with what exit path?[S67][S256][S257][S330]
4. **What support would have made community living real?** Personal assistance, accessible housing, income support, communication access, respite, peer support, assistive technology, transport, or other community infrastructure may be the true missing condition.[S67][S145][S146][S147][S332][S333][S334][S335]
5. **What segregation or control remained?** Who controlled doors, schedules, visitors, spending, medication, sexuality, work, schooling, complaints, or movement?[S330][S331][S334]
6. **What review and remedy remained live?** Could the person contest the placement, change supporters, complain safely, access justice, and actually leave or transition?[S259][S330][S331]
7. **Was dependency doing the moral laundering?** If the stronger party can say "care was provided" while the burdened person still had to live under imposed arrangements because community alternatives were not built, the righteousness claim is presumptively corrupted.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S66][S67][S93][S164][S232][S233][S256][S257][S259][S330][S331][S332][S333][S334][S335]

## Compression sentence

> **Do not let support become title over a life: where disability or dependency is at stake, righteousness requires community-realizing support, live exit, and preserved self-direction rather than care that secures itself by confining the person through dependency.**[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S66][S67][S93][S145][S146][S147][S164][S232][S233][S256][S257][S259][S330][S331][S332][S333][S334][S335]
