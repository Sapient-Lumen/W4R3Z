# Algorithmic Governance, Automated Decision-Making, and No-Rightlessness-by-Model

## Why this surface matters

The archive already rejects privacy overreach, self-review, inaccessible compliance, ranked standing, rightlessness-by-paper, destitution by sanction, schooling by exclusion, and livelihood by domination. What it still lacked was a compact rule for **when those burdens are routed through automated systems themselves** — fraud scores, risk flags, recommender outputs, biometric gates, predictive models, triage engines, ranking tools, and AI-generated summaries that come to function as if they were reasons rather than merely tools.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S43][S44][S45][S46][S66][S67][S93][S153][S154][S232][S233][S251][S252][S253][S254][S255]

That gap matters because unrighteousness in automated governance is often laundered through technical language. A system may be described as neutral, efficient, data-driven, or merely advisory while it is actually deciding who gets stopped, searched, doubted, delayed, denied, surveilled, excluded, priced, or left without effective review. Once the score, flag, or model output does the practical work of judgment while responsibility diffuses into vendors, procurement chains, dashboards, and "the system said so" rhetoric, ordinary standing can be thinned without anyone openly owning the burden.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S43][S44][S45][S46][S66][S67][S93][S153][S154][S232][S233][S251][S252][S253][S254][S255]

## Working-canon thesis

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when a person, office, or institution substantially allocates liberty, livelihood, welfare, schooling, housing, movement, safety, or public voice through automated systems whose operative role is opaque, unchallengeable, proxy-discriminatory, surveillance-hungry, or stripped of meaningful human review, mainly in order to cheapen judgment, scale control, dissolve responsibility, or turn standing into a function of datafied prediction. Artificial-intelligence and automated systems may assist bounded decision-making, but only where human-rights impact assessment, transparency proportionate to the burden, non-discrimination, privacy discipline, meaningful human oversight, contestability, and effective remedy remain live — and where a human bearer still owns the reasons, the override power, and the repair burden.**[S46][S93][S153][S154][S232][S233][S251][S252][S253][S254][S255]

## 1. Assistance is not authority

OHCHR's AI materials are useful because they treat human-rights standards as applying to the design, development, and use of artificial intelligence, and they explicitly ask both what duties States bear and what responsibilities private companies bear in making AI consistent with international human rights law.[S251] The 2025 Working Group report sharpens that point by clarifying that the Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights apply not only to AI developers but also to the procurement and deployment of AI systems by States and by businesses outside the technology sector, while also foregrounding access to remedy.[S255] So the archive now rejects a familiar laundering move: because a system was bought, inherited, or advised by a vendor, the deploying office acts as though the real moral judgment happened elsewhere.

That does not mean every model-assisted workflow is already unrighteous. Offices often need screening, routing, matching, translation, triage support, and error-detection tools. But once the office cannot say what role the system actually played, who could interrupt it, who remained accountable for its effects, and what remedy path remained when it misfired, the system is no longer a bounded aid inside righteous judgment. It is starting to become a substitute sovereign.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S43][S44][S45][S46][S93][S251][S255]

## 2. Some AI uses are not saved merely by optimization or convenience

The 2021 OHCHR report on privacy in the digital age matters because it does not treat all AI deployment as presumptively regulable at the margins. Its summary stresses the urgent need for a moratorium on the sale and use of AI systems that pose a serious risk to human rights until adequate safeguards are in place, and it calls for AI applications that cannot be used in compliance with international human rights law to be banned.[S252] That is a morally important threshold rule. It means righteousness is not exhausted by marginal accuracy gains, cleaner dashboards, or better documentation of the same underlying wrong.

So the archive now rejects a second laundering move: because the tool is fast, scalable, or more statistically polished than the prior human process, it is treated as presumptively righteous. Some systems may indeed improve fairness or consistency. But if a tool's ordinary operation still depends on mass surveillance, hidden scoring, coercive biometric dependence, nonconsensual data capture, discrimination by proxy, or burdens so opaque that affected persons cannot realistically contest them, then the archive should now presume not simple under-regulation but possible inadmissibility.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S66][S67][S93][S153][S154][S232][S233][S252]

## 3. Transparency, human oversight, and contestability are constitutive rather than decorative

The Council of Europe's Framework Convention on AI is useful because it treats the whole lifecycle of AI systems as needing consistency with human rights, democracy, and the rule of law, and it requires risk and impact assessment plus prevention and mitigation measures responsive to context and severity.[S253] UNESCO's Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence adds that human rights and human dignity are the cornerstone of AI governance, with transparency, fairness, and human oversight named as central principles rather than optional extras.[S254] Together these sources support a stricter archive rule: automated governance is not righteous merely because a human was nominally somewhere in the loop.

The real question is whether affected persons remain inside a humanly answerable order. Were they told a system was materially involved? Could someone with authority actually revisit the result? Was enough explanation available to challenge the relevant features, data, or burden? Could review happen before the automated burden hardened into lost income, lost schooling, eviction, detention, deportation, or reputational ruin? If not, then the appearance of oversight is often just another version of self-certifying power.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S43][S44][S45][S46][S93][S153][S154][S251][S253][S254][S255]

## 4. Data hunger and proxy discrimination are standing problems, not just technical bugs

This surface is not only about explanation. It is also about what kinds of data demands and proxy logic a system normalizes. Existing privacy and equality materials already gave the archive reasons to treat unnecessary exposure, status sorting, and effect-based discrimination as morally serious.[S153][S154][S232][S233] AI governance sharpens that lesson because automated systems often learn or reproduce patterns from records saturated with prior exclusion, over-policing, under-service, inaccessible design, and unequal error-correction.[S251][S252][S254][S255]

So the archive should now reject a third laundering move: because a system never names race, disability, migration history, poverty, religion, or other protected status directly, it is treated as neutral. In practice, address, document history, network pattern, prior police contact, caregiving interruption, accent, device stability, payment irregularity, neighborhood, school history, and administrative mismatch may all keep doing status work without saying so openly. When that happens, the system is not merely finding risk. It is helping produce ranked standing through proxies at scale.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S66][S67][S93][S145][S146][S147][S153][S154][S232][S233][S251][S252][S254][S255]

## 5. Working rule

When righteousness is claimed in an AI, algorithmic, or automated-decision case, ask seven questions:
1. **What human claim was actually being burdened?** Liberty, movement, livelihood, welfare, schooling, housing, safety, expression, association, documentation, or access to remedy?[S34][S35][S41][S66][S67][S93][S153][S154][S232][S233][S251][S252][S253][S254][S255]
2. **What did the system actually do?** Recommend, rank, flag, verify, deny, prioritize, profile, generate evidence, or effectively decide?[S43][S44][S45][S46][S251][S253][S255]
3. **What made the role of the system contestable before decisive harm landed?** Notice, explanation, data correction, meaningful human review, override authority, and a real complaint path all matter.[S34][S35][S41][S93][S153][S154][S251][S253][S254][S255]
4. **What data, proxies, or surveillance practices did the system depend on?** Identity systems, biometrics, historical policing data, credit proxies, behavioral monitoring, location trails, or documentation instability may each be doing hidden moral work.[S46][S153][S154][S251][S252][S254]
5. **Whose convenience was the automation really serving?** Budget savings, speed, caseload clearing, vendor lock-in, anti-fraud theater, border hardening, school sorting, labour control, or politically safer deniability should all raise standing alarm.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S43][S44][S45][S46][S251][S255]
6. **Who still owned the reasons, override power, and repair duty?** Procurement chains and vendor contracts do not dissolve public or institutional answerability.[S19][S32][S43][S44][S45][S46][S251][S253][S255]
7. **What redesign would remove the standing defect?** Sometimes the answer is narrower data use, stronger review, less automation, or stronger remedy; sometimes it is a moratorium, ban, or refusal to deploy at all.[S46][S153][S154][S251][S252][S253][S254][S255]

## 6. Compression

> **Do not let a model, score, flag, or automated system decide whose standing counts without answerable reasons, meaningful review, non-discrimination, privacy discipline, and real remedy.** Righteousness may permit bounded technical assistance, but it is burdened when automation becomes the device by which people are ruled through opaque prediction, proxy sorting, scalable suspicion, or vendor-laundered authority.[S46][S93][S153][S154][S232][S233][S251][S252][S253][S254][S255]
