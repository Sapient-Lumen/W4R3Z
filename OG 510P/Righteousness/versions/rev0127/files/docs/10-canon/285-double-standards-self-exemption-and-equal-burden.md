# Double Standards, Self-Exemption, and Equal Burden

## Why this surface matters

The archive already treats **hypocritical self-exemption** as a defeater, but only in scattered references. What it still lacked was a dedicated surface explaining when a righteousness claim fails because one norm is enforced downward, outward, or against rivals while another, quieter norm is reserved for self, allies, donors, favorites, or insiders.[S19][S30][S32][S34][S51][S95]

That gap matters because two opposite mistakes are common:
- **flattened symmetry** — treating every asymmetry of burden as hypocrisy, as though role, vulnerability, or jurisdiction never mattered; and
- **special-pleading sovereignty** — treating rank, side, expertise, success, urgency, or prior service as though they automatically licensed exemptions from norms imposed on everyone else.[S17][S18][S30][S34][S51][S95]

## Working-canon thesis

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when an actor, office, or institution condemns, disciplines, or burdens others under a norm it refuses for itself or its own side without a publicly relevant reason that survives like-cases discipline. Not every asymmetry is hypocrisy: some roles rightly carry heavier burdens or narrower permissions. But status, affinity, power, convenience, and victory do not by themselves justify one law upward and another downward.**[S17][S18][S30][S32][S34][S51][S95]

This is not only a complaint about bad style. It is a claim about standing.

## 1. The problem is not difference alone

Righteousness does **not** require identical treatment in every case. Parents and judges are not the same. Regulators and applicants are not the same. Public officials often face stricter conflict, recusal, recordkeeping, and authorization burdens than ordinary citizens, precisely because they wield entrusted power.[S17][S18][S32][S34]

So the archive does not equate all asymmetry with hypocrisy. The real question is whether the asymmetry tracks something publicly relevant:
- a different role and duty-structure;
- a different vulnerability or dependency relation;
- a different jurisdictional position;
- or a different remedial burden created by one's own earlier act.[S17][S18][S34][S51]

What the archive now rejects is the easier move: *my side is exempt because it is my side; my office is exempt because it is powerful; my case is exempt because my purposes are good; my allies are exempt because enforcement would be inconvenient.*[S19][S30][S34][S51][S95]

## 2. Hypocrisy impairs standing even when the norm is true

The blame literature is useful here because it distinguishes truth of criticism from **standing to wield it**. An accusation can identify a real wrong while still being burdened by hypocrisy, compromised relation, or unequal self-application on the part of the accuser.[S30]

The archive takes a portable lesson from that. A norm used only against subordinates, enemies, competitors, dissidents, or socially weak parties becomes damaged for purposes of righteous claim-making. The underlying rule may still be correct; the claimant's standing to present itself as righteous is impaired because the norm is being used selectively rather than owned reciprocally.[S30]

So this surface sharpens an older archive thought into a cleaner rule:

> **A righteousness claim should normally be willing to travel across analogous cases, including upward toward oneself and sideways toward one's own side, unless a publicly articulable role-difference or burden-difference blocks the analogy.**[S30][S34][S51][S95]

## 3. Equal burden is not identical burden

Equality materials and rule-of-law materials help because they distinguish two things that are often confused: sameness of treatment and nonarbitrary application.[S34][S51][S95] The archive therefore does **not** say that every case must receive numerically identical burden. It says something narrower and more useful:

- **like cases should not be split by rank, affinity, or convenience alone;**
- **unlike cases should not be falsely leveled to hide relevant vulnerability, role, or history;**
- **where asymmetry is claimed, it should be openly justified by reasons that could guide future like cases rather than by ad hoc preference.**[S17][S34][S51][S95]

This matters because a public office may rightly face *stricter* duties than the public, and a caregiver may rightly carry *heavier* burdens than a stranger. Those are asymmetries, but not self-exemptions. By contrast, selective enforcement, donor leniency, impunity for allies, procedural indulgence for insiders, and zero-tolerance rhetoric applied only downward are standard forms of unrighteous double-standard rule.[S19][S32][S34][S95]

## 4. Office is the clearest stress test

Public ethics and rule-of-law materials make the office-facing case especially sharp. Officials are expected to act impartially, avoid preferential treatment, and understand that public service is a public trust rather than a private asset.[S32] Rule-of-law materials likewise treat legal equality and accountability of power-holders as central, while also noting that officials may need both special powers and extra restrictions because they act in the name of the community.[S34] Equality-before-the-law materials sharpen the floor: persons are entitled to equal protection of the law without discrimination.[S95]

The archive therefore adds a compact office-facing discipline:

> **The stronger the office, the less it may reserve clean process, patience, waiver, mercy, or explanation for insiders while offering only severity, opacity, and suspicion to outsiders. Public office may differentiate for public reason; it may not differentially indulge its own people simply because they are its own.**[S18][S19][S32][S34][S95]

This does not bar all discretion. It bars discretion that cannot survive open articulation, record, review, and like-cases discipline.

## 5. The practical diagnostic

When a righteousness claim sounds principled, ask six questions:
1. **What norm is being invoked?** Candor, legality, loyalty, anti-violence, austerity, procedural regularity, anti-corruption, mercy, or something else?[S30][S34]
2. **Whom does it bind in practice?** Everyone analogously situated, or mainly rivals, subordinates, targets, and outsiders?[S19][S30]
3. **What asymmetry is being claimed?** Role, vulnerability, jurisdiction, repair burden, or merely status, closeness, and convenience?[S17][S34][S51]
4. **Would the claimant accept the analogous burden upward or inward?** If not, what principled difference blocks the analogy?[S30][S51][S95]
5. **Is the claimed difference publicly articulable and future-guiding?** Could it be written into a rule, precedent, or reviewable explanation, or is it mostly ad hoc?[S18][S32][S34]
6. **Does the asymmetry burden the stronger or protect the weaker, or does it mainly excuse the stronger and squeeze the weaker?** The latter is where self-exemption usually hides.[S17][S19][S34][S95]

## Compression sentence

> **Do not mistake unequal burden for hypocrisy, but do not let side, status, or power write private exceptions into public norms: righteousness requires reasons that can survive reciprocal and like-cases testing rather than one law for others and another for self.**[S17][S30][S32][S34][S51][S95]
