# Policing stops, profiling, and no-suspicion-by-category

## Core claim

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when police, border, security, or other enforcement actors initiate stopping, questioning, identity checks, searches, watchlisting, investigation, or force mainly through race, colour, descent, ethnicity, religion, nationality, migration status, neighbourhood proxy, or another group marker rather than through individualized and conduct-linked grounds that can be publicly explained and contested.**[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S153][S252][S396][S233][S411][S412][S413][S414]

## 1. The missing moral question is how suspicion begins

The archive already had strong rules about equality, arbitrary intrusion, algorithmic governance, biometrics, and searches. But it still lacked a direct surface for an earlier move: **who gets made visible to suspicion in the first place**. That gap matters because many institutions now defend stop-and-search, identity checks, document demands, neighbourhood saturation, border questioning, intelligence-led operations, or predictive attention as if the only real moral question were what happens after a person has been selected. The current source set says the earlier selection decision is morally loaded too. ICERD article 5 guarantees equality before the law, including equal treatment before tribunals and other organs administering justice.[S233] CERD’s General Recommendation No. 36 and OHCHR’s 2019 guide sharpen the operative rule: law-enforcement determinations should be based on individual conduct and objective criteria, not on membership in a racial, ethnic, national, or similar group.[S411][S412]

That matters for righteousness because category-based initiation does not merely produce occasional unfairness at the margin. It redistributes ordinary civic standing. It teaches some people that moving in public, waiting in transit, driving, gathering, working late, dressing a certain way, speaking with an accent, or carrying certain papers is enough to trigger official attention. Once that is normal, the institution is not merely detecting risk. It is deciding whose presence is already half-suspicious before any individualized reason has been shown.[S18][S19][S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S411][S412][S413][S414]

## 2. Profiling is not only a bad motive problem; it is a standing problem

The 2019 OHCHR publication is especially useful because it defines racial profiling as reliance on generalizations grounded in race, colour, descent, or national or ethnic origin rather than on objective evidence or individual behaviour, and says the practice violates non-discrimination, equality before the law, liberty, security, privacy, freedom of movement, and protection against arbitrary arrest and other interventions.[S412] CERD’s General Recommendation No. 36 matters because it gathers the Committee’s prior practice on racial profiling and records that multiple international mechanisms have treated the practice as a violation of international human rights law.[S411]

The archive therefore needed a more precise rule. Profiling is not wrong only because an officer may harbor bias or insult a targeted person. It is wrong because public power has made category-membership itself do evidentiary work it should not be allowed to do. Righteousness is especially burdened where appearance, surname, language, dress, religion, neighbourhood, or nationality becomes a shortcut for deciding who is to be stopped, searched, documented, questioned, or surveilled.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S233][S411][S412][S414]

## 3. The downstream harms are not morally accidental

The 2025 Expert Mechanism report is useful because it says racial discrimination permeates all layers of the criminal justice system, including before, during, and after trial, and identifies racial profiling, arbitrary deprivation of liberty, lack of legal representation, and unfair convictions as linked rather than isolated defects.[S413] That is morally revealing for the archive because profiling is often defended as a low-level inconvenience: a few extra stops, a little more friction, a bit more questioning. But once stop selection is skewed, later burdens are not random side effects. Search, arrest exposure, use-of-force exposure, pretrial detention, and conviction risk are all more likely to accumulate on the people the system has already decided to treat as pre-suspect.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S402][S411][S412][S413]

So the archive now refuses a familiar laundering move. It will not treat stop initiation as morally light merely because each later step is reviewed under its own doctrine. If the doorway into official scrutiny is itself category-corrupted, then later legality does not by itself repair the standing defect. A stop can be formally documented and still unrighteous if category-membership rather than individualized reason did the real work of selection.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S411][S412][S413]

## 4. Technology and proxies do not cleanse the wrong

CERD’s 2020 recommendation is also useful because it warns that big data, AI, facial recognition, and other new technologies risk deepening racism and discriminatory profiling, and it calls for transparency and human-rights compliance when algorithmic profiling systems are deployed for law-enforcement purposes.[S411] The archive had already admitted a no-rightlessness-by-model rule and a no-identity-by-extraction rule. The present surface adds the missing initiation-stage lesson: a profile does not become righteous merely because it is statistical, automated, or hidden behind neutral-seeming inputs such as postcode, gang list, visa category, travel pattern, prior stop density, or neighbourhood complaint volume.[S153][S252][S404][S405][S411]

This matters because category suspicion is often laundered through proxies. The institution says it did not select by race or migration status; it selected by zone, risk score, intelligence indicator, watchlist, address cluster, document anomaly, or patrol pattern. But if those devices keep doing the same category work while making the evidentiary story harder to challenge, righteousness is not saved by technical indirection. It is made worse by opacity.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S43][S45][S47][S49][S93][S153][S252][S396][S411][S413][S414]

## 5. Xenophobic and migration-linked profiling belong inside the same rule

The 2026 joint general recommendation on eradicating xenophobia towards migrants and others perceived as such is useful because it treats migrants and those perceived as such as a population exposed to a specific family of discriminatory burdens that public policy must actively address.[S414] The archive therefore should not write this surface as if profiling were only a race question in a narrow sense. In practice, migration status, perceived foreignness, accent, name, religion, national origin, language, border geography, and document instability are often braided together. The moral structure is the same when public power decides that certain people should expect more stops, more ID checks, more raids, more document demands, or more suspicion because they look or sound like they may not belong.[S127][S399][S233][S411][S412][S414]

So the archive now adds a no-suspicion-by-category rule rather than a narrower anti-racial-profiling slogan alone. The narrower slogan remains true, but the theory is more portable and more precise when it says: suspicion may sometimes be initiated, but it must not be initiated mainly by category-membership or by proxies that keep doing category work under cleaner names.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S233][S411][S412][S413][S414]

## 6. Working rule

When an actor claims righteousness while relying on stops, checks, or suspicion screens, ask seven questions:
1. **What triggered scrutiny?** Specific conduct, concrete evidence, individualized intelligence, or mainly appearance, category-membership, neighbourhood, document stereotype, or proxy marker?[S233][S411][S412][S414]
2. **What had to be shown before intervention began?** Was there a clear threshold for stopping, questioning, ID checking, searching, or watchlisting, or was broad discretion allowed to do the real work?[S32][S34][S35][S93][S402][S411]
3. **Who was made repeatedly available to scrutiny?** Were Africans and people of African descent, migrants, religious minorities, Roma, Indigenous peoples, poor neighbourhoods, or other protected or vulnerable groups predictably bearing the heavier exposure?[S411][S412][S413][S414]
4. **Did later procedure really cure the earlier defect?** Did review, documentation, or legality check the initiation decision itself, or only ratify what had already been set in motion?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S413]
5. **Was technology laundering the category choice?** Did a score, hotspot map, watchlist, facial-recognition lead, gang database, or proxy variable mask rather than remove the discriminatory initiation logic?[S153][S252][S396][S404][S405][S411]
6. **What ordinary life was burdened?** Movement, work, schooling, protest, family travel, border crossing, privacy, bodily security, or the simple ability to be in public without pre-suspicion?[S93][S412][S413][S414]
7. **What challenge and remedy path existed?** Could a person contest the stop logic, the database or watchlist inclusion, the profiling pattern, or the discriminatory proxy rather than only the final sanction?[S32][S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S411][S413]

## Compression sentence

> **Do not let public power begin from category suspicion: where police, border, security, or other enforcement actors make race, ethnicity, religion, nationality, migration status, appearance, neighbourhood proxy, or another group marker do the real work of deciding who gets stopped, checked, searched, questioned, or tracked, righteousness fails unless individualized, conduct-linked, publicly accountable grounds genuinely govern the initiation decision and real challenge paths exist.**[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S153][S252][S396][S233][S411][S412][S413][S414]
