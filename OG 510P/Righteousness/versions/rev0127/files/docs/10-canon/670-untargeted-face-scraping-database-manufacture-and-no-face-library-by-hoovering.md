# Untargeted face scraping, database manufacture, and no-face-library-by-hoovering

## Core claim

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when public or private actors create, expand, buy, or rely on facial-recognition databases built through untargeted scraping of images from the internet or CCTV, so that ordinary appearance in public or online becomes raw material for later identification, tracking, or search rather than remaining bounded by consent, strict necessity, lawful acquisition, proportionality, retention discipline, and meaningful contestability.**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S438][S449][S452]

## 1. The missing moral question is when ordinary appearance becomes stock for later power

The archive already rejected no-identity-by-extraction, no-rights-by-list, no-standing-by-score, and no-status-by-body-signal. But it still lacked a direct rule for a different temptation: **treating ambient life itself as a harvest field from which searchable facial stock can be built for future use by stronger parties**.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49]

That gap matters because a facial-recognition database manufactured through untargeted scraping does something upstream of later matching. It takes images people posted, images others posted of them, or images captured by cameras in ordinary movement and turns them into a standing reservoir of possible later recognition. The burden is not only at the moment of later identification. It begins when mere appearance is converted into administrative inventory without a relation-specific justification from the person whose face was taken.[S437][S438][S452]

## 2. This is distinct from both identity extraction and sensitive-status inference

The archive needs this as its own canon surface because the wrong is not exhausted by either point-of-use biometric matching or embodied status classification. No-identity-by-extraction asks when bodily or genomic extractability becomes the ordinary condition of access, suspicion, or recognizability. No-status-by-body-signal asks when biometric traces are used to infer protected or sensitive class-membership. **Untargeted face scraping asks a prior question: who was entitled to build the stockpile in the first place, and what happens once public or online presence is treated as permissionless source material for later searchability?**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S438][S449][S452]

That difference matters morally because a regime may later claim each downstream search was targeted, proportionate, or useful while hiding the more basic defect that the searchable reservoir was manufactured through indiscriminate capture. The archive should therefore not let the apparent narrowness of later queries launder the unrighteousness of how the database itself came to exist.[S437][S438][S449][S452]

## 3. Current governance materials already treat untargeted face scraping as a red-line practice

This is no longer only a philosophical anxiety about future surveillance. The AI Act itself prohibits AI systems that create or expand facial-recognition databases through the untargeted scraping of facial images from the internet or CCTV footage, and recital 43 says that practice adds to the feeling of mass surveillance and can lead to gross violations of fundamental rights, including privacy.[S437] The AI Act Service Desk's current Article 5 materials and the Commission's current AI Act overview likewise list untargeted scraping of internet or CCTV material to create or expand facial-recognition databases among the prohibited practices already in force.[S438][S452]

That matters because the line being drawn is not simply about low accuracy or imperfect notice. It marks out a class of uses where building the database by hoovering up ambient faces is itself treated as fundamentally dangerous to rights and standing. The archive should learn from that: some practices are burdened not merely because a later match may be mistaken, but because searchable facial stock has been made by overriding the ordinary moral boundary between appearing and being entered into a durable reservoir of possible future search.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S438][S452]

## 4. Mass-searchability is a standing defect even before a particular search is justified

Council of Europe facial-recognition materials sharpen why this deserves its own canon surface. They frame facial recognition as requiring strict regulation, strong safeguards, and necessity-and-proportionality discipline because the technology can interfere with privacy, expression, assembly, and non-discrimination in ways that are not exhausted by one-off verification uses.[S448][S449] The archive should therefore not treat database manufacture as a morally neutral precondition to later search. A world in which stronger actors may build or buy giant searchable face libraries from ambient life is already a world in which ordinary public presence becomes more governable than it should be.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S448][S449]

So righteousness should ask a sharper question than whether some later identification served security, fraud control, or investigative efficiency. What entitled the actor to accumulate this reservoir of faces at all, what narrower route was unavailable, what deletion or expiry discipline existed, and what kept people from becoming permanently searchable across relations that never originally licensed capture? Where the real power comes from database manufacture rather than a bounded and relation-specific need, the actor is not merely recognizing identity. It is governing through no-face-library-by-hoovering.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S438][S449][S452]

## 5. Righteousness should not let ambient life become a searchable facial commons for the strong

The deeper moral point is portable. A righteous office may sometimes need to identify a person, verify access, or investigate a concrete event. But it should resist a different administrative appetite: the desire to make ordinary life retrospectively searchable by first harvesting faces at scale and only later deciding which persons, events, or populations to query.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S438][S449][S452]

So the archive should reject another laundering move: because the later use was targeted, because the images were publicly visible somewhere, because a vendor assembled the database rather than the deploying authority, or because the scraping happened long before the present query, institutions assume the standing defect has dissolved. But public visibility is not blanket permission for durable biometric stockpiling, vendor distance does not erase acquisition burden, and later selectivity does not purify database manufacture that already made persons ambiently searchable to power.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S438][S449][S452]

## 6. Working rule

When an actor claims righteousness while relying on a facial-recognition database built or expanded through untargeted scraping, ask seven questions:
1. **How was the database made?** Untargeted scraping from the open internet, social media, third-party websites, CCTV, mixed vendor feeds, or another reservoir that treated ordinary appearance as collectible stock?[S437][S438][S452]
2. **What made acquisition lawful and bounded?** Consent where relevant, relation-specific authority, necessity, proportionality, dataset minimization, and protection against ambient overcollection?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S449]
3. **What downstream relation was the stockpile serving?** Policing, border control, fraud prevention, school or workplace monitoring, platform moderation, retail security, advertising, insurance, or another field where later searchability could thin standing?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S438][S452]
4. **What kept the database from becoming effectively permanent?** Deletion, expiry, retention limits, purpose-binding, prohibition on resale or cross-context reuse, and an enforceable path out for those whose images were taken?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S449]
5. **What made the arrangement contestable?** Notice, explanation, challenge to inclusion, correction, deletion, independent oversight, and a real remedy against both database manufacture and later search uses?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S438][S449]
6. **What narrower route was available?** Case-specific evidence gathering, relation-specific verification, consent-based enrollment, ordinary investigative work, or smaller legally acquired datasets rather than ambient face hoovering?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S438][S449]
7. **Was searchable stockpiling doing the real work?** Did the actor's practical power come mainly from having first transformed ordinary presence into a standing facial reservoir for later search, rather than from a bounded and reviewable need arising inside a specific relation?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S438][S449][S452]

## Compression sentence

> **Do not turn ordinary appearance into a standing facial reservoir for later power: where faces from the internet, CCTV, or comparable ambient sources are hoovered into searchable databases, righteousness fails unless acquisition, retention, use, and exit remain narrowly bounded, lawful, necessary, proportionate, and meaningfully contestable.**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S437][S438][S449][S452]
