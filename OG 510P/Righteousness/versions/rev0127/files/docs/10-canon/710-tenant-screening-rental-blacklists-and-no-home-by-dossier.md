# Tenant screening, rental blacklists, and no-home-by-dossier

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when landlords, property managers, public housing actors, platforms, or screening intermediaries use tenant-screening dossiers, eviction or criminal records, credit files, or rental risk scores so that opaque third-party reports do the real work of deciding who gets a home, and on what terms, rather than leaving housing access governed by fair relevance limits, maximum possible accuracy, visibility, dispute, non-discrimination, and answerable human judgment.**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S473][S474][S475][S476][S477]

## 1. The missing moral question is not only whether screening exists, but whether housing access has been made to live under the dossier

The archive already rejected no-homelessness-by-clearance, no-rightlessness-by-model, no-rights-by-list, no-price-by-profile, and no-status-ranked-standing. But it still lacked a direct rule for a different and increasingly common defect: **people are not merely judged by a landlord or agency in ordinary view; they are filtered through third-party screening files and risk summaries that may be hard to see, hard to correct, stale, misleading, or only weakly connected to whether they should be denied or burdened in housing.**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S473][S474][S475][S476][S477]

That gap matters because screening is often defended as routine prudence. The owner only checked history. The property manager only followed the report. The platform only surfaced risk. The larger deposit was only conditional acceptance. The higher rent only reflected background information. The rejection only followed policy. But righteousness has to ask a narrower question: **what actually did the practical work of deciding whether this person could get a home, under what burdens, with what stigma, and with what chance of correcting error before the housing chance was lost?**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S473][S474][S475][S476][S477]

So the archive now needs a canon surface that is not reducible either to homelessness governance or to automated decision-making in general. No-home-by-dossier asks when housing access becomes standing-defective because opaque consumer-reporting files, eviction data, criminal history fields, credit traces, or customized rental risk scores become the practical title by which a person is denied, burdened, or conditionally admitted to a home.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S473][S474][S475][S476][S477]

## 2. Current official materials now name this family clearly enough to justify canon admission

Current CFPB materials are morally revealing because the Bureau's tenant-background-check market report says the market commonly relies on public records including eviction and criminal records, credit reports and scores, and customized rental risk scores and related automated screening solutions.[S473] CFPB's current rental-denial guidance sharpens the procedural burden: where a landlord acts on a tenant screening report, the applicant has rights to an adverse action notice, the name and contact information of the reporting company, a free copy of the report if requested in time, and the right to dispute inaccurate information.[S474] The same guidance also says adverse action is not limited to outright denial: it can include requiring a co-signer, requiring a larger deposit, or charging a higher rent than other applicants.[S474]

FTC guidance widens the same problem from the renter's side. Current FTC materials say tenant background checks may contain information about bill payment, eviction, and criminal history, that renters often do not know exactly what is in the report before applying or how a landlord will use it, and that the reports landlords use may contain outdated, misleading, or other people's information.[S475] HUD materials keep the anti-discrimination edge in view by reaffirming that the Fair Housing Act protects people from discrimination when renting or otherwise seeking housing and that housing discrimination is illegal in nearly all housing, including private and public housing.[S476]

CFPB's TransUnion enforcement action gives the defect concrete shape. The Bureau says the complaint alleged failures to follow reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy of eviction records and failures to identify third-party vendors as sources of eviction and criminal records in consumer disclosures.[S477] CFPB further says inaccurate and misleading tenant-screening information can prolong housing searches, impose additional application fees, force time and money into error correction, cause higher rental payments, and deny housing altogether.[S477] Together these materials show that housing access through contestability-thin dossiers is now a live governance seam rather than a speculative edge case.[S473][S474][S475][S476][S477]

## 3. The righteousness issue is not screening as such; it is housing access governed by opaque, stale, or weakly answerable dossiers

Righteousness should not treat every identity check, reference check, affordability inquiry, or narrowly tailored fraud review as automatically unrighteous. Some bounded screening can be justified: confirming that the applicant exists, preventing identity theft, checking present ability to meet lawful and relevant obligations, or distinguishing a real safety risk from rumor — so long as the inquiry remains relevant, visible enough to answer, non-discriminatory, proportionate to the housing stake, and open to correction before the harm hardens.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S473][S474][S475][S476][S477]

The narrower defect appears where a housing decision rides mainly on records or scores the person cannot realistically inspect in advance, cannot correct in time, cannot understand in relevance terms, or cannot separate from stale public records, mixed files, broad criminal-history use, thin eviction data, or profile-like risk summaries. In those cases the wrong is not simply that information exists. It is that a home becomes conditioned on no-home-by-dossier: the person must pass through a file that others built, interpretive thresholds they do not control, and challenge paths that often arrive only after the apartment, room, or lease opportunity is gone.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S473][S474][S475][S476][S477]

That matters across private rentals, subsidized housing, platform-mediated rentals, campus housing, and other gatekept dwelling arrangements. A dossier can become a standing defect even where no single item is dispositive, because the package as a whole frames the applicant as presumptively risky, administratively costly, or conditionally tolerable. Where that framing does the real work of who gets housed and on what burdened terms, righteousness is often not prudence. It is governance through no-home-by-dossier.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S473][S474][S475][S476][S477]

## 4. Questions righteous judgment should now ask

When an actor claims righteousness while relying on tenant screening, rental background checks, or housing risk dossiers, ask seven questions:

1. **What information was doing the screening work?** Eviction records, criminal history, credit data, debt collections, rental-payment history, identity checks, third-party vendor data, or customized risk scores?[S473][S474][S475][S477]
2. **Could the applicant see and answer the file in time?** Was there real notice, access to the report, enough explanation to contest the relevant items, and enough time to repair before the housing chance was functionally lost?[S474][S475][S477]
3. **Was the information relevant and fresh enough?** Or was stale, misleading, weakly probative, or borrowed information being used as a proxy for present housing worthiness?[S473][S475][S477]
4. **What counted as adverse action?** Denial, conditional acceptance, larger deposit, higher rent, co-signer demand, delayed placement, or silent nonresponse?[S474]
5. **What discrimination or proxy risk was present?** Did the screening criteria or inputs predictably reproduce protected-status burdens, criminal-history overreach, disability burdens, or family-status exclusion?[S474][S476]
6. **Was human judgment real or ceremonial?** Could a competent decision-maker genuinely review context, explain the relevance of the file, depart from the score, and correct error, or did the dossier set the practical outcome in advance?[S473][S474][S475][S477]
7. **Was the dossier doing the real work?** If the opaque report, score, or thin public-record file had been removed, would the same denial or burdened term still have been justified on openly defensible housing terms?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S473][S474][S475][S476][S477]

## 5. Canonical compression

> **Do not let routine-screening rhetoric, background-check formalism, or thin landlord policy language masquerade as righteous housing governance: where opaque tenant dossiers, stale records, or contestability-thin risk screens do the real work of deciding who gets a home, righteousness fails unless relevance, maximum possible accuracy, visibility, dispute, non-discrimination, and genuinely answerable human judgment remain live before the housing opportunity is gone.**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S473][S474][S475][S476][S477]
