# Privacy, Surveillance, and Proportional Legibility

## Why this surface matters

The archive already had contestability, secrecy burdens, anti-reprisal, compelled creed, anti-cover-up, nonabasement, and accommodation. What it still lacked was a compact rule for **what happens when a person is made needlessly legible to the stronger party** — through overbroad search, dossiering, background checking, biometric capture, location tracking, intimate exposure, or indefinite retention.[S34][S93][S114][S153][S154][S155][S156][S157][S158]

That gap matters because two opposite mistakes are common:
- **accountability maximalism** — treating every review or safety problem as solved by collecting more personal data, monitoring more conduct, or retaining more intimate detail; and
- **privacy absolutism** — treating any request for records, screening, search, or disclosure as automatically unrighteous even where a genuine burden, safety risk, or public duty must still be tested and evidenced.[S34][S93][S114][S153][S156][S157]

## Working-canon thesis

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when a person, office, or institution demands, collects, retains, searches, or exposes more personal visibility than the task, risk, or due can justify. Privacy is not impunity and not a veto on review. It is a bounded reserve against arbitrary intrusion, perpetual legibility, and behavior-shaping surveillance. Once intrusion is genuinely necessary, the burden shifts to legality, necessity, proportionality, minimization, access control, and time-bounded retention.**[S93][S114][S153][S154][S155][S156][S157][S158]

## 1. Privacy protects agency, not just secrecy

The archive should not treat privacy as a luxury good for the innocent or as mere embarrassment management. Article 17 of the ICCPR bars arbitrary or unlawful interference with privacy, family, home, and correspondence, while Article 8 of the European Convention protects private and family life, home, and correspondence.[S93][S114] OHCHR's privacy standards page sharpens the point: interferences must be provided by law and subjected to necessity and proportionality review.[S153]

That matters for righteousness because private reserve is part of what lets persons think, associate, test commitments, seek help, and inhabit different roles without being permanently open to inspection. The archive therefore treats privacy not as the enemy of answerability, but as one of the conditions under which answerability can remain human rather than totalizing.[S153][S155][S158]

## 2. Surveillance can wrong even before it openly punishes

The moral problem is not limited to later misuse of data. OHCHR warns that data-intensive technologies let states and businesses track, analyze, predict, and even manipulate behavior at unprecedented scale, with serious risks for dignity, autonomy, privacy, and the enjoyment of rights more generally if they lack effective safeguards.[S154] The Special Rapporteur on privacy makes the same structural point in a calmer register: privacy enables the development and expression of personality, identity, and beliefs and helps people participate in social, economic, cultural, and political life; the mandate therefore focuses on identifying intrusions without compelling justification and bringing surveillance under the rule of law.[S155]

So the archive now treats excessive legibility as a standing problem even before a dossier is overtly weaponized. A school, ministry, employer, prison, platform, or movement can govern people by making them searchable, trackable, and profile-able long before it openly sanctions them. The chill on complaint, belief, association, intimacy, and experimentation may itself be part of the wrong.[S34][S104][S107][S154][S155][S158]

## 3. The right office-facing question is not “can we collect it?” but “why this much?”

Data-protection materials are morally revealing because they express a compression rule the archive needs. The European Commission's GDPR guidance states that where possible it is preferable to use anonymous data and that, where personal data is needed, it should be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary for the purpose.[S156] The European Data Protection Supervisor states the same point more concretely: collect only what is directly relevant and necessary, and keep it only for as long as needed to fulfil that purpose.[S157]

This is exactly the kind of discipline righteousness should want. Stronger actors should not answer every uncertainty, fraud concern, staffing convenience, or managerial appetite by defaulting to more identifiers, more categories, more linkage, more retention, or broader access than the task actually requires. The more righteous posture is usually narrower collection, purpose-fit search, selective access, redaction where possible, anonymization where possible, and deletion or review once the reason for retention has passed.[S153][S156][S157]

## 4. Record preservation and privacy are different burdens, not opposites

This surface does **not** repeal the archive's anti-cover-up rule. Offices may still owe preserved reasons, records, audit trails, and evidence once challenge, repair, or review are reasonably in view.[S34][S121][S122][S123][S124][S125][S126] But that duty does not imply that institutions may demand unlimited intimacy from the people they govern. The archive now distinguishes **records of decisions** from **dossiers on persons**.[S34][S153][S156][S157]

So righteous office should not solve answerability by making the governed maximally visible. It should preserve what is needed to test the decision, while asking whether the same public purpose could be served with less intimate collection, narrower retention, stronger access limits, or role-specific rather than ambient surveillance. The standing defect is often not that some record exists, but that personal legibility has become disproportionate, generalized, or decoupled from the task that was invoked to justify it.[S34][S153][S154][S156][S157]

## 5. Office faces a stricter burden around intimate and belief-adjacent domains

Public office often says it needs information for security, fraud control, classroom order, workforce management, prison safety, immigration control, or administrative efficiency. Sometimes it does. But privacy and conscience materials together show why certain domains are especially burdened: belief, association, communication, location, health, biometrics, and intimate life can become leverage points for retaliation, orthodoxy enforcement, humiliation, exclusion, or chilling silence.[S93][S104][S107][S132][S154][S155][S156][S157]

So righteous office should expect a heavier justificatory burden when it tracks these domains, should prefer need-tied rather than ambient collection, and should be especially suspicious of “just in case” retention, all-purpose internal access, or publication and sharing that outrun the reason originally given. Administrative tidiness, future optionality, institutional curiosity, or “nothing to hide” rhetoric do not carry that burden.[S34][S153][S154][S156][S157][S158]

## 6. Working rule

When a righteousness claim depends on search, surveillance, screening, publication, monitoring, or data retention, ask six questions:
1. **What interest is doing the justificatory work?** Safety, fraud prevention, review, eligibility, care, repair, or something thinner such as curiosity, convenience, or image?[S153][S156][S157]
2. **Could the same aim be served with less legibility?** Anonymous or aggregate data, narrower fields, targeted rather than ambient collection, shorter retention, redaction, or human review rather than indiscriminate capture?[S156][S157]
3. **What exactly was collected or exposed?** Belief, association, location, health, biometrics, intimate communications, family information, or reputationally loaded facts carry different burdens.[S93][S114][S155][S158]
4. **Who could access it, repurpose it, or publish it?** Purpose drift and broad internal visibility are often part of the wrong, not just later misuse.[S153][S156][S157][S158]
5. **How long did the visibility last?** Perpetual or default retention can become its own form of domination when the original reason has passed.[S156][S157]
6. **What chilling effect followed?** Did the practice predictably deter complaint, dissent, belief, intimacy, help-seeking, or ordinary experimentation in how to live?[S104][S107][S154][S155][S158]

## Compression sentence

> **Do not let safety talk, review appetite, or managerial convenience do the work of righteousness when the real method is to make people more searchable, trackable, exposable, or permanently knowable than the task can justify; righteous institutions need enough proof to answer for their acts, not unlimited legibility of the persons under their power.**[S34][S93][S114][S153][S154][S155][S156][S157][S158]
