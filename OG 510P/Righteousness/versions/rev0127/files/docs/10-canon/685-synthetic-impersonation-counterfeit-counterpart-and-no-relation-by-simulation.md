# Synthetic impersonation, counterfeit counterpart, and no-relation-by-simulation

## Core claim

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when public or private actors use AI-generated or AI-manipulated content, cloned voices, synthetic avatars, deepfakes, or undisclosed AI interaction to counterfeit a person, speaker, witness, advisor, official, or human counterpart, so that another party's trust, disclosure, obedience, accusation, consent, or public judgment depends on a relation that is not really there or is materially misdescribed.**[S34][S35][S39][S41][S45][S47][S49][S72][S73][S93][S446][S452][S454][S455]

## 1. The missing moral question is not only whether some content was false, but whether a relation was counterfeited

The archive already rejected nondeception failures, no-agency-by-stealth, no-conviction-by-opacity, and no-self-incrimination-by-script. But it still lacked a direct rule for a different temptation: **letting practical power travel through a person or counterpart who has been synthetically manufactured, impersonated, or quietly replaced.**[S34][S35][S39][S41][S45][S47][S49][S72][S73][S93][S446][S452][S454][S455]

That gap matters because many wrongs of this kind are not captured by asking only whether a sentence was literally false. A caller may sound like a relative, executive, or official. A chatbot may present as a human representative, counselor, tutor, or companion. A clip may look like a confession, endorsement, instruction, or witness account. A document may appear to come from a person whose practical standing is what makes the message actionable. In each case the decisive burden is not merely bad content. It is that **someone is being asked to act under a counterfeit relation**.[S39][S72][S73][S446][S452][S454][S455]

## 2. This is distinct from ordinary fiction, disclosed automation, and general manipulation

The archive needs this as its own canon surface because several nearby wrongs should not be collapsed. Nondeception names the broader duty against lies and misleading presentation. No-agency-by-stealth names systems that impair informed and resistible choice. No-conviction-by-opacity names evidentiary and adjudicative failures. **No-relation-by-simulation asks a narrower question: when does a synthetic or undisclosed system do the real work of making one party believe they are dealing with a person, officeholder, witness, or human interlocutor who is not in fact present in that way?**[S34][S35][S39][S41][S45][S47][S49][S72][S73][S93][S446][S452][S454][S455]

That distinction matters morally because a system can be defended by saying the output was useful, the advice was accurate enough, the message was only a draft, the avatar was only an interface, the voice was only a convenience layer, or the user never expressly asked whether a human was present. But righteousness still has to ask what relation actually bore the person's trust or action. If the practical effect is that another party transfers money, gives data, obeys instructions, offers intimacy, accepts a diagnosis, believes an accusation, or relies on testimony because a human counterpart was simulated or concealed, then the relation itself has been counterfeited.[S39][S72][S73][S446][S452][S454][S455]

## 3. Current governance materials already treat undisclosed AI interaction and synthetic content as live deception and impersonation risks

This is no longer merely a philosophical concern. The Commission's current AI Act materials explain that transparency requirements for certain AI systems aim to address risks of misinformation and manipulation, fraud, impersonation, and consumer deception.[S446] The Commission's current Article 50 guidance says transparency regarding the artificial origin of AI-generated or AI-manipulated content is essential, and it specifies that providers of AI systems that interact with people must inform them that they are interacting with AI rather than a human unless this is obvious. The same guidance says providers of AI-generated or AI-manipulated content must facilitate identification and marking, and that deployers of systems generating or manipulating deepfakes must inform users about the artificial origin of the content, except in defined cases.[S454] The Commission's current policy materials also state that humans should be aware when they are interacting with a machine so they can take an informed decision, and that deepfakes and certain AI-generated public-interest text must be clearly and visibly labelled.[S452]

FTC materials sharpen the same point from a different governance angle. The FTC's current impersonation-enforcement materials say the Impersonation Rule makes it illegal to materially and falsely pose as a government entity or officer or as a business or officer thereof, or to materially misrepresent affiliation with them.[S455] That does not by itself settle the whole moral theory. But it confirms that present institutions already recognize a core wrong here: **counterfeit identity or affiliation can itself be the operational mechanism of harm.**[S446][S452][S454][S455]

## 4. The deeper problem is counterfeit trust-transfer

Accuracy does not exhaust the problem. A synthetic adviser may give decent advice. A cloned voice may convey instructions that a real superior might have given. A chatbot may provide information that is not false. A deepfake may resemble a real speaker closely enough to pass casual review. Yet righteousness should ask a different question: what made the hearer or viewer move? If what moved them was the apparent presence, endorsement, authorship, authority, vulnerability, or intimacy of another person, then practical power has been routed through counterfeit trust-transfer.[S39][S72][S73][S446][S452][S454][S455]

That helps explain why this surface belongs near testimony, deception, office, consent, and fair process all at once. When counterfeit relation does the work, people can be induced to reveal secrets, send money, obey commands, accept evidence, surrender rights, or form attachments under misdescribed conditions. The harm is therefore not only reputational or informational. It is structural: a person is asked to stand in relation to someone who is not actually there in the way that matters.[S34][S35][S39][S41][S45][S47][S49][S72][S73][S93][S446][S452][S454][S455]

## 5. Righteousness should distinguish disclosed simulation and bounded assistance from counterfeit counterparty design

The deeper point is not that every simulation, dubbing, automation, avatar, or synthetic aid is identical. Some machine interaction is obvious. Some synthetic or assistive uses may be openly disclosed, tightly bounded, and non-counterfeit in function. The archive should therefore not pretend the moral task is solved by a slogan against all simulation. It should ask what exactly was being represented, whether disclosure was timely and intelligible, whether the person could recalibrate or refuse, whether authenticity mattered to the decision at hand, and what prevented a bounded tool from quietly becoming a counterfeit counterpart.[S34][S35][S39][S41][S45][S47][S49][S72][S73][S93][S446][S452][S454][S455]

So righteousness should distinguish between a disclosed system that helps draft, translate, demonstrate, or automate within understood limits, and an arrangement in which synthetic or undisclosed interaction makes another person believe a human advisor, witness, superior, friend, or official is what they are confronting. The first may still face burden. The second is much closer to governance through no-relation-by-simulation.[S34][S35][S39][S41][S45][S47][S49][S72][S73][S93][S446][S452][S454][S455]

## 6. Working rule

When an actor claims righteousness while relying on synthetic impersonation, deepfake content, cloned voice or likeness, or undisclosed AI interaction, ask seven questions:
1. **What relation was being invoked?** Human customer-service agent, friend, family member, executive, teacher, clinician, witness, journalist, official, endorser, romantic or companion counterpart, or another role whose identity or humanity mattered to the action taken.[S39][S45][S72][S73][S454][S455]
2. **What exactly was simulated, concealed, or counterfeited?** Voice, face, live chat presence, authorship, affiliation, endorsement, testimony, message provenance, or the fact of human rather than machine interaction.[S446][S452][S454][S455]
3. **What disclosure existed, and when did it arrive?** Was the person told clearly and early enough that they were interacting with AI or synthetic content, or did disclosure arrive only after trust, disclosure, obedience, transfer, or attachment had already been secured?[S446][S452][S454]
4. **What act depended on the counterfeit relation?** Money transfer, credential disclosure, legal or medical consent, workplace compliance, reliance on evidence, public belief, reputational harm, emotional commitment, or another standing-bearing act.[S34][S35][S39][S41][S45][S47][S49][S72][S73][S93][S455]
5. **What authenticity, challenge, or contest path remained live?** Could the person verify identity, inspect provenance, challenge authorship, slow the interaction, or route to a real human before consequential reliance occurred?[S34][S35][S39][S72][S73][S446][S452][S454][S455]
6. **What narrower truthful route could have served the legitimate aim?** Clear disclosure, provenance tools, human escalation, signed communications, bounded automation, or other forms that do not require counterfeit relation to do the work.[S446][S452][S454]
7. **What was the practical effect?** Did the arrangement merely support a disclosed task, or did it make trust, disclosure, obedience, accusation, consent, or judgment depend on no-relation-by-simulation?[S34][S35][S39][S41][S45][S47][S49][S72][S73][S93][S446][S452][S454][S455]

## 7. Compression payoff for the archive

This surface earns its place because it gives the archive a compact way to talk about a family of cases that otherwise remain scattered across deception, testimony, fair process, manipulation, evidence, platform governance, and office ethics. It captures why some AI transparency disputes are not mere UX annoyances, why some deepfake disputes are not only about media literacy, and why some impersonation disputes are not merely fraud in a narrow commercial sense. The archive can now say something more exact: **righteousness is burdened when power routes practical consequence through a counterfeit person or counterpart.**[S34][S35][S39][S41][S45][S47][S49][S72][S73][S93][S446][S452][S454][S455]
