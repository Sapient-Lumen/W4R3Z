# Lethal Force, Arbitrary Killing, and No-Death-by-Convenience

## Why this surface matters

The archive already rejects disappearance architecture, torture, collective punishment, punitive noncare, and convenience custody. What it still lacked was a compact rule for **what happens when power solves danger, disorder, uncertainty, flight risk, or political embarrassment by taking life itself**: police shootings presented as mere tactics, prison killings relabeled as order restoration, border deaths treated as deterrent collateral, protest suppression that quietly crosses into shoot-to-kill posture, battlefield or security language used where the real standard should still be life-preserving restraint, or death in custody explained away as the rough cost of control.[S34][S35][S41][S93][S201][S202][S203][S204][S205]

That missing rule matters because the right-to-life materials do not treat killing as just another heavy burden to be balanced against administrative ease. ICCPR Article 6 protects the inherent right to life and forbids arbitrary deprivation of life.[S93] General Comment No. 36 sharpens the point by treating the right to life as the supreme right from which no derogation is permitted even in public emergency, and by emphasizing that deprivation of life is arbitrary when it exceeds what international law permits, including through unnecessary or disproportionate force.[S201] The Basic Principles on the Use of Force and Firearms make the same burden operational in law-enforcement settings: non-violent means should be tried first, force should be used only if other means are ineffective, and firearms are tightly restricted to cases such as self-defence or defence of others against an imminent threat of death or serious injury.[S202] So the archive should now treat arbitrary killing as a distinct standing defect: **death may not become the easy answer to danger, resistance, backlog, escape, opacity, or political fear**.[S93][S201][S202][S203]

## Working-canon thesis

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when a person, office, or institution takes life without a strictly necessary, lawful, and reviewable basis, or structures force so that killing becomes the easiest way to regain control, prevent flight, quiet resistance, or avoid the burdens of capture, care, de-escalation, and investigation. In policing, custody, border control, protest management, and other law-enforcement settings, lethal force is presumptively exceptional and may be used only under the steepest necessity. In conflict, the moral burden does not disappear: murder and violence to life remain prohibited, especially against persons not taking direct part in hostilities or those hors de combat. Every potentially unlawful death triggers duties of preservation, prompt impartial investigation, truthful accounting, and family-facing explanation rather than disappearance, euphemism, or institutional self-exoneration.**[S93][S201][S202][S203][S204][S205]

## 1. Life is the steepest burden power can impose

The archive already treated detention as one of power's heaviest burdens. Taking life is heavier still. Article 6 matters because it starts from inherent right rather than administrative permission, and General Comment No. 36 matters because it says the right to life is the supreme right and non-derogable even during armed conflict and other public emergencies threatening the life of the nation.[S93][S201] That means a righteous actor does not ask only whether deadly force was understandable, familiar, feared, or useful. The harder question is whether the actor had reached a point where preserving life through narrower means was no longer realistically possible.

That keeps the archive from a recurrent laundering move. Institutions often describe lethal force as a regrettable but normal endpoint of enforcement: the suspect fled, the prisoner would not comply, the crowd became unruly, the checkpoint was tense, the border zone was dangerous, the commander had incomplete information. But where killing is doing the work that patience, planning, equipment, containment, medical response, negotiation, or later capture would otherwise have had to do, righteousness is usually already badly burdened.[S34][S35][S41][S93][S201][S202]

## 2. Arbitrary killing is broader than assassination without paperwork

The archive already learned from Article 9 that arbitrariness is broader than bare illegality. The same lesson matters even more for life. General Comment No. 36 explains that the deprivation of life may be arbitrary even when some domestic legal framework exists, because the deeper burden turns on necessity, proportionality, precaution, and non-discrimination rather than on paperwork alone.[S201] That matters because lethal force is often laundered through formal categories: authorized operation, crowd-control response, pursuit policy, prison regulation, counter-terror raid, border emergency, or engagement rule.

So the archive should now ask a sharper question whenever death is defended: **was life taken because no narrower route remained, or because the institution preferred certainty, speed, intimidation, or officer-side convenience over the harder work of preserving the person alive?** Where fear is generalized, threat assessment is generic, warning is absent without good reason, or force planning predictably drives encounters toward fatal outcomes, righteousness is often not merely tragic. It is governing through disposable life.[S19][S32][S34][S35][S93][S201][S202][S203]

## 3. In law-enforcement settings, lethal force is last-resort force

The Basic Principles are morally revealing because they refuse to let force float free from life-preserving restraint. Non-violent means should be applied as far as possible before resorting to force and firearms, and force or firearms may be used only if other means remain ineffective or without promise of achieving the intended result.[S202] Firearms are especially constrained: intentional lethal use is tied to immediate protection against death or serious injury and to preventing a particularly dangerous escape only under the same steep conditions.[S202]

That helps the archive resist a common distortion. Officers often face real danger and cannot be asked to wait for ideal clarity. But righteousness is not measured only in the final second. It is also measured in planning, training, equipment, distance, patience, warning, cover, medical backup, and command culture. If institutions repeatedly generate encounters in which killing becomes the default path because de-escalation was underbuilt, less-lethal means were unavailable or misused, or policy rewarded overhasty domination, then the defect is not just individual error. It is office designed so that death arrives too cheaply.[S34][S35][S41][S202][S203]

## 4. Custody, protest, and border settings do not lower the burden to preserve life

The Basic Principles are again useful because they address precisely the settings most vulnerable to lethal-force laundering. In assemblies, force should be avoided or minimized, and firearms remain confined to the strict conditions governing lethal force generally.[S202] In custody, force is limited to what is strictly necessary for security or personal safety, and firearms are narrower still.[S202] The 1989 execution-prevention principles sharpen the same point from another angle by expressly covering deaths in custody and excessive or illegal force by public officials, and by insisting that emergency, war, or internal instability do not justify extra-legal, arbitrary, or summary executions.[S203]

So the archive should now reject several recurrent excuses. The fact that a person was already detained does not make their life more expendable. The fact that a crowd was unlawful does not turn every participant into a shootable threat. The fact that a border zone, checkpoint, or security operation is tense does not convert evasion, panic, or mere disobedience into a sufficient reason to kill. Where institutions rely on live fire because the alternative is embarrassment, escape, paperwork, later search, or slower apprehension, righteousness is usually not preserving order under burden but converting inconvenience into death risk.[S19][S34][S35][S41][S93][S202][S203]

## 5. Potentially unlawful deaths trigger investigation duties, not self-cleansing narratives

The archive already rejected cover-up and disappearance architecture. Unlawful-death materials sharpen why those failures matter so much after killing. The execution-prevention principles require thorough, prompt, and impartial investigation of suspected extra-legal, arbitrary, and summary executions, including where relatives or other reliable reports suggest unnatural death in such circumstances.[S203] The Minnesota Protocol is morally useful because it treats the duty to protect life and the procedural duty to investigate potentially unlawful deaths as paired obligations rather than as separate domains.[S204]

This matters because institutions often respond to lethal incidents through euphemism, internal script, or narrowed record: officer-involved event, neutralization, crossfire, less-than-lethal operation with unexpected outcome, apparent suicide, natural causes pending review. But when life has been taken under color of public power, righteousness is not preserved by a minimally plausible story. It requires scene preservation, truthful record, independent and competent inquiry, family-facing explanation, and an answerable path to sanction, reform, and repair where wrongdoing occurred.[S34][S35][S36][S121][S122][S123][S124][S125][S126][S203][S204]

## 6. Conflict does not create a general permission to make life cheap

The archive should not collapse law-enforcement and armed-conflict paradigms into one rule. Still, a portable floor remains. ICCPR's right-to-life materials continue to matter, and ICRC's customary-law summary states that violence to life is prohibited as a norm applicable in both international and non-international armed conflicts.[S201][S205] So even where conflict changes the immediate targeting analysis, righteousness does not become indifferent to life. Murder, execution of detainees, killing of persons hors de combat, and other forms of violence to life remain standing defects that no emergency rhetoric or conflict label can purify.[S41][S93][S203][S205]

This narrower point is enough for the archive at this stage. It need not yet settle every problem about combatancy, direct participation, or remote targeting. It does need to say that war does not dissolve the moral core already visible elsewhere: life is not administratively disposable, and the more distant or hidden the chain from decision to death, the greater the burden of discrimination, verification, and review rather than the lesser.[S41][S93][S201][S205]

## 7. Working rule

When a shooting, strike, custodial death, tactical raid, dispersal action, or other life-taking act is defended under pressure, ask six questions:
1. **What concrete threat was present?** Immediate threat of death or serious injury, active hostilities, custodial attack, escape by a dangerous detainee, or something vaguer?[S93][S201][S202][S203][S205]
2. **Why was lethal force necessary rather than merely faster or safer for the stronger party?** What non-lethal, lower-force, containment, delay, distance, or capture options remained?[S34][S35][S41][S202]
3. **What happened before the fatal moment?** Planning, warning, identification, de-escalation, equipment, medical readiness, command discipline, and encounter design all matter.[S202][S203]
4. **Who reviewed the necessity claim, and how quickly?** Was there preserved scene evidence, full reporting, independent inquiry, and a truthful public account?[S34][S35][S36][S203][S204]
5. **What happened to the dead person's relational claims?** Family notification, dignity of remains, truthful cause and circumstances, and access to remedy remain live after death rather than expiring with it.[S26][S30][S34][S35][S203][S204]
6. **Did institutional design make death too easy?** Training gaps, permissive rules, discriminatory targeting, aggressive tactics, and impunity structures can make the killing systemically convenient even where one actor insists the moment was tragic.[S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S201][S202][S203]

## 8. Compact maxim

> **Do not make death the easy answer.** Where life can still be preserved through narrower means, righteousness requires the harder work of restraint, capture, care, review, and truth. Killing that arrives by convenience, panic culture, secrecy, or administrative impatience is rarely righteous and often stands as a grave defeater.[S93][S201][S202][S203][S204][S205]
