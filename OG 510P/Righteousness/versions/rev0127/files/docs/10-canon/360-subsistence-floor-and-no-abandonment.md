# Subsistence Floor and No-Abandonment

## Why this surface matters

The archive already had threshold-band discipline, portable priority cues, anti-attrition, anti-exploitation, accommodation, and emergency anti-exceptionism. What it still lacked was a compact rule for **what righteousness owes once persons or peoples are at risk of falling below a minimally decent floor of food, water, shelter, and basic care** — and for how scarcity should be handled without laundering abandonment through markets, queues, or bureaucratic regret.[S17][S34][S41][S47][S49][S163][S164][S165][S166][S167][S168][S169]

That gap matters because two opposite mistakes are common:
- **subsistence absolutism** — treating every need claim as automatically overriding every other claim, regardless of scarcity, role, or fair process; and
- **abandonment laundering** — treating hunger, dehydration, exposure, untreated basic illness, or homelessness as unfortunate background conditions, private failure, or market residue rather than as standing-relevant burdens on anyone who claims righteous office or righteous institutional design.[S34][S41][S163][S164][S165][S166][S167][S168][S169]

## Working-canon thesis

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when a person, office, or institution leaves others without reasonable access to the essentials of a minimally decent life — especially food, water, basic shelter or housing, and basic care needed for life in dignity — while treating those floor claims as ordinary preferences, disciplinary leverage, or costs to be offloaded downward. Scarcity can justify triage, but not blind abandonment. Where full provision is impossible, righteousness requires public reasons, equal respect, priority to urgent need and the worst off, special concern for those whose service protects others, and visible answerability for any unmet remainder.**[S163][S164][S165][S166][S167][S168][S169]

## 1. Righteousness has a floor problem, not only a fairness problem

The ICESCR does not talk as though food, housing, health, and subsistence were optional extras. It recognizes a right to an adequate standard of living including food, clothing, and housing, names freedom from hunger as fundamental, and connects health protection to conditions assuring medical service in sickness.[S164] The same treaty also says that no people may be deprived of their means of subsistence and that realization must proceed using maximum available resources without discrimination.[S164]

That matters for righteousness because a theory can sound formally fair while still letting persons drop below the floor needed to stand, deliberate, refuse, heal, or remain present as claim-bearers at all. A regime that protects procedure while normalizing hunger, exposure, and untreated basic need is not merely incomplete. Its standing is damaged at the point where the claim to right order is separated from the material preconditions of life in dignity.[S164][S165][S166][S167][S168][S169]

## 2. The floor is about reasonable access and dignity, not luxury or maximal provision

The archive should avoid a false choice between luxury egalitarianism and abandonment. Social-minimum and needs literatures are useful here because they distinguish a minimally decent floor from total equalization and emphasize **reasonable access** rather than fantasy guarantees of every desired good.[S47][S169] OHCHR housing materials make the same point in practice: the demand is not that every person be handed an ideal home immediately, but that everyone have access to safe, adequate, affordable basic shelter or housing and not be left in homelessness as a standing condition.[S167]

So the archive now states the floor more carefully. Righteousness does **not** require that every actor solve poverty in the large. It does require that no one claiming righteousness treat another person's lack of food, safe water, basic shelter, or essential care as morally negligible when that lack is foreseeable, severe, and answerable to the claimant's own conduct, office, institution, or distributional choice.[S164][S165][S166][S167][S168][S169]

## 3. Scarcity requires triage, but triage is not a permission slip for favoritism or neglect

WHO scarcity guidance is morally useful because it refuses both sentimentalism and brute power. It treats tragic allocation as ethically governable and identifies a compact family of relevant considerations: equality, best outcomes, priority to the worst off, and priority to those tasked with helping others.[S163] It also insists that scarce-resource allocation be run through fair process — transparency, inclusiveness, consistency, and accountability — rather than through hidden ranking, insider access, or improvisation by the already powerful.[S163]

This fits the archive's broader discipline. Need claims are weighty, but not all need claims are identical. When resources are genuinely insufficient, righteousness may have to rank urgency, expected benefit, exposure, dependency, and role-based protection burdens. Yet it still fails if first-come distribution merely tracks privilege, if the poor are asked to absorb the remainder of planning failure, or if scarcity rhetoric is used to hide stockpiling, corruption, self-protection, or selective rescue.[S34][S41][S47][S49][S163]

## 4. Hunger, thirst, exposure, and homelessness may not be redescribed as private problems when office is doing the burdening

The archive already rejected the move of treating public office as innocent whenever markets, families, private charity, or formal opportunity exist in the background. The subsistence floor sharpens that point. OHCHR materials on food define the right in terms of regular and unrestricted access and add that States retain a core obligation to mitigate and alleviate hunger even in natural or other disasters.[S165] OHCHR materials on water stress accessibility, affordability, and priority to those most in need.[S166] Housing materials say that preventing and eliminating homelessness is a minimum-core obligation and that significant deprivation of essential foodstuffs, essential primary healthcare, or basic shelter and housing is a prima facie failure under the Covenant.[S167]

So righteous office should resist several familiar evasions:
1. **market laundering** — "they were free to buy what they needed" when wages, prices, or exclusion made that impossible in practice;
2. **queue laundering** — "they were still in process" when delay predictably meant hunger, exposure, or untreated deterioration;
3. **family laundering** — "their relatives should have helped" when the office itself controlled the decisive channel; and
4. **temporary-shelter laundering** — treating emergency bunking, warehousing, or prolonged encampment as a morally sufficient answer to the claim for dignified and stable access.[S34][S35][S41][S49][S165][S166][S167]

## 5. Righteous response protects the floor first, but still stays answerable about means

Once the floor is in view, some familiar archive pressures intensify rather than disappear. Anti-domination matters because people without food, water, shelter, or essential care are especially vulnerable to coercive bargains.[S49][S70][S71] Anti-exploitation matters because desperate assent is not clean legitimating consent.[S81][S82][S83][S84] Anti-attrition matters because late relief can be relief that arrives after the decisive harm.[S34][S111][S112][S115] Accommodation matters because the route to food, water, housing, or care can be blocked by inaccessible forms, schedules, language, transport, or disability barriers even when a benefit formally exists.[S66][S67][S143][S145][S146][S147]

The floor therefore does not replace the rest of the archive. It concentrates it. Righteous provision must still be non-humiliating, non-corrupt, non-pretextual, and publicly answerable. Bread given in a way that humiliates, shelters run as degrading captivity, water access conditioned on ideological loyalty, or housing relief traded for silence do not become righteous merely because a floor good is present somewhere in the scene.[S19][S32][S34][S49][S54][S93][S167]

## 6. Temporary emergency response is often necessary, but it is not the same as a righteous settlement

Housing materials make this point with unusual clarity: emergency shelter can be a sufficient immediate response to sudden housing loss, but it fails human-rights obligations if it becomes a long-term substitute for adequate housing.[S167] The same structure generalizes. Temporary rations, bottled water, cots, warming centers, emergency clinics, and crisis cash may be righteous first responses. They are not self-justifying end states. Righteousness remains burdened when a claimant quietly normalizes emergency patching as a standing mode of governing the vulnerable.[S34][S159][S162][S165][S166][S167]

So the archive now distinguishes:
- immediate floor protection;
- medium-term stabilization that prevents repeated collapse below the floor; and
- long-term institutional design that stops predictable deprivation from being reproduced as a background feature of the order itself.[S163][S165][S166][S167][S168][S169]

## 7. Working rule

When righteousness is claimed for a budget choice, rationing plan, service cutoff, shelter regime, anti-homelessness policy, benefit condition, or emergency allocation, ask seven questions:
1. **What floor good was at stake?** Food, safe water, basic shelter or housing, essential care, or another condition without which life in dignity was collapsing?[S164][S165][S166][S167]
2. **Was the claimant protecting actual access or merely formal entitlement?** Could affected persons realistically obtain the good, or only in theory?[S165][S166][S167][S169]
3. **Was scarcity real, and if so how was it ranked?** Equality, urgent need, expected benefit, and protection of those helping others may all matter; favoritism and insider capture do not.[S163]
4. **Who was bearing the remainder?** Were the already vulnerable absorbing hunger, thirst, exposure, untreated illness, or displacement because the stronger party found that cheaper or politically easier?[S34][S41][S47][S49][S167]
5. **Was the response degrading or dependency-producing?** Emergency provision that humiliates, coerces, surveils, or indefinitely warehouses can still fail righteousness.[S34][S49][S54][S93][S167]
6. **What public reasons and review survived?** Were the criteria, records, challenge paths, and responsible decision-makers visible enough for later answerability?[S18][S34][S35][S163]
7. **What longer repair remained live?** Did the actor treat emergency mitigation as a bridge toward stable access, or as a permanent excuse for governing below the floor?[S163][S165][S166][S167]

## Compression sentence

> **Treat subsistence-floor claims as heavy but not magical: righteous action protects reasonable access to the essentials of life in dignity, triages scarcity by public reasons rather than privilege, and refuses to rename predictable hunger, thirst, exposure, or homelessness as mere background, market residue, or somebody else's problem.**[S163][S164][S165][S166][S167][S168][S169]
