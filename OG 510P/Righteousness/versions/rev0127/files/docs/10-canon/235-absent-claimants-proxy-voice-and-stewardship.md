# Absent Claimants, Proxy Voice, and Stewardship

## Why this surface is now needed

The archive now has a rule against materially burdening people over their heads. But that rule still left a harder case underdescribed:

> **what if the relevant claimant cannot directly appear, speak, organize, consent, or even yet exist?**

Children, some persons who need support to exercise agency, diffuse publics, and future persons all generate this pressure in different ways. If the archive says only “hear the burdened,” it risks a bad simplification. Some burdened parties can and should be heard directly. Others can only be made present through support, representatives, guardians, trustees, or stewardship structures.

Without a compact rule here, the archive risks two opposite failures:
- treating absence as if it erased claim;
- or letting the proxy simply overwrite the claimant and then speak in the claimant's name without discipline.

## Working-canon thesis

> **When those materially burdened cannot directly appear, righteousness does not treat their absence as no claim. It requires the least substitutive and most accountable form of presence the case allows: support for direct agency where possible; conflict-screened proxy or representative voice where necessary; and stewardship for diffuse or future claimants that is explicit about uncertainty, protective against irreversible extraction, and open to later review or reopening.**[S45][S63][S65][S66][S67]

This is not a single institutional design. It is a portable rule against disappearance-by-absence.

## 1. Absence does not erase claim

The archive now states this more directly: a claimant need not be currently organized, articulate, empowered, or contemporaneously present in order to matter. The affected-standing surface already implied this. Here the archive makes it explicit.

Children are not morally absent merely because they are not fully adult decision-makers. The Convention on the Rights of the Child is useful because it gives the child a right to express views in matters affecting the child and to be heard directly or through a representative, with due weight given to age and maturity.[S66]

Likewise, persons with disabilities are not to be treated as objects whose claims vanish into administrative or familial management. The Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities is useful because it frames persons with disabilities as subjects with rights, requires support in exercising legal capacity, and insists on safeguards that respect rights, will, and preferences rather than simply replacing the person with a manager.[S67]

Future persons intensify the problem further. They cannot now speak back, vote, litigate, or organize. But intergenerational-justice theory is still useful because it argues that present people can stand under duties of justice to future people even though those future people are contingent upon present action and cannot reciprocally affect us now.[S65]

## 2. Support before substitution

The archive now adopts a first ordering rule:

> **where direct agency can be supported, support it before replacing it.**

This rule compresses the child-hearing and disability materials well. Sometimes the right response is not full substitution but accommodation, translation, supported decision-making, age-appropriate hearing, procedural help, or a slower and more legible channel of participation.[S66][S67]

That matters because righteousness is not only about getting the right outcome *for* someone. It is also about not needlessly converting a rights-bearer into an object of management.

## 3. When proxy voice is necessary

Sometimes support is not enough. A guardian, advocate, parent, trustee, ombudsperson, class representative, or public-interest proxy may still be needed.

The archive now treats righteous proxy voice as requiring at least five marks:

### A. The proxy role is named rather than disguised

Say whether this is advocacy, guardianship, substituted judgment, best-interest protection, trusteeship, stewardship for future persons, or another structure. Do not hide power behind generic claims to care.

### B. Conflict is screened rather than presumed away

The proxy should not be treated as self-validating. Family position, expertise, office, or benevolent motive do not remove the risk that the proxy is carrying convenience, ideology, institutional interest, or self-protection rather than the claimant's due.[S45][S67]

### C. Will and perspective are carried where knowable

Where the claimant's own will, preferences, testimony, values, or developing capacities can be known, righteous proxy structure should carry them rather than overwrite them with an outsider's idealized picture of the good.[S66][S67]

### D. Review remains possible

The more substitutive the proxy, the stronger the need for record, periodic review, narrow tailoring, and reopening if the claimant later becomes able to appear more directly or if the proxy arrangement itself produced distortion.[S34][S67]

### E. The proxy does not claim full identity with the absent claimant

This matters especially for future and diffuse claimants. Righteous stewardship does not say “I simply speak as future people would speak.” It says something thinner and more disciplined: some burdens are hard to reverse, some extractions are imposed on people who cannot now contest them, and present actors therefore owe restraint, preservation, and protective margin rather than opportunistic consumption.[S41][S65]

## 4. Stewardship for future and diffuse claimants

The hardest cases are those with no determinate present speaker: future residents, unborn persons, long-tail publics, or people downstream from a policy who are not yet organized.

The archive now treats these cases under a **stewardship** rather than ventriloquism rule.

That means at least four things:
- do not infer that lack of present voice means lack of valid claim;
- be explicit about uncertainty regarding future preferences or diffuse interests;
- give more weight to irreversible losses, locked-in exposures, and hard-to-repair depletion than to present convenience or extraction;
- preserve review, revision, sunset, and later reopening rather than acting as if present power has already spoken for all who come later.[S41][S65]

This rule fits the archive better than either romantic “speaking for the voiceless” rhetoric or skeptical dismissal of absent claimants altogether.

## 5. What this changes in verdicts

This surface gives the archive better verdict language for hard cases. More exact descriptions now include:
- **protective but over-substitutive**;
- **representative in form, conflict-tainted in practice**;
- **supportive of agency rather than paternalistically replacing it**;
- **future-aware and stewardship-disciplined**;
- **presently beneficial but future-extractive**.

These are better than forcing every such case into either “righteous” or “unrighteous” without saying how absence was handled.

## Compressed rule

> **Do not treat absent claimants as absent claims. Support direct agency where possible; where proxy voice is necessary, make it conflict-screened, reviewable, and explicit about its limits; where the claimants are future or diffuse, practice stewardship rather than self-authorized ventriloquism.**[S45][S63][S65][S66][S67]
