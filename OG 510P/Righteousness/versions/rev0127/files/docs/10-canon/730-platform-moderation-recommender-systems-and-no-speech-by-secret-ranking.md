# Platform moderation, recommender systems, and no-speech-by-secret-ranking

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when platforms, app stores, marketplaces, hosting services, or similar gatekeepers make visibility, reach, or account standing depend on opaque moderation decisions, secret ranking or demotion, profiling-based recommendation, or weakly reachable redress such that public voice is practically governed by hidden curation rather than clear rules, stated reasons, user choice, and contestable review.**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S127][S219][S492][S493][S494][S495]

## 1. The missing moral question is not only whether speech is still technically possible, but whether public voice must pass through secret ranking

The archive already rejected no-silencing-by-power, no-rightlessness-by-model, no-agency-by-stealth, and no-relation-by-simulation. But it still lacked a direct rule for a now-common defect: **a person may still be allowed to post, speak, or keep an account, while the practical visibility, reach, or credibility of that speech is being governed through opaque moderation decisions, quiet demotion, profiling-based feeds, or appeal systems too thin to answer the burden they impose.**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S127][S219][S492][S493][S494][S495]

That gap matters because platform actors rarely describe themselves as governing speech. They say they are enforcing terms, reducing spam, improving relevance, protecting users, personalizing feeds, or preserving trust and safety. Some of that can be legitimate. But righteousness has to ask a narrower question: **what actually did the practical work of deciding who could be heard, found, restored, or effectively buried — a clear and contestable rule, or a curation system whose reasons, ranking logic, and review paths stayed too hidden for affected people to answer?**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S127][S219][S492][S493][S494][S495]

So the archive now needs a canon surface that is not reducible either to classic state censorship or to generic algorithmic opacity. No-speech-by-secret-ranking asks when dominant digital intermediaries become standing-defective because they quietly decide practical public voice through explanation-thin moderation and visibility control rather than through rules and review that affected people can genuinely understand and contest.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S127][S219][S492][S493][S494][S495]

## 2. Current official materials now name this family clearly enough to justify canon admission

Current European Commission materials are morally revealing because the DSA transparency page says hosting services must inform users whenever they remove or otherwise restrict access to content and must explain the reasons in a **statement of reasons** containing clear and specific information and a legal or terms-of-service reference, while the Commission's DSA Transparency Database makes those reasons publicly scrutinisable almost in real time.[S492] The Commission's current user-rights page sharpens the structure further by saying platforms must explain moderation decisions clearly to affected users, users can challenge decisions through internal complaint systems, can turn to independent dispute-settlement bodies, and on recommendation systems must be told the main parameters used while very large platforms must offer at least one option not based on profiling.[S493]

The Commission's February 2026 DSA update makes the practical stakes visible: in two years, platforms reversed almost 50 million decisions affecting users' content or accounts; 30% of 165 million appealed moderation decisions had been reversed; and in the first half of 2025, 99% of moderation decisions were taken under platforms' own terms and conditions rather than illegal-content law.[S494] Commission materials on out-of-court dispute settlement add that users may seek review from certified bodies, usually free or low cost, while still retaining court access.[S495]

Together these materials show that no-speech-by-secret-ranking is not a speculative worry but an active governance boundary around how platform moderation, ranking, explanation, and redress may and may not operate.[S492][S493][S494][S495]

## 3. The righteousness issue is not any moderation or ranking as such; it is making hidden curation the practical title of public voice

Righteousness should not treat every removal, spam filter, age gate, recommender, downranking choice, or safety intervention as automatically unrighteous. Platforms and comparable intermediaries can have legitimate reasons to remove illegal content, limit harassment, reduce fraud, constrain impersonation, and offer usable curation or chronological alternatives.[S493][S494][S495] The narrower defect appears where moderation and ranking cease to be bounded governance aids and become the unanswerable infrastructure by which ordinary people are heard or buried.

That defect is clearest where the decisive act is not an open ban but a quiet restriction: reach inexplicably drops, a post becomes hard to find, an account is limited under vague terms, an appeal stalls, or profiling-based feeds decide that one speaker is practically invisible unless they fit the system's optimization logic. In those cases the wrong is not simply that software arranged a feed. It is that a person's practical standing in a major speech environment has been made to pass through no-speech-by-secret-ranking.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S127][S219][S492][S493][S494][S495]

That matters across social platforms, creator systems, marketplaces where seller visibility functions like speech standing, app stores, review systems, community forums, video sites, and other quasi-public digital spaces where being technically allowed to speak is not enough if discoverability, distribution, and restoration are governed by explanation-thin curation.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S127][S219][S492][S493][S494][S495]

## 4. Questions righteous judgment should now ask

When an actor claims righteousness while relying on moderation, ranking, or feed design that affects visibility or account standing, ask seven questions:

1. **What concrete rule or risk justified the intervention?** Was the action actually tied to illegality, safety, fraud, harassment, manipulation, or another bounded concern, or mainly to vague convenience, reputation management, or engagement optimization?[S493][S494][S495]
2. **What exactly happened to the person's practical voice?** Was content removed, reach reduced, an account restricted, recommendations altered, monetization cut, searchability impaired, or all of these together?[S492][S493][S494]
3. **Were reasons clear enough to answer?** Did the person get a statement specific enough to understand what rule, fact pattern, or system logic actually did the work?[S492][S493]
4. **What role did ranking or profiling play?** Did recommender parameters, personalization, or demotion settings substantially determine whether the speech was findable or amplified, and was a non-profiling route available where required?[S493][S494]
5. **Was review real and reachable?** Could the user use internal complaints, independent dispute settlement, or courts without prohibitive delay, opacity, or cost?[S493][S495]
6. **Who bore the heavier burden in practice?** Were journalists, whistleblowers, minority speakers, small creators, dissidents, workers, political actors, or dependent sellers especially exposed because platform visibility is already part of their livelihood or public standing?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S127][S219][S494]
7. **Was secret ranking doing the real work?** If opaque demotion, profiling-based recommendation, or explanation-thin moderation were removed, would the practical visibility outcome still have followed from a clear and contestable rule?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S127][S219][S492][S493][S494][S495]

## 5. Canonical compression

> **Do not let speech standing be governed by secret ranking.** Platforms may moderate and curate for bounded reasons, but righteousness is heavily burdened when visibility, reach, or account standing are made to depend on opaque moderation, hidden demotion, profiling-based recommendation, or weak redress rather than stated reasons, user choice, and contestable review.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S127][S219][S492][S493][S494][S495]
