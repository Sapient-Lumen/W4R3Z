# Affected Standing, Hearing, and Accountable Representation

## Why this surface now

The archive already has contestability, uncertainty discipline, delegation discipline, public-trust discipline, and bearer / competence / authorization discipline.

What it still lacked was a compact rule for a different question:

> **when public power materially burdens people, how must those burdened be made present in the judgment?**

Without such a rule, the archive risks a recurring mistake. It may say that a decision was reviewable, reason-giving, or well-intentioned while leaving the most burdened parties functionally absent from the process that named, allocated, or justified their burden.

That is too thin a view of righteous public action.

## Working-canon thesis

> **Where public power materially burdens identifiable people or communities, righteousness normally requires a domain-fit way of making the burdened present in judgment: direct hearing, meaningful participatory channel, accountable representation, or — where urgency, scale, or incapacity blocks those — a burdened substitute that remains open to later review, challenge, and if needed repair. Decisions taken over their heads are presumptively standing-defective even when benevolent in aim.**[S35][S40][S45][S63][S64]

This is not a demand for plebiscite on every act. It is a rule about **affected standing**.

## 1. Contestability is not enough if the burdened are absent

A process may be formal, reviewable, and even publicly reason-giving while still wronging people by treating them mainly as objects of administration. Public justification is therefore not only a matter of reasons being stated in public language. It is also a matter of whether those who bear the burden were in some serious way **made present** to the judgment.[S35][S45][S63]

The archive now treats this as distinct from generic transparency. A dashboard, statement, or later report may make power more visible without yet making the burdened party present in the decision that mattered.

## 2. Presence does not always mean direct consent

The archive does **not** infer that righteousness requires unanimous consent, universal direct democracy, or face-to-face hearing in every case.

Different domains rightly use different structures:
- direct hearing for adjudication, accusation, exclusion, or individualized burden;
- notice-and-comment, consultation, or participatory channels for broader policy design;
- accountable representation where scale, complexity, or durable institutions make direct participation unrealistic;
- advocacy, guardianship, or protected surrogate voice where those burdened cannot safely or competently appear on their own behalf.[S34][S45][S63][S64]

The rule is therefore not “everyone decides everything.” The rule is narrower:

> **do not call a materially burdening public act righteous if the burdened were simply ruled over without serious uptake, accountable representation, or a justified substitute.**

## 3. Who must be made present

The archive now gives special attention to those who are:
- directly burdened, judged, excluded, displaced, or exposed;
- heavily dependent on the decision-maker or unable to absorb the loss easily;
- structurally easy to ignore because they lack status, resources, or ordinary political access;
- materially affected without being naturally inside the formal jurisdiction or the dominant constituency.[S40][S45][S63]

This matters because democratic and representative theory both warn that formal representation or voting structure can still leave some affected parties effectively absent, especially permanent minorities, disaffected populations, and groups whose perspective is predictably filtered out before reasons are even counted.[S45][S63]

## 4. What counts as accountable presence

The archive now treats four marks as especially important.

### A. Real room for uptake

Participation should happen where there is actual room for it to shape framing, evidence, options, mitigation, or repair rather than functioning only as ceremony.[S64]

### B. Fit between method and burden

The heavier, more targeted, or harder-to-reverse the burden, the stronger the case for direct hearing, individualized notice, or adversarial testing rather than diffuse or purely symbolic participation.[S34][S35]

### C. Representation that can answer back

Where direct presence is impossible, representation should be traceable, answerable, and structured to carry the perspective of those burdened rather than merely using them as a rhetorical object.[S45]

### D. Record of what was heard and what followed

Righteousness does not require that every burdened party gets their preferred outcome. But it does require that the process can show what was raised, what was taken up, what was refused, and why.[S34][S64]

## 5. Emergency, scale, and incapacity raise the burden

Sometimes direct participation really is blocked by tempo, secrecy, dispersed scale, or incapacity. The archive does not deny this.

But those conditions do **not** erase affected standing. They shift the burden into substitute safeguards such as:
- emergency narrowing and sunset;
- temporary surrogate advocacy or guardianship;
- representative sampling or accountable proxy structures;
- preserved record for later challenge;
- post hoc explanation, reopening, and repair if the rushed or closed process wronged those burdened.[S34][S35][S64]

This keeps the archive from two opposite errors:
- naive participationism, which pretends every domain can operate by immediate assembly;
- benevolent over-the-head rule, which treats urgency or scale as a solvent of answerability.

## 6. What this changes in verdicts

This surface gives the archive better verdicts for public action. More exact descriptions now include:
- **substantively plausible but over-the-head**;
- **consultative but uptake-defective**;
- **representatively structured but still exclusion-prone**;
- **temporarily closed yet later answerable and repair-capable**.

These are better than treating all such cases as either righteous or simply invalid.

## Compressed rule

> **No righteous burdening over the heads of the burdened without hearing, accountable representation, or an emergency-burdened substitute that stays answerable to later review and repair.**
