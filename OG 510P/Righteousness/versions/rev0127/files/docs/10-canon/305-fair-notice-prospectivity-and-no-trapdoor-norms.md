# Fair Notice, Prospectivity, and No Trapdoor Norms

## Why this surface matters

The archive already has contestability, pretext, manufactured guilt, anti-reprisal, and anti-domination. What it still lacked was a dedicated surface on **what happens when the standard itself is hidden, retroactive, or too vague to steer by**.[S18][S32][S34][S35][S34][S109][S110][S93]

That gap matters because two opposite mistakes are common:
- **trapdoor normativity** — treating accusation, discipline, exclusion, or penalty as righteous even though the burdened party could only discover the operative standard after already failing it; and
- **notice absolutism** — treating every open-textured standard, discretionary judgment, or evolving norm as automatically illegitimate, even where the rule was publicly signaled, prospectively applied, and kept reviewable enough for ordinary agency.[S18][S34][S35][S34][S109][S110][S93]

## Working-canon thesis

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when an actor, office, or institution imposes serious accusation, discipline, exclusion, or penalty through hidden, retroactive, or unduly vague norms that deny ordinary people a fair chance to know, contest, or conform beforehand. Prospectivity, promulgation, and sufficient clarity matter not only to legality but to governing through agency rather than surprise.**[S18][S32][S34][S35][S34][S109][S110][S93]

This is not a mechanical-code rule. It is an **anti-trapdoor rule**.

## 1. Prospectivity is respect for agency, not mere tidiness

Rule-of-law materials are morally revealing because they connect law's formal virtues to how persons are addressed. Fuller famously treated generality, publicity, prospectivity, clarity, stability, coherence, and practicability as part of law's inner morality, and the *Rule of Law* literature preserves the key point: governance that works through knowable standards treats persons as agents who can plan, orient, and answer for what they do, rather than as targets of ad hoc command or retrospective surprise.[S34]

The archive should borrow that portable lesson without collapsing righteousness into legal philosophy. A righteousness claim is weakened when those burdened were not being addressed as planners at all — when the operative standard arrived late, remained hidden, or stayed so indeterminate that compliance had to depend on guessing what the authority would later say it meant.[S34][S34][S110]

## 2. Retroactive burden is a standing defect

The basic anti-retroactivity point is straightforward. Constitution Annotated materials on the Ex Post Facto Clauses stress that retroactive punishment is suspect precisely because people must have notice of the possible criminal consequences of their actions at the time they act.[S109] ICCPR Article 15 states the point in international-rights form: no one should be held guilty for conduct that was not criminal when committed, and no heavier penalty should later be imposed than the one then applicable.[S93]

The archive should generalize the moral shape beyond criminal law with care. Not every later reinterpretation, clarified guidance, or remedial redesign is unrighteous. But a righteousness claim is burdened where power waits until after the act, then changes the governing standard, intensifies the penalty, or discovers a previously unstated expectation in order to make the burden look deserved after all.[S18][S32][S109][S93]

## 3. Vagueness invites trap and selective enforcement

Fair notice is not only about publication. A rule can be public and still too vague to steer by. Constitution Annotated vagueness materials are useful because they state two portable reasons for concern: people of ordinary intelligence need a reasonable opportunity to know what is prohibited, and explicit standards matter because otherwise enforcement becomes ad hoc, arbitrary, or discriminatory.[S110]

That is why this surface belongs near equal burden, pretext, and anti-domination. Vague standards are dangerous not only because they confuse; they also let the strong decide later whose conduct counts as “disruptive,” “unsafe,” “disloyal,” “unprofessional,” “not a fit,” or “against the spirit” of the rule. A standard that becomes sharp only when applied to the disfavored is often not righteous administration of a rule but discretionary domination concealed inside underdefined language.[S19][S30][S32][S34][S35][S95][S110]

## 4. Office may not govern by moving target or secret test

Public office is the clearest stress test. Offices routinely use standards that are not mathematically precise: judgment, fitness, reasonableness, safety, suitability, conflict risk, reliability. The archive does **not** say that righteous office must reduce all of these to exhaustive checklists. But it does now say that office owes enough public guidance, prospective warning, comparator discipline, and reviewability that ordinary people are not governed by trapdoor criteria.

So office should be suspicious of familiar moves:
- treating unwritten expectation as decisive only after disfavored conduct occurs;
- escalating penalty after the act in a way the actor could not reasonably have anticipated;
- invoking standards like “team fit,” “professionalism,” “security concern,” or “policy spirit” only when a target is weak, inconvenient, or newly oppositional;
- or using secrecy, emergency, or managerial discretion to make the applicable rule legible only to insiders.[S18][S32][S34][S35][S104][S34][S109][S110]

Where that pattern appears, the problem is not only thin drafting. It is that office has stopped governing through public terms and started governing through after-the-fact power.

## 5. Working rule

When a righteousness claim depends on a rule, standard, or expectation, ask six questions:
1. **Was the operative standard public enough before the conduct?** Publication, briefing, notice, training, and settled practice all matter.[S34][S34][S110]
2. **Was it prospective?** Or was the standard, interpretation, or penalty changed after the act in order to make condemnation easier?[S109][S93]
3. **Was it clear enough for ordinary people in that role to steer by?** Precision requirements vary, but pure guesswork is not righteous governance.[S34][S110]
4. **Did the standard constrain like cases?** Or did it become sharp only when applied to the weak, the dissenting, or the expendable?[S30][S34][S35][S95][S110]
5. **What review, warning, or repair existed where ambiguity was unavoidable?** Open-textured standards demand stronger explanation, recorded reasons, and revisability rather than blind deference.[S18][S34][S35][S34]
6. **What made the burden proportionate despite any notice defect?** Emergency, ongoing danger, or role-specific expertise may sometimes justify interim action, but they raise rather than erase the burden of later explanation, narrowing, and repair.[S34][S40][S41][S34][S93]

## Compression sentence

> **Do not call a judgment righteous when the person burdened could only learn the real rule by already violating it: hidden standards, retroactive penalties, and strategically vague norms are trapdoor governance, not clean due-rendering.**[S18][S32][S34][S35][S34][S109][S110][S93]
