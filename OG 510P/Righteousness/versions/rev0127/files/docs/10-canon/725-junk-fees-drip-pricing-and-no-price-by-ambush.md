# Junk fees, drip pricing, and no-price-by-ambush

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when sellers, platforms, intermediaries, or offices make assent depend on hidden mandatory charges, late-stage total-price revelation, preselected paid add-ons, or other price-ambush techniques such that sunk attention and purchase momentum do the real work of agreement rather than leaving payment governed by clear upfront total price, explicit opt-in for extras, and an intelligible chance to compare or walk away.**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S462][S489][S490][S491]

## 1. The missing moral question is not only what the advertised price was, but whether assent had to pass through ambush

The archive already rejected no-obligation-by-friction and no-price-by-profile. But it still lacked a direct rule for a different and common defect: **a price may be posted, searchable, or even technically disclosed somewhere, while the practical agreement is only secured after a person has invested time, chosen seats, entered personal details, committed to travel dates, or otherwise become purchase-committed enough that late mandatory charges become hard to resist.**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S462][S489][S490][S491]

That gap matters because price ambush is often laundered as normal commerce. The fee was always in the terms. The charge is standard in the industry. The person could still abandon the cart. The total appeared before final payment. The add-on was technically optional. But righteousness has to ask a narrower question: **what actually did the practical work of getting assent — a clear and comparable total price, or a sequence that turned sunk attention, urgency, or fatigue into the leverage by which hidden or late charges were accepted?**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S462][S489][S490][S491]

So the archive now needs a canon surface that is not reducible either to generic deception or to profile-based price variation. No-price-by-ambush asks when ticketing, travel, lodging, marketplaces, utilities, event access, service contracting, or similar systems become standing-defective because total price is obscured until the moment when the weaker party is most purchase-committed.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S462][S489][S490][S491]

## 2. Current official materials now name this family clearly enough to justify canon admission

Current FTC materials are morally revealing because the Commission's 2025 rule materials say the Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees prohibits bait-and-switch pricing and other tactics used to hide total prices and mislead people about fees in the live-event ticketing and short-term lodging industries, while preserving the ability to charge fees so long as businesses tell consumers the whole truth up-front about total prices and fees.[S489] The FTC's current FAQ sharpens the structure further by requiring businesses that advertise prices in those sectors to disclose the **total price upfront**, including all charges or fees they know about and can calculate upfront for mandatory goods or services, while allowing only limited later disclosure for taxes, shipping, and optional add-ons before payment is requested.[S490]

Current EU consumer materials widen the same point beyond one sector. Your Europe says consumers must be clearly informed about the **total price, including all taxes and additional charges**, that traders cannot charge for additional services unless the consumer explicitly selected them, and that for travel tickets all taxes, fees, and charges must be included and appear in the total price from the beginning of the booking process.[S491] OECD dark-commercial-patterns materials add the behavioural governance layer by treating hidden costs and drip pricing as paradigmatic techniques for getting people to spend more than they otherwise intended.[S462]

Together these materials show that no-price-by-ambush is not a speculative worry but an active official boundary around how price information, mandatory charges, and add-ons may and may not operate.[S462][S489][S490][S491]

## 3. The righteousness issue is not charging fees as such; it is making hidden or late-stage charges the practical title of agreement

Righteousness should not treat every surcharge, tax, shipping cost, optional add-on, or sector-specific fee as automatically unrighteous. Some differentiated charges can be justified: taxes, shipping, selected extras, or optional protection products can be legitimate where they are actually optional, clearly identified, and disclosed early enough to preserve meaningful comparison and refusal.[S489][S490][S491]

The narrower defect appears where mandatory charges are withheld until late in the transaction, where a low anchor price does the searching and comparison work while the real price appears only after commitment deepens, or where preselected extras and layered fee screens make abandonment costlier than acquiescence. In those cases the wrong is not simply that money is charged. It is that payment has been made to pass through no-price-by-ambush: the person's sunk time, narrowed alternatives, or desire to complete the purchase becomes the leverage that others built and control.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S462][S489][S490][S491]

That matters across concerts, sports, hotels, vacation rentals, flights, rail, delivery, event access, marketplace checkout, utilities sign-up, education fees, and any other domain where a nominal price can be made to do the search-stage work while a different total arrives only once the person has invested enough to feel locked in. Where that architecture does the real work of agreement, righteousness is often not merely setting a price. It is governing through no-price-by-ambush.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S462][S489][S490][S491]

## 4. Questions righteous judgment should now ask

When an actor claims righteousness while relying on fees, add-ons, surcharges, or staged price revelation, ask seven questions:

1. **Was the total price really clear early enough?** Did the person see the full mandatory price in time to compare alternatives before becoming purchase-committed?[S489][S490][S491]
2. **Which charges were actually mandatory?** Were taxes, shipping, and truly optional extras distinguished from fees that had to be paid as part of the same transaction?[S489][S490][S491]
3. **Were add-ons really optional and opt-in?** Or were they preselected, bundled, or framed so that extra payment rode on inertia rather than explicit choice?[S490][S491]
4. **What sunk-cost or urgency pressure had already accumulated?** Had the system arranged seat selection, itinerary commitment, account creation, form completion, or scarcity cues so that walking away became materially harder before the true price was visible?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S462][S489][S490][S491]
5. **Could the person still compare or exit without serious penalty?** Did the architecture preserve a realistic path to back out, compare, or restart elsewhere once the real price was known?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S462][S489][S490][S491]
6. **What exactly was the fee doing?** Was it paying for a genuinely separable optional service, or was it mainly a late-revealed mandatory charge that should have been part of the posted total?[S489][S490][S491]
7. **Was the ambush doing the real work?** If hidden mandatory charges, late total-price revelation, and preselected extras were removed, would the agreement still have been secured mainly by a clear total price rather than by purchase momentum?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S462][S489][S490][S491]

## 5. Canonical compression

> **Do not let posted base prices, late checkout disclosures, industry-standard fees, or nominally optional extras masquerade as righteous pricing: where hidden mandatory charges, drip pricing, or preselected paid add-ons do the real work of securing assent, righteousness fails unless total price is clear upfront, extras are genuinely opt-in, comparison remains meaningful, and late-stage momentum is not used as the practical title of payment.**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S462][S489][S490][S491]
