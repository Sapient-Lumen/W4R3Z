# Legal Capacity, Supported Decision-Making, and No-Civil-Death-by-Guardianship

## Why this surface matters

The archive already rejects inaccessible compliance, ranked standing, custody-as-title, over-the-head rule, benevolent mastery, no-rightlessness-by-paper, and no-rightlessness-by-model. What it still lacked was a compact rule for **what happens when a person is treated as legally present but decisionally absent** — allowed to exist in the system while another actor, office, clinician, judge, platform, or family member effectively speaks *as* the person across money, care, voting, family life, contracts, litigation, housing, or movement.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S63][S64][S66][S67][S145][S146][S147][S153][S154][S232][S233][S256][S257][S258][S259][S260]

That gap matters because unrighteousness here is often laundered through the language of protection. Guardianship, wardship, representative control, mental-capacity screening, best-interest substitution, or administrative management may be described as care, efficiency, anti-fraud protection, or clinical necessity while they are actually doing something more basic: thinning a person's standing to decide, refuse, authorize, marry, parent, vote, litigate, bank, contract, or direct care in their own name. Once that happens, civil life can continue around the person while the person is no longer fully there as a claimant. The archive needed a cleaner rule against that slide.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S45][S63][S64][S66][S67][S93][S145][S146][S147][S153][S154][S232][S233][S256][S257][S258][S259][S260]

## Working-canon thesis

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when a person is stripped in law or practice of the ability to decide, authorize, refuse, hold property, litigate, vote, marry, parent, contract, direct care, or otherwise stand in civic life in their own name because others find it easier, safer, tidier, or more reassuring to govern through guardianship, substituted judgment, incapacity labels, or support structures that override the person's rights, will, and preferences. Support may be necessary and often righteous, but it must remain support for the person's standing rather than a cleaner mechanism for replacing the person. Legal capacity is not a prize for cognitive performance.**[S67][S256][S257][S258][S259][S260]

## 1. Legal capacity is a standing question, not a credentialing exam

Article 12 of the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities matters because it states that persons with disabilities have the right to recognition everywhere as persons before the law, enjoy legal capacity on an equal basis with others in all aspects of life, must have access to support where needed in exercising that capacity, and must receive safeguards that respect rights, will, and preferences rather than merely authorize others to decide for them.[S67] General Comment No. 1 sharpens the same point by rejecting the old assumption that decision-making skill, diagnosis, or assessed mental capacity determines who counts as a full legal actor.[S256]

So the archive should now reject a familiar laundering move: because a person communicates differently, decides slowly, relies on assistance, has psychiatric or intellectual disability, is old, is in crisis, or fails an institutional competence script, the stronger party acts as though legal standing has thinned automatically. Righteousness may take vulnerability seriously without turning vulnerability into civic diminishment. The issue is not whether a person ever needs help. The issue is whether the help preserves the person's authority or quietly replaces it.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S45][S63][S64][S67][S256][S257][S258]

## 2. Support is not substitution

The official materials converge on a point the archive needs in explicit form: support in exercising legal capacity is not the same thing as substitute decision-making. General Comment No. 1 says support must respect the person's rights, will, and preferences and must not amount to substituted decision-making.[S256] The OHCHR indicators resource makes the contrast operational by treating abolition of substituted decision-making, respect for the individual's exercise of legal capacity, and remedies against restriction as structural markers rather than aspirational extras.[S258]

That matters because many institutions announce support while structuring override. A court may appoint a guardian "for protection." A bank may require a controller. A hospital may default to family or staff judgment. A benefits system may allow only managed signatures. A voting system may speak of assistance while effectively deciding who can cast a ballot or which support person may enter the booth. These may sometimes answer real practical problems. But if the supported person cannot meaningfully choose the supporter, revoke or change the arrangement, communicate by recognized means, refuse a proposed act, or trigger review when override happens, then the structure is not merely helping the person act. It is acting over the person with gentler branding.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S45][S63][S64][S67][S256][S257][S258][S259]

## 3. Guardianship can operate as civil death under a caring name

The archive already rejects rightlessness-by-paper and custody-as-title. This surface sharpens an adjacent defect: **civil death by guardianship**. A person may remain physically present yet become practically absent from the transactions through which ordinary standing is exercised. The 2017 report on legal capacity and supported decision-making is useful here because it treats legal capacity as a cross-domain issue touching personhood, property, agreements, community life, and support design rather than as a narrow exception for hard cases.[S257] The access-to-justice guidelines add the practical point that equal recognition before the law must remain usable in investigative, judicial, and other legal settings, not just declarable in abstract.[S259]

So the archive should now ask a sharper question whenever guardianship, trusteeship, representative decision, involuntary treatment authorization, financial management, or court-appointed control is invoked: what exactly could the person still do in their own name after this measure took effect? Could they choose relationships, direct care, access court, hire counsel, manage money, sign a lease, contest the arrangement, or participate in public life? If not, the system may not merely be protecting against abuse or confusion. It may be reallocating personhood itself.[S18][S19][S32][S34][S35][S41][S45][S63][S64][S67][S256][S257][S258][S259]

## 4. Legal-capacity deprivation radiates outward across civic life

This is why the archive should not treat legal capacity as an internal disability-law technicality. Once diminished standing is installed here, many other surfaces start to fail more easily: voting may be denied, court access blunted, contracts blocked, health-care refusals overridden, family roles destabilized, property controlled by others, documents effectively unusable, institutionalization normalized, and algorithmic or bureaucratic systems permitted to treat the person as administratively managed rather than self-directing.[S67][S93][S145][S146][S147][S153][S154][S259][S260]

The current 2026 report on political participation is especially useful because it identifies discriminatory legal restrictions linked to legal capacity as a persistent barrier to participation in political life.[S260] That is morally revealing for the archive. It shows that legal-capacity deprivation still functions as a status-thinning device in living public systems, not merely as a historical abuse already solved.

## 5. Questions the archive should now force

When a righteousness claim depends on guardianship, representative control, supported decision-making, involuntary substitute authorization, or incapacity language, the archive should now ask at least five things explicitly:

1. **What domain of standing was really at stake?** Voting, property, contracting, marriage, parenting, litigation, treatment, residence, documents, digital access, or something else?[S67][S256][S257][S259][S260]
2. **What made support necessary, and what made substitution unavoidable rather than merely easier for the stronger party?**[S67][S256][S257][S258]
3. **How were the person's own will, preferences, communication, and chosen supports made effective rather than ceremonially consulted?**[S67][S256][S258][S259]
4. **What review, exit, remedy, and conflict screening kept the arrangement from hardening into durable civic diminishment?**[S67][S256][S258][S259]
5. **What other rights were predictably thinned once capacity was restricted — voting, marriage, parenting, housing, court access, money control, or ordinary public participation?**[S67][S257][S259][S260]

## Compact rule

> **Do not solve difficulty, risk, disability, ageing, crisis, or administrative complexity by converting support into title over another person's civic life.** Righteousness may justify support, interpretation, slower process, accessible communication, chosen assistance, advance planning, or tightly bounded safeguards, but it is burdened once standing itself is managed through guardianship, substituted judgment, or incapacity scripts that make the person live in law mainly through somebody else's will.[S67][S256][S257][S258][S259][S260]
