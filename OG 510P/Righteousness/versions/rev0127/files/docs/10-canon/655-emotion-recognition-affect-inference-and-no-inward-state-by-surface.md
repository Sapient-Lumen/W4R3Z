# Emotion recognition, affect inference, and no-inward-state-by-surface

## Core claim

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when public or private actors treat inferred emotion, engagement, sincerity, attitude, personality, intention, or similar inward state as administratively usable fact on the basis of face, expression, or comparable surface signals, so that those inferences do the real work of screening, disciplining, sorting, persuading, or burdening persons rather than serving a tightly bounded, low-stakes, contestable, and genuinely protective purpose.**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S438][S446][S447][S448][S449]

## 1. The missing moral question is when outward traces are treated as title to interiority

The archive already rejected no-identity-by-extraction, no-rightlessness-by-model, no-orthodoxy-by-power, no-standing-by-score, and no-thought-by-extraction. But it still lacked a direct rule for a different temptation: **treating surface signals as if they authorized institutions to know and govern the inside of the person**.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49]

That gap matters because not every standing defect requires direct brain access. A school, employer, platform, insurer, or authority may instead claim to read inward state from expression, demeanor, or other outward cues and then let that inferred state decide trust, productivity, risk, teachability, sincerity, or compliance. The danger is not only mismeasurement. It is that persons become governable through officially managed guesses about what they feel, mean, or are like inside.[S438][S446][S447][S448]

## 2. Bounded safety or accessibility uses are not the same as governance through inward-state inference

The archive does **not** need to say that every system noticing fatigue, distress, or incapacity is automatically unrighteous. The AI Act's own structure is useful precisely because it does not ban everything under one label. The Commission's current materials say the prohibition targets AI systems used to infer emotions in workplace and education settings, while preserving a narrow exception for medical or safety reasons.[S446][S447]

That distinction is morally load-bearing. A narrowly bounded use genuinely aimed at immediate safety or care is one thing. A workplace engagement monitor, classroom attentiveness tool, hiring screen, customer-service tone scorer, insurance risk tool, or fraud or security workflow that makes inferred emotion or intention do the real work of discipline or access is another. The second is not simply richer evidence. It is a governing posture in which administrative power starts treating interiority as something available by surface capture.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S446][S447][S448]

## 3. Current governance materials already treat emotion inference as a red-line area

This is no longer only a philosophical complaint about overconfident institutions. The European Commission's current AI Act materials explicitly list emotion recognition in the workplace and education institutions among prohibited AI practices, except for medical or safety reasons, and the AI Act Service Desk says the prohibition covers AI systems used to infer emotions of a natural person in those settings.[S446][S447]

That matters for the archive because lawmakers are not merely saying some models need better accuracy. They are marking out a class of uses where dependency plus inward-state inference is itself morally suspect. The point travels beyond the statute: where someone's job, schooling, evaluation, or treatment depends on an institution claiming privileged access to what they feel or intend, righteousness bears a steep burden even before one asks whether the system works especially well.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S446][S447]

## 4. Council of Europe materials widen the warning from emotion to personality, mental health, and worker engagement

The Council of Europe facial-recognition materials sharpen why this deserves its own canon surface. Its guidelines frame facial recognition as a human-dignity and rights problem requiring strict safeguards, while the accompanying Council of Europe statement says affect-recognition technologies can identify emotions and be used to detect personality traits, inner feelings, mental health condition, or workers' level of engagement, and that these uses pose important risks in fields such as employment, access to insurance, and education.[S448][S449]

That widening is morally revealing. Once institutions are allowed to treat expression or similar surface cues as official evidence of inner feeling, attitude, personality, or mental condition, the administrative appetite does not stay narrow. It expands from emotion to character, from distress to fitness, from attentiveness to merit, and from demeanor to deserved standing. The archive should therefore treat no-inward-state-by-surface as a distinct standing discipline rather than as a small footnote to biometrics or model governance.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S448][S449]

## 5. Righteousness may judge conduct without pretending to own the inside of the person

The deeper moral point is portable. Offices and institutions often do need to respond to outward conduct: disruption, threat, incapacity, neglect, or actual performance may matter. But there is a large difference between judging observable conduct and claiming licensed access to inward state. The first can sometimes be necessary for public order or bounded administration. The second risks turning ambiguity, cultural variance, disability difference, stress, masking, or strategic overconfidence into official burden.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S446][S448][S449]

So the archive should reject another laundering move: because the inference is statistical, non-invasive, or presented as supportive, offices assume it is morally cleaner than direct coercion. But some uses are standing defects precisely because they make a person's feelings, sincerity, attention, or supposed interior disposition newly administrable by stronger parties.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S438][S446][S448][S449]

## 6. Working rule

When an actor claims righteousness while relying on emotion recognition or affect inference, ask seven questions:
1. **What inward state was being claimed?** Emotion, engagement, stress, intent, deception, attitude, personality trait, mental-health condition, or something comparably interior?[S448][S449]
2. **What surface was doing the work?** Facial image, expression, behavioral trace, or some other outward signal presented as evidence of the inside?[S448][S449]
3. **What relation was the system supposedly serving?** Safety, medical care, accessibility, teaching support, workplace management, hiring, discipline, customer analytics, insurance, fraud control, policing, or border control?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S446][S447][S448][S449]
4. **What made the inference contestable?** Notice, explanation, correction, refusal without penalty, appeal, human override, and a path to separate observed conduct from guessed interiority?[S438][S446][S447]
5. **What narrower route was available?** Direct communication, self-report, ordinary performance review, accommodation, case-specific evidence, or immediate safety intervention without routine inward-state scoring?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S446][S447]
6. **Who bore the interpretive risk?** Did ambiguity, masking, cultural difference, disability, trauma, stress, or nonconformity predictably get converted into suspicion or disadvantage for the burdened person?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S448][S449]
7. **Was inward-state inference doing the real work?** Did the institution quietly replace judgment about acts and bounded relations with a claimed right to classify what the person feels, means, or is like inside?[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S438][S446][S448][S449]

## Compression sentence

> **Do not let face, expression, or comparable surface signals become a routine administrative title to what a person supposedly feels, intends, or is like inside: where inward-state inference does the real work of discipline, screening, persuasion, ranking, or exclusion, righteousness fails unless the use remains tightly bounded, genuinely protective, low-stakes, contestable, and not dependency-exploitative.**[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S93][S438][S446][S447][S448][S449]
