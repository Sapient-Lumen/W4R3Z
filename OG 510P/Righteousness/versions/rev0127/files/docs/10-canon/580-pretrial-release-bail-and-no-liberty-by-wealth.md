# Pretrial release, bail, and no-liberty-by-wealth

## Core claim

> **Righteousness is burdened, and often defeated, when liberty before trial is distributed mainly by money, fixed housing, document stability, sureties, or compliance conditions that poorer and more precarious people cannot realistically satisfy, rather than by an individualized judgment of necessity and genuinely reachable alternatives to detention.**[S93][S128][S373][S374][S375][S376][S377][S378][S379]

## 1. Before conviction, liberty is the moral baseline

ICCPR article 9 matters here because it says pretrial detention should not be the general rule and that release pending trial is the presumptive structure, even though guarantees to appear may sometimes be required.[S93] General Comment No. 35 sharpens the same point by saying detention pending trial must rest on an individualized determination that it is reasonable and necessary, that vague standards should not do the work, that mandatory detention by charge type is defective, and that courts must examine alternatives such as bail or other conditions.[S373]

That matters for righteousness because pretrial procedure is one of the most common places where institutions smuggle moral judgment in before adjudication. The language is about flight risk, compliance, and court attendance. But the lived question is often simpler: who can go home while waiting, who must remain inside, and who is treated as too unstable, too poor, too homeless, too undocumented, too unsupported, or too socially disfavoured to be trusted with ordinary liberty? Once those background inequalities are doing the real sorting work, the institution is no longer merely preserving trial integrity. It is making wealth and settled circumstance into a gateway to freedom before conviction.[S34][S35][S41][S47][S49][S93][S373][S377][S378]

## 2. Money and material stability are not neutral risk proxies

The contemporary OHCHR detention materials are morally revealing because they show how release rules can be formally open while practically selective. In Canada, the Working Group on Arbitrary Detention warned that onerous bail conditions can excessively affect marginalized groups and that unnecessary criminalization of bail breaches can push people back into detention through violations that are not themselves serious crimes.[S376] In Australia, the Working Group reported that restrictive bail tests, standalone bail offences, and the treatment of homelessness or lack of fixed address as bail risk can remand people because of underlying disadvantage rather than genuine danger.[S377] In the Bahamas, the Working Group found that bail conditions often exceed the financial means of suspects and their families and that lack of affordable legal assistance can further weaken real access to release.[S378]

This helps the archive name a familiar laundering move. Money deposit, surety, stable address, phone access, transport, reporting ability, curfew compliance, clean records, and lawyer access are described as neutral indicators of reliability. But in practice they are often measures of how much social and economic infrastructure a person already has behind them. Where those conditions are doing the real work, institutions are not merely measuring risk. They are redistributing liberty toward people whose lives are already easier to read as orderly.[S34][S35][S41][S47][S49][S93][S373][S376][S377][S378]

## 3. Non-custodial alternatives still owe dignity and real feasibility

The Tokyo Rules matter because they treat non-custodial measures as part of the criminal-justice system at all stages, including pretrial, and say they should reduce resort to imprisonment, follow minimum-intervention discipline, remain accountable and reviewable, protect dignity, and avoid further restriction beyond what was actually authorized.[S374] That is a crucial correction because release conditions are often defended as the humane alternative to detention even when they are set up to fail.

Righteousness therefore cannot stop at the question whether a court technically avoided jail. It must also ask whether the alternative was materially reachable. Could the person comply without money they did not have, an address they did not control, digital access they could not afford, transportation they could not secure, or family resources they could not safely rely on? If reporting rules, electronic monitoring, curfews, exclusion zones, treatment mandates, sureties, or repeated appearances are loaded onto a person who lacks the means to carry them, then the system may only have shifted the site of coercion from cell to condition. And when breach is then criminalized, release becomes a staged path back into custody.[S34][S35][S41][S47][S49][S374][S376][S377]

## 4. Women and carers are often punished through the structure of release itself

The Bangkok Rules sharpen the archive because they say authorities should recognize the particular risk of abuse women face in pretrial detention, develop gender-specific diversion and pretrial alternatives, and give due consideration to background, family ties, victimization history, and caretaking responsibilities.[S375] Recent OHCHR women's-incarceration materials add that women in poverty often cannot secure bail or timely legal representation and can then face further violence and deeper social harm during pretrial detention.[S379]

That matters because pretrial detention and release design are often written as if the default defendant were socially unencumbered. But many defendants are primary carers, survivors of violence, poor women, migrants, disabled people, or persons whose apparent “instability” is partly the residue of prior domination. A release rule can therefore look mild on paper while actually severing childcare, forcing return to abusive households, making community treatment unreachable, or detaining the person because no financially credible surety exists. Where those background asymmetries are ignored, office is not merely applying a uniform process. It is writing gendered and classed disadvantage directly into the liberty decision.[S34][S35][S41][S45][S47][S49][S67][S375][S379]

## 5. Righteous pretrial release requires live review and support, not just formal permission

Body of Principles and article 9 materials matter because they preserve prompt judicial control, court challenge, and the idea that detention must remain justified rather than simply administratively continued.[S93][S128][S373] The current Working Group materials sharpen a practical implication: where remand is driven by homelessness, inability to pay, thin legal aid, impossible conditions, or automatic tightening after breach, the release system is not doing its job merely because an order was written down.[S376][S377][S378]

So righteous office should ask not only whether detention was avoidable in theory, but whether liberty was actually made reachable in practice. Was legal assistance available early enough to contest detention? Was there bail accommodation, transport help, interpretation, phone access, child-care continuity, trauma-informed support, or another material bridge between paper release and real appearance? Were conditions periodically re-examined as circumstances changed, or did the institution convert a first impression of risk into continuing confinement? Without those supports and review mechanisms, a system can claim to prefer liberty while in fact reserving it for those who already possess money, paperwork, and social backing.[S34][S35][S41][S47][S49][S93][S128][S373][S376][S377][S378][S379]

## 6. Working rule

When an actor claims righteousness while bail, remand, or release conditions are at issue, ask seven questions:
1. **What liberty-bearing interest was actually at stake?** Release pending trial, prompt hearing, family life, work continuity, housing retention, child care, treatment access, or the presumption that accusation alone should not collapse ordinary liberty?[S93][S128][S373][S375]
2. **What mechanism did the real burden work?** Cash bail, surety demand, fixed-address requirement, reporting condition, electronic monitoring, curfew, phone or transport dependence, breach criminalization, or remand-by-default?[S373][S374][S376][S377][S378]
3. **Who carried the burden in practice?** Poor defendants, homeless people, migrants, racialized groups, women with caretaking responsibilities, disabled people, youth, or others whose lives institutions already read as unstable?[S34][S35][S41][S47][S49][S375][S376][S377][S378][S379]
4. **What narrower necessity was actually shown?** What concrete and individualized reason justified detention or this specific condition rather than a less restrictive and more reachable alternative?[S93][S373][S374]
5. **Was release materially reachable?** Counsel, interpretation, transport, communication, bail accommodation, child-care continuity, documentation, and realistic reporting capacity matter because paper release without the means to comply may still be custody by other means.[S374][S376][S377][S378][S379]
6. **What review remained live?** Did the court re-examine necessity, relax conditions when the original basis weakened, and avoid turning minor breach into automatic deeper confinement?[S93][S128][S373][S376]
7. **Was liberty really being sorted by wealth or social legibility?** If the institution can say “bail was available,” “the person had conditions instead of jail,” or “they only had to comply,” while the real selection was being done by money, housing, lawyer access, family backing, or easy-read respectability, the righteousness claim is presumptively corrupted.[S34][S35][S41][S47][S49][S373][S374][S376][S377][S378][S379]

## Compression sentence

> **Do not let accusation plus poverty become pretrial custody: where liberty before trial depends mainly on money, stable housing, or conditions that only the socially buffered can satisfy, rather than on individualized necessity and genuinely reachable alternatives, righteousness fails unless the burdens are narrowly justified, materially feasible, and continuously reviewable.**[S93][S128][S373][S374][S375][S376][S377][S378][S379]
