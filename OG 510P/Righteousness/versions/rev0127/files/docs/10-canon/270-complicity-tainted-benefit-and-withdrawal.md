# Complicity, Tainted Benefit, and Withdrawal

## Why this surface matters

The archive now has interruption, delegation, anti-domination, truthfulness, gift, and exploitation surfaces. What it still lacked was a compact rule for **wrong that arrives through other hands**: when a person, office, firm, movement, or public can say "I did not do the original act" — and still be burdened because it enabled, normalized, profited from, or knowingly remained entangled with the wrong.[S19][S29][S43][S85][S86][S87]

That missing rule matters because two opposite mistakes are common:
- **doer idolatry** — treating direct physical or formal authorship as if only the prime mover could be implicated, while facilitators, beneficiaries, approvers, and sheltering institutions claim innocence by distance; and
- **total-taint panic** — treating every indirect connection, inherited benefit, or mere presence near wrongdoing as if it created identical guilt, identical remedies, and identical withdrawal duties for everyone involved.[S29][S43][S85]

## Working-canon thesis

> **Righteousness is burdened not only by directly doing wrong but also by knowingly enabling, legitimizing, sheltering, or continuing to profit from wrong done by others where one has causal contribution, practical assistance, leverage, or a live duty to resist. Indirect relation does not automatically equal full culpability, but neither does it erase involvement. The righteous question is not only "Did you do it?" but also "How were you connected, what did you know, what did you enable or retain, and what mitigation, withdrawal, or repair became due once the connection was visible?"**[S19][S29][S43][S85][S86][S87]

## 1. Complicity is broader than direct perpetration

The archive already rejects obedience laundering and many-hands evasion, but it has needed a clearer surface to explain why. Wrong is often carried by chains: procurement, financing, cover stories, gatekeeping, silence, routing, technical support, political authorization, institutional shelter, beneficial uptake, or strategic refusal to know. In such cases the point is not that every link is identical to the principal wrongdoer. It is that righteousness cannot make direct authorship the only morally serious relation to wrong.[S19][S29][S43][S85]

The UN Global Compact's Principle 2 states that businesses should make sure they are not complicit in human rights abuses, and it usefully glosses complicity as being implicated in abuse another actor is causing.[S85] It then identifies two elements often present in complicity: some act or omission that helps, facilitates, legitimizes, assists, or encourages the abuse, and knowledge that one's conduct could provide such help.[S85] That is helpful for the archive because it keeps the concept relation-facing: complicity is about how one is connected to another's wrong, not only whether one was the original hand that struck.

## 2. Distance changes degree, not always kind

The archive therefore now rejects a shallow innocence test:

> **"We did not commit the abuse ourselves, so righteousness is untouched."**

That conclusion is often too quick. The dirty-hands literature is already in the archive because it tracks how responsibility and remainder can spread beyond the most visible actor in political settings.[S29] The collective-responsibility literature likewise matters because group structures can distribute authorship, answerability, and forward-looking duties without making them disappear.[S43] And the moral-responsibility literature helps by recognizing derivative responsibility for outcomes where an agent's responsible action stands in the right causal and epistemic relation to what followed.[S11][S43]

So the archive now distinguishes at least four positions:
- **causing or ordering** the wrong;
- **contributing practical assistance, legitimization, routing, or cover** to it;
- **being directly linked through operations, products, services, or institutional relationships** even without having contributed in the narrower sense; and
- **being socially adjacent or historically benefited** in a way that may thicken inquiry, mitigation, or reparative duty without collapsing into identical blame.[S29][S43][S85][S86][S87]

The point is not to force one label over every case. It is to stop distance from doing moral magic.

## 3. Tainted benefit is a standing burden

Complicity is not only about who swung the tool. It is also about who keeps the upside. The Global Compact materials explicitly note the idea of **beneficial complicity**: one may benefit from abuse even without having positively caused it.[S85] The archive should use that carefully. Not every inherited or indirect benefit proves full complicity; otherwise ordinary life under unjust institutions would become unanalyzably totalized. But benefiting from evident wrong is at least a burden against easy self-certification.

So the archive now treats retained gain, privileged access, discounted inputs, suppressed competition, reputational advantage, career lift, political quiet, and clean-hands branding under others' dirty labor as morally salient. Once the benefit is visible, righteousness asks what became due: disclosure, refusal of the gain, restitution, safer sourcing, institutional redesign, advocacy, remediation, or withdrawal may all become live.[S19][S26][S27][S28][S85][S86][S87]

## 4. Direct linkage still creates duties

The due-diligence materials are especially useful here because they avoid the false choice between "you caused it" and "it is none of your business." OHCHR describes human-rights due diligence as identifying and assessing impacts a company may cause or contribute to through its own activities, **or which may be directly linked to its operations, products, or services by its business relationships**; it then stresses prevention, mitigation, tracking, and communication oriented to risks to people.[S87] The OECD likewise frames responsible-business due diligence as a risk-based process for identifying and addressing real and potential negative impacts across operations, supply chains, and business relationships.[S86]

The archive takes a portable lesson from that structure. Where one is directly linked to serious wrong through institutional, economic, technical, or official relationship, righteousness may demand more than private inward disapproval. It may require inquiry, leverage, pressure, redesign, refusal, or exit. Indirectness can change what is owed, but it does not automatically reduce the burden to nothing.[S19][S53][S85][S86][S87]

## 5. Withdrawal is not the only remedy, but sometimes it is due

This is where the archive needed precision. A person or office entangled with wrongdoing should not jump immediately to one dramatic move for every case. Sometimes the righteous response is to document, protest, narrow support, add safeguards, shift vendors, strengthen review, or repair harms one helped cause. Sometimes a public or institutional actor should use its leverage before exiting if exit would simply hide the problem and leave victims worse off. But sometimes continued participation itself becomes laundering: one's presence legitimizes the scheme, keeps the gains flowing, or supplies the practical help without which the abuse would weaken.[S19][S29][S52][S53][S85][S86][S87]

So the archive now treats withdrawal as a **burdened but real** option. It is neither always obligatory nor merely performative. The key question is whether staying is still a credible path of mitigation and answerable pressure, or whether staying has become enabling participation by other means.

## 6. Office and institutions have heightened anti-complicity burdens

This matters especially for office. Public institutions do not merely face private purity questions; they can authorize, normalize, subsidize, conceal, or purchase wrong at scale. Office therefore acquires a stricter anti-complicity burden: not to let procurement, intelligence sharing, contracting, delegated implementation, secrecy, or chain-of-command distance do the work of moral absolution.[S19][S32][S34][S43][S53][S76]

Righteous office should therefore ask not only whether a directive, partnership, or vendor relation is formally lawful, but whether it is making the office a route by which abuse becomes administratively ordinary. When that risk becomes visible, the live responses include heightened review, narrowed authority, traceable conditions, disclosure up the chain, refusal to certify, recusal from the workflow, protected reporting, remediation, and if necessary termination of the relationship.[S19][S32][S34][S43][S53][S76][S85][S87]

## 7. Working rule

When wrongdoing is being done by others but one's own relation to it is in question, ask seven questions:
1. **What is the connection?** Causation, contribution, authorization, routing, cover, direct linkage, beneficial uptake, or mere adjacency?[S29][S43][S85][S87]
2. **What did the actor know or have reason to know?** Was ignorance innocent, cultivated, institutionally convenient, or already warned against?[S19][S53][S85]
3. **What practical help or legitimation did the actor provide?** Money, logistics, legal cover, information, silence, branding, certification, procurement, or public reassurance?[S19][S43][S85]
4. **What leverage existed?** Could the actor interrupt, condition, redesign, escalate, or expose the arrangement rather than simply continue it?[S53][S86][S87]
5. **What benefits were retained?** Profit, access, office advantage, political quiet, reputational cleanliness, or convenience purchased by others' burden?[S19][S26][S27][S85]
6. **What response became due?** Inquiry, disclosure, mitigation, remedy, restitution, refusal, substitution, redesign, or withdrawal?[S26][S28][S53][S86][S87]
7. **Was continued participation still credible as pressure, or had it become laundering?**[S19][S29][S52][S53][S85]

## Compression sentence

> **Do not let distance, delegation, or another actor's primary authorship purify a relation that is still helping, benefiting from, or normalizing wrong; once the connection is visible, righteousness asks what inquiry, leverage, refusal, remediation, or withdrawal became due.**[S19][S29][S43][S53][S85][S86][S87]
