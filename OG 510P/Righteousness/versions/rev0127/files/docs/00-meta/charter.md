# Charter

## Purpose

Build a **small, cumulative, deeply considered archive** for theorizing righteousness.

The archive is not trying to preserve everything ever said about righteousness. It is trying to preserve the smallest body of thought that can still support:

- comparative philosophical work,
- cross-tradition translation without easy flattening,
- practical moral evaluation,
- future extension into institutions, law, technology, and governance.

## Why righteousness deserves separate treatment

Three recurring facts make the topic worth isolating.

1. In many traditions, righteousness is **thicker than mere right action**. It concerns who or what stands rightly with respect to others, obligations, truth, or divine order.[S01][S02][S04][S06][S09]
2. It is often **closely tied to justice**, especially where vulnerable people, corrupt gain, and public order are at issue, but it is not exhausted by distributive fairness alone.[S01][S02][S10][S11][S12][S13]
3. It regularly serves as a **stress concept**: the real test comes when gain, ritual conformity, fear, or status compete with what is due.[S04][S09][S10][S12]

## Archive law

- Prefer compression over accumulation.
- Prefer crosswalks over parallel silos.
- Prefer explicit uncertainty over smooth inflation.
- Prefer one live working theory plus bounded rivals over a hundred disconnected notes.
- Put bold but under-supported thoughts in quarantine.

## Research posture

This revision treats righteousness as a candidate **narrow-waist concept** between thicker metaphysical or theological pictures above and more operational moral tests below.

That may fail. If it does, the failure should be visible and local rather than distributed as ambient vagueness across the archive.
