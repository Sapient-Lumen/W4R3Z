## What this revision does

- preserves the starter canon;
- adds a canon surface on special ties, loyalty, and impartial bounds;
- clarifies that righteousness is neither flat impartiality nor unrestricted loyalty, and that special ties may thicken the due in some relationships while private ties inside impartial public office often trigger restraint rather than privileged service;
- sharpens overview, working-theory, office, neighbor-map, method, and failure-mode surfaces around bounded partiality, representation, favoritism, and anti-nepotism;
- improves archive hygiene by making lint reject canon-order drift inside `ARCHIVE_INDEX.md`.


## What not to assume yet

- that all traditions mean the same thing by *righteousness*;
- that righteousness reduces to justice;
- that righteousness is merely interior character;
- that righteousness is merely rule-following;
- that this archive has already mapped how wide the threshold-band is across traditions, offices, or emergencies;
- that the archive has settled how far reparative duties extend once direct culpability becomes diffuse or inherited;
- that confession, apology, or humble language can instantly restore righteous standing once a defeater has been exposed;
- that every defeater of righteousness is categorical rather than presumptive and possibly remediable;
- that office or institutional righteousness can be certified by inaccessible motive, sealed process, or insider assurance without meaningful review;
- that once one valid claim has priority, the unmet remainder disappears or carries no later burden of explanation, mitigation, or repair;
- that one righteous-seeming act establishes settled righteous standing, or that one grave failure settles the whole temporal verdict without regard to present standing and trajectory;
- that uncertainty excuses thin inquiry, prejudicial disbelief, or irreversible burden-shifting without preserved review and later answerability;
- that once a task is delegated to a proxy, contractor, committee, bureaucracy, or system, the original office or institution has delegated away its standing burden too;
- that conflict among valid claims can be judged rightly without saying which claims involved need, dependency, induced reliance, creator-burden, or the hardest-to-repair loss.
- that every righteous decision under public rules is either straight rule-application or else arbitrary exception; the archive has only a starter account of equity and its guardrails.
- that every compromised role should be met by the same response; the archive has only a starter account of when righteousness requires objection, recusal, disclosure, resignation, or burdened public breach.
- that the archive has settled when mercy is righteous rather than a disguised pardon for the powerful; it has only a starter rule for sanction, clemency, and repair-compatible restraint.

- that every public office is literally a private-law fiduciary relation; the archive now admits a public-trust / nonownership cue, not a total constitutional reduction.
- that the archive has settled when emergency action beyond ordinary channels can still be described as righteous, and when it must instead be typed as burdened interruption, overreach, or defective usurpation.
- that the archive has settled when direct hearing is required, when accountable representation is enough, and when urgency or incapacity justifies only temporary over-the-head substitutes followed by later review or repair.
- that the archive has settled which proxy, guardianship, or stewardship structures are strong enough to carry absent claimants without overwriting them, especially across children, supported decision-making, diffuse publics, and future persons.
- that the archive has settled when special ties rightly thicken what is due, when they are bounded permissions only, and when public roles must instead answer them with recusal, disclosure, or anti-nepotism restraint.
