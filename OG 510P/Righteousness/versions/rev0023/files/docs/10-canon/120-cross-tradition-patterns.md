# Cross-Tradition Patterns

This document does not claim identity across traditions. It records the strongest recurrences that justify a shared research program.

## Greek virtue / justice line

Aristotle's Book V treats justice as a relation-facing completeness of virtue: not merely private goodness, but virtue exercised toward one's neighbor and the common advantage.[S01] SEP's overview likewise notes that justice as virtue originally attaches to individual character even though modern theory often relocates justice primarily to institutions.[S02]

**Compression:** righteousness inherits from this line a strong neighbor-facing and claim-facing structure.

## Biblical and Jewish prophetic line

The Hebrew materials repeatedly pair justice with righteousness and tie them to public judgment and the defense of the wronged. Deuteronomy presses pursuit of justice; Amos couples justice and righteousness against empty worship; Isaiah explicitly turns toward the wronged, orphan, and widow.[S10][S11][S12]

**Compression:** righteousness is anti-ritualist when ritual substitutes for justice, and it is socially diagnostic rather than merely inward.

## Christian Thomist line

Aquinas roots moral virtue in practical reason and natural law, preserving an account in which virtue is neither arbitrary feeling nor detached rule fetish.[S06]

**Compression:** righteousness needs right reason and ordered ends, not merely good intention.

## Islamic line

Qur'an 2:177 offers an unusually compact compression of righteousness: it refuses reduction to directional ritual and instead binds righteousness to belief, giving, pledge-keeping, and patience under suffering.[S09]

**Compression:** righteousness is not ceremony alone; it has covenantal, distributive, and endurance dimensions.

## Confucian line

Confucian *yi* is repeatedly contrasted with gain and tied to incorruptibility in public responsibility; Mencius deepens this with the notion that righteousness must be cultivated from an incipient moral capacity.[S04][S05]

**Compression:** righteousness is what survives the lure of profit and the corrosion of office.

## Modern secular differentiation

Kant sharply grounds rightness in principle rather than outcome or character value.[S07] Rawls relocates justice toward the fair ordering of institutions and cooperation among free and equal citizens.[S13] Care ethics insists that dependency and relationship are morally basic rather than peripheral.[S08]

**Compression:** modern theory usefully disaggregates dimensions that older traditions often held together: principle, institution, and relation.

## What now seems primary

Taken together, the source families push the archive toward a relation-first model. Chinese ethics and care ethics make relation and dependency morally basic; Aristotle centers justice in relation to others; the justice literature applies evaluation across persons, laws, policies, and structures; and common-good reasoning explains why public roles change what one owes.[S01][S08][S14][S15][S16]

This does **not** erase the importance of virtue or institutions. It instead suggests a priority order:

- relations explain the live claim,
- persons answer to it,
- institutions stabilize or distort it.

## Synthesis pressure

The comparative picture suggests that a salient theory of righteousness should be able to handle all of the following at once:

- virtue and character,
- action and office,
- valid claims and fairness,
- vulnerable parties and dependency,
- covenant / promise / fidelity,
- institutional order,
- repair after breach.

Any theory missing most of these may still be interesting, but it is probably a partial theory.
