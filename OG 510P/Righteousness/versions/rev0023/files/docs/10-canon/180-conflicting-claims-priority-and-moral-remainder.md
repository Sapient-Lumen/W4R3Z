# Conflicting Claims, Priority, and Moral Remainder

## Why this surface now

The archive now has a portable core, threshold-band, defeaters, repair, requalification, and contestability. But it still lacked a compact rule for a common hard case:

> **what does righteousness require when more than one valid claim is real, and not all of them can be fully met together?**

Without such a surface, the archive risks two opposite mistakes:
- pretending that one clean principle mechanically solves every collision of duties;
- or speaking as if any tragic conflict automatically dissolves righteousness into mere damage control.

Both mistakes are too easy.

## Working-canon thesis

> **Righteousness under conflict is not the satisfaction of every valid claim. It is truthful triage among real claims: prioritizing the graver, less substitutable, more vulnerable, more specially borne, or more wrongly intensified claim without laundering the unsatisfied remainder. Where some due cannot be met, righteousness names the loss, carries the residue, and becomes answerable to later explanation, mitigation, or repair.**[S08][S17][S26][S29][S37][S38]

This is not a complete algorithm. It is a discipline for better verdicts. The archive now supplements it with a dedicated starter surface on portable cues and burden placement in `docs/10-canon/200-portable-priority-cues-and-burden-placement.md`.

## 1. Conflicts can be real without making morality unintelligible

The moral-dilemmas literature begins from a simple point: conflicts between moral requirements are a recognizable part of moral life.[S37]

That matters for this archive because righteousness should not be built on the fantasy that every serious case contains only one genuine claim and one fake one. Some cases involve promise against protection, loyalty against public duty, confidentiality against warning, or one urgent rescue against another.

The archive therefore now rejects **single-claim simplification**.

## 2. Priority is structured, not arbitrary

The archive does **not** adopt a universal ranking algorithm. But it now treats some priority cues as portable and morally serious.

### A. Graver and less reparable harms normally press harder

Where one option would expose others to grave or difficult-to-repair harm, righteousness normally gives that claim special weight. Ross's pluralist framework is useful here because duties of non-maleficence, fidelity, gratitude, reparation, and beneficence can all be real at once, while the all-things-considered obligation still depends on their comparative weight in the case.[S38]

### B. Specially borne claims matter

Some claims press harder because they arise from role, promise, office, dependence, prior wrongdoing, or entrusted power. Special-obligation theory is helpful here: not every claim is owed equally to everyone in every context, and some obligations are intensified by the relationship or office in which the agent stands.[S17]

### C. Vulnerability and dependence matter

Care-ethical and justice-facing materials already in the archive support a further cue: where burdens will predictably fall on the more dependent, exposed, or structurally weaker party, righteousness should not treat that as morally incidental.[S08][S12][S15]

### D. Self-created conflicts are burdened

When an agent created the collision through prior wrongdoing, bad planning, corruption, or impossible promises, the conflict does not become morally clean merely because a hard choice now exists. The conflict itself may already carry culpable residue.[S29][S37]

These cues do not abolish judgment. They discipline it.

## 3. No clean wins where valid claims remain unsatisfied

This revision adds a negative rule:

> **do not describe a priority-correct action as wholly clean when a valid claim was still left unmet.**

The dilemmas literature tracks moral residue; the dirty-hands literature tracks cases where even a defended choice may remain burdened or tragic.[S29][S37]

So the archive now rejects two familiar rhetorics:
- **clean-win rhetoric** — "we chose the right priority, therefore nothing remainder-bearing occurred";
- **tragic-exception rhetoric** — "the case was tragic, therefore righteousness is no longer the right category at all."

A better verdict can be: **priority-correct but remainder-bearing**.

## 4. Unmet claims can generate later duties

When a valid claim could not be fully met, that does not always end the matter.

An unmet but still valid claim may generate later duties of:
- explanation and truthful record;
- apology or acknowledgment where an identifiable party bore an unchosen burden;
- compensation, restitution, or mitigation where feasible;
- institutional redesign so the same conflict is less likely to recur.

This is where the archive's breach-responsive repair rule reconnects to conflict. Not every tragic choice is a wrongful breach. But once one party carried uncompensated loss or wrongful burden-shifting, righteousness may require more than sorrow; it may require remainder-sensitive repair.[S26][S27][S28]

## 5. What this changes in verdicts

This surface improves the verdict vocabulary again.

More exact verdicts now include:
- **priority-correct and remainder-bearing**;
- **office-faithful but debt-incurring**;
- **tragic triage without righteous self-purification**;
- **conflict honestly carried and later repaired where possible**;
- **multiple valid claims, one action defensible, no clean victory**.

This helps the archive resist both scorekeeping and sanctimony.

## Office-facing note

For office, the rule is even sharper.

Where public officials face claim conflict, righteousness normally requires:
- making the priority rule legible enough to be reviewed;
- refusing factional or self-protective triage;
- preserving record of who bore the remainder;
- and opening later mitigation, compensation, or review where the unmet claim can still be answered in part.[S17][S18][S32][S34][S35]

Public triage without record, explanation, or later answerability is therefore heavily burdened.

## Compressed rule

> **Righteousness under conflict is not clean maximization and not helpless lament. It is disciplined priority among valid claims, plus honest carriage of the remainder.**
