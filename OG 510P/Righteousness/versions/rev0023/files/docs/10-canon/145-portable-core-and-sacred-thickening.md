# Portable Core and Sacred Thickening

## The pressure

The archive wants a theory of righteousness that is **portable across traditions** without pretending that all traditions mean the same thing.

That creates a real risk:
- if portability means "strip away religion until only thin secular residue remains," the archive will falsify major source families;
- if righteousness is treated as essentially sacred in every serious use, the archive loses comparative reach and cannot explain why related non-theistic or publicly translated accounts still look structurally recognizable.

The best current compression is this:

> **Righteousness has a portable core and tradition-thick forms.**

## Portable core

The portable core is not a lowest-common-denominator slogan. It is the smallest structure that still explains why the archive's source families keep converging.

The current candidate core is:

> **answerability to valid claims under pressure, with fidelity to due order and openness to repair.**

That core remains visible across Aristotle's justice-as-virtue, Confucian *yi* under gain pressure, care-ethical relation and dependency, Rawlsian concern with fair terms among free and equal citizens, and the Abrahamic anti-ritual materials already in the archive.[S01][S04][S08][S09][S13][S14]

So the archive does **not** currently treat reference to God, covenant, salvation, or grace as necessary for every recognizable use of righteousness.

## Thick sacred forms

Sacred traditions do not merely decorate the portable core. They often do three further things.

### 1. They identify the source of the due

Instead of leaving valid claims at the level of human reciprocity or political morality alone, they place those claims within divine command, covenantal relation, created order, or worship-worthy reality.[S06][S09][S10][S11][S21]

### 2. They intensify diagnosis

The prophetic and Qur'anic materials do not merely add pious language to an already secular moral picture. They sharpen diagnosis by exposing ritual substitution, hypocrisy, hardness toward the vulnerable, and betrayal of covenant under the gaze of God.[S09][S11][S12]

### 3. They thicken repair and hope

Sacred frames often connect righteousness with repentance, mercy, forgiveness, sanctification, or grace in ways that exceed a merely political or interpersonal vocabulary.[S06][S21]

## Why portability is still honest

A portable core is justified here for two reasons.

First, religion and morality have long been intertwined, but they are not simply identical; philosophical reflection has repeatedly tried to distinguish moral structure from specifically theological grounding.[S21]

Second, modern pluralist political thought requires some form of publicly shareable justification when coercive power is used over diverse citizens. Public reason therefore supports a distinction between a person's full motivating doctrine and the reasons fit to justify law or office in common life.[S18]

That distinction does **not** show sacred righteousness is unreal. It shows that a theory may need both:
- a thicker interpretation of what ultimately grounds righteousness; and
- a thinner public translation for shared institutions.

## Archive rule

The archive now adopts this working-canon rule:

> treat **portable core** and **sacred thickening** as analytically distinguishable, but do not treat the thick forms as optional decoration where a tradition makes them constitutive.

A further canon surface now sharpens what comes next: even when a portable core is defensible, traditions may recognize it as identity, incomplete identity, analogue, or rejection, and those recognition judgments must be kept separate from the question of whether a translated form can serve as a public module for shared institutions. See `docs/10-canon/150-recognition-status-and-translation-burden.md`.

In other words:
- for comparative theory, ask what structure survives translation;
- for internal interpretation, ask what the tradition says righteousness finally answers to.

## Office implication

For public office in pluralist settings, sacred conviction may still motivate the officeholder. But the justification of coercive action should not rely only on the authority of a contested comprehensive doctrine.[S18][S20]

So righteous office may be religiously motivated **and** publicly translated.

## What this resolves

This move improves the archive in three ways:

1. it preserves the comparative program without pretending that Abrahamic materials are just secular justice talk in costume;
2. it explains why non-theistic and publicly translated theories can still belong inside the righteousness research program; and
3. it prevents a common failure mode: treating "portable" as a euphemism for flattening.

## What remains open

The archive still does not know, tradition by tradition, which recognition status is most defensible.

That mapping stays live in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.json`.
