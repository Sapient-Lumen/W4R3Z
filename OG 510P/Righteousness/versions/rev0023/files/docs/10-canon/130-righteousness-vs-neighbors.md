# Righteousness vs. Neighboring Concepts

## Why this distinction matters

The word *righteousness* often becomes useless because it is allowed to float among adjacent concepts. This archive therefore fixes a small boundary map.

## Goodness

**Goodness** is the widest neighboring term. It concerns what is valuable, choiceworthy, admirable, or worth promoting overall.

Righteousness is narrower. It asks not simply whether something is good, but whether one is standing and acting rightly with respect to what is due.

A beautiful outcome may be good without having been achieved righteously. A sacrifice may be righteous even when it is locally costly.

## Justice

**Justice** is the closest neighbor.

Justice is usually strongest at the level of due, fairness, rights, distributions, and legitimate institutional order.[S02][S13][S15]

Righteousness overlaps with justice but keeps an extra emphasis on:
- the agent's posture under temptation,
- fidelity rather than mere compliance,
- pledge / covenant / trustworthiness,
- and repair after breach.

A system can satisfy a thin procedural fairness test yet still be unrighteous if it launders cruelty, evasion, or abandonment of the vulnerable. And after breach, righteous response reaches beyond abstract justice-talk into the more specific sequence of acknowledgment, reparative action, and changed standing.

## Piety / holiness

Religious traditions often connect righteousness with right relation to God or sacred order.[S06][S09][S10]

But the archive rejects the thought that piety alone is enough. The prophetic and Qur'anic materials in this starter source set both reject ritual or directional conformity when detached from justice, mercy, aid, or promise-keeping.[S09][S11][S12]

The archive now states the positive side more clearly as well: sacred orientation may be constitutive inside some traditions, but comparative portability requires distinguishing the **core structure** of righteousness from the **thicker sacred interpretations** that ground and intensify it. That distinction is developed in `docs/10-canon/145-portable-core-and-sacred-thickening.md`.[S18][S20][S21]

## Loyalty / impartiality

Loyalty and impartiality are not simple opposites, and righteousness should not be collapsed into either one. Some relationships really do intensify what is due: promises, friendships, family ties, entrusted care, and some representational roles can justify bounded partiality rather than flat equal treatment in every context.[S17][S45][S68]

But righteousness also has to police the corruption of loyalty. When proximity, kinship, faction, or constituency is used to erase baseline due, excuse falsehood, or bend a public office away from fair administration, the result is not righteous loyalty but captured partiality. In adjudicative or allocative office, special ties often count not as reasons for preference but as reasons for recusal, disclosure, or anti-nepotism constraint.[S32][S69]

## Integrity

Integrity is about non-fragmentation, coherence, and standing by what one takes to be right.

Righteousness requires integrity, but integrity alone is insufficient. One may be intensely integrated around a corrupt moral view.

## Legality / compliance

Legality tracks rule-conformity.

Righteousness may require legality, but legality is not the measure of righteousness. Unjust laws, biased offices, and cleverly compliant bad faith make that obvious.[S01][S07][S13][S15]

## Legitimacy

Political legitimacy concerns justified authority: whether institutions or decisions have the right sort of standing to rule, coerce, or bind. Contemporary theory often treats legitimacy as weaker than justice, and therefore also weaker than the thicker demand this archive places under righteousness.[S20]

A decision or office can be legitimate yet still fall short of righteousness when it is evasive, partisan, corruptible, or closed to repair.

## Virtue

Virtue is broader than righteousness because it includes excellences that are not centrally about due-ness or neighbor-facing claim structure.

Righteousness is a special moral concentration of virtue around due, faithfulness, judgment, and breach-responsive repair.

## Heroism / supererogation

Supererogatory action goes beyond the call of duty: morally admirable, often costly, yet not strictly owed.[S24]

Righteousness can be displayed in such action, but it should not be defined by it. Otherwise the concept loses its threshold function and collapses into saintliness, aspiration, or maximizing beneficence.[S24][S25]

## Forgiveness / reconciliation

Forgiveness and reconciliation are important near-neighbors once wrong has occurred, but the archive does not collapse righteousness into either one.[S26]

Repair can require acknowledgment, compensation, institutional correction, or restored public standing even where trust is not rebuilt and victims are under no duty to forgive. Pressure on victims to forgive in order to certify closure is therefore a failure mode, not evidence of righteous completion.[S26][S28]

## Working boundary sentence

> Righteousness is the claim-facing, standing-sensitive, pressure-tested, repair-accountable region where virtue, justice, and fidelity meet.
