# Levels, Carriers, and Priority

## The question

When we call something *righteous*, what kind of thing are we primarily talking about?

The archive now takes a sharper position:

> **Righteousness is relation-first, person-carried, and institution-shaped.**

That is the current best compression.

## What this means

### 1. Relation-first

Righteousness is most fundamentally about whether a pattern of action answers rightly to the claims that arise between parties who stand in some morally significant relation: neighbor and neighbor, promisor and promisee, judge and litigant, ruler and ruled, caregiver and dependent, citizen and citizen.[S01][S08][S14][S16]

This relation-first thesis explains why righteousness repeatedly shows up around *due-ness*, fairness, pledge, vulnerable parties, and office rather than around isolated self-perfection alone.[S01][S09][S11][S12][S15]

### 2. Person-carried

Persons are the primary accountable carriers of righteousness because persons deliberate, perceive temptation, keep or break faith, and can acknowledge and repair breach. Aristotle's justice-as-virtue framing, Confucian talk of righteousness under gain pressure, Qur'an 2:177's emphasis on pledge-keeping and patience, and the virtue tradition more broadly all keep agency in view.[S01][S04][S09][S12]

So the archive does **not** dissolve righteousness into impersonal structure.

### 3. Institution-shaped

Institutions matter because they stabilize, distribute, amplify, or corrupt the relations within which persons act. Modern justice theory treats laws, policies, and social structures as proper sites of assessment, and Rawls places special weight on the basic structure that allocates the benefits and burdens of social life.[S13][S15]

An institution is righteous when it makes it easier to recognize valid claims, harder to profit from domination, and more realistic to secure correction and repair.

## Why this is better than the rival priorities

### Against person-first as the whole story

A person-first theory misses two stubborn facts:

1. many of the gravest unrighteousnesses are role-mediated and structural rather than merely personal; and
2. public offices change what upright action requires.

The same individual may act permissibly as a private citizen yet unrighteously as a judge, prosecutor, legislator, or executive if private gain or factional loyalty displaces the claims attached to office.[S04][S16]

### Against institution-first as the whole story

An institution-first theory misses the pressure-tested character of righteousness.

Rules and structures do not themselves resist gain, tell the truth after breach, or refuse corrupt advantage. Persons do. A formally decent institution staffed by bad-faith agents can remain recognizably unrighteous in operation.[S01][S04][S07]

### Against relation-only talk

Pure relation-talk can become too vaporous. Relations require carriers and forms. Someone must answer to the claim, and some arrangement must either support or frustrate right answerability.

## Public roles are the clearest test case

Public roles reveal the tri-level structure especially clearly. The common good tradition argues that citizens and officeholders stand in a political relationship that requires them to give one another's interests a certain status in practical reasoning, and that public officials must not reason as if they were merely private actors pursuing private advantage.[S16]

This supports a useful archive rule:

> the more a role allocates burdens, protections, judgment, or public trust, the less plausible purely private reasoning becomes.

## Operational rule

When assessing righteousness, ask in this order:

1. **Relation:** what relation or claim-structure is live here?
2. **Carrier:** which persons or offices had to answer to it?
3. **Structure:** what institutional arrangement helped or hindered right answerability?

## Status of this move

This is stronger than a live speculation but weaker than final doctrine.

The archive therefore treats the triad **relation-first / person-carried / institution-shaped** as **working canon**: strong enough to organize the archive, still open to defeat by later comparative work.
