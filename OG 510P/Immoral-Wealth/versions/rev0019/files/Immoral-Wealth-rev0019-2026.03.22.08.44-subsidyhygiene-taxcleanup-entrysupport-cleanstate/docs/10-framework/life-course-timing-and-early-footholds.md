# Life-course timing and early footholds

## Thesis

A morally acceptable wealth order should give most people a durable foothold **early enough to shape adulthood**.

Ownership that arrives mainly in late middle age, after long exposure to rent extraction and debt pressure, or only through eventual inheritance is too delayed to count as full openness. A society can look less unequal in a cross-section while still failing because security arrives **too late** for those without wealthy parents.

## Why the archive adds this refinement

OECD's 2025 opportunity work says wealth transfers are associated with a **larger wealth premium when they are received earlier in life**.[S19]

OECD's inheritance-tax report adds a second warning: wealth is expected to remain concentrated among **older cohorts** for longer periods, and the age at which people inherit is likely to keep rising with longer life expectancy.[S05]

OECD's recent housing and wealth work adds a third: rising house prices have made homeownership less accessible for **younger cohorts**, especially those from lower-income or lower-wealth households.[S13][S19]

Taken together, these facts mean a society can improve aggregate shares while still leaving younger adults locked out of the period of life when asset access matters most.

## The timing test

The archive's current best judgment is that a morally acceptable society should pass four timing tests:

1. **Most people can reach a first durable foothold before midlife**
   - Positive net wealth, secure housing tenure, or a lasting capital claim should arrive early enough to shape family formation, mobility, and life planning.
2. **Inheritance supplements adulthood rather than gating it**
   - Bequests can remain part of family life, but ordinary housing access, entrepreneurship, and long-run security should not depend mainly on waiting for parental death.
3. **Cohort balance does not rely on incumbent lock-in**
   - Older cohorts can remain secure without pricing younger cohorts out of ownership and then returning access only through delayed transfer.
4. **Public institutions shift entry forward**
   - Social inheritance, housing access, debt relief, universal services, and non-predatory credit should move the first foothold earlier in life rather than asking households to endure decades of insecurity.

## What does not count as success

The archive should treat the following as weak or false progress:

- improving aggregate wealth shares while large parts of the under-40 population remain asset-poor or tenure-insecure,
- treating late-life inheritance as an adequate substitute for early asset access,
- celebrating a broad owner middle that exists mainly among older incumbents,
- using tax-favored savings vehicles that mostly help those already in stable careers and housing.

## Practical use

Use the timing refinement when evaluating reforms:

- do households without parental help gain a durable foothold **before** typical inheritance age?
- are policy gains widening early access to housing, saving, and long-run claims, or mostly protecting incumbent owners?
- is the society broadening ownership across cohorts, or mainly promising that today's excluded young will inherit much later?
- does the public response move security earlier in life, or merely make late arrival slightly less unequal?
