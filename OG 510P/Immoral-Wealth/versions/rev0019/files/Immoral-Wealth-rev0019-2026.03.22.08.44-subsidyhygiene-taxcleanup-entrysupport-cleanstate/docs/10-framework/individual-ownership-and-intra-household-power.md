# Individual ownership and intra-household power

## Thesis

A society can look less unequal at the **household** level and still remain morally defective at the **person** level.
If housing, business equity, pensions, savings, or land appear broadly distributed only because they are booked to households while one adult inside the household holds most of the title, control, or exit security, the archive should treat that as a partial failure rather than a pass.

This matters most where marriage, cohabitation, divorce, widowhood, migration, informal work, or care interruptions leave one partner economically exposed despite living in a household that looks secure on paper.

## Why the archive cannot stop at household wealth

Most cross-country wealth data are household based, and the archive will continue to use them for the main percentile scoreboard.
But the moral object is not only the household balance sheet.
It is whether **persons** have durable enough claims to avoid dependence, coercion, or trapped exit.

OECD's 2025 opportunity work warns that household-level measures can hide important inequality: the effect of gender becomes much stronger when inequality of opportunity is measured using **individual earnings** rather than household market income.[S27]
OECD's 2025 household-wealth note adds a second signal: on average across the OECD, **single men hold about USD 43 000 more wealth than single women**, with large gaps in a sizeable minority of countries.[S13]
World Bank gender-asset sources add a third: women often trail men in **account and land ownership**, and formal household membership alone does not guarantee equal access to the benefits of property if rights are not individually secured.[S28][S29]

That means the archive has to ask not only **how much a household owns**, but also **who inside the household can rely on it, control it, borrow against it, keep it after rupture, and pass it on**.

## What counts as failure

A country should be treated as only partially successful if any of the following patterns are widespread:

- primary residences or land are notionally family assets but formal title is heavily skewed toward one adult,
- liquid accounts, business equity, or pension claims are formally individual but systematically concentrated in one partner,
- marital or cohabitation property law leaves the lower-earning or care-burdened adult weakly protected on separation, widowhood, or household conflict,
- unpaid-care patterns and interrupted employment histories predict much weaker pension entitlements for one sex,
- social inheritance or ownership-spreading tools are routed mainly through households rather than to persons,
- household-level statistics flatter the distribution while large person-level ownership gaps persist.

A morally acceptable wealth order should not require adults to remain in a household relationship merely to retain housing security, retirement prospects, or basic asset access.

## Measurement questions

Use household wealth as the macro backbone, but add person-level checks where possible:

1. who is named on the title to the primary residence and other major real assets?
2. what share of adults have an individual financial account, a direct pension entitlement, and a direct long-run asset claim?[S28][S30]
3. how large are person-level wealth gaps among singles and among older adults after household pooling stops masking them?[S13][S30]
4. how often do law and practice ensure equal rights in divorce, widowhood, inheritance, and sale or mortgage decisions?[S29]
5. are capital-building instruments delivered to persons, or only to tax units / households?

## Program implications

This is not a sixth axis.
It is a **cross-cutting person-level condition** on the archive's existing floor, circulation, and timing doctrines.

Programmatically, it pushes the archive toward:

- default joint titling or equivalent co-ownership protection for assets acquired during partnership,
- equal inheritance and marital-property enforcement in practice, not only on paper,[S29]
- pension credits and portable entitlements that do not punish unpaid care,
- direct-to-person social inheritance and savings architecture rather than household-routed ownership,
- person-level account access and payment rails,
- emergency asset-protection rules in divorce, widowhood, domestic abuse, or displacement settings,
- better person-level asset data where household-only reporting currently hides dependence.[S27][S28][S29][S30]

## Rule of judgment

The archive should not call a society acceptable if it improves the **household** distribution while leaving many adults — disproportionately women in current evidence — without direct title, direct liquid control, direct retirement rights, or credible exit security.

A decent wealth order should broaden ownership not only across percentiles, but across **persons**.
