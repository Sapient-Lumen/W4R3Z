# Predistribution: labor and wages

## Thesis

A society cannot seriously compress immoral wealth inequality if it leaves labor markets organized around weak bargaining power and high employer leverage.

## Why this is upstream

Wealth is downstream from earnings, bargaining power, and household extraction. If wages are systematically held below productivity, then later asset-building policies must fight a headwind every year.

OECD work notes that stronger worker representation and collective bargaining can counter disproportionate employer wage-setting power, while excessive labor-market concentration and non-competes / non-poaching agreements worsen wage outcomes.[S08] OECD also treats collective bargaining and worker voice as key labor rights with clear effects on job quality.[S09]

The newer OECD bargaining-coverage brief sharpens the point: across the OECD, collective bargaining coverage fell from **47% in 1985 to 33.6% in 2023/24**, while union density fell from **30% to 15%** over roughly the same period.[S14] The IMF's 2024 G20 note similarly links stronger redistribution, worker-rights protections, and labor-market institutions that limit dualization to better inequality outcomes.[S11]

## Program elements

- sectoral or pattern bargaining where feasible,
- robust minimum wages indexed to median wages or productivity,
- restrictions on non-competes and non-poaching agreements,
- stronger labor standards enforcement,
- full-employment macro policy where possible,
- worker representation in firm governance,
- portable benefits for contingent workers.

## Why this matters for wealth

Predistribution does three things that transfers alone do not:

1. raises the capacity to save,
2. lowers dependence on debt,
3. reduces the rate at which capital owners pull away from earners.

## Guardrail

Do not confuse labor policy with the entire solution. Stronger wages without asset-spreading can still leave housing, inheritance, and capital income doing most of the governing.
