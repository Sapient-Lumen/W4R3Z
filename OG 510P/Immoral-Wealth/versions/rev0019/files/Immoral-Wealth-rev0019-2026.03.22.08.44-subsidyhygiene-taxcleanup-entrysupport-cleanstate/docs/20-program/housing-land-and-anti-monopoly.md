# Housing, land, and anti-monopoly

## Thesis

A society can raise wages and still fail morally if housing and market power keep turning broad gains into private rents.

rev0004 adds a sharper claim: housing is not just another asset class. It is one of the main places where the archive's **share target** and **control target** intersect.

## Housing

OECD reporting shows housing is a major burden for low-income households. In many OECD countries, more than 40% of low-income owners with mortgages or low-income private renters spend over 40% of disposable income on housing; subsidized renters are less likely than private-market renters to face that burden.[S07]

OECD's 2025 household-wealth work also says housing is a key component of household wealth, that homeownership has historically had an equalising effect, but that rising prices, limited social housing, and restrictive policies are making homeownership harder for young and low-wealth households.[S13] OECD's 2025 distributional-wealth paper likewise says housing is the largest household asset and that inequality is driven more by financial assets, with housing moderating concentration for some households.[S16]

That means housing can either:

- broaden security and middle-class ownership, or
- become a machine that sorts society into protected owners and rent-exposed non-owners.

rev0016 adds a stricter reading: even when owner balance sheets improve, the archive should ask whether those gains came from **broader secure access** or mainly from **incumbent revaluation** that worsens entry for later households.[S07][S13]

### Program elements

- expand social and non-speculative housing,
- tax land and property in ways that discourage passive extraction,
- legalize more housing where supply constraints are binding,
- protect tenants from coercive churn and predatory fees,
- use land banks or public options where private concentration blocks access,
- tie major rezoning, density gains, and infrastructure-led uplift to value capture, affordable-housing obligations, or long-run public claim,
- manage public land strategically rather than treating it as disposable cash,
- treat housing security as wealth policy, not only welfare policy.

## Anti-monopoly / anti-rent

OECD work notes that competition has weakened in many OECD countries since 2000, with greater concentration and higher markups.[S10] In labor markets, OECD also notes that employer concentration and weak bargaining institutions can worsen wage outcomes.[S08]

### Program elements

- stronger merger control with labor-market analysis,
- anti-monopsony enforcement,
- limits on vertical and data-driven lock-in where concentration compounds rent extraction,
- scrutiny of sectors that convert necessity into recurring extraction: housing platforms, logistics bottlenecks, health, utilities, finance.

## Principle

Do not let a successful redistribution policy be silently reabsorbed by landlords, monopolists, or financial toll collectors.
And do not mistake rising house prices alone for egalitarian progress if access to ownership is narrowing at the same time.[S13][S16]

rev0015 adds an implementation warning here as well: housing-entry subsidies, tax breaks, or credit expansion should not be counted as success unless distributional analysis checks whether they broaden access or simply capitalise into land prices, fees, or incumbent windfalls. rev0018 adds a companion rule: when public infrastructure or rezoning creates land-value uplift, the archive should prefer infrastructure levies, developer obligations, strategic public-land management, or similar tools that return part of the gain to affordable housing, infrastructure, or public/social wealth.[S07][S13][S31][S34][S35]


rev0019 adds a narrower subsidy-hygiene rule: the archive should treat mortgage-interest relief, uncapped main-residence gain preferences, and buyer subsidies that mainly enlarge bids as part of the **wealth problem**, not as neutral pro-middle-class background policy. OECD's housing-taxation work says mortgage-interest relief has been found regressive and ineffective at raising homeownership rates, uncapped main-residence capital-gains exemptions provide vastly greater benefits to wealthier households, and housing tax relief can raise house prices when supply is fixed.[S36]
