# Prevention and response doctrine

## Thesis

A society should not wait for full oligarchy before acting against immoral wealth inequality.
But it also should not pretend that every unequal society requires the same force, speed, or legal posture.

rev0003 therefore distinguishes **three modes**:

1. **prevention** — broadening ownership before domination hardens,
2. **correction** — reversing serious concentration before it becomes regime-defining,
3. **anti-oligarchy response** — confronting a distribution that is already reproducing rule by wealth.

rev0006 adds a further warning: some societies may not yet look fully oligarchic on snapshot shares while already becoming **dynastic**. rev0007 adds another: some societies may improve temporarily while remaining structurally primed for **reconcentration**. rev0008 adds a companion warning: some societies may improve on private wealth metrics while still pushing too much essential risk back onto households. rev0011 adds another: some societies may broaden household capital claims while still leaving stewardship power concentrated in a narrow intermediary layer. rev0012 adds another: some societies may look broader in later-life balance sheets while still forcing younger adults to wait too long for first durable footholds. rev0013 adds one more: some societies may look broader at the household level while still leaving one adult inside many households weakly titled, weakly protected, or unable to exit with a fair share. rev0014 adds another: some societies may look broader in national averages while still leaving historically subordinated groups shut out of the main wealth-building channels. The doctrine therefore has to notice domination, closure, timing, durability, over-privatized insecurity, silent ownership, internal household dependence, group-level exclusion, the difference between a theory that sounds right and a budget process that can actually carry it, the difference between broad wealth built through open access and broad wealth built mainly through scarcity rents and bottleneck power, and the difference between a system that lets owners bear losses and one that repeatedly shifts concentrated downside onto the public, and the difference between public action that widens common wealth and public action that quietly capitalises into private land and development windfalls.[S05][S07][S10][S13][S20][S21][S22][S26][S27][S28][S29][S30][S31][S34][S35]

## Why a mode distinction is needed

The empirical backdrop is already extreme. WIR 2026 reports that across world regions the bottom 50% holds only **1% to 5%** of wealth, while the top 10% holds **60% to 74%** and the top 1% often holds a quarter to nearly half of all wealth.[S02] OECD reports that the richest 10% own **52%** of household wealth on average across OECD countries and **79%** in the United States.[S03]

At the same time, OECD's 2025 household-wealth note says wealth inequality fell in parts of the late 2010s but the picture has become mixed amid the cost-of-living crisis, and rising house prices have made entry harder for young and low-wealth households.[S13] OECD's opportunity work adds that inherited circumstances explain a large share of inequality in outcomes and that parental homeownership is becoming more predictive of adult homeownership in many OECD European countries.[S18][S19] OECD's integrity outlook also says lobbying safeguards are among the weakest elements of OECD integrity frameworks and that the basic elements of lobbying frameworks are in place in **less than half** of OECD countries.[S15]

That combination means a serious archive needs a doctrine for both **prevention** and **response**, for spotting the point where unequal societies begin hardening into hereditary ones, and for noticing when apparent progress is too easy to reverse.

## Mode 1 — Prevention

Use prevention mode when the society is still inside the archive's main red lines and wealth has not yet become a reliably self-protecting governing technology.

### Priority package

- strengthen bargaining power and labor standards,
- lower extraction through housing and universal services,
- strengthen the social floor where weak health, care, pension, or other protections are forcing excess self-insurance,
- build social inheritance and public wealth,
- reform capital-gains and inheritance taxation before dynastic channels deepen,
- install basic transparency around lobbying, political finance, procurement, and beneficial ownership,
- and set early stewardship rules for major retirement and pooled-investment channels before asset-manager concentration hardens into governance concentration, while moving first durable footholds earlier through housing-entry, debt, and social-inheritance design rather than relying on late family transfer, while securing person-level title, account access, pension rights, and fair exit protections so household broadening is not merely nominal, while putting early disaggregated measurement and barrier removal in place so excluded groups are not left behind inside apparently broad gains, while leaning against rent-heavy and revaluation-heavy gains that enrich incumbents without widening entry, while hardening rescue and resolution rules early enough that systemically important wealth does not quietly retain public downside protection, while tying major rezoning, infrastructure, and public-land decisions to value capture or durable public-purpose conditions, and while wiring distributional impact analysis into the budget cycle early enough that major packages can still be redesigned before passage.[S04][S05][S07][S10][S12][S13][S15][S19][S20][S26][S27][S28][S29][S30][S31][S34][S35]

### Governing idea

Do early, broad, legitimacy-building work while the system is still reformable without emergency posture, while generational openness can still be preserved at relatively low cost, and before reconcentration pressures make each reform cycle shorter-lived.

## Mode 2 — Correction

Use correction mode when **one or more red lines are breached**.

Typical signs:

- bottom-half wealth remains near-assetless,
- top shares remain visibly regime-shaping,
- public wealth is persistently negative,
- publicly created land and development uplift is repeatedly privatised with little recapture,
- housing costs prevent entry into ownership for large parts of the working and middle population,
- parental position is becoming too strong a predictor of who reaches durable security,
- secure ownership is arriving too late for younger and non-inheriting households even where older cohorts look broad-owner,
- or household-level security is hiding large person-level gaps in title, liquid control, or exit protection.[S02][S05][S07][S13][S18][S19][S27][S28][S29][S30]

### Priority package

- accelerate bottom-half asset building,
- move faster on inheritance and capital-gains reform,
- use stronger anti-monopoly and anti-rent tools,
- deploy anti-avoidance and cross-border enforcement earlier,
- slow rent, markup, and asset-entry pressures that would otherwise undo gains,
- reduce privatized essential-risk exposure that is forcing the lower and middle distribution to hoard precautionary wealth,
- reduce debt encumbrance where broad ownership is too fragile to survive ordinary shocks,
- harden loss-sharing, recovery, and conditionality rules where large private downside is still likely to spill quickly onto the public balance sheet,
- recapture more of the value created by infrastructure, zoning, and public-land decisions where those channels are feeding private windfalls without widening access,
- broaden owner voice where pension or fund capitalism is widening exposure without widening stewardship,
- pull asset access forward in the life course where the system is relying too heavily on late inheritance,
- correct person-level ownership skew through joint-titling, pension-credit, account-access, inheritance, and marital-property enforcement where household-level measures are too flattering,
- make broad-middle beneficiaries visible so reform is not experienced only as punishment from above,
- institutionalise ex ante and ex post distributional analysis so the correction package can actually be tested and revised rather than asserted, and explicitly distinguish entry-opening gains from incumbent-only asset appreciation.[S04][S05][S07][S10][S13][S19][S20][S26][S31]

### Governing idea

Correct concentration before it hardens into a self-defending political order or a semi-hereditary asset regime.

## Mode 3 — Anti-oligarchy response

Use anti-oligarchy response mode when **multiple red lines are breached**, or when a major red-line breach coexists with clear wealth-to-rule conversion.

Typical signs:

- the top tail is strong enough to veto reform,
- lobbying and political-finance systems are opaque,
- anonymous or intermediated money shapes politics,
- procurement and licensing channels are capture-prone,
- beneficial ownership opacity hides both concentration and influence,[S15]
- media concentration or covert sponsorship lets large fortunes set the public agenda with weak accountability,
- headline wealth shares improve somewhat while control of key assets remains tightly concentrated,[S16]
- most large-firm stewardship sits with a narrow asset-manager layer while beneficiaries remain largely voiceless,[S26]
- adult ownership and security are visibly closing around birth background and family transfer,
- and younger adults are being asked to wait until too late in life for secure tenure, positive wealth, or first durable capital.[S05][S18][S19]

### Priority package

- harden political-finance and lobbying law quickly,
- require beneficial-ownership transparency and usable asset records,
- tighten procurement, competition, and conflict-of-interest safeguards,
- compress dynastic and windfall accumulation more aggressively,
- build public/social wealth fast enough that ordinary households gain visible countervailing power,
- democratise stewardship in major retirement and pooled-investment channels where mass ownership remains largely silent,
- and pair deconcentration with strong social-floor protections, timing repair, debt-fragility reduction, person-level ownership repair, group-specific barrier removal, anti-rent and anti-revaluation measures that reopen entry, strict rescue governance and upside capture where public backstops are unavoidable, and a public distributional-budgeting discipline so wealth reform does not leave households exposed while elites regroup or while excluded groups are left behind.[S04][S05][S07][S10][S13][S15][S17][S19][S21][S22][S24][S25][S26][S27][S28][S29][S30][S31]

### Governing idea

Once wealth is already reproducing rule, polite sequencing becomes insufficient. Deconcentration and anti-capture must run in parallel. rev0006 adds that once wealth is also reproducing **closure**, anti-dynasty measures cannot wait for a gentler later phase. rev0007 adds that when concentration reliably **rebuilds itself**, governments need durable anti-rent, anti-markup, anti-avoidance, and public-wealth tools rather than one-off corrective gestures.

## Decision shortcut

- **Zero major red-line breaches** → prevention.
- **One breach** → correction.
- **Two or more breaches, or one breach plus overt capture, dynastic closure, severe life-course timing failure, obvious rapid reconcentration, severe over-privatized insecurity, highly concentrated silent-ownership stewardship, severe internal household dependence hidden by household-level measures, or entrenched group-level exclusion hidden by national averages, or deep implementation incapacity that leaves the state unable to test or correct major distributional measures, or headline wealth broadening that is clearly being purchased through scarcity rents, markups, and entrant exclusion, or a regime in which concentrated private downside is still routinely socialised with weak burden sharing or recovery, or a development regime in which major public action repeatedly creates private windfalls while public wealth and affordable access remain weak** → anti-oligarchy response.

This is a constitutional judgment tool, not a mechanical algorithm. The value is that it forces the archive to answer: **how bad is the regime, really, and what force does that level of badness warrant?**
