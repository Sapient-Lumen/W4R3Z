# Social inheritance, public wealth, and baby bonds

## Thesis

If private wealth reproduces itself through family lines, a democratic society needs some countervailing mechanism of **social inheritance**: a universal or near-universal capital stake, larger for those born into less wealth, delivered through publicly governed institutions.

## Why this matters

Current inequality regimes let some children inherit portfolios while others inherit debt, precarious housing, and thin options. A one-time adult capital grant cannot eliminate inequality, but it can change the starting line in a way that income support alone does not.

The Urban Institute's modeling of a U.S. federal baby-bonds proposal finds that such a program would increase wealth for all affected families, disproportionately benefit Black and Hispanic families, and reduce projected racial-ethnic wealth gaps; in their modeled proposal, average balances at age 18 were estimated around $26,000 to $27,000 for Black and Hispanic recipients and about $18,000 for white recipients.[S12]

rev0006 adds a tighter reading: social inheritance matters not only because it broadens ownership, but because it weakens the **birth lottery** itself. rev0012 adds a further timing claim: social inheritance should arrive **early enough** to shape adulthood rather than merely soften later-life inequality. rev0013 adds a person-level claim: social inheritance should usually be routed to persons, not merely to households, so it creates direct title and direct exit-secure ownership. rev0014 adds a group-level claim: social inheritance should be designed and monitored so it actually reaches groups that have been persistently excluded from ordinary asset-building channels. OECD's 2025 opportunity work explicitly includes child development accounts, capital and inheritance taxation, and social housing in the policy mix for levelling the playing field.[S18][S19][S27][S29]

## Design principles

- universal base, progressive supplements,
- automatic enrollment,
- professionally managed public fund structure,
- restricted use for durable wealth-building purposes,
- anti-capture rules so colleges, landlords, or lenders cannot simply absorb the grant.

## Floor connection

rev0005 adds a narrower warning: social inheritance is a **floor-completing** institution, not a substitute for the floor itself.

A society still fails if people receive a long-run account but lack liquid buffers, secure housing, or relief from predatory debt.[S07][S17]
The point is to give ordinary households a lasting capital claim **in addition to** the near-term resilience that keeps them from selling tomorrow to survive today.

## Circulation connection

rev0006 adds a second warning: social inheritance is only successful if it reopens entry into assets for people without wealthy parents.

So evaluate it by asking:

- did it reduce the importance of parental wealth for first-asset acquisition?
- did it widen ownership without simply bidding up scarce assets?
- did it arrive early enough in life to matter before family formation, housing lockout, or debt burdens set trajectories?
- did it reduce dependence on late inheritance as the main way non-rich households ever get a stake?[S05][S19]
- did it create direct person-level claim rather than only pooled household benefit?[S27][S29][S30]
- did take-up and long-run asset effects reach historically disadvantaged groups rather than mainly already-advantaged households?[S19]

## Public wealth connection

This is one reason positive public wealth matters. WIR 2026 emphasizes that many rich regions now have near-zero or negative public wealth, weakening their capacity to invest in collective goods.[S02] A society that wants broad capital ownership cannot rely only on taxing yesterday's private fortunes; it also needs publicly governed assets that generate tomorrow's common returns. rev0018 adds a narrower funding and balance-sheet claim here: when infrastructure, rezoning, and strategic public-land decisions create large site-value uplifts, part of that value should normally flow back into public/social wealth rather than capitalising almost entirely into private fortunes.[S34][S35]

## Working institutional options

- baby bonds / public trust accounts,
- social wealth funds,
- public venture or dividend funds,
- universal retirement seed accounts,
- permanent social housing and land banks as non-cash collective wealth,
- land-value-capture and strategic-public-land instruments that turn publicly created uplift into durable common assets.

## Social-floor guardrail

rev0008 adds a guardrail: social inheritance should **complement**, not substitute for, healthcare, pensions, housing security, and other durable social protections.
A society that gives young adults capital grants while leaving them exposed to major health, care, education, or old-age risks is still forcing too much self-insurance onto households.[S21][S22]
