# Political firewalls against wealth rule

## Thesis

If immoral wealth inequality is partly about **domination**, then redistribution alone is not enough.
A society also needs institutions that make it harder to convert money into law, regulation, procurement, media agenda-setting, and enforcement leniency.

## Why this belongs in the archive

OECD's Anti-Corruption and Integrity Outlook 2024 says the basic elements of lobbying frameworks are in place in **less than half of OECD countries**, and that low transparency around lobbying increases the risk that policymaking is **unduly influenced**.[S15] The same OECD report flags anonymous donations as a serious concern in many countries, notes that many political parties do not comply with transparency regulations, and says most countries do not know whether they are mitigating revolving-door risks.[S15]

A serious inequality program therefore has to block the channels by which concentrated wealth protects itself.

## Program elements

### 1. Political-finance hardening

- full, timely disclosure of large donations and intermediated spending,
- low disclosure thresholds for meaningful influence,
- limits on dark-money routing,
- public matching or public finance structures that reduce dependence on large donors.

### 2. Lobbying transparency

- mandatory lobbying registers,
- disclosure of clients, targets, topics, and spending,
- disclosure rules for digital influence, think-tank funding, and third-party campaigns,
- searchable public logs of meetings and submissions.[S15]

### 3. Revolving-door and conflict controls

- cooling-off periods,
- stronger declarations of interest,
- verification and sanctions,
- rules for advisory bodies and expert panels so they cannot become unacknowledged capture routes.[S15]

### 4. Beneficial ownership and shell transparency

- public or tightly accessible beneficial-ownership records,
- link ownership transparency to procurement, subsidy receipt, and regulated sectors,
- use the same infrastructure for tax enforcement and anti-corruption.

### 5. Procurement and competition integrity

- transparent tendering,
- ownership disclosure for bidders,
- monitoring of repeat winners and suspicious concentration,
- competition-policy independence strong enough to resist corporate pressure.

### 6. Stewardship and proxy-power safeguards

- voting-policy disclosure for large asset managers and major pension / pooled-investment channels,
- member or asset-owner direction mechanisms where feasible,
- scrutiny of concentrated proxy and stewardship bottlenecks,
- conflict rules where the same intermediaries sit across large parts of the corporate economy without equivalent beneficiary voice.[S26]

### 7. Media-pluralism safeguards

- ownership transparency,
- rules against covert influence and disguised sponsorship,
- limits on concentration where private wealth can set the public agenda without accountability.

### 8. Rescue-governance safeguards

- clear ex ante rules that distinguish protecting critical functions from protecting incumbent wealth,
- burden-sharing and recovery requirements when public support is unavoidable,
- payout, buyback, bonus, and executive-discipline rules during and after support,
- public-upside capture through equity, warrants, levies, or comparable recovery devices where taxpayers are exposed.[S32][S33]

## Principle

Treat anti-capture institutions as **wealth-distribution infrastructure**.
If the wealthy retain privileged channels to shape rules, they can often recover losses faster than ordinary households can compound gains. rev0017 adds that they can also keep inequality constitutionally skewed if crisis support repeatedly protects concentrated private downside on softer terms than ordinary households ever receive.[S32][S33]


## Legibility layer

rev0009 makes one implication more explicit: anti-capture rules fail if the actors behind money, media stakes, procurement bids, and lobbying channels cannot be identified reliably.

So the firewall package should include:

- beneficial-ownership disclosure tied to political finance, lobbying, procurement, and major media holdings,
- conflict-of-interest screening that looks through shell entities and nominees,
- sanctions for non-disclosure or false disclosure that are strong enough to matter,
- data systems that allow regulators to connect donors, contractors, owners, and officeholders when the same underlying controllers recur.[S15][S23]

A democracy should not have to guess who is trying to rule it. rev0011 adds that it also should not have to guess **who is voting the capital of millions of households on their behalf**.[S26]
