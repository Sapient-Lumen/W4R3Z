# Wealth legibility, registries, and enforcement

## Thesis

A morally acceptable wealth order must be **visible enough to govern**.
If major fortunes can be hidden behind trusts, shell companies, nominee owners, fragmented land records, or weak cross-border reporting, then the society cannot reliably measure concentration, tax large fortunes and transfers, police capture, or tell whether reform is actually working.[S04][S23]

This is not a sixth distributive axis in the same sense as shares or circulation.
It is an **implementation precondition**.
A society may announce a humane target, but if top-tail ownership is materially illegible, the target is only partly constitutional and mostly aspirational.

## Why this matters

The archive already argues that immoral inequality is partly about domination, dynastic closure, and reconcentration.
All three get worse when ownership is hard to trace. rev0011 adds that, in an intermediated system, it is not enough to trace only the legal owner and the ultimate beneficiary; the archive also needs to know **who exercises the votes and on what mandate**.[S26]

- **Domination** becomes easier because wealth can influence politics through opaque intermediaries.
- **Dynastic preservation** becomes easier because assets can move through trusts, foundations, gifts, and lightly monitored entities.[S04]
- **Reconcentration** becomes harder to detect because top-tail wealth can migrate across wrappers faster than ordinary statistics can follow.

OECD and Global Forum reporting describes beneficial ownership transparency as critical for combating tax evasion and illicit flows, and reports that beneficial ownership transparency remains the **biggest challenge** in many reviewed jurisdictions.[S23]
The IMF's 2024 note on taxing wealth also emphasizes that reforms aimed at the top need loophole-closing, enforcement, and cross-border information sharing to work in practice.[S04]

## The legibility minimum

The archive should treat the following as a minimum visible-wealth package:

1. **Beneficial ownership visibility**
   - The state must be able to identify the natural persons who ultimately own or control major companies, trusts, foundations, partnerships, and comparable vehicles.[S23]
2. **Real-estate and land legibility**
   - Property, parcel, and title systems must allow the linking of assets to real controllers rather than only to thin legal wrappers.
3. **Transfer visibility**
   - Large gifts, inheritances, trust changes, and major inter vivos transfers must leave auditable trails.[S04]
4. **Cross-border information reach**
   - Tax authorities need workable access to financial-account, entity, and other exchange channels across jurisdictions.[S04][S23]
5. **Registry interoperability**
   - Company, land, procurement, political-finance, and tax data should be linkable enough to detect concentration, conflicts, and disguised self-dealing.
6. **Top-tail enforcement capacity**
   - The state must be able to audit complex structures, value hard-to-price assets, and sustain litigation against sophisticated avoidance.
7. **Stewardship legibility in pooled ownership**
   - Where assets are held through funds, pensions, insurers, or nominees, beneficiaries and regulators should be able to tell who votes, who sets engagement policy, and whether those mandates can be directed or contested.[S26]

## What should count as failure

The archive should treat the following as strong warning signs:

- major property, firm, or fund ownership remains effectively anonymous,
- trusts and similar arrangements routinely defeat inheritance, gift, or wealth-transfer scrutiny,
- large political donations, media stakes, procurement awards, or land accumulations cannot be tied to ultimate owners,
- top-tail compliance is inferred rather than demonstrated,
- reform depends on data that the state cannot consistently collect, reconcile, or verify,
- large pools of household capital are visible financially but opaque in stewardship terms.

A country that reports improving shares while leaving these blind spots in place should be judged cautiously.
Part of the apparent gain may be measurement failure.

## Program surface

Legibility is not only a tax-administration topic.
It belongs to the full anti-inequality program:

- **measurement** — without visible ownership, concentration metrics are soft;
- **tax** — capital-gains, inheritance, and transfer rules are only as strong as the traceability of assets and controllers;
- **anti-capture** — anonymous or intermediated ownership weakens donor, lobbying, procurement, and media safeguards;
- **housing and land** — anti-rent policy is blunted if concentrated ownership can be fragmented across shells;
- **public trust** — households are less likely to support demanding redistribution when they believe the top can simply disappear behind wrappers.

## Design principles

1. **Trace the natural person**
   - Do not stop at the legal entity if real control sits elsewhere.
2. **Prefer narrow, durable state capacity over theatrical disclosure**
   - Public registers matter, but so do verification, sanctions, and the ability to use the information operationally.
3. **Link legibility to actual policy triggers**
   - Registry work should directly support tax enforcement, procurement screening, anti-monopoly review, and media-pluralism monitoring.
4. **Do not use privacy rhetoric to excuse oligarchic opacity**
   - Privacy for ordinary households is not the same as secrecy for societally powerful concentrations.
5. **Sequence for use, not symbolism**
   - Start with the asset classes and channels most connected to capture, tax leakage, and everyday extraction.

## Compact program elements

- beneficial-ownership registers with verification and update duties,
- integrated gift / inheritance / trust reporting rules,
- linked land-cadastre and company-ownership data where feasible,
- strong audit and valuation teams for high-net-worth structures,
- cross-border information-sharing capacity,
- procurement, lobbying, and political-finance systems that reference ultimate beneficial owners,
- voting and stewardship disclosure rules for major pooled-investment channels,
- publication or controlled disclosure rules proportionate to the public power of the asset or actor involved.

## Archive rule

The archive should therefore read a good-looking wealth distribution with one more question attached:

**Is this distribution visible enough to govern, tax, and defend democratically?**

If not, the society should be treated as only partially compliant even when some headline measures improve.
