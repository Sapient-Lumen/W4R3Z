# Measurement and scoreboard

## Five-axis scoreboard

rev0007 makes the archive's operational target explicit in a stronger way.
A country should now be judged across five linked axes:

1. **shares** — how much wealth broad slices own,
2. **floor** — whether ordinary households have resilient assets,
3. **control** — whether key assets, and the stewardship over pooled capital, remain too concentrated,
4. **circulation** — whether adult asset position remains too strongly inherited,
5. **reconcentration pressure** — whether the system keeps rebuilding concentration after reform.

Those five axes should be interpreted with nine cross-cutting calibrations:

- **how much security is socialised rather than forced back onto household balance sheets**,
- **how visible the relevant ownership and transfer channels actually are**,
- **how debt-encumbered ordinary ownership actually is**,
- **how much broad household capital comes with real voice rather than silent beneficial exposure**,
- **how early in the life course ordinary households reach a first durable foothold**,
- **how much wealth that looks broad at the household level is actually held and controllable by persons inside households**,
- **how much apparently broad national wealth still bypasses historically subordinated groups**,
- **how much broad wealth growth is actually being generated through open access rather than scarcity rents and incumbent revaluation**,
- **how much concentrated private downside is still being shifted onto taxpayers or public balance sheets**.

## Axis 1 — Share scoreboard

1. **Bottom 50% wealth share**
   - Main anti-assetlessness metric.
2. **Middle 40% wealth share**
   - Main broad-middle metric.
3. **Top 1% and top 0.1% wealth shares**
   - Main oligarchy / domination metric.
4. **Net public wealth**
   - Main collective-capacity metric.[S02]
5. **Labor share of income**
   - Main predistribution metric. WIR 2026 reports a global fall in labor's share from about 61% in 1980 to 53% in 2025.[S02]

## Axis 2 — Asset-floor scoreboard

6. **Positive-net-wealth rate in the bottom 50%**
   - Persistent negative wealth should become unusual rather than common.[S16]
7. **Liquid-buffer / financial-fragility rate**
   - Main resilience metric. OECD policy work highlights the importance of having enough liquid assets to stay above the poverty line for at least three months after a sudden income loss.[S17]
8. **Low-income housing cost overburden and secure-tenure access**
   - Main housing-floor metric.[S07]
9. **Bottom-half participation in durable long-run asset claims**
   - Retirement wealth, cooperative equity, social inheritance accounts, or other lasting claims.
10. **Debt-distress / debt-overhang incidence in the bottom half**
    - Tracks whether apparent wealth gains are being cancelled by extractive liabilities.

## Axis 3 — Composition, control, and stewardship dashboard

11. **Asset mix of the bottom 50%**
    - How much is liquid savings, housing equity, retirement wealth, business wealth, or debt?
12. **Asset mix of the middle 40%**
    - Is the middle diversified, or mostly dependent on one asset class such as owner housing?
13. **Concentration of voting/control rights in large firms**
    - Wealth is not only cash-value; control rights matter.
14. **Landlord / land concentration in high-pressure markets**
    - Rising housing wealth can equalise ownership for some households while landlord concentration deepens dependency for others.[S13][S16]
15. **Beneficial-ownership transparency coverage**
    - A society cannot govern concentration it cannot see.[S15][S23]
16. **Public/social ownership in strategic sectors**
    - Tracks whether collective capacity is real or only rhetorical.
17. **Stewardship concentration in intermediated ownership**
    - Track how much listed-equity voting and engagement is effectively pooled in the largest asset managers or proxy chains.[S20][S26]
18. **Pass-through / member-directed voting coverage**
    - Tracks whether asset owners, pension members, or comparable beneficiaries can shape the stewardship attached to their capital.[S26]
19. **Asset-owner mandate specificity and voting disclosure**
    - Do managers disclose enough for beneficiaries and regulators to tell whose priorities are being executed?[S26]

OECD's 2025 distributional-wealth paper remains the main composition anchor here: it reports that wealth is concentrated in the top quintile, the bottom quintile often has negative wealth, inequality is driven by financial assets, and housing is the largest household asset.[S16]
rev0011 adds a stewardship anchor from OECD's 2025 report on institutional investors, which reports very large asset-manager shares of listed equity in several markets, high company-level concentration of institutional ownership, and practical interest in pass-through voting where asset owners want more say over stewardship.[S26]

## Axis 4 — Circulation and anti-dynasty dashboard

20. **Share of inequality of opportunity explained by inherited circumstances**
    - Main openness metric. OECD's 2025 opportunity work says this is at least **over 25%** on average across the OECD.[S18]
21. **Parental-background contribution to inequality of opportunity**
    - Main birth-lottery metric. OECD reports parental background contributes **over 60%** of inequality of opportunity in most countries.[S18]
22. **Intergenerational persistence of homeownership / first-asset access**
    - Main housing-entry metric. OECD reports the parental-homeownership advantage rose to **18.7%** in 2023 in OECD European countries, from **12.7%** in 2011.[S19]
23. **Large inheritance and gift concentration**
    - Main dynastic-transfer metric.
24. **Young-adult positive-net-wealth and secure-tenure rates**
    - Main early-life foothold metric.
25. **Timing of first durable asset access / inheritance receipt**
    - Tracks whether a society is relying too heavily on late inheritance or delayed footholds. OECD's 2025 opportunity work says transfers have a larger wealth premium when received earlier in life, while OECD's inheritance-tax report warns that inheritance age is likely to rise as wealth remains concentrated among older cohorts for longer.[S05][S19]

## Axis 5 — Reconcentration-pressure dashboard

26. **Labor-share trend and wage-to-profit drift**
    - If labor's share keeps falling, broad wealth gains are unlikely to endure.[S02][S14]
27. **Markup / concentration trend in major sectors**
    - Tracks whether product-market power is quietly rebuilding extraction.[S10][S20]
28. **Housing-price growth relative to first-entry affordability**
    - Distinguishes broadening ownership from incumbent windfalls.[S07][S13][S19]
29. **Top-tail effective-tax and compliance gap**
    - Tracks whether the largest fortunes are genuinely within reach of the tax system.[S04][S06]
30. **Public/social-wealth growth relative to private-asset booms**
    - Tests whether public balance sheets keep pace when private wealth surges.[S02]

## Cross-cutting calibration — Social floor and required private wealth

rev0008 adds a cross-cutting calibration rather than a sixth axis: ask how much essential risk is being pushed back onto households.

31. **Health out-of-pocket burden / catastrophic health spending**
   - A rise here means households need more precautionary private wealth just to remain secure.[S21]
32. **Pension adequacy / first-tier protection / old-age poverty risk**
   - A weak retirement floor means the archive should read higher private saving partly as forced self-insurance rather than freedom.[S22]
33. **Other essential-service exposure**
   - Where data are available, track whether housing, care, education, or disability costs are forcing asset depletion, delayed family formation, or excess indebtedness.[S07][S21][S22]

Use these measures to interpret, not replace, the five main axes.
A country can improve private wealth shares while still failing if basic security remains too privatized.

## Cross-cutting implementation precondition — Visible wealth

34. **Beneficial-ownership registry coverage and verification quality**
   - Coverage matters, but so do verification, update duties, and sanction credibility.[S23]
35. **Land / company / trust / tax data linkability**
   - Tracks whether the state can connect the main ownership surfaces rather than reading each in isolation.
36. **Automatic or on-request cross-border information reach**
   - Major top-tail enforcement test for offshore wealth and entity opacity.[S04][S23]
37. **High-net-worth audit and valuation capacity**
   - Tracks whether the state can meaningfully enforce against sophisticated structures rather than only against transparent wage earners.

Use these measures to judge whether the five-axis scoreboard is genuinely governable or only partly observed.

## Cross-cutting calibration — Encumbrance and debt quality

38. **Household debt-to-disposable-income and distribution by age / wealth group**
   - High aggregate debt does not prove failure by itself, but it is a warning sign when broad ownership depends on large future payment streams.[S24]
39. **Debt-service stress, arrears, delinquency, and forced-sale risk**
   - Main fragility metric for distinguishing durable ownership from thin equity under pressure.[S17][S25]
40. **Leverage at first entry / negative-equity exposure**
   - Tracks whether asset access requires dangerous leverage for households without parental help.[S13][S19][S24]

Use these measures to interpret, not replace, the five main axes.
A country can look more equal on net wealth while still failing if broad ownership is only a leveraged residual claim.

## Cross-cutting calibration — Individual ownership and intra-household power

rev0013 adds one more cross-cutting calibration: ask whether household-level wealth is translating into **person-level independence**.

41. **Person-level title or co-title coverage for primary housing and other major assets**
   - Tracks whether adults have direct claim rather than only presumed benefit through household membership.[S28][S29]
42. **Individual account access and direct pension-entitlement coverage**
   - Tests whether adults have direct liquid control and direct long-run claims rather than only indirect household pooling.[S28][S30]
43. **Person-level wealth gaps where pooling is limited or absent**
   - Use singles, separated adults, widows/widowers, or other person-level data where available to detect gaps hidden by household aggregation.[S13][S30]
44. **Exit-security / marital-property / inheritance-rights quality**
   - Tracks whether the lower-earning or care-burdened adult can leave, inherit, or retain a fair claim without severe asset collapse.[S29]

Use these measures to interpret, not replace, the five main axes.
A country can look broad-owner at the household level while still failing morally if person-level title, liquid control, pension rights, or exit protection remain badly skewed.

## Cross-cutting calibration — Group stratification and excluded groups

rev0014 adds one more cross-cutting calibration: ask whether apparently broad national wealth is actually becoming **open across relevant groups**.

45. **Disaggregated median / mean wealth and positive-wealth rates for historically subordinated groups**
   - Main check against national averages laundering group closure.[S13]
46. **Group gaps in secure tenure, homeownership, and first-asset access**
   - Tracks whether entry channels remain systematically slower or more closed across groups.[S13][S19]
47. **Group gaps in account access, pension participation, land title, business ownership, or entrepreneurship finance where data exist**
   - Tests whether asset-building routes are open in practice rather than only in law.[S19]
48. **Equality-data coverage and usability for wealth analysis**
   - Measures whether the state can actually see barriers by race/ethnicity, migrant status, disability, sexual orientation and gender identity, religion, caste, Indigenous status, or other locally relevant lines, with legal safeguards and privacy protection.[S13]
49. **Anti-displacement and access to high-opportunity places**
   - Tracks whether vulnerable groups are being priced or screened out of areas where wealth, jobs, schools, and services compound advantage.[S19]

Use these measures to interpret, not replace, the five main axes.
A country can improve national wealth shares and still fail morally if historically subordinated groups remain systematically excluded from the main wealth-building channels.


## Cross-cutting implementation discipline — Distributional budgeting and state capacity

rev0015 adds one more operating surface: ask whether the state can actually **carry the archive into the budget process**.

50. **Ex ante distributional-impact coverage for major budget measures**
   - Tracks whether governments test significant tax, spending, housing, pension, and wealth-building measures early enough to shape design rather than only defend a finished package.[S31]
51. **Ex post distributional evaluation and correction coverage**
   - Tracks whether governments revisit promises, compare outcomes to forecasts, and correct packages that capitalised into prices, markups, or debt pressure instead of broad security.[S31]
52. **Administrative-data linkage and model capacity**
   - Measures whether tax, transfer, pension, land, housing, and other relevant data can be linked well enough to see wealth-relevant effects rather than only income snapshots.[S31]
53. **Public distributional statements with usable disaggregation**
   - Tracks whether major budgets publish understandable distributional effects across income segments, age, gender, household form, region, and other relevant characteristics where lawful and feasible.[S31]

Use these measures to judge whether the archive's program is merely articulated or actually governable inside ordinary fiscal decision-making.


## Cross-cutting calibration — Non-extractive wealth formation

rev0016 adds one more cross-cutting calibration: ask whether apparently broad wealth is being built through **open and durable access** or through **scarcity, bottleneck power, and revaluation**.

54. **Entry-adjusted housing wealth growth**
   - Tracks whether owner gains are accompanied by stable or improving first-entry affordability rather than financed by worsening access for non-owners.[S07][S13][S19]
55. **Rent / fee / markup absorption of wage or transfer gains**
   - Tests whether broad household gains are being clawed back through landlord power, monopoly pricing, or other bottleneck charges.[S10][S20]
56. **Share of wealth growth driven by revaluation versus new saving / broad new ownership**
   - Distinguishes broadening through new entry and accumulation from broadening that mostly rewards already-positioned owners.[S13][S16]
57. **Profit and capital-gain concentration relative to productive investment dispersion**
   - Flags situations where wealth gains look strong while investment remains weak or concentrated in a small set of dominant firms.[S20]

Use these measures to interpret, not replace, the five main axes.
A country can improve wealth shares and still fail morally if the gains are mainly being manufactured through scarcity rents, markups, fees, or incumbent revaluation that worsen entry for later households.


## Cross-cutting calibration — Downside discipline and loss-bearing

rev0017 adds one more cross-cutting calibration: ask whether apparently broad wealth sits inside a regime where **major downside remains publicly absorbed**.

58. **Recovery rate of public rescue costs from shareholders, creditors, levies, equity stakes, warrants, or comparable mechanisms**
   - Tracks whether unavoidable public support is actually recovered from the sector or owners that generated the losses.[S32]
59. **Use of broad untargeted asset purchases or guarantees versus targeted function-preserving support**
   - Distinguishes stabilising households and critical functions from backstopping incumbent asset prices.[S33]
60. **Loss-sharing and restructuring intensity in major rescue episodes**
   - Tracks whether shareholders, unsecured creditors, executives, and managers bear meaningful downside before taxpayers do.[S32]
61. **Post-support payout, dividend, buyback, bonus, and restructuring discipline**
   - Tests whether support really changed the terms of risk bearing or simply preserved the upside after a brief interruption.[S32]

Use these measures to interpret, not replace, the five main axes.
A country can improve wealth shares and still fail morally if concentrated owners continue to rely on recurrent public downside protection.

## Cross-cutting calibration — Publicly created value and common-asset capture

rev0018 adds one more cross-cutting calibration: ask whether land, housing, and development wealth are being shaped by **publicly created uplift with meaningful common recapture**.

62. **Share of major infrastructure, rezoning, or development-approval decisions covered by explicit value-capture tools or binding affordability / infrastructure obligations**
   - Tracks whether collective action that raises site value is being partly socialised rather than simply privatised.[S34][S35]
63. **Affordable-housing, infrastructure, or public-purpose contributions relative to estimated land-value uplift**
   - Tests whether obligations are scaled to the gain rather than treated as token add-ons.[S35]
64. **Strategic public-land retention / leasehold / disposal discipline**
   - Tracks whether public land is managed as a long-run common asset instead of sold off cheaply with weak future public claim.[S35]
65. **Public/social-wealth gains from land-value capture relative to major publicly created uplifts**
   - Measures whether public action strengthens the common balance sheet as well as private site values.[S02][S34][S35]

Use these measures to interpret, not replace, the five main axes.
A country can improve wealth shares and still fail morally if infrastructure, zoning, and public-land decisions keep manufacturing large private windfalls with little return to common wealth or wider access.


## Cross-cutting calibration — Tax expenditures and subsidy hygiene

rev0019 adds one more implementation calibration: ask whether the state is quietly rebuilding concentration through **upside-down subsidies** delivered in the tax code or through poorly targeted wealth-building support.

66. **Fiscal cost of major asset-related tax expenditures and subsidies by income / wealth position where measurable**
   - Tracks whether hidden spending is disproportionately flowing to already-positioned owners or high-balance savers rather than to entrants or lower-wealth households.[S31][S36][S37]
67. **Share of support delivered through deductions / exclusions versus capped refundable, matched, or fixed-amount instruments**
   - Distinguishes open-ended subsidy structures that scale with tax rate, mortgage size, or account balance from flatter instruments more likely to widen access.[S37]
68. **Capitalization rate of major housing subsidies and tax preferences**
   - Tests whether home-buyer support, mortgage relief, or similar measures actually widen entry or mostly raise bids, land prices, and incumbent windfalls.[S31][S36]
69. **Distribution of retirement-saving support across low, middle, and high earners**
   - Flags cases where the state calls a savings subsidy inclusive while the strongest behavioural effect and largest tax benefit sit higher up the income distribution.[S37]

Use these measures to judge whether the public sector is broadening ownership cleanly or quietly financing incumbent advantage through the hidden wealth state.

- non-compete prevalence,

- campaign-finance concentration,
- beneficial-ownership transparency coverage,
- registry interoperability and trust reporting quality,
- ratio of large inheritances to median lifetime earnings,
- age at first meaningful asset ownership without parental transfer,
- median or distributional age of large inheritance receipt,
- share of major retirement or pooled-fund assets under member-directed or pass-through voting,
- concentration of proxy-voting power among the largest managers,
- share of primary residences with joint or otherwise equal person-level title,
- person-level pension-entitlement gaps and individual-account access gaps,
- distributional outcomes after divorce, widowhood, or household dissolution,
- disaggregated wealth, secure-tenure, and credit gaps across historically subordinated groups,
- equality-data coverage for wealth, land, and business-asset analysis,
- share of wealth growth coming from house-price or financial-asset revaluation versus new saving and new entry,
- affordability-adjusted owner gains in high-pressure housing markets,
- rent / markup absorption of wage, transfer, or tax-policy gains,
- share of major rescue or backstop costs recovered from owners, creditors, or the sector,
- frequency of broad untargeted asset support relative to targeted household / function support,
- share of major upzoning or infrastructure projects with explicit land-value capture or affordability obligations,
- public-land disposals with affordability, leasehold, or public-purpose conditions,

### Prevention mode

Use when the society is still inside the archive's red lines, anti-capture systems are functioning at a minimally credible level, key asset classes are not strongly concentrated in opaque oligarchic form, major ownership is visible enough for enforcement to work, floor failure is not widespread, inherited advantage is not tightening its grip over entry into ownership and adult security, reconcentration pressure is not obviously undoing broad gains, essential risks are not being pushed back onto households at clearly punitive levels, broad household ownership is not obviously dependent on fragile leverage, broad financial claims are not mostly silent while stewardship remains narrowly bottlenecked, and ordinary households are reaching first durable footholds early enough that the system is not relying on delayed inheritance to look open, and household-level broad wealth is not masking large person-level dependence inside households, and broad national gains are not still bypassing historically subordinated groups, and apparent broad gains are not mainly being generated through scarcity rents, fee extraction, markups, or incumbent revaluation that worsen later entry, and major concentrated downside is not being routinely transferred onto taxpayers or public balance sheets.[S05][S07][S10][S13][S17][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S24][S25][S26][S27][S28][S29][S30][S32][S33]

### Correction mode

Use when **one red line is breached**: for example bottom-half wealth is too thin, the top tail is too thick, public wealth remains persistently negative, housing overburden is structurally high, widespread financial fragility reaches well into the middle, ownership of key assets remains too concentrated despite improving headline shares, parental background becomes too strong a predictor of adult asset position, broad gains are being steadily reabsorbed through rents, markups, and top-tail reconcentration, essential risks are being pushed back onto households too far, broad ownership is too debt-fragile, capital exposure broadens while stewardship stays pooled in a narrow intermediary layer, or secure ownership arrives too late for younger and non-inheriting households, or person-level title and exit security remain badly skewed inside apparently secure households, or excluded groups remain structurally shut out of wealth-building channels despite aggregate gains, or broad gains are coming mainly through scarcity rents, markups, fees, or incumbent revaluation that tightens entry for later households, or concentrated owners can still rely on repeated public downside protection during stress, or infrastructure, rezoning, and public-land decisions keep generating large private windfalls with weak common recapture.[S05][S07][S10][S13][S17][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S24][S25][S26][S27][S28][S29][S30][S32][S33][S34][S35]

### Anti-oligarchy response mode

Use when **multiple red lines are breached**, or when a major breach coexists with obvious wealth-to-rule conversion, entrenched dynastic closure, timing failure so severe that early adulthood remains assetless without family wealth, material opacity, rapid reconcentration, severe over-privatized insecurity, or stewardship concentration so extreme that mass ownership remains largely voiceless, or internal household dependence is severe enough that household-level broadening is clearly misleading, or group-based exclusion remains entrenched enough that national broadening is obviously overstated, or the state still lacks the budget-process capacity to test, publish, and correct the distributional effects of major packages in time to matter, or the apparent broadening of wealth is clearly being purchased through scarcity rents, bottleneck pricing, and entrant exclusion, or public authorities repeatedly absorb major downside for concentrated private risk with weak recovery or burden sharing, or a development regime repeatedly gives away socially created land and planning uplift while public wealth and affordable access remain weak, or the tax code keeps delivering top-heavy asset subsidies that widen incumbent advantage while reform claims to broaden ownership.[S05][S07][S10][S15][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S26][S27][S28][S29][S30][S31][S32][S33][S34][S35][S36][S37]

The point of the trigger ladder is to stop the archive from treating all unequal societies as equally bad, while also stopping it from treating better headline shares as enough when control, legibility, encumbrance, social protection, stewardship, life-course timing, person-level ownership, group-level openness, implementation capacity inside the budget process, public-value capture, subsidy hygiene, or wealth-source quality still fail.
