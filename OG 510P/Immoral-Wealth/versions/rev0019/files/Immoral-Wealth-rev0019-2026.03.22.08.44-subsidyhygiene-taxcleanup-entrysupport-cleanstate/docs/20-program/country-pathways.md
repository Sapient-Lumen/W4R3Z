# Country pathways

## Thesis

The archive needs one moral north star but more than one implementation pathway.
A country with deep capital markets and expensive housing needs a different first move from a country whose main problem is oligarchic state capture or weak tax administration.

rev0005 added one common requirement across all pathways: each pathway must specify **how the bottom half reaches a real asset floor**, not only how the top is taxed.
rev0006 adds another: each pathway must specify **how the society reopens generational circulation**, not only how it improves the cross-sectional snapshot.
rev0008 adds a cross-cutting condition: each pathway must specify **which basic risks are socialised and which are still being pushed back onto household balance sheets**.
rev0009 adds another implementation condition: each pathway must specify **how major wealth, land, and transfer channels become legible enough to govern**. rev0011 adds another control condition: each pathway should say **whether broad household capital comes with real stewardship voice or mostly silent exposure**. rev0012 adds a circulation condition: each pathway should say **whether early adulthood gets a real foothold or mostly a promise of later inheritance**. rev0013 adds a person-level condition: each pathway should say **whether ownership broadens across adults or mainly across households on paper**. rev0014 adds a group condition: each pathway should say **whether historically subordinated groups are actually entering the main wealth-building channels or still being sorted out of them**.

## Four rough archetypes

### 1. Rich asset-price democracies

Typical pattern:

- high private wealth,
- expensive housing,
- thin bottom-half wealth,
- finance / property channels that reabsorb gains,
- high private need for self-insurance where healthcare, pensions, education, or care are costly.

Recent OECD work emphasizes that rising house prices have made it increasingly difficult for young and low-wealth households to enter the housing market, worsening wealth disparities.[S13] OECD opportunity work adds that parental homeownership is becoming more predictive of adult homeownership in many OECD European countries.[S19]

Priority stack:

- housing supply plus anti-speculation / anti-rent measures,
- liquid-buffer and debt-relief tools for the lower half,
- safer housing-entry and mortgage structures where broad ownership currently depends on dangerous leverage,
- capital-gains and inheritance reform,
- social inheritance / baby bonds,
- bargaining power,
- expansion of health, care, pension, and other social-floor protections where private self-insurance is doing too much work,
- explicit encumbrance metrics where homeowner majorities look secure in net-wealth terms but remain debt-fragile,
- political-finance and lobbying firewalls,
- beneficial-ownership, trust, and property-legibility upgrades for the top tail,
- pension and pooled-fund stewardship reform where institutional ownership is large enough that mass ownership otherwise remains mostly silent,[S26]
- joint-title, direct-account, and pension-credit reforms where household balance sheets overstate person-level independence,
- and disaggregated lending, tenure, and anti-displacement checks where aggregate gains may still be bypassing excluded groups.[S13][S27][S28][S29][S30]

### 2. Social democracies with aging homeowner majorities

Typical pattern:

- lower top-tail concentration than Anglo oligarchies,
- stronger bargaining systems,
- better public services,
- growing insider-outsider divide between older asset holders and younger entrants,
- risk that the society looks broad-owner on paper mainly because wealth is concentrated in older cohorts while younger households wait longer for entry.

OECD evidence shows bargaining coverage remains highest where multi-employer systems persist.[S14]

Priority stack:

- preserve sectoral bargaining,
- expand youth liquid and housing access,
- limit inherited advantage,
- maintain positive public wealth,
- prevent housing scarcity from rebuilding inequality through property channels,
- protect the social floor so later privatization does not silently raise required household wealth,
- keep ownership registries and trust / gift reporting strong enough that top-tail opacity does not reopen dynastic channels,
- and prevent retirement-system concentration from turning broad saving into narrow stewardship,
- while checking that younger, lower-earning, or care-burdened adults hold direct rights rather than only indirect household claims,
- and while checking whether migrant, minority, disabled, or otherwise excluded groups are entering ownership and high-opportunity places at similar rates rather than being screened out.[S13][S19][S27][S29][S30]

### 3. Middle-income oligarchic / capture-prone states

Typical pattern:

- extreme top concentration,
- weak enforcement,
- land, procurement, and political connection channels more salient than sophisticated portfolio taxation.

In these settings, integrity systems matter early. OECD notes that lobbying safeguards are among the weakest parts of integrity frameworks and that transparency gaps leave countries open to undue influence.[S15]

Priority stack:

- beneficial ownership transparency,
- procurement integrity,
- land and cadastre reform,
- integrated registries before high-complexity top-tail tax promises,
- universal services and floor-building,
- direct public, cooperative, or member-governed funds where private intermediation would otherwise recreate elite control,
- simple savings and buffer architecture,
- simpler enforceable taxes before high-complexity wealth-tax designs,
- avoid importing rich-country privatized-risk models that raise the private wealth needed for ordinary security,
- and avoid household-targeted ownership schemes that leave person-level title and exit rights unclear or weak,
- while using disaggregated data and anti-discrimination enforcement so simplified mass programs do not just reproduce old group exclusions under a new label.[S19][S28][S29]

### 4. Resource or rent-fund states

Typical pattern:

- concentrated natural-resource or sovereign-rent streams,
- danger that public wealth is privatized by elites before it becomes common wealth.

Priority stack:

- convert resource rents into transparent public wealth,
- harden governance of public funds,
- distribute part of returns through universal services, social inheritance, or citizen dividends,
- create ordinary-household liquid and housing floors rather than only sovereign assets,
- convert public rents into usable services and protections rather than balance-sheet symbolism alone,
- prevent extraction contracts and procurement from becoming oligarchic pipelines,
- tie sovereign or rent-fund governance to visible ownership and contractor disclosure,
- and route citizen-facing ownership or dividend rights to persons rather than only to household heads or informal gatekeepers,
- while tracking whether Indigenous, regional, ethnic, caste, or migrant-status exclusions are being reopened inside the distribution of resource rights.

## Cross-cutting rule

Do not import an instrument without checking **state capacity, balance-sheet structure, the main local rent channel, and the main local dynastic channel**.
A country can believe in the same moral target and still need a different sequence.

## What should still stay common across pathways

Whatever the country type, the archive keeps six constants:

1. bottom-half assetlessness is unacceptable,
2. the bottom half should gain a real asset floor, not only paper claims,
3. the top tail must not become a governing estate,
4. housing and land cannot be treated as a side issue,
5. anti-capture institutions are part of distributional policy, not an optional ethics appendix,
6. people without wealthy parents must retain real routes into ownership and security,
7. public and social provision must reduce the amount of private wealth households need merely to stay safe,
8. major ownership and transfer channels must be legible enough that the program is enforceable in fact, not only in law,
9. broad household capital should not remain mostly silent while stewardship stays bottlenecked in a narrow manager layer,[S26]
10. secure ownership should arrive early enough that the pathway is not relying mainly on delayed inheritance or older-cohort release,[S05][S13][S19]
11. broad wealth should reach adults as persons, not only households as aggregates, so title, liquid control, pension rights, and exit security are not left badly skewed,[S13][S27][S28][S29][S30]
12. broadening should also reach historically subordinated groups rather than stopping at national averages that still hide structural exclusion.[S13][S19]


## Cross-path requirement — fiscal operating discipline

Across all archetypes, the archive now treats **distributional-budgeting capacity** as an implementation multiplier.
High-capacity states should embed ex ante and ex post distributional analysis directly into the annual budget process.
Lower-capacity states should start with a smaller minimum bundle: a few linked administrative datasets, a published distributional statement for major packages, and priority modelling for housing, transfers, pensions, and tax changes rather than a universal model of everything at once.[S31]
