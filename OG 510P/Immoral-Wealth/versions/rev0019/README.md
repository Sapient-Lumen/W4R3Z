# Immoral-Wealth — rev0019

## When public support helps people already inside

Imagine a housing-entry subsidy that lets buyers bid more, but leaves the number of available homes unchanged. Some of the promised help may become a higher selling price rather than a more attainable foothold. Or imagine retirement support whose value grows with a person's tax rate and ability to contribute: the nominal invitation to save is shared, but the public help is not equally usable.

These are illustrative ways into this edition's [tax-expenditure and subsidy argument](files/Immoral-Wealth-rev0019-2026.03.22.08.44-subsidyhygiene-taxcleanup-entrysupport-cleanstate/docs/20-program/tax-expenditures-and-subsidy-hygiene.md). Its question is not simply whether a government redistributes through visible taxes and payments. It asks whether less visible preferences simultaneously rebuild the wealth of people already positioned to benefit. Support for ownership must be judged from the entrant's side as well as the incumbent's balance sheet.

The [main Immoral-Wealth guide](../../README.md) develops the larger argument about independence, domination and inherited access. Rev0019 gives that argument a concrete budget question: **who can actually use public support, and who ultimately keeps its value?**

## Follow the benefit, not just the label

The defining note separates questions that a phrase such as “middle-class support” can conceal.

- **Initial recipient:** does relief go to an existing owner, a new entrant, an employer, an intermediary or a broad base of households?
- **Delivery mechanism:** is it a deduction, exclusion, preferential rate, credit, matching contribution, fixed sum, direct provision or supply investment?
- **Price and fee effects:** after transactions and market responses, how much remains with the intended recipient?
- **Dependence on prior advantage:** does greater taxable income, a larger mortgage or a larger existing balance automatically attract more help?
- **Alternative design:** could a capped, refundable, matched, fixed-amount, common-floor or supply-side measure serve the purpose better?

The distinction between initial receipt and retained benefit is central. A household can genuinely receive assistance while another party captures part of its value through a price or fee. Conversely, a subsidy is not defeated merely by naming that possibility: the extent of capture and the available alternatives need investigation.

The note's phrase “upside-down subsidies” names a design that offers greater support through advantages the better-positioned already possess. It is not a claim that every housing preference, retirement incentive or public subsidy has the same effects.

## Housing and retirement test different mechanisms

Read the [housing and land argument](files/Immoral-Wealth-rev0019-2026.03.22.08.44-subsidyhygiene-taxcleanup-entrysupport-cleanstate/docs/20-program/housing-land-and-anti-monopoly.md) beside the subsidy note. A rise in current owners' asset values can coincide with harder entry for the next household. The proposed response joins entry support to attention to supply, land, rents and control; it does not treat a larger purchasing allowance as sufficient evidence of wider access.

Retirement support presents a related but different problem. A design routed through tax relief and large contributions may be most usable by those already able to save. The note therefore considers matching contributions, fixed sums, refundable credits, defaults and stronger public pensions where private incentives are fiscally costly and weakly targeted. The archive favors these redesigns over open-ended, rate-driven relief, but does not establish a universal ranking or promise that any one device eliminates unequal access. The point is to inspect how an instrument works rather than infer its distribution from its benevolent name.

## Put the hidden budget inside the evaluation

The [distributional-budgeting essay](files/Immoral-Wealth-rev0019-2026.03.22.08.44-subsidyhygiene-taxcleanup-entrysupport-cleanstate/docs/20-program/distributional-budgeting-and-state-capacity.md) brings these preferences into the same scrutiny as direct expenditure. For distributional purposes, foregone tax revenue should not disappear merely because it sits on the revenue side of a budget.

Its proposed discipline has a sequence: examine likely effects before adoption, publish who gains and what remains uncertain, then check outcomes and revise the design. Relevant outcomes include usable buffers, debt pressure, secure tenure, timely entry, individual claims and access across groups, not only annual income or aggregate asset values. Linking administrative and survey evidence is a proposed means of making effects visible, with confidentiality protected; it is not an implemented system supplied by this edition.

This also qualifies the [tax program](files/Immoral-Wealth-rev0019-2026.03.22.08.44-subsidyhygiene-taxcleanup-entrysupport-cleanstate/docs/20-program/tax-capital-gains-inheritance-and-wealth.md). Raising a visible tax while leaving another channel that replenishes incumbent advantage may undermine the package's own purpose. The critique is of the combination, not a demand for one universal wealth tax or indiscriminate subsidy removal.

## What this checkpoint adds, and what remains to show

The [changelog](files/Immoral-Wealth-rev0019-2026.03.22.08.44-subsidyhygiene-taxcleanup-entrysupport-cleanstate/CHANGELOG.md) identifies rev0019 as a compact addition: one program note, two source anchors and targeted changes to measurement, budgeting, housing, tax and sequencing. Earlier revisions already examined publicly created land-value gains and who absorbs private losses. This edition turns to the quieter allocation decisions embedded in routine asset-building support.

The [objections and failure modes](files/Immoral-Wealth-rev0019-2026.03.22.08.44-subsidyhygiene-taxcleanup-entrysupport-cleanstate/docs/20-program/counterarguments-and-failure-modes.md) keep capitalization, avoidance, backlash and public-fund capture in view. A reader should also ask what evidence would show that the proposed redesign really widened entry rather than merely moved the benefit elsewhere. The framework supplies questions and institutional proposals; it does not establish their effects by repeating them across documents. The original [source ledger](files/Immoral-Wealth-rev0019-2026.03.22.08.44-subsidyhygiene-taxcleanup-entrysupport-cleanstate/SOURCES.md) is the route into its empirical premises, rather than treating an archived summary as a current policy verdict.
