# Predistribution: labor and wages

## What this note is for

This note turns one of the archive's oldest upstream claims into a **stable operating stack**.
A society can tighten inheritance tax, tax capital gains better, or even compress the top tail somewhat and still rebuild immoral wealth inequality if ordinary households keep entering adult life through weak bargaining positions, low or eroding wage floors, unstable hours, and contingent benefits.
So the archive treats **labor-market power and earning power** as part of wealth-distribution infrastructure rather than as a separate income-side chapter.[S02][S08][S09][S11][S14]

## Minimum operating rule

Any serious wealth-broadening package should do six things at once:

1. raise **worker bargaining power** rather than relying only on later redistribution,
2. keep **wage floors** from eroding in real terms,
3. reduce **employer monopsony and artificial mobility barriers**,
4. extend enforceable standards through **fissured, temporary, and contingent work**,
5. lower the amount of security workers must privately self-insure through firm-tied benefits or private buffers,
6. and make sure higher earnings can actually become **lower debt, safer saving, and earlier footholds** rather than being immediately reabsorbed through rent, fees, or other tolls.[S08][S09][S11][S14][S17][S21][S22][S40][S41]

No single minimum-wage rise, tax credit, or short labor-market boom is enough if the rest of the stack still leaves ordinary workers weakly positioned every year.

## The main false passes

### 1. Employment is high but worker power stays weak

A country should count less labor-side success when unemployment is temporarily low but bargaining coverage, worker voice, and standard-setting institutions continue to weaken.
That leaves the lower half dependent on short-cycle tightness instead of durable wage-setting power.[S08][S09][S11][S14]

### 2. Pay rises but households do not get more saveable room

Nominal wage gains count less when housing costs, debt service, health costs, care costs, or other forced private burdens absorb them before they become durable security or wealth.[S07][S17][S21][S22]

### 3. Asset-building is asked to compensate for chronically weak pay

A society should count less wealth-side success when it tells households to solve weak earnings through retirement tax incentives, dangerous leverage, or individual saving heroics.
Predistribution matters because households with structurally weak earnings are being asked to save out of too little.[S17][S21][S22][S37]

### 4. Rights exist on paper but disappear in contingent or fragmented work

Labor standards count less when subcontracting, temporary work, platform models, misclassification, weak take-up, or firm-tied portability gaps make the rights hard to claim or keep in ordinary working life.[S09][S11][S40][S41]

## The standing labor and earning-power stack

### 1. Collective bargaining and worker voice

Core moves:

- preserve or rebuild collective bargaining coverage,
- use sectoral, pattern, coordinated, or extension mechanisms where fragmented firm-by-firm bargaining leaves too many workers uncovered,
- protect worker voice and representation,
- and treat bargaining institutions as part of the architecture that stops productivity and power asymmetries from flowing almost entirely upward.[S08][S09][S11][S14]

### 2. Robust wage floors and predictable uprating

Core moves:

- maintain meaningful statutory or bargained wage floors,
- update them regularly enough that inflation does not quietly hollow them out,
- use transparent review and uprating rules,
- and remember that a wage floor is a **floor**, not a substitute for bargaining, enforcement, or broader wealth policy.[S09][S11][S14]

### 3. Anti-monopsony, mobility, and no-lock-in rules

Core moves:

- restrict non-competes and non-poaching arrangements where they trap workers,
- reduce employer wage-setting power in concentrated labor markets,
- protect the ability to move to better firms,
- and read labor-market dynamism from the worker side: mobility matters when it actually improves outside options and access to higher-quality firms.[S08][S09]

### 4. Enforcement against fissuring, wage theft, and false self-employment

Core moves:

- strengthen labor inspection and standards enforcement,
- make sure firms cannot evade minimum standards by pushing work into contractors, temporary chains, or platform wrappers,
- treat misclassification and routine under-enforcement as distributive problems rather than technical leakage,
- and judge the labor stack partly by whether low-power workers can claim the rights formally announced for them.[S09][S11][S40]

### 5. Portable protections and lower forced self-insurance

Core moves:

- reduce dependence on single-employer benefit attachment where it strands or disciplines workers,
- improve portability across jobs, sectors, and statuses,
- pair labor reform with healthcare, pension, care, and income-security systems that lower forced private buffer needs,
- and stop mobility from being punished by benefit gaps or forfeiture.[S17][S21][S22][S40][S41]

### 6. Worker-side claim on productivity gains

Core moves:

- strengthen channels through which productivity gains reach wages rather than only markups or upper-tail compensation,
- where institutionally feasible, widen worker voice in firm governance or wage-setting beyond narrow managerial discretion,
- and read wage growth together with labor's share so the archive can tell whether ordinary work is still financing concentration from below.[S02][S08][S09][S11]

## How to read the stack

### 1. This note is upstream, but not sufficient on its own

The archive does **not** say that wages solve wealth inequality by themselves.
A society can raise wages and still fail if housing entry closes, inheritance dominates, top-tail capital income stays lightly taxed, or common wealth remains too weak.
But a society that neglects bargaining power and earning power is forcing every later wealth policy to fight a fresh headwind each year.

### 2. Judge predistribution by whether it creates room to hold and keep assets

Ask whether stronger labor institutions are producing:

- more liquid resilience,
- less debt dependence,
- earlier saving,
- safer housing entry,
- and less desperation selling or borrowing.

If the answer is no, then the labor-side gains are not yet cashing out as wealth broadening.[S17][S24][S25]

### 3. Labor reform and anti-rent reform are complements

The archive does not want a society where wage gains are immediately re-taxed by landlords, gatekeepers, or concentrated firms.
So the labor stack should be read together with housing, anti-monopoly, and subsidy-cleanup work rather than as a substitute for them.[S07][S08][S10][S36]

## Practical shortcut

Ask seven things:

1. are bargaining coverage and worker voice broadening or still eroding,
2. do wage floors update predictably enough to stop quiet real erosion,
3. can workers move to better firms or are monopsony and lock-in rules trapping them,
4. are standards still real inside temporary, subcontracted, or platform work,
5. can workers change jobs without losing core protections,
6. are higher earnings becoming lower debt and earlier footholds rather than higher rents and fees,
7. and is capital still pulling away so fast that labor-side repair is being outrun from above?

If several of those answers are weak at once, the archive should assume that later wealth-broadening policy is still fighting an unnecessary and permanent upstream headwind.
