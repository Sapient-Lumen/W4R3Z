# Political firewalls against wealth rule

## What this note is for

This note turns the archive's framework claim in [`../10-framework/wealth-to-rule-conversion.md`](../10-framework/wealth-to-rule-conversion.md) into a **program operating stack**.
A society can improve headline wealth shares and still fail morally if concentrated wealth retains privileged routes into law, regulation, procurement, media agenda-setting, or crisis bargaining.
So the archive treats **political firewalls** as part of wealth-distribution infrastructure rather than as optional ethics garnish.[S15][S23][S26][S32][S33]

## Minimum operating rule

Any serious anti-inequality program should build a visible anti-capture stack that does six things at once:

1. makes influence **legible**,
2. limits the direct purchase of access,
3. blocks hidden controller channels,
4. hardens procurement and regulatory decisions against favoritism,
5. prevents concentrated intermediaries from quietly governing pooled capital without beneficiary voice,
6. and stops crisis support from becoming a standing subsidy to incumbent wealth.[S15][S23][S26][S32][S33]

No single firewall is enough if the remaining channels stay open.
A society can have disclosure in one lane and still drift toward wealth rule through another.

## The standing firewall stack

### 1. Political-finance hardening

The first firewall is to make large-money influence harder to hide and harder to buy.

Core moves:

- full and timely disclosure of large donations and major intermediated spending,
- low thresholds for reporting meaningful influence,
- strong rules against anonymous and dark-money routing,
- auditable public records,
- and public-finance or matching structures that reduce dependence on large donors.[S15]

The point is not to ban disagreement.
It is to stop concentrated wealth from purchasing a systematically louder constitutional voice than ordinary citizens can command.

### 2. Lobbying and public-decision transparency

The second firewall is to make influence around rule-making visible while it is still influence, not only after it has become law.

Core moves:

- mandatory lobbying registers,
- disclosure of clients, topics, and target institutions,
- searchable logs of relevant meetings and submissions,
- transparency rules for think-tank funding and third-party campaigns,
- and clear digital-influence disclosure where policy advocacy is being paid for.[S15]

The archive treats this as routine democratic plumbing, not a scandal-only tool.

### 3. Conflict-of-interest and revolving-door controls

The third firewall is to narrow the route from private wealth into public discretion by way of personnel churn, undeclared interests, or advisory capture.

Core moves:

- cooling-off periods where risks are high,
- credible declarations of interest,
- verification and sanctions that matter,
- conflict rules for ministers, regulators, and senior officials,
- and governance rules for expert panels and advisory bodies so they do not become unacknowledged wealth-entry points.[S15]

The archive especially cares about this where tax, competition, finance, land, procurement, and privatization decisions are concentrated.

### 4. Beneficial ownership tied to public power

The fourth firewall is to make the real controllers behind assets and entities identifiable wherever public power is at stake.

Core moves:

- public or tightly accessible beneficial-ownership records,
- up-to-date reporting obligations with meaningful penalties,
- look-through capacity for trusts, nominees, and layered entities,
- and linkage of ownership visibility to procurement, subsidy receipt, regulated sectors, major media stakes, and other power-relevant domains.[S23]

Without this layer, anti-capture rules remain too easy to route around.
A state cannot reliably police influence, favoritism, or top-tail compliance if it cannot tell who is behind the relevant entities.

### 5. Procurement, licensing, and competition integrity

The fifth firewall is to harden the large administrative surfaces where wealth often purchases favorable treatment without changing headline legislation.

Core moves:

- transparent tendering and award records,
- ownership disclosure for bidders and concession holders,
- monitoring of repeat winners and suspicious concentration,
- clear conflict rules in awarding and supervision,
- and competition institutions with enough independence to resist dominant-firm pressure.[S15][S23]

This matters because a formally democratic legal order can still drift into wealth rule through contracts, permits, concessions, enforcement discretion, and regulatory delay.

### 6. Stewardship and proxy-power safeguards

The sixth firewall is to stop a narrow layer of intermediaries from exercising corporate power on behalf of millions of savers with too little visibility or instruction.

Core moves:

- disclosure of voting policies and stewardship mandates,
- pass-through or member-direction mechanisms where feasible,
- stronger voice for asset owners and beneficiaries,
- scrutiny of concentrated proxy and stewardship bottlenecks,
- and conflict rules where the same intermediaries sit across broad parts of the economy without equivalent beneficiary control.[S26]

This is where the archive connects anti-capture policy to intermediated ownership.
Broad financial claims do not by themselves democratize control if the votes remain bottlenecked.

### 7. Media-pluralism and information-order safeguards

The seventh firewall is to reduce the chance that concentrated wealth can dominate public agenda-setting while hiding behind formal press or sponsorship pluralism.

Core moves:

- ownership transparency,
- disclosure of sponsored advocacy and covert influence,
- scrutiny of concentration in agenda-setting channels,
- and rules strong enough to keep public discourse from becoming another quiet bottleneck of wealth rule.[S15][S23]

The archive treats this carefully because not every large media actor is oligarchic.
But a society should not have to guess who is shaping the public conversation or on whose behalf.

### 8. Rescue governance and anti-soft-landing privilege

The eighth firewall is to stop crises from becoming moments when concentrated private downside is socialized on unusually generous terms.

Core moves:

- distinguish protecting critical functions from protecting incumbent fortunes,
- require burden-sharing before or alongside public support,
- impose payout, buyback, bonus, and executive-discipline limits during support,
- favor targeted and recoverable support over broad asset-holder backstops where possible,
- and capture public upside through equity, warrants, levies, fees, or comparable recovery devices when taxpayers are exposed.[S32][S33]

This matters because repeated soft landings for concentrated actors can preserve both their balance sheets and their later bargaining power.

## How to read the firewall stack

### 1. Legibility comes early

The archive should usually build ownership visibility and influence transparency early, because every later firewall works worse when controllers remain obscure.
See [`wealth-legibility-registries-and-enforcement.md`](wealth-legibility-registries-and-enforcement.md).

### 2. One open channel can defeat several closed ones

A society can tighten donations yet still fail if procurement, shell ownership, rescue governance, or stewardship bottlenecks remain wide open.
So the archive reads anti-capture performance as a **stack**, not as one symbolic reform.

### 3. These are not substitutes for distributional reform

Political firewalls do not replace housing reform, labor power, inheritance policy, tax reform, or social inheritance.
They protect those gains from later neutralization.
The archive therefore treats them as **co-equal infrastructure**, not as an alternative ideology.[S15][S23][S26]

### 4. Wealth-to-rule can force a harder mode

If the firewall stack is badly open, the society may need **anti-oligarchy response** even when some wealth shares look less extreme than in another case.
See [`prevention-and-response-doctrine.md`](prevention-and-response-doctrine.md) and [`mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md`](mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md).

## Practical shortcut

Ask eight things:

1. can large money in politics be seen in time,
2. can lobbying pressure be traced while rules are still being written,
3. can conflicts and revolving-door routes be screened and enforced,
4. can the real controllers behind relevant entities be identified,
5. can procurement and licensing be audited for favoritism,
6. can pooled capital be governed without a narrow stewardship bottleneck,
7. can concentrated wealth dominate the information order without disclosure,
8. and can crises still deliver softer terms to large incumbents than to everyone else?

If several of those answers are weak at once, the archive should assume wealth-to-rule risk is active and treat anti-capture reform as core program work rather than optional cleanup.
