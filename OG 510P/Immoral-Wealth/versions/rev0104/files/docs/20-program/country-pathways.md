## One target, different starts

The archive keeps one **working attractor** and one common constitutional test set.
But it does **not** assume that every society should lead with the same instrument or the same sequence.
Pathway choice should respond to four things:

1. **where mass failure sits now** — assetlessness, debt fragility, housing exclusion, weak services, or person-level dependence,[S07][S13][S16][S17][S21][S22][S27][S28][S29][S30]
2. **where reconcentration pressure sits now** — land, housing scarcity, monopoly, procurement, opaque ownership, or resource rents,[S10][S15][S20][S23][S34][S35][S36]
3. **which implementation rails already exist** — household delivery, ownership visibility, distributional scoring, top-tail enforcement, integrity, and crisis recovery,[S04][S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]
4. **which political mode the country is in** — prevention, correction, or anti-oligarchy response.[S15][S23][S31]

So the archive's rule is simple:
**keep the target common, vary the first package and the speed of escalation.**

## What should stay common across all pathways

Whatever the starting state, a good pathway should still move toward the same six constitutional conditions:

1. **bottom-half assetlessness stops being normal**,
2. **the middle becomes socially thick rather than thin and debt-dependent**,
3. **the top tail remains rich but non-ruling**,
4. **public/social wealth materially lowers forced private buffer needs**,
5. **circulation stays open across generations, groups, places, and persons**,
6. **gains survive an ordinary political-budget cycle and an ordinary asset / credit cycle**.[S02][S03][S13][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S31]

A country pathway is therefore **not** a national excuse for picking a different moral end state.
It is a decision about where to begin, what to gate, and what to compress first.

## Four recurring starting states

### 1. Rich asset-price democracies

#### Typical failure pattern

These countries often have high average private wealth and respectable household balance sheets on paper, while the bottom half remains thinly capitalised and younger or lower-wealth households face increasingly exclusionary housing entry.
The danger is a flattering owner society in which broad wealth is debt-fragile, late-arriving, and continuously reabsorbed through housing scarcity, high prices, fees, and financial intermediation.[S07][S13][S16][S17][S19][S36]

#### Lead with

- housing supply plus anti-speculation / anti-rent measures,
- lower forced private buffer needs through stronger health, pension, care, and income-security design,
- safer liquid-buffer and starter-asset tools for the lower half,
- earlier footholds through social inheritance or baby-bond-type channels,
- inheritance, capital-gains, and top-tail tax reform,
- pension and pooled-fund stewardship reform where mass ownership is otherwise mostly silent,
- and political-finance, lobbying, and beneficial-ownership rules strong enough to stop the upper tail from vetoing correction.[S04][S05][S07][S12][S21][S22][S23][S26][S31][S36][S37]

#### Do not lead with

- a homeownership story that depends on dangerous leverage,
- household asset-building that capitalises immediately into land prices,
- or rhetorically maximal top-tail tools before visibility and enforcement rails are real.[S04][S13][S23][S34][S35][S36]

### 2. Social democracies with aging homeowner majorities

#### Typical failure pattern

These countries often start from stronger bargaining systems and stronger public provision, but risk drifting into a protected older owner bloc and a weaker entrant cohort.
The main danger is not only top-tail excess.
It is a slow **intergenerational closure** in which younger adults arrive too late, housing scarcity quietly hardens insider advantage, and household broadening flatters person-level dependence.[S05][S13][S14][S19][S22][S27][S30]

#### Lead with

- preserve bargaining coverage and the social floor,
- widen youth liquid access and non-speculative housing entry,
- clean up inherited housing and transfer advantage,
- maintain clearly positive public/social wealth,
- keep registries and transfer visibility strong,
- and check that direct rights reach younger adults, women, migrants, minorities, and other excluded groups rather than only households as aggregates.[S05][S13][S14][S19][S23][S27][S28][S29][S30]

#### Do not lead with

- complacency about a broad-owner snapshot dominated by older cohorts,
- or gradual privatization of health, care, pension, and housing security that silently raises the private wealth households need merely to stay safe.[S13][S21][S22]

### 3. Middle-income oligarchic or capture-prone states

#### Typical failure pattern

These countries often face extreme concentration through land, procurement, political connection, and weak enforcement before they face the full menu of rich-country portfolio-complexity problems.
The immediate danger is that wealth already behaves like rule: policy can be announced, but capture, opacity, and administrative weakness stop it from becoming a durable distributional shift.[S15][S23][S31]

#### Lead with

- beneficial-ownership and transfer transparency,
- land, cadastre, and procurement integrity reform,
- linked registries before highly complex top-tail promises,
- universal or near-universal floor building,
- simple, enforceable taxes before elegant high-complexity tools,
- direct public, cooperative, or member-governed vehicles where private intermediation would simply recreate elite control,
- and savings, payment, and title architecture that gives ordinary people usable claims early.[S04][S15][S23][S31][S38][S44]

#### Do not lead with

- imported rich-country privatized-risk models that increase forced self-insurance,
- highly valuation-dependent headline instruments before visibility exists,
- or household-targeted ownership stories that leave person-level title, control, and exit rights unclear.[S04][S17][S21][S22][S23][S27][S28][S29]

### 4. Resource or rent-fund states

#### Typical failure pattern

These countries can show large public or quasi-public revenue streams while ordinary households remain thinly protected and elite groups privatize the commanding rents.
The key constitutional danger is that common wealth exists in principle but is captured before it becomes common security or common bargaining power.[S20][S31][S34][S44]

#### Lead with

- convert resource and land rents into transparent public/social wealth,
- harden fund governance, contractor disclosure, and procurement rules,
- route part of returns into universal services, social inheritance, or citizen-dividend structures,
- build ordinary-household liquid and housing floors rather than relying on sovereign balances alone,
- and track whether regional, Indigenous, ethnic, caste, or migrant-status exclusions are being reproduced inside the rent-distribution system.[S12][S15][S31][S34][S44]

#### Do not lead with

- sovereign-balance-sheet symbolism without citizen-side rights,
- or extraction regimes that promise future national benefit while current contract, land, and procurement channels remain oligarchic.[S15][S31][S34][S44]

## Cross-path selection rules

A country pathway is better when it follows five compact rules.

### 1. Fix the main washout channel early

Do not let the first package ignore the mechanism most likely to reabsorb gains.
In one country that will be housing scarcity.
In another it will be procurement capture, resource privatization, or weak beneficial-ownership visibility.[S15][S23][S34][S35][S36]

### 2. Protect households while the harder rails are being built

Floor repair, claimability, and entry widening should not wait for a perfect state.
But high-gate top-tail tools should not be treated as the whole credible program before the visibility and enforcement rails exist.[S04][S23][S31][S39][S40][S41]

### 3. Keep person-level and excluded-group broadening in view

A pathway fails if it improves household or national averages while women, younger adults, migrants, minorities, Indigenous people, disabled people, or other excluded groups remain screened out of the main wealth-building channels.[S18][S19][S27][S28][S29][S30][S44]

### 4. Let the mode change speed, not the moral target

Prevention, correction, and anti-oligarchy response differ in pace and legal posture.
They should not become excuses for changing what counts as a good wealth order.[S15][S23][S31]

### 5. Keep the pathway legible enough to score

If a pathway cannot say which rails are present, which are missing, which tools are gated, and where the next durable units of wealth should land, it is not yet a pathway.
It is a slogan.[S23][S31]

## Relation to the implementation stack and the modes

Read pathways as **weighting maps**, not as standalone doctrines.

- [`minimum-viable-implementation-stack.md`](minimum-viable-implementation-stack.md) tells you **which tools can begin now, which must build rails while rolling out, and which should remain gated**.
- [`transition-sequencing.md`](transition-sequencing.md) tells you **what tends to come earlier and later**.
- [`mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md`](mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md) tells you **whether the country is still in prevention, has moved into correction, or is already facing anti-oligarchy response conditions**.

Country pathways answer a narrower question:
**given that starting state, where should the package bite first?**

## Practical shortcut

If the archive needs a one-sentence rule, use this:

**hold the target constant, identify the country's main washout and capture channels, begin the floor and entry work that existing rails can already deliver, build visibility and integrity faster than concentration can adapt, and escalate top-tail compression only on bases the state can actually see, score, and defend.**
