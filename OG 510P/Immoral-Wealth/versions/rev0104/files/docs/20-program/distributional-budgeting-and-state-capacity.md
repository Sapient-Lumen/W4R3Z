# Distributional budgeting and state capacity

## What this note is for

This note turns one of the archive's quiet background requirements into a **stable fiscal operating stack**.
A society can announce good goals, pass good statutes, and still rebuild concentration through the budget if scoring arrives too late, hidden spending stays off-ledger, implementation capacity is assumed rather than funded, or ex post failure is treated as a side issue.
So the archive treats **distributional budgeting and state capacity** as part of wealth-distribution infrastructure rather than as a technical appendix for finance ministries.[S13][S19][S31][S34][S36][S37]

## Minimum operating rule

Before a major wealth-relevant package is locked, governments should require a **wealth-aware distributional score** that is early enough to change design, broad enough to catch hidden reversals, and concrete enough to shape staffing, delivery, enforcement, and later repair.[S31]

A serious budgeting discipline should usually do **seven things at once**:

1. score **packages rather than isolated instruments**, because tax, housing, labor, public-wealth, and anti-capture effects often interact,
2. score **wealth formation, floor integrity, closure, public-value capture, and durability**, not only annual disposable-income effects,[S13][S19][S31][S34][S35][S36][S37]
3. run **early enough to reshape design** rather than merely decorate a near-final package,[S31]
4. use linked administrative and program data that are **good enough to see who actually gets through**, not just who is formally eligible,[S27][S28][S29][S30][S31]
5. budget for **delivery, enforcement, appeals, auditing, and maintenance capacity** instead of assuming the machinery will somehow appear,
6. treat **tax expenditures, guarantees, concessional finance, and publicly created uplift** as distributionally decisive budget choices rather than neutral background settings,[S31][S34][S35][S36][S37]
7. and create an **ex post correction loop** so packages are revised when take-up failure, capitalization, exclusion, or avoidance defeats the forecast.[S21][S22][S31][S36][S37]

A package should count less as a success when one table looks progressive but the wider budgeting record shows that footholds remain weak, public value is leaking upward, or the delivery state was never funded to make the promise real.

## The main false passes

### 1. A package looks progressive on current income, but weakens wealth formation

A country should count less budgeting success when the headline score shows better annual cash flow while the same package leaves asset entry, savings room, debt stress, secure tenure, or early footholds largely unchanged.
The archive does not treat disposable-income tables as enough when the question is durable wealth broadening.[S13][S19][S31]

### 2. Eligibility is broad on paper, but the claiming machine is thin in practice

A society should count less success when the budget announces broad support but underfunds staffing, outreach, appeals, adjudication, portability, renewal, or error correction.
That pattern can turn apparently universal design into selective non-take-up or slow attrition.[S27][S28][S29][S30][S31]

### 3. Public investment is scored on the spending side, but not on the uplift side

The archive should count less seriousness when infrastructure, rezoning, corridor investment, procurement, or place-making decisions are budgeted as public outlays while the land or asset-value gains they create are barely measured and weakly recaptured.[S34][S35]

### 4. Hidden spending stays off the main distributional page

A country should count less progress when deductions, exclusions, concessional rates, guarantees, subsidized credit, or retirement-saving relief are treated as technical background rather than as real distributive choices with owner-skewed or price-capitalizing effects.[S31][S36][S37]

### 5. Capacity is invoked rhetorically, but not budgeted concretely

The archive should count less seriousness when governments say a reform depends on enforcement, valuation, registry upkeep, casework, or litigation capacity but do not fund the teams, systems, and maintenance windows required to carry that work.
Unfunded capacity claims are often just a slower way of saying that delivery will be partial.[S23][S31]

### 6. Forecasts are published once, then never confronted with reality

A society should count less budgeting success when ex ante tables are treated as self-validating and no routine ex post review asks whether a package capitalized into prices, missed excluded groups, shifted burdens within households, or was arbitraged by sophisticated actors.[S21][S22][S31][S36][S37]

## The standing budgeting and state-capacity stack

### 1. Early ex ante scoring

Core moves:

- run distributional analysis before political lock-in,
- test multiple design variants rather than a single preferred package,
- and include likely incidence, capitalization, and delivery-failure channels rather than assuming clean statutory transmission.[S31][S36][S37]

The archive wants budgeting to shape design early, not merely describe it late.

### 2. Wealth-aware linked data and administrative visibility

Core moves:

- connect the administrative records needed to see wealth-relevant outcomes,
- follow households and persons through delivery, not only formal eligibility,
- and make disaggregation good enough to detect group, cohort, household, and place-based closure where law and data permit.[S13][S27][S28][S29][S30][S31]

The point is not maximal data collection for its own sake.
It is to stop governments from claiming distributional success with instruments they cannot actually observe in operation.

### 3. Delivery, enforcement, and maintenance budgeting

Core moves:

- budget explicitly for staff, outreach, case handling, appeals, audits, registry upkeep, valuation support, and compliance work,
- identify which agencies or public bodies actually carry each reform lane,
- and treat maintenance and renewal as part of the policy rather than as optional overhead.[S23][S31]

The archive prefers a smaller deliverable package over a larger package whose operating machinery is never funded.

### 4. Public-value capture and hidden-spending scoring

Core moves:

- score tax expenditures like spending,
- score guarantees and concessional finance like real state support,
- and score land or asset uplift generated by public action for who receives it and how much is recaptured for common use.[S31][S34][S35][S36][S37]

This is where budgeting meets the archive's concern with the **hidden wealth state**.

### 5. Public distributional statements with uncertainty and failure channels

Core moves:

- publish compact statements of who is expected to gain and lose,
- identify the main uncertainty ranges,
- and name the main failure channels such as non-take-up, within-household masking, price pass-through, rent capitalization, or avoidance.[S27][S28][S29][S30][S31][S36][S37]

The archive wants public honesty about where a package might fail, not polished tables that imply false precision.

### 6. Ex post review, repair, and automatic revision triggers

Core moves:

- revisit major packages after implementation,
- compare observed outcomes with the original score,
- and attach automatic review or redesign triggers when the package misses entrants, fuels revaluation, or mainly enriches incumbents.[S21][S22][S31][S36][S37]

A policy that modeled well but washed out in practice should count as a design problem to be repaired, not as a success to be narrated.

### 7. Package-level sequencing and anti-backloading discipline

Core moves:

- budget earlier delivery for floor repair, secure tenure, and first footholds,
- refuse packages that push present exposure onto households while backloading most of the gain,
- and ask whether the budget ordering strengthens or weakens later reform capacity.[S17][S18][S19][S31]

This is where distributional budgeting meets the archive's wider rule against **promissory offsets and backloaded justice**.

## How to read this stack

### 1. Budgeting is not a sidecar to the wealth program

The archive does not treat budgeting as a technical ministry function that follows the real politics.
The budget is one of the main places where distributional claims are either made concrete or quietly reversed.

### 2. Better modelling does not substitute for better delivery

A state can be sophisticated at microsimulation and still weak at claiming, person-level delivery, ownership visibility, or top-tail enforcement.
This note therefore belongs together with [`minimum-viable-implementation-stack.md`](minimum-viable-implementation-stack.md), not above it.

### 3. A budget can look progressive while rebuilding concentration

That can happen through housing revaluation, upside-down tax relief, thin claiming systems, silent within-household masking, or underfunded enforcement.
The archive therefore reads budget quality through the same wider constitutional screens it uses elsewhere.[S13][S27][S28][S29][S30][S31][S34][S36][S37]

## Practical shortcut

Ask seven things before a major package is locked:

1. does the score reach **wealth and floor outcomes**, not only annual income,
2. was it done **early enough to change design**, not just defend it,
3. are the main **delivery and enforcement bodies funded**,
4. are hidden spending and public uplift treated as part of the budget story,
5. are the likely **failure channels named publicly**,
6. is there a real **ex post repair loop**,
7. and does the package bring delivered gains forward rather than asking households to wait behind speculation?

If several of those answers are weak at once, the archive should assume that the budget process is still capable of quietly undoing the program even when the statute book looks more ambitious.
