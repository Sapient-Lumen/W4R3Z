# Minimum viable implementation stack

## What this note is for

This note turns the archive's implementation lane into a **stable gate-and-rail operating stack**.
It answers a narrower question than ideal policy design:
**what has to be materially real before a wealth-relevant package should count as governable rather than merely announced?**

The archive rejects both perfectionism and voluntarism.
It does **not** want floor-building, tenant protection, labor repair, or simple entry-opening tools postponed until the state has a near-perfect administrative machine.
But it also does **not** want ambitious top-tail, anti-capture, or crisis-recovery claims treated as operationally serious when the delivery, visibility, integrity, and repair rails beneath them are still missing.[S04][S12][S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## Minimum operating rule

A credible wealth-broadening package should usually answer **four implementation questions**:

1. **Can ordinary households actually receive, keep, understand, and carry the measure through ordinary transitions?**
2. **Can the state see enough of the relevant owners, transfers, prices, and controllers to govern the measure honestly?**
3. **Can the package be scored, defended, and corrected when hidden exclusions, capitalization, or washout appear?**
4. **Can the package survive concentrated resistance, crisis derogation, and post-passage dilution?**

If the answer is badly weak on one of those questions, the archive should discount the measure's practical credibility.
If the answer is weak on several at once, the archive should stop treating the measure as a live governing instrument and classify it as aspirational, gated, or dependent on parallel rail-building.[S12][S15][S23][S24][S25][S31][S32][S33][S39][S40][S41]

## The main false passes

### 1. A delivery channel exists on paper, so the package is called implemented

A payment, account, protection, or entitlement does not count at full strength merely because it can technically be created.
The archive should discount it when take-up is weak, renewal is hard, continuity is fragile, gatekeepers can stall it, or ordinary users need specialist navigation just to preserve it.[S12][S24][S25][S39][S40][S41]

### 2. A top-tail rule is announced before the state can see or value the base

The archive should discount high-end tax, anti-avoidance, or anti-concentration tools when major holdings, transfers, trusts, or controllers remain too opaque to trace and value reliably.
In that setting, the visible rich often bear more of the burden than the truly difficult top tail.[S04][S23]

### 3. A package looks progressive in budget tables but not in lived incidence

A package counts less when the state cannot test whether gains are being washed out through rents, fees, debt pressure, exclusion, timing failure, or hidden subsidy capitalization.
A good statutory story with a bad incidence story is not a practical pass.[S13][S31][S36][S37]

### 4. A reform passes once and then becomes easy to hollow out

The archive should discount measures that can be diluted through procurement, political-finance dependence, weak integrity rules, soft enforcement, or ordinary administrative drift.
A paper win that concentrated wealth can quietly rewrite is not yet durable implementation.[S15][S31]

### 5. Crisis support is treated as implementation success even when recovery tools are missing

Emergency support counts less as a governing success when sunset, burden sharing, clawback, dilution, levy, or later repair mechanisms are too weak to make public-loss protection credible.
Without that rail, each crisis teaches concentrated actors that downside remains publicly socialized on favorable terms.[S31][S32][S33]

## The six standing rails

### 1. Household delivery and continuity rail

Core requirement:
ordinary households need a workable route to receive, understand, retain, and transport claims across routine life changes.

What matters here is not only identity or payment plumbing.
It is also claiming friction, renewal burdens, portability, protection against routine seizure or forced depletion, and resistance to ordinary gatekeepers who can stall or nullify a nominal entitlement.[S12][S24][S25][S39][S40][S41]

This is why the archive treats household-side usability as part of implementation, not a soft afterthought.
A claim that arrives only for experts, only for the administratively persistent, or only for people with unusually stable lives is not a broadly governable claim.

### 2. Ownership, transfer, and control visibility rail

Core requirement:
the state must be able to connect major assets and major transfers to real controllers, beneficiaries, and decision rights.

This does not require omniscience.
It does require that land, firms, funds, trusts, large gifts, large inheritances, and major control rights leave trails that can actually be checked.[S04][S23]

Without this rail, top-tail measures become easiest to apply where wealth is already legible and easiest to evade where concentrated wealth is already most opaque.

### 3. Distributional scoring and readback rail

Core requirement:
major packages should face ex ante and ex post reading that reaches beyond annual cash-income tables.

The archive wants scoring that can see wealth formation, floor security, early-foothold timing, exclusion, capitalization into rents, hidden subsidy washout, and whether formal gains actually survive delivery friction.[S13][S31][S36][S37]

This rail links implementation to the archive's scoreboard discipline.
Without it, governments can keep calling packages egalitarian while quietly rebuilding concentration through prices, fees, and timing failure.

### 4. Top-tail assessment and enforcement rail

Core requirement:
the state needs enough audit, valuation, dispute, and tracing capacity to challenge sophisticated concentration rather than only the visible upper middle.

This rail matters most for recurrent net wealth taxation, hard inheritance enforcement, aggressive anti-avoidance work, and any package that depends on tracing opaque legal wrappers or hard-to-price assets.[S04][S23]

The archive therefore treats top-tail enforcement as a distinct rail, not something that can be assumed from ordinary revenue administration.

### 5. Integrity and anti-capture rail

Core requirement:
passage is not enough.
The state needs working integrity rules strong enough to keep concentrated wealth from rewriting implementation after enactment.

That includes lobbying visibility, political-finance rules, procurement integrity, revolving-door controls, conflict-of-interest discipline, and institutional resistance to quiet carve-outs, selective softening, or administrative favoritism.[S15][S31]

A measure that exists only until the first licensing round, funding cycle, procurement negotiation, or tax-ruling campaign is not yet a durable governing instrument.

### 6. Emergency recovery and repair rail

Core requirement:
when crises force public support, the state needs real tools for sunset, restructuring, burden sharing, dilution, clawback, levy, payout restriction, and later recovery.

Otherwise the emergency state quietly becomes a standing subsidy to concentrated downside insulation.
This rail matters not only in formal finance crises but anywhere concentrated actors can socialize losses and re-privateize control once calm returns.[S31][S32][S33]

## The three gate families

### A. Begin-now measures

These usually should **not** wait for ideal top-tail visibility or elite valuation capacity:

- labor-law and bargaining-power repair,
- tenant protections and anti-eviction rules,
- social housing and non-speculative housing supply,
- universal or near-universal services that reduce forced precautionary saving,
- simple protected-minimum rules for modest savings or starter assets,
- obvious upside-down subsidy cleanup,
- and simple social-inheritance or starter-asset tools that can ride existing household delivery rails.[S07][S09][S12][S21][S22][S24][S25][S31][S36][S37][S39]

The archive's reason is practical: these measures already have enough administrable surface to begin, and delaying them until every elite-governance rail is perfected usually protects the status quo more than it protects implementation quality.

### B. Build-with-stack measures

These can often begin before the stack is ideal, but only if rollout **visibly builds the missing rails at the same time**:

- inheritance and gift reporting upgrades,
- land-value capture and public-uplift recapture,
- targeted debt relief and refinancing repair,
- pass-through-voting and stewardship reform in pooled savings,
- public-wealth or social-wealth vehicles that require governance credibility,
- and more finely targeted ownership-broadening tools.[S05][S23][S26][S31][S34][S35]

The archive should distrust packages that depend on future administrative strengthening but do not actually build it.

### C. Gated measures

These should not be treated as the archive's **headline credible core instruments** where the minimum stack is still badly missing:

- recurrent net wealth taxes on complex top-tail portfolios,
- aggressive anti-avoidance packages that depend on tracing opaque entities and valuing hard-to-price assets,
- highly targeted transfer enforcement without transfer visibility,
- and crisis-support promises that claim taxpayer protection without real recovery tools.[S04][S23][S32][S33]

The point is not that these tools are unimportant.
It is that the archive should not call them operationally real before the supporting rails are materially present.

## How to read the stack by mode

### Prevention

In prevention mode, move early on begin-now measures while building visibility, scoring, and integrity before concentration hardens further.

### Correction

In correction mode, rail-building has to run in parallel with the main package.
Otherwise the society announces corrective tools faster than it can deliver or defend them.

### Anti-oligarchy response

In anti-oligarchy response, weak rails are often part of the regime problem itself.
Opacity, capture, and one-sided emergency support are no longer back-office flaws; they are part of how wealth reproduces rule.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## Practical shortcut

Ask seven questions:

1. **Which measures can begin now on existing rails without pretending the state is stronger than it is?**
2. **Which measures only count if rollout itself builds the missing rails?**
3. **Which measures should stay gated for now because the state still cannot see, score, defend, or recover them credibly?**
4. **Where are the household-side failure points — take-up, portability, gatekeeping, seizure, operability, or continuity — that would make delivery look better on paper than in life?**
5. **Which missing rail is mainly technical, and which missing rail is really an anti-capture problem?**
6. **If crisis support became necessary tomorrow, could the state actually recover public value later?**
7. **After all of that, is the package still being described more ambitiously than the rails beneath it justify?**

A country pathway that cannot answer those questions does not yet have a credible implementation story.
