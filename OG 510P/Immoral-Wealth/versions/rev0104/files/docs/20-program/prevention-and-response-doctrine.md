# Prevention and response doctrine

## What this note is for

This note turns the archive's mode lane into a **stable governing-posture doctrine**.
The archive needs more than one kind of answer because not every unequal society is situated the same way.
Some societies still have time to broaden ownership before domination hardens.
Some already need corrective force because serious breach is underway.
And some have crossed far enough into opacity, veto, and wealth-to-rule that deconcentration and anti-capture can no longer wait for a gentler later phase.[S02][S03][S13][S15][S18][S19][S23]

So this note is **not** mainly about how to score a case.
That job belongs to [`mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md`](mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md) and [`measurement-and-scoreboard.md`](measurement-and-scoreboard.md).
This note is about a different question:

**once the archive knows the society's posture, what is that posture for, what must become first-order, and what should not be postponed under that mode?**

## Minimum operating rule

Modes are **constitutional governing postures**, not moods, party brands, or simple tax temperatures.
The archive should choose the **softest mode that can still realistically stop hardening and reverse the relevant breach**, but it should not pretend a softer mode is enough when concentration has already become self-protecting.

That means rejecting two errors at once:

1. **under-escalation** — waiting for fully consolidated oligarchy before changing governing posture, and
2. **mode compression** — acting as if prevention, correction, and anti-oligarchy differ only in rhetorical tone rather than in what becomes non-deferrable.[S13][S15][S18][S19][S23]

The archive therefore treats the three modes this way:

- **prevention** asks how to broaden fast enough that hard closure does not become the next constitutional fact,
- **correction** asks how to reverse serious breach before it becomes politically self-defending,
- **anti-oligarchy response** asks how to break wealth-rule channels while repairing the floor and reopening entry at the same time.

All three modes still need floor repair, early footholds, anti-rent discipline, legibility, and durability work.
What changes is **which lanes become first-order, what pace is justified, and what can no longer be postponed**.

## The main false passes

### 1. The archive reads mode from headline shares alone

A society should count less clarity when it treats one stock distribution snapshot as enough to place the mode.
Timing failure, person-level masking, wealth-to-rule conversion, opacity, and repair debt can justify a harder posture even when simple shares look somewhat softer.[S15][S18][S19][S23]

### 2. A reform-friendly electoral moment is mistaken for prevention mode

A society should count less optimism when it assumes that one sympathetic government, one manifesto, or one reform window means the order is still in prevention.
The relevant question is not whether reform language exists.
It is whether the order is still meaningfully reformable without first confronting hard capture, top-tail opacity, or crisis-laundered veto power.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

### 3. Anti-capture work is treated as something that can always wait until later

The archive should count less realism when it assumes broadening can proceed first and integrity, lobbying, procurement, beneficial-ownership, or emergency-repair architecture can be added once the main economic work is done.
Sometimes those rails are exactly what determines whether the economic work survives.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

### 4. Correction is treated as a midpoint average rather than a distinct posture

Correction is not simply “prevention, but more.”
It is the mode for societies where serious breach is already present but ordinary democratic corrective government is still materially possible.
A society should count less precision when it collapses correction into either a soft prevention mood or a full anti-oligarchy emergency.[S18][S19][S23]

### 5. Anti-oligarchy is reduced to “tax the top harder”

The archive should count less seriousness when anti-oligarchy response is described as nothing more than more aggressive taxation.
Anti-oligarchy response is a broader posture: anti-capture, visible ownership, key-asset governance, crisis loss bearing, public counterweight, and deconcentration fast enough that wealth cannot keep rewriting the rules while households wait.[S04][S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## The standing mode doctrine

### 1. Prevention

**Core question**
- Can broadening still outrun hardening?

**Governing aim**
- Build a bounded-top, thick-middle, real-floor order before closure, dynastic sorting, and wealth-rule become too strong to reverse through ordinary reform.

**What becomes first-order**
- labor bargaining power and wage-floor repair,
- housing-entry reopening and anti-rent discipline,
- universal or near-universal services that reduce forced private buffering,
- early social-inheritance and starter-claim design,
- and early anti-capture / legibility rail building so later correction does not begin from institutional weakness.[S07][S13][S15][S18][S19][S31]

**What should not be postponed**
- basic lobbying, political-finance, procurement, and beneficial-ownership visibility,
- basic protected-minima and anti-punishment floor rules,
- and sequencing choices that stop broad gains from being capitalized into land, fees, or incumbent markups.[S15][S23][S31][S36]

### 2. Correction

**Core question**
- How can serious breach be reversed before it hardens into wealth-protected rule?

**Governing aim**
- Move a society back inside the archive's constitutional envelope where the floor is real, first footholds arrive earlier, dynastic closure weakens, and concentrated wealth can no longer rely on ordinary drift to reproduce itself.

**What becomes first-order**
- faster bottom-half asset repair,
- stronger anti-dynasty and capital-income reform,
- stronger housing / land / anti-monopoly discipline,
- person-level title and portability where household measures flatter reality,
- and parallel build-out of delivery, legibility, and enforcement rails so corrective tools are actually governable.[S04][S05][S07][S18][S19][S23][S31]

**What should not be postponed**
- implementation capacity,
- anti-reconcentration discipline,
- and visible middle-broadening design that keeps correction from being framed as pure punishment without entry repair.[S20][S23][S31]

### 3. Anti-oligarchy response

**Core question**
- How can a society break wealth-rule channels while also protecting households from the transition cost?

**Governing aim**
- Confront a wealth order that is already reproducing governing leverage through opacity, lobbying, procurement, media or key-asset concentration, crisis privilege, or elite regrouping speed.

**What becomes first-order**
- hard anti-capture and conflict rules,
- rapid ownership and transfer visibility,
- deconcentration of key governing chokepoints,
- stronger burden sharing and repair duties in crises,
- public/social counterweight strong enough to matter politically,
- and top-tail compression fast enough that elites cannot use time, opacity, or emergency privilege to outrun reform.[S15][S16][S17][S23][S31][S32][S33]

**What should not be postponed**
- floor protection,
- housing and price-capitalization discipline,
- person-level security,
- and ordinary-life continuity protections.
Anti-oligarchy response is not allowed to become a pure elite contest while households absorb instability below.[S07][S24][S25][S31][S32][S33]

## How modes should mix without collapsing

The archive does **not** treat the modes as sealed boxes.

- Prevention still includes some anti-capture work because early rail building is often a no-regret move.
- Correction still includes broad middle-building because a purely top-end story will not stabilize the order.
- Anti-oligarchy response still includes floor and entry work because anti-capture without household broadening leaves the social basis of reform too thin.

So mixed packages are normal.
What matters is that the archive still names the **dominant posture** correctly.
If wealth-rule is already strong, the society should not keep describing itself as prevention simply because it also funds housing or baby bonds.
If the society is still broadly reformable, the archive should not act as though every move requires the most conflict-maximal posture available.

## Escalation and de-escalation discipline

Escalate when:

- a major constitutional breach appears or hardens,
- wealth-to-rule conversion becomes materially stronger,
- opacity or implementation sabotage blocks ordinary correction,
- one-cycle gains wash out through rents, fees, reconcentration, or crisis laundering,
- or emergency derogations accumulate without real repair.[S15][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33]

De-escalate only when:

- the softer posture is supported by real evidence rather than missingness or public-relations optimism,
- improvements survive a meaningful durability window,
- wealth-to-rule channels are materially weaker rather than merely quieter,
- and unresolved emergency privilege or repair debt has been substantially reduced rather than normalized.[S06][S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## How to use this note

Use the archive in this order:

1. place the case with [`measurement-and-scoreboard.md`](measurement-and-scoreboard.md) and [`mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md`](mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md),
2. use this note to decide **what the chosen posture is for** and what becomes non-deferrable under it,
3. then use [`transition-sequencing.md`](transition-sequencing.md), [`country-pathways.md`](country-pathways.md), and [`minimum-viable-implementation-stack.md`](minimum-viable-implementation-stack.md) to decide how that posture can actually be carried.

That is the point of the doctrine.
It keeps the archive from treating every society as the same case, but it also keeps the archive from pretending that posture choice is a matter of vibes once measurement, capture, timing, and durability are on the table.
