# Transition sequencing

## What this note is for

This note turns the archive's sequencing lane into a **stable ordering and overlap stack**.
The archive does not assume that the right policy list, in any order, will produce the right constitutional result.
Sequence matters because badly ordered reform can leave decisive choke points hidden, ask exposed households to bridge-finance the transition, reopen entry only on paper, announce hard top-tail tools before the state can really administer them, or let emergency exceptions recreate concentrated advantage before durability work is in place.[S04][S07][S15][S17][S18][S19][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33][S34][S35][S36][S37][S38]

So the question here is not whether a society eventually touches the right domains.
It is **what should move early, what can overlap, what should usually wait for stronger rails, and what should never be backloaded behind promises of later justice**.

## Minimum operating rule

A serious anti-concentration program should usually do **seven things at once**:

1. build enough **legibility and anti-capture capacity early** that later measures can bite,
2. deliver **floor protection and anti-punishment** early enough that ordinary households are not financing the transition with present insecurity,
3. reopen **entry and first footholds** before relying on late circulation through bequests or older-cohort wealth,
4. strengthen **labor power and common wealth** so bottom-half accumulation does not depend only on top-end cleanup,
5. move on **visible, lower-mobility rents and domestic choke points** before betting the whole program on the hardest mobile or valuation-heavy instruments,
6. build **exception architecture, repair duties, and gated implementation rails** before the next crisis or the hardest tools arrive,
7. and **lock in durability** before a normal political-budget cycle or asset cycle can wash apparent gains back out.[S07][S12][S15][S17][S18][S19][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33][S34][S35][S36][S37][S38]

The archive therefore rejects two sequencing mistakes at once:
**serial backloading**, where present breaches are asked to wait politely for later compression, and **sequencing theater**, where governments announce the right future staircase while today’s order still routes around it.

## The main false passes

### 1. The policy list is correct, but the order leaves the existing wealth constitution intact for too long

A society should count less seriousness when it can name floor reform, entry reform, labor reform, anti-capture reform, and top-tail reform, but sequences them in a way that lets opaque ownership, political leverage, or incumbent rents dominate the period in which later steps must still pass.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

### 2. Early “help” mainly capitalises into prices, fees, or other gatekeeper tolls

A society should count less progress when it leads with demand-side support, tax preferences, or credit expansion before supply, tenure, anti-rent, and anti-capture rules are strong enough to stop incumbent owners from absorbing the gain.[S21][S22][S31][S35][S36][S37]

### 3. Hard top-tail tools are announced before visibility, valuation, and enforcement rails are real

A country should count less realism when it campaigns on recurrent wealth taxation, aggressive offshore enforcement, or other high-gate instruments before beneficial ownership, transfer tracing, valuation, dispute capacity, and cross-border use capacity are materially present.[S04][S15][S23][S38]

### 4. Floor repair is postponed until after growth, compression, or circulation arrive

The archive should count less justice when exposed households are told to wait for later redistribution, later inheritance reform, or later macro gains while present debt fragility, housing precarity, seizure risk, benefit cliffs, and forced self-insurance remain in place.[S07][S17][S18][S19][S21][S22][S34]

### 5. Exception governance and repair rules are left for later

A society should count less seriousness when it treats crisis architecture as a technical appendix that can be designed after the next shock.
That often means the emergency becomes the route through which unreformed hierarchy re-enters ordinary policy.[S31][S32][S33]

### 6. Durability work is treated as a final garnish rather than part of the main order

A country should count less success when it improves one cycle of outcomes but leaves campaign finance, lobbying opacity, rescue governance, subsidy inventories, distributional budgeting, and reconcentration monitoring weak enough that the next cycle can reverse the gain.[S15][S20][S31][S32][S36]

## The standing sequencing and overlap stack

### 1. Legibility, diagnosis, and early anti-capture come first

Core move:
make the order readable enough to govern and visible enough to defend.

That usually means moving early on:
- basic distribution mapping,
- ownership and control visibility,
- subsidy inventory,
- lobbying and political-finance transparency,
- procurement and conflict screening,
- rescue-architecture review,
- and identification of the current implementation rails that already exist versus those that still need building.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33][S36][S37]

The archive does **not** require perfect state capacity before anything begins.
It does require enough early visibility and anti-capture work that later reforms are not trying to move through a black box.

### 2. Floor stabilization and anti-punishment should move early enough to matter now

Core move:
reduce present exposure before asking households to carry more transition risk.

That usually points toward:
- stronger universal or broadly shared services,
- lower housing precarity,
- protection against spend-down traps, seizure, and benefit-cliff punishment,
- better claimability, portability, and person-level control,
- and less forced private buffering for predictable health, care, pension, disability, unemployment, and similar shocks.[S07][S17][S21][S22][S39][S40]

The archive treats this as constitutional sequencing, not compassion add-on.
A weak floor corrupts the rest of the program because later gains must work against preventable present erosion.

### 3. Reopen entry and move footholds forward in time

Core move:
get first durable footholds to people early enough for adulthood rather than waiting for late circulation through private inheritance.

That usually means:
- opening secure-tenure and first-home routes without simple price capitalisation,
- building social-inheritance or starter-capital channels,
- restoring access in places and groups that incumbency has closed,
- and protecting person-level title, exit rights, and non-forfeiture across ordinary transitions.[S18][S19][S21][S22][S43]

The archive is explicit that a society should **reopen entry before it congratulates itself for eventual circulation**.

### 4. Raise labor power and common wealth while entry channels are opening

Core move:
stop asking household balance-sheet reform to compensate for chronically weak earning power or weak public counterweight.

That usually means moving on:
- bargaining coverage,
- wage floors and uprating,
- anti-monopsony and mobility rules,
- public or social wealth where appropriate,
- and recapture of public uplift or scarcity rents into common assets, lower household costs, or wider access.[S12][S21][S31][S35]

This is where the archive avoids a common sequencing mistake:
trying to do wealth reform only as late redistribution after labor and public-value channels have already been left weak.

### 5. Compress the top tail on bases the state can really see and defend

Core move:
move on concentrated capital and dynastic transfer, but in an order that matches real visibility and enforcement.

That usually means tightening earlier on:
- visible capital-income alignment,
- recipient-based inheritance and gift taxation,
- anti-avoidance and wrapper cleanup,
- visible transfer-event reporting,
- and lower-mobility rents and permissions,
while gating the hardest valuation-heavy or offshore-rich tools behind stronger legibility and dispute capacity.[S04][S05][S15][S23][S31][S35][S38]

The archive does **not** say “wait forever.”
It says top-tail compression should rest on real administrative bite rather than rhetorical overreach.

### 6. Build exception architecture and repair duty before the next stress episode

Core move:
write the emergency constitution early enough that crises do not become the mechanism of regression.

That means setting up in advance:
- household-first stabilization defaults,
- burden-sharing and upside-capture rules,
- sunset and clawback machinery,
- payout and buyback restrictions where relevant,
- transparency during emergency use,
- and a standing repair ledger so public downside creates concrete repayment obligations rather than amnesia.[S31][S32][S33]

This lane is cross-cutting rather than terminal.
The archive treats it as early infrastructure because emergency regressions can erase years of ordinary reform.

### 7. Lock in durability before the next cycle undoes the gain

Core move:
make reform harder to reverse politically, fiscally, and institutionally.

That usually means hardening:
- campaign-finance and lobbying controls,
- procurement and conflict safeguards,
- stewardship and ownership screening,
- distributional budgeting and ex post review,
- current subsidy inventories,
- and reconcentration monitoring tied to escalation rather than ceremonial reporting.[S15][S20][S31][S32][S36]

The archive wants durability work treated as part of the main program, not as a late polish step after the “real” economic reforms are done.

## How to read the stack

### 1. Read sequencing through gates and overlaps, not through a rigid staircase

The archive uses an order because badly ordered reform fails predictably.
But the phases are not watertight compartments.
In practice, floor protection, entry opening, labor-power work, top-tail compression, and anti-capture hardening should often overlap once the minimum rails exist.
The question is not whether they overlap, but **which lanes are allowed to lag without corrupting the whole program**.

### 2. Earlier usually means: more no-regret, more visible, more household-protective, less valuation-heavy

When several feasible openings exist, the archive tends to move earlier on:
- visible domestic choke points,
- immobile rents,
- owner-subsidy cleanup,
- claim security,
- protected modest footholds,
- and starter-capital or entry work that does not simply bid up incumbent prices.[S23][S31][S35][S36][S37][S38]

It tends to move later, or gate more tightly, on measures whose success depends on visibility, valuation, or cross-border enforcement capacity the state still lacks.[S04][S23][S38]

### 3. Read the sequence through mode

In **prevention mode**, the archive can move with more spacing, but must still resist backloading.
In **correction mode**, the lanes overlap much more heavily because serious breaches mean a neat staircase becomes too slow.
In **anti-oligarchy response mode**, visibility, anti-capture, entry reopening, top-tail constraint, and durability hardening become immediate co-equals with floor repair because wealth-to-rule conversion is already part of the constitution.
See [`prevention-and-response-doctrine.md`](prevention-and-response-doctrine.md) and [`mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md`](mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md).

### 4. Read the sequence through the implementation stack

A society should not promise what its delivery, visibility, dispute, and emergency rails cannot yet support.
That is not timidity.
It is how the archive separates **begin-now measures**, **build-with-the-stack measures**, and **still-gated measures** without giving hidden wealth or incumbent privilege a free pass.
See [`minimum-viable-implementation-stack.md`](minimum-viable-implementation-stack.md) and [`wealth-legibility-registries-and-enforcement.md`](wealth-legibility-registries-and-enforcement.md).

## Program implication

The archive's sequencing judgment is deliberately practical.
It says:

1. **make the order visible and less capture-prone early,**
2. **deliver floor protection before households are asked to wait for later justice,**
3. **reopen entry before relying on late circulation,**
4. **raise labor power and common wealth while broadening work is underway,**
5. **compress the top tail on bases the state can actually govern,**
6. **write emergency and repair rules before the next stress episode,**
7. and **lock in durability before the next cycle turns a soft pass into reversal**.[S04][S07][S12][S15][S17][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33][S34][S35][S36][S37][S38]
