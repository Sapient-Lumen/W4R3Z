# Jurisdictional mobility and anti-blackmail rules

## What this note is for

This note turns the archive's mobility lane into a **stable anti-blackmail operating stack**.
The archive does not deny that capital, legal residence, booking structures, and ownership vehicles can move across borders or be rearranged to evade rules.
But it also does not allow **threatened mobility to become a standing constitutional veto** over floor-building, anti-rent reform, anti-dynasty taxation, political-firewall hardening, or public-value capture.
So the question here is not whether mobility matters.
It is how to govern under real mobility constraints **without confusing bargaining leverage with genuine social indispensability, and without campaigning on instruments whose bases the state still cannot see or hold**.[S04][S15][S23][S31][S38]

The archive's standing judgment remains a middle rule.
IMF's wealth-tax guidance says top-end taxation works only with anti-avoidance law, enforcement, and cross-border information sharing, rather than by statute alone.[S04]
OECD / Global Forum work says beneficial-ownership transparency and implementation gaps remain a core challenge in tracing real owners and controllers.[S23]
At the same time, OECD's 2025 Global Forum update shows cross-border tax transparency is not a fantasy condition: automatic exchange now operates across 116 jurisdictions and covers 171 million accounts with almost EUR 13 trillion in assets.[S38]
That means the archive should neither surrender to exit rhetoric nor pretend that the hardest cross-border tools are already frictionless.

## Minimum operating rule

A serious anti-concentration program should usually do **seven things at once**:

1. sort policy instruments by **actual mobility and domestic choke-point visibility**,
2. refuse to let **elite exit threats veto floor protection or anti-capture basics**,
3. move first on **immobile rents, public permissions, and visible domestic channels**,
4. build **legibility, anti-arbitrage, and dispute capacity** before overselling the hardest mobile-base measures,
5. distinguish **productive capacity threats** from threats to preserve tax privilege, regulatory privilege, or incumbent rents,
6. treat cross-border cooperation as **buildable operating capacity** rather than either magic or fantasy,
7. and judge concessions by whether they preserve **real social capacity** rather than mainly preserving concentrated wealth's bargaining power.[S04][S15][S23][S31][S34][S35][S36][S37][S38]

The archive therefore rejects two bad habits at once:
**mobility fatalism**, where every investor complaint becomes a veto, and **mobility theater**, where governments promise top-tail compression through bases that are still too hidden, too mobile, or too weakly valued to defend.

## The main false passes

### 1. The state retreats from low-mobility reform because some other instrument is mobile

A society should count less seriousness when it leaves land rents, development windfalls, lobbying opacity, procurement favoritism, or open-ended owner subsidies untouched while blaming global capital mobility in the abstract.
Many decisive choke points remain domestic even in financially open economies.[S15][S34][S35][S36][S37]

### 2. Every exit threat is treated as proof of real productive indispensability

A society should count less realism when it equates a threat to stop receiving special treatment with a threat to stop producing social value.
The archive wants governments to ask what is actually at risk: jobs, real investment, critical production, or merely a privileged return stream.[S15][S31]

### 3. High-mobility top-tail tools are announced before the state can really see the base

A country should count less progress when it promises recurrent net wealth taxation, aggressive offshore enforcement, or other valuation-heavy top-tail measures while beneficial ownership, transfer tracing, valuation, and cross-border information use remain weak.
That often produces selective pressure on the visible upper middle rather than durable compression of the real dynastic core.[S04][S23][S38]

### 4. Concessions preserve incumbent wealth while broadening work is delayed

The archive should count less success when governments trade away tenant protection, early-capital broadening, subsidy cleanup, or political-firewall hardening in order to calm highly concentrated actors.
Those concessions often worsen the distributional order even if they temporarily quiet elite complaints.[S07][S15][S31]

### 5. International coordination is treated as either impossible or already solved

A society should count less seriousness when it speaks as if nothing can be done before perfect global agreement, or as if one domestic statute has already neutralized mobility constraints.
The archive wants a buildable middle: use the cooperation that exists, widen it where possible, and sequence harder tools behind real visibility and enforcement gains.[S04][S23][S38]

## The standing mobility and anti-blackmail stack

### 1. Classify the policy surface by mobility, not by rhetoric

Core move:
separate **low-mobility bases**, **medium-mobility bases**, and **high-mobility / high-gate bases** before deciding what should move first.

- **Low-mobility / domestic choke-point bases** include land value, development uplift, franchise and license conditions, procurement access, political-finance rules, lobbying rules, beneficial-ownership disclosure for domestic assets and entities, and owner-favoring subsidy cleanup.[S15][S23][S34][S35][S36][S37]
- **Medium-mobility bases** include many visible capital gains, residence-linked reporting channels, gifts and inheritances, and domestic anti-avoidance rules around wrappers, deferrals, and transfers.[S04][S05][S23]
- **High-mobility / high-gate bases** include the most opaque offshore-rich top-tail portfolios, hard-to-value entity chains, and taxes whose bite depends on records or valuations the state still lacks.[S04][S23][S38]

The archive's first rule is simply not to let the language of global competition flatten these distinctions.

### 2. Protect the constitutional minimum from mobility vetoes

Core move:
refuse to treat floor integrity, anti-rent protection, claim security, and anti-capture basics as optional luxuries that must wait for elite comfort.

The archive is especially unwilling to delay measures such as tenant protection, universal or near-universal services, protected modest balances, simple starter-capital tools, or political-firewall hardening just because concentrated actors dislike the reform climate.
Those are not the same thing as a fragile high-mobility tax base, and they should not be held hostage to it.[S07][S12][S15][S21][S22][S31]

### 3. Move first on immobile rents, public permissions, and visible domestic channels

Core move:
start where the state already has traction.

That usually means moving earlier on:
- land-value and development-gain capture,
- housing and owner subsidy cleanup,
- public-land and franchise conditions,
- procurement transparency and ownership disclosure,
- licensing and concession rules,
- anti-rent and anti-monopoly measures,
- and domestic reporting around large transfers and visible capital claims.[S31][S34][S35][S36][S37]

The archive treats this as more than pragmatic sequencing.
It is also moral triage: if governments leave visible domestic rents intact while invoking global mobility, they are often choosing the wrong battlefield.

### 4. Build legibility, anti-arbitrage, and dispute capacity before overselling the hardest tools

Core move:
match the ambition of the instrument to the visibility and enforcement rails that actually exist.

For mobility-sensitive top-tail measures, the state should ask whether it can:
- identify beneficial owners and controllers,
- trace major gifts, inheritances, and transfers,
- value hard-to-price assets well enough to sustain disputes,
- link entity, land, tax, and procurement records,
- and make real use of cross-border information rather than merely receiving it.[S04][S23][S38]

The archive does **not** say that harder tools should be abandoned until the state is perfect.
It says they should be treated as **gated** rather than sold as already-credible core instruments.

### 5. Distinguish real productive dependence from bargaining blackmail

Core move:
interrogate what is actually being threatened.

Governments should ask:
- what jobs, capabilities, supply functions, or investments are genuinely at risk,
- over what time horizon,
- whether the threatened activity is already receiving public subsidy or privileged treatment,
- whether the threat mainly protects a rent stream or special tax position,
- and whether the same real capacity could be preserved through cleaner, more conditional instruments.[S15][S31][S32][S33]

This is where the archive refuses a common elite translation trick:
turning "do not reduce my privileged return" into "the economy will collapse if you govern me normally."

### 6. Treat cross-border cooperation as real operating capacity

Core move:
use existing international rails, while building more.

The archive should read automatic information exchange, beneficial-ownership work, anti-avoidance cooperation, and treaty-based enforcement as **operating levers already partly in existence**, not as a distant utopia.[S23][S38]
But it should also read them as uneven, incomplete, and politically contestable.
So the right posture is neither "wait for perfect coordination" nor "pretend coordination costs are trivial."
It is to combine domestic sequencing with outward coordination, and to let stronger cross-border tools ratchet in as visibility improves.[S04][S23][S38]

### 7. Concede, if at all, only through narrow, conditional, public-interest tests

Core move:
when a government does make a concession on mobility grounds, force it through a strict public-interest screen.

A concession should count less as legitimate when it:
- preserves land or monopoly rents,
- widens opacity,
- deepens subsidy skew,
- weakens floor or entrant protections,
- socializes downside while preserving upside,
- or arrives without sunset, clawback, review, or measurable public return.[S15][S31][S32][S33][S36][S37]

The archive is not absolutist about every concession.
It is strict about *what kind* of concession counts as defensible.
The burden should sit on those seeking relief to show real public necessity, not on ordinary households to keep absorbing concentration for fear of elite displeasure.

## How to read the stack

### 1. Mobility is real, but uneven

The archive rejects both naïve sovereignty and learned helplessness.
Some tax bases, ownership forms, and legal claims are easier to move or relabel than others.
But many decisive rents, permissions, and advantages remain rooted in domestic law, land, regulation, public contracting, and visible institutions.
That unevenness is what makes sequencing possible.[S04][S15][S23][S34][S35][S36][S37][S38]

### 2. Start with the least deniable choke points

The first serious question is not "what is the most ambitious instrument imaginable?" but "where can the society bite now without letting obvious domestic rents hide behind global rhetoric?"
That usually points toward immobile rents, visible transfers, anti-capture rules, owner-subsidy cleanup, and legibility-building before the hardest offshore-rich tools carry the rhetorical weight of the whole program.[S15][S23][S31][S34][S35][S36][S37]

### 3. Read mobility through the implementation stack

A country should not promise what its visibility, valuation, or dispute capacity cannot yet support.
That is not a retreat from ambition.
It is how ambition becomes ratchetable rather than theatrical.
See [`minimum-viable-implementation-stack.md`](minimum-viable-implementation-stack.md) and [`wealth-legibility-registries-and-enforcement.md`](wealth-legibility-registries-and-enforcement.md).

### 4. Read mobility through the mode doctrine

In prevention mode, low-gate and low-mobility reforms should move early before exit rhetoric hardens.
In correction mode, mobility-sensitive tools can widen, but only alongside visible legibility and anti-arbitrage work.
In anti-oligarchy response mode, elite exit threats themselves may be part of the governing problem, which makes anti-blackmail discipline and political firewalls first-order rather than supplementary.
See [`prevention-and-response-doctrine.md`](prevention-and-response-doctrine.md) and [`mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md`](mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md).

## Program implication

The archive's judgment is deliberately narrower than both surrender and bravado.
It says:

1. **mobility constraints are real, but stratified,**
2. **the constitutional minimum should not be vetoed by threatened exit,**
3. **immobile rents and domestic choke points should usually move first,**
4. **the hardest mobile-base tools should be gated behind real legibility and enforcement,**
5. **cross-border cooperation should be treated as a buildable rail rather than a fantasy condition,**
6. and **any concession should have to prove public necessity rather than merely preserve elite bargaining power**.[S04][S15][S23][S31][S34][S35][S36][S37][S38]
