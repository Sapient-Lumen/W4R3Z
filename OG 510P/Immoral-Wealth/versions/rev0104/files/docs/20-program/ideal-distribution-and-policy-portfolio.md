# Ideal distribution and policy portfolio

Use this note after reading [`../10-framework/target-selection-rules.md`](../10-framework/target-selection-rules.md) and [`../10-framework/minimum-asset-floor.md`](../10-framework/minimum-asset-floor.md).

It gives the archive's **most direct programmatic answer** to two practical questions:

**what wealth distribution should count as morally acceptable, and how can a society move toward it without magical thinking?**

## The archive's answer in one paragraph

A morally acceptable wealth order is **not flat equality**.
It is a **bounded-top, thick-middle, real-floor, open-circulation order**.
The archive's working private-wealth attractor remains roughly **20 / 50 / 20 / 10** for the bottom 50 / middle 40 / next 9 / top 1, with a narrow acceptable family around it, **top 0.1% at roughly 3% or less**, and **clearly positive public/social wealth** acting as a real counterweight.[S02][S03]
But those shares count only if they cash out as a **bottom-half supermajority clearing a real floor**, a top tail that cannot convert wealth into rule, earlier and wider access to first durable footholds, open circulation across generations and groups, and gains durable enough to survive ordinary cycles of rent extraction, asset inflation, opacity, and public downside absorption.[S13][S16][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## Part I — What kind of distribution is ideal?

The archive's answer is easiest to read as **six things that should be true at once**.

### 1. The bottom half should own enough for assetlessness to stop being normal

The archive's central share-side judgment remains that the **bottom 50% should hold about 20% of private wealth**, with something like **15-25%** counting as the narrow acceptable band only when the floor is genuinely passing.[S02][S03]
That is not a symbolic inclusion target.
It is meant to end the familiar pattern in which the bottom half has almost no net wealth, large negative pockets, weak liquid buffers, weak title security, or fragile housing positions that disappear under ordinary stress.[S13][S16][S17][S25]

### 2. The middle should be thick enough to be socially real

The **middle 40% should hold about half of private wealth**.
The archive wants a society with a broad owner-middle rather than one split between a thin upper owner class and a large renter-debtor majority.
That means ordinary households must hold wealth through channels they can actually keep and use: secure housing positions, liquid savings, pension rights, and other durable claims rather than mostly paper exposure flattering a narrow upper tier.[S02][S03][S13][S16][S22]

### 3. The top tail should remain rich but non-ruling

The archive does **not** aim at strict sameness.
It accepts meaningful inequality.
But it treats the top tail as constitutionally too large once it can systematically convert wealth into political rule, land control, media influence, crisis design power, or dynastic closure.
That is why the archive's working guide remains **top 1% around 10%**, with **8-15%** the acceptable band only under harder surrounding tests, and **top 0.1% at 3% or less** inside that slice.[S02][S03]

### 4. Public and social wealth should do real constitutional work

A near-ideal order is **not** just a better private split.
It also has **clearly positive public/social wealth** strong enough to lower the amount of private wealth households must privately hoard merely to remain safe.
When health, pensions, care, housing security, and other background protections are weak, households need larger private buffers just to stay afloat, and headline private broadening flatters reality.[S02][S17][S21][S22]

### 5. Circulation should stay open across generations, groups, and the life course

A distribution is not near-ideal if adult asset position remains too inherited, if first durable footholds arrive too late, if excluded groups remain shut out, or if household-level broadening hides person-level dependence.
The archive therefore treats **earlier footholds, wider circulation, and person-level control** as part of the ideal itself rather than optional fairness add-ons.[S05][S18][S19][S27][S28][S29][S30]

### 6. The order should be durable rather than flattering

A society does not pass because it shows one good-looking snapshot.
It has to survive ordinary political-budget cycles and ordinary asset / credit cycles without rapid reconcentration.
That means a near-ideal distribution must also show low enough washout into rents, markups, subsidies, opacity, and public downside absorption that gains are not merely temporary.[S20][S23][S31][S32][S33][S36][S37]

## What the ideal is **not**

To keep the archive from drifting into false answers, the ideal should **not** be misread as any of the following:

- **not average equality** — the archive is judging a constitutional order, not chasing identical balances,
- **not household-only equality** — person-level title, control, and exit security matter,
- **not paper inclusion** — locked-up, sweepable, fragile, or unusable claims count less,
- **not late-life circulation** — bequests arriving late do not substitute for earlier footholds,
- **not private-only success** — weak public counterweight can make even improved private shares constitutionally thin,
- **not one good year** — durability matters as much as the direction of movement.

## Part II — How can that order be achieved?

The archive's answer is a **six-lane portfolio**.
A society reaches a better wealth order by rebuilding the whole accumulation regime, not by waiting for one heroic instrument.

### Lane 1 — lower forced private buffer needs

Strengthen the social floor so households do not need abnormally large private balances just to survive predictable risks.
That means serious health, pension, care, income-stabilisation, and housing-security design, plus claimability and operability good enough that protections can actually be used.[S17][S21][S22][S39][S40][S41]

### Lane 2 — open earlier footholds and wider entry

Move first durable claims earlier in life and make them reachable without wealthy parents.
That includes social inheritance, baby-bond-style tools, safe low-balance saving channels, debt-pressure reduction, and secure-tenure / non-speculative housing-entry routes.[S07][S12][S18][S19][S39]

### Lane 3 — deconcentrate land, housing, and choke-point rents

Attack the mechanisms that quietly wash egalitarian policy back upward.
That means land-use and housing reform, public capture of publicly created land value, anti-monopoly and anti-monopsony measures, and cleanup of upside-down subsidies that mostly enrich incumbent owners or capitalise into prices.[S07][S08][S10][S34][S35][S36][S37]

### Lane 4 — compress the top tail and dynastic channels

Use inheritance, gift, capital-gains, and where administratively justified wealth-tax or wealth-equivalent tools to shrink concentrated fortunes and dynastic closure.
The archive does **not** treat this lane as optional, but it does insist that aggressive top-tail work should be paired with the visibility and enforcement rails needed to make it real.[S04][S05][S06][S18][S19][S23][S38]

### Lane 5 — build public, social, and common wealth

A morally acceptable order also needs common assets strong enough to change bargaining power and background security.
That includes public/social wealth vehicles, social housing where appropriate, public balance-sheet repair, public uplift capture, and social inheritance channels that give ordinary people a direct stake in common wealth creation.[S02][S12][S21][S22][S34][S35]

### Lane 6 — lock in visibility, anti-capture, and durability

Install the rails that stop gains from being hidden, vetoed, or reversed.
That means beneficial-ownership visibility, linked registries, transfer visibility, distributional budgeting, political-finance and lobbying firewalls, procurement integrity, rescue-cost recovery, and active monitoring of reconcentration pressure.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33][S38]

## How the same portfolio changes by mode

The archive's six lanes stay standing in every mode, but their **speed, legal posture, and emphasis** change with conditions.

### Prevention mode

Use prevention mode where the order is still reformable without overt oligarchic emergency.
The emphasis is on **widening footholds before closure hardens**:

- strengthen lanes **1, 2, 3, 5, and 6** early,
- install anti-capture and visibility before larger distributive fights intensify,
- and scale lane **4** as state capacity and enforcement rails strengthen.[S15][S18][S19][S31]

### Correction mode

Use correction mode once one or more major constitutional breaches are already present but ordinary corrective government remains plausible.
The emphasis is on **faster deconcentration plus faster floor repair**:

- accelerate lanes **2, 3, 4, and 6**,
- pair them with strong lane **1** protection so households do not bear the transition cost,
- and widen person-level title and durability where household broadening has been flattering.[S04][S05][S13][S19][S31]

### Anti-oligarchy response mode

Use anti-oligarchy response mode when wealth is already reproducing rule or vetoing reform.
The emphasis is on **front-loaded anti-capture and anti-dynasty work**:

- harden lanes **4 and 6** quickly,
- use lanes **1 and 5** to keep households from absorbing the shock of conflict or transition,
- and push lane **3** hard enough that housing, land, and concentrated choke points cannot quietly neutralise the rest of the package.[S15][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33]

For the archive's stable mode doctrine, read [`prevention-and-response-doctrine.md`](prevention-and-response-doctrine.md) and [`mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md`](mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md).

## Operating rule under real-world constraint

When time, administrative capacity, or political capital are tight, the archive's answer is **not** to choose the most rhetorically exciting instrument and neglect the rest.
The safest compact rule is:

1. **protect households from ordinary wipeout**,
2. **stop housing, land, and subsidy washout**,
3. **build visibility and anti-capture rails early**,
4. **open earlier footholds for entrants**,
5. **scale top-tail compression as enforcement credibility improves**,
6. **keep building public/social counterweight so private concentration is never the whole constitution**.

That rule is deliberately practical.
It helps the archive avoid two familiar failures at once:
- waiting too long to confront concentrated wealth,
- and announcing top-tail ambition without the rails needed to prevent evasion, washout, or elite veto.

## Practical shortcut

If the archive needs a one-sentence answer, use this:

**A near-ideal wealth order is one where the bottom half owns enough to escape mass assetlessness, the middle owns enough to be socially real, the top stays below wealth-to-rule levels, public/social wealth is materially positive, circulation stays open, and a six-lane portfolio steadily lowers forced private exposure, widens early footholds, blocks rent washout, compresses dynastic concentration, builds common wealth, and locks the result behind anti-capture rails.**
