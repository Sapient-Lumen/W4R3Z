# Measurement and scoreboard

## What this note is for

This note turns the archive's moral doctrine into a **stable measurement / reading stack**.
A society can improve one flattering average and still fail morally if the bottom half remains fragile, if the middle looks thicker only because of leveraged housing gains, if public support quietly capitalises into incumbent prices, or if concentrated wealth continues to purchase rule through channels that ordinary share tables do not show.
So the archive does **not** want a single triumphal inequality score.
It wants a disciplined way to decide **what should count as success, what should count less than it first appears, and what should still trigger escalation even when the snapshot looks softer**.[S02][S13][S15][S16][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S31][S34][S36]

## Minimum reading rule

A serious scoreboard should usually do **seven things at once**:

1. read **five axes together** rather than collapsing them into one weighted average,
2. apply **veto-first screening** so catastrophic failure on the floor, control, timing, or anti-capture surfaces is not averaged away,
3. discount gains that are **nominal, debt-fragile, household-masked, overly delayed, or hard to defend in ordinary life**,
4. treat opacity as a **burden-of-proof problem** rather than as a permission slip for easy certification,
5. read stock snapshots together with **new-gain incidence and washout pressure**, not only with headline asset shares,
6. require **durability through ordinary cycles** rather than celebrating one benign moment,
7. and let strong **wealth-to-rule markers** raise the implied operating mode even when some distributional averages look less extreme.[S13][S15][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31]

The archive therefore rejects two common measurement mistakes.
It rejects **single-number comfort**, where many different failures are blended into one neat index.
And it rejects **headline-share tunnel vision**, where a society is declared acceptable because one distribution table softened while control rights, entry conditions, timing, or ordinary holder reality remained badly skewed.

## The five standing axes

The scoreboard keeps five standing axes.
They are not five equal moral values to be traded off against one another.
They are five linked ways a wealth order can fail or improve.

### 1. Shares and public counterweight

This axis answers the simplest starting question: **who owns how much, and how much collective counterweight exists against private concentration?**

Core readings:

1. **bottom 50% wealth share** — main anti-assetlessness reading,
2. **middle 40% wealth share** — main broad-middle reading,
3. **top 1% and top 0.1% shares** — main domination-risk reading,
4. **net public wealth / public-social wealth strength** — main collective counterweight reading,[S02][S13]
5. **labor share of income** — main upstream predistribution reading.[S02]

This axis matters because a society should not need elaborate interpretation before it can admit that ordinary people own too little and the top tail owns too much.
But the archive also does **not** let this axis rule alone.
A softer share table can still hide weak footholds, household masking, late arrival, or concentrated rule.

### 2. Floor and holder reality

This axis asks whether ordinary households have a **real, resilient, person-usable floor** rather than paper inclusion or reversible exposure.
The archive reads it through the compact floor spine in [`../10-framework/minimum-asset-floor.md`](../10-framework/minimum-asset-floor.md).

Core readings:

1. **positive-net-wealth rate in the bottom half** — negative wealth should become unusual rather than normal,[S16]
2. **liquid-buffer / fragility rate** — ordinary resilience against mild shocks,[S17]
3. **housing security, adequacy, and defensibility near the floor** — tenure, affordability, adequacy, and non-punishing entry,[S07][S13][S24][S43][S44]
4. **net holdability of common footholds** — whether carrying costs quietly eat the gain,[S07][S42]
5. **participation in portable, person-usable long-run claims** — retirement wealth, cooperative equity, social inheritance, and similar claims that are actually vested and usable,[S19][S41]
6. **debt-distress incidence** — apparent gains should not be mostly cancelled by extractive liabilities,
7. **person-level adequacy and control** — household-level wealth counts less when one person lacks real claim or exit security.[S43]

This axis is where the archive most aggressively discounts false passes.
Gross balances, formal accounts, or household aggregates should count less when the holder cannot keep, defend, carry, or actually use the supposed foothold.

### 3. Control and wealth-to-rule risk

This axis asks whether concentrated wealth also remains concentrated **control**.
The archive treats that as a first-order constitutional surface rather than a side issue after the ownership table is read.

Core readings:

1. **asset composition of bottom-half and middle wealth** — diversified footholds count more than one fragile asset class,[S16]
2. **concentration of control rights in key firms and sectors** — legal value without voice can flatter democratization,
3. **landlord / land concentration in high-pressure markets** — rising owner wealth for some can coexist with deeper dependency for others,[S13][S16]
4. **beneficial-ownership visibility and registry coverage** — concentration that cannot be seen cannot be cleanly governed,[S15][S23]
5. **public/social ownership in strategic sectors** — tracks whether collective counterweight is real,
6. **stewardship concentration and pass-through coverage in intermediated ownership** — checks whether pooled capital is still governed by a narrow managerial chain,[S20][S26]
7. **wealth-to-rule conversion markers** — lobbying weakness, political-finance weakness, media concentration, procurement favoritism, rescue favoritism, and similar channels should act as escalators rather than footnotes.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

A society should therefore count less improvement when the table says wealth is broader but the power to direct firms, shape media, block reform, or win tailored public treatment remains highly concentrated.

### 4. Circulation and timing

This axis asks whether adult life chances stay too strongly inherited, too late-arriving, or too closed across cohorts and groups.
The archive does not judge circulation by estate-tax rhetoric alone.
It asks whether real footholds arrive in time for people without wealthy parents.[S05][S18][S19]

Core readings:

1. **inheritance and circumstance persistence** — including circumstance-linked inequality and large-transfer concentration,[S18][S19]
2. **age at first durable foothold** — whether secure claims arrive early enough to shape adulthood,[S05][S19]
3. **cohort wealth gaps** — younger cohorts should not be structurally locked out while older averages flatter the picture,
4. **group-stratified access to asset-building channels** — broadening counts less when excluded groups remain blocked,
5. **person-level ownership within households** — intra-household hierarchy can hide behind household totals.

This axis is how the archive resists a common laundering move:
calling a society open because some wealth circulates eventually, even though it arrives mostly through late inheritance or only after decades of locked-out adulthood.

### 5. Reconcentration pressure and path quality

This axis asks whether the society is actually moving toward the target or quietly rebuilding the old order.
A better-looking distribution can still be a temporary pause before the same hierarchy returns.[S06][S10][S20][S31][S34][S36]

Core readings:

1. **entry affordability and access to high-opportunity places** — broadening counts less when the places where life chances compound remain closed,[S07][S34][S43]
2. **labor-share, markup, and rent pressure** — tracks whether concentration is being rebuilt from below,[S02][S06]
3. **downside discipline and bailout asymmetry** — concentrated actors should not repeatedly privatise upside while the public absorbs downside,[S10][S32][S33]
4. **hidden-wealth-state / upside-down-subsidy pressure** — public support should not mainly refill incumbent wealth,[S31][S36][S37]
5. **marginal incidence of new durable wealth** — ask where the next real gains land after capitalization, debt, timing, and control are accounted for,[S35]
6. **durability through normal cycles** — gains should survive a normal political-budget cycle and a normal asset / credit cycle.

This is the axis that keeps the archive from confusing **revaluation** with **broadening**.
If most new durable gains still land with incumbents, large owners, gated places, or protected intermediaries, the path is still wrong even if one cross-sectional table looks better.

## The main false passes

The archive should be especially alert to seven false passes.
These are recurring ways a society can look improved while the moral order remains wrong.

### 1. Shares improve, but the floor remains weak

A society should count less success when the ownership table softens while bottom-half households still lack liquid buffers, defensible tenure, affordable entry, or portable person-usable claims.
The archive does not read thin statistical inclusion as a morally adequate floor.[S17][S19][S21][S22][S24][S41]

### 2. Net worth improves, but the gain is debt-fragile or carrying-cost heavy

A country should count less progress when housing or business assets raise measured wealth while leverage, maintenance, charges, insurance, taxes, or refinancing risk leave the holder highly exposed.
A foothold that self-erodes in ordinary life should be discounted.[S07][S42]

### 3. Household wealth rises, but person-level control remains skewed

A society should count less broadening when a household aggregate looks stronger but one adult lacks title, control, exit security, or practical bargaining power inside the household.
Household-level reporting should not be allowed to hide person-level dependency.[S28][S29][S30][S43]

### 4. Current-income tables look progressive, but timing and circulation remain closed

A country should count less success when current cashflow improves while first durable footholds still arrive very late, large transfers remain highly concentrated, or younger cohorts remain locked out.
The archive does not let a better annual-flow table wash out a hardening birth lottery.[S18][S19]

### 5. Public support expands, but the gain mainly capitalises into incumbent prices or fees

A society should count less improvement when credits, tax relief, subsidised finance, or other support mostly raise house prices, rents, provider fees, or incumbent balance sheets.
That is not clean broadening; it is often a disguised transfer upward.[S31][S34][S36][S37]

### 6. Headline inequality softens, but wealth still buys rule

A country should count less success when ownership is modestly broader yet large fortunes still dominate lobbying, media, procurement, licensing, or rescue terms.
Wealth-to-rule conversion is an override surface, not a decorative extra.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

### 7. One benign snapshot hides a fragile or exceptional settlement

A society should count less success when the good reading depends on crisis support, temporary asset booms, unusually low rates, one favorable budget window, or measurement that has not yet been stress-tested through ordinary cycles.
The archive wants durable ratchet effects, not one-cycle relief.[S21][S22][S32][S33]

## How to read the scoreboard in practice

Read the scoreboard in a fixed order.

### 1. Start with veto and proof screens

Before any soft summary is offered, ask whether there is already a veto-level failure on the floor, control, timing, group exclusion, or anti-capture surfaces.
Then ask whether opacity, top-tail invisibility, or contested measurement should shift the burden of proof against easy certification.
See [`../10-framework/anti-averaging-and-constitutional-vetoes.md`](../10-framework/anti-averaging-and-constitutional-vetoes.md) and [`../10-framework/burden-of-proof-under-opacity-and-uncertainty.md`](../10-framework/burden-of-proof-under-opacity-and-uncertainty.md).

### 2. Read the five axes together

Use the axes to describe the actual shape of the wealth order.
Do not skip from one favorite metric directly to a national verdict.
The archive especially resists verdicts based only on top shares, only on median wealth, or only on disposable-income changes.

### 3. Run the false-pass scan

After the basic readings, ask what may be flattering the picture:
- debt offset,
- household masking,
- delayed arrival,
- subsidy capitalization,
- opacity,
- or benign exceptional support.

This is the step that keeps the scoreboard from certifying paper broadening.

### 4. Ask where the next durable gains are landing

A society moving toward the archive's target should show that the next serious durable gains are landing with entrants, lower-wealth households, younger cohorts, excluded groups, and public/social counterweights rather than mainly with incumbent owners or high-pressure locations.
See [`../10-framework/marginal-incidence-and-direction-of-change.md`](../10-framework/marginal-incidence-and-direction-of-change.md).

### 5. Read for durability, then for mode

Only after the prior steps should the archive ask whether the reading is durable enough to justify softer posture or whether wealth-to-rule, opacity, or reconcentration pressure still imply a harder mode.
That final mode rule remains in [`mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md`](mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md).

## Mode implications

The scoreboard is not just descriptive.
It feeds the archive's choice among **prevention**, **correction**, and **anti-oligarchy response**.

- **Prevention** fits cases where the society sits near the acceptable envelope, no veto surface is badly breached, the floor is broadly passing, wealth-to-rule channels are not strong, and entrant-weighted gains look durable.
- **Correction** fits cases where one or more axes show serious but still governable failure, or where headline shares look softer than the underlying closure, timing, or floor reality.
- **Anti-oligarchy response** fits cases where multiple axes are badly breached, opacity is strategic, or concentrated wealth is already buying governing leverage strongly enough to override softer share readings.

The key point is simple.
The archive should not ask only **"how unequal is this society?"**
It should ask **"what kind of wealth order is this, how is it being flattered, and what governing posture does the full reading actually require?"**
