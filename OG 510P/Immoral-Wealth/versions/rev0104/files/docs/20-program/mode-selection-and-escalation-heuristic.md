# Mode selection and escalation heuristic

## Purpose

This note gives the archive's **operational triage map**.
It answers a narrower question than the main doctrine note in [`prevention-and-response-doctrine.md`](prevention-and-response-doctrine.md):

**given the evidence in front of us, which mode should the archive place this society in right now?**

Use it after reading:

- [`../10-framework/target-selection-rules.md`](../10-framework/target-selection-rules.md)
- [`../10-framework/minimum-asset-floor.md`](../10-framework/minimum-asset-floor.md)
- [`measurement-and-scoreboard.md`](measurement-and-scoreboard.md)
- [`prevention-and-response-doctrine.md`](prevention-and-response-doctrine.md)

## Why a triage rule is needed

The archive does not want two opposite errors:

1. **under-escalation** — waiting for fully consolidated oligarchy before changing governing posture, and
2. **over-escalation** — treating every unequal but still reformable order as if it already requires emergency anti-capture posture.[S13][S15][S18][S19][S20][S23]

A stable heuristic is therefore useful even though calibration remains provisional.
It keeps mode choice from becoming atmospheric, improvised, or too dependent on whichever indicator is most salient on a given day.[S02][S15][S18][S19][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## Read mode choice through trigger screens first

Do not apply the triage rule as a free-floating average.
Read it through these trigger screens first.

### 1. Veto-first screen

Catastrophic breach on a constitutional surface should not be smoothed away by stronger scores elsewhere.
Read mode choice through the archive's veto logic first, especially on the floor, wealth-to-rule, timing, inclusion, and legibility surfaces.
See [`../10-framework/anti-averaging-and-constitutional-vetoes.md`](../10-framework/anti-averaging-and-constitutional-vetoes.md).

### 2. Proof-asymmetry screen

Do not certify a soft mode when decisive surfaces are hidden, strategically contested, or weakly measured.
Opacity should make comfortable de-escalation harder, not easier.
See [`../10-framework/burden-of-proof-under-opacity-and-uncertainty.md`](../10-framework/burden-of-proof-under-opacity-and-uncertainty.md).

### 3. Durability-and-repair screen

Do not soften mode choice on one benign snapshot, one rebound, or one crisis-response episode.
Durability windows and unresolved repair debt should be read before any comfortable downgrade.
See [`../10-framework/durability-windows-and-ratchet-tests.md`](../10-framework/durability-windows-and-ratchet-tests.md) and [`emergency-exceptions-sunsets-and-repair-duties.md`](emergency-exceptions-sunsets-and-repair-duties.md).

### 4. Wealth-to-rule override

Strong money-into-rule channels can justify a harder mode even when headline shares look somewhat softer.
See [`../10-framework/wealth-to-rule-conversion.md`](../10-framework/wealth-to-rule-conversion.md).

### 5. Delivery-and-mobility reality check

Do not mistake a rhetorically available package for a realistically available one.
Mode choice should still ask whether the minimum delivery, integrity, visibility, and anti-blackmail rails are materially real.
See [`minimum-viable-implementation-stack.md`](minimum-viable-implementation-stack.md) and [`jurisdictional-mobility-and-anti-blackmail-rules.md`](jurisdictional-mobility-and-anti-blackmail-rules.md).

## Three input families

After the trigger screens, judge the case on three input families.

### 1. Constitutional breach count

Ask whether the society is materially outside the archive's acceptable envelope on the main surfaces:

- private wealth shares,
- bottom-half floor passage,
- public/social counterweight,
- key-asset control,
- intergenerational circulation,
- life-course timing,
- and reconcentration pressure.[S02][S13][S16][S17][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22]

### 2. Closure and masking pressure

Ask whether apparently acceptable averages are being flattered by:

- heavy debt encumbrance,
- household masking of weak person-level rights,
- excluded-group blockage,
- silent intermediary control,
- subsidy substitution, paper claims, or delayed claims standing in for real footholds,
- or extractive gain channels that reward incumbency more than entry.[S13][S20][S25][S26][S27][S28][S29][S30][S31]

### 3. Wealth-to-rule and governability failure

Ask whether concentrated wealth is already purchasing governing leverage through:

- lobbying and political-finance weakness,
- beneficial-ownership opacity,
- capture-prone procurement, licensing, or privatization,
- media or key-infrastructure concentration,
- repeated public absorption of concentrated downside,
- or reform conditions in which elites can regroup faster than households can secure durable footholds.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## Stable triage rule

### Prevention

Use **prevention** only when all of the following are broadly true:

- there are **no major constitutional breaches**,
- closure and masking pressures are present only in mild or early form,
- there is **no strong wealth-to-rule signal**,
- and ordinary broad-based reform still looks politically and administratively plausible.[S13][S18][S19][S31]

This does not mean the society is already good.
It means the archive should lead with early broadening work before concentration hardens into self-defense.

### Correction

Use **correction** when any of the following are true:

- **one major constitutional breach** is clearly present,
- **two or more moderate breaches** reinforce one another,
- headline shares look tolerable but closure is visibly hardening through timing failure, dynastic tightening, or group exclusion,
- or the society still looks governable through ordinary democratic channels but no longer looks safely self-correcting.[S05][S13][S18][S19][S21][S22][S25][S31]

The archive's default bias is that serious timing failure, serious person-level masking, or serious excluded-group blockage should push a society at least into correction even when older-cohort balance sheets still look decent.

### Anti-oligarchy response

Use **anti-oligarchy response** when any of the following are true:

- **multiple major breaches** are present at once,
- one major breach coexists with strong **wealth-to-rule conversion**,
- top-tail opacity is severe enough that the real ownership structure cannot be credibly measured or governed,
- the top tail can already **veto or hollow out reform** through lobbying, media power, party finance, procurement, courts, or administrative capture,
- or repeated crisis support has shown that concentrated owners keep the upside while the public keeps the downside.[S02][S15][S23][S32][S33]

At that point anti-capture work is no longer a late institutional garnish.
It becomes co-equal with floor repair, entry reopening, and top-tail compression.

## Practical count rule

For quick archive use:

1. **Zero major breaches** and no strong capture signal → **prevention**.
2. **One major breach**, or **two reinforcing moderate breaches** → **correction**.
3. **Two major breaches**, or **one major breach plus one strong capture / opacity breach** → **anti-oligarchy response**.
4. Escalate **one level immediately** if wealth-to-rule conversion is already strong enough to block later reform.
5. Do not place a society in **prevention** unless the absence of major breach is positively supported rather than merely unmeasured.
6. Do not soften the mode just because public wealth, services, household aggregation, or future promises make the snapshot look temporarily softer; the archive's substitution and anti-backloading rules still apply.[S05][S12][S15][S18][S19][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## What should count as a major breach

Treat the following as **major** by default:

- bottom-half near-assetlessness or clear failure of the floor passage rule,
- top-tail concentration above the archive's constitutional ceiling,
- strong dynastic closure or timing failure,
- oligarchic control of key assets or stewardship,
- strong opacity around top-tail ownership or transfers,
- clear evidence that large wealth is already purchasing durable political veto,
- or reconcentration pressure strong enough that recent gains are unlikely to survive a normal cycle.[S02][S05][S15][S18][S19][S20][S23][S26]

Treat person-level masking, group exclusion, debt fragility, and non-extractive-formation failure as **moderate-to-major** depending on severity and mutual reinforcement.

## De-escalation rule

Do not lower the mode after a single good year or a temporary post-crisis rebound.
De-escalation should require:

- real evidence rather than strategic missingness,
- improvement that survives a meaningful durability window,
- materially stronger entrant access, floor breadth, governance visibility, and loss-bearing discipline,
- and substantial repair of any crisis derogations that previously justified a harder posture.

That keeps the archive from mistaking a pause in concentration for a constitutional settlement.[S05][S06][S13][S20][S31][S32][S33]

## Why this note matters

The archive now has a cleaner division of labour:

- [`target-selection-rules.md`](../10-framework/target-selection-rules.md) says **what kind of order should count as acceptable**,
- [`prevention-and-response-doctrine.md`](prevention-and-response-doctrine.md) says **what each mode is for**,
- this note says **how to place a case into one mode rather than another**.

That makes the archive more decision-usable without pretending the calibration question is finished.
