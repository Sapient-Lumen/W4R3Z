# Durability windows and ratchet tests

## What this note is for

This note turns one of the archive's older time-side judgments into a **stable durability rule**.
It answers a narrower question than the fuller reading stack in [`../20-program/measurement-and-scoreboard.md`](../20-program/measurement-and-scoreboard.md):
**when should an apparent improvement count as durable enough to matter for constitutional judgment, pathway choice, and possible de-escalation rather than as one flattering plateau?**

The archive does not treat time as a decorative afterthought.
A society can look better for one survey wave, one budget cycle, one housing upswing, one temporary compliance burst, or one post-crisis calm period and still remain organised around reconcentration, dynastic closure, and public downside transfer.[S05][S06][S13][S20][S31][S32][S33]

So this note keeps the temporal side of the archive **compact but functional**.
It distinguishes **provisional improvement** from **durable improvement**, then asks whether the gains have begun to ratchet into a form that is harder to reverse than before.
That is meant to stop two opposite mistakes at once:

1. reading every temporary easing as constitutional progress, and
2. acting as if nothing counts until perfect irreversibility arrives.

## Minimum reading rule

Read improvement through a **window-plus-ratchet** rule.
A case should count as durably improved only when two things are true together:

1. the gain has survived a **provisional durability window** long enough to face ordinary political-budget turnover and ordinary asset / credit pressure, and
2. at least some important reversal channels are now materially **harder to use than before**.[S06][S20][S31][S32][S33]

This is not calendar fetishism.
The archive does not care about time for its own sake; it cares about whether the gain can live through the ordinary forces that usually rebuild hierarchy: lobbying pressure, fiscal retreat, markup power, rent capitalisation, tax arbitrage, weak enforcement, asset repricing, and crisis-side public absorption of private losses.[S05][S13][S20][S31][S32][S33]

So a good-looking snapshot should still count less when it is driven mainly by:
- incumbent asset-price inflation that worsens later entry,
- one-off measures that depend on annual discretion,
- emergency stabilisation that softens losses without changing future burden sharing,
- temporary reporting or compliance spikes without durable enforcement architecture,
- or survey improvements that flatter older incumbents while younger cohorts are still being priced out.[S05][S13][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## The six main false passes

### 1. One good snapshot is mistaken for a constitutional settlement

A single improved reading should count as progress, but not yet as durable status.
The archive should resist the familiar mistake of reading one benign moment as if the underlying conflict were settled.[S20][S31]

### 2. Incumbent-side revaluation is mistaken for broadening

Measured wealth can improve because existing owners gained from price inflation while entrants, renters, and younger households face worse affordability, thinner buffers, and later footholds.
That is often a false plateau, not durable broadening.[S05][S13]

### 3. One-budget-cycle reform is mistaken for a ratchet

Announcing or passing one reform package counts for less when delivery, audit, withholding, renewal, enforcement, or automatic funding are still weak enough that a change of government can quickly hollow the package out.[S04][S23][S31]

### 4. Post-crisis calm is mistaken for repaired loss-bearing

A system can look safer after public support operations while still leaving the next cycle's downside architecture essentially unchanged.
If the public is still quietly absorbing concentrated private risk, the archive should read the calm as fragile.[S32][S33]

### 5. Cross-sectional improvement is mistaken for cohort durability

The archive should count less success when today's measured distribution improves while the next cohort faces worse access to housing, earlier debt dependence, later footholds, or weaker person-level claims.[S05][S13][S18][S19]

### 6. Temporary visibility or compliance is mistaken for durable governance

A short enforcement burst, disclosure campaign, or information exchange gain counts for less if ownership can still drift back into opacity, avoidance, or fragmented oversight once pressure eases.[S23][S31]

## The six standing durability surfaces

### 1. Political-budget survival

The gain should survive at least one ordinary period of fiscal pressure, coalition turnover, or budget reprioritisation strongly enough that the main broadening mechanisms do not simply disappear with the next governing change.[S20][S31]

### 2. Asset / credit-cycle survival

The gain should also survive an ordinary change in housing conditions, interest rates, credit availability, or market repricing without instantly restoring the old hierarchy.[S06][S13][S20]

### 3. Floor ratchet

The bottom-half floor should still broadly pass after mild ordinary stress.
If liquid resilience, secure tenure, or modest protected reserves vanish under routine pressure, the archive should treat the apparent improvement as provisional.[S07][S17][S24][S25]

### 4. Entrant ratchet

Entrants and younger cohorts should still be improving, or at least not being sacrificed to flatter the incumbent snapshot.
If access worsens for the next cohort, durable status should usually be withheld.[S05][S13][S18][S19]

### 5. Governance ratchet

Visibility, anti-capture, withholding, audit, budgeting, and ordinary enforceability should now be stronger enough that later clawback or leakage is harder than before.
The archive is not looking for perfection here, but it is looking for real friction against reversal.[S04][S23][S31]

### 6. Downside and repair ratchet

The next stress episode should be less able to reload hierarchy through public loss absorption, weak clawback, soft resolution, or exceptional support with no repair duty.
If the crisis architecture is unchanged, durable status should count for less.[S32][S33]

## Fast use rule

Use the time side this way in quick classification:

1. ask whether the visible improvement has cleared a provisional durability window at all,
2. if not, classify it as **improving but provisional** rather than as durably improved,
3. if yes, ask whether the six standing durability surfaces are broadly strengthening together,
4. discount the case immediately when entrant access worsens, public downside transfer remains central, or the governance gains are too easy to reverse,
5. de-escalate only when both **levels** and **reversal-resistance** have improved enough that one normal shock or one normal turnover is no longer likely to unwind the gain.

Default shortcut:
- **better levels + no cleared window** -> real improvement may be underway, but treat it as provisional;
- **cleared window + weak ratchets** -> treat the case as fragile rather than durably settled;
- **cleared window + broad ratchet passage** -> stronger ground for de-escalation;
- **better levels + worsening entrant access or renewed public downside transfer** -> read the case as a likely false plateau.[S05][S13][S20][S31][S32][S33]

## Program handoff

This note should sharpen the archive in four places.

First, it should make [`../20-program/measurement-and-scoreboard.md`](../20-program/measurement-and-scoreboard.md) stricter about distinguishing temporary improvement from durable constitutional progress.

Second, it should make [`../20-program/mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md`](../20-program/mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md) slower to de-escalate after one good cycle or one good cross-section.

Third, it should make [`../20-program/transition-sequencing.md`](../20-program/transition-sequencing.md) more explicit that some rails need to be built early because otherwise later gains will be too easy to reverse.

Fourth, it should make [`../20-program/emergency-exceptions-sunsets-and-repair-duties.md`](../20-program/emergency-exceptions-sunsets-and-repair-duties.md) stricter about counting crisis calm as success only when it also changes who bears losses next time.

## Bottom line

The archive should now read durability in this order:

1. **Treat one good snapshot as improvement, not settlement.**
2. **Require a real durability window before calling gains durable enough for de-escalation.**
3. **Look for ratchets that make reversal harder than before, not only better levels.**
4. **Judge the gain from the entrant side and the next-cycle side, not only from incumbent comfort.**
5. **Discount any plateau that still depends on easy opacity, easy clawback, or repeated public downside transfer.**

The point is not to wait forever before judging.
The point is to stop calling a wealth order repaired when the next ordinary turnover or stress episode can still rebuild the same hierarchy almost on schedule.[S05][S20][S31][S32][S33]
