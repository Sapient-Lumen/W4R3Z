# Publicly created value and common-asset capture

## What this note is for

This note turns one of the archive's older cross-cutting claims into a **stable constitutional reading rule**.
It answers a narrower question than the housing / land program note in [`../20-program/housing-land-and-anti-monopoly.md`](../20-program/housing-land-and-anti-monopoly.md):
**when should asset gains created substantially by collective action count as something that should partly return to common wealth, wider access, or durable public capacity rather than harden entirely into private fortunes?**

The archive does not treat all appreciation alike.
A household saving out of wages into a pension, a cooperative building productive equity, and a landowner receiving a large uplift from rezoning, transit, infrastructure, public guarantees, or strategic public land decisions are **not morally equivalent pathways**.
When public action is doing a large share of the value creation, the archive should ask not only who received the gain, but whether any meaningful share flowed back into common wealth, affordable access, or lower future dependence.[S02][S13][S34][S35]

## Minimum reading rule

Read publicly created value through a **recapture-first** rule.
When collective action predictably creates private asset gains, the archive should count the case as stronger only if three things are happening together:

1. a meaningful share of the uplift is being **recaptured** for public/social use,
2. the recapture design is helping to widen **access or common wealth**, not only to book revenue,
3. and the value-creation channel is not mainly functioning as a **quiet incumbent subsidy** that later flatters the measured distribution while leaving entry, public balance sheets, or future bargaining power worse.[S31][S34][S35][S36]

This is not a rule that every gain created under public conditions should be confiscated.
It is a rule against treating publicly generated windfalls as morally neutral by default.
A society that keeps broadening one side of the balance sheet while repeatedly giving away collectively created uplift on the other side is still rebuilding hierarchy.[S02][S20][S34][S35]

## The five main false passes

### 1. Public uplift is mistaken for ordinary earned appreciation

A gain counts less when it is driven mainly by infrastructure, rezoning, development rights, planning permission, public guarantees, or strategic public land decisions rather than by new productive contribution from the holder.[S34][S35]

### 2. Revenue capture is mistaken for broadening

A government can recoup some money and still fail if the recapture does not widen secure entry, durable affordability, public/social wealth, or lower future dependency.
The archive wants **common-value return**, not just another revenue headline.[S02][S34][S35]

### 3. One-off levies are mistaken for durable common wealth

A single charge or negotiated payment counts for less when the broader regime still keeps handing future uplift to private owners while public land, public housing capacity, or public balance sheets remain weak.[S02][S35]

### 4. Public land disposal is mistaken for neutral housekeeping

Repeatedly selling public land for near-term cash with weak affordability, leasehold, resale, or value-recovery conditions is often a transfer of future common wealth into private hands rather than a neutral fiscal tidy-up.[S35]

### 5. Housing or development success is read from incumbent gains rather than entrant access

A place can look wealthier because publicly created uplift flows into existing landowners while later entrants face higher prices, thinner tenure security, and larger dependence on debt or parental wealth.
That is not the archive's idea of broadening.[S13][S19][S34][S35]

## The six standing public-uplift pathways

### 1. Infrastructure and transport uplift

Transit, utilities, flood protection, and other infrastructure investments often raise nearby land and development values.
The archive should ask whether part of that uplift returns to affordable access, infrastructure finance, land banks, or public/social wealth rather than becoming a free private windfall.[S34][S35]

### 2. Rezoning, planning, and development-right uplift

Administrative permission to build more, build higher, or change use can generate large site gains.
The archive should treat those gains as especially suitable for recapture because the value change often comes from a collective legal decision rather than private productive effort alone.[S34][S35]

### 3. Strategic public-land appreciation

Public authorities often control land assembly, release timing, and site preparation.
If public land is improved, assembled, reclassified, or connected through public action, the archive should prefer strategic retention, lease, or conditioned release over simple disposal into private appreciation paths.[S35]

### 4. Public guarantees, insurance, and downside absorption

Public credit support, guarantees, and loss absorption can raise private asset values or lower private risk without an equivalent public upside claim.
Where the state is materially de-risking the gain, the archive should ask why the public is not sharing more meaningfully in the upside.[S31][S32][S33]

### 5. Regulatory scarcity and franchise value

Sometimes the public creates asset value not by building something physical, but by allocating scarcity, licenses, concession rights, exclusive access, or other bottleneck permissions.
The archive should count those gains less like natural market earnings and more like publicly conferred privilege requiring stronger common return.[S10][S34]

### 6. Place-making and coordinated public capacity

Schools, safety, parks, sanitation, and broader public investment ecosystems can cumulatively raise local asset values.
Where collective place-making is doing a large share of the value creation, the archive should ask whether common wealth institutions are sharing in the result or only financing the conditions for private gain.[S02][S34][S35]

## Fast use rule

When asset values rise quickly after public action, use this order:

1. **Ask what portion of the gain was collectively created.**
2. **Ask whether any of that gain is being recaptured in a durable form.**
3. **Ask whether recapture widens access, common wealth, or future bargaining power.**
4. **Ask whether public land, guarantees, or planning power are being used strategically or simply converted into private windfalls.**
5. **Discount the apparent distributional success** if incumbent revaluation is outrunning entrant access or public balance-sheet strengthening.

Default shortcut:

- **large public role + weak recapture** -> treat the gain as a constitutional warning sign;
- **large public role + partial recapture but weak access effects** -> treat the case as only partial success;
- **large public role + durable recapture into access and common wealth** -> count the case as stronger on both the public-counterweight and reconcentration surfaces.[S02][S20][S34][S35]

## Program handoff

This note should make the archive stricter in three places.

First, it should harden the housing and land program against stories in which owner wealth rises because public action bids up sites while access keeps closing.
See [`../20-program/housing-land-and-anti-monopoly.md`](../20-program/housing-land-and-anti-monopoly.md).

Second, it should strengthen the archive's preference for routing publicly created value into **durable common wealth** that can finance social inheritance, infrastructure, land banks, or other broadening institutions instead of leaving the gain in narrow private hands.
See [`../20-program/social-inheritance-public-wealth-and-baby-bonds.md`](../20-program/social-inheritance-public-wealth-and-baby-bonds.md).

Third, it should stop tax expenditures, guarantees, and development policy from being read as neutral when they are quietly creating asset value for incumbents without a matching public claim.
See [`upside-down-subsidies-and-the-hidden-wealth-state.md`](upside-down-subsidies-and-the-hidden-wealth-state.md) and [`../20-program/tax-expenditures-and-subsidy-hygiene.md`](../20-program/tax-expenditures-and-subsidy-hygiene.md).

## Bottom line

The archive should now read publicly created value in this order:

1. **Do not treat collective uplift like morally neutral appreciation.**
2. **Ask whether meaningful recapture is happening at all.**
3. **Count recapture fully only when it strengthens access, common wealth, or durable public capacity.**
4. **Discount incumbent-rich success stories when public action is mainly capitalising into private fortunes.**
5. **Treat weak recapture as a reconcentration warning even when headline wealth measures improve.**

The point is not to deny all private gain under public conditions.
The point is to stop calling a wealth order acceptable while collective action keeps generating large fortunes with too little common return.[S02][S34][S35]
