# Acceptable envelopes and substitution limits

## What this note is for

This note is the archive's **envelope-side companion** to [`target-selection-rules.md`](target-selection-rules.md).
It answers a narrower question than the full target-selection procedure:
**when should a wealth distribution near the archive's preferred center count as an acceptable variation, and what kinds of substitution should make that claim fail?**

The archive keeps **20 / 50 / 20 / 10** as its central attractor.
But it does **not** treat one exact percentile tuple as the only morally acceptable destination.
A society with stronger public/social wealth, stronger universal services, secure tenure, earlier footholds, and weaker dynastic closure may need a bit less **private** bottom-half wealth than a society that pushes more risk back onto households.
A society with unusually strong lower-half footholds may also sit a bit to the other side of the same center.
What the archive rejects is turning that limited flexibility into an all-purpose excuse.
Public wealth, pooled capital, or strong services can change **how much private wealth is needed**; they do not excuse bottom-half assetlessness, late footholds, person-level dependence, hidden control, or top-tail rule.[S02][S13][S16][S18][S19][S21][S22][S26]

## Minimum reading rule

Read envelope flexibility as **narrow and conditional**.
A lower private bottom-half share can count as near-acceptable only when common provision and public/social wealth really reduce household exposure.
A higher bottom-half share can count as near-acceptable only when it reflects real footholds rather than leveraged owner counts, incumbent revaluation, or fragile paper gains.
Nothing in this note softens the archive's judgment on timing, visibility, anti-capture, stewardship, or person-level security.[S05][S13][S15][S18][S19][S23][S26]

## The working family of acceptable envelopes

These are not three unrelated ideals.
They are three nearby ways a society can remain broadly free, broadly owning, and non-oligarchic.

### 1. Common-wealth-heavy envelope — roughly **15 / 55 / 20 / 10**

This can count as near-acceptable only when public wealth is clearly positive and usable,[S02] core protections are strong enough to reduce required private precautionary balances,[S21][S22] secure tenure is real where owner-occupation is not,[S07] early adulthood still gets real footholds rather than mainly later parental transfer,[S18][S19] and top-tail money-into-rule channels are still tightly contained.[S15][S23]

This is **not** permission for a near-assetless bottom half.
It is acceptable only when common wealth and common protection are doing real anti-domination work.

### 2. Balanced broad-owner envelope — roughly **20 / 50 / 20 / 10**

This remains the archive's **default attractor**.
It is still the clearest all-purpose target for rich and upper-middle-income societies that need both visible lower-half thickening and a large plural middle without pretending exact equality is the aim.

### 3. Bottom-thick dispersed-owner envelope — roughly **25 / 45 / 20 / 10**

This can count as near-acceptable only when the extra lower-half share reflects **real footholds**: liquid buffers, safer debt structures, secure housing positions, direct long-run claims, and wider access to productive capital that is not washed out by fees, leverage, or locked stewardship.[S13][S16][S17][S24][S25][S26]

A somewhat thinner middle can be tolerated here only if the middle still remains large enough to function as a plural civic majority rather than a squeezed belt between a large lower half and a re-powering elite top.

## What can vary across the family

The archive allows **limited** variation in four places:

1. the private bottom-half share can move within a narrow band depending on how much common provision really reduces required self-insurance,[S21][S22]
2. the middle can be somewhat thicker or somewhat thinner depending on whether lower-half thickening is real without creating polarization,
3. housing security can be delivered through ownership or durable tenure when households still have stable, affordable, non-arbitrary control of their homes,[S07][S13]
4. and some capital claims can be pooled rather than direct only when stewardship and voting are not bottlenecked inside a narrow manager layer.[S26]

## The substitution limits

The archive should be explicit about what can and cannot substitute for what.

### Allowed but limited substitutions

1. **Strong public/social provision can reduce required private buffers.** Better health, pensions, care, and income protection mean households do not need the same volume of private emergency wealth merely to remain safe.[S21][S22]
2. **Secure tenure can partly substitute for owner-occupation in the housing part of the floor test.** A household can be secure without owning its dwelling when tenure is durable, affordable, and not easily revoked.[S07]
3. **Public/social wealth plus early social inheritance can partly substitute for a somewhat larger private bottom-half share.** Common wealth matters when it is institutionally usable and reaches people early enough to shape adult life.[S02][S18][S19]

### Forbidden substitutions

1. **Public wealth cannot substitute for a direct lower-half floor.** Strong services do not make it acceptable for ordinary households to remain near-assetless, deeply indebted, or one shock from dependence.[S16][S17][S21][S22]
2. **Home equity cannot substitute for liquidity and debt quality.** Paper housing wealth counts less when it rests on thin equity, high servicing strain, refinancing dependence, or entry exclusion.[S13][S16][S24][S25]
3. **Late inheritance cannot substitute for early footholds.** A society still fails when secure ownership mainly arrives after adulthood has already been shaped by parental position.[S05][S18][S19]
4. **Household wealth cannot substitute for person-level rights.** Aggregate household gains do not count fully when one adult still lacks direct title, usable account access, pension rights, or credible exit security.[S27][S28][S29][S30]
5. **Broad fund ownership cannot substitute for democratic stewardship.** Mass capital exposure is incomplete if voting and engagement remain concentrated inside a narrow manager layer.[S26]
6. **Positive public wealth cannot substitute for anti-capture if the top tail still rules.** A society does not pass simply because it owns more publicly if large fortunes can still dominate media, lobbying, procurement, rescue design, or rulemaking.[S15][S23]

## Fast comparison rule

When the archive compares countries, periods, or reform packages near the target zone, use this order:

1. **Start from the default attractor.** Begin with **20 / 50 / 20 / 10** rather than searching for clever exceptions.
2. **Ask why the case belongs off-center.** Movement toward **15 / 55 / 20 / 10** needs an unusually strong public/social counterweight and early-entry story. Movement toward **25 / 45 / 20 / 10** needs unusually real, resilient, entrant-friendly lower-half thickening.
3. **Screen for forbidden substitutions.** Reject the case if apparently softer shares are being bought with late inheritance, debt fragility, household masking, stewardship bottlenecks, or top-tail rule.
4. **Stop if a veto family is still live.** If floor failure, strong opacity, money-into-rule conversion, or severe timing failure remains active, nearby numerical envelopes are not yet morally interesting.
5. **Escalate to the fuller rule when the case is close or flattering.** Use [`target-selection-rules.md`](target-selection-rules.md) when the comparison depends on many companion judgments.

## Bottom line

The archive's target is **not** one frozen point.
But it is also **not** an anything-goes corridor.
It is a **small constitutional family** around a broad bottom, a thick middle, a bounded top, and real public/social counterweight.

The question is never only whether a country sits near a pleasing share vector.
The question is whether the society has earned any deviation from the default center without hiding fragility, delay, dependence, or domination somewhere else.[S02][S13][S16][S18][S19][S21][S22][S23][S26]
