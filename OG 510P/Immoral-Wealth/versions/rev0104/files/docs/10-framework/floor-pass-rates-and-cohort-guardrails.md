# Floor pass rates and cohort guardrails

## Thesis

A society should not count as having solved the **bottom-half floor** merely because a slim majority scrapes over the line, or because older homeowners make the averages look safer than the next cohort really is.

The archive should now treat the floor as a **population standard with cohort guardrails**.
The default operating rule is:

- the floor should count as broadly passed only when **roughly two-thirds or more** of bottom-half households clear a core floor test,
- no major younger-adult or excluded-group cohort should sit far below that rate,
- and the pass should not depend mainly on late-life asset holding while younger adults remain locked out of secure entry.[S07][S13][S16][S17][S19]

This is a **provisional constitutional rule**, not a claim that one exact number is timeless.
But the archive should move away from the idea that **50% plus one** is enough.

## Why the archive needs this rule

The archive already says a morally acceptable order needs more than better aggregate shares.
It needs a real floor: positive net wealth, liquid resilience, secure housing, a durable long-run claim, and debt that does not routinely wipe out the gains from ownership.

What it lacked was a compact answer to a practical question:
**how many households have to clear that floor before the society can be said to have one?**

That matters because the empirical pattern is already clear.
OECD's recent household-wealth work says the bottom quintile often has **negative wealth** and that housing remains households' largest asset.[S16]
OECD work on financial fragility says many middle-income households lack enough liquid assets to stay above the poverty line for even a few months after an income shock.[S17]
OECD work on opportunity and inheritances says parental homeownership and early transfers still shape adult outcomes strongly, and that timing matters: wealth received earlier in life has larger effects on later outcomes.[S19]

So the archive should not allow a society to pass the floor test just because:

- many older households eventually accumulate housing wealth,
- a narrow upper slice of the bottom half looks safer,
- or public averages hide the fact that younger adults, renters, and excluded groups remain too fragile or too delayed.

## From the floor bundle to a countable pass

Use the current floor bundle from [`minimum-asset-floor.md`](minimum-asset-floor.md).
For population scoring, the archive should provisionally treat a household as a **core floor pass** when:

1. it has **positive net wealth**,
2. it has either a **liquid shock buffer** or a **secure housing position**,
3. it is not in a clearly **predatory or chronic debt-distress position**,
4. and it has at least one **durable long-run asset claim** beyond a momentary cash balance.[S07][S13][S16][S17]

This is intentionally compact.
Different surveys and administrative systems will operationalise the items differently.
The point is to avoid a fake pass in which a household has one asset on paper but no resilience, no tenure security, or no credible long-run claim.

## Provisional pass-rate rule

Until the archive develops a better cross-country calibration, use this default rule for the **bottom 50%**:

- **Constitutional pass zone:** about **70% or more** of bottom-half households clear the core floor test.
- **Watch / weak pass zone:** about **60% to 69%** clear it. This may be tolerable only if cohort and group guardrails are also healthy and reconcentration pressure is low.
- **Clear floor failure:** **below 60%**.
- **Major floor breach:** **below 50%**, or a rate only temporarily propped up by asset revaluation, subsidy inflation, or older-cohort balance sheets.

The archive's direction here is deliberate:
a morally acceptable wealth order should require a **supermajority**, not a knife-edge majority.
The bottom half is too large and too central to democratic stability for the archive to call a society acceptable when floor security is still a coin flip.

## Cohort guardrails

A floor pass should also fail when it is being carried mainly by age.

The archive should therefore apply two default cohort guardrails:

1. **Under-40 guardrail**
   - The under-40 bottom-half pass rate should not sit more than about **15 percentage points** below the all-age bottom-half pass rate for long.
2. **Early-foothold guardrail**
   - Young-adult positive-wealth and secure-tenure rates should be high enough that ordinary adulthood is not organised around waiting for later inheritance, partnership rescue, or parental property help.[S05][S19]

Where the overall bottom-half pass rate looks acceptable but the under-40 floor is collapsing, the archive should treat that as a **timing failure**, not as a genuine pass.

## Group guardrails

Where disaggregated data exist, the archive should not accept a national floor pass when historically subordinated or systematically excluded groups remain far below it.

A workable default is:

- treat a persistent gap of roughly **15 percentage points or more** between the general bottom-half pass rate and the pass rate for a major excluded group as a serious warning,
- and treat much larger or durable gaps as a **group-stratification failure** even when the headline national rate looks respectable.[S13][S19]

This keeps the archive from mistaking a broad national average for genuinely open wealth-building.

## What this implies for doctrine

This note changes how the archive should read floor breaches in practice:

- a society should move at least into **correction** when the bottom-half floor pass rate sits below the rough supermajority threshold for a sustained period,
- a society should treat **under-40 collapse**, large excluded-group gaps, or fake passes driven by debt-fragile owner-occupation as escalation factors,
- and a society should usually count **sub-50% floor passage** as a **major constitutional breach** in the mode-selection heuristic.[S13][S17][S19][S25]

The point is not to fetishise one threshold.
It is to stop calling a society decent when ordinary asset security remains too thinly spread to anchor democratic independence.

## What this changes in the archive

This note resolves one open issue in provisional form.
The archive should no longer ask whether the floor requires a **simple majority** or a **supermajority** pass standard.
Its default answer is now: **supermajority, with cohort and group guardrails**.

The live empirical questions become narrower:

- which exact survey mappings best capture the core floor test in different country types,
- how tight the young-adult and excluded-group guardrails should be,
- and whether the default pass zone should be closer to the high 60s or low 70s once more country evidence is assembled.[S07][S13][S16][S17][S19]
