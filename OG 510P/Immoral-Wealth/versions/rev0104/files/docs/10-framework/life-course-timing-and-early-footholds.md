# Life-course timing and early footholds

## What this note is for

This note turns one of the archive's core circulation claims into a **stable timing rule**.
It answers a narrower question than the fuller target procedure in [`target-selection-rules.md`](target-selection-rules.md):
**when must a first durable foothold arrive for a society to count as open in adulthood rather than merely promising security much later?**

The archive does **not** treat wealth timing as a secondary detail.
A society can improve snapshot shares, raise the wealth of older households, or make inheritance look more equal while still failing because ordinary adults without rich parents spend the decisive early part of life locked out of housing, liquid margin, and durable claims.[S05][S13][S18][S19]

That means this note is not just about whether people eventually receive something.
It is about whether secure entry arrives **early enough to shape ordinary adult life**: where to live, whether to form a household, whether to change jobs, whether to take risk, whether to escape humiliating dependence, and whether adulthood itself is open before family death or long incumbent waitlists deliver relief.[S05][S13][S19]

## Minimum reading rule

Read timing as a **constitutional part of openness**, not as a cosmetic refinement.
A society should count as more just only when most people without rich parents can reach a first durable foothold early enough that it changes adulthood in practice rather than arriving mainly after decades of rent extraction, fragile debt, or delayed inheritance.[S05][S13][S18][S19]

The archive's compact intuition is:
**security that arrives mainly after the main life-shaping decisions have already been forced under scarcity is not fully open adulthood.**

This note therefore reads delayed arrival as a serious downgrade.
It should be read more harshly when the apparent broadening of wealth depends mainly on:
- later-life inheritance,
- older-cohort incumbent lock-in,
- delayed access to housing or secure tenure,
- early claims that are too locked-up or too easy to strand,
- or public policy that protects owners late while leaving young adults to absorb the hard part of the wealth regime alone.

## The six main false passes

### 1. Better snapshot shares are mistaken for open adulthood

A society should count less progress when aggregate shares improve while large parts of the under-40 population remain asset-poor, tenure-insecure, or trapped in delayed first entry.[S13][S19]

### 2. Eventual inheritance is mistaken for timely freedom

Later-life bequests should count for less when ordinary adulthood still depends on waiting for parental death or family transfer before housing access, entrepreneurship, or durable security become realistic.[S05][S19]

### 3. Older broadening is mistaken for cohort openness

A society should count less success when ownership looks broad mainly because older incumbents remain secure longer, while younger cohorts are priced out or forced into thinner and later entry.[S05][S13]

### 4. Early claims are mistaken for life-shaping footholds

A grant, account, or formal asset should count for less when it exists early in name but is too locked-up, too penalty-laden, too gatekeeper-dependent, or too non-portable to change real early-adult choices.

### 5. Small timing improvements are mistaken for resolved birth-lottery dependence

A society should count less success when reforms move entry slightly forward but parental wealth still strongly determines who gets secure housing, lower debt, or the ability to take ordinary economic risks in early adulthood.[S18][S19]

### 6. Late repair is mistaken for equal life chances

Catch-up support later in life matters, but it should not be read as a full substitute for secure earlier entry when the missing early foothold already shaped education, work, family formation, mobility, and bargaining position for decades.[S05][S19]

## The six standing timing surfaces

### 1. Age of first durable foothold

The archive should ask when ordinary adults without rich parents typically reach a first durable foothold: positive net position, stable liquid margin, secure tenure, or a portable capital claim that can shape planning rather than just signal eventual future security.

### 2. Housing-entry timing

Housing is central because it anchors where adulthood is lived.
The timing surface is stronger when younger adults can reach secure and affordable tenure without waiting for large gifts, inheritances, or long incumbent protection cycles.[S13][S19]

### 3. Transfer timing rather than transfer existence alone

Transfers count differently depending on when they arrive.
The archive should read earlier-life transfers more favorably than later-life ones because earlier receipt changes the path of adulthood more deeply, while delayed receipt often merely compensates after major stratifying decisions have already been made.[S05][S19]

### 4. Lock-up, portability, and ordinary usability in the early phase

An early claim counts more when it can survive ordinary movement across jobs, households, and places and when it is usable without severe penalty or waiting rules.
Early appearance on paper should count for less when access is delayed or transition-fragile.

### 5. Cohort balance and incumbent overhang

The timing surface is stronger when younger cohorts are not being asked to wait unusually long while older cohorts hold appreciating assets, secure positions, or delayed-transfer advantages for longer periods.[S05][S13]

### 6. Public institutions that shift entry forward

The timing surface is stronger when social inheritance, non-predatory housing access, debt relief, child development accounts, public wealth channels, and anti-rent policy move first footholds earlier in life rather than asking households to endure insecurity until middle age.[S18][S19]

## Fast use rule

Use this note in four steps:

1. **Ask when the first real foothold arrives.**  
   Do ordinary people without rich parents usually gain secure tenure, liquid resilience, or durable capital claims before the main adult life path is already set?
2. **Discount delayed or unusable arrival.**  
   Count less progress when the foothold depends on late inheritance, severe lock-up, poor portability, or thin formal claims that do not change real choices.
3. **Check cohort openness rather than older incumbent comfort alone.**  
   Read the case more harshly when the owner middle is aging in place while younger cohorts remain late entrants.
4. **Prefer policies that move entry forward.**  
   Count more progress when public action shifts footholds earlier in life rather than merely compensating later.

Default shortcut:

- **early broad footholds** -> read the case more favorably because adulthood is more genuinely open;
- **late or inheritance-gated footholds** -> read the case more harshly because the society is outsourcing freedom to parental timing;
- **mixed case** -> ask whether the missing early foothold is still doing major life-shaping damage even if later balances look somewhat better.

## Program implication

Once this timing surface is read clearly, two policy mistakes become easier to avoid.
First, the archive should stop mistaking later-life equalisation for open adulthood.
Second, it should stop treating inheritance reform, housing reform, and social inheritance as separable questions when they are all partly about **when** secure entry arrives.

That is why this note sits between circulation doctrine and the main program lanes.
A society moves closer to justice when it gives people a durable foothold early enough to shape ordinary adult life without needing wealthy parents, extraordinary luck, or decades of waiting.

## Bottom line

The archive should now read timing in this order:

1. **Ask when the first durable foothold typically arrives for people without wealthy parents.**
2. **Discount later-life inheritance and older-cohort lock-in as substitutes for open adulthood.**
3. **Count early claims more favorably only when they are usable, portable, and life-shaping in practice.**
4. **Prefer reforms that move secure entry forward rather than backloading justice into later decades.**

The point is not to fetishise youth as such.
It is to stop calling a wealth order open when adulthood is still gated by delay.[S05][S13][S18][S19]
