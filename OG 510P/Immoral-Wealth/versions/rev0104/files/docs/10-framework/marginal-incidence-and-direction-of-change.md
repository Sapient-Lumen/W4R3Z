# Marginal incidence and direction of change

## Thesis

A society should not be judged only by its current stock distribution.
It should also be judged by the **direction of its next increments**.

rev0035 adds a compact framework rule:
**ask who gets the next durable unit of net wealth after incidence, capitalization, timing, debt, and control are taken into account.**
If the next round of gains created or shaped by public policy, credit conditions, land-use decisions, tax design, retirement incentives, crisis support, or market structure still flows mainly to already-positioned owners, then the society is not moving toward a morally acceptable wealth order even if the headline snapshot softens a little.[S13][S16][S19][S31][S34][S36][S37]

## Why the archive needs this rule

The archive already had nearby doctrines:

- [`upside-down-subsidies-and-the-hidden-wealth-state.md`](upside-down-subsidies-and-the-hidden-wealth-state.md) asks who actually benefits from nominally pro-ownership support,[S31][S36][S37]
- [`publicly-created-value-and-common-asset-capture.md`](publicly-created-value-and-common-asset-capture.md) asks whether collective uplift is being privatized,[S34][S35]
- [`promissory-offsets-and-backloaded-justice.md`](promissory-offsets-and-backloaded-justice.md) asks whether present injustice is being washed by future promises,
- and [`asymmetric-tradeoffs-and-no-regret-priorities.md`](asymmetric-tradeoffs-and-no-regret-priorities.md) asks whether one gain is being purchased by worsening another constitutional surface.

What the archive had not yet said compactly is that **transition quality itself has a distributive shape**.
Two societies can have similarly unjust starting stocks yet be moving in opposite moral directions:

- one directs new subsidies, public uplift, tax preference, and first-entry support toward entrants, younger adults, excluded groups, and common wealth;
- the other lets the next wave of gains capitalize into incumbent land values, higher-balance retirement accounts, monopoly markups, late inheritances, or protected top-tail portfolios.

That difference matters because a wealth order is reproduced not only by the size of current fortunes, but by **who repeatedly captures the next round of gains**.

## The marginal-incidence rule

For regime judgment and reform design, the archive should ask:

1. **Who receives the next net gain?**
   - After taxes, subsidies, rents, fees, leverage, and price effects are considered, do the next increments mainly widen first footholds or mainly deepen incumbent position?[S31][S36][S37]
2. **Who captures publicly created uplift?**
   - When infrastructure, rezoning, public land strategy, or local growth raise site values, does the next increment widen access or mainly enrich current landholders?[S34][S35]
3. **Who gets earlier, stronger compounding time?**
   - A smaller early foothold can matter more than a larger late transfer; the archive should read timing into the marginal path, not only into the endpoint.[S05][S18][S19]
4. **Who holds the usable claim?**
   - A new claim should count less if it is heavily debt-fragile, highly intermediated, weakly titled, or unable to shape real household autonomy.[S13][S16][S24][S25]
5. **Who keeps the governance and downside terms?**
   - New capital claims should count less if control remains bottlenecked or if the public still absorbs exceptional downside while concentrated owners keep the upside.[S26][S32][S33]

This is the compact archive intuition:
**a society is moving the right way when the next durable units of wealth are entrant-weighted, floor-thickening, earlier in life, more directly held, and less easily reconcentrated.**

## What counts as a good direction of change

Treat a path as directionally broadening when several of the following are true:

1. **The next public supports are bottom-heavy or entrant-heavy**
   - Flat credits, capped matches, fixed nominal contributions, baby bonds, starter assets, rental-to-ownership bridges, or supply-linked support do more to widen first footholds than open-ended deductions scaled by tax bracket or balance size.[S12][S31][S36][S37]
2. **The next housing and land gains widen access**
   - More of the next round of local uplift is recaptured or translated into secure entry, affordable tenure, or common wealth rather than into incumbent windfalls.[S34][S35]
3. **The next retirement and long-run claims are broadened earlier**
   - New support builds claims for households with weaker inherited position rather than merely topping up already-strong accumulators.[S05][S19][S37]
4. **The next gains survive ordinary shocks**
   - New footholds come with lower debt fragility, better liquidity, or stronger public floor support rather than with thinner equity and greater exposure to ordinary reversals.[S17][S24][S25]
5. **The next gains are less exclusionary**
   - Reforms widen access for younger cohorts and historically excluded groups rather than mainly rewarding incumbents already inside the main compounding channels.[S13][S19]
6. **The next gains thicken common wealth as well as private wealth**
   - Some of the next round of growth, rents, or uplift strengthens public/social wealth, not only private concentration.[S02][S34][S35]

## What counts as a bad direction of change

Treat a path as morally suspect even if headline stocks improve when several of the following are true:

1. **Incumbent capitalization dominates**
   - Housing or land support mostly raises seller prices, landlord rents, or site values faster than it widens entry.[S07][S34][S35][S36]
2. **Support scales with prior position**
   - The next fiscal effort mainly rewards households with higher tax rates, larger balances, existing property, or easier access to formal saving channels.[S31][S36][S37]
3. **Later cohorts must buy into a more expensive system**
   - Broad gains for incumbents are financed by worse entry terms, higher leverage, or longer waits for the next cohort.[S05][S13][S19]
4. **The next claims are thin, silent, or fragile**
   - New wealth is mostly paper appreciation, highly intermediated claims, or leverage-backed equity that does not materially widen durable autonomy.[S13][S16][S24][S25][S26]
5. **Public support keeps socializing downside**
   - The next cycle still leaves the public carrying large rescue or stabilization burdens while concentrated owners keep unusually favorable terms.[S32][S33]

A society does not move toward the archive's target just because some percentile share drifts downward at the top or upward at the bottom.
It moves toward the target when the **new gains themselves** are being routed more democratically.

## Operational rule

When the archive evaluates a reform, a transition package, or a country's current direction, it should run this compact test:

1. **Where will the next 100 units of net wealth probably land?**
2. **How much of that landing is visible after incidence and capitalization rather than only in statutory form?**
3. **How much reaches entrants, younger adults, excluded groups, or common wealth rather than incumbent owners?**
4. **How much arrives early enough, securely enough, and directly enough to change life chances?**
5. **Would the path still look like broadening if judged from the entrant side rather than from the incumbent side?**

If the answers point toward incumbent capture, delayed compensation, heavier leverage, or public downside transfer, the archive should treat the path as **directionally suspect** even before the final stock distribution is remeasured.

## Program implication

The archive should prefer packages whose **marginal incidence** is:

- **entrant-weighted** rather than incumbent-weighted,
- **early-life** rather than late-life,
- **person-visible** rather than merely household-averaged,
- **common-wealth-building** rather than purely price-capitalizing,
- **debt-lightening** rather than leverage-deepening,
- and **recapture-aware** rather than windfall-tolerant.[S12][S31][S34][S35][S36][S37]

This does not replace the stock target.
It tells the archive how to judge whether current policy is actually moving toward that target rather than merely talking in its direction.

## What this changes

rev0035 makes four archive claims explicit:

1. **transition paths have a distributive shape, not only endpoints;**
2. **the next durable units of wealth matter morally because they reproduce or unwind the existing order;**
3. **headline softening should count less when marginal gains still flow mainly to incumbents;**
4. and **reform packages should be judged partly by whether their next-round incidence is entrant-weighted, earlier, more direct, and less easily reconcentrated.**
