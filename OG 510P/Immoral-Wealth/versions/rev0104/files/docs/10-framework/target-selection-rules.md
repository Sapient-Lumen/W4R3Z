# Target selection rules

## Purpose

The archive does not choose an ideal wealth distribution by aesthetic taste alone.
It chooses a target by asking which distribution is most likely to sustain **independent ordinary adulthood, broad plural ownership, low wealth-to-rule conversion, open circulation across cohorts and groups, real public/social counterweight, and low reconcentration pressure** rather than merely posting attractive averages.[S02][S16][S18][S19][S20][S26][S31][S34]

This note is the archive's **compact target-selection procedure**.
It replaces an accreted revision-history style with a stable map:
- a default attractor,
- a narrow acceptable envelope,
- five constitutional tests,
- and a companion-rule map for discounting false passes.

## Default attractor and acceptable envelope

The archive's default attractor remains:

- **20 / 50 / 20 / 10** for the bottom 50 / middle 40 / next 9 / top 1.

The archive does **not** treat that as a sacred point.
It treats it as the center of a **narrow acceptable family**, roughly:

- **15 / 55 / 20 / 10**
- **20 / 50 / 20 / 10**
- **25 / 45 / 20 / 10**

But nearby envelopes count only when they also satisfy the archive's constitutional tests.
A society should not get near-acceptable treatment just because one share vector looks close if the bottom half still lacks a real floor, the top tail still converts wealth into rule, footholds still arrive too late, public/social wealth is too weak, or reconcentration pressure is still high.[S02][S05][S16][S18][S19]

For the fuller envelope doctrine, see [`acceptable-envelopes-and-substitution-limits.md`](acceptable-envelopes-and-substitution-limits.md).
For red lines and nearby numerical warnings, see [`ideal-wealth-distribution-bands.md`](ideal-wealth-distribution-bands.md).

## Constitutional reading order

Read any proposed target in this order:

1. **Veto-first**
   - Do not average away a catastrophic constitutional breach. See [`anti-averaging-and-constitutional-vetoes.md`](anti-averaging-and-constitutional-vetoes.md).

2. **Proof asymmetry**
   - Do not certify easy success when decisive surfaces are hidden, weakly measured, or strategically contested. See [`burden-of-proof-under-opacity-and-uncertainty.md`](burden-of-proof-under-opacity-and-uncertainty.md).

3. **Durability and repair debt**
   - Do not de-escalate on one benign snapshot, and do not treat unresolved emergency derogations as self-cleaning. See [`durability-windows-and-ratchet-tests.md`](durability-windows-and-ratchet-tests.md) and [`../20-program/emergency-exceptions-sunsets-and-repair-duties.md`](../20-program/emergency-exceptions-sunsets-and-repair-duties.md).

4. **No bad tradeoffs, no promissory laundering**
   - Do not buy one constitutional gain by worsening another, and do not wash current breach with delayed or weakly deliverable future compensation. See [`asymmetric-tradeoffs-and-no-regret-priorities.md`](asymmetric-tradeoffs-and-no-regret-priorities.md) and [`promissory-offsets-and-backloaded-justice.md`](promissory-offsets-and-backloaded-justice.md).

5. **Wealth-to-rule override**
   - Strong money-into-rule channels can force escalation even when simple shares look somewhat softer. See [`wealth-to-rule-conversion.md`](wealth-to-rule-conversion.md).

6. **Path quality**
   - Prefer entrant-weighted, bottom-weighted, and common-wealth-building paths over incumbent-weighted revaluation stories. See [`marginal-incidence-and-direction-of-change.md`](marginal-incidence-and-direction-of-change.md).

7. **Implementation realism and anti-blackmail sequencing**
   - Prefer targets that can be approached through administrable floor-building, visible domestic choke points, and immobile rents before relying on the hardest high-mobility instruments. See [`../20-program/minimum-viable-implementation-stack.md`](../20-program/minimum-viable-implementation-stack.md) and [`../20-program/jurisdictional-mobility-and-anti-blackmail-rules.md`](../20-program/jurisdictional-mobility-and-anti-blackmail-rules.md).

## The five constitutional tests

A target is acceptable only if it can plausibly pass **all five** tests.

### 1. Broad-shares test

A target must imply a society in which:

- the **bottom 50%** owns enough wealth that mass assetlessness is no longer normal,
- the **middle 40%** holds a large enough share to sustain a broad plural middle,
- the **top 1%** and especially the **top 0.1%** are kept below clear domination ranges,
- and public balance-sheet weakness is not so severe that private concentration remains the only effective buffer or rule source.[S02][S16]

This is the archive's headline-distribution test, not the whole moral test.
Shares matter, but they are only the first screen.

### 2. Real-floor test

A target must imply a **real bottom-half asset floor**, not merely positive averages or thin household-level balances.

Use the archive's floor spine in [`minimum-asset-floor.md`](minimum-asset-floor.md). In compact form, the floor must be broad enough and strong enough that bottom-half households usually have:

- positive net wealth,
- liquid resilience,
- secure and adequate housing,
- retainable margin after ordinary carrying costs,
- some durable long-run claims,
- manageable debt,
- and person-level adequacy / control.

The default reading remains **supermajority plus cohort guardrails**: the floor should not count as constitutionally real just because a bare majority clears it, or because older cohorts flatter the average while younger adults and excluded groups lag badly behind.[S16][S17][S18][S19]

### 3. Governable-control test

A target must imply a society whose key assets are governable without oligarchic bottlenecks.

That means asking not only **who owns wealth by value**, but also:

- who controls major firms, land, infrastructure, finance, and media,
- how concentrated stewardship remains inside pooled ownership,
- how visible beneficial ownership and transfer channels are,
- and whether top-tail wealth still converts into lobbying, agenda power, rescue favoritism, procurement advantage, or other rule-setting leverage.[S20][S23][S26]

A target should count less when it softens household shares a little while leaving the commanding heights and money-into-rule channels essentially intact.

### 4. Open-circulation-and-timing test

A target must imply a society that is open across generations, cohorts, groups, and ordinary life stages.

That means asking whether:

- parental background still predicts adult asset position too strongly,
- late inheritance is being mistaken for open access,
- first durable footholds arrive early enough to shape adulthood,
- historically subordinated groups remain locked out of the main accumulation channels,
- person-level ownership inside households remains badly skewed,
- or high-opportunity places remain territorially closed even when national averages look softer.[S05][S18][S19][S34]

A morally acceptable target is not just less concentrated; it is **more open**.

### 5. Durable-counterweight-and-low-reconcentration test

A target must imply a society in which broadening does not quietly rebuild the old order.

That means asking whether:

- public/social wealth is materially real rather than rhetorically thin,
- ordinary households are not forced into excessive precautionary self-insurance,
- hidden subsidies and tax expenditures are not mainly rewarding incumbent owners,
- broad gains are not mainly extractive rents or incumbent revaluation,
- downside is not repeatedly socialised for concentrated actors,
- and new durable gains are landing with entrants, younger cohorts, excluded groups, or common wealth strongly enough to survive normal political and asset cycles.[S02][S10][S20][S31][S35]

This is the archive's anti-false-arrival test.
A target is weak when it can be reached only on paper, only briefly, or only by routes that immediately regenerate concentration.

## Practical selection procedure

When the archive has to choose or compare targets, use this procedure:

1. **Start from the default attractor**
   - Begin with **20 / 50 / 20 / 10** rather than inventing a fresh shape each time.

2. **Test nearby envelopes**
   - Allow movement inside the narrow acceptable family only if the five constitutional tests still pass.

3. **Screen for vetoes and hidden weakness**
   - Reject targets that look good only because floor failure, opacity, rule conversion, late footholds, or reconcentration are being averaged away.

4. **Prefer early footholds over late compensation**
   - Prefer targets that imply earlier bottom-half and entrant security, not just later inheritance or after-the-fact repair.

5. **Prefer real counterweight over private-buffer substitution**
   - Prefer targets with stronger public/social wealth and lower forced private self-insurance.

6. **Prefer entrant-weighted paths**
   - When two target paths look similar on paper, choose the one that routes the next durable gains toward entrants, excluded groups, younger adults, and common wealth rather than incumbents.

7. **Prefer the administrable path**
   - When the moral ranking is close, prefer the target that can be approached with credible delivery, visibility, and anti-blackmail sequencing rather than with rhetorical instruments that lack governing rails.

## Bottom line

The archive chooses wealth targets by asking which distribution can plausibly sustain all of the following at once:

- a **supermajority-clearing real floor**,
- a **bounded top tail** below wealth-to-rule levels,
- **early and person-real footholds** rather than delayed or fragile paper claims,
- **open circulation** across cohorts, groups, and places,
- **real public/social counterweight** to private concentration,
- and **low reconcentration pressure** over ordinary cycles.

That is why the archive keeps a numerical center but refuses to treat target choice as numerology.
The point is not to hit an elegant ratio.
The point is to choose the wealth order most consistent with democratic governability, open life chances, and non-dominating security.[S02][S16][S18][S19][S20]
