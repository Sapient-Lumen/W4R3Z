# Ideal wealth distribution bands

## What this note is for

This note is the archive's **number-side companion** to [`target-selection-rules.md`](target-selection-rules.md).
It answers a narrower question than the full target-selection procedure:
**how exact are the archive's preferred wealth-share numbers, what counts as close enough, and what should make a seemingly good share pattern fail anyway?**

The archive keeps numerical bands because wealth concentration is not morally readable without some quantitative anchor.
But it does **not** treat the ideal distribution problem as numerology.
A country should not pass just because one share vector looks tidy while the bottom half still lacks a real floor, the top tail still governs, footholds still arrive too late, or broadening is still being washed out by rents, opacity, or rapid reconcentration.[S02][S03][S16][S18][S19][S20][S23][S26][S31]

Use this note together with:

- [`target-selection-rules.md`](target-selection-rules.md)

## Minimum reading rule

Read the bands as a **disciplining range**, not as a self-sufficient moral test.
A country that sits near the archive's preferred shares should still fail when one of the surrounding constitutional surfaces is badly broken.
A country somewhat outside the central point can still count as near-acceptable only when the floor, control, timing, public-counterweight, and durability tests are strong enough that the deviation is genuinely narrow rather than a flattering substitute.[S02][S16][S18][S19]

## The working bands

The archive's current best judgment remains:

- **Bottom 50%:** target **20%** of private household wealth, with a rough acceptable band of **15-25%**
- **Middle 40%:** target **50%**, with a rough acceptable band of **45-55%**
- **Next 9%:** target **20%**, with a rough acceptable band of **15-25%**
- **Top 1%:** target **10%**, with a rough acceptable band of **8-15%**
- **Top 0.1%:** target **3% or less** inside the top-1 slice
- **Public wealth:** target **clearly positive** net public wealth and a growing public/social wealth pool rather than the weak or negative public balance sheets now common in rich regions.[S02]

The archive's default attractor remains **20 / 50 / 20 / 10**.
It treats that as the center of a **narrow acceptable envelope**, not a sacred single point.
The point of the envelope is to permit modest institutional variety without giving away the main moral structure: a broad bottom, a thick middle, a bounded top, and a real public/social counterweight.

## What the bands are trying to secure

### 1. Bottom-half assetlessness should stop being normal

The bottom half should hold enough wealth that zero-or-negative-wealth status is no longer the ordinary condition of mass adulthood.[S16]
That does **not** mean everyone holds the same portfolio.
It means that broad ownership and resilient footholds cease to be exceptional.

### 2. The middle should be thick enough to be socially real

The middle 40% should hold enough of total wealth that society is not effectively split between a narrow owner class and a large renter-debtor class.
The archive therefore treats a large middle share as a structural condition of plural ownership, not as decorative egalitarian optics.

### 3. The top tail should stay below wealth-to-rule levels

The top 1%, and especially the top 0.1%, can remain rich.
The archive's claim is narrower: they should not remain rich enough to function as a governing estate, a rescue-favored estate, or a near-permanent command layer across firms, media, land, finance, and politics.[S02][S03][S20][S26]

### 4. Public/social counterweight should be materially real

Positive public or social wealth matters because it reduces how much ordinary households must self-insure privately and because it prevents private concentration from becoming the only serious buffer, allocator, or rescuer in the system.[S02][S21][S22]

### 5. The target should be demanding without becoming fictional

The archive wants a distribution far more equal than current rich-country patterns, but it does **not** want a fake exactness that ignores institutional variety, productive pluralism, or the fact that societies can approach the same moral shape through somewhat different portfolios and timing.[S02][S03][S13]

## The main false passes

### 1. The shares look softer, but the floor is still too weak to be real

A country should not get near-acceptable treatment when bottom-half households still lack resilient net wealth, liquid security, secure housing, manageable debt, usable claims, or person-level adequacy.[S16][S17][S25]
See [`minimum-asset-floor.md`](minimum-asset-floor.md).

### 2. The shares look softer, but control remains oligarchic

A country should not pass when key assets, stewardship rights, or money-into-rule channels remain tightly bottlenecked even though household shares soften somewhat.[S20][S23][S26]
See [`asset-composition-and-control.md`](asset-composition-and-control.md), [`intermediated-ownership-and-stewardship.md`](intermediated-ownership-and-stewardship.md), and [`wealth-to-rule-conversion.md`](wealth-to-rule-conversion.md).

### 3. The shares look softer, but circulation is still closed

A country should not pass when adult asset position is still too inherited, first footholds still arrive too late, excluded groups remain shut out, or high-opportunity places remain territorially closed.[S05][S18][S19][S34]
See [`intergenerational-circulation.md`](intergenerational-circulation.md), [`life-course-timing-and-early-footholds.md`](life-course-timing-and-early-footholds.md), and [`spatial-concentration-and-place-based-closure.md`](spatial-concentration-and-place-based-closure.md).

### 4. The shares look softer, but the public counterweight is too weak

A country should not pass when households still need oversized private war chests just to survive sickness, unemployment, care burdens, housing stress, or old age because public/social protection remains too thin.[S21][S22]
See [`social-floor-and-private-wealth.md`](social-floor-and-private-wealth.md) and [`forced-self-insurance-and-private-buffer-coercion.md`](forced-self-insurance-and-private-buffer-coercion.md).

### 5. The shares look softer, but the route is flattering rather than durable

A country should not pass when better-looking shares are being propped up by opacity, incumbent revaluation, hidden subsidies, repeated public downside absorption, or rapid reconcentration pressure.[S07][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33][S36]
See [`burden-of-proof-under-opacity-and-uncertainty.md`](burden-of-proof-under-opacity-and-uncertainty.md), [`upside-down-subsidies-and-the-hidden-wealth-state.md`](upside-down-subsidies-and-the-hidden-wealth-state.md), and [`reconcentration-pressure.md`](reconcentration-pressure.md).

## Warning ranges and red lines

The archive keeps a few compact numerical warnings near the share axis:

- **Bottom-50 share below 10%** — strong warning that mass assetlessness is still structurally normal.[S02]
- **Top-1 share above 20%** — strong warning that the top tail is still too constitutionally large.[S02][S03]
- **Top-0.1 share above 5%** — strong warning that ultra-concentrated dominance remains live.
- **Persistent negative net public wealth** — warning that private concentration is facing too little public/social counterweight.[S02]
- **Large groups of low-income households spending over 40% of disposable income on housing** — warning that broadening is still being undermined by housing squeeze and forced private buffering.[S07]

These are **warning thresholds**, not the whole decision rule.
A country can fail without crossing every warning, and it should not pass just because it narrowly misses one threshold while deeper vetoes remain.

## How to use this note

When the archive needs a quick answer on the number side, do four things:

1. **Start from the default attractor**
   - Use **20 / 50 / 20 / 10** unless there is a strong reason to compare nearby envelopes.
2. **Ask whether the deviation is actually narrow**
   - Treat movement inside the rough acceptable bands as potentially acceptable only when it does not rely on hidden substitution or a broken companion surface.
3. **Screen for false passes**
   - Use the floor, control, circulation, counterweight, and durability checks before giving moral credit for nicer-looking shares.
4. **Escalate to the fuller target-selection rule when needed**
   - When the case is close, contested, or flattering, stop here and use [`target-selection-rules.md`](target-selection-rules.md) plus [`acceptable-envelopes-and-substitution-limits.md`](acceptable-envelopes-and-substitution-limits.md).

## Bottom line

The archive keeps a numerical center because **shares still matter**.
A morally acceptable wealth order needs a broad bottom, a thick middle, a bounded top tail, and real public/social counterweight.

But the archive refuses to let the numbers do all the moral work.
The question is never just whether a distribution looks close to an elegant ratio.
The question is whether the wealth order is actually moving away from oligarchic concentration and toward non-dominating security, open circulation, and democratic governability.[S02][S16][S18][S19][S20]
