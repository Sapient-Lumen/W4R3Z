# Immoral-Wealth

A compact research archive for thinking about **immoral wealth inequality**: what it is, what an acceptable wealth distribution would look like, and what policy mix can move a society toward it without collapsing productive capacity or democratic legitimacy.

## Core judgment in one paragraph

This archive treats wealth inequality as **immoral** when it hardens into a durable order of **domination and closure**: when the bottom half lacks a real asset floor; when the top tail can convert wealth into political rule; when inheritance and parental position decide too much of adult life; when key assets remain too concentrated to govern democratically; when public/social wealth is too weak to offset private concentration; and when apparent broadening is washed out by debt fragility, quietly self-eroding carrying costs, forced self-insurance, punitive spend-down traps, opacity, gatekeeper-dependent claims, weakly defensible claims, expert-dependent operability mazes, subsidy capitalisation, territorial closure, late footholds, and rapid reconcentration.[S07][S13][S16][S17][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S34][S36][S40][S44][S45]

## Current working target

The archive's central attractor remains **20 / 50 / 20 / 10** for the bottom 50 / middle 40 / next 9 / top 1. It treats that as a **narrow acceptable family** rather than a single sacred point: roughly **15 / 55 / 20 / 10**, **20 / 50 / 20 / 10**, and **25 / 45 / 20 / 10** can all count as near-ideal only when the archive's floor, circulation, control, public-counterweight, timing, and anti-reconcentration rules also pass.
