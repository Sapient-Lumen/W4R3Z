# Municipal infrastructure backlogs and service wealth

Infrastructure is stored public wealth. Roads, bridges, schools, water systems, sewer systems, stormwater, energy distribution, broadband, parks, libraries, and public buildings reduce the private costs households must bear to live, work, care, and recover from shocks.[S246][S247][S248][S249]

When local infrastructure is under-maintained, the costs do not vanish. They are shifted into car repairs, bottled water, boil notices, sewer backups, higher insurance, utility rates, missed work, poor health, longer commutes, and tax or fee increases. Deferred maintenance is therefore a wealth-order issue, not only an engineering issue.[S248][S249][S258]

## Core rule

A case cannot count public/common wealth at face value if the local infrastructure layer that makes ordinary life livable is underfunded, deteriorating, or financed through regressive user fees that undermine the lower-half floor.

## Stress points

- small systems with weak administrative capacity;
- aging water, wastewater, stormwater, and road assets;
- grant programs requiring applications, matches, engineering capacity, and reimbursement float;
- debt aversion or weak credit access that delays maintenance;
- special assessments and user fees that convert capital needs into household debt;
- climate-risk exposure that raises borrowing and maintenance costs;
- one-time federal aid that does not create durable maintenance revenue.[S245][S246][S248][S249][S257][S258]

## Verdict consequence

Infrastructure backlogs harden a verdict when they make ordinary households self-insure against public-system failure. They can also turn otherwise affordable places into self-eroding footholds. The right evidence is not only total capital need, but asset condition, rate burden, service interruptions, repair timing, fiscal capacity, grant absorption, and household incidence.
