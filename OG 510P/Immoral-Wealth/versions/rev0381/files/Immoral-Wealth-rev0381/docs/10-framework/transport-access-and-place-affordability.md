# Transport access and place affordability

Cheap housing is not affordable when it requires expensive, unreliable, or unavailable transportation. Transportation access determines whether households can reach work, school, care, food, health care, courts, public offices, and social networks without turning time, fuel, vehicle debt, repairs, insurance, tickets, and missed trips into wealth drains.[S254][S255][S259]

The archive therefore treats transportation as place-based public wealth. Transit, walkability, safe roads, paratransit, regional job access, and vehicle alternatives can reduce private buffer coercion. Car-dependent sprawl, poor transit, unsafe streets, and long commutes can make otherwise low-cost places self-eroding.[S254][S259]

## Core rule

A housing or place case cannot pass from rent/mortgage cost alone. It must score combined housing-plus-transportation burden, transportation insecurity, job/care access, disability access, and the degree to which households must buy private vehicles to compensate for weak public infrastructure.[S254][S255]

## False passes

- **cheap-rent pass:** housing costs look low but transportation pushes combined burden above adequacy.
- **car-ownership pass:** vehicles are counted as assets without subtracting debt, maintenance, insurance, fuel, and enforcement exposure.
- **transit-map pass:** a route exists, but frequency, reliability, safety, disability access, and job timing fail.
- **regional-growth pass:** jobs are created, but low-wealth households cannot reach them without costly relocation or vehicle dependence.

## Policy consequence

When transportation is the fastest washout, route to service frequency, fare affordability, paratransit, job-access planning, safe streets, car-repair/insurance stabilization, anti-fine-and-fee enforcement, housing near transit, and combined H+T affordability tests.[S254][S255][S259]
