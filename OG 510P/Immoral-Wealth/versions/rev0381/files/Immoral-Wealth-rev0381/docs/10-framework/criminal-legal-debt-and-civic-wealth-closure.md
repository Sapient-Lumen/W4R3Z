# Criminal-legal debt and civic wealth closure

## Core claim

Criminal-legal debt is not ordinary consumer debt. It is state-backed extraction that can carry civic, mobility, custody, employment, housing, record-clearing, probation, and incarceration consequences. When it falls disproportionately on subordinated groups, it is an anti-averaging veto surface.

Urban Institute work frames criminal-legal fines and fees as routine state/local revenue devices and documents that many people charged fines or fees carry unpaid balances.[S110] Its 2026 Black economic mobility brief reports that nearly one in five working-age adults had a household fine/fee charge in 2024 and that Black adults were charged at higher rates than White adults.[S111]

## Civic-wealth channel

The failure is not only the dollars owed. The moral weight comes from attached consequences:

- driver's license suspension or transport loss;
- probation/parole extension or supervision costs;
- court warrants or arrest risk;
- inability to clear records;
- wage garnishment or benefit offset;
- fees for public defenders, monitoring, incarceration, calls, commissary, or medical care;
- family extraction through bail, support, calls, visits, and replacement income;
- employer, landlord, and lender screening.

## Certification rule

A wealth order cannot pass the group/person gate if legal-system contact creates durable wealth penalties for a subordinated group and the case lacks ability-to-pay protection, debt forgiveness, license/record decoupling, and anti-revenue-dependence rules.

A case cannot pass the floor gate when public institutions finance themselves by imposing debts on the very people whose floor they are supposed to protect.

## Evidence order

1. total outstanding criminal-legal debt and known unknowns;
2. shares charged and shares unpaid, by race/caste/ethnicity/gender/age/disability/region;
3. legal consequences for nonpayment;
4. whether ability to pay is assessed before imposition and before sanction;
5. whether debts block record clearing, voting, driving, custody, housing, or employment;
6. share of court/local budgets financed by fines and fees;
7. forgiveness, sunset, and appeal mechanisms.

## Program routing

The opening package is usually not a cash transfer alone. It is:

1. abolish administrative fees and pay-to-stay charges;
2. ability-to-pay assessment for fines;
3. automatic waiver for poverty/disability/care hardship;
4. decouple debt from license, voting, record clearing, probation, and jail;
5. forgive legacy debt;
6. replace local/court revenue with general funding;
7. publish subgroup and budget dependence data.
