# Media, expertise, and agenda capture

Wealth becomes rule not only by buying votes or lobby meetings. It also becomes rule by shaping what appears urgent, respectable, impossible, dangerous, or technically unavoidable. Media ownership, public advertising, sponsored expertise, think tanks, platform reach, and litigation messaging can all decide whether a wealth-order reform reaches the political agenda with a fair hearing.[S188][S189][S190][S191]

## Agenda-power test

Ask whether ordinary people can identify:

1. who owns or controls major media outlets relevant to the case;
2. who funds policy research, advocacy, and expert testimony;
3. who buys major issue advertising and digital persuasion;
4. whether state advertising or public subsidies create editorial dependence;
5. whether concentrated owners also hold stakes in land, finance, procurement, regulated sectors, or political finance;
6. whether rival voices have enough institutional survival capacity to contest the narrative.

Media capture is serious for the archive because it lets wealth protect itself upstream. A tax rule can be blocked before draft text exists; a housing reform can be reframed as neighborhood destruction; a registry can be called privacy protection for ordinary owners while mainly shielding controllers.

## Read media pluralism as a wealth-order condition

The 2025 Media Pluralism Monitor reads pluralism through fundamental protection, market plurality, political independence, and social inclusiveness. The archive imports that structure as a stress surface: ownership concentration and opaque sponsorship matter because they decide whether floors, footholds, taxes, public assets, and remedies get a fair public contest.[S190][S191]

## False passes

- **Many outlets, one controller class.** Formal outlet count does not solve agenda concentration if common owners, funders, advertisers, or political patrons dominate.
- **Disclosure without cross-linkage.** Ownership files count less if they cannot be linked to procurement, lobbying, party finance, regulated-sector interests, land ownership, or foundation/DAF sponsorship.
- **Expertise without conflicts.** Technical evidence counts less when funding, clients, or beneficial owners are hidden.
- **Public media without independence.** Public service media can be a counterweight only if its governance, funding, and editorial independence are shielded from government and donor capture.

## Program implication

A case with media/agenda warning should add a rail package before certification:

- media ownership transparency;
- concentration and cross-ownership review;
- transparent and fair state-advertising allocation;
- funding disclosure for policy research and third-party campaigns;
- whistleblower, journalist, and anti-SLAPP protections where relevant;
- linked registers for media owners, beneficial owners, procurement, lobbying, and political donations;
- pluralistic public-interest funding that does not become patronage.
