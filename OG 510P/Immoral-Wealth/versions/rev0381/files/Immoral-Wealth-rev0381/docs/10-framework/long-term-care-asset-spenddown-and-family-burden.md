# Long-term care, asset spenddown, and family burden

Long-term care can turn lifetime savings into a temporary care-payment reserve. A wealth order cannot claim an adequate old-age floor merely because households have housing or pension assets if severe care needs require rapid asset spenddown, unpaid family labor, or poverty-level eligibility to obtain support.[S277][S278]

## Spenddown test

Score long-term-care systems by out-of-pocket costs, eligibility thresholds, asset tests, home-equity treatment, waiting lists, quality, availability of home/community care, informal-carer burden, and whether support arrives before assets are exhausted. A program that protects people only after they become poor is a late rescue, not a wealth-preserving floor.[S277][S278]

## Family-burden test

Informal care can be valuable and chosen. It becomes a wealth-order breach when family members lose earnings, pensions, health, migration options, or education because the public system offloads care without pay, respite, or portability. The case should score both the person needing care and the family members whose wealth formation is interrupted.[S278]

## Routing

Route severe long-term-care spenddown to Gate 8 life-interruption, Gate 16 household-market extraction, and Gate 7 public/common counterweight. Where care markets are dominated by private-equity ownership, real-estate extraction, or opaque quality, also route through enforcement and market-structure screens.
