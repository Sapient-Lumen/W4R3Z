# Legal identity, documentation, and account rails

A person cannot use wealth claims that the state, banks, courts, landlords, insurers, employers, schools, or platforms cannot recognize. Legal identity and account rails are therefore not administrative trivia. They are part of the wealth order.

World Bank ID4D reports that roughly 800 million people lack official ID and that many more lack digitally verifiable identity for online transactions.[S124] UNICEF’s birth-registration update records a large population of children whose births are not registered or who lack proof of registration.[S125] The World Bank Findex and G2Px work show that account ownership and digital public payments can expand access, including for women and people receiving public transfers, but design determines whether inclusion becomes control or just a new gate.[S120][S126]

## The identity-to-claim chain

For each case, score whether ordinary claimants have:

1. civil registration or other recognized proof of personhood;
2. a current legal name and matching records;
3. address or residence proof that does not punish homelessness, migration, informal tenure, or displacement;
4. disability, care, household, employment, income, or student-status proof where relevant;
5. safe account, wallet, card, cash-out, or other payment rail;
6. ability to authenticate without a smartphone, biometric failure, English-only portal, permanent address, or paid intermediary;
7. appeal rights when records mismatch.

A household-level record is not enough when the moral question is person-level control. A woman, disabled adult, young person, migrant worker, tenant, informal worker, or elder may be counted inside a household while lacking the document or rail needed to control a claim.

## Account rails are wealth rails

The archive should treat banking, postal banking, mobile money, public cards, credit unions, low-cost payment accounts, and cash-out networks as part of the floor architecture. In the United States, the FDIC survey shows that unbanked and underbanked households still exist even after long-run improvements.[S263] In Kenya, mobile-money rails are a central financial-access channel and must be scored as infrastructure, not merely fintech.[S135][S136]

But an account is not automatically a pass. It must be:

- low cost;
- usable for public payments;
- protected from predatory fees and unauthorized deductions;
- private enough for person-level control;
- available through assisted/offline channels;
- interoperable enough to avoid platform lock-in;
- accompanied by dispute resolution and cash-out access;
- safe for low-literacy and low-connectivity users.

## Documentation as closure

Documentation requirements become immoral when they systematically convert public claims into expert-only claims. Common closure points include:

- birth certificate missing or inaccessible;
- name mismatch across ID, tax, school, bank, immigration, marriage, or property records;
- unstable address or informal housing;
- inherited property without clear title;
- disability/care proof that requires time, medical visits, or expert paperwork;
- app-based identity checks that fail for old phones, poor connectivity, language barriers, biometric mismatch, or shared devices;
- household forms that expose people to abusers or gatekeepers.

## Pass rule

A case can pass the legal-identity/account rail screen only when the people most likely to need the floor and first foothold can prove themselves, receive payments, contest errors, and control claims without humiliating, costly, or discretionary processes.

A case fails or receives veto pressure when identity, documentation, or account access systematically excludes subordinated groups from assets, public transfers, disaster aid, housing, work, credit, or legal title.
