# Utility affordability, disconnection, and essential-service claims

Energy and water access are not ordinary consumer choices. They are preconditions for health, food storage, work, school, communication, caregiving, medical equipment, heat safety, cooling safety, sanitation, and housing stability.[S250][S251][S252][S253]

A household can own assets and still lack a real floor if temporary arrears can trigger disconnection, reconnection fees, collections, credit damage, eviction pressure, or unsafe living conditions. Utility arrears are a liability-stack surface and a conversion-rail surface: public help does not count until it prevents service loss or restores service without predatory leakage.[S251][S252][S253]

## Core rule

A case cannot pass floor reality when essential utilities are unaffordable for lower-income households and shutoff protections are too weak, opaque, seasonal, or slow to preserve health and work continuity.

## What to score

- energy and water burden by income, place, race, age, disability, and medical vulnerability;
- arrears, notices, completed disconnections, reconnection timing, and repeat shutoffs;
- protections during extreme heat, cold, medical vulnerability, infancy, disability, and disasters;
- affordability rates, percentage-of-income plans, arrears management, and debt forgiveness;
- public assistance take-up, timeliness, and direct payment conversion;
- utility capital needs that are passed through into rates;
- reporting mandates by utility and household subgroup.[S250][S251][S252][S253]

## Policy consequence

Utility policy belongs in the wealth package when utility costs and disconnections become the fastest washout from floor to debt. Corrective packages include arrears cancellation, percentage-of-income payment plans, shutoff moratoria, medical-baseline protections, direct public payments, water affordability programs, transparent disconnection data, weatherization, and infrastructure grants that reduce rate pressure.
