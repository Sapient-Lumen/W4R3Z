# Enforceability, remedies, and standing

A wealth reform is not real merely because it is enacted. It is real when affected people and institutions can **invoke it, prove it, receive it, keep it, and repair breaches** without expert-only access, patronage, or years of litigation. rev0309 therefore adds enforceability as a moral condition rather than a technical afterthought.[S176][S177]

## Rule

A case cannot pass the archive's constitutional screen when its anti-closure package depends on rights with no usable standing, claims with no affordable remedy, registries with no verification, taxes with no audit capacity, benefits with no appeal path, or public assets with no protection against capture.

This doctrine extends the conversion layer. Conversion asked whether nominal claims become usable wealth. Enforceability asks whether the claimant can defend that conversion when an agency, employer, landlord, family gatekeeper, intermediary, controller, or wealthy defendant resists.

## Standing test

A person, group, public trustee, agency, or civil-society actor must have a clear path to contest:

- denial of a benefit, account, title, or public-dividend claim;
- illegal seizure, offset, clawback, or fee leakage;
- exclusion from housing, credit, procurement, inheritance, pension, or ownership records;
- beneficial-ownership concealment that blocks tax, procurement, AML, asset-recovery, or group-closure analysis;
- capture of public/common wealth by insiders.

Where no actor has standing, the case receives an `enforceability_void` warning even if the policy looks well designed.

## Remedy test

A remedy counts only when it is timely, affordable, comprehensible, enforceable against the resisting party, and not destructive of the claimant's ordinary life. A six-year court process may be a formal remedy but not a wealth-order remedy for a household facing eviction, benefit loss, credit damage, or inheritance-title blockage.

Minimum remedy indicators:

1. notice in plain language;
2. evidence access;
3. appeal or review before irreversible loss;
4. independent decision-maker;
5. interim protection against dispossession or seizure;
6. fee-shifting, legal aid, ombud, representative action, or public enforcement where individual claims are too small;
7. actual collection or implementation after victory.

## Enforcement mirror

The same rule applies upward. A state cannot claim to compress top-tail rule if high-wealth nonfilers, opaque partnerships, offshore accounts, trusts, shell companies, or complex private-business valuations cannot be audited, challenged, valued, and collected. The IRS tax-gap and high-wealth enforcement sources in rev0309 make this concrete: recognized liabilities and third-party information are not enough unless the agency has stable capacity to act.[S162][S163][S164]
