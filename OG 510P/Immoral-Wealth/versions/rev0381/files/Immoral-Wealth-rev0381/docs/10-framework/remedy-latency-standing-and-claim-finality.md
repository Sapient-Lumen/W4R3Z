# Remedy latency, standing, and claim finality

A legal right is not a wealth asset unless it can be converted into timely control.

The cube should therefore treat remedies as part of the wealth distribution itself. A remedy that arrives after eviction, account closure, medical interruption, wage loss, credit damage, job loss, family break, or forum abandonment may still matter legally, but it no longer functions as a stabilizing asset for the person who needed it.

## Core distinction

- **Formal entitlement:** the person can point to a statute, contract, benefit, appeal, claim, or defense.
- **Operational remedy:** the person can invoke it, survive the waiting period, prove the facts without expert-only burden, act collectively where individual claims are uneconomic, and restore collateral harms.

The first is a paper right. The second is a wealth-protective asset.

## Public/private mirror

The same test applies to public and private systems:

- public benefits with slow appeals;
- fraud-control systems with false positives;
- credit, tenant, insurance, or employment files with bad data;
- arbitration clauses and class waivers;
- wage-theft or consumer harms where individual claims are too small;
- public backstops where ordinary claimants must sue or appeal while intermediaries receive automatic liquidity.

The cube should score the remedy's survival window, not merely its doctrinal existence.
