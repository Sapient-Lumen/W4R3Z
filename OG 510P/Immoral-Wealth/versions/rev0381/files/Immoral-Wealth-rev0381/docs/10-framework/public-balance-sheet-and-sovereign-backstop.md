# Public balance sheet and sovereign backstop

A wealth order cannot be certified by private wealth shares alone when the state also creates hidden assets and liabilities through debt, guarantees, insurance funds, central-bank balance sheets, public credit, social-insurance promises, and crisis rescues.

Gate 20 asks whether the public balance sheet is a **rule-bound counterweight** or a **hidden transfer machine**. It is a counterweight when public assets, credit, guarantees, and insurance protect ordinary claimants and recover public upside. It is a hidden transfer machine when losses are socialized, gains remain private, and future public capacity is consumed by interest, underfunded promises, or opaque contingent liabilities.[S345][S346][S350][S368]

## Failure labels

- `backstop-privileged`: private asset holders receive public rescue priority without matching loss-bearing, fees, warrants, equity, or conditions.
- `claim-haircut`: statutory or expected claims are likely to be cut because promised public backing is not durably financed.
- `interest-crowded`: debt service narrows the public capacity needed for floors, infrastructure, care, housing, and crisis repair.
- `guarantee-hidden`: credit guarantees or insurance programs are treated as free until stress arrives.
- `public-upside-lost`: the state takes downside risk without retaining upside warrants, equity, fees, or repayment claims.
- `public-asset-raided`: sovereign or public assets are used as patronage, fiscal patching, or elite allocation rather than rule-bound claimant security.
- `quasi-fiscal-blind`: central-bank losses, deferred assets, or remittance suspensions are ignored because they do not look like ordinary spending.

## Certification rule

A case cannot receive a comfort verdict when public capacity is doing hidden work but the scoreboard lacks: public-debt/interest stress, social-insurance claim security, public-pension funding, contingent-liability disclosure, guarantee pricing, deposit-insurance prefunding, crisis-backstop conditions, central-bank quasi-fiscal visibility, and public-asset governance.

This rule does not condemn public debt, social insurance, guarantees, or crisis lending. It condemns the false pass where the public balance sheet quietly protects owners, creditors, and incumbents while future households absorb reduced public capacity or claim haircuts.
