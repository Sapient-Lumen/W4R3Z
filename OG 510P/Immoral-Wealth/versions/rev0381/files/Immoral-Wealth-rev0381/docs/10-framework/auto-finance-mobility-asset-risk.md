# Auto finance, repossession, and mobility-asset risk

In car-dependent places, a vehicle is often not discretionary consumption. It is a work, school, care, health, and safety rail. Auto finance can therefore create a special threshold failure: the household loses both an asset and the mobility needed to earn income and repair the loss.[S254][S255][S266]

## Non-pass rule

Do not count vehicle ownership as a foothold without scoring loan-to-value, negative equity, repair affordability, insurance burden, repossession risk, deficiency-balance risk, and transport substitutes. A financed car that is underwater and necessary for work can be a coercive private infrastructure charge, not a secure asset.[S266][S267]

## Repossession as threshold moment

Repossession is not merely collection. It can trigger job loss, missed medical care, school disruption, credit damage, deficiency debt, and repeat high-cost borrowing. If public transit and local job access are weak, repossession should route through both Gate 15 and Gate 16.[S255][S266]

## Evidence pack

Use origination terms, APR/fees, loan-to-value, negative-equity rollover rates, repossession notices and completions, deficiency balances, force-placed insurance, complaint data, repair-cost burdens, transit accessibility, and subgroup incidence. A case cannot comfort-certify when vehicle finance is a hidden requirement for reaching ordinary public goods and jobs.[S266][S267]
