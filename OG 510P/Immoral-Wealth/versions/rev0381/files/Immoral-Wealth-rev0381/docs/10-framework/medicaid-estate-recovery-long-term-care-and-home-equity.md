# Medicaid estate recovery, long-term care, and home-equity preservation

Long-term care is a late-life wealth-order stress test. A household can spend decades building modest home equity and still lose intergenerational transfer capacity when care need forces spenddown or estate recovery.

## Core distinction

Medicaid estate recovery is not simply a collection rule. It is a rule deciding whether the public care floor is treated as a social claim or as a recoverable lien against modest family wealth.[S332][S334]

## Scorecard questions

1. Which services trigger recovery: nursing facility only, HCBS, related hospital/prescription services, or all Medicaid services?[S332]
2. Are hardship waivers clear, automatic, and usable by low-income heirs?[S333]
3. Is recovery limited to probate estates or expanded estate definitions?
4. Are surviving spouses, disabled children, minor children, caregiver children, siblings with equity interest, and low-income heirs protected?
5. Is notice understandable before care election, not only after death?
6. Does recovery interact with racialized home-equity gaps and heirs-property title problems?[S334][S335]

## False passes

- **Care-access pass / estate-loss fail:** care is available only after households surrender the one asset that could become intergenerational foothold.
- **Waiver pass / claimability fail:** hardship waivers exist but require legal skill, deadlines, or documentation heirs lack.
- **HCBS pass / recovery fail:** home-based care keeps people at home but still produces estate claims.

## Certification effect

Gate 19 hardens Gate 7 and Gate 16. Public care provision is a counterweight only if its financing does not silently convert modest home equity into posthumous public recovery while high-end assets escape through planning.
