# Fissured workplaces, platforms, and employer responsibility

A workplace is fissured when the entity with practical economic control is not the entity that formally signs the paycheck. Franchising, subcontracting, staffing, temp agencies, app platforms, warehouse contractors, janitorial vendors, home-care agencies, construction chains, and logistics networks can all produce this split.[S222][S227][S228][S229]

The moral question is whether responsibility follows control.

## Control signals

Score the lead firm, platform, franchisor, buyer, or contractor when it materially controls:

- prices, commissions, fees, piece rates, or dispatch;
- scheduling, routing, account suspension, ratings, or performance metrics;
- hiring standards, training, uniforms, equipment, customer data, or brand requirements;
- subcontractor margins so tightly that wage compliance becomes unrealistic;
- retaliation, deactivation, arbitration, or dispute pathways;
- access to benefits, safety, leave, injury claims, or wage records.[S223][S227][S228][S229]

## False pass

A case gets a false pass when workers have nominal claims against a thin intermediary while the durable surplus and rule-setting power sit upstream.

## Certification consequence

Fissuring routes to Gate 11 and Gate 14 together: the archive needs both legal remedy and productive-power scoring. Without joint responsibility, misclassification enforcement, platform transparency, and wage-theft remedies, public transfers may merely subsidize lead-firm business models.
