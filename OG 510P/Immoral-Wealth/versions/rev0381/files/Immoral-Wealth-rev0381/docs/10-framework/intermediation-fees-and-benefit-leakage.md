# Intermediation fees and benefit leakage

Small claims are often destroyed after they arrive. A $500 refund, public dividend, emergency grant, wage payment, rent support, child account, or insurance payment can be morally overstated if claimants must surrender part of it to banks, payday lenders, check cashers, tax preparers, transfer agents, landlords, creditors, court systems, or account penalties.

This note adds a conversion-leakage screen. The question is not only whether the public or private claim was paid. It is how much survived as **claimant-controlled useful value**.

## Leakage channels

Score at least these channels where relevant:

- overdraft and NSF fees;
- check-cashing and money-order fees;
- prepaid-card, transfer, remittance, cash-out, ATM, or wallet fees;
- paid tax-preparation and refund-advance costs;
- debt collection and garnishment;
- benefit offset or recoupment;
- court fines, fees, surcharges, and license-restoration payments;
- rent arrears, late fees, application fees, broker fees, and security-deposit churn;
- account minimums, dormancy fees, and inactivity penalties;
- platform fees for gig or informal workers;
- scams and high-pressure intermediaries around unclaimed funds, disaster claims, or child accounts.

CFPB data show why this matters: even after substantial overdraft/NSF reductions, reported consumer payments remained in the billions in 2023.[S262] FDIC’s unbanked/underbanked survey also makes clear that access to mainstream banking and reliance on nonbank channels remain distributional questions.[S263]

## Conversion value

For each claim, record:

```text
nominal claim
minus application/intermediary costs
minus account/payment fees
minus required travel/time/childcare costs
minus debt offsets/garnishment
minus delay penalties incurred while waiting
minus clawback risk reserve
= usable claimant value
```

The archive should prefer this “usable claimant value” over nominal program size when judging floors, public/common wealth, starter assets, and emergency repair.

## Fee leakage as washout

Fee leakage is a washout channel, not merely an efficiency issue. It is morally important when the leakage is:

- concentrated among low-balance households;
- triggered by volatility or late public payments;
- attached to protected minima;
- produced by monopoly, platform, banking, tax-prep, court, or landlord power;
- unavoidable because safe public/free channels are absent;
- hidden inside account terms or bundled products.

A system that gives with one hand and extracts with another may still be better than no transfer. But it cannot count the gross transfer as a real floor.

## Policy implications

The first package may be fee repair before new transfer design when leakage is large. No-regret moves include:

- free public filing and prefilled claims where legally possible;
- direct deposit plus safe card/cash-out options;
- low-cost basic accounts or public account rails;
- overdraft/NSF, junk-fee, and account-fee restrictions;
- protected benefit accounts insulated from seizure;
- clear disclosure of any third-party fee;
- bans or caps on refund-anticipation extraction;
- automatic account tracing and no-fee claims for matured public starter accounts.

A fee repair may look small relative to wealth shares. It can still be decisive at the threshold moment.
