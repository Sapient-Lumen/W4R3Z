# Social-insurance promises and claim security

Social insurance is a public wealth claim only when it is financed, legally durable, administratively claimable, and politically protected enough to serve as real security. A projected benefit that ordinary people plan around but that later receives a sudden haircut is not a full floor.

Score social insurance as a claim-security surface, not only as annual spending. Ask:

1. Is the promise legally defined and enforceable?
2. Is the trust fund, contribution base, or general-revenue backstop adequate under realistic demographic and productivity assumptions?
3. Are benefits progressive enough to support lower-wealth claimants?
4. Are claimants protected from sudden age, formula, COLA, or eligibility changes?
5. Do women, caregivers, disabled people, migrants, and interrupted workers receive adequate credits and portability?[S351][S352][S356]

When a program is large enough to substitute for private retirement wealth, Gate 20 hardens Gate 2 and Gate 7. The program can count as public counterweight only if its financing and claim rules are credible across ordinary lifetimes.
