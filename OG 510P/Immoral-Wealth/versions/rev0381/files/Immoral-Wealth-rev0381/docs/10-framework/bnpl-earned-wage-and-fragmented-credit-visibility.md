# BNPL, earned-wage access, and fragmented credit visibility

BNPL and earned-wage-access products can be useful bridges. They also create a visibility problem: small obligations can multiply across providers, reporting systems, payroll rails, subscriptions, and apps. A case should not treat each product as harmless simply because the individual advance or installment looks small.[S264][S265][S272]

## Visibility test

The case must ask:

- Can the household see total obligations across providers before taking another advance or installment?
- Can supervisors see repeat use, stacking, late fees, charge-offs, disputes, and repayment failure?
- Are fees, tips, expedite charges, and payroll deductions presented as real cost?
- Are refund/dispute rights live when the underlying good or service fails?
- Does product design encourage attention to small payments while obscuring the total price and cumulative load?[S264][S265]

## Wage-claim test

Earned-wage access sits between wage timing and credit. The cube should score it by worker control, fee/tip pressure, employer partnership, payroll priority, recourse, disclosures, repeat use, and whether faster wage access substitutes for adequate pay frequency, emergency savings, or public liquidity rails.[S272]

## Routing

Fragmented-credit visibility belongs to Gate 10 and Gate 16 at the same time: measurement uncertainty when the market cannot be seen, and household-market extraction when the unseen structure drains savings or weakens bargaining power.
