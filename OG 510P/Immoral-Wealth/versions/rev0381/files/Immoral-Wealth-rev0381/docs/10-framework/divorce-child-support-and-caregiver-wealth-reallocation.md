# Divorce, child support, and caregiver wealth reallocation

Wealth orders are often scored through households, but threshold moments happen to persons. Divorce, separation, child support, unpaid care, and retirement-account division determine whether household assets become person-level security or a new round of dependency.

## Family-law wealth screens

- **Retirement division.** Retirement accounts and pensions may be marital property, but conversion often depends on QDROs, plan rules, tax treatment, administrative timing, and legal assistance.[S329]
- **Gendered old-age security.** Lower lifetime earnings, caregiving, and pension gaps can make divorce and widowhood decisive wealth shocks.[S328]
- **Child support pass-through.** Child support supports child and custodial-household security only when collections reach families rather than being retained to reimburse public assistance.[S330][S331]
- **Arrears and enforcement.** Enforcement can protect children, but unpayable arrears, license suspension, incarceration, credit damage, and government-owed debt can also reduce household wealth formation.[S330][S331]
- **Caregiver recognition.** Family law should test whether unpaid care is reflected in property division, support, pension rights, and later-life security.

## Anti-averaging rule

A household can look asset-rich while one adult has no independent account, title, pension claim, work history, or post-separation housing access. Gate 19 therefore links to person-level control, not household net worth alone.

## Certification effect

A case cannot pass person-level ownership if divorce, child support, or care allocation reliably converts one partner or caregiver into dependence, especially where legal help is needed to make nominal rights convert.
