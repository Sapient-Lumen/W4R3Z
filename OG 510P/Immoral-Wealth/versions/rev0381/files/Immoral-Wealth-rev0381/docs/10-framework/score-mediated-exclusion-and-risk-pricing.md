# Score-mediated exclusion and risk-priced wealth closure

rev0315 adds the score layer. A household can have income, a benefit claim, a job offer, a rental application, a bank account application, or an insurance quote and still fail the wealth-order test because a private or public scoring system converts old hardship into present exclusion.

The cube already scores household-market extraction. Score-mediated exclusion is different. The tollgate is not only a fee; it is a dossier, model, score, fraud flag, background screen, identity-proofing failure, or individualized price. It can block the threshold moment before a household ever reaches the price.[S283][S286][S288][S290]

## Core mechanism

A score-mediated system becomes a wealth-order breach when it does four things at once:

1. collects or infers past hardship, identity traces, public records, location, health-adjacent data, criminal/legal records, or consumer behavior;
2. converts the trace into an eligibility, price, limit, risk, fraud, or priority decision;
3. gives the claimant weak notice, thin reasons, or slow correction;
4. repeats across credit, rent, work, insurance, banking, utilities, benefits, and prices.

When this happens, poverty becomes sticky. A missed bill, eviction filing, medical collection, unpaid overdraft, fraud alert, mismatched identity record, or algorithmic suspicion can raise the cost of the next necessary step. The result is not simply exclusion; it is compounding disadvantage.

## False passes

- **score-cleaned pass:** the case reports average access but omits those screened out before application.
- **error-remedy pass:** dispute rights exist, but correction arrives after the apartment, job, insurance, account, or benefit is lost.
- **thin-adverse-action pass:** the applicant receives a denial notice but not a usable reason or route to fix the underlying model input.
- **specialty-report blind spot:** credit files are checked but banking, tenant, employment, insurance, utility, retail-return, or subprime specialty reports are not.
- **fraud-control pass:** fraud prevention is treated as inherently legitimate without scoring false positives, documentation burden, manual alternatives, and appeal timing.
- **personalized-price pass:** all households can buy the product, but data-driven price segmentation quietly charges more to the people least able to exit.

## Moral test

The moral question is not whether risk can ever be priced. The question is whether score systems make ordinary people pay repeatedly for low bargaining power, thin documentation, past hardship, group disadvantage, or opaque data errors while higher-wealth households can avoid, correct, insure against, or buy around those systems.

A case cannot pass when score systems are so pervasive that ordinary threshold moments require a clean dossier rather than adequate need, capacity, or legal entitlement.
