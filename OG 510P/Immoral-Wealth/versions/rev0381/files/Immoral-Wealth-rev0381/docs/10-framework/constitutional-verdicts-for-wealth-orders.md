# Constitutional verdicts for wealth orders

## What this note is for

The archive already says **what target to prefer**, **what warnings to watch**, and **how to read a live scoreboard**.
What it still needs in one place is the shortest stable answer to a different question:
**when should a real society count as morally failing, merely improved, provisionally near-passing, or genuinely close enough to the archive's target to count as constitutionally acceptable?**

This note is that **compact verdict rule**.
It is not a new theory branch.
It is a small junction note that turns the archive's target, floor, circulation, control, and durability doctrines into one pass/fail reading order.[S02][S03][S13][S142][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## Minimum verdict rule

A society should count as **near-ideal or constitutionally acceptable** only when all of the following are broadly true at once:

1. its wealth shares sit **inside the archive's narrow acceptable family** rather than merely outside the worst ranges,
2. the **bottom-half floor is broadly passed in real life** rather than flattered by thin averages or household masking,
3. **top-tail wealth is too weak to buy rule routinely** through politics, media, rescue privilege, opacity, or stewardship chokepoints,
4. **circulation is open enough** that parental wealth, late inheritance, place closure, and group exclusion no longer dominate entry into adult economic security,
5. **public/social counterweight is materially real** and reconcentration pressure is low enough that the settlement looks durable rather than lucky,
6. and the case is supported by **affirmative evidence**, not by opacity, missingness, or one unusually benign cycle.[S02][S13][S15][S142][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33]

This rule is deliberately stricter than **the shares look nicer now**.
The archive does not want to certify a society as morally acceptable merely because the distribution table softened while dependency, closure, and money-into-rule remained structurally strong.

## The four verdicts

### 1. Structurally immoral / failing order

Use this verdict when the society is still clearly outside the archive's constitutional floor.
Typical signs include:

- bottom-half near-assetlessness remains normal,
- the top 1% and especially the top 0.1% remain inside domination ranges,
- public/social counterweight stays too weak or negative,
- first footholds still arrive too late or too parent-dependent,
- wealth still buys routine governing leverage,
- or reconcentration pressure is strong enough that visible gains are likely to wash out quickly.[S02][S13][S142][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33]

This verdict should usually dominate even if one lane improved.
A better tax table, one new asset-building program, or one temporarily softer wealth series is not enough to change the constitutional reading.

### 2. Correction-required / outside the acceptable envelope

Use this verdict when the society has moved off the worst floor but still fails one or more decisive constitutional tests.
Common patterns are:

- the share vector is nearer the archive's center but the floor still is not broadly passable,
- housing, debt, fees, or monopoly tolls still wash gains upward,
- dynastic closure and late entry remain too strong,
- public/common wealth remains too thin,
- or control and opacity remain too concentrated for democratic governability.[S07][S13][S18][S19][S20][S23][S31][S34][S36][S39][S139][S50]

This is the archive's most common **improving but still failing** verdict.
It means the society is no longer read as sheer abandonment, but it still has not earned acceptable-order status.

### 3. Provisional near-pass / inside the numbers but not yet certified

Use this verdict when the society appears numerically close to the archive's preferred family yet still has unresolved veto surfaces or insufficient proof.
Typical reasons are:

- the lower half clears the floor only thinly or only for older cohorts,
- person-level control, excluded-group access, or place openness still lags,
- top-tail control or beneficial-ownership opacity remains too high,
- the good reading has not yet survived a durability window,
- or the public side is still too weak to keep the order from sliding backward.[S13][S15][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33][S47][S48]

This verdict is important because it blocks a common mistake:
calling a society acceptable because it is **near the numbers** while the constitutional conditions for keeping those numbers cleanly and durably are still incomplete.

### 4. Near-ideal / constitutionally acceptable order

Use this verdict only when the society is both **near the archive's preferred distribution** and **clean on the main constitutional surfaces**.
In compact form, that means:

- the share vector is inside the narrow acceptable family centered on **20 / 50 / 20 / 10**,
- the bottom half broadly passes a real floor with resilient, person-usable footholds,
- top-tail concentration is below the archive's warning ceilings and no longer reliably converts into rule,
- circulation is materially more open across class background, cohorts, groups, and place,
- public/social wealth or other collective counterweight is clearly positive and usable,
- and the settlement looks durable through ordinary political and economic weather rather than dependent on a single favorable moment.[S02][S03][S13][S142][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33]

The archive still treats this verdict as **near-ideal**, not utopian perfection.
It marks a society that has become morally defensible by the archive's lights, not one that has escaped all remaining inequality or political conflict.

## The six screens behind the verdict

### 1. Share screen

Ask first whether the society is even inside the archive's narrow acceptable family.
Start from **20 / 50 / 20 / 10** for the bottom 50 / middle 40 / next 9 / top 1, use the working bands in [`ideal-wealth-distribution-bands.md`](ideal-wealth-distribution-bands.md), and keep the working target of **3% or less** for the top **0.1%** together with clearly positive public/social wealth.[S02][S03][S142]

### 2. Floor screen

Ask whether bottom-half households actually have a **real floor**: positive and resilient net worth, manageable debt, secure housing, some person-usable long-run claims, and enough claimability and protection that the foothold survives ordinary life.[S142][S17][S21][S22][S25][S39][S139][S45][S50]

### 3. Control screen

Ask whether concentration by value has truly weakened **concentration by control**.
A society should count less safe when major firms, land, media, strategic infrastructure, or pooled capital remain effectively directed by a narrow upper tier even after headline shares soften.[S15][S20][S23][S26][S31][S32][S33]

### 4. Circulation screen

Ask whether the society is actually more **open**.
That means earlier footholds, weaker dependence on parental wealth, less group stratification, less place closure, and less household masking of person-level dependency.[S18][S19][S27][S28][S29][S30][S47][S48]

### 5. Counterweight-and-durability screen

Ask whether the order has enough public/common counterweight and enough anti-reconcentration strength to survive normal cycles.
A society should count less finished when broadening still depends on asset booms, unusually cheap credit, temporary fiscal windows, or continuing owner-favoring subsidy design.[S02][S20][S21][S22][S31][S32][S33][S36]

### 6. Proof screen

Ask whether the good reading is **positively demonstrated**.
Opacity, incomplete beneficial ownership, contested valuation, or other strategic missingness should block clean certification rather than operating as a presumption of safety.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## Fast use rule

Use this note in six steps:

1. **Check the numbers first, but only first.**  
   If the case is still plainly outside the archive's acceptable family, it is not near-ideal regardless of other improvements.
2. **Read the floor before celebrating broadening.**  
   Ask whether most people near the bottom actually have a resilient foothold rather than thin paper inclusion.
3. **Read control and circulation together.**  
   Ask whether concentrated wealth still directs key decisions and whether adult entry is still too inherited or too late.
4. **Ask whether the order can hold.**  
   A near-pass that cannot survive ordinary cycles, policy change, or asset-price normalization has not earned certification.
5. **Treat opacity as a stop sign.**  
   Hidden top-tail ownership or weak controller visibility should usually block a clean pass verdict.
6. **Only then choose the verdict.**  
   Far outside the screens -> **structurally immoral / failing**; improving but still outside the envelope -> **correction-required**; inside the numbers but still unresolved -> **provisional near-pass**; inside the numbers and clean across the main screens -> **near-ideal / constitutionally acceptable**.

## Non-substitution rule

Do **not** let any of the following upgrade the verdict beyond what the screens support:

- one appealing reform headline,
- one favorable business cycle,
- one broadened household balance-sheet series,
- one stronger public fund without household circulation,
- one stronger floor without anti-capture durability,
- or one anti-oligarchy instrument without actual floor repair and earlier footholds.

The archive's verdicts are meant to be **joint**.
A society should not be graded up because one strong lane is being used to excuse failure on the others.
