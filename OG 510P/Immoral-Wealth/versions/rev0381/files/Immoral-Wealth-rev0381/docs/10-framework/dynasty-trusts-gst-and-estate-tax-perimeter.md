# Dynasty trusts, GST, and estate-tax perimeter

High-end intergenerational transfer operates through a different legal stack than ordinary inheritance. Trusts, GST planning, valuation discounts, entity layering, family offices, charitable vehicles, and very high estate/gift exemptions can move or shelter control across generations.

## Perimeter questions

1. What is the estate/gift/GST exemption and rate, and how much high-end wealth is outside the practical estate-tax base?[S430][S326]
2. Do dynasty or perpetual trusts avoid transfer taxation beyond one generation?[S343]
3. Are trust beneficiaries, settlors, protectors, trustees, and controllers visible to tax, beneficial-ownership, and conflict-of-interest systems?
4. Are valuation discounts, grantor-trust rules, GRATs, IDGTs, family LLCs, and similar structures monitored for wealth-rule effects?
5. Does philanthropic or trust control become political or institutional rule?

## False passes

- **Estate-tax-rate pass:** statutory rate is high, but exemption and planning perimeter leave only a tiny number of estates exposed.
- **Beneficiary pass:** beneficiaries receive wealth without control visibility, while trustees/protectors preserve family-line power.
- **Trust-legality pass:** trust is legal and fiduciary, but the cumulative effect is perpetual class closure.

## Certification effect

The cube should not treat ordinary probate friction and high-end trust planning as symmetrical. If modest estates face probate, recovery, and title traps while very large estates have professionalized avoidance and control-preserving structures, Gate 19 blocks open-circulation comfort.
