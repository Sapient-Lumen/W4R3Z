# Housing market power and renter/resident seniority — rev0357

rev0322 adds a doctrine gap that earlier housing files did not fully isolate: **housing tenure can look formally available while the resident is residual in the private market-power stack.** The cube therefore distinguishes three related but different channels.

1. **Pricing coordination:** rent is set through shared data, common recommendation engines, or pricing practices that reduce independent landlord competition.[S414][S415][S416]
2. **Corporate-landlord systems:** renters face all-in price opacity, fee stacks, repair delays, renewal pressure, and deposit remedies that are hard to invoke at household scale.[S417][S418][S419][S420][S426]
3. **Nominal ownership with subordinated land control:** manufactured-home residents may own the home but rent the land, leaving wealth exposed to lot rent, community sale, chattel finance, and mobility lock-in.[S421][S422][S423][S424][S425]

## Seniority rule

A housing claim cannot certify as wealth-protective unless the ordinary resident is senior to the extraction channel. The cube should ask:

- Can the renter/resident see the all-in price before commitment?
- Can the resident exit without forfeiting school access, repair claims, deposits, home value, or moving costs?
- Can the claimant contest price, fee, maintenance, deposit, or lot-rent decisions quickly enough to preserve the asset or tenancy?
- Is market power visible by owner, beneficial owner, vendor, algorithm, parcel, and neighborhood?
- Does remedy reach the household, or only prospectively change conduct?

If the answer is no, the case belongs in the rental-market-power router even when aggregate market shares look small or the formal legal right exists.
