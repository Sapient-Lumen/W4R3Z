# Procurement, privatization, and public-value leakage

Public wealth is not only a balance-sheet item. It can leak through procurement, privatization, concessions, emergency contracting, public-private partnerships, land sales, infrastructure franchises, and outsourced welfare delivery.

## Rule

A case cannot count public/common wealth as a counterweight when the fiscal state systematically transfers public value to opaque, politically connected, or weakly monitored private controllers.

This is a wealth-order issue because procurement capture converts public revenue into private fortunes while leaving ordinary claimants with weaker services, lower trust, higher taxes, and fewer common assets.

## Procurement screen

Ask whether major public spending has:

- beneficial-owner disclosure for bidders and subcontractors;[S183]
- conflict-of-interest checks against officials, parties, donors, and family networks;
- competitive tendering or published emergency exceptions;
- contract, change-order, delivery, and beneficial-owner data in linked format;
- remedies for excluded bidders and harmed claimants;
- audit and asset-recovery pathways;
- sanctions and debarment that survive political turnover.

UNODC's UNCAC asset-recovery framework matters here because public-value leakage is not repaired by scandal alone; states need tools to identify, trace, freeze, preserve, seize, confiscate, manage, and return stolen assets.[S182]

## Privatization screen

Privatization or concession arrangements require an additional test:

1. Was the public asset fairly valued?
2. Were future monopoly rents capped or shared?
3. Are service duties enforceable?
4. Can the public retake or reprice the asset after failure?
5. Are beneficial owners and financiers visible?
6. Is the public left bearing downside risk while private controllers capture upside?

Where public downside and private upside coincide, route to `public_value_leakage` and `wealth_to_rule`.
