# Marginal incidence and direction of change

## What this note is for

This note turns one of the archive's most important **path-quality claims** into a stable reading rule.
It answers a narrower question than the fuller implementation and measurement notes in [`../20-program/transition-sequencing.md`](../20-program/transition-sequencing.md) and [`../20-program/measurement-and-scoreboard.md`](../20-program/measurement-and-scoreboard.md):
**when should a society count itself as moving in the right direction only if the next durable units of net wealth are actually landing with entrants, younger adults, weaker-balance households, or common wealth rather than mostly deepening incumbent position?**

The archive already judges wealth orders by **stock distribution, floor integrity, control, timing, circulation, and reconcentration risk**.
What this note adds is a compact rule for reading the **flow side of reproduction**.
Two countries can look similarly unjust in their current snapshot and still be moving in morally different directions.
One may route the next round of gains toward first footholds, earlier security, lower debt exposure, and common/public claims.
The other may let the next round of gains capitalise into incumbent land values, higher-balance accounts, monopoly margins, or later inheritances while calling the path progressive.[S13][S19][S31][S34][S36][S37][S47]

This note is therefore not a separate axis so much as a **direction-of-change rule**.
It tells the archive how to read subsidy design, housing policy, tax preferences, public-value capture, timing, and sequencing once we stop asking only who owns what now and start asking **who is being set up to own the next durable gains**.[S13][S19][S27][S31][S34][S36][S37][S47]

## Minimum reading rule

Read every reform path **at the margin**.
A society should get more moral credit when the next durable units of wealth are entrant-weighted, earlier in life, person-usable, less debt-fragile, and harder to reconcentrate.
It should get less credit when the next practical gains created or shaped by policy, credit conditions, market structure, or public uplift mainly thicken incumbent holdings, inflate scarce asset prices, reward already-large balances, or leave the next cohort buying into a more expensive system.[S13][S19][S31][S34][S36][S37][S47]

The archive's compact intuition is:
**a society is not moving toward a better wealth order unless the next durable gains are being routed better than the last ones were.**

That means this note reads current direction more harshly when apparent improvement depends mainly on:
- subsidies or tax preferences that still scale with prior position,
- owner support that mostly capitalises into prices,
- late compensation that arrives after long periods of exposure,
- nominal wealth gains that remain highly leveraged or shock-fragile,
- common or publicly created value that still ends up as private windfall,
- or political stories about eventual inclusion while parental background still strongly shapes practical access to the main compounding channels.[S05][S13][S19][S24][S25][S31][S34][S36][S37][S47]

## The six main false passes

### 1. A softer snapshot is mistaken for a better direction

A country should count less progress when some share metrics improve while the next round of gains still lands mainly with already-positioned owners.
A less bad stock can still be reproduced by a bad flow regime.[S13][S142][S31][S34][S36]

### 2. Gross statutory support is mistaken for marginal incidence

The archive should discount reforms that look entrant-friendly in statute but whose practical gains are absorbed by prices, fees, spreads, or provider margins before ordinary households keep them.[S31][S34][S36]

### 3. Later compensation is mistaken for present redirection

A society should count less success when younger or weaker-balance households are asked to endure worse entry, higher leverage, or delayed independence now in exchange for a promised later transfer that may arrive late, contingently, or unevenly.[S05][S19]

### 4. Any bottom-half gain is mistaken for an opening path

The archive should count less progress when marginal gains technically touch lower groups but do not materially widen first footholds, reduce family dependence, or weaken the role of inherited position in adult security.[S13][S19][S27][S47]

### 5. Paper appreciation is mistaken for durable broadening

A society should count less progress when the next gains are mostly nominal, highly intermediated, weakly titled, or so debt-fragile that mild shocks can strip them away before they change bargaining power.[S24][S25][S26]

### 6. Growth or public intervention is mistaken for democratised gain flow

The archive should count less success when new growth, public investment, tax relief, or rescue support still leaves the public socialising downside while incumbent owners keep the better upside and bargaining terms.[S20][S32][S33][S34]

## The six standing marginal-incidence surfaces

### 1. Entrant-versus-incumbent landing surface

The archive should ask where the **next durable unit** is actually landing.
A path counts more when the next gains widen first footholds, stabilize weaker-balance households, or improve entry on safer terms.
It counts less when those gains mainly deepen incumbent owner position or reward households already inside the main compounding channels.[S13][S19][S31][S36][S37]

### 2. Timing-and-compounding surface

The archive should ask **when** the next gain arrives.
An earlier foothold that starts compounding during adulthood often matters more than a larger later transfer.
So marginal direction counts more when gains arrive early enough to alter work, family, housing, and risk decisions rather than merely compensating after those windows have closed.[S05][S18][S19]

### 3. Price-capitalisation-and-leakage surface

The archive should ask how much of the next support survives as real household gain after prices, rents, land values, fees, and spreads adjust.
Support that mostly enlarges bids should count less than support that remains as access, security, or retained claim.[S31][S34][S36]

### 4. Debt-quality-and-survivability surface

The archive should ask whether the next gains survive ordinary shocks.
Marginal progress counts less when new footholds require high leverage, thin buffers, rollover risk, or fragile liquidity conditions that leave the gain easy to reverse.[S17][S24][S25]

### 5. Control-and-loss-bearing surface

The archive should ask who controls the next claims and who carries the downside.
A path counts less when households receive thin cash-flow exposure while governance rights remain bottlenecked, or when the public still absorbs exceptional losses on better terms than ordinary households receive.[S26][S32][S33]

### 6. Intergenerational-opening surface

The archive should ask whether the next gains are weakening or reproducing dependence on parental background.
A path counts more when new gains reduce the practical importance of homeowner parents, inherited wealth, advantaged childhood conditions, or insider family support.
It counts less when parental background still strongly shapes who can seize the next opportunities even after headline reform.[S13][S19][S27][S47]

## Fast use rule

Use this note in four steps:

1. **Ask where the next 100 units of net wealth probably land.**  
   Judge the path after incidence, capitalisation, and leakage, not only from statutory design.[S31][S34][S36]
2. **Ask whether the gains arrive early enough and safely enough to change life chances.**  
   Earlier, debt-lighter, shock-surviving gains count more than later or more fragile ones.[S05][S17][S19][S24][S25]
3. **Ask whether the path is opening entry or merely topping up incumbents.**  
   The test is whether weaker-balance households and the next cohort are getting safer footholds, not whether incumbent owners are becoming richer first.[S13][S19][S36][S37]
4. **Ask whether the path is weakening inherited advantage.**  
   Count more when the next gains reduce dependence on parental education, wealth, and childhood circumstance rather than leaving the same families best placed to capture them.[S27][S47]

Default shortcut:

- **entrant-weighted, early, leak-resistant, shock-surviving gains** -> count more favorably because the society is truly changing who gets the next footholds;
- **incumbent-weighted, late, capitalising, leveraged, inheritance-dependent gains** -> discount because the direction of change is still reproducing hierarchy;
- **mixed case** -> judge from the next cohort's side and ask whether the path would still look good if current incumbents were not the reference class.

## Program handoff

This note should make the archive stricter in four places.

First, it should harden the archive's reading of subsidy and tax design.
A reform should count less when the next fiscal effort still scales with larger balances, higher tax rates, or owner position.
See [`upside-down-subsidies-and-the-hidden-wealth-state.md`](upside-down-subsidies-and-the-hidden-wealth-state.md) and [`../20-program/tax-expenditures-and-subsidy-hygiene.md`](../20-program/tax-expenditures-and-subsidy-hygiene.md).

Second, it should sharpen housing and land judgment.
A path should count less when the next wave of public uplift, scarcity, or credit support still flows mainly into incumbent site values rather than safer entry or common capture.
See [`publicly-created-value-and-common-asset-capture.md`](publicly-created-value-and-common-asset-capture.md) and [`../20-program/housing-land-and-anti-monopoly.md`](../20-program/housing-land-and-anti-monopoly.md).

Third, it should keep sequencing honest.
The archive should prefer packages that front-load security, access, and durable footholds instead of asking the already exposed to wait for later compensation.
See [`promissory-offsets-and-backloaded-justice.md`](promissory-offsets-and-backloaded-justice.md) and [`../20-program/transition-sequencing.md`](../20-program/transition-sequencing.md).

Fourth, it should strengthen intergenerational judgment.
A society should count more progress when the next gains materially weaken the role of family background in who can accumulate, hold, and keep wealth on usable terms.
See [`intergenerational-circulation.md`](intergenerational-circulation.md), [`life-course-timing-and-early-footholds.md`](life-course-timing-and-early-footholds.md), and [`../20-program/social-inheritance-public-wealth-and-baby-bonds.md`](../20-program/social-inheritance-public-wealth-and-baby-bonds.md).

## Bottom line

The archive should now read direction of change in this order:
**who gets the next durable gains, when they get them, how much survives prices and debt, who controls them, who bears the downside, and whether the path is actually weakening inherited advantage.**

A society is not moving toward an acceptable wealth order merely because the stock snapshot looks a little better.
It is moving toward one only when the **next durable units of wealth are being routed in a more open, earlier, safer, and less inherited way than before**.[S13][S19][S31][S34][S36][S37][S47]
