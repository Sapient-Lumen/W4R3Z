# Liability stack and negative wealth

## Core claim

A wealth order can fail from below even when its official distribution table looks stable. The failure occurs when ordinary households do not merely lack assets but are **actively encumbered** by liabilities that interrupt threshold moments, damage credit access, attach sanctions, or drain future earnings before accumulation can begin.

Negative or fragile net worth should therefore be read as a structure, not only as a number. The archive distinguishes:

- **productive and reversible debt**: borrowing that buys a durable claim, has fair terms, and can be serviced without losing the floor;
- **defensive debt**: debt taken to survive illness, repair, care, rent, food, relocation, or benefit delay;
- **status debt**: debt that changes civic, credit, immigration, licensing, or legal status;
- **recursive debt**: debt whose fees, penalties, collections, or interest generate additional debt;
- **non-dischargeable or hard-to-discharge debt**: debt that follows the person through ordinary reset opportunities.

A lower-half floor does not pass when many people must finance predictable social risks with high-friction private liabilities.[S104][S105][S106]

## Why this is not already captured by net wealth

Net-wealth tables may record debt, but they do not always show its moral role. Two households with the same negative wealth can occupy different orders:

1. one has a mortgage, safe title, fixed payments, and a durable asset;
2. the other has medical collections, education debt without labor-market payoff, court fees, payday debt, suspended license risk, and a damaged credit file.

The first may still be a foothold. The second is often dependency with a balance attached.

## Liability-stack screen

A case has liability-stack pressure when at least three of the following are live:

| Screen | Watch signal | Fail signal | Route |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medical/debt exposure | medical bills or delayed care are common | medical debt affects credit, borrowing, rent, or employment access | floor / health |
| Student-debt drag | degree access depends on borrowing | repayment/default delays household formation or wealth entry | early footholds / debt |
| Criminal-legal debt | fines and fees are common | nonpayment triggers license, probation, record, jail, or collection consequences | group veto / civic closure |
| Collections/credit drag | collections are widespread | collections block housing, utilities, auto access, or business entry | early footholds / housing |
| High-cost credit | fringe credit fills ordinary gaps | rollover/fee structure converts income into extraction | floor / anti-extraction |
| Care/disability interruption | care costs or income loss are common | asset tests, earnings penalties, or extra costs force spend-down | floor / disability |

## Certification effects

A case cannot receive a real-floor pass while the liability stack is `fail` or `veto_pressure`. The proper verdict is usually:

- `correction_required` when the liability stack is broad but rails exist;
- `emergency_repair` when debt blocks food, shelter, care, safe exit, or medical access;
- `captured_immoral` when public institutions rely on debt extraction from subordinated groups.

## Evidence order

Request evidence in this order:

1. share of households with negative or near-zero net worth;
2. debt-service and delinquency by income, wealth, age, race/caste/gender/disability, migrant status, and region;
3. medical debt, student debt, court debt, arrears, collections, and high-cost credit separately;
4. whether the debt affects housing, utilities, transport, employment, licensing, immigration, custody, or civic participation;
5. whether bankruptcy, forgiveness, hardship relief, income-based repayment, charity care, or ability-to-pay review actually works without expert rescue;
6. whether public agencies, courts, hospitals, schools, or utilities themselves create the liability.

## False passes

Do not pass a case merely because aggregate household debt looks manageable. The decisive question is who carries which liabilities at which threshold moment.

Do not pass a case merely because education debt is associated with higher average earnings. It fails when debt produces delayed household formation, default, family-gated program choice, or racially/personally unequal repayment burdens.[S379][S108][S109]

Do not pass a case merely because fines and fees are a small share of public revenue. They fail when the state extracts from people least able to pay and attaches legal or mobility consequences.[S110][S111]
