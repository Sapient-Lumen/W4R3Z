# Hidden ownership, offshore structures, and control claims

Wealth is morally assessed by **control**, not by nominal legal form. A corporation, trust, foundation, family office, fund, nominee, partnership, offshore entity, or legal arrangement may be a real owner for legal administration while still concealing the natural persons who benefit from or direct the wealth.

rev0308 therefore separates four questions:

1. **legal title:** who is recorded as the owner;
2. **beneficial enjoyment:** who receives income, consumption, collateral value, or exit value;
3. **control:** who can direct sale, vote, pledge, distribute, or block use;
4. **political conversion:** who can use the structure to influence law, land, media, procurement, parties, or public rescue.

A wealth order cannot pass the visibility gate when these four layers cannot be connected at reasonable cost.[S431][S155]

## Offshore is not only evasion

Offshore and entity structures can be legal. The archive does not treat every offshore structure as illicit. The moral problem is that such structures can make wealth **unmeasurable, unaccountable, un-taxable, un-sanctionable, or politically deniable**. Automatic exchange of information has reduced some offshore tax evasion, but the existence of progress is not equivalent to full visibility.[S152][S153]

## Trusts and legal arrangements

Trusts and similar arrangements deserve a separate control screen because legal title and beneficial enjoyment can be deliberately split. A case must ask whether registers or enforcement systems can identify settlors, trustees, protectors, beneficiaries or classes of beneficiaries, and any person exercising ultimate effective control.[S431]

The case fails a comfort pass when:

- trust registers are unavailable, non-public where public interest requires them, or unverifiable;
- protectors, enforcers, reserved powers, letters of wishes, or nominee chains conceal control;
- foreign legal arrangements have sufficient local links but no local reporting duty;
- real estate, private companies, or political donations can be routed through opaque entities;
- sanctions, tax, land-use, or campaign-finance enforcement cannot connect asset to controller.

## Ownership visibility as a public rail

Ownership visibility is a public rail like a payment system or court registry. It is not a mere administrative convenience. Without it, the archive cannot distinguish a benign broad-property order from a concentrated control order with cosmetic dispersion.

Minimum visibility rails:

1. verified beneficial-owner data for legal persons;
2. verified beneficial-owner/control data for trusts and legal arrangements;
3. property-register linkage to beneficial owners;
4. periodic update and sanctions for stale or false entries;
5. competent-authority access across borders;
6. public-interest access where land, procurement, parties, media, or critical infrastructure are implicated;
7. audit sampling and enforcement statistics.

Where these rails are missing, the proper verdict is not “unknown, therefore neutral.” It is `proof_debt` with an opacity penalty, escalating to `correction_required` when hidden ownership touches housing, land, political finance, critical firms, or top-tail taxation.
