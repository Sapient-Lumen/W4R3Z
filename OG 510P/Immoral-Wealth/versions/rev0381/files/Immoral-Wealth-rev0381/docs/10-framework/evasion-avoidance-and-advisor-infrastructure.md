# Evasion, avoidance, and advisor infrastructure

The top tail is not a passive object of law. It has lawyers, accountants, family offices, banks, trustees, valuation experts, lobbyists, private funds, shell entities, migration options, and patience. This advisor infrastructure changes the moral status of an instrument.

## Rule

A policy that would work against ordinary claimants but not against advised wealth receives an `asymmetric_enforcement` warning. Where the asymmetry is severe, the policy can become an upside-down subsidy: the compliant middle pays, the lower half is screened, and the top tail exits through complexity.

## Avoidance map

Common channels:

- entity fragmentation;
- trust and foundation layering;
- related-party loans and valuation discounts;
- private-business and private-fund marks;
- offshore account or entity chains;
- residence and exit planning;
- debt engineering;
- charitable or philanthropic wrappers;
- procurement-company opacity;
- litigation delay.

## Rail implications

Anti-avoidance design must be paired with:

1. beneficial-owner verification;
2. common reporting and exchange of information;[S178][S180][S181]
3. high-net-worth audit teams;
4. valuation-safe-harbour rules that do not become loopholes;
5. information returns for trusts, partnerships, private funds, and related-party transactions;
6. promoter/advisor penalties;
7. collection-before-dissipation tools;
8. international cooperation.

The Global Forum's transparency work and AEOI peer reviews are relevant because offshore accounts and crypto-assets cannot be treated as invisible once exchange rails exist, but the 2025 review also shows that practical effectiveness still varies by jurisdiction.[S178][S180]
