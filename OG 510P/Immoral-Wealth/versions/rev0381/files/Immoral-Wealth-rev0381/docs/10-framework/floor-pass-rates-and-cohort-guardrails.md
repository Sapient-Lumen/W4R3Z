# Floor pass rates and cohort guardrails

## What this note is for

This note turns one of the archive's remaining loose questions into a **stable floor-count rule**.
It answers a narrower question than the fuller floor construction in [`minimum-asset-floor.md`](minimum-asset-floor.md):
**how broadly must the floor actually be passed before a society can claim that the bottom half has a real foothold?**

The archive already rejects the idea that better aggregates are enough.
A society can improve median wealth, soften one distributional share, or raise owner-occupation somewhat while still leaving too many ordinary households below a real floor, leaving younger adults stranded outside early footholds, or leaving excluded groups far behind the headline pass rate.[S13][S18][S19][S25][S52]

So this note supplies a compact counting rule.
It treats the floor as a **population standard with cohort and group guardrails**, not as a bare average and not as a simple-majority comfort test.
That is meant to block three common mistakes at once:

1. mistaking a thin majority for a broad constitutional pass,
2. letting older or incumbent cohorts wash out young-adult exclusion,
3. and letting national averages hide durable gaps for excluded groups.

## Minimum reading rule

Read the floor as a **broad-pass condition**.
A society should count the bottom half as having a real floor only when a clear supermajority of bottom-half households clear the archive's core floor test, when the next adult cohort is not far weaker than the all-age bottom-half picture, and when excluded groups are not being left durably below the national pass rate.[S13][S18][S19][S25][S52]

The archive's compact intuition is:
**a real floor is not present when ordinary security is still a coin flip, when the next cohort must wait too long for first footholds, or when the apparent pass depends mainly on older incumbents carrying the averages.**

This note therefore treats floor passage as a constitutional screen.
It reads the pass more harshly when the apparent success is mainly:
- a thin majority,
- a life-cycle lag that pushes security too late,
- a young-adult collapse masked by older ownership,
- an excluded-group failure hidden by national averaging,
- a calm-weather pass that does not survive ordinary shocks,
- or a headline pass that relies on one narrow asset channel rather than the archive's fuller floor spine.

## The six main false passes

### 1. Fifty percent plus one is mistaken for a real floor

A society should not count the floor as broadly passed merely because a slim majority clears it.
The bottom half is too large and too central to democratic independence for the archive to treat a knife-edge majority as constitutionally comfortable.[S13][S18][S25]

### 2. Older-cohort security is mistaken for broad all-age success

The floor should count for less when later-life housing wealth or late asset accumulation makes the all-age picture look safer than the actual entry conditions facing younger adults.[S13][S19][S52]

### 3. Delayed footholds are mistaken for timely footholds

A society should count less success when young adults can reach the floor only after long dependence on parents, partnership rescue, inheritance timing, or later-life balance-sheet repair.
A late foothold is not equal to an early foothold in its effects on autonomy, family formation, and cumulative life chances.[S19][S52]

### 4. National averages are mistaken for broad group passage

A national floor pass should count for less when historically subordinated or systematically excluded groups sit well below the overall pass rate, even if the national headline looks decent.[S18][S19]

### 5. One favorable floor component is mistaken for the full bundle

A society should count less success when many households clear one visible component, such as gross homeownership or headline net wealth, while still failing on resilience, debt quality, security of tenure, or durable claim quality.[S13][S25]

### 6. Temporary or calm-weather passage is mistaken for a stable pass

The floor should count for less when apparent passage is being carried by temporary revaluation, temporary subsidy conditions, or a benign macro period rather than by durable broad security that survives ordinary shocks.[S13][S25]

## The six standing pass-rate surfaces

### 1. Bottom-half supermajority passage

The archive's default constitutional attractor is that **roughly 70% or more** of bottom-half households should clear the core floor test.
That is not a sacred timeless number, but it is the archive's standing rule of thumb for a broad pass rather than a flattering near-miss.

### 2. Weak-pass and failure ranges

Use these default bands until the archive has a better cross-country calibration:
- **Constitutional pass zone:** about **70% or more** of bottom-half households clear the core floor test.
- **Watch / weak pass zone:** about **60% to 69%** clear it; this is not comfortable and should usually require healthy cohort and group guardrails plus low reconcentration pressure.
- **Clear floor failure:** **below 60%**.
- **Major floor breach:** **below 50%**, or an apparent pass rate propped up mainly by temporary valuation, subsidy capitalisation, or older-cohort balance sheets.

### 3. Under-40 guardrail

The under-40 bottom-half pass rate should not sit persistently far below the all-age bottom-half pass rate.
As a working default, a gap of about **15 percentage points or more** is a serious warning that the society is living off older security while failing to reproduce open entry for the next cohort.[S13][S19][S52]

### 4. Early-foothold guardrail

Young adults should be able to reach positive position, stable tenure, and a durable first claim early enough that normal adulthood is not organised around waiting for inheritance, household merger, or prolonged parental subsidy.
Where the overall floor pass looks decent but early footholds are badly delayed, the archive should read this as a timing failure, not a genuine broad pass.[S19][S52]

### 5. Excluded-group guardrail

Where disaggregated data exist, the archive should not accept a national floor pass when major excluded groups remain durably below it.
As a working default, a persistent gap of around **15 percentage points or more** between the overall bottom-half pass rate and the pass rate for a major excluded group is a serious warning, and much larger durable gaps should count as a group-stratification failure.[S18][S19]

### 6. Full-bundle rather than single-axis passage

The relevant pass rate is the pass rate for the archive's **core floor bundle**, not for one flattering component in isolation.
Passage should therefore be counted more cautiously when a jurisdiction can show positive net wealth or owner-occupation but not enough liquidity, durability, protection, debt quality, or ordinary shock survival to make the foothold real.[S13][S25]

## Fast use rule

Use this note this way in quick comparisons:

1. start with the core floor bundle from [`minimum-asset-floor.md`](minimum-asset-floor.md),
2. ask what share of bottom-half households actually clear it in function rather than on paper,
3. do not call the floor broadly passed unless the rate is near or above the archive's rough supermajority range,
4. then check whether under-40 households and major excluded groups are tracking close enough to the headline rate,
5. and discount the pass immediately when it depends mainly on late-life holdings, one favorable asset class, or calm-weather conditions.

A society should therefore count as closer to justice when the bottom-half floor is becoming broadly normal, early enough, and open across cohorts and groups rather than narrowly achieved, late, and incumbent-carried.

## Program implication

This note should change the archive's operational reading in three ways.

1. In [`../20-program/measurement-and-scoreboard.md`](../20-program/measurement-and-scoreboard.md), it clarifies that floor reporting should track pass rates for the bottom half, younger cohorts, and major excluded groups rather than relying on one national headline.
2. In [`../20-program/mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md`](../20-program/mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md), it clarifies that **sub-60% passage** is ordinarily a correction case and that **sub-50% passage**, large cohort collapse, or large excluded-group gaps should usually count as stronger constitutional failure.
3. In [`target-selection-rules.md`](target-selection-rules.md), it clarifies that the floor is not just one axis among many but a broad-pass condition that helps determine whether a candidate wealth distribution is even admissible.

## Bottom line

The archive should no longer treat the floor as passed because a thin majority clears a minimum test.
Its standing rule is stricter:
**the bottom-half floor should be read as a supermajority condition with cohort and group guardrails, and a society should count as morally weaker when the apparent pass is late, narrow, incumbent-carried, or unevenly closed to the next cohort and excluded groups.**
