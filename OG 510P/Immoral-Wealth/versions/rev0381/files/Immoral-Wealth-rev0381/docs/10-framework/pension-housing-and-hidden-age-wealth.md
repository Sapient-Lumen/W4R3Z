# Pension, housing, and hidden age wealth

Household wealth can look broad while entry remains closed. The main culprits are owner-occupied housing gains, private pension valuation, and age composition.

## Why this matters

A middle-heavy wealth distribution may reflect older homeowners and pension claims rather than open circulation for young adults, renters, migrants, single adults, or care-burdened households. That does not make pension or housing wealth unreal; it means the case needs threshold-moment tests.

## Three cautions

### 1. Pension valuation is not liquid foothold

Private pension wealth can support retirement security, but it usually cannot pay a deposit, exit an unsafe household, cover a repair shock, or start a business. It should not be counted as a first-foothold equivalent without a liquidity and control note.[S87][S89]

### 2. Housing equity can be positional

Housing wealth is useful, but it may also be the price of entry rising faster than wages. A homeowner-rich society can be renter-closed if the deposit/guarantee threshold is family-gated.[S87][S88][S91]

### 3. Age hides moral timing

A society may allow eventual wealth accumulation only after decades of rent, debt, and fragile entry. The archive should ask whether people have a defensible foothold **when** they need it, not just whether older cohorts later record wealth.[S18][S19][S52]

## Rule

Pension and housing wealth can support a pass only after the case separately scores liquidity, timing, person-level control, renter access, intergenerational entry, and shock survivability.
