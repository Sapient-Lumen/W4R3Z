# Legal attack surface and constitutional constraint

The archive should not recommend instruments as if courts, constitutional text, administrative procedure, federalism, treaty commitments, and privacy law were frictionless. Legal attack surface is a design variable.

## Rule

A package that is morally attractive but legally fragile must be routed as `design_with_constraints`, not as a direct command. The cube does not abandon hard reforms; it separates **ends** from legally durable pathways.

## U.S. tax example

`Moore v. United States` upheld the mandatory repatriation tax but expressly did not bless every possible wealth-tax design.[S168] That means U.S. top-tail compression should keep multiple tracks open:

- inheritance, gift, and dynasty-trust reform;
- mark-to-market or deemed-realization rules tied to income-tax concepts where legally viable;
- exit taxes and anti-deferral rules;
- corporate, partnership, and private-fund reporting;
- property, land, and state-level instruments;
- enforcement of existing liabilities before speculative new instruments.

The archive's decision matrix should therefore distinguish a **moral target** from the **least brittle legal pathway**.

## Privacy/transparency example

Beneficial ownership is another attack surface. EU law after the CJEU reset shifted from unrestricted public access to legitimate-interest access, while still maintaining central registers and interconnection through BORIS.[S172][S173][S175] The lesson is not that transparency should be abandoned. The lesson is that transparency rails must be privacy-tested, role-based where necessary, reviewable, and still usable by journalists, researchers, civil society, procurement bodies, tax authorities, AML authorities, and affected claimants.

## Attack-surface map

For each case, ask:

1. **Constitutional text:** Is the instrument within the taxing, spending, property, equality, privacy, or due-process power?
2. **Administrative law:** Was the rule adopted with adequate process, evidence, and statutory authority?
3. **Federalism / localism:** Which level of government controls land, tax, welfare, courts, registries, and enforcement?
4. **Treaty / cross-border limits:** Does the package require exchange-of-information or coordinated minimum-tax rules?[S166][S167][S178]
5. **Rights balancing:** Does transparency or seizure intrude on privacy, data protection, or property rights without proportional design?
6. **Remedy realism:** Can claims be enforced before the relevant household or public asset is already lost?

When the attack surface is high, the verdict should not become softer; the pathway should become more careful.
