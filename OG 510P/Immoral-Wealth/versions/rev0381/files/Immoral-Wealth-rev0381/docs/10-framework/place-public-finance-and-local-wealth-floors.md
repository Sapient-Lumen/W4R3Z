# Place public finance and local wealth floors

rev0313 adds the place-public-finance layer. A wealth order is not only a distribution of private balance sheets and national public claims. It is also a map of local public services, infrastructure, schools, utilities, transportation, and fiscal capacity. These place-based public assets can either reduce private dependence or intensify it.[S243][S244][S245][S246]

The core claim is simple:

> **A lower-half floor is not real when ordinary households must buy privately what their locality cannot finance publicly.**

Local public finance matters because it decides whether children get adequate schools and buildings, whether water and sewer systems function, whether utility bills become debt and disconnection, whether transportation links jobs to homes, whether disaster recovery preserves services, and whether local governments can maintain assets without regressive fees, special assessments, or deferred maintenance.[S238][S242][S248][S249][S250][S251][S254]

## Why this belongs inside wealth doctrine

Place-level fiscal capacity can convert housing wealth into public advantage. It can also convert poverty, depopulation, environmental risk, and weak tax bases into a self-reinforcing public-service deficit. When a family must choose between a cheaper home with high transportation costs, weak schools, unsafe water, poor stormwater protection, fragile utilities, and slow disaster recovery, the home price is not the full wealth story.[S240][S244][S254][S257]

The archive therefore treats local fiscal capacity as a public-wealth counterweight screen. Public wealth counts only when it is geographically reachable, adequately funded, claimable, maintained, and protected from local capture.

## False passes

| False pass | Meaning |
|---|---|
| national-public-spending pass | National spending is counted without checking whether poor or excluded places receive functioning services. |
| property-tax-localism pass | Local fiscal autonomy is counted as democratic control while property-wealth fragmentation creates unequal childhood and infrastructure floors. |
| grant-announcement pass | Federal/state grants are counted before localities can match, apply, administer, maintain, and absorb them. |
| low-tax pass | Low local taxes are celebrated while infrastructure is deferred, utility debt rises, schools deteriorate, or fees replace taxes. |
| cheap-housing pass | Cheap housing is counted as affordability without scoring transportation, utilities, insurance, schools, and service access. |
| disaster-aid pass | Disaster assistance is counted before reimbursement timing, revenue loss, local borrowing, and service continuity are scored. |

## Certification consequence

Place public finance is Gate 15. It does not replace Gate 7 on public/common wealth; it localizes it. A case cannot comfort-certify when a national public counterweight exists on paper but local public-service floors are sharply unequal, under-maintained, debt-financed through regressive charges, or dependent on brittle local tax bases.

The opening package may need school-finance equalization, facilities capital, water/wastewater grants, utility affordability and shutoff guardrails, transportation access, fiscal-stress monitoring, disaster operating bridges, local revenue flexibility, anti-abatement rules, or intergovernmental transfer redesign.[S238][S240][S245][S246][S248][S250][S256][S258]
