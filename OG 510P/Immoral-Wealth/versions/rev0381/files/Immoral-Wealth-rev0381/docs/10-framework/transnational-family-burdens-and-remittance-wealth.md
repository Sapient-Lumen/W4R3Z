# Transnational family burdens and remittance wealth

Remittances are wealth transfers, insurance transfers, debt-service transfers, and care transfers. They can help families build homes, pay school fees, repair shocks, and reduce poverty. But they can also reveal a failure: households are surviving through cross-border family obligation because local wages, floors, pensions, healthcare, or housing systems are too weak.[S218]

## Core distinction

Do not code remittances as automatically good or bad.

- They are **wealth-supporting** when low-cost corridors, safe payment rails, fair exchange rates, documentation access, and recipient control turn earnings into durable assets.
- They are **wealth-draining** when recruitment debt, visa precarity, wage theft, high transfer fees, forced family support, exchange-rate spreads, or deportation risk turn migration into a fragile private welfare state.[S216][S217][S219]

## Wealth-order screen

A case should score:

1. remittance dependence as share of household income where available;
2. corridor cost and FX spread;
3. recruitment debt and employer/gatekeeper control;
4. recipient control, especially gender and elder-control issues;
5. portability of pension, injury, wage, and social-insurance claims;
6. whether remittances are buying first footholds or merely paying for survival shocks;
7. whether the sending jurisdiction externalizes social reproduction costs to migrant families.

## False pass

A country cannot pass as having a real floor merely because families receive remittances. Private kin transfers are not a public floor unless the case separately proves voluntary, low-cost, stable, non-coercive, person-controlled, and asset-forming conversion.
