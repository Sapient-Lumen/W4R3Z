# Investor citizenship, residence, and status markets

Citizenship and residence are not just border categories. They allocate claims: movement, work, tax residence, asset access, banking access, political rights, inheritance planning, social benefits, and protection from removal. When status can be purchased, the wealth order gains a new top-tail conversion channel.

## Distinction

- **Citizenship by investment** grants nationality or a pathway so short and transactional that citizenship becomes close to a commodity.
- **Residence by investment** grants residence, visa access, or tax positioning through investment or asset purchase.
- **Preferential newcomer tax regimes** may be legitimate attraction tools, but they can also create status-ranked tax citizenship.

OECD warns that some CBI/RBI schemes may be misused to hide offshore assets and undermine tax transparency, and the European Commission identifies security, money-laundering, tax-evasion, corruption, transparency, and oversight risks.[S203][S205]

## Wealth-order test

A status market becomes a wealth-order breach when:

1. it lets wealth buy rights unavailable to ordinary long-term workers, caregivers, refugees, or residents;
2. it helps conceal tax residence or beneficial ownership;
3. it provides an escape hatch from domestic accountability;
4. it pushes housing or land prices in entry markets;
5. it gives nonresident wealth local property claims without local claimant obligations;
6. it weakens solidarity by treating public membership as a purchasable asset.[S204][S206]

## Not all attraction regimes fail

A skilled-worker, humanitarian, family-unification, or transparent investment route can be defensible when it has genuine residence, tax-compliance, labor, anti-corruption, housing, and public-contribution rails. The problem is not foreigners or mobility. The problem is **wealth-ranked access to membership and claims**.
