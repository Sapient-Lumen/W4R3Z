# Capital flight and macro-stability as wealth-order risk

Wealth reforms can fail when private asset flight, foreign-currency stress, sudden stops, or legal evasions force emergency retrenchment. That does not make all capital controls or exit taxes good. It means wealth-order certification must ask whether the jurisdiction has macro-financial rails strong enough to prevent reform from becoming a destabilizing run.[S213][S214]

## Relevant channels

- rapid movement of financial assets after tax or transparency announcements;
- real-estate exit into offshore vehicles;
- owner relocation without asset relocation;
- crypto or private-market channels outside reporting rails;
- FX pressure and balance-of-payments stress;
- litigation through investment treaties or tax treaties;
- domestic elites using flight threats to weaken reform design.

## Design principle

The archive should reject two lazy answers:

1. **Ignore exit risk.** That lets reform collapse under predictable flight and countermobilization.
2. **Let exit threats veto justice.** That treats elite mobility as a constitutional trump card.

The middle path is a rail package: pre-announced transition rules, deemed disposal, exit tax, anti-deferral, information exchange, beneficial-owner verification, treaty compatibility, capital-flow stress tests, and targeted macroprudential/capital-flow management where appropriate.[S56][S180][S181][S213][S214]

## Certification rule

Capital flight becomes a verdict issue only when it plausibly changes feasibility, public solvency, claim conversion, or macro stability. Mere anecdotal threats do not count as proof. Use a proof-debt label until stock-flow, taxpayer, asset, FX, and enforcement evidence is available.[S210][S211][S212]
