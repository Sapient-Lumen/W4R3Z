# Jurisdiction shopping, exit, and claimant perimeter

rev0311 adds a cross-border layer to the wealth-order test. A domestic package can look coherent and still fail if wealth, legal status, tax residence, assets, or claims can be moved across borders in ways ordinary claimants cannot match.

The moral problem is not mobility itself. Mobility can protect refugees, workers, families, entrepreneurs, and politically exposed dissenters. The problem is **asymmetric mobility**: concentrated wealth can shop for tax residence, citizenship, secrecy, courts, property markets, and political access, while ordinary people remain tied to jobs, care, language, documentation, visas, remittance obligations, and local public services.[S203][S204][S215]

## Core rule

A case cannot receive a comfort verdict when domestic wealth reform depends on borders that are porous for the wealthy and hard for everyone else.

This creates three questions:

1. **Exit blackmail:** can top-tail actors credibly threaten to leave, move assets, or change residence in order to block taxation, transparency, labor rules, housing rules, or enforcement?[S210][S211][S212]
2. **Status shopping:** can money buy citizenship, residence, tax treatment, or account access that ordinary migrants cannot obtain under comparable contribution, attachment, or need?[S203][S205][S206]
3. **Claimant perimeter:** who counts as a claimant for public/common wealth, social insurance, legal remedies, labor protection, and political voice—citizens, residents, workers, contributors, children, long-term migrants, undocumented people, or only formal nationals?[S216][S217]

## False passes

| False pass | Meaning |
|---|---|
| domestic-only pass | case looks acceptable only because offshore assets, emigrant wealth, or nonresident owners are out of frame |
| exit-vetoed | policy is abandoned or weakened because the wealthy threaten to leave before evidence proves a decisive stock loss |
| passport-privileged | citizenship/residence can be purchased more easily than ordinary migrants can earn secure status |
| resident-excluded | people who work, rent, remit, or pay taxes are excluded from public-wealth claims and political voice |
| remittance-drained | family safety nets rely on migrants sending money through costly or unstable corridors |
| capital-flight-fragile | reforms trigger destabilizing outflows because macro-financial and tax rails are unprepared |
| treaty-misaligned | tax, benefits, pensions, inheritance, ownership, or enforcement rules fail at the border |

## Certification consequence

Jurisdiction shopping is a hardening surface, not a reason to abandon reform. It moves the package toward:

- exit taxes and deemed-disposal rules;
- trailing residence and source-based taxation where appropriate;
- CRS/AEOI and beneficial-owner verification;
- anti-treaty-shopping rules;
- residence/citizenship-by-investment risk screens;
- migrant-inclusive social-protection and remedy rules;
- capital-flow stress testing; and
- remittance-cost and payment-rail protections.[S38][S180][S181][S213][S214][S218][S219]

## Case pressure

rev0311 adds five calibration cases: EU investor citizenship/residence, UK non-dom transition, global high-wealth mobility and exit threats, Gulf migrant/public-wealth perimeter, and remittance-dependent transnational family burden.
