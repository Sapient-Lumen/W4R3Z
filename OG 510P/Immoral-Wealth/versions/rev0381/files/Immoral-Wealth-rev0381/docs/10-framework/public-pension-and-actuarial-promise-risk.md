# Public pension and actuarial promise risk

Public pensions are deferred wages and public promises. They can be a stabilizing middle-wealth institution when adequately funded and broadly shared. They become a wealth-order risk when optimistic assumptions, benefit politics, asset volatility, or local fiscal weakness create future pressure to cut services, raise regressive taxes, or reduce benefits for younger and lower-wage cohorts.[S355][S356]

The case question is not simply whether a pension plan is underfunded. It is who bears the adjustment when assumptions fail:

- retirees through benefit cuts or COLA erosion;
- workers through contribution hikes or lower wages;
- residents through property taxes, fees, or service cuts;
- future entrants through two-tier benefits;
- ordinary claimants through reduced public investment.

Gate 20 links pension funding to Gate 15 because local service wealth can be compressed by pension debt even when national indicators look healthy.
