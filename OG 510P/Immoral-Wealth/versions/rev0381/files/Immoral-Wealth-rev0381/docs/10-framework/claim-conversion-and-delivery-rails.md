# Claim conversion and delivery rails

rev0307 adds a conversion layer. A wealth order does not pass merely because a public program, credit, account, title, or transfer exists on paper. It must show that the intended person can **find, claim, receive, keep, and convert** the claim without expert help, family rescue, gatekeeper permission, avoidable fees, or punitive after-the-fact recapture.

This note generalizes earlier claimability doctrine. The archive already rejected paper floors and locked-up claims. The rev0307 move is stricter: **claim conversion is its own certification surface**. It applies to refundable tax credits, cash transfers, social insurance, public dividends, starter accounts, housing benefits, disaster aid, inheritance/title claims, pensions, public-housing access, legal-aid entitlements, and any public/common wealth device that is supposed to become person-level security.[S120][S263][S122][S123][S126][S139][S140]

## Conversion sequence

Every nominal claim must survive ten steps:

1. **eligibility** — the person is inside the legal class;
2. **awareness** — the person knows the claim exists;
3. **documentation** — identity, address, household, work, disability, care, title, or income proofs are obtainable;
4. **application** — the form, portal, office, or default route is accessible;
5. **verification** — errors, mismatches, and reviews are not punitive or endless;
6. **payment rail** — the person can receive funds through a safe account, mobile wallet, card, cash route, or other usable rail;
7. **timeliness** — the claim arrives before rent, medicine, repair, court, school, or debt deadlines;
8. **retention** — the claim is not lost to offsets, clawbacks, seizure, household gatekeepers, or high fees;
9. **conversion** — the claim can become rent paid, repair made, tuition covered, debt avoided, business stake, safe exit, or liquid buffer;
10. **renewal** — the claim does not disappear through paperwork churn, recertification traps, or sudden technology changes.

Failure at any step can turn a generous nominal program into a false pass.

## False-pass labels

Use these labels when a case has money or rights on paper but no reliable claimant conversion:

- `entitlement-invisible`: eligible people do not know or cannot understand the claim;
- `form-blocked`: forms, language, offices, portals, documentation, or expertise demands prevent use;
- `ID-blocked`: civil-registration, legal-ID, name-match, digital-ID, or address proof failures block access;
- `accountless`: the person lacks a safe payment rail or must use costly nonbank channels;
- `delayed-past-need`: the claim arrives after the threshold moment has already failed;
- `fee-leaked`: the claim is drained by overdraft, NSF, check-cashing, paid filing, transfer, or account fees;
- `offset-captured`: the claim is seized by old debts, benefit offsets, court debt, tax debt, or private creditors;
- `clawback-shadowed`: later recapture risk makes people rationally afraid to claim;
- `household-captured`: the legal recipient is not the effective controller;
- `app-only-excluded`: the delivery route assumes phone, data, literacy, language, or digital trust that some claimants lack;
- `renewal-churned`: the claim is lost through recurring verification rather than changed need;
- `title-unfinalized`: the person occupies or inherits an asset but cannot prove control enough to finance, repair, insure, or keep it.

## Moral rule

A nominal public claim is morally relevant only to the extent that it becomes **usable person-level control at the threshold moment**. A benefit received six months late, a child account that matures but is not found, a disaster grant blocked by title proof, or a tax credit that requires paid intermediaries is not equivalent to a live asset.

The same rule applies to private and common wealth. A pension, account, housing right, land title, trust benefit, or dividend must be scored by claimant conversion, not institutional size.

## Evidence burden

A case seeking `acceptable` or `near_ideal` certification must answer:

- what share of eligible claimants receive the claim;
- how long it takes from eligibility to usable receipt;
- which documentation blocks are common;
- whether payment rails are safe, low-cost, and person-controlled;
- how much is lost to fees, offsets, garnishment, paid intermediaries, or account penalties;
- whether renewal or recertification causes interruption;
- whether disabled people, migrants, women, young adults, renters, rural residents, language minorities, caste/racial groups, and people without stable addresses face higher conversion failure;
- whether errors are appealable without professional help;
- whether offline, assisted, or cash-compatible routes remain available.

Missing conversion evidence is ordinary evidence debt in low-stakes cases. It is hard evidence debt when the case relies on social transfers, starter assets, tax credits, social housing, public dividends, disaster aid, or legal/title claims for moral certification.

## Routing

- Route `ID-blocked`, `accountless`, and `app-only-excluded` to legal-identity, payment-rail, and DPI repair.
- Route `fee-leaked`, `offset-captured`, and `clawback-shadowed` to protected-minima, anti-seizure, and intermediation-fee rules.
- Route `renewal-churned` to automatic enrollment, periodic data matching, and no-wrong-door delivery.
- Route `title-unfinalized` to title clearance, probate, legal aid, disaster-proof alternatives, and heirs-property protection.
- Route `household-captured` to person-level ownership and intra-household power screens.

The conversion layer does not replace the floor or early-foothold gates. It asks whether those gates can actually be crossed.
