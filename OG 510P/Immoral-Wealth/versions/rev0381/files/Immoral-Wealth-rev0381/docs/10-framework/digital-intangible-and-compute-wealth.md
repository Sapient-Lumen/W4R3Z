# Digital, intangible, and compute wealth

## What this note is for

This note adds a digital/intangible surface to the archive's top-tail, formation, and wealth-to-rule screens. It asks when wealth in **software, data, compute, cloud infrastructure, models, intellectual property, platforms, private-market stakes, and network control** becomes a new path to dependency, closure, or rule.

Use this note with [`asset-composition-and-control.md`](asset-composition-and-control.md), [`intermediated-ownership-and-stewardship.md`](intermediated-ownership-and-stewardship.md), [`wealth-to-rule-conversion.md`](wealth-to-rule-conversion.md), [`../20-program/predistribution-labor-and-wages.md`](../20-program/predistribution-labor-and-wages.md), and [`../20-program/measurement-and-scoreboard.md`](../20-program/measurement-and-scoreboard.md).

## Minimum rule

A wealth order cannot be certified from household asset shares alone when the main new compounding assets sit in intangible, private, platform, or compute-controlled channels that ordinary households cannot access, inspect, or contest. Digital wealth should be read by **ownership, control, access, and rule-conversion power**, not only by market capitalization.[S20][S60][S68][S69]

## Why this surface matters

AI and intangible capital can intensify wealth inequality in at least five ways:

1. returns accrue to owners of models, compute, cloud, data, IP, platforms, and high-skill firms;
2. labor-market gains may concentrate among workers with complementary skills and bargaining power;
3. private-market allocation can reserve the largest upside for already-wealthy households and institutions;
4. network effects and data advantages can raise entry barriers for firms and workers;
5. digital infrastructure can become political and informational leverage.

BIS research using 86 countries over 2010-19 associates AI investment with higher income inequality and higher top-decile income shares, while IMF work models different inequality channels under AI adoption.[S60][S68] World Bank data on AI infrastructure also highlights global concentration in data-center capacity and server access, which matters for cross-country wealth-order comparisons.[S69]

## False passes

### 1. Public-market inclusion is mistaken for broad ownership

Index-fund ownership does not automatically democratize digital wealth if voting, private allocation, founder control, data rights, and strategic infrastructure remain concentrated.

### 2. Wage complementarity is mistaken for ownership access

Some workers may gain from AI complements, but that does not answer who owns the capital, infrastructure, and private upside.

### 3. Consumer access is mistaken for asset access

Using digital services is not the same as owning claims on the platforms, data, compute, or IP that compound from that use.

### 4. Start-up dynamism is mistaken for open circulation

A visible start-up ecosystem can still be closed if entry depends on elite networks, private capital access, family risk buffers, or restricted compute.

## Scoreboard fields

| Field | Ask |
|---|---|
| compute_access_concentration | Who controls high-end compute, cloud credits, and model infrastructure? |
| data_asset_control | Who owns, can monetize, or can exclude others from valuable data? |
| platform_rent_intensity | Are platform fees/terms extracting from workers, sellers, or small firms? |
| private_market_allocation | Can ordinary savers access upside, or only wealthy accredited/private investors? |
| IP_and_model_control | Are IP/model rights becoming durable entry barriers? |
| AI_labor_share_effect | Does adoption reduce labor share or shift bargaining power? |
| digital_political_leverage | Can digital owners shape speech, information, procurement, security, or regulation? |

## Routing consequence

Digital/intangible concentration can route to:

- **predistribution:** bargaining power, training, portable benefits, labor standards, noncompetes, procurement access;
- **competition:** interoperability, data portability, anti-monopoly, platform neutrality, cloud/compute access;
- **public/common wealth:** public compute, public data trusts, open infrastructure, public equity stakes where public value creates private upside;
- **tax and rails:** private-asset valuation, capital-gains realization, international tax coordination, beneficial ownership;
- **political firewalls:** lobbying, media/information control, procurement and regulatory capture.

## Program implication

Do not let the archive become a twentieth-century asset map. Housing, land, and inherited finance remain central, but future top-tail formation may increasingly come from intangibles, compute, data, and private allocations. The scoreboard should therefore ask not only **who owns houses and stocks**, but **who owns the bottlenecks through which new claims are created**.
