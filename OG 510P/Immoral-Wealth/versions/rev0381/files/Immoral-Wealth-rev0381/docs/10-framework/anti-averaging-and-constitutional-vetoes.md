# Anti-averaging and constitutional vetoes

## What this note is for

This note turns one of the archive's oldest guardrails into a **stable constitutional override rule**.
It answers a narrower question than the fuller target note in [`target-selection-rules.md`](target-selection-rules.md):
**what kinds of failure are too central to freedom, entry, non-rule, and governability to be averaged away by strengths elsewhere?**

The archive does not treat the wealth order as a weighted average in which excellence on one surface cancels catastrophe on another.
A society can post respectable share vectors, stronger services, or a softer-looking middle while still failing morally because one decisive constitutional surface remains badly breached: the bottom half still lacks a real floor, wealth already converts into rule, footholds arrive too late, major groups remain excluded, or the ownership order is too hidden to govern.[S13][S15][S142][S18][S19][S23][S31]

## Minimum reading rule

Read the archive **veto-first**.
Near-strengths may mitigate near-weaknesses only inside a zone where the main constitutional minima are already being cleared.
They do **not** cancel categorical failure on a decisive surface.
Opacity should make the reading **harder**, not easier.
And a society should not be called morally acceptable merely because public/social wealth is positive, the middle share is respectable, or one axis has improved while the constitutional breach remains live elsewhere.[S15][S142][S18][S19][S21][S22][S23][S31]

## The five main false passes

### 1. A strong average is mistaken for an acceptable order

A country does not pass merely because several indicators look decent in combination.
The archive rejects moral laundering by aggregation.
If one decisive surface remains badly failed, a flattering average should not rescue the case.

### 2. Softer-looking shares are mistaken for a cleared floor

A respectable middle share, positive public wealth, or somewhat lower top concentration does not clear the case if too much of the bottom half still lives with negative or near-zero net wealth, fragile tenure, weak liquid buffers, or ordinary shock exposure.[S13][S142][S17][S21][S22]

### 3. Household or national averages are mistaken for real inclusion

The archive should not treat household membership or national broadening as enough when person-level title remains weak, intra-household power is badly skewed, or historically subordinated groups remain outside the main asset-building channels.[S27][S28][S29][S30]

### 4. Partial visibility is mistaken for governability

Disclosure, registries, or nominal transparency count for less when they are partial, weakly enforced, strategically incomplete, or too hard to use in practice.
A wealth order that remains too hidden to tax, police, or contest does not pass because some formal reporting exists.[S15][S23]

### 5. One benign cycle is mistaken for a cleared constitutional breach

A temporary rebound, one reform package, or one less-bad snapshot should not clear a decisive veto while reconcentration pressure, repair debt, or closure dynamics remain materially active.[S02][S20][S31]

## The five standing veto families

### 1. Floor veto

Treat this veto as active when too much of the bottom half remains wealth-poor in function: negative or near-zero net wealth is common, liquid buffers are weak, secure tenure is thin, predatory debt remains common, or the archive's floor-pass rule is not broadly met.[S07][S142][S17][S25]

### 2. Rule-conversion veto

Treat this veto as active when concentrated wealth can already translate into political-finance dependence, lobbying advantage, procurement or licensing favoritism, supervisory lenience, media agenda power, or repeated crisis favoritism.
For the dedicated companion doctrine, see [`wealth-to-rule-conversion.md`](wealth-to-rule-conversion.md).[S15][S20][S23][S32][S33]

### 3. Closure-and-timing veto

Treat this veto as active when durable ownership remains too strongly inherited, too delayed, or too closed to entrants without wealthy parents: early footholds are weak, transfer timing is too late, and each new cohort faces harder entry even while incumbent balance sheets look comfortable.[S05][S13][S18][S19]

### 4. Inclusion veto

Treat this veto as active when apparently broad wealth is being laundered through household or national averages while one adult inside many households remains weakly titled or major historically subordinated groups remain structurally excluded from the main channels of safe asset formation.[S27][S28][S29][S30]

### 5. Legibility veto

Treat this veto as active when the main ownership, transfer, land, trust, company, or cross-border channels remain too illegible for serious democratic oversight, taxation, anti-capture enforcement, or crisis loss attribution.[S04][S23]

## Fast use rule

Use the archive in this order:

1. **Screen for vetoes first.**
2. **Only then** compare where the society sits inside or outside the preferred envelope.
3. **Only after that** use weighted judgment across shares, floor quality, public/social wealth, circulation, control, and reconcentration.

Default shortcut:

- **zero clear vetoes** -> weighted comparisons inside the archive's envelope meaningfully matter;
- **one clear veto** -> the society should not be called morally acceptable and should usually move at least into **correction**;
- **multiple vetoes**, or **one veto plus strong wealth-to-rule or opacity pressure** -> the archive should usually harden toward **anti-oligarchy response**.[S15][S23][S31]

This does not eliminate judgment.
It stops a simpler and worse mistake: mistaking a high average score for a constitutionally acceptable order.

## Non-substitution rule

Do **not** let the following excuse a serious constitutional veto:

- positive public wealth,
- a respectable middle share,
- stronger average services,
- one round of ownership broadening,
- partial transparency,
- or one soft year after a harder period.

Those facts may matter **after** the decisive minima are cleared.
They do not by themselves clear the minima.
For the companion rule on nearby variation, see [`acceptable-envelopes-and-substitution-limits.md`](acceptable-envelopes-and-substitution-limits.md).

## Program implication

Once a veto is materially active, the archive should stop treating the relevant repair as decorative cleanup.
Floor repair, anti-capture work, entry reopening, inclusion correction, or legibility building becomes part of the core package rather than a later refinement.
That is why this note sits directly under target choice, measurement, and mode selection.
It tells the archive when weighted balancing should give way to **constitutional override**.

## Bottom line

The archive should now read anti-averaging in this order:

1. **Ask first whether one decisive surface is still badly breached.**
2. **Refuse flattering averages, partial visibility, or one-cycle improvements as substitutes for clearing that breach.**
3. **Use the five veto families to decide whether the case is constitutionally acceptable before weighted comparison begins.**
4. **Harden mode and program posture once a veto remains materially active.**

The point is not to turn every mild imperfection into constitutional emergency.
The point is to stop calling an order acceptable while one of the conditions of ordinary independence, open entry, visible governability, or non-rule is still being systematically denied.[S15][S142][S18][S19][S23][S31]
