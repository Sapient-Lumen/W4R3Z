# Education debt and human-capital encumbrance

## Core claim

Education can be a foothold, but education debt can also be a lien on future threshold moments. The archive should not count degree access as open circulation unless it asks who borrows, on what terms, with what completion risk, and with what repayment consequences.

The U.S. federal student-loan portfolio remains large: Federal Student Aid reported 42.8 million recipients and about $1.7 trillion in federal student loans in March 2026.[S379] The New York Fed's 2026:Q1 household-credit report recorded student-loan serious delinquency at 10.3% of balances, up from 9.6% in the prior quarter.[S108]

## Human-capital false pass

A degree is not automatically a wealth asset. The pass depends on:

- completion and credential value;
- debt amount and interest/fee exposure;
- income-contingency and hardship design;
- default, delinquency, garnishment, tax-refund offset, and credit-report consequences;
- whether family wealth lets some students avoid debt or choose higher-return programs;
- whether borrowers from subordinated groups face unequal repayment burdens after graduation.

The Federal Reserve's SCF-based student-debt work is important because it links student loans to the wider household balance sheet and race/ethnicity measurement problems, rather than treating debt as a standalone education statistic.[S109]

## Threshold moments affected

Education debt can block:

- first lease or deposit;
- car or transport purchase;
- graduate/professional school choice;
- first home purchase;
- business formation;
- childbirth/care timing;
- safe exit from a household;
- retirement saving during early compounding years.

## Certification rule

A case cannot receive an early-foothold pass when the normal route to credentials requires debt that predictably delays or blocks the first independent household, savings buffer, or safe-exit option.

A case cannot receive an anti-averaging pass when family-paid education and borrower-financed education are pooled as the same credential outcome.

## Policy routing

- If debt is driven by tuition/cost exposure: route to public price discipline, grants, living-cost support, and program-level value screens.
- If default is driven by income volatility: route to automatic income-based repayment, interest suspension, hardship pauses, and credit-report protection.
- If group gaps are dominant: route to targeted debt relief, completion support, family-wealth-neutral admissions/aid, and subgroup repayment audits.
- If parent debt is dominant: route to parent-loan limits, grant substitution, and household-level retirement-protection screens.
