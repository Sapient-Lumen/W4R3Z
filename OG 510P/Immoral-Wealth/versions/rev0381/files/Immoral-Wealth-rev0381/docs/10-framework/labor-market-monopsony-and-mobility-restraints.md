# Labor-market monopsony and mobility restraints

Labor-market power is a wealth-order problem when employers, lead firms, or platforms can hold down pay or conditions because workers lack real outside options. This is not limited to explicit noncompetes. It includes local employer concentration, occupational licensing, training-repayment demands, benefits lock-in, immigration sponsorship, no-poach arrangements, platform ratings, customer lists, franchise restrictions, and predictable-hours scarcity.[S225][S226][S232][S233]

## Mobility is a threshold moment

The cube should treat **job switching** as a threshold moment. A worker who cannot switch jobs, start a competing business, refuse a bad schedule, keep benefits during transition, or preserve credentials has a thinner claim to future wealth.

A worker-mobility screen should ask:

1. Are noncompetes, de facto noncompetes, repayment clauses, or no-poach arrangements legally restricted?
2. Are health insurance, pension vesting, immigration status, housing, or childcare so job-tied that exit is not real?
3. Does the relevant local labor market have enough employers for workers to bargain credibly?
4. Can workers carry ratings, data, benefits, licenses, and credentials across employers/platforms?
5. Does antitrust/labor enforcement treat employer buyer power as a wage-and-wealth issue?[S222][S225][S232][S233]

## Certification consequence

Monopsony or mobility-restraint evidence does not automatically make a case captured. It does harden proof burden. A case that relies on private earnings for the floor cannot pass if job switching is not practically available to the people whose savings are supposed to rise.
