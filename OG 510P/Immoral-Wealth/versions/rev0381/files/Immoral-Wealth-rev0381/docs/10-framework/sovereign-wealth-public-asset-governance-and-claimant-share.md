# Sovereign wealth, public asset governance, and claimant share

A sovereign wealth fund or public asset portfolio counts as public counterweight only when it is rule-bound, transparent, professionally governed, politically insulated, and connected to claimant security rather than patronage. A large fund can still fail if residents have no durable claim, withdrawals are discretionary, investment mandates are opaque, or elite projects capture the upside.[S369][S370][S371]

## Public asset pass conditions

- mandate is clear and public;
- investment risk is disclosed over long horizons;
- deposits and withdrawals follow a fiscal rule;
- governance separates professional management from patronage allocation;
- public reports disclose costs, returns, benchmark, voting/stewardship, and risk;
- ordinary claimants receive benefits through dividends, services, stabilization, pensions, or reduced taxes;
- intergenerational distribution is explicit;
- climate and transition risks are treated as long-horizon financial risks.

Public assets do not erase private wealth concentration unless they reduce ordinary dependence and are shielded from capture.
