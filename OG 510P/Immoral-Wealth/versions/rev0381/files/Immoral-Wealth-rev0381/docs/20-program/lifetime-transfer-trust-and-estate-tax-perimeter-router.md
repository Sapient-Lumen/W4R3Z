# Lifetime transfer, trust, and estate-tax perimeter router

Use this router to decide whether top-tail intergenerational transfer machinery requires tax, registry, anti-avoidance, or political-firewall response.

## Audit sequence

1. Estimate lifetime gifts, bequests, and expected inheritances by percentile and group.[S321][S322][S324]
2. Identify estate/gift/GST exemption level, filing threshold, effective tax exposure, and revenue.[S430][S326]
3. Check annual exclusion, direct tuition/medical exclusions, valuation discounts, grantor-trust strategies, GRAT/IDGT/family-entity planning, and intra-family loans.
4. Determine whether trusts can last beyond ordinary perpetuities limits and whether GST tax blocks or permits dynasty structures.[S343]
5. Check beneficial-ownership visibility of trusts, trustees, protectors, settlors, and controllers.
6. Check political/philanthropic/family-office influence when dynastic wealth becomes rule power.

## Routing choices

- lower or progressive estate/gift/GST thresholds;
- stronger gift reporting and valuation rules;
- anti-perpetuity or anti-dynasty trust rules;
- trust beneficial-ownership/control registries;
- valuation discount limits where control is retained;
- estate-tax enforcement and high-wealth audit capacity;
- public early-entry substitutes where private transfers dominate threshold moments.

## Certification rule

If ordinary families face probate and estate-recovery exposure while high-end families can professionalize transfer avoidance and control preservation, Gate 19 fails open-circulation comfort even if measured bequests alone explain only part of wealth inequality.
