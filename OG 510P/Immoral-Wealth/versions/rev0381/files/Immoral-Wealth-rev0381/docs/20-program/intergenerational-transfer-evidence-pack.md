# Intergenerational transfer evidence pack

A Gate 19 case needs evidence at five levels.

## 1. Transfer timing and distribution

- lifetime gifts by age, amount, purpose, and group;
- down-payment help, tuition support, co-signing/guarantees, family business stakes;
- expected and received inheritances;
- transfer value relative to local threshold costs.[S321][S322][S324]

## 2. Administrative conversion

- probate filing rates, cost, delay, and small-estate usage;
- intestacy rates and will access;
- heirs-property prevalence and partition outcomes;
- beneficiary-claim failures for retirement accounts, life insurance, and starter accounts.[S335][S336][S337]

## 3. Late-life preservation

- LTSS spenddown rates;
- Medicaid estate recovery claims, collections, waivers, and demographics;
- guardianship filings, counsel rates, accountings, restoration petitions, and exploitation reports.[S332][S333][S338][S340][S342]

## 4. Family-law person-level control

- property division outcomes by gender/income;
- QDRO completion and retirement-account division;
- child-support pass-through, arrears, enforcement sanctions, and distribution to families;
- legal-aid coverage for divorce, custody, support, and elder/fiduciary matters.[S328][S329][S330][S331]

## 5. High-end perimeter

- estate/gift/GST returns and effective exposure;
- trust filings and duration;
- family-office/trust control visibility;
- valuation discounts and audit adjustments;
- dynasty/planning prevalence and political/philanthropic control signals.[S430][S326][S343]

## Evidence-debt rule

When data are missing because family wealth is private, trusts are opaque, probate/title data are local, or family-law outcomes are sealed, the case should record proof debt. Missingness is not neutral if it hides top-tail transfer machinery or low-wealth title loss.
