# Investor mobility and CBI/RBI screen

## Minimum screen

For any residence/citizenship-by-investment or preferential newcomer regime, record:

1. genuine residence and integration requirements;
2. tax residence consequences;
3. source-of-funds checks;
4. sanctions, corruption, PEP, and money-laundering checks;
5. beneficial-owner and account-reporting implications;
6. property-market effects;
7. revocation and appeal rules;
8. public reporting on approvals, refusals, intermediaries, and contributions;
9. whether ordinary workers, refugees, family members, and long-term residents have less secure pathways than investors.[S203][S204][S205]

## Red flags

- minimal or symbolic residence;
- reliance on intermediaries with opaque compensation;
- acceptance of property purchase as primary public contribution in a constrained housing market;
- tax-residence certificates disconnected from actual residence;
- weak verification of source of wealth;
- legal status that creates access to a broader union or treaty area without shared oversight;
- post-approval privacy that blocks public-interest auditing.

## EU-specific calibration

The CJEU Malta ruling is a hard calibration point: commercialising the grant of nationality of a Member State, and by extension Union citizenship, was held incompatible with EU-law duties of sincere cooperation and mutual trust.[S206]

## Verdict effect

High-risk CBI/RBI does not automatically make an entire wealth order immoral. It does block comfort certification until the case shows that status markets do not function as secrecy, tax, housing, political-access, sanctions, or impunity rails for concentrated wealth.
