# Enforcement budget, staffing, and case selection

A reform package should include an enforcement budget and staffing theory, not merely a statutory command.

## Minimum enforcement budget table

For any top-tail, registry, procurement, or asset-recovery package, specify:

| Capacity | Minimum question | Failure sign |
|---|---|---|
| Staff | Are there enough trained staff for high-complexity cases? | cases age out or only low-complexity cases are pursued |
| Skills | Does the team include valuation, partnership, trust, private-fund, forensic-accounting, data, and litigation skills? | ordinary auditors face elite advisor teams alone |
| Data | Can the agency match tax, registry, property, financial-account, procurement, and beneficial-owner data? | manual searches and fragmented records |
| Selection | Are models tested for high-wealth complexity and bias? | only easy cases or low-income correspondence audits rise |
| Collection | Can assessed liabilities be secured before asset flight? | paper assessments without payment |
| Appeal | Are appeals timely and independent? | enforcement collapses into delay strategy |
| Oversight | Are criteria and outcomes audited without quota abuse? | either political targeting or enforcement paralysis |

## High-wealth enforcement indicators

Use these fields in cases:

- high-income/high-wealth audit workforce trend;
- case-selection model coverage;
- partnership/trust/private-entity expertise;
- nonfiler initiative results;
- recognized-debt collection rate;
- time from selection to assessment;
- time from assessment to collection;
- share of high-complexity cases closed no-change;
- taxpayer-rights safeguards;
- funding durability.

The U.S. case demonstrates why this table matters: Treasury reports major recoveries from targeted wealthy nonfiler and delinquent-debt work, while GAO identifies gaps in research data, model quality, hiring/training, and central management.[S163][S164]
