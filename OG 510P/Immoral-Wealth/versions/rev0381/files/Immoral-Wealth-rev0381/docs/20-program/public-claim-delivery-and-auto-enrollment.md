# Public claim delivery and auto-enrollment

The archive should now treat public-claim delivery as a design problem with moral stakes. A benefit that requires eligible people to discover, interpret, file, wait, appeal, and renew perfectly is not just administratively inefficient. It shifts the proof burden onto those with the least slack.

OECD work on modernising access to social protection emphasizes take-up, outreach, data use, and delivery modernization across countries.[S139] U.S. evidence on EITC participation and SNAP paperwork interruption shows why claim conversion must be measured, not assumed.[S122][S140]

## Default design hierarchy

When the claim is central to floor or early-foothold certification, prefer:

1. **automatic enrollment** when eligibility is already known;
2. **pre-filled claim** when the state has enough data but needs consent or confirmation;
3. **no-wrong-door filing** when multiple agencies can start the same claim;
4. **assisted filing** through trusted public/nonprofit channels;
5. **simple application** only when automation would create privacy, accuracy, or exclusion risks;
6. **complex application** only as a last resort, and never as the only route for protected minima.

## Auto-enrollment pass conditions

Auto-enrollment is not automatically good. It passes only when:

- notice is clear;
- opt-out or correction rights exist;
- data matching is accurate enough and auditable;
- claimants are not exposed to immigration, policing, debt-collection, or household-abuse risk through data sharing;
- payment rails are claimant-controlled;
- errors can be fixed quickly;
- people without stable address, smartphone, bank account, English fluency, or digital literacy are still reached.

## Renewal rule

A floor is not stable if it is repeatedly re-litigated. Renewal should be proportional to the likelihood of changed eligibility and the harm of interruption.

Use this order:

- stable eligibility: automatic continuation;
- slowly changing eligibility: periodic passive confirmation;
- variable eligibility: simplified reporting with grace periods;
- high-error programs: targeted review, not universal burden;
- suspected fraud: individualized due process, not population-wide suspension.

## Delivery package

When conversion failure is central, the opening package should combine:

- outreach and rights notice;
- prefilled or automatic claims;
- safe payment rails;
- fee and seizure protection;
- offline/assisted access;
- error correction;
- subgroup measurement;
- renewal stabilization.

This is a floor package even when it looks like administration. The moral surface is whether the lower half can actually receive and keep the claims the system says they have.
