# School-finance equalization and facilities router

Use this router when the dominant breach is early-entry inequality transmitted through school district wealth, residential segregation, facility quality, or local tax effort.[S238][S239][S240][S242]

## When this lane leads

Lead with school finance when:

- housing wealth determines school access or facilities quality;
- poor/high-need districts tax harder for weaker services;
- state aid equalizes income but not racialized segregation;
- facilities, lead, HVAC, broadband, transport, or special-education capacity create unequal childhood thresholds;
- pandemic or temporary aid masked a recurring formula gap.[S238][S241][S242]

## Opening package

1. Weighted, need-adjusted operating aid.
2. Equalized local tax capacity or regional tax-base sharing.
3. Facilities capital that does not require poor districts to overmatch.
4. Poor-household property-tax relief that does not starve districts.
5. Race/place/disability outcome audit.
6. Maintenance-of-effort and post-relief cliff guardrails.

## No-pass conditions

A case cannot pass early-foothold or group-veto gates when school finance lets incumbent property wealth buy a superior public childhood while excluded places face lower capital, thinner staffing, or higher tax effort. The corrective package should be treated as wealth policy, not merely education policy.
