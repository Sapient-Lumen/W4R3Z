# Intergenerational transfer gate and family-wealth scorecard

## Gate 19 — intergenerational transfer, family-law allocation, and estate finality

A case cannot pass open circulation when transfer machinery gives some families early, protected, tax-optimized, title-final wealth while others face late, cloudy, spenddown-exposed, court-dependent, or household-gatekept claims.

Gate 19 asks: **Can ordinary people receive, keep, control, divide, protect, and transmit modest wealth across family transitions without needing dynastic planning, expensive counsel, or household gatekeeper permission?**

## Scorecard fields

| Field | Pass | Warning | Fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| inheritance/lifetime transfer access | public/social substitutes offset family transfer gaps | transfer gaps visible but partly offset | threshold moments depend on parental/family transfers |
| bequest expectation gap | low expectation inequality or strong substitutes | large gaps but late-life relevance uncertain | expected inheritance maps strongly onto class/race/caste entry |
| early lifetime gift gate | public grants/loans/guarantees replace family gates | partial substitutes | deposits, tuition, guarantees, or business stakes depend on parents |
| estate/gift/GST perimeter | high-end transfers visible and taxed/limited | high exemption or planning gaps | dynasty planning shields top-tail transfer power |
| trust visibility | controllers and beneficiaries visible to tax/BO systems | partial visibility | trusts hide control or rule-making influence |
| probate access/timing | low-cost, fast, assisted administration | delay/legal-cost burden | probate cost destroys modest estates |
| intestacy/title finality | title clears and heirs can defend assets | title cloud risk | heirs property or intestacy makes ownership unusable |
| partition protection | UPHPA-like protections and legal help | adopted but weak implementation | forced-sale partition threatens family land |
| Medicaid estate recovery | narrow, transparent, hardship-protected | state variation | public care recovery consumes modest home equity |
| caregiver inheritance recognition | caregivers protected without litigation | partial | unpaid care produces poverty while assets pass elsewhere |
| guardianship due process | counsel, notice, less-restrictive alternatives, review | uneven | control can be removed without practical defense |
| fiduciary oversight | accountings, audits, restitution, reporting | partial | fiduciary abuse can drain assets undetected |
| elder exploitation response | trusted contacts, holds, SARs, recovery pathways | partial | exploitation losses destroy late-life wealth |
| divorce property division | person-level assets convert cleanly | legal assistance needed | rights exist but fail through cost/timing/QDRO complexity |
| child support pass-through | collections reach children/families | partial pass-through | collections repay government or arrears trap payors |

## Certification effects

- Gate 19 hardens Gate 3 because early lifetime transfers often decide deposit, tuition, business, and mobility moments.
- Gate 19 hardens Gate 4 because inheritance, probate, and family-law systems can preserve racialized/gendered/group closure.
- Gate 19 hardens Gate 5 because care spenddown, estate recovery, probate cost, and family-law litigation can wash out modest assets.
- Gate 19 hardens Gate 7 because public care and social inheritance only count when they do not claw back modest family wealth.
- Gate 19 hardens Gate 9 because inheritance and family-law rights must convert into title, accounts, payments, and person-level control.
- Gate 19 hardens Gate 18 because recovery after shock often depends on whether assets are exempt, titled, inherited, divided, or controlled.

## Opening package

Start with public early-entry substitutes, will/probate/title legal aid, simplified small-estate administration, heirs-property protection, estate-recovery hardship and scope limits, fiduciary accounting and counsel rights, elder-exploitation transaction safeguards, QDRO/retirement-division assistance, full child-support pass-through to families, arrears reset for uncollectible government-owed debt, and high-end transfer perimeter hardening.
