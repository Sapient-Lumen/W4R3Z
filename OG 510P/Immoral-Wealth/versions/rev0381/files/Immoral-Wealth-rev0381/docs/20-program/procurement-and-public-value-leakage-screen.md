# Procurement and public-value leakage screen

Use this screen whenever a case counts public wealth, social investment, infrastructure, state-owned enterprises, public housing, natural-resource rents, sovereign funds, or emergency spending as a counterweight.

## Red flags

- single-bid or no-bid awards without published justification;
- politically connected suppliers or common owners among bidders;
- no beneficial-owner disclosure;
- weak contract publication;
- repeated cost overruns or change orders;
- no debarment or conflict enforcement;
- emergency powers continued after emergency;
- privatization with public downside and private upside;
- missing asset-recovery tools.

## Minimum evidence pack

1. procurement law and emergency-exception rules;
2. open contracting data or equivalent publication;
3. beneficial-owner disclosure rules for bidders and subcontractors;[S183]
4. conflict-of-interest and related-party checks;
5. audit findings and sanctions;
6. asset-recovery law and outcomes;[S182]
7. evidence that public service quality or common-asset value was preserved.

## Verdict effect

- `pass`: public/common wealth can count toward counterweight.
- `watch`: counterweight counts only with leakage discount.
- `warning`: public wealth is vulnerable; route to governance hardening.
- `fail`: public wealth cannot be counted as a counterweight because it is a capture channel.
