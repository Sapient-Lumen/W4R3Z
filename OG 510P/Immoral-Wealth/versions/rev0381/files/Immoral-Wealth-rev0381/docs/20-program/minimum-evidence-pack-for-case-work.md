# Minimum evidence pack for case work

## What this note is for

This note gives the archive a **minimum evidence standard for real-case judgment**.
It sits between [`case-application-protocol.md`](case-application-protocol.md), which says **how a case memo should be written**, and [`measurement-and-scoreboard.md`](measurement-and-scoreboard.md), which says **how a case should be read once evidence exists**.
Its job is to answer a narrower question:
**what is the thinnest evidence pack that makes a country, city, sector, or cohort memo worth trusting at all?**[S02][S13][S15][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33]

The archive does **not** require a maximal data room before it can think.
But it also should not let a memo claim confidence on the basis of one wealth-share table, one household average, or one policy announcement.
A serious case can begin with incomplete evidence, yet it must say **which minimum blocks are present, which are proxied, and which remain missing enough to harden the burden of proof against a soft pass**.[S13][S15][S17][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## Minimum operating rule

A case memo should usually not claim more than **provisional** confidence unless it can show **seven evidence blocks** in some form:

1. distribution shares and public/common counterweight,
2. floor and holder reality,
3. the dominant washout channel,
4. top-tail control and visibility,
5. circulation and timing,
6. group and person-level distribution,
7. and governing rails / path credibility.[S02][S07][S13][S15][S142][S17][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33][S34][S36][S41][S43]

Not every block must be measured perfectly before the archive can judge.
But every block must be handled explicitly.
For each one, the memo should say whether the block is **measured directly**, **covered only by a proxy**, **contested across sources**, or **still materially missing**.
Missingness and conflict are part of the verdict discipline, not reasons to act as if the case were cleaner than it is. For source precedence and disagreement handling, use [`source-order-and-conflict-resolution.md`](source-order-and-conflict-resolution.md).[S01][S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## The seven required blocks

### 1. Distribution shares and public/common counterweight

Every serious case needs one compact picture of the ownership order itself.
At minimum that means the nearest available reading of:
- bottom 50 / middle 40 / next 9 / top 1 wealth shares,
- the top 0.1% where visibility permits,
- and net public wealth or the nearest credible public/social counterweight proxy.[S02][S03][S13][S142][S20]

A case may proceed with imperfect granularity.
But if the memo cannot even show the broad ownership split and the strength or weakness of public/common counterweight, it is not yet doing the archive's core job.
It is describing atmosphere rather than a wealth order.[S02][S13][S142][S20][S23]

### 2. Floor and holder reality

Every serious case needs evidence on whether the lower half has **real footholds** rather than formal inclusion.
At minimum, the memo should show the nearest available reading of:
- positive-net-wealth rates or debt-distress at the lower half,
- liquid-buffer or fragility evidence,
- housing security / affordability / adequacy near the floor,
- and whether common footholds are actually holdable after carrying costs, claims friction, and ordinary shocks.[S07][S13][S17][S19][S21][S22][S24][S41][S42][S43]

This block exists because the archive will not let a flattering ownership table stand in for resilient person-usable security.
If the lower half cannot keep, defend, or survive ordinary life with what it notionally owns, the memo should count the case as morally weaker than the headline shares suggest.[S17][S19][S21][S22][S41][S42][S43]

### 3. Dominant washout channel

Every serious case needs one explicit reading of the channel most likely to undo visible gains.
Usually that means one or more of:
- housing and land capitalization,
- debt service and refinancing pressure,
- monopoly, fee, or markup extraction,
- subsidy capture,
- or repeated public downside absorption.[S07][S10][S20][S31][S32][S33][S34][S36]

This block does not require a giant model.
It requires a plain statement of which washout path is live and what evidence supports that diagnosis.
Without that, a memo will often recommend measures that look progressive on paper while the main upward escalator keeps running.[S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33][S36]

### 4. Top-tail control and visibility

Every serious case needs some evidence not just on who owns wealth, but on who **controls** strategically important wealth and whether that control is governably visible.
At minimum, the memo should try to show:
- top-tail concentration at the highest visible level,
- beneficial-ownership or controller visibility,
- concentration in key sectors or assets,
- and any salient wealth-to-rule markers such as lobbying dominance, media leverage, procurement favoritism, regulatory tailoring, or rescue asymmetry.[S15][S20][S23][S26][S31][S32][S33]

Where the top tail is opaque, the archive should not drift into comfort.
Opacity is itself a reason to harden the proof burden against easy certification.
A memo may still proceed, but it should say that the case is being judged under weakened visibility conditions.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

### 5. Circulation and timing

Every serious case needs some evidence on whether footholds arrive **early enough** and **widely enough** to loosen dynastic closure.
At minimum, the memo should try to show:
- inheritance or transfer concentration,
- age at first durable foothold or the nearest usable timing proxy,
- cohort wealth gaps,
- and whether new adult life chances remain highly parent-dependent.[S05][S18][S19][S20]

This block matters because a society can look milder in annual-flow terms while still operating as a late-entry regime in which secure footholds arrive mainly through family transfer or after decades of exclusion.
The archive does not treat that as genuine openness.[S05][S18][S19][S20]

### 6. Group and person-level distribution

Every serious case needs at least one disaggregation pass.
At minimum, a memo should try to show whether gains and footholds are being denied or diluted across major excluded groups and whether household-level reporting is hiding person-level dependency inside families.[S21][S22][S28][S29][S30][S43]

A case may lack perfect subgroup data.
But if the memo cannot say anything about group stratification or person-level control, it should state that limitation and resist clean certification.
The archive does not allow household averages or national means to erase structured exclusion.[S21][S22][S28][S29][S30][S43]

### 7. Governing rails and path credibility

Every serious case needs some evidence on whether the proposed move is actually governable.
At minimum, the memo should show the nearest available reading of:
- fiscal room and distributional budget shape,
- registry / delivery / enforcement rails,
- anti-capture integrity,
- and whether the proposed opening package can begin now, build rails while moving, or remains honestly gated.[S04][S15][S23][S24][S25][S31][S32][S33][S39]

This block keeps the archive from two opposite errors:
claiming that a paper package is already real when delivery rails are absent, and using weak state capacity as a blanket excuse to postpone the easiest floor, labor, housing, or foothold-opening moves.[S04][S07][S09][S12][S21][S22][S23][S24][S25][S31][S39]

## Proxy rule

The archive allows **proxy evidence**, but only under three conditions:

1. the memo says clearly that the reading is a proxy rather than a direct measure,
2. the proxy runs in the right moral direction for the question at hand,
3. and the memo explains what important thing the proxy still cannot see.[S13][S15][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31]

Examples of acceptable-but-limited proxy use:
- using housing-cost burden and eviction / arrears pressure as a proxy for weak floor reality when direct liquid-buffer data are poor,
- using estate or transfer concentration as a proxy for circulation closure when cohort timing data are weak,
- using ownership registries plus sector concentration as a proxy for top-tail control where controller maps are incomplete,
- using benefit take-up, backlog, or error-recovery data as a proxy for weak claimability or operability.[S05][S07][S15][S18][S19][S23][S24][S25][S31]

Proxy use should therefore narrow the memo, not inflate its confidence.
When too many blocks are proxy-only, the verdict may still be severe, but the memo should speak more cautiously about fine-grained package design.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## The five main false completions

### 1. One share table is treated as the whole case

A memo is not evidence-complete merely because it can show top and bottom shares.
Without floor reality, washout, control, circulation, and path credibility, the archive still does not know whether the case is broadly owned, holdable, governable, or durable.[S13][S15][S17][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23]

### 2. Household wealth is treated as person-level security

A memo is not evidence-complete when wealth is measured only at the household level and the case says nothing about title, control, exit security, or bargaining power inside households.[S28][S29][S30][S43]

### 3. Policy announcement is treated as path credibility

A memo is not evidence-complete merely because a government announced a package.
If delivery, registries, visibility, enforcement, or anti-capture rails are absent, the archive should count the package as aspirational rather than as governed fact.[S04][S15][S23][S24][S25][S31][S32][S33]

### 4. Missing top-tail visibility softens the verdict

A memo is not evidence-complete when the most powerful owners and controllers are still partly hidden.
That condition should usually make the archive less willing to certify the case, not more willing.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

### 5. Too much evidence is gathered in the wrong place

A memo is not evidence-complete just because it has many tables.
If the evidence room is thick on macro averages and thin on floor integrity, timing, washout, and control, the archive is still missing the morally decisive surfaces.[S13][S17][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23]

## Fast use rule

Use this note in five steps:

1. **Check for all seven blocks before writing a confident memo.**  
   Mark each one as direct, proxy, or materially missing.
2. **Refuse false completeness.**  
   One strong block does not erase five weak ones.
3. **Let missingness harden proof burdens.**  
   Especially on top-tail control, excluded groups, and person-level access.[S15][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33]
4. **Build the memo from the strongest morally decisive blocks first.**  
   Start with the verdict, target distance, dominant breach, and washout channel, using the minimum evidence pack to support each one.
5. **Keep evidence collection proportional.**  
   Gather enough to judge the case honestly, not so much that every application becomes a new archive.

## Bottom line

The archive should now be able to say two things more clearly in future case work:

1. **what a case memo must report**, via [`case-application-protocol.md`](case-application-protocol.md),
2. **and what evidence has to exist before that report deserves confidence**, via this note.

That keeps future case writing both thinner and more honest.
The archive does not need maximal measurement before it can judge, but it does need a **minimum evidence pack** strong enough to stop atmospheric description, policy theater, and share-table tunnel vision from standing in for real diagnosis.[S13][S15][S17][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## rev0304 additions to the minimum pack

A case memo now needs five additional fields before it can ask for more than a provisional verdict:

1. **Case type:** rich asset-price democracy, social-democratic housing-constrained, public-housing asset state, middle-income land/legibility, resource-dividend public wealth, climate-insurance stress surface, or another clearly named type.
2. **Threshold moments:** at least three decisive moments from [`threshold-moment-registry.md`](threshold-moment-registry.md), scored as open, watch, family-gated, gatekeeper-dependent, self-eroding, or missing.
3. **Scoreboard instance:** a JSON file validated against [`scoreboard-schema.json`](scoreboard-schema.json).
4. **Fastest washout:** the concrete channel most likely to reabsorb gains in the case.
5. **Proof-debt priority:** missing fields ordered by whether they can change verdict, route, veto, instrument feasibility, or description.

A case lacking these fields may still be useful as a note, but it should not be treated as a calibrated case.

## rev0307 additions to the minimum pack

A case memo that relies on benefits, tax credits, public dividends, starter accounts, disaster aid, title correction, payment rails, or any other nominal claim now needs a **conversion evidence block** before it can ask for certification.

The block should report at least five fields:

1. **Take-up and awareness:** whether the eligible population actually receives the claim, or whether opt-in, paperwork, recertification, or information burdens leave a material eligible group outside the system.[S122][S123][S139][S140]
2. **Identity and documentation:** whether people can prove eligibility, personhood, residence, ownership, occupancy, or control without circular requirements that exclude the people the program is supposed to reach.[S124][S125][S130][S131][S132]
3. **Account and payment rails:** whether the claim can be paid and held safely through accessible accounts, cards, mobile money, public wallets, or offline alternatives.[S120][S263][S126][S127][S135][S136]
4. **Leakage, offset, and fee exposure:** whether overdraft, account, check-cashing, tax-prep, debt-offset, seizure, or intermediary charges materially shrink the claim before it becomes durable wealth.[S263][S262][S129]
5. **Renewal, error correction, and person-level control:** whether recipients can keep the claim, fix errors, appeal denials, avoid silent lapse, and exercise control independent of household gatekeepers.[S120][S124][S126][S133][S134][S139][S140]

A case can still be diagnosed when these data are incomplete, but it should carry evidence debt. The stricter rule is: **a policy cannot pass as wealth-building merely because it creates an entitlement, account, grant, or transfer. It must also show that the intended person can convert the claim into usable, retainable, and governable wealth.**[S120][S263][S126][S262][S130][S133][S139][S140]


## rev0308 added minimum: measurement pack

Every full country case should now include a measurement pack: source family, wealth perimeter, year, unit, treatment of pensions/public claims, top-tail audit status, national-accounts reconciliation status, survey-quality warnings, beneficial-owner visibility status, and valuation/liquidity haircut status. Subsystem cases must explain which parts are out of scope.[S142][S143][S150]


## rev0309 enforcement/remedy evidence addendum

Every active case should now ask whether the evidence pack includes:

- standing and remedy access for claimants;
- legal aid, ombud, representative action, or public enforcement where individual enforcement is unrealistic;
- enforcement-agency staff, skill, budget, data, audit, and collection capacity;
- legal attack-surface assessment for the leading instruments;
- beneficial-owner registry coverage, verification, and durability;
- exchange-of-information and offshore rails where top-tail flight is material;
- procurement/privatization leakage evidence where public/common wealth is counted;
- asset-recovery tools and outcomes where corruption or capture is material.

Missing enforcement evidence is not neutral. It can block certification under Gate 11.

## rev0310 addendum — democratic-power evidence pack

For any case where wealth-to-rule conversion, housing/place closure, media agenda power, or reform survival is material, add the Gate 12 evidence pack:

- campaign, party, independent-expenditure, and third-party-spending data;
- donor concentration, source-of-funds checks, and dark-money or donor-obscuring vehicles;
- lobbying-register coverage, clients, subjects, meetings, advisory panels, and sanctions;
- funding transparency for think tanks, sponsored expertise, third-party campaigns, and digital ads;
- media ownership, concentration, state-advertising, and public-service media governance;
- local-land-use veto points and renter / would-be-resident voice where housing or place is live;
- enforcement-body independence, investigatory powers, data access, budget, and sanctions;
- post-enactment countermobilization and repeal/hollowing indicators.[S184][S186][S188][S189][S190][S193][S194][S195][S198]

Missing data is hard evidence debt when the missingness is produced by donor secrecy, opaque nonprofits, shell entities, foreign-linked vehicles, sponsorship opacity, or fragmented local records.



## rev0311 addendum — jurisdictional mobility evidence pack

When cross-border mobility may affect the verdict, add:

- high-wealth taxpayer/resident stock and flow data;
- asset location and ownership vehicle data;
- tax-residence enforcement evidence;
- exit-tax/deemed-disposal/source-tax/trailing-residence legal rails;
- CRS/AEOI and beneficial ownership coverage;
- CBI/RBI or preferential newcomer regime design and uptake;
- migrant claimant-perimeter evidence for labor, injury, health, housing, pension/end-of-service, remedy, and voice;
- remittance corridor costs, recruitment debt, wage-claim, deportation/status shock, and recipient-control evidence;
- capital-flow and FX stress indicators where macro stability is implicated.[S203][S207][S213][S214][S216][S218][S219]
## Household-market-extraction evidence pack — Gate 16

For any case whose pass depends on disposable income, account ownership, vehicle access, care access, or modest savings, collect: household debt and delinquency; overdraft/NSF and late-fee incidence; high-cost small-dollar credit exposure; account/nonbank use; BNPL/EWA repeat-use and total-obligation data; auto negative equity and repossession; all-in price and cancellation-friction rules; childcare price/supply and labor-force effects; long-term-care out-of-pocket costs, asset tests, and family-care burden; complaint/redress outcomes; and subgroup incidence.[S260][S262][S263][S264][S266][S273][S277]
