# Wage-floor, collective-bargaining, and wealth-formation router

This router decides when the opening package should lead with pre-distribution rather than transfer, tax, or housing instruments.

## Lead with workplace power when

- wage growth does not create saveable room;
- union or bargaining coverage is too thin to discipline wage-setting;
- minimum wages are below ordinary living-cost thresholds;
- workers have unstable hours, unpredictable scheduling, or inadequate benefits;
- wage theft or retaliation prevents rights from converting;
- low-wage sectors are also high-rent, high-debt, high-care, or high-immigration-status-risk sectors.[S220][S221][S230][S231][S234]

## Do not overclaim

A higher wage floor or stronger bargaining regime does not automatically fix housing scarcity, top-tail rule, parent-backed early entry, or climate risk. It does, however, change whether people have income left to save, whether debt becomes avoidable, and whether public transfers are forced to compensate for extractive labor markets.

## Minimum evidence pack

Collect: wage distribution, hours volatility, benefit access, collective-bargaining coverage, union election/remedy conversion, wage-theft claims and recovery, local labor-market concentration, noncompete prevalence, misclassification exposure, and worker-ownership coverage.[S220][S222][S230][S231][S232][S235][S237]
