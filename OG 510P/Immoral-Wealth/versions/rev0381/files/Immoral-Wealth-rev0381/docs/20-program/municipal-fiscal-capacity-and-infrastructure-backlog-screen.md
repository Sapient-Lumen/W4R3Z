# Municipal fiscal capacity and infrastructure backlog screen

This screen asks whether the local public asset base is maintained well enough to function as public wealth. It is especially important where the case relies on municipal services, water systems, sewer systems, streets, stormwater, schools, public buildings, parks, or transit as substitutes for private wealth.[S245][S246][S247][S248][S249]

## Minimum variables

- recurring revenue growth relative to service cost growth;
- reserves, debt service, credit access, and legal revenue flexibility;
- capital-improvement plan and asset-condition inventory;
- water/wastewater/stormwater need relative to rate base;
- grant application, match, reimbursement, engineering, and procurement capacity;
- workforce capacity and maintenance backlog;
- incidence of fees, assessments, rates, fines, and deferred repairs by income/place group.[S246][S258]

## Fail triggers

- local officials defer maintenance because operating services and capital needs compete;
- small or poor jurisdictions cannot apply for or match grants;
- public assets deteriorate while household utility rates, fees, and special assessments rise;
- infrastructure failure creates health, time, repair, or business costs for ordinary households;
- climate risk increases maintenance or borrowing costs faster than the local tax base can absorb.[S245][S246][S257][S258]

## Package route

Pair capital grants with operating capacity. Infrastructure money without local staff, engineering, procurement, match relief, long-term rate affordability, and maintenance revenue can create a grant-announcement false pass.
