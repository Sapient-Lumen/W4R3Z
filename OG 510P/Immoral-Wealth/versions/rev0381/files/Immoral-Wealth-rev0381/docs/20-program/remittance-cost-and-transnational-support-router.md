# Remittance-cost and transnational-support router

## Diagnostic question

Are remittances creating durable wealth, or are they replacing a failed floor?

## Route table

| Finding | Route |
|---|---|
| high transfer cost or FX spread | payment competition, low-cost public/digital rails, fee disclosure |
| recruitment debt | licensing, joint liability, recruitment-fee bans, cross-border enforcement |
| wage theft or unpaid end-of-service benefits | labor inspection, escrow, wage-protection systems, portable claims |
| family support replaces public floor | strengthen local floor, health/care/pension claims, emergency aid |
| recipient lacks person-level control | account access, gender-sensitive payment rails, legal identity, safe accounts |
| deportation/status shock interrupts support | portability, savings protection, legal-aid and transition claims |
| remittances fund footholds | protect against land/title fraud, fee leakage, and household gatekeeper capture |

## Evidence

Use World Bank remittance-scale and price data, corridor-specific costs, household surveys, and migration-status evidence. Do not count remittance inflows as public wealth or floor adequacy without conversion evidence.[S218][S219]
