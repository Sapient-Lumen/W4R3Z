# Source order and conflict resolution

## What this note is for

Real-case work in this archive will often face **partial, lagged, or disagreeing evidence**.
One source will see broad wealth shares well but miss the very top tail.
Another will see housing stress, debt pressure, or take-up failure well but say much less about controllers and dynastic concentration.
A third will describe announced policy beautifully while saying little about lived incidence or delivery reality.

The archive therefore needs a compact rule for **which source should lead on which question, and what to do when sources conflict**.
Its aim is to keep future country, city, sector, or cohort memos **thin, cited, and non-atmospheric** without faking precision, averaging away disagreement, or letting source conflict become permission for soft certification.[S01][S13][S15][S142][S20][S23][S31]

## Minimum operating rule

When evidence is thin or conflicting, a serious case memo should usually do **six things at once**:

1. **choose the lead source by moral surface, not by convenience or rhetorical friendliness,**
2. **prefer the source that sees the relevant failure most directly,** even if that source is less tidy than a flattering aggregate,
3. **treat known undercapture as undercapture,** not as a clean lower-concentration finding,
4. **never average conflicting sources into fake consensus** when they are measuring different things or when one is a structural lower bound,
5. **mark each claim as direct, proxy, or contested** and say why,
6. and let **opacity or conflict harden proof burdens** for certification more than they harden proof burdens for concern.[S01][S13][S15][S142][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31]

The archive's standing rule is therefore simple:
**use the source closest to the morally decisive surface, keep visible disagreement visible, and let missingness or undercapture make the case harder to certify rather than easier to excuse.**

## The six main false passes

### 1. The cleanest number is treated as the truest number

A case should count less seriousness when it selects the tidiest table rather than the source that best sees the relevant wealth surface.
A smooth household survey table can be useful, but not if it is being used to overrule better top-tail or controller evidence.[S01][S13][S142][S23]

### 2. Conflicting sources are averaged into a fake midpoint

The archive should count less honesty when disagreement is handled by taking the midpoint between unlike sources.
That often launders structural undercounting, timing mismatch, or surface mismatch into a false impression of careful balance.[S01][S15][S142][S20][S23]

### 3. Source disagreement is treated as a reason to go soft

A case should count less realism when opacity or measurement conflict is used to postpone judgment indefinitely.
Where the upper tail, controller structure, or transfer pathways are hard to see, the archive should usually become **less willing to certify comfort**, not more willing.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

### 4. Stock sources are asked to answer holder-reality questions

A wealth-share table does not by itself answer whether lower-half households can withstand ordinary shocks, keep claims through transitions, or avoid spend-down traps.
The archive should count less seriousness when stock-distribution sources are asked to settle floor-integrity questions they cannot directly see.[S13][S17][S21][S22][S25][S39][S139][S41]

### 5. Policy announcement sources are treated as incidence sources

A budget statement, statutory schedule, or reform announcement does not by itself show whether gains stick, are capitalised upward, or are delivered through workable rails.
The archive should count less seriousness when legal or budget text substitutes for lived readback.[S31][S34][S36][S37]

### 6. Household or national aggregates are treated as enough for excluded groups or person-level power

A case should count less completeness when national means, household balances, or broad ownership shares are allowed to speak for excluded groups, individual title, or bargaining power inside households.
Those require more disaggregated or person-level evidence than the broad aggregate alone can provide.[S21][S22][S28][S29][S30][S43]

## The standing source order by surface

### 1. Distribution shares and public/common counterweight

For the ownership order itself, the archive should usually lead with **distributional national-accounts style series and harmonized wealth-share sources** that explicitly try to align household wealth with national aggregates and recover the upper tail better than household surveys alone can.[S01][S02][S13][S142]

Use broad household surveys here as supporting evidence for composition and lower-half conditions, but do **not** let them silently overrule better top-share or public-wealth readings when survey undercapture is the likely problem.[S01][S13][S142]

### 2. Floor and holder reality

For floor integrity, the lead source should usually be the one closest to **lived holder conditions**:
financial-fragility evidence, debt stress, insolvency or arrears evidence, housing-cost pressure, living-space adequacy, take-up and access friction, and asset-poverty or minimum-asset evidence.[S07][S17][S21][S22][S24][S25][S39][S139][S41][S42][S43][S45][S50][S51]

The archive should therefore prefer direct evidence on fragility and claim-holding over broad net-worth averages when the question is whether the lower half actually has a real floor.[S17][S21][S22][S24][S25][S39][S139][S41][S45][S50]

### 3. Washout and incidence

For washout, the lead source should usually be the one that can see **where visible gains leak back upward**:
housing capitalization, rent or fee pressure, debt-service burdens, monopoly or markup extraction, upside-down subsidy, or repeated public downside absorption.[S07][S10][S20][S31][S32][S33][S34][S36][S37][S42][S46][S197]

That means statutory progressivity or announced generosity should count for less than incidence, pricing, capitalization, and readback evidence when the archive is asking whether the gain really sticks.[S31][S34][S36][S37]

### 4. Top-tail control and visibility

For top-tail power, the archive should usually lead with the source closest to **controller visibility**:
beneficial-ownership and tax-transparency evidence, registry and controller evidence, sector concentration, stewardship concentration, lobbying and integrity evidence, and public records on repeated rescue privilege or downside socialization.[S15][S23][S26][S31][S32][S33][S54]

A source that sees broad shares well but cannot see wrappers, controllers, or hidden concentration is not enough on its own for this surface.
Top-tail power is not only about owning wealth; it is about being able to direct, shield, or convert it into rule.[S15][S23][S26][S31][S32][S33]

### 5. Circulation and timing

For inherited closure, timing, and early footholds, the archive should usually lead with the source closest to **transfer concentration and cohort passage**:
inheritance and transfer evidence, intergenerational opportunity evidence, cohort timing, and measures that show whether secure footholds arrive mainly through family position or only very late in life.[S05][S18][S19][S27][S47][S52]

A cross-sectional wealth stock can support this surface, but it cannot settle it by itself.
The archive wants to know not only who owns what now, but how adult life chances are being opened or closed across cohorts.[S05][S18][S19][S47][S52]

### 6. Group and person-level distribution

For excluded-group passage and intra-household power, the archive should usually lead with **disaggregated and person-level evidence** wherever available: gendered asset control, pension gaps, title and ownership access, and other direct evidence on who actually controls and can exit with the claim.[S21][S22][S28][S29][S30][S43]

National means and household totals can remain in the memo, but they should count as incomplete on this surface unless they are paired with person-level or disaggregated evidence.[S21][S22][S28][S29][S30][S43]

### 7. Governing rails and path credibility

For whether a package is actually governable, the archive should usually lead with the source closest to **delivery, enforcement, integrity, and readback**:
budgeting practice, registry and visibility capacity, take-up and access administration, anti-capture integrity, and evidence on whether difficult tools are matched to real rails.[S15][S23][S24][S31][S39][S139]

The archive should therefore give less weight to announced intent than to evidence that the measure can actually be delivered, checked, and kept real after passage.[S15][S23][S24][S31][S39][S139]

## Conflict-handling rule

When sources conflict, sort the conflict into **three types**.

### 1. Surface mismatch

Sometimes two sources are both useful because they are answering **different questions**.
A distributional-accounts series may be best for broad wealth shares while fragility, housing, or insolvency sources are best for floor reality.
Do **not** force those into one synthetic number.
Keep both and say which moral surface each one is carrying.[S01][S13][S142][S17][S21][S22][S24][S25]

### 2. Structural lower-bound conflict

Sometimes one source is a known or likely **lower bound** on concentration, opacity, or controller power.
When that happens, the archive should not average it equally with a source built to recover more of the hidden or upper-tail surface.
Use the weaker source as a floor or understating check, not as a veto against the stronger reading.[S01][S13][S15][S142][S23]

### 3. Genuine same-surface contest

Sometimes two serious sources are trying to measure the **same surface** and still disagree.
In that case the memo should state the bracket plainly, prefer the reading with the cleaner fit to the archive's moral question, and explain what remains uncertain.
The archive may still judge the case, but it should avoid false decimal certainty and should often harden the proof burden for any comforting verdict.[S15][S20][S23][S31]

## Fast use rule

Use this note in six steps:

1. **Pick a lead source for each evidence block, not one master source for the whole memo.**  
   One case usually needs different leaders for shares, floor reality, washout, control, timing, group passage, and rails.
2. **Label each important claim as direct, proxy, or contested.**  
   That keeps thin case work honest without turning it into a giant methods appendix.
3. **Do not average unlike sources into one comfort number.**  
   Keep surface mismatch visible instead.
4. **Treat likely undercapture as a lower bound, not as reassurance.**  
   Especially on top shares, opacity, controller structure, and large transfers.[S01][S15][S142][S23]
5. **Let conflict harden certification more than concern.**  
   The archive should usually become less willing to say "near-pass" when key surfaces are still blurred.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]
6. **Keep policy direction tied to robust findings, not fake precision.**  
   A memo can still say which breach dominates and which opening package fits even when the exact decimal is contested.

## rev0308 source conflict rule

When wealth estimates conflict by source family, do not average them. Label each source family, state the perimeter, produce a moral interval, and route the verdict through the top-tail audit. Survey-only estimates are demoted when administrative, rich-list, or tax-capitalized evidence shows materially higher top concentration.[S146][S150][S160]
