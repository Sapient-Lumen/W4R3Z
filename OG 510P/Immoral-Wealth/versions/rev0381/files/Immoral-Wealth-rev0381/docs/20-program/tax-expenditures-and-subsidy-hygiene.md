# Tax expenditures and subsidy hygiene

## What this note is for

This note turns one of the archive's quietest but most persistent warnings into a **full stable subsidy-hygiene / hidden-support stack**.
A society can tighten headline tax rates, expand transfers, or announce new entry programs and still rebuild incumbent wealth if large parts of the state keep subsidising already-advantaged balance sheets through deductions, exclusions, preferential rates, underpriced credit, buyer support, or badly designed “asset-building” incentives.
So the archive treats **tax expenditures and subsidy hygiene** as part of the main wealth program rather than as a technical appendix on the revenue side of the budget.[S31][S36][S37]

The point is broader than classic tax expenditures alone.
The same moral problem appears whenever public support is routed through a form that quietly scales with existing asset position, tax liability, borrowing capacity, or provider pricing power.
A measure can therefore look like help for savers, homeowners, or families while functioning in practice as a refill mechanism for upper-tier wealth, a bid subsidy for constrained markets, or a soft protection device for incumbent owners and financial intermediaries.[S31][S36][S37]

## Minimum operating rule

A serious subsidy-hygiene package should usually do **seven things at once**:

1. put major **tax expenditures and wealth-relevant subsidies on the main fiscal ledger** rather than treating them as neutral defaults,
2. publish the **beneficiary and capture profile** before calling the support egalitarian,
3. favor delivery forms that do **not scale automatically with tax rate, balance size, mortgage size, or borrowing capacity**,
4. test whether support is **opening entry or merely capitalising into prices, rents, fees, and incumbent valuations**,
5. redirect public help toward **entrants, lower-wealth households, and person-usable footholds** rather than toward already-thick balance sheets,
6. place major supports on a **review, sunset, and redesign cycle** instead of letting them compound invisibly for decades,
7. and read hidden support together with the wider wealth program on **housing, retirement saving, tax design, budgeting, and public/social counterweight**.[S22][S31][S36][S37][S41]

No deduction, exclusion, concessional rate, credit, subsidy, or guarantee should count as presumptively benign just because it sits off the main spending line or carries a “middle-class asset building” story.

## The main false passes

### 1. The support sounds broad, but most value lands with incumbent owners

A society should count less success when an instrument is marketed as broad ownership support but most value accrues to households that already own appreciating assets, hold the largest balances, or can claim the largest deductions.[S36][S37]

### 2. The support helps on paper, but mainly raises bids, prices, or fees

A country should count less success when buyer subsidies, tax preferences, or subsidised credit mostly capitalise into higher house prices, higher rents, provider fees, or richer intermediary margins.
In that case the state is subsidising gatekeepers more than entrants.[S31][S36]

### 3. The largest subsidy goes to those with the highest tax liability, biggest account, or strongest borrowing position

A society should count less progress when public support rises automatically with marginal tax rate, mortgage size, contribution capacity, or existing balances.
That delivery logic usually reproduces prior advantage instead of offsetting it.[S36][S37]

### 4. Hidden spending stays outside the main distributional scorecard

A package should count less as a wealth-equality measure when major tax expenditures and subsidies remain outside ex ante and ex post incidence review.
If the state scores only visible spending while leaving large owner-favoring relief off the books, the overall program can look progressive while quietly rebuilding concentration.[S31]

### 5. A subsidy once justified by access survives long after it mainly protects incumbents

A society should count less success when an instrument is defended by an old access story even after housing supply constraints, asset inflation, market concentration, or take-up patterns have turned it into an upper-tier windfall.
Past rationale does not excuse present incidence.[S31][S36][S37]

### 6. “Asset building” support exists, but lower-wealth households would do better with a different form

The archive should count less success when low- and modest-wealth households receive weaker help through deductions or exclusions than they would through matching contributions, fixed nominal subsidies, refundable credits, direct provision, or stronger common floors.[S22][S37][S41]

### 7. Hidden support is cleaned up in one lane while quietly reopened in another

A society should count less progress when it narrows one visible owner subsidy but leaves similar upward scaling active through retirement incentives, concessional credit, capital-gains relief, savings wrappers, or other less visible channels.
The archive judges the whole hidden-support stack, not one headline reform in isolation.[S31][S36][S37]

## The standing subsidy-hygiene / hidden-support stack

### 1. Put hidden spending on the main ledger

Core moves:

- identify major wealth-relevant tax expenditures, credit subsidies, and owner-favoring preferences,
- publish their fiscal cost alongside comparable direct spending,
- and require them to face the same distributional and durability questions as line-item programs.[S31]

The archive's basic rule is simple: **off-budget form does not change on-budget moral effect**.

### 2. Publish beneficiary and capture profiles

Core moves:

- show who receives the first-order benefit,
- show who captures the second-order benefit after price or fee effects,
- disaggregate by wealth position, age or cohort, and relevant excluded groups where law and data permit,
- and distinguish entrants from incumbents rather than speaking only of households in the aggregate.[S31][S36][S37]

A measure should not keep its “middle-class” label once the beneficiary profile shows heavy skew toward upper-tier incumbents.

### 3. Favor flatter delivery forms over upside-down scaling

Core moves:

- prefer capped credits over open-ended deductions where possible,
- prefer refundable or direct support where tax liability would otherwise ration access,
- use matching contributions or fixed nominal subsidies when the goal is to help lower-income households save,
- and be skeptical of support structures that become larger simply because the recipient already has a larger mortgage, income, or account balance.[S36][S37]

The archive is not committed to one legal form.
It is committed to delivery forms that do **not** automatically reward prior advantage.

### 4. Run anti-capitalisation tests before calling support pro-entry

Core moves:

- test whether housing support expands supply and access or mostly enlarges bids,
- check whether education, pension, childcare-linked, or financial-product subsidies mostly raise provider margins,
- and redesign or replace supports that behave more like price support for incumbents than wealth formation for entrants.[S31][S36]

This is why the archive reads subsidy hygiene together with housing, land, and anti-monopoly work.

### 5. Clean up housing preferences first where they are rebuilding closure

Core moves:

- review mortgage-interest relief,
- review uncapped main-residence capital-gains exemptions,
- review first-buyer subsidies that mainly raise bids,
- and shift toward entrant-opening supply, tenure security, and public-value capture instead of open-ended owner subsidies.[S36]

Housing matters first because it is where hidden support most often launders itself as ordinary aspiration while quietly entrenching incumbent advantage.

### 6. Redesign retirement-asset support around equalisation and adequacy

Core moves:

- reduce reliance on tax-rate-dependent pension incentives when the goal is broad saving,
- use matching contributions, fixed nominal subsidies, or refundable designs where they better reach low-income savers,
- pair private saving support with strong public pensions and portability rules,
- and judge retirement support by net broadening rather than participation headlines alone.[S22][S37][S41]

The archive's standing warning is that voluntary private saving subsidies can look inclusive while distributing the strongest public support to people already best placed to save.

### 7. Put every major subsidy on a review, sunset, and redesign cycle

Core moves:

- require periodic review of incidence, take-up, capitalisation, and durability,
- attach sunset or redesign triggers where the original access rationale has failed,
- and treat long-lived hidden spending as part of the archive's anti-capture problem when politically protected beneficiaries block correction.[S15][S31][S36]

A subsidy that cannot be re-examined after its incidence turns upside down has become part of the hidden wealth state the archive is trying to shrink.

## Fast operating rule

Use this note in four steps:

1. **Ask whether the support is really on the main ledger.**  
   Count less honesty when a large owner-favoring preference is treated as background tax structure rather than as public spending by another route.[S31]
2. **Ask who actually gets the value.**  
   Separate first-order recipients from second-order captors after price, fee, and balance-sheet effects.[S31][S36][S37]
3. **Ask whether the delivery form rises with prior advantage.**  
   Count less progress when help gets larger because the recipient already has higher tax liability, a bigger mortgage, stronger borrowing capacity, or a larger savings balance.[S36][S37]
4. **Ask whether a flatter or more direct form would broaden wealth better.**  
   Prefer capped, refundable, matched, fixed-amount, direct, or supply-opening alternatives when they reach lower-wealth households more reliably.[S22][S36][S37][S41]

Default shortcut:

- **visible hidden spending + skewed beneficiary profile + upside-down scaling + price capture** -> assume the state is still quietly refilling incumbent wealth;
- **capped or flat support + entrant-focused design + low capitalisation + live review** -> count as a cleaner subsidy-hygiene lane;
- **mixed case** -> weight the judgment by first-order incidence, second-order capture, and whether the support strengthens real footholds rather than merely enlarging incumbent balance sheets.

## Non-substitution rule

Do **not** let the following excuse a weak hidden-support lane:

- progressive headline tax rates while large owner subsidies remain off-ledger,
- one-time entrant grants while open-ended mortgage or asset preferences still scale upward,
- housing supply reform treated as a full substitute for cleaning upside-down housing tax relief,
- pension participation headlines treated as proof that retirement subsidies are well targeted,
- generic “middle-class” rhetoric without a beneficiary and capture profile,
- or a promise to compensate later through transfers while hidden wealth support continues refilling upper-tier balances in the present.

Those moves may matter once the subsidy lane is genuinely cleaned up.
They do **not** by themselves clear the lane.

## Program implication

Once this stack is read clearly, subsidy design stops looking like a side issue and starts looking like one of the archive's main **state-refill channels**.
That changes sequence.
The archive should usually move earlier on mortgage preferences, uncapped owner relief, upside-down retirement incentives, and other hidden supports than governments prefer, because weak subsidy hygiene lets tax reform, wage reform, housing repair, and social inheritance operate against a state that is still quietly rebuilding incumbent wealth from the side.[S15][S31][S36][S37]

## Bottom line

The archive should now read the hidden-support lane in this order:

1. **Put major tax expenditures and wealth-relevant subsidies on the main fiscal ledger.**
2. **Show who gets the first-order benefit and who captures the second-order gain.**
3. **Prefer capped, refundable, matched, fixed-amount, or otherwise flatter delivery over upside-down scaling.**
4. **Treat price capitalisation and fee capture as central moral tests, not technical afterthoughts.**
5. **Clean up housing and retirement supports first where they are quietly rebuilding closure and incumbent balance-sheet advantage.**
6. **Keep every major hidden-support channel on review, sunset, and redesign rails.**

The archive's subsidy judgment is therefore not: **public support yes or no**.
It is: **stop the state from quietly refilling concentrated wealth through hidden, owner-favoring support while claiming the visible package is egalitarian**.
