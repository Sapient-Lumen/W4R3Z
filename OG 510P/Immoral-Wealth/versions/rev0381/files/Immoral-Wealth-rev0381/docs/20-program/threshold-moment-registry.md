# Threshold-moment registry

## Purpose

The archive should stop treating wealth only as an end-state stock. Many immoral wealth orders reveal themselves at threshold moments: the deposit, guarantee, repair, care, legal, title, relocation, or claim moment where one person needs a defensible buffer and another needs a gatekeeper.

A society can show rising average wealth and still fail if ordinary threshold moments are passable mainly by people with parental backing, inherited location, household-controlled claims, or elite access.

## Registry

## Certification rule

A case cannot receive an open-circulation pass unless the dominant threshold moments are passable without one of the following becoming decisive:

- parental deposit, guarantee, bridge loan, or emergency transfer;
- inherited safe location or school/place access;
- household gatekeeper control over the relevant claim;
- hidden debt, fee, or expert-navigation burden;
- ability to absorb an ordinary shock that poorer peers cannot absorb;
- discretionary public or private gatekeeper favor.

This rule does not condemn every family gift. It condemns wealth orders where family backing becomes the normal threshold technology for crossing into secure adulthood, ownership, business formation, or safe place.[S18][S19][S52][S73]

## How to use in cases

Every case memo should name the three most decisive threshold moments and state whether each is:

- **open:** ordinary lower/middle entrants can cross it without family or patronage;
- **watch:** some public or market support exists but the distribution is uneven;
- **family-gated:** parental or inherited position is materially decisive;
- **gatekeeper-dependent:** employer, landlord, household, sponsor, administrator, or political intermediary can interrupt the claim;
- **self-eroding:** recurring costs or risk exposure convert the foothold into a drain;
- **missing:** the case lacks adequate evidence.

## rev0314 cost-of-life threshold moments

- **Bill due date / fee trigger:** a small timing mismatch becomes overdraft, late fee, NSF, re-presentment, or high-cost liquidity use.[S262][S268][S271]
- **Repossession notice:** loss of a financed vehicle threatens work, care, credit, and deficiency debt at once.[S266][S267]
- **App/payroll credit stacking:** BNPL or earned-wage obligations multiply before total payment load is visible.[S264][S272]
- **Childcare gap:** care price, waitlist, hours mismatch, or subsidy cliff forces reduced work, training loss, or family dependence.[S273][S275]
- **Long-term-care need:** severe care requirement triggers asset spenddown or unpaid family labor before adequate public support arrives.[S277][S278]


## rev0317 family-transfer threshold moments

Gate 19 adds threshold moments that are easy to miss if inheritance is treated only as a late-life bequest:

- **first private transfer moment:** tuition help, down payment, guarantee, family loan, business stake, emergency liquidity;
- **will/beneficiary designation moment:** whether modest assets have a clear successor before incapacity or death;
- **probate/title moment:** whether the estate clears into usable title, account access, or sale/refinance authority;
- **care-need moment:** whether LTSS spenddown or estate recovery consumes the only transferable asset;
- **capacity-control moment:** whether guardianship/fiduciary control preserves or redirects person-level wealth;
- **family-separation moment:** whether divorce, QDROs, support, and care recognition convert household wealth into person-level claims;
- **child-support distribution moment:** whether collections reach children/families or are retained as public reimbursement;
- **dynasty-perimeter moment:** whether top-tail families can shelter control across generations through trusts, exemptions, and planning.


## rev0318 public-balance-sheet threshold moments

Gate 20 adds threshold moments that are easy to miss when public assets or public promises are treated as static totals:

- **claim-haircut moment:** a social-insurance, pension, or public-benefit promise reaches depletion, formula change, COLA erosion, or eligibility repair;
- **interest-crowdout moment:** debt service begins to displace the services, enforcement, housing, care, infrastructure, or disaster capacity relied on by lower-wealth households;
- **guarantee-call moment:** a loan guarantee, mortgage insurance, deposit insurance, disaster loan, or credit program moves from hidden exposure to actual public loss;
- **systemic-rescue moment:** financial or corporate rescue protects payment/deposit/credit functions but may also protect asset holders, creditors, or managers;
- **central-bank-remittance moment:** balance-sheet losses or deferred assets reduce fiscal flows while remaining outside ordinary appropriations politics;
- **public-asset-withdrawal moment:** sovereign or public assets are drawn down, raided, or redirected under stress;
- **public-upside-recapture moment:** the state either captures or loses warrants, equity, fees, repayment, or clawbacks after taking downside risk.
