# Disaster fiscal capacity and rebuild-debt router

Disasters test whether public wealth is liquid enough to preserve services. A locality can have valuable assets and still fail the floor if revenue collapses, reimbursement is delayed, borrowing costs rise, and essential services are cut during recovery.[S256][S257]

## What to score

- pre-disaster reserves and liquidity;
- property-tax exposure to declining or damaged assessed values;
- insurance availability for public assets;
- FEMA/state reimbursement timing and local cash-flow capacity;
- community disaster loan eligibility and repayment/forgiveness terms;
- municipal-bond spread or rating sensitivity to climate risk;
- mitigation funding access and grant-absorption capacity;
- distribution of rebuilding aid across homeowners, renters, small businesses, and excluded groups.[S256][S257]

## Routing rule

When local disaster finance is weak, do not count home equity, local public assets, or infrastructure at face value. Route to mitigation grants, operating bridges, reimbursement prepayment, debt-service relief, public-asset insurance, relocation/buyout justice, and renter/business continuity supports.

## False pass

A disaster-aid promise is a false pass until the locality can keep schools, water, emergency response, transit, courts, and health services operating without imposing regressive debt or forcing out low-wealth residents.
