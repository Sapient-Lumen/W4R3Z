# Policy package pathway library

A wealth-order case should end with a pathway, not a shopping list. rev0305 adds a compact pathway library so cases can route from breach to package.

## Pathway A — floor-first stabilization

Use when the bottom half cannot keep modest wealth.

Sequence: protected asset floor → claimability redesign → benefit-cliff repair → debt/fee shield → liquid buffer → simple default savings/ownership rails.

Avoid: top-heavy accounts that are locked, asset-tested, expert-only, or politically easy to claw back.[S17][S21][S22][S24][S25]

## Pathway B — early-entry anti-family-gate

Use when deposits, guarantees, education stakes, business starts, or safe exit depend on parents.

Sequence: child/young-adult capital stake → renter/deposit support not tied to parental wealth → public guarantee alternative → inheritance/gift recording → anti-advantage tax and grant design → first-foothold default rails.[S18][S19][S52][S73]

## Pathway C — housing and land washout control

Use when rent, land prices, speculation, repair costs, or insurance drain gains.

Sequence: social/nonmarket housing or land bank → tenant and repair protection → land-value/property-tax redesign → local ownership concentration screen → climate/insurance adaptation → zoning and infrastructure alignment.[S34][S35][S42][S43][S59][S418][S374][S95][S96]

## Pathway D — group/person veto closure

Use when race, caste, gender, Indigenous status, region, citizenship, migrant status, or household control blocks ownership.

Sequence: group-specific evidence → title/control reform → targeted repair instruments → anti-discrimination enforcement → reparative or constitutional claim route → veto monitoring.[S57][S58][S66][S76][S85][S98][S99][S100]

## Pathway E — top-tail and dynastic compression

Use when the dominant problem is wealth-to-rule or dynastic lock-in.

Sequence: beneficial ownership and trust registry → inheritance/gift/exit-tax rail → capital-gains reform → philanthropic/DAF/foundation governance → competition/procurement/media-firewall reforms → public integrity enforcement.[S04][S05][S06][S31][S36][S56][S435][S64]

## Pathway F — public/common counterweight conversion

Use when public wealth exists but claimant-level security is weak.

Sequence: governance transparency → distribution rule → resident/citizen access test → portability and inclusion screen → stabilization reserve → anti-patronage rules.[S77][S78][S369][S81][S93][S95][S96]

## Pathway G — legibility-first state-capacity build

Use when the state cannot see or enforce the wealth order.

Sequence: cadastre/land registry → beneficial ownership registry → tax administration → courts/procurement integrity → subgroup statistics → public reporting → only then complex taxes or transfers.[S01][S04][S07][S76][S101][S102]


## Pathway H — liability-stack floor repair

Use when medical debt, student debt, court debt, collections, high-cost credit, disability/care costs, or credit-file drag block ordinary threshold moments.

Sequence: protected minima and anti-seizure rules → debt-type separation → medical/student/legal debt shields → hardship and income-based repayment automation → forgiveness/sunset rules → credit-report and lease-screening limits → subgroup liability reporting.[S104][S105][S106][S379][S108][S109][S110][S111]

Avoid: treating debt relief as a one-time transfer while the underlying institutions keep producing status debt.

## Pathway I — secure-renter and non-owner foothold

Use when ownership is not the main or only feasible foothold, but renting can be made secure enough to substitute.

Sequence: tenure stability → affordability/residual-income target → maintenance and accessibility enforcement → broad social/nonprofit supply → portability and anti-discrimination → renter savings and non-housing asset rails → loophole monitoring.[S196][S116][S117][S118][S119]

Avoid: ownership-only subsidies that raise prices, or narrow residual social housing that cannot discipline the wider rental market.

## Package rule

Lead with the lane that closes the dominant breach; add the lane that blocks the fastest washout; install rails before instruments that require those rails; never certify until the veto gates have evidence.

## Pathway J — claim-conversion and auto-enrollment repair

Use when benefits, tax credits, public transfers, or social claims exist but eligible people do not reliably receive and keep them.

Sequence: eligibility mapping → automatic/pre-filled enrollment → safe payment rail → assisted/offline access → fee/offset protection → renewal stabilization → fast appeal/error correction → subgroup conversion reporting.[S120][S263][S122][S123][S126][S139][S140]

Avoid: counting gross program spending as a floor when claimants face non-take-up, paperwork churn, delayed payments, paid intermediaries, or account fees.

## Pathway K — starter-asset claim finality

Use when child accounts, baby bonds, public starter accounts, or social-inheritance devices are central to early footholds.

Sequence: automatic account opening → progressive public seed/top-up → low-cost default investment → claimant notice before maturity → no-fee tracing → maturity conversion support → protection from household/creditor capture → account-use monitoring.[S133][S134][S137][S138]

Avoid: treating accounts as delivered assets before they are found, claimed, person-controlled, and usable at the relevant threshold moment.

## Pathway L — title, probate, and disaster-repair finality

Use when property exists in use but cannot be financed, insured, repaired, defended, or transferred because title, probate, occupancy, or documentation is weak.

Sequence: legal aid/title search → probate simplification → co-owner mediation and buyout support → partition-sale protections → alternative disaster documentation → repair/adaptation funds for cloudy-title households → anti-predatory acquisition rules → title-risk data by subgroup.[S130][S131][S132]

Avoid: counting homeownership as a full foothold when owners cannot convert title into repair, credit, insurance, disaster aid, fair sale, or intergenerational transfer.


## rev0311 pathway — jurisdictional mobility and claimant perimeter

Use when domestic repair is exposed to cross-border status, tax residence, exit, asset-flight, migrant-exclusion, or remittance-conversion failure.

Package:

1. evidence-calibrated exit-risk dashboard;
2. exit tax / deemed disposal / source-tax / trailing-residence design where needed;
3. CRS/AEOI + beneficial ownership + trust/entity linkage;
4. CBI/RBI and preferential newcomer regime screen;
5. migrant claimant-perimeter and portability scorecard;
6. remittance cost, recruitment-debt, and wage-claim rails;
7. capital-flow stress test for macro-fragile jurisdictions;
8. treaty and legal-durability review.

Do not let advisory-industry exit narratives veto repair without evidence. Do not ignore actual asset flight where macro and enforcement rails are weak.[S56][S180][S181][S203][S210][S211][S212][S213][S214][S216][S219]
