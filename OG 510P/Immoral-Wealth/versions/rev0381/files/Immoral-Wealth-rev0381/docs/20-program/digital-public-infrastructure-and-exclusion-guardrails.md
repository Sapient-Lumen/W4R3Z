# Digital public infrastructure and exclusion guardrails

Digital public infrastructure can turn state capacity into person-level security: identity, payments, and data exchange can reduce transaction costs, speed public transfers, and expand access. But DPI can also convert welfare, finance, and public services into app-gated, ID-gated, surveillance-prone, vendor-dependent systems.

World Bank Findex, ID4D, G2Px, and DPI materials are useful anchors because they treat accounts, identity, and payment rails as infrastructure rather than optional service design.[S120][S124][S126][S127]

## Positive role

DPI can support the archive’s goals when it:

- opens accounts or wallets for excluded people;
- pays benefits quickly and predictably;
- reduces leakage and travel/waiting costs;
- improves women’s person-level control;
- supports portable claims across place, job, school, health, and household transitions;
- creates auditable public rails rather than private gatekeeper dependence;
- allows policy to act during crisis without inventing new delivery channels.

Kenya’s mobile-money ecosystem is a calibration case for the upside: broad formal financial access and mobile-money usage show how payment rails can become ordinary infrastructure.[S135][S136]

## Exclusion risks

DPI fails the conversion gate when it creates or intensifies:

- no-ID exclusion;
- no-phone or no-smartphone exclusion;
- data-cost and connectivity exclusion;
- biometric mismatch or name-match failure;
- language and disability inaccessibility;
- account freezes without fast appeal;
- public/private data sharing that deters claimants;
- household surveillance or abuser control;
- vendor lock-in and fee extraction;
- loss of cash/offline alternatives;
- algorithmic denial without explanation.

## Guardrails

A DPI-based wealth/floor package should include:

1. legal right to alternative documentation;
2. assisted and offline channels;
3. public-interest fee caps;
4. data minimization and purpose limitation;
5. independent audit and redress;
6. interoperability and portability;
7. claimant choice among payment providers where feasible;
8. protection for shared-device, low-literacy, disabled, rural, migrant, and older users;
9. emergency fallback when systems fail;
10. no denial solely because a private vendor or automated match cannot verify the person.

## Certification rule

DPI can strengthen a floor only if the people most likely to need the floor can actually use it. A system can be technologically advanced and morally regressive if it shifts the burden of proof onto people with the least documentation, least connectivity, or least institutional trust.
