# Mode selection and escalation heuristic

## What this note is for

This note turns the archive's triage lane into a **full stable mode-selection stack**.
It answers a narrower question than [`prevention-and-response-doctrine.md`](prevention-and-response-doctrine.md):
**given the evidence in front of us right now, which governing posture should the archive place this society in?**

The archive needs this note because posture choice should not be atmospheric, rhetorical, or driven by whichever indicator is currently loudest.
A serious judgment has to decide whether the case is still mainly about **prevention**, already requires **correction**, or has crossed far enough into wealth-rule that **anti-oligarchy response** becomes co-equal with floor repair and entry reopening.[S02][S15][S18][S19][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## Minimum operating rule

A serious mode judgment should usually do **six things at once**:

1. read the case through the archive's **trigger screens** before reading averages,
2. distinguish **major constitutional breach** from merely moderate warning,
3. read **closure and masking** together with headline distribution,
4. ask whether ordinary democratic **correction is still materially available**,
5. escalate when **capture, opacity, or repair debt** make softer posture implausible,
6. and de-escalate only on **durable proof** rather than on temporary calm.[S05][S06][S13][S15][S18][S19][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33]

The standing rule is therefore:
**choose the softest posture that still matches the live breach, but make it hard enough that the order cannot keep hardening while the archive congratulates itself for staying moderate.**

## The main false passes

### 1. Headline shares are read before the trigger screens

A society should count less seriousness when it decides mode from a flattering top-line distribution snapshot before reading veto surfaces, opacity, durability, entrant access, or wealth-to-rule channels.
The archive does not allow a tolerable-looking average to overrule a catastrophic breach that already sits somewhere decisive.[S02][S13][S15][S18][S19][S20][S23]

### 2. Missingness or opacity is treated as permission to stay soft

A mode judgment fails when hidden ownership, weak transfer visibility, noisy registries, or strategically contested measures are read as evidence for prevention rather than as reasons to distrust comfortable de-escalation.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

### 3. One rebound or flagship reform is mistaken for governability

The archive should count less seriousness when a single reform, one post-crisis rebound, or one good cycle is treated as proof that prevention still fits.
Mode choice should ask whether gains are durable, whether repair debt is shrinking, and whether elites can still regroup faster than households can secure footholds.[S05][S06][S20][S31][S32][S33]

### 4. Closure and masking are treated as secondary because averages look decent

A society should count less accuracy when apparently decent household or national balances are allowed to hide person-level weakness, late footholds, excluded-group blockage, debt-fragile access, intermediary control, or place-based screening.[S13][S18][S19][S21][S22][S27][S28][S29][S30]

### 5. Wealth-to-rule is treated as a side issue rather than a mode override

The archive should count less realism when capture, lobbying, procurement bias, media concentration, stewardship concentration, or crisis privilege are discussed as background texture instead of as reasons the posture may already need to harden.[S15][S142][S17][S23][S31][S32][S33]

### 6. The hardest posture is chosen atmospherically rather than diagnostically

A society should count less seriousness when anti-oligarchy response is chosen mainly because the politics feels ugly, the top looks rich, or commentators sound alarmed.
The harder posture should be triggered by live breach, capture, opacity, repair debt, and blocked governability rather than by mood alone.[S15][S18][S19][S23][S31][S32][S33]

### 7. De-escalation is granted because conflict eased, not because channels weakened

The archive should count less restraint when a society moves back toward prevention because elites sound calmer, macro conditions improved briefly, or the most visible conflict cooled, while the channels that justified escalation remain materially intact.[S06][S15][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## The standing mode-selection stack

### 1. Read trigger screens before averages

Do **not** place the case by free-floating average.
Read it through five standing trigger screens first.

**Veto-first screen**
- Catastrophic breach on the floor, top-tail ceiling, person-level passage, excluded-group access, or wealth-to-rule surface should not be smoothed away by better-looking scores elsewhere.[S02][S13][S18][S19][S20][S23]

**Proof-asymmetry screen**
- Hidden ownership, weak visibility, contested registries, or strategically missing data should make softer posture harder to certify, not easier.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

**Durability-and-repair screen**
- Do not soften mode choice on one benign snapshot, one rebound, or one crisis response that still leaves large repair debt behind.[S05][S06][S20][S31][S32][S33]

**Wealth-to-rule override**
- Strong money-into-rule channels can justify a harder posture even when headline wealth shares still look only moderately bad.[S15][S142][S17][S23][S31][S32][S33]

**Rail-reality screen**
- Do not classify a society as if ordinary correction were still comfortably available when delivery, integrity, visibility, and anti-blackmail rails are mostly rhetorical.[S04][S15][S23][S31][S38]

### 2. Sort the case into three input families

After the trigger screens, sort the evidence into three families.

**Constitutional breach count**
- Ask how far the case is outside the archive's acceptable envelope on the main surfaces: bottom-half floor passage, top-tail concentration, public/social counterweight, key-asset control, intergenerational circulation, life-course timing, and reconcentration pressure.[S02][S13][S142][S17][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22]

**Closure and masking pressure**
- Ask whether apparently tolerable averages are being flattered by debt encumbrance, household masking, excluded-group blockage, intermediary control, subsidy substitution, delayed claims, or extractive gain channels that reward incumbency more than entry.[S13][S20][S25][S26][S27][S28][S29][S30][S31]

**Wealth-to-rule and governability failure**
- Ask whether concentrated wealth is already buying governing leverage through lobbying, party finance, opacity, media power, capture-prone procurement, repeated downside socialization, or reform conditions in which elites can regroup faster than households can secure durable claims.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

### 3. Weight major breaches before reinforcing moderate ones

Treat the following as **major by default**:

- bottom-half near-assetlessness or clear failure of the floor passage rule,
- top-tail concentration outside the archive's warning ceiling,
- strong dynastic closure or serious timing failure,
- oligarchic control of key assets or stewardship chokepoints,
- severe opacity around top-tail ownership or transfer pathways,
- clear evidence that wealth already purchases durable political veto,
- or reconcentration pressure strong enough that recent gains are unlikely to survive a normal cycle.[S02][S05][S15][S18][S19][S20][S23][S26]

Treat person-level masking, excluded-group blockage, debt fragility, place-based closure, and non-extractive-formation failure as **moderate-to-major** depending on severity and reinforcement.
The archive's bias is that several reinforcing moderate breaches should often be read like one major breach rather than treated as harmless fragments.

### 4. Place the case into the dominant posture

#### Prevention

Use **prevention** only when all of the following are broadly true:

- there are **no major constitutional breaches**,
- closure and masking pressures are present only in early or mild form,
- wealth-to-rule signals are weak rather than merely hidden,
- and ordinary democratic reform still looks materially plausible on real rails rather than on policy imagination alone.[S13][S18][S19][S31]

Prevention therefore means **broadening still has a real chance to outrun hardening**.
It does not mean the society is already good.

#### Correction

Use **correction** when any of the following is true:

- **one major breach** is clearly present,
- **two or more reinforcing moderate breaches** are hardening together,
- headline shares look tolerable but closure is tightening through late footholds, dynastic screening, debt fragility, or excluded-group blockage,
- or the society is still governable through ordinary channels but no longer looks safely self-correcting.[S05][S13][S18][S19][S21][S22][S25][S31]

The archive's default bias is that serious timing failure, serious person-level masking, or serious excluded-group blockage should push a society at least into correction even when older-cohort balance sheets still look decent.

#### Anti-oligarchy response

Use **anti-oligarchy response** when any of the following is true:

- **multiple major breaches** are present at once,
- one major breach coexists with strong **wealth-to-rule conversion**,
- top-tail opacity is so severe that the real ownership structure cannot be credibly measured or governed,
- the upper tail can already **veto, hollow out, or launder reform** through lobbying, media, procurement, party finance, courts, or administrative capture,
- or repeated crisis support shows that concentrated owners keep the upside while the public keeps the downside.[S02][S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

At that point anti-capture work is no longer institutional garnish.
It becomes co-equal with floor repair, entry reopening, and top-tail compression.

### 5. Escalate on capture, opacity, and repair debt before comfort

Escalate **one level immediately** when:

- wealth-to-rule conversion is already strong enough to block later reform,
- decisive surfaces are too opaque for a softer posture to be positively supported,
- crisis derogations or public backstops have accumulated without real repair,
- reform sabotage, elite regrouping speed, or mobility blackmail makes ordinary correction materially implausible,
- or apparent gains are repeatedly washed out through rents, fees, reconcentration, or delayed-justice substitution.[S05][S06][S15][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33]

That rule is what stops the archive from waiting for fully consolidated oligarchy before hardening the posture.

### 6. De-escalate only on durable proof

Do **not** lower the posture after one good year or one calmer political moment.
De-escalation should require:

- real evidence rather than strategic missingness,
- improvement that survives a meaningful durability window,
- materially stronger entrant access, floor breadth, governance visibility, and loss-bearing discipline,
- substantial repair of emergency derogations or crisis privileges,
- and visibly weaker wealth-to-rule channels rather than merely quieter ones.[S05][S06][S15][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33]

That keeps the archive from mistaking a pause in concentration for a constitutional settlement.

## Fast operating rule

Use this note in six steps:

1. **Read the trigger screens first.**  
   Start with veto surfaces, proof asymmetry, durability, wealth-to-rule override, and rail reality before reading the case as an average.[S05][S06][S13][S15][S18][S19][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33]
2. **Count major breach honestly.**  
   Ask whether the case has zero, one, or multiple major constitutional failures once closure and masking are read in full rather than filtered out.[S02][S13][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22]
3. **Read governability, not just distribution.**  
   Ask whether ordinary correction is still materially available or whether capture, opacity, and sabotage are already hard enough to require a stronger posture.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]
4. **Place the dominant posture.**  
   Zero major breach and weak capture -> **prevention**; one major breach or reinforcing moderates -> **correction**; multiple major breaches or major breach plus strong capture/opacity -> **anti-oligarchy response**.[S18][S19][S23][S31]
5. **Apply the override rules.**  
   Escalate one level if wealth-to-rule, opacity, repair debt, or blocked reform capacity make the softer classification unsafe.[S05][S06][S15][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33]
6. **Demand durable proof for de-escalation.**  
   A softer posture must be earned by durable weakening of the channels that justified the harder one, not by temporary calm or nicer rhetoric.[S06][S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

Default shortcut:
- **clean trigger screens + no major breach + weak capture** -> prevention;
- **one major breach or several reinforcing moderates with ordinary governability still real** -> correction;
- **major breach plus strong capture / opacity / repair debt, or several major breaches at once** -> anti-oligarchy response;
- **mixed case** -> let the decision be weighted by wealth-to-rule strength, entrant access, and durability rather than by the friendliest headline share.

## Non-substitution rule

Do **not** let any of the following keep the archive in a softer posture than the case deserves:

- one attractive headline reform,
- a flattering household or public balance-sheet snapshot,
- partial broadening work that leaves capture channels open,
- crisis support that stabilized markets without repairing burden sharing,
- future promised gains standing in for present footholds,
- or some good program lane somewhere else in government.

Likewise, do **not** let hard posture language excuse thin floors, household instability, or absent implementation rails.
The archive asks a stricter question:
**does the chosen posture actually match the live breach, and are the non-deferrable duties of that posture being carried together rather than selectively?**

## Bottom line

The archive now has a cleaner division of labor.
[`prevention-and-response-doctrine.md`](prevention-and-response-doctrine.md) says **what each posture is for**.
This note says **how to decide which posture the case belongs in right now**.
The stable rule is simple: screen first, count breach honestly, harden on capture and opacity before comfort, and soften only when durable proof says the softer posture is real rather than wishful.
