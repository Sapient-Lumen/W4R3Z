# Front-door operator cards

## Card 1 — moral screen

Wealth inequality becomes immoral when wealth operates as **dependency, closure, and rule**.

- **Dependency:** people must accept domination, bad terms, or unsafe arrangements because they lack usable fallback assets.
- **Closure:** ordinary routes into housing, land, business, savings, pensions, education, safe place, or political voice are gated by birth, group, household power, or inherited position.
- **Rule:** concentrated wealth can shape law, enforcement, information, rescue terms, media, procurement, or market structure for itself.[S15][S23][S26][S54][S188][S189]

## Card 2 — target family

Near-ideal wealth distribution means **bounded top, thick middle, real floor, open circulation**.

Default private-wealth target family:

| Group | Target share |
|---|---:|
| Bottom 50 | about 20% |
| Middle 40 | about 50% |
| Next 9 | about 20% |
| Top 1 | about 10% |
| Top 0.1 | about 3% or less |

Use the target as a certification family, not as a sacred point. Public/social wealth is a separate required counterweight, not a hidden fifth bucket.[S02][S03][S13][S142]

## Card 3 — non-pass rule

A case does not pass from shares alone. It must also pass:

1. real lower-half floor;
2. early non-family footholds;
3. person-level title/control/exit screen;
4. group closure screen;
5. washout closure;
6. top-tail rule screen;
7. public/common counterweight screen.[S17][S18][S19][S21][S22][S57]

## Card 4 — common false passes

| False pass | Meaning |
|---|---|
| paper-floor | legal promise exists, usable support does not |
| spend-down-trapped | help requires staying assetless or liquidating modest footholds |
| cliff-punished | modest gains trigger abrupt support loss |
| locked-up | wealth exists only behind future-only or hardship-gated access |
| shock-fragile | ordinary interruption can force destructive sale or debt |
| self-eroding | carrying costs, fees, taxes, insurance, or repairs eat the gain |
| buffer-coerced | private wealth is needed because common insurance is too weak |
| family-gated | early entry works only with parental threshold backing |
| place-closed | compounding places are unaffordable or inaccessible |
| group-closed | national averages hide durable group exclusion |
| top-tail-rule | wealth converts into political or institutional control |
| opacity-blocked | ownership/control cannot be seen well enough to govern |
| money-vetoed | large donors, dark money, or independent spending can reshape the reform menu |
| agenda-captured | media, expertise, or sponsored policy channels make alternatives politically invisible |
| entrant-voiceless | local land-use process excludes renters and would-be residents from entry decisions |
| exit-vetoed | reform ambition is lowered because of untested elite migration or asset-flight threats |
| passport-privileged | membership, tax residence, or mobility is easier to buy than to earn through ordinary residence/work |
| claimant-perimeter-excluded | public/common wealth counts citizens while resident workers or migrants lack claims |
| remittance-floor | private cross-border family support is mistaken for a public floor |
| backstop-privileged | public rescue protects asset holders or creditors without public upside recovery |
| claim-haircut | public/pension/social-insurance promise is counted before financing durability is proven |
| guarantee-hidden | public credit, insurance, or bailout exposure is treated as free until stress arrives |
| public-asset-raided | sovereign or public assets are vulnerable to fiscal patching, patronage, or elite projects |

## Card 5 — breach-to-package route

| Dominant breach | First lane | Second lane | Rail lane |
|---|---|---|---|
| floor failure | protected floor, claimability, anti-seizure | debt/fee washout closure | take-up, appeals, protected minima |
| family-gated early entry | social inheritance / starter assets | housing and debt washout closure | asset protection, low-fee defaults |
| housing/place closure | supply + social/nonprofit/shared-equity housing | land-value capture / anti-displacement | local ownership and title data |
| group closure | universal-plus-targeted repair | enforcement and title/control repair | disaggregated privacy-safe data |
| top-tail rule | tax/compression/political firewalls | beneficial ownership and anti-monopoly | valuation, audit, registry rails |
| thin public counterweight | public/social wealth buildout | claimability and rule-bound access | anti-patronage governance |
| climate self-erosion | insurance/repair/resilience support | relocation / land-use / housing protection | hazard and premium data |
| AI/intangible concentration | compute/data/IP/platform competition and access | tax/private-market rails | ownership and market-structure data |

## Card 6 — verdict phrases

- **Near-ideal / acceptable:** share family plus all constitutional screens substantially pass.
- **Acceptable but vulnerable:** broadly passes, with named monitoring stress surfaces.
- **Correction-required:** one or more central breaches block certification.
- **Emergency repair:** ordinary-life security is failing quickly.
- **Captured / immoral:** wealth has hardened into dependency, closure, and rule.

## Card 7 — proof debt rule

Missing evidence raises the proof burden for comforting verdicts. It does not block action. A case can still route to repair under proof debt, but it cannot be certified as acceptable while the missing field could hide floor failure, group closure, top-tail rule, or opacity.[S01][S13][S15][S23]

# rev0305 operator card — certify by gates, not vibes

Use the seven gates:

1. share plausibility;
2. floor reality;
3. threshold moments;
4. anti-averaging/group-person vetoes;
5. washout/reconcentration;
6. wealth-to-rule/capture;
7. public/common counterweight conversion.

Then choose the opening pathway from [`policy-package-pathway-library.md`](policy-package-pathway-library.md).

Never treat a subsystem success as a full pass. Alaska and Vienna are useful because they show what a real counterweight can do, but each remains a subsystem case until connected to full distribution, group, floor, top-tail, and claimant evidence.

Never treat pension or housing wealth as first-foothold wealth without liquidity, timing, renter, age, and person-control checks. Use [`../10-framework/pension-housing-and-hidden-age-wealth.md`](../10-framework/pension-housing-and-hidden-age-wealth.md).


## Card 11 — enforceability and remedy

Question: Can the claimant, agency, trustee, journalist, researcher, prosecutor, tax authority, procurement body, or court actually enforce the rule?

False pass: a reform exists on paper, but standing, remedies, audit capacity, registry verification, legal durability, or capture controls are missing.

## Card 12 — democratic non-domination

Question: Can concentrated wealth still dominate the reform agenda through political finance, dark money, lobbying, sponsored expertise, media agenda power, local land-use veto, strategic litigation, or post-enactment countermobilization?

False pass: a reform survives the statute book but not the power map. Disclosure appears too late, donors or sponsors remain hidden, lobbying records miss advisory channels, media ownership or state advertising shapes the agenda, or incumbent property holders locally veto entry while excluded entrants lack voice.[S184][S186][S188][S190][S195][S198]

Route: use [`democratic-power-gate-and-firewall-scorecard.md`](democratic-power-gate-and-firewall-scorecard.md), [`political-finance-and-lobbying-rail-matrix.md`](political-finance-and-lobbying-rail-matrix.md), [`media-pluralism-and-agenda-power-screen.md`](media-pluralism-and-agenda-power-screen.md), [`local-land-use-democracy-and-entry-router.md`](local-land-use-democracy-and-entry-router.md), and [`reform-survival-and-countermobilization-monitor.md`](reform-survival-and-countermobilization-monitor.md).



## Card 13 — jurisdictional mobility and claimant perimeter

Question: Can wealth, legal status, tax residence, assets, or claims move across borders in ways that defeat the domestic wealth-order package while ordinary claimants remain bound to place, work, care, documentation, and migration rules?

False pass: a domestic reform looks strong, but top-tail actors can exit or shift assets without accrued obligations; investors can buy residence or citizenship; migrant workers are outside the public-wealth claimant perimeter; or remittances are counted as family security without scoring fee leakage, recruitment debt, wage theft, status risk, and recipient control.[S203][S204][S210][S211][S216][S218][S219]

## rev0312 workplace-power card

Ask whether the wealth order is trying to repair downstream what it first prevents upstream.

| Surface | Operator question | Route |
|---|---|---|
| saveable wages | do wages, hours, benefits, and schedules leave room to save? | wage-floor/bargaining router |

| responsibility follows control | can workers reach the lead firm/platform/franchisor that controls the economic terms? | joint-employer/fissuring router |

## rev0313 place-public-finance card

Before counting public wealth as a counterweight, ask whether the household can use the local public floor where it lives. Score schools, facilities, utilities, infrastructure, transportation, disaster operating capacity, and municipal fiscal capacity. If the case depends on public systems but local tax bases, grant absorption, or service quality fail, route to Gate 15 before issuing a comfort verdict.[S238][S245][S246][S250][S251][S254][S256]

Fast prompts:

- Does local property wealth buy better childhood public assets?
- Are water, sewer, roads, schools, and stormwater maintained without regressive fees?
- Are energy and water burdens protected from shutoff and collections?
- Does cheap housing remain affordable after transport cost and access?
- Can the locality keep services running after disaster without debt-washing residents?


## rev0314 household-market-extraction card

Before counting income, benefits, or modest assets as wealth-forming, ask whether the household leaks the first buffer through private cost-of-life tollgates. Score overdraft/NSF fees, credit-card late fees, high-cost small-dollar loans, unbanked/underbanked costs, auto negative equity, repossession, BNPL and earned-wage visibility, junk fees, subscription friction, childcare, and long-term-care spenddown.[S260][S262][S263][S264][S266][S273][S277]

Fast prompts:

- Is short-term liquidity bought repeatedly through fees or high-cost credit?
- Is a car necessary for work or care but underwater or repossessable?
- Can the household and regulator see total BNPL/app/payroll obligations?
- Are all-in prices, recurring charges, and cancellation rights clear before commitment?
- Do childcare or long-term-care costs force labor-force exit, family dependence, or asset spenddown?

Use [`household-market-extraction-gate-and-scorecard.md`](household-market-extraction-gate-and-scorecard.md) before giving a comfort verdict based on disposable income, public transfers, or modest asset holdings.


## rev0315 score-mediated-exclusion card

Before counting a job, rental unit, account, insurance policy, benefit, public payment, or affordable price as reachable, ask whether the person can pass the relevant data shadow. Score credit reports, specialty reports, tenant screens, employment dossiers, insurance scores, surveillance prices, fraud flags, identity-proofing systems, and public-benefit algorithms.[S281][S283][S286][S288][S290][S291][S295][S296]

Fast prompts:

- Which report, score, model, vendor, or fraud flag affects the decision?
- Does the person see the exact reason before the transaction is lost?
- Can they dispute accuracy and relevance fast enough to preserve the apartment, job, account, benefit, or insurance?
- Are medical debt, eviction filings, non-conviction records, old hardship, or identity-theft traces suppressed where weakly predictive?
- Are insurance and personalized prices audited for proxy discrimination and unaffordable risk spreads?
- Do public-benefit algorithms provide notice, human review, emergency continuation, and subgroup false-positive audits?

Use [`score-mediated-exclusion-gate-and-scorecard.md`](score-mediated-exclusion-gate-and-scorecard.md) before giving a comfort verdict based on access, affordability, benefit delivery, insurance coverage, or ordinary market participation.


## rev0316 fresh-start card

Before counting a household as secure after a shock, ask whether the person can actually restart. Score bankruptcy access, discharge, exemptions, wage garnishment, bank-account levy, judgment liens, debt-collection due process, default-judgment repair, foreclosure/loss mitigation, eviction and foreclosure record cleanup, criminal-record clearance, collateral-consequence repair, and protected recovery tools.[S301][S304][S305][S306][S307][S309][S315][S316]

Fast prompts:

- Can the person keep enough wages and bank balance to preserve rent, food, transport, care, utilities, and recovery savings?
- Are car, tools, household goods, documents, phone, and modest home equity protected at realistic values?
- Does bankruptcy discharge the debts creating permanent lock, and can ordinary people file?
- Do collection courts require proof and real notice before default judgments become garnishment or liens?
- Are eviction, foreclosure, and criminal legal records sealed or suppressed quickly enough to preserve housing, work, and licensing access?
- Do private vendors and specialty reports obey clean-slate, sealing, and correction rules?

Use [`fresh-start-gate-and-recovery-scorecard.md`](fresh-start-gate-and-recovery-scorecard.md) before giving a comfort verdict based on shock survivability, recovery, clean slate, bankruptcy, or record repair.


## Gate 19 card — intergenerational transfer and family-wealth allocation

Ask this whenever a case depends on inheritance, family help, housing transfers, retirement beneficiaries, probate, care need, or household wealth.

**Fast test:** can a modest-wealth person receive, divide, protect, and transmit wealth without expensive counsel or family gatekeeper control, while high-end families are prevented from using trusts and exemptions to harden dynastic closure?

**Failure labels:** `parent-backed`, `bequest-late`, `probate-trapped`, `heirs-title-clouded`, `estate-recovery-clawed`, `guardian-controlled`, `fiduciary-drained`, `child-support-not-passed-through`, `QDRO-nonconverting`, `dynasty-shielded`.

**Route:** use [`intergenerational-transfer-gate-and-family-wealth-scorecard.md`](intergenerational-transfer-gate-and-family-wealth-scorecard.md) and then choose the probate/title, estate-recovery, fiduciary, family-law, or high-end transfer perimeter router.


## Gate 20 card — public balance sheet and sovereign backstop

Ask this whenever the case relies on public wealth, social insurance, pension promises, debt capacity, deposit insurance, mortgage guarantees, federal credit, central-bank facilities, sovereign funds, or crisis rescue.

**Fast test:** who receives the public backstop, who pays the debt service or assessments, whose claims get cut under stress, and does the public receive upside when it takes downside risk?

**Failure labels:** `backstop-privileged`, `claim-haircut`, `interest-crowded`, `guarantee-hidden`, `public-upside-lost`, `public-asset-raided`, `quasi-fiscal-blind`.

**Route:** use [`public-balance-sheet-gate-and-sovereign-backstop-scorecard.md`](public-balance-sheet-gate-and-sovereign-backstop-scorecard.md), then choose the fiscal-risk, pension/social-insurance, bailout/upside-recapture, credit-guarantee, debt-service, or sovereign-asset router.[S345][S346][S350][S351][S357][S360][S363][S369]
