# Worker ownership and broad-based equity design

Worker ownership can be a wealth-formation rail when it broadens claims on enterprise surplus. It fails when it becomes a tax, succession, retention, or public-relations tool that excludes ordinary workers or concentrates their retirement risk in one employer.[S235][S236]

## Design score

| Test | Pass question |
|---|---|
| breadth | Are low-wage, part-time, immigrant, younger, and frontline workers covered? |
| value | Is the claim material relative to wages, debt, rent, and retirement needs? |
| vesting | Do ordinary tenure patterns allow workers to keep the claim? |
| liquidity | Can workers convert value at retirement, exit, crisis, or transition without severe leakage? |
| diversification | Is employer-stock concentration limited or offset by pensions/savings? |
| governance | Do workers receive information, voice, board rights, or cooperative control where promised? |
| downside | Are workers protected if the firm fails, is sold, or loads debt onto the plan? |
| enforcement | Are fiduciary valuation and transaction protections real? |

## Certification consequence

Employee ownership can support a better verdict only when the design score is visible. Otherwise it remains proof debt. Broad-based equity may harden the middle but it does not answer wage-floor, mobility, or remedy failures by itself.
