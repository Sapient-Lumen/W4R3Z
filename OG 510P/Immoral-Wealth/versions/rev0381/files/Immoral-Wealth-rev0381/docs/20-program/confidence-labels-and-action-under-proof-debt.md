# Confidence labels and action under proof debt

## What this note is for

This note gives the archive a **thin rule for stating confidence without pretending to know more than the evidence can carry**.
Future case work should not speak as if one global confidence level covers everything.
A memo can have strong reason to call the verdict harsh, only medium reason to prefer one opening package over another, weak reason to trust current delivery rails, and almost no reason to certify de-escalation yet.
Without a compact rule here, future memos will keep improvising between two bad habits:
**false precision** and **soft uncertainty theater**.

The archive's standing answer is that confidence should be **split by question**, not reported as one mood for the whole memo.
And the archive should demand **more proof to soften a verdict or certify a pass** than it demands to begin lower-regret floor repair, washout closure, legibility, claimant-burden reduction, or anti-capture rail-building.[S04][S07][S15][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33][S39][S139]

## Minimum operating rule

Every serious case memo should state **four confidence labels separately**:

1. **Verdict confidence** — how strongly the evidence supports the constitutional judgment.
2. **Package confidence** — how strongly the evidence supports this opening package over the next-best serious alternative.
3. **Rail confidence** — how strongly the evidence supports the claim that the current state can honestly begin, deliver, monitor, and defend the package.
4. **Pass / de-escalation confidence** — how strongly the evidence supports any softer verdict language, any easing of posture, or any claim that the case is close enough to certify rather than merely improving.

That split matters because the archive is intentionally asymmetric.
It does **not** require perfect proof before starting every corrective move.
But it also does **not** let partial proof buy a comforting verdict, a celebratory narrative, or a fake sense that the dangerous surfaces are already under control.[S15][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33][S39][S139]

## The four standing labels

### 1. Certification-grade

Use this label when the case has **broad, source-tested, surface-matched evidence** on the decisive questions, little live opacity on the top tail or key controllers, and enough durability evidence to justify a real softening of the archive's posture.
This is the grade required for claims like:
- **provisional near-pass** with meaningful confidence,
- **near-ideal / constitutionally acceptable** language,
- real **de-escalation** out of a harder mode,
- or strong assurance that a package is not only promising but durably landing.[S15][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33]

### 2. Action-grade

Use this label when the case has enough evidence to identify the dominant breach, the fastest washout channel, the likely best opening package, and the honest rail limits, **but not enough to certify comfort**.
This is often enough for:
- a firm **correction-required** or **structurally failing** verdict,
- a real opening-package choice,
- begin-now floor repair,
- claimability and automatic-enrolment cleanup,
- tenant, wage, registry, subsidy-hygiene, or anti-washout moves,
- and rail-building for harder tools later.[S04][S07][S15][S21][S22][S23][S31][S39][S139]

### 3. Directional

Use this label when the evidence is strong enough to show **where danger likely sits** or which channel is plausibly reabsorbing gains, but not strong enough to support narrow calibration or a settled package comparison.
Directional evidence can still justify:
- protective starter moves,
- legibility-building,
- anti-secrecy and beneficial-ownership work,
- lower-half and entrant-side protections,
- burden-sharing conditions on support,
- and refusal to narrate the case as passable.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33][S39][S139]

Directional evidence is **not** enough for precise rate-setting, confident revenue projections, triumphal pass language, or relaxed watch rules.

### 4. Too thin to certify

Use this label when the evidence is so incomplete, mismatched, or strategically obscured that the memo can only make **narrow claims**.
In that situation the archive should usually do three things:
1. name the most serious missing surfaces,
2. avoid exact calibration or comfortable verdict softening,
3. and favor evidence-building plus low-regret protections rather than pretending the case is unreadable and therefore acceptable.[S15][S23][S31][S39][S139]

## The archive's asymmetry rule

The archive should require **stronger proof to go soft than to stay vigilant**.
That means:

- it should take **more** proof to certify a pass than to justify continued correction;
- **more** proof to de-escalate than to keep watch surfaces live;
- **more** proof to claim a package is durably landing than to admit that it is still provisional;
- and often **less** proof to begin claimability repair, tenant protection, wage-room repair, registry work, subsidy cleanup, or burden-sharing conditions than to postpone them in the name of elegance or waiting for perfect data.[S07][S15][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33][S39][S139]

This asymmetry is not bias.
It is a response to a recurring real-world pattern: opacity, delay, and thin evidence usually cluster around the most powerful actors and the most flattering narratives.
So uncertainty should more often harden certification standards than freeze ordinary protective action.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## The main false passes

### 1. One confidence label is given for the whole memo

A case memo counts less when it says only that confidence is “high” or “mixed” without separating the verdict, the package choice, the current rails, and the softer-certification claim.
That invites hidden slippage from a confident diagnosis into an overconfident celebration.

### 2. Thin proof is allowed to soften the verdict

Where controller visibility is weak, beneficial ownership is murky, top-tail valuation is contested, or excluded-group access is poorly measured, the archive should usually become **less** willing to certify comfort, not more willing.[S15][S23][S31]

### 3. Thin proof is wrongly used to block lower-regret protection

A case memo also counts less when defenders of delay say we cannot move on floor passage, claimant burden, tenant security, subsidy hygiene, registry-building, or burden-sharing until every long-run parameter is known.
That is often just paralysis dressed as rigor.[S07][S21][S22][S23][S31][S39][S139]

### 4. Calibration confidence is borrowed from verdict confidence

A memo can be very confident that a case is failing while only moderately confident about whether the first serious package should lean more heavily on one lane than another.
It should say so.
A strong verdict does not automatically justify narrow numerical calibration or a one-instrument fantasy.[S04][S15][S20][S23][S31]

### 5. Rail confidence is borrowed from announced policy intent

The archive should not treat legislation, press language, or official aspiration as proof that delivery, monitoring, recovery, and anti-capture rails are actually present.
Rail confidence needs its own evidence.[S23][S31][S32][S33]

### 6. De-escalation confidence is borrowed from package launch or early gains

A launched package, a better trend line, or one good cycle should not be allowed to buy confident pass language on its own.
Softer certification needs durability, closure, and cleaner proof than early motion does.[S20][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## Confidence-to-action map

### A. What needs certification-grade proof

Usually reserve certification-grade proof for:
- pass or near-pass language,
- real de-escalation,
- claims that anti-oligarchy danger has materially receded,
- durable-success narratives,
- and high-burden exact calibration that depends on strong top-tail visibility, strong valuation confidence, or strong implementation credibility.[S04][S15][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33]

### B. What action-grade proof can justify now

Action-grade proof is often enough for:
- serious verdicts on the failing side,
- first-package choice,
- floor-repair and claimant-burden reduction,
- lower-half and entrant-side foothold opening,
- rent-washout closure,
- beneficial-ownership and registry expansion,
- subsidy-hygiene cleanup,
- and burden-sharing conditions on public support.[S07][S15][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33][S39][S139]

### C. What directional proof can justify now

Directional proof is often enough for:
- watch retention,
- narrow anti-opacity measures,
- refusal to certify comfort,
- temporary protective restrictions,
- and rail-building steps that reduce future proof debt even before the whole package is fully calibrated.[S15][S23][S31][S39][S139]

### D. What “too thin” should trigger

When the memo is too thin to certify, the archive should not respond by narrating neutrality.
It should narrow the claim, show the missing surfaces, and bias toward **getting the evidence plus defending the likely losers in the meantime**.

## Minimal memo add-on

A future case memo can usually stay thin by adding one short block near the end:

- **Verdict confidence:** certification-grade / action-grade / directional / too thin
- **Package confidence:** certification-grade / action-grade / directional / too thin
- **Rail confidence:** certification-grade / action-grade / directional / too thin
- **Pass or de-escalation confidence:** certification-grade / action-grade / directional / too thin
- **Main proof debt:** one sentence
- **Action justified now despite proof debt:** one sentence

That is usually enough.
The archive does not need long epistemology sections in every case memo.
It just needs a stable shorthand that stops confidence from leaking across categories.

## Fast use rule

Use this note in six steps:

1. **Separate the questions.** Never report one overall confidence mood for the whole memo.
2. **Label the verdict first.** The archive is often more confident that a case is failing than it is about the exact package composition.
3. **Label the package second.** Say whether the chosen package clearly beats the next-best option or only does so provisionally.
4. **Label the rails third.** Do not borrow delivery confidence from policy intent or legal announcement.
5. **Label pass / de-escalation confidence last and hardest.** This is where the archive should usually be most demanding.
6. **End with what can move now anyway.** Confidence discipline should narrow and stage action, not excuse drift or comfort.

## Bottom line

The archive now has a compact answer to a recurring case-work problem:
**how sure are we, about which part, and what follows now?**
Split confidence into verdict, package, rails, and softer-certification confidence.
Require more proof to certify comfort than to justify vigilance and lower-regret correction.
That is how the archive can stay thin, honest, and decision-useful without collapsing into either fake certainty or fake humility.[S04][S07][S15][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33][S39][S139]
