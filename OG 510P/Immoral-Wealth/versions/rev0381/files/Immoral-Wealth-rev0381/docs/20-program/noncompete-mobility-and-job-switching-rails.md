# Noncompete, mobility, and job-switching rails

Job switching is a wealth threshold. It is how workers claim raises, escape bad managers, start firms, convert skill into pay, and avoid becoming dependent on one employer.

## Rail matrix

| Restraint | Wealth-order risk | Minimum rail |
|---|---|---|
| noncompete | suppresses switching and business formation | ban or narrow income/role/time/notice limits with private remedy |
| de facto noncompete | repayment, forfeiture, customer-list, or training-cost penalty functions like a ban | functional test based on whether switching is realistically penalized |
| no-poach / franchise restraint | lateral mobility blocked across nominal employers | antitrust/labor enforcement and contract disclosure |
| benefits lock-in | health care, pension, visa, childcare, or housing tied to one job | portability and transition protection |
| platform lock-in | ratings, reviews, data, and account access cannot travel | data portability, appeal, and human review |
| local monopsony | few employers and high search costs | antitrust review, wage boards, public hiring/training, transport/housing access |

## Current U.S. reading

The FTC national noncompete rule is not in effect and not enforceable after vacatur and dismissal moves, so worker-mobility certification must look to state law, sectoral rules, contract design, antitrust enforcement, and case-by-case remedies rather than assuming a national rail.[S225][S226]
