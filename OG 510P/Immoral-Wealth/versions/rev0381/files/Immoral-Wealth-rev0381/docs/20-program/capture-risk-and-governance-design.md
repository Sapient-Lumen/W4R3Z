# Capture-risk and governance design

A public institution that controls assets, audits, procurement, registries, land, housing, licenses, or benefits can either counter wealth rule or become one of its instruments.

## Capture-risk indicators

- leadership removable at will for enforcement decisions;
- opaque appointments or conflict waivers;
- budget controlled by regulated/captured actors;
- no independent audit;
- weak whistleblower protection;
- procurement or concessions awarded to related parties;
- revolving-door concentration;
- enforcement against opponents but not allies;
- suppression of statistics or registry access;
- judicial or appeal capture.

## Governance design minimums

1. statutory mandate;
2. ring-fenced budget or transparent appropriations;
3. published criteria and annual reports;
4. independent audit and inspector/ombud role;
5. conflict and beneficial-owner disclosure;
6. appeal and judicial review;
7. open data where privacy allows;
8. civil-society, journalist, researcher, and claimant access where legitimate;
9. emergency powers with sunset and after-action review.

The World Justice Project's 2025 evidence on global rule-of-law decline is a stress warning: capture-proof design is not ornamental. It determines whether reforms survive when checks on power deteriorate.[S177]
