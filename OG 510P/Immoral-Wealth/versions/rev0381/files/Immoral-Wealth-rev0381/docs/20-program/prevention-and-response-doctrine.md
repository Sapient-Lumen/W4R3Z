# Prevention and response doctrine

## What this note is for

This note turns the archive's posture lane into a **full stable governing-posture stack**.
The archive needs more than one policy package, but it also needs more than one **constitutional tempo**.
A society that is still broadly reformable should not always be governed as though wealth-rule were already consolidated.
But a society where closure, opacity, veto power, crisis laundering, and top-tail rule are already hardening cannot be read as though it were still safely in an early prevention phase.[S02][S03][S13][S15][S18][S19][S23][S31][S32][S33]

So this note answers a narrower question than the direct-answer note and a different question than the measurement note:
**once the archive knows the case's posture, what becomes first-order, what must stop being deferrable, and what kinds of delay should count as moral or constitutional failure under that posture?**

## Minimum operating rule

A serious governing-posture doctrine should usually do **seven things at once**:

1. keep the archive's **constitutional target fixed** across all modes rather than letting posture choice smuggle in a softer destination,
2. choose the **softest posture that can still realistically stop hardening and reverse the active breach**,
3. treat posture as a question of **what becomes non-deferrable**, not just of rhetorical intensity,
4. escalate when **wealth-to-rule conversion, opacity, closure, or emergency privilege** are materially strengthening rather than waiting for complete oligarchic consolidation,
5. refuse to let harder modes become an excuse to **suspend floor repair, entrant protection, or ordinary-life continuity**,
6. refuse to let softer modes become an excuse to **postpone anti-capture rails, legibility, or anti-dynasty work** once those are already structurally necessary,
7. and require evidence for both **escalation and de-escalation** rather than allowing political mood or reformist self-description to decide the posture by itself.[S13][S15][S18][S19][S23][S31][S32][S33]

The archive therefore rejects two opposite mistakes at once.
It rejects **under-escalation**, where a society keeps narrating itself as broadly normal long after closure and wealth-rule are already hardening.
And it rejects **mode inflation**, where every serious inequality case is described as if it already required the most conflict-maximal posture available.

The standing rule is:
**hold the target constant, choose posture from breach and governability, and read mode mainly as a decision about what can no longer wait.**

## The main false passes

### 1. The archive reads posture from one flattering distribution snapshot

A society should count less clarity when it treats one headline share table as enough to place the mode.
Timing failure, floor thinness, person-level masking, top-tail opacity, and wealth-to-rule conversion can justify a harder posture even when one stock snapshot looks less extreme.[S15][S18][S19][S23]

### 2. A reform-friendly government is mistaken for prevention mode

A country should count less realism when it assumes that one sympathetic election, manifesto, or governing coalition means the order is still safely in prevention.
The relevant question is not whether reform language exists.
It is whether the order is still materially correctable without first confronting entrenched veto, top-tail opacity, procurement capture, emergency privilege, or elite regrouping speed.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

### 3. Anti-capture work is treated as something prevention can always postpone

The archive should count less seriousness when broadening lanes are allowed to proceed while lobbying, political-finance, procurement, ownership visibility, conflict-of-interest, and emergency-repair rails are deferred as later governance polish.
In many cases those rails are what decide whether prevention remains real rather than theatrical.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

### 4. Correction is reduced to a rhetorical midpoint

Correction is not simply prevention with slightly angrier language.
It is the posture for cases where serious breach is already present but ordinary democratic corrective government is still materially possible.
A society should count less precision when it collapses correction into either a soft prevention mood or a total anti-oligarchy emergency.[S18][S19][S23]

### 5. Anti-oligarchy is reduced to “tax the top harder”

The archive should count less seriousness when anti-oligarchy response is described as little more than more aggressive taxation.
That posture is broader: anti-capture, visible ownership, key-asset governance, burden sharing in crises, public/social counterweight, and deconcentration fast enough that concentrated wealth cannot keep rewriting the rules while households wait.[S04][S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

### 6. Harder posture is allowed to crowd out household protection

A society should count less success when a harder mode becomes a reason to tolerate price pass-through, household instability, administrative chaos, or thin floor protection because elite confrontation is treated as the only real work.
The archive does not allow anti-capture struggle above to be purchased with greater insecurity below.[S07][S24][S25][S31][S32][S33]

### 7. De-escalation is granted on optimism rather than evidence

The archive should count less restraint when it moves back to a softer posture because conflict has become politically inconvenient, because elites sound calmer, or because the most visible symptoms are temporarily quieter.
De-escalation has to be earned by durable weakening of the actual channels that justified escalation.[S06][S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## The standing governing-posture stack

### 1. One constitutional target, three postures

All three postures still aim at the same destination:

1. the bottom half stops living near assetlessness,
2. the middle becomes socially thick rather than thin and debt-fragile,
3. the top remains rich but non-ruling,
4. public/social wealth materially lowers forced private buffering,
5. circulation opens across generations, groups, places, and persons,
6. and gains survive ordinary political, budgetary, and credit cycles.[S02][S03][S13][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23]

What changes across the postures is **not the moral destination**.
What changes is the answer to three practical questions:
- which breaches are already live,
- which lanes are now first-order,
- and what can no longer be deferred without effectively choosing surrender.

### 2. Prevention

**Core question**
- Can broadening still outrun hardening?

**Governing aim**
- Build a bounded-top, thick-middle, real-floor order before closure, dynastic sorting, and wealth-rule become too strong to reverse through ordinary reform.

**What becomes first-order**
- labor bargaining power and wage-floor repair,
- housing-entry reopening and anti-rent discipline,
- universal or near-universal services that reduce forced private buffering,
- early social-inheritance and starter-claim design,
- and basic anti-capture and legibility rail-building so later correction does not begin from administrative weakness.[S07][S13][S15][S18][S19][S31]

**What should not be postponed**
- basic lobbying, political-finance, procurement, and beneficial-ownership visibility,
- basic protected-minima and anti-punishment floor rules,
- and sequencing choices that stop broad gains from being capitalized into land, fees, or incumbent markups.[S15][S23][S31][S36]

The archive reads prevention as a **closing-window posture**.
It is not a calm default for every society that still uses democratic language.

### 3. Correction

**Core question**
- How can serious breach be reversed before it hardens into self-protecting wealth rule?

**Governing aim**
- Move a society back inside the archive's constitutional envelope where the floor is real, first footholds arrive earlier, dynastic closure weakens, and concentrated wealth can no longer rely on ordinary drift to reproduce itself.

**What becomes first-order**
- faster bottom-half asset repair,
- stronger anti-dynasty and capital-income reform,
- stronger housing, land, and anti-monopoly discipline,
- person-level title and portability where household measures flatter reality,
- and parallel build-out of delivery, legibility, and enforcement rails so corrective tools are actually governable.[S04][S05][S07][S18][S19][S23][S31]

**What should not be postponed**
- implementation capacity,
- anti-reconcentration discipline,
- visible middle-broadening design that keeps correction from being framed as pure punishment without entry repair,
- and early repair of the most exclusionary channels rather than only later balance-sheet cleanup.[S18][S19][S20][S23][S31]

The archive reads correction as the posture for **serious but still governable breach**.
It should be distinct enough to justify faster and less deferrable work without pretending every case has already crossed into oligarchic emergency.

### 4. Anti-oligarchy response

**Core question**
- How can a society break wealth-rule channels while also protecting households from the transition cost?

**Governing aim**
- Confront a wealth order that is already reproducing governing leverage through opacity, lobbying, procurement, media or key-asset concentration, crisis privilege, elite regrouping speed, or self-defending veto structures.

**What becomes first-order**
- hard anti-capture and conflict rules,
- rapid ownership and transfer visibility,
- deconcentration of key governing chokepoints,
- stronger burden sharing and repair duties in crises,
- public/social counterweight strong enough to matter politically,
- and top-tail compression fast enough that elites cannot use time, opacity, or emergency privilege to outrun reform.[S15][S142][S17][S23][S31][S32][S33]

**What should not be postponed**
- floor protection,
- housing and price-capitalization discipline,
- person-level security,
- portability and ordinary-life continuity,
- and repair duties that keep anti-oligarchy response from becoming a pure contest among elites while households absorb instability below.[S07][S24][S25][S31][S32][S33]

The archive therefore refuses an anti-oligarchy script that is all elite confrontation and no household stabilization.
A harder posture still fails if ordinary people experience it mainly as more risk, thinner continuity, or longer waiting.

### 5. Mixed packages without posture collapse

The archive does **not** treat the three postures as sealed boxes.
Prevention still needs some anti-capture work.
Correction still needs broad middle-building and ordinary-life entry repair.
Anti-oligarchy response still needs floors, footholds, and non-elite legitimacy.

So mixed packages are normal.
What matters is that the archive still names the **dominant posture** correctly.
If wealth-rule is already strong, the society should not keep describing itself as prevention simply because it also funds housing or baby bonds.
If the society is still broadly reformable, the archive should not jump reflexively to the most conflict-maximal posture available.

### 6. Escalation and de-escalation discipline

Escalate when:

- a major constitutional breach appears or hardens,
- wealth-to-rule conversion becomes materially stronger,
- opacity or implementation sabotage blocks ordinary correction,
- one-cycle gains wash out through rents, fees, reconcentration, or crisis laundering,
- or emergency derogations accumulate without real repair.[S15][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33]

De-escalate only when:

- the softer posture is supported by real evidence rather than missingness or public-relations optimism,
- improvements survive a meaningful durability window,
- wealth-to-rule channels are materially weaker rather than merely quieter,
- emergency privilege and repair debt have been substantially reduced rather than normalized,
- and the society is not simply passing the conflict cost downward while declaring elite calm upward.[S06][S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## Fast operating rule

Use the archive this way:

1. place the case with [`measurement-and-scoreboard.md`](measurement-and-scoreboard.md) and [`mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md`](mode-selection-and-escalation-heuristic.md),
2. use this note to decide **what becomes first-order under the chosen posture**,
3. use [`transition-sequencing.md`](transition-sequencing.md), [`country-pathways.md`](country-pathways.md), and [`minimum-viable-implementation-stack.md`](minimum-viable-implementation-stack.md) to decide how that posture can actually be carried,
4. and keep checking whether the chosen posture is still soft enough to fit the case but hard enough to stop hardening.[S15][S18][S19][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## Non-substitution rule

A society does **not** get to claim that a softer posture remains appropriate because it is doing some good broadening work somewhere.
Nor does it get to claim that a harder posture is justified while using that posture to excuse thin floors, household instability, or permanent emergency looseness.
The archive asks a stricter question:
**is the chosen posture actually matched to the live breach, and are the non-deferrable duties of that posture being carried together rather than selectively?**

## Bottom line

The archive uses prevention, correction, and anti-oligarchy response as **governing postures for the same constitutional project**.
The key question is never just how angry or ambitious the politics sound.
It is whether the society is still at a stage where broadening can outrun hardening, whether serious breach already needs faster correction, or whether wealth-rule channels have become strong enough that anti-capture and deconcentration can no longer wait.
