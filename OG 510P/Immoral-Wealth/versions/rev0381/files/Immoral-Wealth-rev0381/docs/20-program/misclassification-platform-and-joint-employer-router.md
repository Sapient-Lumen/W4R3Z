# Misclassification, platform, and joint-employer router

Use this router when a case shows low pay, thin benefits, unstable schedules, or weak remedies in a labor market dominated by contractors, platforms, franchisees, staffing agencies, subcontractors, or temp firms.

## Route order

1. **Classification:** is the worker economically dependent on the firm or genuinely in business for themself?[S223][S224]
2. **Control:** who controls price, schedule, dispatch, work rules, ratings, customer access, safety, tools, and termination/deactivation?[S227][S228][S229]
3. **Remedy:** which entity has assets, records, and legal duty to fix wage theft, retaliation, safety, discrimination, injury, or benefit failures?[S228][S237]
4. **Conversion:** can the worker actually claim wages, overtime, leave, benefits, data, appeals, and bargaining rights before the loss becomes irreversible?

## Platform rule

Platform work is not automatically exploitative and not automatically autonomous. Score actual dependence and control. The EU Platform Work Directive supplies a useful rail set: legal presumption, supporting measures, algorithmic-management transparency, human oversight, consultation, protection from dismissal/deactivation for exercising rights, and safeguards for intermediated platform work.[S229]

## Joint-employer rule

When the legal standard narrows to direct and immediate control, the archive should not assume responsibility follows actual economic power. It should record an evidence debt: whether workers can reach the entity that sets margins, standards, data, and discipline.[S227][S228]
