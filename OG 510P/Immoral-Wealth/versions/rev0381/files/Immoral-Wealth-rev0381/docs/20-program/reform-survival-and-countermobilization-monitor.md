# Reform survival and countermobilization monitor

A wealth-order reform is not certified at enactment. rev0310 adds a post-enactment survival monitor for reforms that threaten concentrated rents.

## Monitor windows

- **0-6 months:** legal challenge, emergency stays, agency rulemaking capture, implementation delay.
- **6-24 months:** budget cuts, loophole lobbying, guidance softening, staffing shortages, enforcement discretion, narrative reversal.
- **2-5 years:** repeal attempts, regulatory fragmentation, data collapse, court narrowing, local noncompliance, evasion industry adaptation.
- **5+ years:** institutional normalization or quiet hollowing.

## Warning indicators

- reform depends on one election cycle or one minister/regulator;
- large affected actors rapidly outspend claimant groups in lobbying or litigation;
- implementing agency lacks ring-fenced budget, data, or staff;
- public reporting is delayed until after key decisions;
- media or sponsored-expertise campaigns dominate the public explanation;
- courts narrow standing, remedy, or scope before implementation matures;
- affected wealth holders shift to less visible vehicles faster than enforcement adapts.

## Ratchet requirements

A reform can move from `correction_required` toward `acceptable_but_vulnerable` only when monitoring shows:

1. stable funding and staffing;
2. live enforcement and sanctions;
3. usable public data;
4. claimant standing and timely remedies;
5. reduced influence opacity;
6. no major reconcentration through substitute channels.

Without this monitor, a case may be celebrating a statute rather than an institutional fact.[S188][S198][S199]
