# Standing, claims, appeals, and corrective remedies

A claimant-facing wealth reform needs a remedy ladder that ordinary people can climb.

## Remedy ladder

1. **Automatic correction** — the system detects and fixes underpayment, non-enrollment, title error, offset error, or account misallocation without a claimant battle.
2. **Light-touch administrative claim** — plain-language form, no lawyer required, reachable offline and online.
3. **Independent administrative review** — independent enough to correct the agency or intermediary.
4. **Ombud / advocate / representative action** — for patterns, small claims, group harms, and repeated denials.
5. **Court or tribunal** — timely, affordable, with interim protection.
6. **Public enforcement** — where individual litigation is unrealistic.
7. **Structural remedy** — data correction, injunction, disgorgement, title repair, asset recovery, or agency practice change.

## Non-negotiables

- no irreversible dispossession before meaningful review;
- no expert-only proof burden for low-resource claimants;
- no appeal path that costs more than the claim;
- no remedy that depends on household gatekeepers when the harm is intra-household control;
- no private arbitration or waiver that disables public-law wealth protections;
- no sealed settlement that hides repeated public-value leakage.

OECD access-to-justice work treats unmet legal needs as interlinked with social and economic outcomes, and emphasizes accessible, affordable, high-quality services, governance infrastructure, evidence, and sustained investment.[S176]
