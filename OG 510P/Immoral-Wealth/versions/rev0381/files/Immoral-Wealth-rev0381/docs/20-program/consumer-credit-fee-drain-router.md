# Consumer-credit fee-drain router

Use this router when household income looks adequate but buffers do not form because the household repeatedly pays for liquidity, late timing, account access, or failed payments.[S260][S262][S268][S271]

## Routing sequence

1. **Identify the trigger:** income volatility, benefit delay, medical/care shock, car repair, rent/utilities, childcare, or underbanked status.
2. **Map the product chain:** overdraft, NSF, credit-card late fee, payday/title/pawn/installment loan, BNPL, earned-wage access, debt collection, subscription, or auto finance.
3. **Score repeat use:** one-time smoothing is different from recurring use for necessities.
4. **Score cost visibility:** APR, dollar fee, tip pressure, expedite fee, late fee, add-on, total price, and cancellation cost.
5. **Score remedy timing:** refund, dispute, forbearance, payment plan, complaint, and legal defense must arrive before wage garnishment, repossession, eviction, service shutoff, or credit damage.
6. **Route package:** if the drain is structural, do not prescribe only financial literacy. Use product design, fee caps, all-in price disclosure, safe account rails, hardship rules, public liquidity, and enforcement capacity.[S263][S269][S279]

## Package menu

- low-fee transaction accounts and safe small-dollar liquidity;
- limits on repeated withdrawals and re-presentment fees;
- clear total-cost and dollar-fee disclosures;
- ability-to-repay or affordability rules where repeat use is common;
- fee caps and anti-evasion definitions;
- debt-collection and credit-reporting correction rails;
- public emergency liquidity tied to claim conversion;
- complaint and redress systems with subgroup monitoring.

## Avoid

Do not route repeated high-cost borrowing to budget counseling alone. Counseling may help at the margin, but a wealth-order breach exists when the product structure makes volatility profitable and alternatives unavailable.
