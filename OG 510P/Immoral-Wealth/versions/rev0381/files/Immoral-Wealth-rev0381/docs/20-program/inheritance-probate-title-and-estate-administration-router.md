# Inheritance, probate, title, and estate-administration router

Use this router when a case depends on family wealth transmission or inherited property.

## Step 1 — classify the transfer

- lifetime gift or guarantee;
- education, housing, business, or emergency transfer;
- death-time bequest;
- retirement-account beneficiary claim;
- intestate succession;
- trust distribution;
- jointly titled or payable-on-death transfer;
- family land/heirs property.

## Step 2 — identify the failure point

| Failure point | Typical symptom | Route |
|---|---|---|
| no early transfer substitute | deposits/tuition/business entry parent-backed | public starter assets, baby bonds, public guarantees |
| will/probate access gap | estate too small for counsel, too large to ignore | legal aid, simplified small-estate route |
| intestacy | title fragments or excludes intended caregivers | will access, default-rule reform, caregiver protection |
| heirs property | no marketable title; partition risk | title clearing, UPHPA, buyout/fair-value safeguards |
| beneficiary error | account goes to wrong person or cannot be claimed | beneficiary audit, claim conversion, notice rails |
| tax/trust perimeter | high-end transfer sheltered | estate/gift/GST and trust-control review |

## Required evidence pack

- transfer timing and amount by income/wealth/race/gender/caste/region;
- probate cost/time by estate size;
- small-estate thresholds and legal-aid coverage;
- intestacy and heirs-property prevalence;
- partition-action outcomes;
- estate/gift/GST filing and revenue data;
- trust/control visibility and planning perimeter data.

## Default ruling

If only high-wealth families can afford transfer planning while low/modest-wealth families face probate and title traps, mark Gate 19 as warning or fail even when formal inheritance law is neutral on paper.
