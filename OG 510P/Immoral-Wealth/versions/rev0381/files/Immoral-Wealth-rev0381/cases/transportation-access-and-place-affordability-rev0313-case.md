# Transportation access and place affordability case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Correction required.** Housing affordability cannot be certified from rent or mortgage costs alone. The place must also let ordinary households reach work, school, care, food, health, and public offices without excessive transport costs, vehicle debt, unreliable service, or time poverty.[S254][S255]

## Dominant breach

The dominant breach is **mobility-cost washout**. Cheap housing can become a false foothold when car dependence, weak transit, long commutes, repair costs, fuel, insurance, fines, or inaccessible paratransit erase the apparent savings.

## Core reading

The H+T Index gives the cube a combined affordability frame: place costs include housing plus transportation. University of Michigan’s transportation-insecurity work shows why unreliable mobility is a material hardship, not a lifestyle preference. ITF/OECD accessibility work gives the comparative rail for treating access as a social-inclusion and infrastructure problem.[S254][S255][S259]

## Gate 15 finding

Gate 15 is **warning/fail depending on locality**. A housing case must add H+T cost, transport insecurity, disability access, job/care reachability, and vehicle-dependence debt before passing.

## Opening package

Frequent transit, fare capping, paratransit, safe streets, transit-oriented affordable housing, car-repair and insurance stabilization, anti-fine-and-fee mobility protection, and job-access planning.

## Evidence debt

Combined H+T burden by income, transportation insecurity rates, transit frequency/reliability, disability access, vehicle debt and repair cost, fines/fees/impoundment exposure, and job/care access by neighborhood.

## Rev0335 hardening note

The case is no longer seed-calibrated. BTS transportation-cost-burden evidence is now added as an official household-cost anchor and must be combined with H+T affordability, transportation-insecurity, and accessibility evidence.[S254][S255][S259][S467] A place cannot pass on cheap rent if work, school, care, health, court, and food access require high transportation spending, unreliable transit, vehicle debt, or fee/impoundment exposure.
