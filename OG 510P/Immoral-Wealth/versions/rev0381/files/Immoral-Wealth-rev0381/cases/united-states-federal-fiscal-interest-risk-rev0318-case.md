# United States federal fiscal and interest-risk case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Correction required.** CBO projects large deficits and debt rising from 101% of GDP in 2026 to 120% in 2036; GAO and IMF-style fiscal-risk guidance make this a public-capacity stress surface rather than a neutral macro backdrop.[S345][S346][S347][S349]

## Dominant breach

The breach is fiscal-capacity compression: high deficits and rising debt-service claims can narrow the public room needed for floors, infrastructure, care, enforcement, and crisis repair.

## Gate 20 finding

Gate 20 is **blocked for comfort certification** unless the case proves that public downside, debt service, and backstop obligations are transparent, financed, incidence-scored, and paired with ordinary-claimant protection or public upside recovery.

## Opening package

Publish a distributional fiscal plan that protects high-return public investment and lower-half floors, scores tax expenditures against direct supports, stress tests interest-rate paths, and prevents fiscal repair from falling first on services, localities, caregivers, disabled claimants, and young households.

## Evidence debt

Program-level incidence of net interest; tax-expenditure protection during consolidation; subgroup effects of debt-service adjustment; maturity and rate-sensitivity data; state/local pass-through effects.

## rev0319 Gate 20 operationalization addendum

rev0319 converts this from a short Gate 20 launch memo into a structured public-balance-sheet stress row. The scoreboard now includes a `public_balance_sheet_register`, `gate_20_subgates`, a `seniority_waterfall`, and a full 20-gate inventory.

### Active Gate 20 subgates

- **20A_debt_interest_capacity — blocked:** Debt and net-interest pressure are material enough to block counting fiscal capacity as an unconditional counterweight.
- **20H_generational_intergovernmental_incidence — watch:** Future cohorts and local public-service users may bear repair if consolidation is not incidence-scored.

### Seniority waterfall under stress

1. **Treasury creditors** — Legally senior payment stream. Interest is paid before many discretionary service choices become visible.
2. **Statutory entitlement claimants** — Legally strong but repair path contested. Benefit formulas are law but financing stress can induce prospective cuts or tax changes.
3. **Appropriated services and grants** — Politically contingent. Infrastructure, enforcement, care supports, and local grants can absorb crowdout.
4. **Future cohorts and local households** — Residual repair payers. Can pay through taxes, thinner services, delayed investment, or local fee/tax pressure.

### Case-specific evidence debt

- net-interest incidence by income/place/cohort
- tax-expenditure protection during consolidation
- state/local pass-through and service-crowdout path
- maturity/rate sensitivity under recession stress
- lower-half floor protection in repair package

### Operator implication

The case cannot use aggregate public capacity as a benign counterweight unless the stress waterfall shows ordinary claimants are protected before asset holders, creditors, or intermediaries, or unless public downside is paired with enforceable upside recovery and transparent loss sharing.

## rev0328 substantive hardening addendum

rev0328 promotes the paired scoreboard from `seed` to `active` because the evidence now supports operational scoring rather than placeholder doctrine.

### Active finding

Federal interest risk is a claimant-seniority problem. Rising net interest and long-term debt pressure should not be scored as generic fiscal anxiety until the case identifies whether ordinary public claimants, tax expenditures, asset holders, creditors, states, or future cohorts are senior during repair.
