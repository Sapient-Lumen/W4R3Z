# Germany renter-security case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Verdict:** `acceptable_but_vulnerable` with correction pressure.  
**Mode:** `mixed` / prevention-correction.  
**Dominant breach:** secure renting partly substitutes for ownership, but shortage, low social-housing breadth, and rising rent burden threaten the substitution.  
**Fastest washout:** rent increases, shortage, social-housing scarcity, and regional access pressure.

## Why this case was added

Germany tests whether the archive is too homeownership-centric. A wealth order should not fail merely because many households rent. The right question is whether non-owners have durable use security, affordable housing costs, savings room, and safe exit.

Germany is a strong calibration case because it has a majority-renter tradition and tenant-protection institutions, while also facing renewed housing shortage and rent-pressure warnings.[S117][S118][S119]

## Current reading

The case should not be treated as a full pass. The German rental model is institutionally serious, but the pass is conditional:

- tenant protections and renter subsidies make non-ownership more defensible than in owner-subsidy systems;[S117]
- household wealth remains unevenly distributed, so renter security cannot substitute for every missing asset claim;[S115]
- the German Council of Economic Experts stresses shortages, access limits, and a social-housing stock much narrower than Vienna's broad model;[S116]
- rent increases hit low-income households, single parents, and people living alone especially hard, which creates anti-averaging pressure.[S119]

## Threshold moments

1. first lease without parental guarantee;
2. rent increase or move in a tight city;
3. family-size change;
4. migration or relocation;
5. disability/accessibility interruption;
6. first attempt to build non-housing wealth while renting.

## Proxy status

This case is mostly proxy-calibrated. Rentership, legal protections, rent-burden evidence, social-housing limits, and PHF wealth data support the reading. Direct missing evidence includes parental guarantor use, deposit rejection, savings accumulation by renters, subgroup eviction pressure, and person-level control after family change.

## Opening package

Lead with renter-security preservation rather than an ownership-only program:

1. expand social/nonprofit/municipal housing where shortages are binding;
2. target low-income renters, single parents, and migrant-background households for affordability and access protection;
3. preserve tenant stability while reducing construction and access bottlenecks;
4. measure renter savings room and non-housing wealth accumulation;
5. prevent furnished/temporary or other loopholes from becoming a rent-control escape hatch.

## Reopening triggers

- rent overburden rises among the bottom income quintile;
- social-housing stock continues to fall as a share of rental housing;
- construction completions remain far below need;
- renter savings rates diverge further from owner savings rates;
- subgroup access to social/nonprofit housing deteriorates.
