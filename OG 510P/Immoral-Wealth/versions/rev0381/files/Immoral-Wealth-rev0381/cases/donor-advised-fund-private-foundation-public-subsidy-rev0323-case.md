# Donor-advised funds, private foundations, and public-subsidy timing control — active case memo

## Current holding

This case now treats charitable vehicles as public-subsidy and timing-control infrastructure, not merely private generosity. The key problem is that a tax benefit can be claimed at contribution while public benefit, payout timing, investment control, fees, family involvement, and ultimate beneficiary incidence remain delayed or aggregated. The current evidence package includes IRS charitable-vehicle materials, private-foundation statistics, DAF regulatory material, the DAF Research Collaborative annual report, and JCT tax-expenditure estimates. [S434] [S435] [S436] [S437] [S378]

The archive therefore blocks any charitable-vehicle comfort claim that relies only on aggregate payout or nominal charitable purpose. The case asks whether the public subsidy buys timely public benefit, transparent control, and recoverable public upside.

## Unit of analysis

- **Jurisdiction:** United States charitable-vehicle tax subsidy system.
- **Subsystem:** `charitable_vehicle_public_subsidy_timing_case`.
- **Dominant breach:** public tax subsidy can be converted into private timing discretion when donor-advised funds, private foundations, or related vehicles separate deduction timing from payout timing and control visibility.
- **Fastest washout:** contribution-year deduction, donor advisory privileges, foundation family control, account-level dormancy, investment/fee leakage, and weak public recapture can wash out the claimed public benefit.
- **Verdict:** `correction_required` with `medium` confidence.

## Minimum evidence package

The case now requires a subsidy-timing waterfall: donor deduction date, sponsoring organization or foundation control, advisory/family-control rights, asset balance, payout date, recipient class, fees/expenses, dormant or low-payout balance, and tax-expenditure value. Aggregate payout rates are useful context but do not prove timely public benefit without account- or vehicle-level distribution evidence.

## Correction path

The correction path is a public-benefit ledger. A case must connect public subsidy to public benefit over time and state whether recapture, payout minimums, dormant-account rules, fee transparency, or beneficiary-facing reporting exists.

## What would change the verdict

A softer verdict would require account-level or vehicle-level evidence that subsidized charitable assets move to public benefit in a timely, transparent way with limited donor/family control. A harder verdict would be justified by evidence of persistent dormant assets, related-party fees, family employment/control, or tax-expenditure growth without commensurate public-benefit timing.

## Limits

This case does not say charitable giving has no public value. It says that the cube should score private charitable vehicles as a public-balance-sheet/subsidy question whenever public tax expenditure funds private timing discretion.
