# Insurance scoring and surveillance-pricing case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Proof debt / correction required.** Insurance can protect assets only if access and premiums are not themselves governed by opaque credit, location, behavioral, or external-data scores that punish low-wealth households.[S291][S292][S293][S294]

## Dominant breach

The breach is **risk-priced foothold erosion**. Credit-based insurance scores, external consumer data, and predictive models can make required or practical coverage unaffordable precisely for households with the least capacity to self-insure.[S291]

## Core reading

The NAIC says credit-based insurance scores are used in underwriting and rating and can affect premiums.[S291] New York, Colorado, and the NAIC model bulletin provide live governance rails for insurer AI, external data, predictive models, unfair discrimination, and examination authority.[S292][S293][S294]

The FTC surveillance-pricing study broadens the concern: individualized pricing can use location, browsing history, demographics, shopping history, and behavior to set different prices for the same goods or services.[S290]

## Gate 17 finding

Gate 17 is **warning-heavy and unresolved**. Certification needs factor visibility, disparate-impact testing, vendor audit access, consumer correction rights, and affordability evidence for households exposed to loss.

## Opening package

Model inventory, external-data inventory, adverse-action factor specificity, proxy/disparate-impact testing, regulator examination authority, limits on credit/location/behavioral variables for essential coverage, affordability backstops, and surveillance-pricing disclosure/data-minimization rules.

## Evidence debt

Premium spreads by credit/score decile and subgroup, external-data factor inventories, model-audit results, denial/cancellation reasons, consumer correction outcomes, and price-personalization incidence.

## rev0330 activation note

The insurance/pricing case is now an active essential-protection case. The risk is not only actuarial classification; it is the conversion of credit, external consumer data, and surveillance variables into private tolls on insurance, mobility, housing, and other threshold protections.

Operational certification remains blocked unless the scoreboard can prove transaction-preserving correction: specific notice, report/model visibility, human review, dispute or appeal timing before the threshold moment is lost, deletion/suppression after relief, and subgroup outcome evidence.
