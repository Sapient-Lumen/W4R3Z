# United States noncompete and worker-mobility case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Proof-debt correction required.** The national FTC noncompete rule is not in effect and not enforceable, so a U.S. worker-mobility pass cannot rely on federal rulemaking that failed legal durability.[S225][S226]

## Dominant breach

The dominant breach is **mobility-restraint uncertainty**: workers' ability to switch jobs, start firms, and bargain for higher pay depends heavily on state law, contract design, litigation, and employer practice.

## Core reading

Noncompetes and de facto mobility restraints matter because job switching is a wealth threshold. When a worker cannot credibly exit, wages, benefits, working conditions, and business formation are less claimable. The failed national rule also shows why Gate 11 and Gate 14 must be linked: an economically plausible worker-power reform still needs legal durability.

## Gate 14 finding

Gate 14 is **watch**. Some states restrict noncompetes, and other legal theories remain available, but the federal rail is absent.

## Opening package

Build a mobility-restraint inventory: noncompetes, repayment clauses, forfeiture-for-competition clauses, no-poach agreements, benefit lock-in, licensing barriers, immigration sponsorship lock-in, data/rating portability, and local labor-market concentration.

## Evidence debt

State-by-state prevalence, income thresholds, de facto clauses, enforcement patterns, job-switching effects, wage effects, and business-formation effects.
