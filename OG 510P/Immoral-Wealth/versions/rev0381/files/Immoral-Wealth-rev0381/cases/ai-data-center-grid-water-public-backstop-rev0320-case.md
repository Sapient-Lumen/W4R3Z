# AI data-center grid/water public backstop case — active Gate 20 case

## Verdict

**Correction required.** This case should now be treated as a live public-balance-sheet stress case. Data-center electricity demand has become a live cost-allocation, reliability, water, and public-finance question. DOE/LBNL estimate U.S. data centers consumed about 4.4% of U.S. electricity in 2023 and could reach roughly 6.7% to 12% by 2028; FERC is addressing large-load and co-location tariff questions; and PJM/EIA now localize the risk in data-center-heavy zones such as Dominion/Virginia. ## What must be scored

The public question is not whether compute capacity is useful. It is who becomes senior when speed-to-power, transmission, generation, water, wastewater, tax-base concessions, and reliability costs collide. A case cannot pass merely because a project is framed as AI, national competitiveness, or economic development. It must show that ordinary ratepayers, water users, small businesses, municipalities, and future taxpayers are not residual insurers for speculative load forecasts. ## Seniority correction

Data-center uptime is not automatically senior to ordinary service wealth. The minimum passable package is: direct assignment of network upgrades, collateral or completion security, minimum bills and exit fees, enforceable load-flexibility commitments, water-impact fees, community-benefit terms, subsidy clawbacks, and public reporting. ## Rev0347 localized load-incidence note

Rev0347 moves the case from national projection to place-specific incidence. PJM reports broad large-load adjustments tied to data-center load and projects RTO summer peak load growth averaging 3.6% annually over the next 10 years, with a 2036 summer peak of 222,106 MW. EIA localizes the burden in Virginia/Dominion: summer peak load was 23,905 MW in 2025, 23% above 2019; winter peak was 25,413 MW in 2025-26, 45% above 2019-20; and PJM expects the Dominion zone to see the largest absolute summer-peak increase from 2026 through 2030 largely because of data-center load. The remaining proof debt is not whether the load is real; it is whether tariff, collateral, minimum-bill, exit-fee, water, and no-stranded-cost terms keep ordinary ratepayers and municipalities from becoming residual insurers.[S494][S495][S492]


## Rev0348 tariff-incidence note

Rev0348 partially closes the most urgent unfinished proof debt: the case no longer stops at load forecasts, FERC/PJM dockets, or generic “minimum bills needed” language. It now binds concrete state and utility tariff/service-agreement instruments. Ohio supplies PUCO adoption plus AEP Ohio Schedule DCT mechanics: 25 MW threshold, load-study fees, load-ramp commitments, an initial term of ramp plus eight years, collateral equal to 50% of total minimum charges for customers below specified credit/liquidity thresholds, minimum demand charges capped at 85% of contract capacity, capacity-reassignment rules conditioned on avoiding stranded investment, behind-the-meter controls, and exit-fee mechanics. Virginia supplies the SCC/Dominion GS-5 package: separate large-load class, 14-year contract obligation, 85% T&D minimum charges, generation-demand minimum charges, collateral, and future interconnection-process review. Pennsylvania supplies a model-tariff proceeding with cost-causation, CIAC, tiered collateral, minimum contract terms, low-income contribution, and flexible-service concepts. Wisconsin supplies both a We Energies tariff decision and an Alliant/Beaver Dam service-agreement decision that add 15-year terms, lower applicability thresholds, transmission-cost-shift revisions, full-benefits resource treatment, termination-charge safeguards, reporting, transparency, and future stand-alone large-load tariff requirements. [S496] [S497] [S498] [S499] [S500] [S501]

The remaining debt is now sharper and less doctrinal: compute the actual class-cost/rate impact and attach project-level water permits, abatement contracts, community-benefit clauses, no-stranded-cost clauses, and final orders/service agreements. The cube should not treat these tariff safeguards as a pass; it should treat them as the minimum evidence package for asking whether existing customers are actually held harmless.

## Rev0352 large-load-guidelines and Project Evest redaction-risk note

Rev0352 turns the Louisiana watch into a checklist rather than a slogan. They are non-binding, but the document says failure to address the Commission's concerns is a risk on the utility. [S516] [S520]

That is progress because Project Evest can now be tested line by line. May testimony says the Project Evest package would require seven new 1x1 CCCT generators, three battery resources, a 500/230kV interconnection substation, more than 250 miles of new 500kV/230kV transmission, and roughly $13 billion of generation capital, with additional transmission totals redacted. It also says the customer would participate in FRP, FAC, storm and resilience riders, would pay minimum monthly charges, had already made long-lead-item contributions, and that ELL proposes quarterly monitoring reports while preserving later prudence review. [S517]

The same evidence keeps the case failed. X-37921 is also a process warning. ## Rev0359 project-instrument sprint

Rev0359 moves the case from broad AI/data-center load concern into project-instrument triage. The verified edge count rises from 6 to 20 for this case, but certification remains blocked.

The most useful advance is that Louisiana's large-load standard is now represented as a test surface, not a conclusion: customer-caused transmission and interconnection costs should be assigned to the load, stranded asset and early-exit risks should be covered with rates/security/guarantees, and local/environmental impacts plus actual-load/cost-recovery performance should be reported [S516]. U-37425 now supplies a project-order instrument for the Meta/Laidley case: generation and transmission revenue requirements are eligible for regulated recovery subject to prudence, the private ESA/Related Agreements were not approved by Staff, parent guarantees and security are required, customer-caused material upgrades are not supposed to enter other customers' rates, and quarterly reports must track budget, schedule, financing, business, local/permitting, customer-performance, and environmental issues [S511].

River Bend remains the riskiest public-upside/local-incidence gap. The cube now binds Hut 8's water-infrastructure promise, the PILOT distribution reporting, Act 434/HB466 rebate authority, local reporting that rebate details remain unsettled, West Feliciana public-records access points, and Hut 8's private secured-note financing [S526][S535][S530][S536][S537][S527]. Those are real surfaces, but they are not yet the primary instruments. The next evidence must be the signed water agreement, IDB resolution, PILOT lease/distribution table, rebate ordinance, utility-service/line-cost treatment, and first implementation reports.

Operational consequence: do not add more general AI doctrine until the project instruments are bound. The case fails certification if the public cannot audit who bears upgrade, water, local-service, stranded-asset, and rebate-distribution risk after redactions and implementation are accounted for.
