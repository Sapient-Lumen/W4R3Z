# United States consumer-finance fee-drain case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Correction required.** U.S. households face a mature set of liquidity and fee tollgates: credit-card, overdraft/NSF, high-cost small-dollar credit, nonbank financial services, junk-fee sectors, and uneven state protections. Some bank fees have fallen, but the remaining exposure is concentrated precisely where buffers are thinnest, so a lower-half floor cannot pass from income or account ownership alone.[S260][S262][S263][S268]

## Dominant breach

The dominant breach is **private liquidity pricing as a tax on volatility**. Households with balances avoid many fees; households without buffers pay for late timing, failed payments, short-term credit, and nonbank account rails.

## Core reading

This case links Gate 8 and Gate 16. Gate 8 sees the liability-stack outcome; Gate 16 names the market mechanism that repeatedly prevents buffer formation. The archive should not count a household as wealth-forming while recurring fees, high-cost credit, or opaque prices consume the first surplus.[S271][S279]

## Gate 16 finding

Gate 16 is **blocked** unless the case shows low incidence of repeat fees/borrowing for necessities, safe account access, low-cost emergency liquidity, all-in price disclosure, and remedies that arrive before credit damage, wage garnishment, shutoff, eviction, or repossession.

## Opening package

Low-fee account rails, safe small-dollar credit, payment-withdrawal protections, fee caps or cost-based limits, all-in price disclosure, easy cancellation, credit-report correction, complaint/redress rails, and subgroup monitoring.

## Evidence debt

Repeat fee incidence by income/race/age/disability, nonbank product costs, overdraft and late-fee concentration, household total payment load, debt-collection outcomes, and state-by-state high-cost installment lending protections.
