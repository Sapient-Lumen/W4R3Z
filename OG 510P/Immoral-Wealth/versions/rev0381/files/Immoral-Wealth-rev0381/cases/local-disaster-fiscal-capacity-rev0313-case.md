# Local disaster fiscal capacity and rebuild-debt case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Correction required / provisional.** Disaster exposure can turn local public wealth into a liquidity test. A locality may have schools, roads, water systems, and tax base on paper but still fail when disaster destroys revenue, reimbursement is delayed, and operating costs spike.[S256][S257]

## Dominant breach

The dominant breach is **public-service continuity under climate and disaster fiscal stress**. If disaster recovery requires local governments to borrow, cut services, delay payroll, or raise regressive fees before aid arrives, household footholds become self-eroding.

## Core reading

FEMA’s Community Disaster Loan program is a useful calibration rail because it exists precisely for local governments suffering substantial disaster-related revenue loss. Carnegie’s local climate-risk work adds the forward-looking concern: climate risk can affect property values, maintenance costs, tax revenue, and municipal-bond pricing.[S256][S257]

## Gate 15 finding

Gate 15 is **watch/blocked** in high-risk places without operating bridges, mitigation funding, liquidity, fair rebuilding rules, and claimant protections for renters, small businesses, and low-wealth residents.

## Opening package

Pre-disaster mitigation, local liquidity bridges, reimbursement acceleration, disaster operating grants, public-asset insurance, climate-risk disclosure, debt-service relief, and anti-displacement rebuilding rules.

## Evidence debt

Revenue-loss estimates, FEMA reimbursement timing, reserve levels, borrowing costs, public-asset insurance, property-value exposure, renter aid, small-business continuity, and whether mitigation grants reach low-capacity places.

## Rev0335 hardening note

The case is no longer seed-calibrated.
