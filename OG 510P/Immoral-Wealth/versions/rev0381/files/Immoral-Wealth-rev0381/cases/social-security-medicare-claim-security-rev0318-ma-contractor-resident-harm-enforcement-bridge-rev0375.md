# Medicare Advantage contractor, resident-harm, and enforcement bridge — rev0375

This pass moves the MA branch from “appeal burden exists” toward the harder missing row: **who reviewed the request, which vulnerable enrollee group was affected, whether ongoing care was protected, and whether audit/enforcement produced restoration**. The case remains **not certified current**.

## What must no longer be averaged away

- Contractor identity: OIG reports that naviHealth processed half of the reviewed SNF admission requests, denied 14% of them, and had 97% of appealed SNF denials overturned [S579].
- Resident vulnerability: OIG reports SNF-level-care requests for nursing-home residents were denied 40% of the time, versus 11% for all other enrollees [S579].
- Request-level data need: OIG recommends regular request-level prior-authorization data collection with service type and contractor information, and the report page states CMS did not explicitly concur or nonconcur with the three recommendations [S579].

## New accountability bridge

- CMS program-audit materials and record layouts are an acquisition route, but CMS cautions that protocols and data-collection specifications are for audit/monitoring and are not themselves policy interpretation [S597].
- CMS enforcement actions include CMPs, intermediate sanctions, and terminations when plan sponsors fail program/contract requirements, but an enforcement list is not automatically beneficiary remedy [S598].
- The CY2026 final rule gives concrete obligation fields: approved inpatient admissions generally must be honored except obvious error/fraud, concurrent decisions fall within organization-determination/appeal rules, providers must receive notice when submitting requests, and enrollee liability cannot be settled before a contracted-provider claim decision [S599].
