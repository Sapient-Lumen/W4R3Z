# United States liability-stack case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Verdict:** `emergency_repair` as a subsystem stress case; reinforces the national U.S. `correction_required` verdict.  
**Mode:** `mixed` / floor-first plus anti-averaging.  
**Dominant breach:** liability stack converts illness, education, disability, and legal-system contact into blocked threshold moments.  
**Fastest washout:** medical debt/collections, student-loan delinquency, court/fines/fees, disability/care interruption, and credit-file drag.

## Why this case was added

The U.S. national case already tracks racial closure, family-gated entry, housing/debt washout, and top-tail rule. This subsystem case isolates a missing mechanism: **liabilities as active closure**.

A person can be poor because they lack assets. A person can also be made structurally dependent because hospitals, schools, courts, lenders, collectors, landlords, and credit bureaus hold claims against their future. The second condition requires its own gate.

## Current reading

The liability stack fails the real-floor gate and pressures the threshold-moment gate:

- medical debt and care costs remain broad enough to affect credit, borrowing, and care decisions;[S104][S105][S106]
- federal student debt remains large, with serious delinquency visible after payment resumption;[S379][S108]
- student-debt distribution must be tied back to household balance sheets and race/ethnicity composition, not treated as a neutral credential loan;[S109]
- fines and fees create state-backed debt and can carry civic/mobility consequences, with Black households facing disproportionate burden;[S110][S111]
- disability and care interruptions combine earnings penalties, extra costs, housing/access needs, and asset-test risks.[S112][S113]

## Gate readings

| Gate | Reading | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Floor reality | fail | ordinary shocks can become debts, collections, skipped care, or legal/credit blocks. |
| Threshold moments | fail | first lease, credit, car, education, care, disability onset, and safe exit can all be debt-mediated. |
| Anti-averaging | veto pressure | debt burdens and consequences differ by race, disability, age, income, household form, and place. |
| Washout | fail | debt service, fees, collections, and credit drag drain transfers and income. |
| Wealth-to-rule | watch | not the lead breach, but institutional revenue dependence and creditor/lobby channels matter. |
| Public/common counterweight | warning | formal programs exist, but too many claims require private debt or expert navigation before relief. |

## Threshold moments

1. first medical bill or diagnosis;
2. skipped care because of cost;
3. student-loan repayment restart or default;
4. court fine/fee assessment;
5. license/record/credit consequence from unpaid debt;
6. disability onset;
7. caregiving interruption;
8. first lease or credit application after collections.

## Proxy status

This case uses convergent proxy evidence. It does not yet have one unified person-level liability dataset combining medical, student, court, disability/care, and credit consequences. The convergence is still strong enough to fail the subsystem gate because multiple independent sources show liabilities interrupting floor and threshold moments.

## Opening package

1. medical-debt and collections shield;
2. automatic charity-care/coverage/claimability screening;
3. student-loan income protection, default repair, and subgroup repayment audits;
4. abolition of administrative criminal-legal fees, ability-to-pay fines, and legacy debt forgiveness;
5. disability/care income and asset-test repair;
6. credit-file, lease-screening, and license/record decoupling;
7. liability-stack reporting by subgroup.

## Reopening triggers

- student-loan serious delinquency rises or remains elevated;
- medical debt in collections grows or reappears through credit-report channels;
- skipped care because of cost rises;
- fines/fees or court debt remain attached to license, record, probation, or jail consequences;
- disability-onset evidence shows forced spend-down, housing loss, or household dependence.
