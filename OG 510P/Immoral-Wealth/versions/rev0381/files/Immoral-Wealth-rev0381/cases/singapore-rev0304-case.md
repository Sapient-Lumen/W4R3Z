# Singapore public-housing case — rev0355

## Case header

- **Case:** Singapore
- **Memo ID:** `singapore-rev0304-case`
- **Status:** active case / public-housing stress case
- **Date:** 2026-05-21
- **Case type:** public-housing asset state
- **Dominant breach:** public housing as broad foothold versus assetization, eligibility, lease, price, and person-level access constraints
- **Fastest washout:** resale price escalation, family-status gating, lease decay, CPF lock-up, and exclusion of non-citizen or lower-rights residents
- **Verdict:** **acceptable-but-vulnerable for citizen public-housing footholds; provisional / correction-required for full wealth-order certification**

## Why this case matters

Singapore tests the archive's most important counterexample to the assumption that broad private home equity must come from laissez-faire markets. HDB says it has built over 1.25 million flats and houses close to 80% of Singapore's resident population.[S78] HDB key statistics and annual reporting make public housing a live, measurable foothold system rather than a marginal program.[S77]

That does not automatically certify the wealth order. The case asks whether a state-shaped ownership system gives broad, rule-bound, early, usable, person-level footholds — or whether it turns a public asset into a price-rising, family-status-gated, lease-limited, and citizenship-bounded channel.

## The core tension

Singapore's public-housing system may pass several surfaces that Anglo housing systems fail: scale, planning, supply discipline, public land control, and ordinary middle access. But it creates its own false-pass risks:

- **assetization:** a public foothold becomes a speculative or status asset;
- **lease-limited wealth:** 99-year lease decay can make nominal wealth less durable across cohorts;
- **family-status gating:** eligibility and flat type may track household form rather than person-level need;
- **CPF lock-up:** wealth can be real but purpose-bound or retirement/housing-bound rather than liquid;
- **resident boundary:** migrant workers and non-citizens can be outside the best foothold path;
- **resale-price pressure:** broad ownership may coexist with entry inflation for later cohorts.[S77][S79][S86]

## Threshold-moment diagnosis

The decisive Singapore moments are:

1. eligibility for a first subsidized flat;
2. BTO waiting time and location choice;
3. ability to form a household outside preferred family forms;
4. CPF withdrawal and lock-up rules;
5. resale upgrade or downgrade decision;
6. lease-decay risk for older flats;
7. access for lower-rights residents and migrant workers.

A public-housing state can be morally superior to a private scarcity regime and still fail if the foothold is too conditional, household-gated, or price-inflated for later cohorts.

## Opening package

1. **Protect the public-housing foothold from assetization.** Keep supply, eligibility, resale, and financing rules oriented toward broad entry rather than incumbent capital gains.[S77][S79]
2. **Person-level access checks.** Audit whether singles, divorced people, widows, disabled people, low-income elderly people, and migrant/permanent-resident groups have defensible housing claims.[S86]
3. **Lease and liquidity clarity.** Treat lease decay and CPF lock-up as present-accessibility questions, not just technical finance.
4. **Public-land counterweight.** Preserve public land and planning capacity as non-patronage common-wealth rails rather than selling them into permanent private scarcity.
5. **Top-tail data.** Full wealth-order certification requires household wealth shares, private-business ownership, land/control rights, and family-office/philanthropic data.

## What would change the verdict

The public-housing foothold can remain **acceptable-but-vulnerable** if entry remains broad, supply stays responsive, resale prices do not close later cohorts out, and person-level exceptions are narrowed. It moves toward **correction-required** if public housing becomes mainly an inherited asset ladder or if eligibility/lease/lock-up rules leave substantial groups without usable, defensible footholds.

## Required next evidence

- bottom 50 / middle 40 / top 1 / top 0.1 private wealth shares;
- HDB ownership, resale, waiting time, and price-to-income by cohort and household type;
- CPF balances by age, income, gender, and housing status;
- lease-decay exposure by income and age;
- citizen / permanent resident / migrant worker access and exclusion surfaces;
- family office, private business, land, and top-tail ownership data.
