# OECD long-term care asset-spenddown case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Correction required.** Long-term-care need can erase modest wealth and shift unpaid labor to family members. Across countries, a public pension or housing asset does not settle old-age wealth security if care costs arrive before public support or require asset depletion first.[S277][S278]

## Dominant breach

The dominant breach is **late-life care need as asset liquidation trigger**. A household may appear secure until severe care need converts accumulated wealth into a temporary payment source.

## Core reading

This case qualifies pension/housing wealth and social-floor claims. The cube should ask whether older people and their families can access care without poverty-first eligibility, sale of modest assets, forced family labor, or geographically unequal care supply.[S277][S278]

## Gate 16 finding

Gate 16 is **warning-to-blocked** depending on country design. Certification requires evidence on out-of-pocket costs, asset tests, home/community care, wait time, quality, informal-care burden, carer income/pension loss, and whether support preserves assets before spenddown.

## Opening package

Universal or social-insurance long-term-care coverage, home/community care, asset-protection thresholds, paid family care/respite, carer pension credits, portability, quality enforcement, and early claim conversion.

## Evidence debt

Out-of-pocket costs by severity and income, poverty after care costs, asset tests, waiting lists, family-carer labor loss, gendered burden, home-care availability, and cross-country comparability.
