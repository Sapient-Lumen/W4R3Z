# Federal Reserve balance-sheet and quasi-fiscal visibility case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Correction required.** The Federal Reserve reports large balance-sheet assets, a deferred asset tied to accumulated negative net income, daily/weekly operating surfaces, and current IORB settings; financial-stability and emergency-lending reports show why off-budget backstops need distributional audit.[S359][S360][S361][S362][S372][S484][S485][S486]

## Dominant breach

The breach is quasi-fiscal opacity: central-bank balance sheets can stabilize markets, affect asset prices, and change Treasury remittances without being scored like ordinary public spending.

## Gate 20 finding

Gate 20 is **blocked for comfort certification** unless the case proves that public downside, debt service, and backstop obligations are transparent, financed, incidence-scored, and paired with ordinary-claimant protection or public upside recovery.

## Opening package

Treat central-bank facilities, remittance suspensions, securities portfolios, emergency lending, and financial-stability interventions as public balance-sheet surfaces; require transparency, sunset, facility loss reporting, and distributional incidence analysis.

## Evidence debt

Distributional effects of balance-sheet policy; who benefits from emergency lending; remittance-incidence modeling; relation to asset prices; household versus financial-market stabilization benefits.

## rev0319 Gate 20 operationalization addendum

rev0319 converts this from a short Gate 20 launch memo into a structured public-balance-sheet stress row. The scoreboard now includes a `public_balance_sheet_register`, `gate_20_subgates`, a `seniority_waterfall`, and a full 20-gate inventory.

### Active Gate 20 subgates

- **20D_crisis_backstop_governance — watch:** Emergency facilities need beneficiary, condition, and sunset visibility.
- **20E_public_upside_recovery — missing:** Public upside recovery from monetary backstops is not systematically scored.
- **20F_central_bank_quasi_fiscal_visibility — blocked:** Deferred assets and remittance suspension are visible but not yet distributionally incidence-scored.

### Seniority waterfall under stress

1. **Payment and Treasury-market functioning** — Operationally senior. Monetary operations prioritize market functioning and policy implementation.
2. **Reserve holders/facility users** — Receive liquidity/interest flows by rule. Distributional incidence needs clearer public reporting.
3. **Treasury remittances/public budget** — Residual when net income is negative. Deferred asset delays remittances but does not impair Fed operations.
4. **Non-asset households** — Indirect claimants. They may experience prices, rates, jobs, and fiscal effects without direct upside.

### Case-specific evidence debt

- remittance-suspension incidence on Treasury and public services
- interest-on-reserves distribution across banks/MMFs and households
- facility beneficiary identity and terms
- asset-price stabilization distribution
- quasi-fiscal loss reporting cadence and public comprehension

### Operator implication

The case cannot use aggregate public capacity as a benign counterweight unless the stress waterfall shows ordinary claimants are protected before asset holders, creditors, or intermediaries, or unless public downside is paired with enforceable upside recovery and transparent loss sharing.
