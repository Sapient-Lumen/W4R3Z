# Brazil case — rev0355

## Provisional verdict

`correction_required`, with `captured_immoral` risk in land and racialized closure domains.

Brazil is a **land/race/top-tail case**. The case tests whether a society with democratic institutions and social-policy capacity can pass while rural land concentration, racial wealth gaps, high asset concentration, and violence/state-capacity limits continue to shape wealth access.

IBGE's rural-area atlas and agricultural census materials show extreme land-size skew: very small establishments are numerous but occupy a small share of area, while very large establishments occupy a much larger share.[S101][S102] A 2025 Brazil wealth study finds white Brazilian families have roughly 1.5 to 2 times the wealth of Black and Brown families in available proxy measures.[S100]

## Dominant breach

`land_concentration_plus_racialized_wealth_gap_and_top_tail_pressure`

## Gate reading

- **Share plausibility:** warning/fail. Top-tail and land concentration require a hard correction.
- **Floor reality:** warning. Social programs matter, but informal work, violence, debt, housing, and regional inequality weaken the floor.
- **Threshold moments:** fail. Land, formal credit, safe location, first business stake, and education transitions are strongly stratified.
- **Anti-averaging:** veto pressure. Race, region, gender, rural/urban, Indigenous and quilombola land claims must not be averaged away.[S100][S101]
- **Washout:** fail. Land/rent, debt, violence, climate shocks, and local capture can erase gains.
- **Wealth-to-rule:** warning/fail. Landed, financial, media, procurement, and sectoral concentration need controller evidence.
- **Public/common counterweight:** warning. Public programs and development banks can help, but capture and uneven access are active risks.

## Opening package

Use Pathway D, G, C, and E:

1. race/region/gender wealth and ownership statistics;
2. cadastre, land-title, and beneficial-ownership rails;
3. land-concentration and rural credit repair;
4. urban housing and service-floor strengthening;
5. top-tail inheritance/capital/anti-avoidance review;
6. anti-violence, Indigenous/quilombola title, and environmental enforcement as wealth-order policy.

## Evidence debt

- WID-compatible top 1/top 0.1 wealth shares;
- direct racial wealth data beyond proxies;
- land title/control by race, gender, region, Indigenous/quilombola status;
- political economy of agribusiness/finance/media/procurement;
- climate and violence washout measures.

## Reopening trigger

Reopen after new household wealth surveys, land census releases, race/wealth microdata, or major tax/land/title reforms.
