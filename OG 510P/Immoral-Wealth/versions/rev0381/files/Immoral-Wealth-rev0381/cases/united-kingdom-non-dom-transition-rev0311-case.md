# United Kingdom non-dom transition case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Correction-required / watch.** The UK transition from domicile-based remittance rules to a residence-based system is a major status-tax repair, but the new four-year foreign-income-and-gains relief and uncertain behavioural response keep the case in watch rather than comfort certification.[S207][S208][S209]

## Dominant breach

The dominant breach is **tax-status stratification for internationally mobile wealth**. A resident class previously could live in the jurisdiction while shielding foreign income and gains unless remitted. The reform narrows that breach, but still uses newcomer relief to compete for mobile high-income/high-wealth residents.[S207][S208]

## Why this case exists

This is the cube's main case for distinguishing tax repair from exit panic. It asks whether a jurisdiction can end inherited status privilege without either preserving too much privilege or letting unverified exit narratives veto reform.[S209][S210][S211]

## Gate 13 finding

Gate 13 is **watch**. The repair improves residence-tax alignment, but certification depends on evidence about exits, remaining trust protections, FIG take-up, revenue, housing-market effects, and whether ordinary long-term residents face harsher claim rules than mobile entrants.

## Opening package

Pair residence-based taxation with data publication, anti-deferral, trust and inheritance-tax monitoring, exit-risk evidence, and a clear ordinary-resident comparison.

## Evidence debt

Actual migration and tax-base response, FIG claims, trust restructuring, revenue by cohort, high-value property changes, and distribution of relief by wealth/income.

## rev0336 hardening note

rev0336 hardens the UK non-dom transition as a reform-watch case: the shift to residence-based taxation is real, but FIG take-up, transfer-of-assets/trust/IHT restructuring, OBR revenue uncertainty, and actual high-wealth exits must be verified before the case can move out of watch.[S207][S208][S209][S469]
