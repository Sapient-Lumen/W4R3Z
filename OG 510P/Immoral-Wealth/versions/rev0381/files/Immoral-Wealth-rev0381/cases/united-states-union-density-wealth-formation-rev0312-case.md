# United States union density and wealth-formation case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Correction required.** The United States cannot count ordinary earnings as a broad wealth-formation path without scoring thin collective-bargaining coverage. BLS reports a 10.0% union membership rate in 2025 and 11.2% worker representation, far below the 1983 membership rate of 20.1%.[S220]

## Dominant breach

The dominant breach is **weak collective wage-setting and low bargaining coverage**. The worker may formally be free, but individual bargaining often does not create enough saveable room for housing, care, debt, and emergency buffers.

## Core reading

Union representation is not only a labor-rights indicator. It is a pre-distribution indicator: whether wage gains, benefits, hours, grievance procedures, and job security can become person-level wealth formation. BLS earnings tables show union-represented workers with higher median weekly earnings than nonunion workers, though the cube should not infer causal effects from headline medians alone.[S221]

## Gate 14 finding

Gate 14 is **watch/blocked**. The system has working collective-bargaining institutions but coverage is too narrow to carry a national wealth-order pass.

## Opening package

Pair wage-floor policy with bargaining coverage, first-contract conversion, anti-retaliation remedies, sectoral/area wage boards where appropriate, and enforcement budget.

## Evidence debt

Sectoral bargaining coverage, first-contract success, wage-theft recovery, union-election delays, nonunion wage floors, and interaction with housing/debt/care costs.
