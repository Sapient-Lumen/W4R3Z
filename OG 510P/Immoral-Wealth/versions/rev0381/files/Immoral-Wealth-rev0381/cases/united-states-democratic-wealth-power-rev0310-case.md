# United States democratic wealth-power case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Correction required with veto pressure.** The U.S. case already failed the basic wealth-share, floor, family-gate, liability, measurement, and enforcement screens. rev0310 adds that the political-power surface is itself a live blocker: large independent expenditures, dark-money routing, donor and lobbying capacity, state-level money, and local land-use vetoes can all reshape the feasible wealth-reform set.

## Dominant breach

The dominant democratic breach is **large-money and opacity leverage over the reform agenda**, with local homeowner veto as a second-order route into place closure. The issue is not that all political spending is illegitimate. The issue is that high-wealth actors can speak, litigate, advertise, lobby, and delay at scales ordinary claimants cannot match.[S184][S185][S186][S201]

## Why this case exists

The U.S. is the archive's richest stress case for the difference between formal voting equality and unequal constitutional voice. It also tests whether a reform that would repair floors, housing, tax enforcement, or ownership visibility can survive the actors most exposed to loss.

## Core reading

FEC cycle data show large aggregate campaign and committee flows and $4.4 billion in independent-expenditure disbursements in the 2023-2024 cycle. FEC guidance states independent expenditures are not contributions and are not subject to limits. Brennan Center analysis estimated more than $1.9 billion in dark money in 2024 federal races and says that figure likely understates the total because some categories cannot be reliably tracked.[S184][S185][S186]

## Gate 12 finding

Gate 12 is **blocked** until the case can show timely donor/spending visibility, dark-money transfer-chain disclosure, meaningful independent-expenditure and coordination enforcement, state-level political-finance coverage, lobbying/advisory transparency, and local-land-use entrant voice.

## Opening package

Route through political-finance and lobbying rails first, then local-land-use democracy where housing/place closure is dominant. Add dark-money transfer-chain disclosure, digital ad archives, state-level data integration, small-donor/public matching options, strong anti-coordination enforcement, and reform-survival monitoring.

## Evidence debt

Hard evidence debt remains on donor concentration by policy domain, industry-specific lobbying tied to wealth-order bills, state-level judicial and ballot-measure spending, think-tank/sponsored-expertise funding, and local-zoning veto maps.
