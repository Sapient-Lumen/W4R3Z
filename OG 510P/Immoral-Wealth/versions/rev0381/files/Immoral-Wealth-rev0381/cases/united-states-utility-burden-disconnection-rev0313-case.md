# United States utility burden and disconnection case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Emergency repair / correction required.** Essential-service access is too fragile for a real-floor pass where energy and water burdens, arrears, and disconnections are widespread. DOE’s LEAD tool shows low-income energy burdens far above non-low-income burdens, and EIA’s national disconnection data make completed shutoffs visible at a scale that must be scored as floor interruption rather than ordinary billing friction.[S250][S251]

## Dominant breach

The dominant breach is **essential-service interruption through utility affordability and shutoff risk**. This breach can convert a temporary cash shortfall into spoiled food, missed work, medical danger, unsafe heat/cold exposure, reconnection fees, collections, and housing instability.

## Core reading

Utility policy crosses three gates: Gate 2 floor reality, Gate 8 liability stack, and Gate 15 place public finance. Columbia’s energy-insecurity work gives the cube policy rails: better reporting, fair rate design, shutoff limits, and prioritization of disinvested neighborhoods. The LIHWAP implementation evidence shows why water assistance must be scored by restoration/prevention of service loss, not only dollars appropriated.[S252][S253]

## Gate 15 finding

Gate 15 is **blocked** unless the case can show low burdens, robust shutoff protections, transparent data, fast restoration, and public assistance that actually reaches households before service loss.

## Opening package

Percentage-of-income utility rates, arrears management, shutoff moratoria for protected conditions and extreme weather, direct payment rails, water affordability programs, weatherization, and mandatory disconnection reporting.

## Evidence debt

Disconnections by utility and household subgroup, arrears by geography, reconnection timing, medical-vulnerability protections, water-shutoff data, assistance take-up, and climate/heat interaction.
