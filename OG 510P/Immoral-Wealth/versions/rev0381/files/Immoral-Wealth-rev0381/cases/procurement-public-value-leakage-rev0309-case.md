# Procurement and public-value leakage case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Verdict:** `provisional` as a public-counterweight stress case.  
**Mode:** anti-capture governance and public-value protection.  
**Dominant breach:** public/common wealth cannot count as a counterweight when procurement, concessions, emergency spending, or privatization leak common value to opaque insiders.  
**Fastest washout:** public revenue is recycled into private fortunes while services and claimant trust degrade.

## Why this case was added

The archive increasingly relies on public/common wealth as a substitute or complement to private buffers. rev0309 adds the missing protection: public wealth must not be counted when the spending and contracting system is captured.

UNODC's UNCAC asset-recovery framework emphasizes tracing, freezing, preserving, seizing, confiscating, managing, and returning stolen assets.[S182] The World Bank's 2025 procurement/beneficial-ownership example shows how ownership disclosure can be used to screen conflicts, common owners, collusion, bid rigging, and anti-competitive practices in public procurement.[S183]

## Gate readings

| Gate | Reading | Reason |

| Gate 6 — wealth-to-rule | warning | procurement can directly convert political access into private wealth |

## Proof debt

1. Add procurement BO disclosure status to country cases with large public investment claims.
2. Track single-bid/no-bid awards and emergency exceptions.
3. Link contractors to beneficial owners and political/conflict data.
4. Track audit findings, debarment, recovery, and contract-performance outcomes.
5. Discount public wealth where leakage cannot be bounded.

## Opening package

Before counting public/common wealth as a substitute for private wealth, run the procurement and public-value leakage screen. Where leakage is severe, route to transparency, conflict control, beneficial ownership, open contracting, and asset recovery.
