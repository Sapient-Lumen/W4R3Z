# Vienna social housing case — rev0355

## Provisional verdict

`acceptable_but_vulnerable` as a housing subsystem; `provisional` as a full wealth-order case.

Vienna is a **nonmarket housing buffer case**. The City of Vienna reports about 220,000 municipal flats and 200,000 cooperative flats; it also describes eligibility and access rules.[S95] External research commonly treats Vienna as an unusually large social-housing system, with more than 40% of units in the social-housing sector.[S96]

The case tests whether social housing can substitute for private asset accumulation by suppressing rent washout and reducing the need for defensive housing wealth.

## Dominant breach

`housing_washout_buffer_with_eligibility_and_transferability_questions`

## Gate reading

- **Share plausibility:** provisional. This is a city/subsystem case, not a full national wealth distribution.
- **Floor reality:** watch/pass for residents with access. Housing stability can reduce the private buffer burden.
- **Threshold moments:** watch. The lease/access moment may be much less family-gated, but eligibility, waiting, and migrant/residency rules matter.
- **Anti-averaging:** warning. The claimant boundary is central: who can enter the system, when, and under what status?
- **Washout:** pass/watch. Rent speculation is structurally dampened, but maintenance, energy/climate retrofit, and supply pressure remain.
- **Wealth-to-rule:** watch. Large public/social ownership can reduce landlord rule, but governance must remain public-facing.
- **Public/common counterweight:** pass/watch. Strong as a housing counterweight, not automatically a complete wealth floor.

## Opening package

Use Pathway C and F:

1. treat social housing as a washout-blocking asset substitute;
2. measure access by age, migrant status, household form, disability, and income;
3. protect maintenance and climate retrofit funding;
4. score portability and exit;
5. compare private renter outcomes with social/cooperative tenant outcomes.

## Evidence debt

- distribution of access by subgroup and residency status;
- waiting times and informal barriers;
- long-term maintenance and climate retrofit financing;
- whether reduced rent burden converts into savings, education, care security, or business starts;
- relation between city housing model and national wealth shares.

## Reopening trigger

Reopen if eligibility rules, supply share, rent burden, climate retrofit costs, or social-housing governance materially change.
