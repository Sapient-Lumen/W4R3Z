# India case — rev0355

## Provisional verdict

`captured_immoral`.

India is an **extreme top-tail plus caste/gender/title closure case**. World Inequality Lab estimates for 2022–23 put the top 1% wealth share at 40.1%, the top 0.1% at 29.7%, the top 10% at 65.0%, the middle 40% at 28.6%, and the bottom 50% at 6.4%.[S97] This fails the target-family screen before the archive even reaches caste, gender, title, informality, or state-capacity questions.

## Dominant breach

`extreme_top_tail_plus_caste_gender_title_and_informal_floor_closure`

## Gate reading

- **Share plausibility:** fail. Top-tail concentration is far outside target-family bands.[S97][S103]
- **Floor reality:** fail/warning. Bottom-half assets are too thin relative to shocks, informal work, care, health, land, and credit risks.
- **Threshold moments:** fail. Land/title, education, marriage, migration, business, and housing thresholds are mediated by family, caste, gender, region, and credit.
- **Anti-averaging:** veto pressure. Caste, religion, gender, rural/urban, region, and household-control splits are not secondary variables.[S98][S99]
- **Washout:** fail. Debt, health, informal credit, land insecurity, housing, dowry/marriage costs, and climate risk can rapidly erase gains.
- **Wealth-to-rule:** fail. Billionaire and top-one-percent concentration create rule-conversion pressure.
- **Public/common counterweight:** warning. Public programs matter but do not yet override closure and thin private floor.

## Opening package

Use Pathway G, D, A, and E:

1. land/title and person-level ownership records;
2. caste/gender/religion/region wealth statistics;
3. protected floor and health/debt shock shields;
4. inheritance, land, and marital-property enforcement;
5. beneficial ownership and top-tail tax capacity;
6. public goods that reduce the need for private defensive wealth.

## Evidence debt

The case already fails on top-tail concentration. Remaining evidence debt affects package order and subgroup veto detail:

- direct caste/gender wealth/control split;
- land title by person, sex, caste, and religion;
- informal debt and health-shock washout;
- top-tail/offshore/business-group ownership;
- regional housing and climate-risk exposure.

## Reopening trigger

Reopen after new WID/national wealth estimates, caste/gender land data, or major tax/land/beneficial ownership reforms.
