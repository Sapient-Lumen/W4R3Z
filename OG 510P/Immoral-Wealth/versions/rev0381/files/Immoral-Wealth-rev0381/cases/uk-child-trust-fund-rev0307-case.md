# UK Child Trust Fund case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Verdict:** `provisional` as a starter-asset subsystem case.  
**Mode:** `proof_debt` / correction.  
**Dominant breach:** publicly seeded accounts were real, but claim finality at maturity was incomplete.  
**Fastest washout:** lost-account friction, weak tracking, low awareness, and private-provider fragmentation.

## Why this case was added

The UK Child Trust Fund is one of the archive’s best calibration cases for starter assets. It shows both the promise and the danger of account-based social inheritance.

The promise: a public seed was attached to children, including children whose parents did not open accounts. The danger: a program can create accounts and still fail to deliver mature assets into the hands of intended young adults. HMRC reported in 2025 that 758,000 young people had not yet claimed matured Child Trust Funds, with an average value around £2,242.[S133] The National Audit Office found earlier that many matured accounts remained unclaimed and that HMRC had opened a large number of accounts when parents did not do so.[S134]

## Current reading

This case is neither a simple success nor a simple failure.

It is a success because the assets are real and legally attached to young people. It is a failure pressure because claim conversion remains incomplete. The account must be discoverable, reachable, low-fee, and usable at the threshold moment. If the intended adult does not know the account exists or cannot find the provider, the moral value is delayed and weakened.

## Threshold moments

1. 16th birthday and first right to manage;
2. 18th birthday and first right to withdraw/reinvest;
3. school-to-work or school-to-university transition;
4. first housing deposit or training cost;
5. leaving care or unstable family contact;
6. lost-provider search;
7. decision to save, withdraw, invest, or transfer.

## Lessons for starter assets

A starter account must include:

- automatic opening or fail-safe public opening;
- claimant notification before maturity;
- no-fee public account tracing;
- provider continuity rules;
- school, care-system, and community outreach;
- simple digital and nondigital claim routes;
- progressive top-ups or matches so higher-income family contributions do not dominate;
- clear rules for youth in care, estranged youth, disabled youth, and young people without parental support.

## Doctrine consequence

The archive should not count a starter-account program at face value. It should count **matured, found, fee-protected, claimant-controlled, usable balances**. Anything else belongs in evidence debt.
