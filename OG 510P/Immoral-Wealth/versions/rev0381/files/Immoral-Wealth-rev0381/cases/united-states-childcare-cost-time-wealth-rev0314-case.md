# United States childcare cost and time-wealth case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Correction required / emergency watch for affected households.** Childcare costs and availability are not simply household expenses; they shape whether parents can work, train, save, move, and preserve autonomy. When one child's full-day care can rival rent or consume a large share of median family income, childcare becomes a cost-of-life tollgate.[S273][S274]

## Dominant breach

The dominant breach is **care cost as earnings and savings interruption**. The household pays directly for care and indirectly through reduced hours, missed work, foregone advancement, delayed training, partner dependence, and family-gated backup.

## Core reading

This case links Gate 16 to workplace power and person-level control. The childcare problem is not only the price of care; it is whether care exists at the hours, location, quality, and stability needed to make work and asset-building real. Census evidence on childcare costs and mothers' labor-force participation makes this a wealth-formation question, not merely a family-policy question.[S275]

## Gate 16 finding

Gate 16 is **blocked** unless the case can show affordable net care, adequate supply, hours fit, low subsidy cliffs, claimable assistance, and protection against forced labor-force exit.

## Opening package

Public or capped-price childcare, supply-side provider funding, caregiver wage/quality standards, subsidy simplification, copay cliffs reduction, extended-hours and transport-aware care, paid leave, caregiver credits, and person-level portability.

## Evidence debt

County-level price/supply, waitlists, hours fit, subsidy take-up and cliffs, parental labor-force exits, race/gender/class subgroup effects, provider wages, quality and safety, and family backup dependence.
