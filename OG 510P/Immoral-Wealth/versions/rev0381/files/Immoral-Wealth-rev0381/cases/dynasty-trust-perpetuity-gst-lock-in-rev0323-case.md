# Dynasty trust, perpetuity duration, GST lock-in, and control opacity — active case memo

## Current holding

This case now treats dynasty trusts as an active control-duration problem rather than a seed example. The relevant risk is not merely that wealth is inherited. It is that trust law can separate nominal ownership, beneficial enjoyment, fiduciary title, investment control, protector/advisor powers, tax allocation, and beneficiary remedy access across time. That separation can make dynastic wealth appear less concentrated, less controlled, or less claimable than it is. [S427] [S428] [S431] [S439] [S440]

The controlling frame is a three-part map: duration, control, and visibility. Duration asks how long the vehicle can persist under governing law and asset class. Control asks who can direct investment, distribution, removal, decanting, migration, and protector powers. Visibility asks whether tax authorities, beneficiaries, creditors, spouses, public registries, or competent authorities can identify those control parties.

## Unit of analysis

- **Jurisdiction:** U.S. trust jurisdictions, with Delaware and South Dakota used as duration/opacity stress surfaces rather than exhaustive coverage.
- **Subsystem:** `dynasty_trust_perpetuity_gst_lock_in_case`.
- **Dominant breach:** a trust can preserve family control and tax advantages while distributing legal title, beneficial interests, and control powers across parties who are difficult for ordinary claimants to observe.
- **Fastest washout:** long duration, GST allocation, decanting, trust migration, directed-trust powers, and weak beneficiary/creditor visibility can wash out intergenerational comparability.
- **Verdict:** `correction_required` with `medium` confidence.

## Minimum evidence package

The case now requires a jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction table for trust duration and asset-class limits; a control-party table for settlor, trustee, protector, advisor, investment committee, distribution committee, beneficiary, and decanting actor; a GST-allocation/perpetuity-risk note; and a claimant-remedy note describing who can obtain accountings or beneficial-ownership information.

## Correction path

The correction path is to make the trust-control map unavoidable. A case cannot cite a dynasty trust merely as “family planning” or “tax planning.” It must say who controls which powers, for how long, under which law, with what information rights and what public or beneficiary remedy.

## What would change the verdict

A softer verdict would require enforceable beneficial-ownership, accounting, and control-party disclosure that remains accurate after migration, decanting, or protector substitution. A harder verdict would be justified by evidence that high-wealth transfer planning routinely uses long-duration trusts to defeat tax incidence, creditor visibility, marital/community-property claims, or beneficiary remedy access.

## Limits

This case does not claim every trust is abusive. It targets high-duration, high-discretion, low-visibility structures where private control can outlast ordinary claimant and democratic review cycles.
