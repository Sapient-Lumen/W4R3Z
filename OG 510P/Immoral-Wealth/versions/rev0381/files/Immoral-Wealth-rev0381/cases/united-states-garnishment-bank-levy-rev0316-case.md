# United States garnishment, bank levy, and judgment protection case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Correction required.** Federal wage-garnishment limits exist, but state exemption systems determine whether post-judgment collection preserves a household's recovery floor.[S304][S305][S306]

## Dominant breach

The dominant breach is **non-self-executing and uneven protected minima**. A family can have formal exemption rights and still lose rent money, car access, work tools, or a bank balance if protection requires fast legal action after a freeze or garnishment.[S305][S320]

## Gate 18 finding

Gate 18 is **blocked for comfort certification** where protected wages and bank balances are too low, nonautomatic, or hard to claim.

## Opening package

Self-executing bank-account minima; stronger wage floors; protected-benefit tracing after deposit; realistic car/tools/home-goods exemptions; multilingual notices; bank/employer compliance; hardship modification; and data on freezes, releases, and household outcomes.

## Evidence debt

State-by-state self-execution, account-freeze volumes, exemption claim success, wage-garnishment orders, employer compliance, bank fees after freeze, and protected-balance adequacy by household type.

## Rev0331 active scoreboard hardening

Rev0331 makes this an active protected-recovery-floor case. Wage and bank-account protection must be automatic or fast enough to preserve rent, utilities, transport, food, medicine, and work tools before levy or turnover.
