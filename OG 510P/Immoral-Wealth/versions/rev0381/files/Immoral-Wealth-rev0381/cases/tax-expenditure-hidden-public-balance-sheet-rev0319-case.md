# Tax expenditures as a hidden public balance-sheet allocation — rev0369 claim migration

## Current noncertifying verdict

This case is now a **locator-bound, noncertifying claim packet**. The cube may say that federal tax expenditures allocate public fiscal capacity through the tax code and that some large preferences are concentrated among higher-income claimants. It may not yet say that every tax expenditure is harmful, that Treasury rows can be summed into a grand total, or that the final economic beneficiary of each preference is proved. [S378] [S574] [S575] [S576] [S577]

## What is proved enough to use

Treasury defines tax expenditures as revenue losses attributable to special exclusions, deductions, credits, preferential rates, and deferrals, and frames them as alternatives to spending or regulatory programs. The FY2027 tables are current-law estimates based on law enacted as of July 31, 2025, and they warn against a simple grand total because provisions interact. [S574] [S577]

Treasury's top ten-year rows make the scale concrete: employer medical exclusion, imputed rental income, defined-contribution plans, capital gains, home-sale exclusion, QBI/pass-through deduction, defined-benefit plans, accelerated depreciation, charitable contributions, and step-up basis at death are all large enough to matter for a public-balance-sheet gate. [S574]

CBO's distributional work supplies the incidence warning: preferential rates on capital gains/dividends and the QBI deduction skewed sharply upward in 2019, while refundable credits and some broad preferences qualify any blanket anti-tax-expenditure verdict. [S575]

CBO's Public Law 119-21 distribution route also prevents the cube from treating tax provisions alone as the whole story: household resources can change through federal taxes, transfers, in-kind programs, state fiscal responses, other spending, and revenues. [S576]

## Certification blockers

1. For each top preference, bind statutory section, current-law ten-year estimate, claimant incidence, economic incidence, interaction-adjusted package effect, and refresh trigger.
2. Distinguish upward-skewed exclusions/deductions/preferential rates from refundable credits and broad ordinary-claimant supports.
3. Build a direct-spending counterfactual: what would the same public capacity buy if appropriated to floors, enforcement, public assets, or lower-half household claims?
4. Identify public-upside recovery: recapture, clawback, phaseout, minimum tax, sunset review, or equivalent public claim when private asset values rise.
5. Track Public Law 119-21 and future tax changes through CBO/JCT distributional channels, including state response and other spending/revenue effects.

## Decision rule

Keep the case **not certified current** until the cube can walk a major preference from legal authority to claimant incidence to fiscal residual bearer to public-upside recovery or explicit absence of recovery. [S574] [S575] [S576]
