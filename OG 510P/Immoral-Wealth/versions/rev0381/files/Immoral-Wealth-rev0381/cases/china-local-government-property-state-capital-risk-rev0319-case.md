# China local-government, property, and state-capital risk case — rev0355

## Verdict

**correction required.** This is a Gate 20 stress case, not a full country certification. The unit of analysis is the public/private balance-sheet surface that can turn a nominal private market, tax preference, credit program, or public promise into a public-risk allocation.

## Dominant breach

property-sector adjustment, local-government finance, LGFVs, and state-capital channels can convert development finance into hidden public balance-sheet stress.

## Fastest washout

local debt repair and property losses compress household wealth, municipal services, land revenue, and bank/credit stability.

## Gate 20 subgate findings

- **20A_debt_interest_capacity — watch:** Debt and revenue stress can compress local public capacity.
- **20C_contingent_liabilities_guarantees — blocked:** Implicit guarantees and LGFV/state-capital obligations require register treatment.
- **20D_crisis_backstop_governance — watch:** Rescue priority among households, developers, banks, and local governments needs clarity.
- **20H_generational_intergovernmental_incidence — watch:** Local residents and future taxpayers can bear repair after land-revenue and property stress.

## Seniority waterfall under stress

1. **Homebuyers needing completion** — High social priority but not always legally senior. Presale completion can be protected even when property values fall.
2. **Banks/creditors/LGFVs** — Often central to stability repair. Financial-stability claims can outrank household wealth repair.
3. **Local governments/public services** — Revenue-stressed intermediaries. Land finance weakness compresses local service capacity.
4. **Households/future taxpayers** — Residual repair bearers. Can pay through wealth loss, taxes, service cuts, or slower growth.

## Main scoreboard fields

- `public_balance_sheet_gate`: warning — China-style state/local/property balance-sheet links require Gate 20 treatment.
- `china_local_government_hidden_debt_exposure`: warning — IMF Article IV material highlights local-government/property-sector spillovers and debt overhang.
- `property_sector_public_balance_sheet_spillover`: warning — Property-sector adjustment can hit households, developers, banks, LG finances, and public services.
- `state_local_intergovernmental_burden_shift`: warning — Hidden-debt swaps and local finance repair can shift burden across levels of government.
- `contingent_liability_disclosure`: missing — LGFV, SOE, and implicit guarantee exposure needs standardized public register.

## Opening package

The case should not receive a comfort verdict until it has a public balance-sheet register, a claimant seniority waterfall, stress assumptions, ordinary-claimant protection, and public-upside recovery where public downside is used.

## Evidence debt

- LGFV debt perimeter and official/hidden debt reconciliation
- developer, household, bank, SOE, and local-government loss waterfall
- land-sale revenue dependency and public-service incidence
- household presale/housing-wealth protection versus developer/creditor rescue
- central-local burden-shift rules and public-upside recovery

## Research and speculation

The speculative risk is that this surface will look private or technocratic until a shock arrives. At that point, public authorities may protect market functioning, creditors, property values, or payment continuity first, while ordinary households absorb price increases, service cuts, claim delays, debt collection, or intergenerational repair. The cube should therefore score rescue priority before the rescue occurs, not only after an obvious bailout.
