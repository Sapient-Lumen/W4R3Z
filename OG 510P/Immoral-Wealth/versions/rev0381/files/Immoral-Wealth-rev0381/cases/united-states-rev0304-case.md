## Why this case remains first

The United States is the archive's highest-pressure first case because it combines high private wealth, deep financial markets, weakly universalized private-risk protection, strong local housing exclusion, racialized group closure, and large top-tail political/economic infrastructure. It is therefore a bad test if the archive wants a friendly example and a good test if the archive wants to avoid false passes.

The case does not fail because it has markets, entrepreneurship, homeownership, or private savings. It fails because too many first footholds require a family balance sheet, too many footholds are vulnerable to rent, debt, care, insurance, and crisis costs, and too much top-tail wealth can convert into rule through political finance, lobbying, private vehicles, philanthropy, stewardship, and asset-price rescue channels.[S12][S57][S435][S64][S418][S70]

## Threshold-moment diagnosis

The decisive moments are not only annual income moments. They are threshold moments:

1. **first independent lease or down payment;**
2. **first major car, medical, legal, or relocation shock;**
3. **college or training debt choice;**
4. **first business start, license, or unpaid-care interruption;**
5. **first mortgage-rate / insurance / repair shock;**
6. **first inheritance, gift, guarantee, or family bridge loan.**

A wealth order where those threshold moments are routinely passable only with parental deposits, guarantees, debt capacity, inherited place, or family emergency liquidity is not open-circulation even if it later reports broad retirement-account participation. This is the core rev0302 family-gate result carried forward into case work.[S18][S19][S52]

## Opening package

1. **Lane 4 — early footholds first.** Build non-family entry routes: child/social-inheritance accounts, first-home/renter mobility supports not capitalized into prices, first-business credit with anti-extraction rules, and early retirement/liquid buffer matching for low-wealth young adults.[S12][S18][S19]
2. **Lane 5 — washout closure second.** Housing supply, tenant security, land-value capture, anti-concentration screens, debt/fee controls, insurance-risk mitigation, and repair/retrofit supports must prevent the first gains from being taken back.[S34][S36][S42][S418][S374]
3. **Lane 6 — top-tail rails.** Beneficial ownership, trust/foundation/DAF oversight, capital-gains/inheritance reform, political firewalls, and public-rescue burden sharing must be credible before the case can leave anti-oligarchy watch.[S04][S05][S15][S23][S56][S435]
4. **Lane 2 — floor repair throughout.** Health, care, unemployment, disability, minimum-income asset rules, bankruptcy/anti-seizure protections, and automatic claiming are not side programs; they determine whether modest wealth survives ordinary life.[S21][S39][S139][S50]

## What would improve the verdict

The case moves toward **acceptable-but-vulnerable** only if the next evidence cycle shows all of the following at once: bottom-half net and liquid wealth improve; first footholds become materially less parent-backed; racialized and person-level wealth gaps close in control and title, not just income; housing and insurance costs stop functioning as a private tax on entry; top-tail wealth vehicles become more visible and bounded; and public-risk absorption stops mainly protecting existing asset holders.

It moves toward **anti-oligarchy response** if top-tail wealth-to-rule channels intensify while floors and early footholds remain weak.

## Required next evidence

- DFA/Z.1/WID reconciliation of the extracted 2025:Q4 shares, including top 0.1 control visibility, private-vehicle/trust/DAF/family-office adjustment, and liquid-or-usable wealth cuts;
- parental deposits, guarantees, co-residence, education support, and inter vivos transfer timing by income/wealth/race/place;
- local rental ownership concentration and entry-price effects in high-growth metros;
- homeowners-insurance nonrenewal, premium, deductible, and coverage loss by county/income/race;
- beneficial-owner, trust, private foundation, DAF, family-office, and private-entity concentration measures.
