# United States municipal infrastructure and fiscal capacity case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Correction required.** Local infrastructure is too uneven and fiscally strained to let the archive count national public wealth as a uniform local service floor. NLC’s 2025 fiscal work shows cities moving from recovery to restraint, while the 2026 municipal infrastructure survey points to water, sewer, stormwater, streets, and treatment systems as continuing pressure points.[S245][S246]

## Dominant breach

The dominant breach is **public-service wealth deterioration under uneven municipal capacity**. A household’s real floor depends on whether the locality can maintain essential systems without pushing costs into rates, fees, special assessments, debt, or service cuts.

## Core reading

The infrastructure layer adds an asset-maintenance test. ASCE’s national report card is not a moral verdict, but it is a warning that public assets require sustained investment. EPA’s clean-water and drinking-water needs surveys give the cube hard stress anchors for water, wastewater, stormwater, pipes, treatment, storage, and lead-service-line replacement.[S247][S248][S249]

## Gate 15 finding

Gate 15 is **warning/fail depending on locality**. The national case cannot comfort-certify without local asset-condition inventories, fiscal capacity, grant absorption, rate incidence, and small-system evidence.

## Opening package

Capital grants paired with technical assistance, match relief, ratepayer affordability, asset-management requirements, small-system consolidation/support where appropriate, and recurring maintenance revenue.

## Evidence debt

Local capital-improvement plans, asset grades by neighborhood, rate impacts, grant match capacity, debt service, workforce shortages, procurement delays, and small-system technical capacity.
