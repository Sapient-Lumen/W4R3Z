# Remittance dependence and transnational family burden case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Subsystem watch.** Remittances can build wealth, but they cannot substitute for a public floor without a conversion test. The case remains watch because remittance dependence may signal weak local wages, weak care/floor systems, or migration-status coercion, and transfer costs can drain gains.[S218][S219]

## Dominant breach

The dominant breach is **private cross-border insurance burden**. Families use migration earnings to fund survival, care, education, housing, health, and shock response that should not depend entirely on private kin obligation.

## Why this case exists

The cube previously scored claim conversion inside jurisdictions. This case adds cross-border conversion: migrant earnings must survive recruitment debt, wage theft, status shocks, FX spread, transfer fees, household gatekeepers, and recipient-control issues before they count as durable wealth.

## Gate 13 finding

Gate 13 is **watch**. Remittances can support asset formation only when corridors are low-cost, claims are safe, and recipient control is clear. Otherwise they are evidence of a private welfare state under strain.[S216][S218][S219]

## Opening package

Use the remittance router: reduce corridor costs, regulate recruitment, protect wages and end-of-service claims, improve recipient account access, and distinguish survival remittances from asset-forming remittances.

## Evidence debt

Corridor-specific transfer costs, recruitment debt, wage theft, deportation/status shock, recipient control, remittance use by asset versus survival category, and household-level distribution by gender/age.

## rev0336 hardening note

rev0336 reframes remittances as household-support conversion rails: scale is not enough; fees, exchange spreads, recruitment debt, wage theft, recipient control, public-service substitution, and payment-rail remedies decide whether transfers become wealth.[S218][S219][S471]
