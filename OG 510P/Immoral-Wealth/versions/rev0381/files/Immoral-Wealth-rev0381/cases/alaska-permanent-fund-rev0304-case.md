# Alaska Permanent Fund case — rev0355

## Case header

- **Case:** Alaska Permanent Fund / Permanent Fund Dividend
- **Memo ID:** `alaska-permanent-fund-rev0304-case`
- **Status:** active case / subjurisdictional public-wealth stress case
- **Date:** 2026-05-21
- **Case type:** resource dividend / public-common wealth
- **Dominant breach:** public-common asset converted into person-level cash claim, but too small and politically contested to certify a lower-half floor
- **Fastest washout:** volatility, state-budget tradeoffs, eligibility boundaries, cost of living, climate/infrastructure exposure, and insufficient protected accumulation
- **Verdict:** **provisional public/common counterweight pass; not a wealth-order pass**

## Why this case matters

The Alaska Permanent Fund is a compact test of the archive's public/common wealth doctrine. The state holds a resource-derived financial asset and pays residents a direct dividend. APFC's 2025 annual report provides the fund-governance and performance anchor, while the official PFD site gives the current dividend amount and payment schedule.[S81][S82]

The case is valuable precisely because it is not enough. A dividend can be broad, rule-like, and person-level while still failing to create a real lower-half floor. It tests whether the archive can distinguish a genuine common-wealth claim from a full pass.

## Threshold-moment diagnosis

The decisive moments are:

1. annual dividend receipt;
2. whether the dividend is saved, consumed, garnished, or absorbed by necessities;
3. whether children receive protected long-term accumulation;
4. whether rural and Indigenous communities face higher costs that erase the dividend;
5. whether state budget politics reduce the dividend or public services;
6. whether resource rents are being converted into broad security or used as a substitute for broader tax/floor policy.

## Opening package

1. **Keep the common-wealth claim broad and legible.** Preserve audited governance, transparent reporting, and clear eligibility.[S81][S82]
2. **Harden part of the dividend into protected footholds.** Consider child accounts, anti-garnishment protections, matched savings, and emergency-buffer defaults rather than assuming cash alone survives.[S12]
3. **Avoid dividend-versus-service false choices.** A common-wealth cash claim should not hollow out health, infrastructure, education, or climate adaptation.
4. **Measure distributional use.** Track who saves, loses, garnishes, spends on necessities, or uses the dividend for mobility, repair, education, or debt.
5. **Export cautiously.** The model travels only where public assets, rule-bound governance, and anti-patronage rails are credible.

## What would change the verdict

The case would strengthen if dividends were protected, child-linked, countercyclical for low-wealth households, and paired with durable public services. It weakens if fiscal conflict converts the fund into patronage, if high living costs erase the dividend, or if eligibility and enforcement rules exclude groups that most need a floor.

## Required next evidence

- distribution of PFD receipt and use by income, race/Indigenous identity, region, household size, and age;
- share saved versus used for debt, rent, food, energy, repair, migration, education, or medical costs;
- garnishment and seizure exposure;
- interaction with public-services funding;
- fund governance, political interference, and intergenerational sustainability.
