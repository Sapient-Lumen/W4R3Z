# United States fissured workplace and joint-employer case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Correction required.** Fissured workplace arrangements can make rights non-converting when responsibility does not follow economic control. The NLRB's broader 2023 joint-employer rule was withdrawn after court vacatur, and DOL's 2026 proposed wage-and-hour joint-employer rule remains unsettled.[S227][S228]

## Dominant breach

The dominant breach is **responsibility/control separation**: a lead firm may influence margins, standards, schedules, brand rules, data, or productivity requirements while the formal employer carries thin legal responsibility.

## Core reading

BLS data show meaningful use of alternative arrangements: independent contractors, on-call workers, temporary-help agency workers, and contract-firm workers. The cube should not treat all alternative work as bad, but it must test whether workers have real autonomy, benefits, remedies, bargaining, and claim conversion.[S222]

## Gate 14 finding

Gate 14 is **blocked/watch** for fissured sectors unless the case can show joint responsibility, classification enforcement, wage-theft remedies, and record access.

## Opening package

Route through classification, control, remedy, and conversion: who controls economic terms, who holds records/assets, who must bargain or pay, and how quickly workers can recover wages or benefits.

## Evidence debt

Sector-level subcontracting chains, franchise contract terms, temp/staffing margins, wage theft by tier, worker misclassification, retaliation, and actual recovery rates.
