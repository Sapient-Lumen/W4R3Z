# Norway GPFG public-asset governance case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Acceptable but vulnerable.** The GPFG reports large value, returns, asset allocation, mandate structure, and long-horizon governance; sovereign-fund principles and OECD guidance show why governance and public purpose remain certification gates.[S369][S370][S371]

## Dominant breach

The breach is public-asset conversion: Norway’s fund is a strong public balance-sheet asset, but even strong sovereign wealth must be scored for rule-bound governance, withdrawal discipline, investment risk, and claimant link rather than treated as automatic equality.

## Gate 20 finding

Gate 20 is **blocked for comfort certification** unless the case proves that public downside, debt service, and backstop obligations are transparent, financed, incidence-scored, and paired with ordinary-claimant protection or public upside recovery.

## Opening package

Maintain rule-bound fiscal withdrawal, transparent reporting, professional management, stewardship/risk disclosure, claimant-linked public benefits, and stress testing for drawdowns, market concentration, climate transition, and political raiding.

## Evidence debt

Distribution of fund-financed public benefits; withdrawal-rule stress; claimant link for migrants/residents; asset concentration and technology exposure; climate/transition risk; intergenerational tradeoffs.

## rev0319 Gate 20 operationalization addendum

rev0319 converts this from a short Gate 20 launch memo into a structured public-balance-sheet stress row. The scoreboard now includes a `public_balance_sheet_register`, `gate_20_subgates`, a `seniority_waterfall`, and a full 20-gate inventory.

### Active Gate 20 subgates

- **20G_public_asset_sovereign_wealth_governance — passed:** GPFG governance is rule-bound, transparent, professionally managed, and protected by fiscal-rule discipline.
- **20H_generational_intergovernmental_incidence — watch:** Intergenerational and claimant-link questions remain live even in a strong public-asset case.

### Seniority waterfall under stress

1. **Future/current Norwegian public** — Collective owner under fiscal rule. The public owns the upside, but budget translation is political.
2. **Professional fund governance** — Rule-bound steward. Mandate and reporting reduce raiding/capture risk.
3. **Current budget users** — Beneficiaries through fiscal-rule transfers. Claim is indirect through public budget, not individual fund account.
4. **Future cohorts** — Protected by saving rule. Smoothing and withdrawal discipline preserve intergenerational claim.

### Case-specific evidence debt

- claimant link between fund value and ordinary floor/services
- withdrawal-rule stress under low returns/oil transition
- intergenerational distribution of fund use
- domestic inequality offset produced by fund-financed services
- raiding firewall under political stress

### Operator implication

The case cannot use aggregate public capacity as a benign counterweight unless the stress waterfall shows ordinary claimants are protected before asset holders, creditors, or intermediaries, or unless public downside is paired with enforceable upside recovery and transparent loss sharing.
