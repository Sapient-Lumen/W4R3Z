## Why this case matters

South Africa forces the archive to confront a case where the moral problem is not merely high top shares. The deeper breach is durable racialized closure across land, labor-market access, place, state capacity, and ownership visibility. OECD's 2025 survey says South Africa has one of the highest measured levels of wealth and income inequality in the world and reports unemployment averaging 32.5% in 2023/24.[S74] The World Bank's country overview likewise points to persistent poverty, inequality, infrastructure gaps, service-delivery challenges, and elevated crime.[S75]

The archive should therefore not begin with a narrow wealth-tax template. A top-tail instrument may be necessary, but the opening question is whether the state can build legible, defensible, non-patronage footholds while closing land/place/labor exclusion and avoiding elite capture.

## The land/legibility problem

The official land audit remains useful because it shows both a racialized ownership surface and a measurement-limit surface. It classifies private individual ownership by race, gender, and nationality, but explicitly does not racially classify companies, trusts, and other entities.[S76] That is exactly the archive's legibility problem: a case can have intense racialized land closure and still lack transparent entity/control data for the very channels through which wealth is held.

## Threshold-moment diagnosis

The decisive South African moments are:

1. safe access to school, work, and transport from historically segregated places;
2. first formal job and stable earnings foothold;
3. land/title regularization and defensible tenure;
4. access to electricity, water, broadband, and local public services;
5. first affordable credit without predatory terms;
6. ability to form a business without patronage, licensing chokepoints, or infrastructure failure;
7. protection from crime, eviction, and informal expropriation.

A financial asset-building policy that ignores these moments will be washed out before it becomes a durable wealth floor.

## Opening package

1. **Rails before hard top-tail dependence.** Build land records, beneficial-owner registers, procurement integrity, valuation capacity, and enforcement credibility.[S15][S23][S31][S76]
2. **Floor and service repair.** Treat electricity, transport, school, healthcare, local services, and safety as wealth-order instruments because they determine whether ordinary people can hold and use assets.[S74][S75]
3. **Land/title and place repair.** Move from symbolic land rhetoric to defensible tenure, productive access, spatial integration, and anti-patronage allocation.
4. **Labor-entry and youth footholds.** Pair asset-building with job creation, training, infrastructure, and business-entry supports; otherwise the lower-half floor remains paper.[S74]
5. **Top-tail and anti-capture measures.** Tax and wealth instruments should target visible rents, land windfalls, procurement capture, monopoly, and dynastic transmission, but must be sequenced with rails.

## What would change the verdict

The case improves only if the lower half gains real claimable floors, youth and women gain entry into work and assets, land/title reform becomes legible and non-patronage, infrastructure reliability improves, and top-tail/property/control channels become visible enough to govern. It remains **captured-immoral** if racialized closure persists while reforms are captured by incumbents or patronage networks.

## Required next evidence

- WID/national household wealth shares for bottom 50 / middle 40 / top 1 / top 0.1;
- race, gender, location, and age wealth shares;
- land/title ownership by individual and entity/beneficial owner;
- procurement, licensing, and monopoly concentration metrics;
- youth/female employment and first-asset formation;
- household debt, informal credit, and seizure/eviction risk.
