# EU beneficial-ownership legitimate-interest case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Verdict:** `acceptable_but_vulnerable` as a transparency-rights balancing case.  
**Mode:** registry access design and privacy-tested visibility.  
**Dominant breach:** beneficial-ownership transparency must remain usable after privacy and data-protection constraints narrow unrestricted public access.  
**Fastest washout:** access filters become so slow, narrow, or discretionary that journalists, researchers, civil society, procurement bodies, and affected claimants cannot use the data.

## Why this case was added

rev0308 added beneficial-owner visibility. rev0309 asks whether visibility survives rights balancing. EU infrastructure is a useful contrast to the U.S. BOI reversal: central registers and interconnection remain, but unrestricted public access has been replaced by legitimate-interest access in many contexts.

BORIS links national central registers holding beneficial-owner information for companies, legal entities, trusts, and similar arrangements.[S172] Directive 2024/1640 and Regulation 2024/1624 form part of the new AML framework for registers, obliged entities, and beneficial-ownership transparency.[S173][S174] France's implementation page shows concrete legitimate-interest categories and a process for access certificates.[S175]

## Proof debt

1. Compare Member State legitimate-interest procedures and approval rates.
2. Track time to access, rejection grounds, appeal routes, and certificate portability.
3. Determine whether journalists, researchers, civil society, and procurement bodies get practical access.
4. Test whether historical ownership and control-structure information is available.
5. Audit whether BO data is verified and linked to procurement/property/tax systems.

## Opening package

Treat EU BO as a stronger rail than no registry, but do not score it as fully public. The pass depends on legitimate-interest access being fast, predictable, appealable, and usable for anti-closure work.
