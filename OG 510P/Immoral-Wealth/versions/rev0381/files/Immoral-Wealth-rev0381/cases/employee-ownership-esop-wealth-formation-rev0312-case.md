# Employee ownership and ESOP wealth-formation case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Promising but proof-debt constrained.** Employee ownership can be a genuine wealth-formation rail, but the cube should not count it without breadth, vesting, valuation, liquidity, diversification, governance, and downside-risk evidence.[S235][S236]

## Dominant breach

The dominant breach is **ownership rhetoric versus usable claim**. Broad-based participation can build wealth; narrow, illiquid, concentrated, or backloaded claims may not help workers at threshold moments.

## Core reading

DOL frames employee ownership as broad workforce participation in ownership, while NCEO reports large ESOP scale: millions of participants and substantial assets. Scale alone does not certify. The archive needs the claim-conversion question: who is covered, when the claim vests, how it is valued, when it can be used, and whether workers carry too much employer-specific risk.[S235][S236]

## Gate 14 finding

Gate 14 is **watch/pass only with design evidence**. Worker ownership can improve the verdict in cases with low private asset formation, but only when it is broad-based, convertible, and protected.

## Opening package

Adopt the four-part test: broad, real, convertible, protected. Then add governance, fiduciary enforcement, diversification, and low-wage worker inclusion.

## Evidence debt

Distribution of account balances by wage/tenure/race/gender, forfeitures, payout timing, valuation disputes, firm failure exposure, worker voice, and interaction with retirement security.
