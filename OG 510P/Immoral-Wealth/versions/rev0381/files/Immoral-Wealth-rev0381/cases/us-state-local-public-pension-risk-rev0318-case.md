# U.S. state and local public-pension risk case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Correction required.** State pension funding gaps remain material; comparative pension evidence shows old-age security is a wealth-order surface rather than a narrow employment-benefit issue.[S355][S356][S347]

## Dominant breach

The breach is intergovernmental burden shift: public-pension promises can stabilize worker wealth, but underfunding and assumption errors can later pressure local services, taxes, tiers, and infrastructure.

## Gate 20 finding

Gate 20 is **blocked for comfort certification** unless the case proves that public downside, debt service, and backstop obligations are transparent, financed, incidence-scored, and paired with ordinary-claimant protection or public upside recovery.

## Opening package

Require funding discipline, transparent assumptions, downside stress tests, intergenerational incidence, protection for lower-paid public workers and retirees, and service-impact scoring before counting pensions as local public wealth.

## Evidence debt

Plan-level funded ratios; discount-rate sensitivity; tier disparities; public-service crowdout; property-tax/user-fee effects; retiree poverty and benefit adequacy by worker class.

## rev0319 Gate 20 operationalization addendum

rev0319 converts this from a short Gate 20 launch memo into a structured public-balance-sheet stress row. The scoreboard now includes a `public_balance_sheet_register`, `gate_20_subgates`, a `seniority_waterfall`, and a full 20-gate inventory.

### Active Gate 20 subgates

- **20B_claim_security — watch:** Public-pension claim security varies by plan and law; underfunding can compress services or worker claims.
- **20H_generational_intergovernmental_incidence — watch:** Local service users and younger workers can bear amortization and contribution pressure.

### Seniority waterfall under stress

1. **Retirees with protected benefits** — Often legally protected. Legal protection may seniorize pension checks over service expansion.
2. **Bondholders and creditors** — Can be senior in distress. Debt covenants may compete with pension and service claims.
3. **Current workers and local services** — Adjustment margin. Contribution increases, wage restraint, or service cuts can become the shock absorber.
4. **Future residents** — Deferred repair payers. Under-amortization shifts costs forward.

### Case-specific evidence debt

- plan-level funded-ratio stress using conservative returns
- service-cut versus contribution-increase incidence by locality
- worker/retiree legal protection map
- state aid and intergovernmental burden-shift path
- pension-obligation-bond and amortization risk

### Operator implication

The case cannot use aggregate public capacity as a benign counterweight unless the stress waterfall shows ordinary claimants are protected before asset holders, creditors, or intermediaries, or unless public downside is paired with enforceable upside recovery and transparent loss sharing.

## rev0368 private-markets fee, liquidity, and valuation addendum

rev0368 converts the case from broad public-pension underfunding concern into a locator-bound public-balance-sheet acceptance test. The current perimeter now includes: official Census ASPP scale and cash-flow data [S454]; Public Plans Data/GASB plan-reporting caveats [S572]; Pew evidence on alternative-asset growth, reported fees, and transparency needs [S570]; S&P/NASRA current-risk evidence on private equity/private debt contribution volatility, illiquidity, capital calls, fee structures, valuation subjectivity, and limited disclosure [S571]; and the AIC industry-side contrary claim that private equity has delivered strong long-run net-of-fee returns for many public pension portfolios [S573].

The active finding remains **not certified current**. These sources prove a risk and counter-risk perimeter, not a final verdict. Certification now requires plan-level private-market commitment schedules, unfunded commitments, capital calls, distributions, management fees, carried interest, performance fees, valuation policy, continuation-vehicle exposure, secondary-sale/redemption limits, audited net-of-fee returns, legal seniority, and service/tax/worker incidence.

Gate 20E is no longer `not_applicable` for this case. If public workers, retirees, taxpayers, or local service users bear downside from private-market opacity or illiquidity, the cube must ask whether the public receives net-of-fee value, clawbacks, fee offsets, governance rights, transparency, downside sharing, and enforceable manager accountability.
