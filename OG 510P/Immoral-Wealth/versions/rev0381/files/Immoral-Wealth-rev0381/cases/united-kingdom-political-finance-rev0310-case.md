# United Kingdom political-finance case — rev0355

## Verdict

**Watch with correction required on source-of-funds and enforcement powers.** The UK has a visible electoral-finance regulator and donation reporting, but rev0310 keeps the case open because current 2026 reviews identify source-of-funds, overseas donor, and regulator-power concerns.

## Dominant breach

The dominant breach is **permissibility and source-of-funds uncertainty**, not absence of any formal transparency system. A transparent donation total can still be too weak if the system cannot verify whether the money behind the donor is permissible and politically independent.[S192][S193][S194]

## Why this case exists

This case tests a different democratic-power failure mode from the U.S.: not unlimited federal independent expenditure, but party-finance permissibility, foreign financial interference, and regulator investigation powers.

## Core reading

The Electoral Commission reported almost £65 million in accepted donations and public funds in 2025 while also saying parts of the system need strengthening. The Joint Committee recommended stronger checks for long-term overseas donors and expanded Electoral Commission access to financial-institution, crypto-platform, and HMRC information. The Rycroft Review examined political finance laws, parties' role in combating foreign financial interference, and effective enforcement.[S192][S193][S194]

## Gate 12 finding

Gate 12 is **watch / partially blocked** until reforms show stronger source-of-funds checks, overseas-donor tests, information-sharing, enforcement powers, and timely public reporting.

## Opening package

Add donor-source verification, overseas-resource tests, Electoral Commission investigatory powers, party due-diligence obligations, and post-reform monitoring of sanctions and compliance quality.

## Evidence debt

Evidence debt remains on donor concentration, unincorporated associations, company-donor profitability/source checks, loans and non-cash support, candidate-level surplus routing, lobbying and hospitality cross-links, and enforcement outcomes.
