# Pattern confidence and export burdens

## What this note is for

The archive can now do three things cleanly in cross-case work:
choose a comparison set, extract a recurring pattern, and screen whether any lesson is portable beyond the compared cases.
What it still lacked was the thinner bridge between those moves:
**how much confidence belongs to the pattern itself, how much belongs to the mechanism story, how much belongs to portability, and how much belongs to any doctrine consequence.**

That gap matters because cross-case notes can drift in two opposite ways:
- they can sound more certain than the comparison actually earned,
- or they can keep every recurring lesson quarantined because the archive never states what burden has actually been met.[S15][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33]

This note adds a compact confidence bridge for **pattern-level claims**.
It is not a new scoreboard, not a doctrine note, and not a replacement for the archive's single-case confidence labels.
It is a **cross-case export discipline**.

## Minimum rule

A cross-case pass should usually keep **four confidence questions** separate:

1. **pattern-existence confidence** — are these cases really showing the same recurring failure, win, or watch result?
2. **mechanism confidence** — is the same deeper mechanism likely doing the work across the cases, rather than a superficial resemblance?
3. **portability confidence** — how much of that lesson actually travels beyond the compared cases?
4. **doctrine-consequence confidence** — is the pattern strong enough to justify a bridge, promotion pressure, repair pressure, or doctrine revision burden?

Do not compress those into one comfort score.
The archive should make it easy to say:
**high confidence that a pattern exists, moderate confidence about the mechanism, low confidence that it travels broadly, and very low confidence that doctrine should move yet.**

## What usually raises pattern confidence

Pattern confidence rises when the same result survives comparison across cases that differ in visible surface features but keep converging on the same deeper line of failure or success.
Ordinarily that means some combination of:

- the cases are comparably live and on the same claim level,
- the same dominant breach or washout keeps reappearing,
- the same rail failure or durability failure keeps reappearing,
- the pattern survives across more than one institutional or territorial surface,
- and the comparison is not doing all its work through one unusually flattering or unusually damaged dataset.[S04][S15][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33]

A recurring pattern becomes especially interesting when cases with different headline share profiles keep collapsing on the same deeper mechanism:
for example, different visible distributions but the same rent-washout channel, the same claimant-burden failure, or the same weak durability window.

## What should cap or lower confidence

Confidence should stay lower when any of these dominate:

- the comparison depends mainly on one vivid flagship case,
- the cases are stale, weakly recertified, or mismatched in freshness,
- the cases do not actually share the same claim level,
- the mechanism story changes from case to case while the prose pretends it does not,
- portability depends on unnamed rails,
- or the whole pattern is still mostly a measurement-architecture artefact rather than a social one.

The archive should treat these as reasons to narrow the claim, not as reasons to speak in a softer but still global tone.

## The archive's four pattern-confidence labels

Keep the labels coarse.
Ordinarily a cross-case note only needs:

- **high**
- **moderate**
- **low**
- **too thin to rate cleanly**

That is enough.
The archive should prefer short honest labels to fake precision.

## How the four confidence questions differ

### 1. Pattern-existence confidence

This is the confidence that a recurring pattern is really present across the included cases.
It rises when the same result survives comparison on the archive's fixed operator spine.
It falls when the similarity is mostly atmospheric or rhetorical.

### 2. Mechanism confidence

This is the confidence that the same deeper process is doing the work across the cases.
A pattern can have moderate existence confidence and low mechanism confidence if the cases look similar at the surface but reach that surface through very different routes.

### 3. Portability confidence

This is the confidence that the lesson travels beyond the cases actually compared.
Route the actual portability tier through [`pattern-portability-and-transfer-limits.md`](pattern-portability-and-transfer-limits.md).
This note does not replace that tiering.
It just makes the archive say how much confidence belongs to the portability call itself.

### 4. Doctrine-consequence confidence

This is the confidence that the pattern should do more than sit inside the compared cases.
It is the burden relevant to:
- extracting one narrow bridge,
- putting pressure on an existing doctrine note,
- promoting a lesson into doctrine,
- or declining all of those for now.

Low doctrine-consequence confidence does **not** mean the comparison was useless.
It can still be enough to guide portfolio choice, watch posture, or a narrow case-family note.

## Allowed export postures

After the four labels are stated, the archive should choose one export posture:

1. **comparison only** — the pattern is real enough to mention inside the compared set, but not to export.
2. **case-family only** — the pattern is good enough for this family of cases, but not beyond it.
3. **portable only with named rails** — the pattern likely travels, but only with explicit transfer conditions.
4. **bridge candidate** — the pattern is strong enough to justify one narrow active bridge, not a doctrine rewrite.
5. **doctrine-pressure candidate** — the pattern is strong enough to put pressure on an existing live rule or to begin a promotion question.

Do not skip from vivid comparison straight to doctrine motion by atmosphere.
Choose the smallest export posture the confidence labels can honestly bear.

## What two, three, or more cases usually buy

The archive should stay coarse here too.
It does **not** need a sacred case-count threshold.
But it should recognise rough differences in burden.

- **2 cases** can be enough to show that a supposed common mechanism is plausible, or that a claimed contrast is real.
- **3 cases** can often show whether the pattern survives first contrast rather than just one pairing.
- **4 or more cases** may strengthen confidence, but only if they add real contrast instead of rhetorical padding.

More cases do not automatically raise doctrine-consequence confidence.
A crowded set built from similar surfaces can still be weaker than a smaller set that survives sharper contrast.

## The smallest good output

A retained cross-case pass should usually add only these five lines after the main comparison finding:

1. **pattern-existence confidence**
2. **mechanism confidence**
3. **portability confidence**
4. **doctrine-consequence confidence**
5. **export posture**

Then use [`pattern-rival-explanations-and-disconfirmation.md`](pattern-rival-explanations-and-disconfirmation.md) to name the strongest rival explanation, the strongest measurement rival, the cleanest disconfirmation trigger, and the **smallest next proof burden** that would most change one of those labels.
If the note wants more than that, it is usually becoming a shadow doctrine memo.

## The main false moves this note is meant to stop

### 1. Letting tone carry confidence

A comparison can read smooth, serious, and cumulative while still earning only moderate or low export confidence.
This note is meant to make the archive say that directly.

### 2. Smuggling portability through recurrence alone

Repeated appearance is not the same as broad transfer.
The pattern may be real and still be case-family only.

### 3. Smuggling doctrine pressure through portability alone

A lesson can travel somewhat and still not be strong enough to revise doctrine.
That is why portability confidence and doctrine-consequence confidence must stay separate.

### 4. Collapsing mechanism into pattern existence

The archive should be able to say:
**the pattern looks real, but the deeper mechanism story is still weak.**
That sentence prevents elegant but premature generalization.

## Fast use rule

When a cross-case note has extracted a real pattern:

1. **Label pattern-existence confidence.**
2. **Label mechanism confidence.**
3. **Label portability confidence.**
4. **Label doctrine-consequence confidence.**
5. **Choose the smallest export posture those labels can bear.**
6. **Name the smallest next proof burden instead of widening the claim by prose.**

## Bottom line

The archive now has a compact answer to a question that appears **after** comparison and **before** bigger reuse:
**how much confidence belongs to the pattern itself, how much belongs to the mechanism story, how much belongs to portability, and how much belongs to any doctrine consequence?**
Keep those burdens separate, choose the smallest export posture that the labels actually support, and prefer honest narrow carry-forward to fast elegant generalization.
