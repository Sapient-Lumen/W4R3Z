# Review cadence and durability-window selection

## What this note is for

The archive now has a compact rule for choosing the **next scoreboard** and the **main evidence debt** after a real-case memo is written.
What it still lacked was a similarly thin rule for the time side:
**how often should the case be looked at again, how quickly should misses force reroute, and how long should softer language survive before it earns harder confidence?**

This note gives the archive that missing timing bridge.
It is not an empirical claim that every country or sector needs the same calendar.
It is a compact operating rule for choosing:

1. a **review cadence**,
2. a **reroute horizon**,
3. and a **durability window**.

It does **not** decide whether an already-written memo is still safe to reuse later; that reuse question belongs to [`case-staleness-and-recertification.md`](case-staleness-and-recertification.md).

## Minimum reading rule

Choose timing by the **speed of plausible miss**, not by the prettiest published series.
The archive should look again soon enough to catch the main washout or delivery failure before it becomes the new baseline, and it should require softer certification to survive long enough to face ordinary political-budget turnover and ordinary asset / credit pressure rather than one flattering phase.[S05][S06][S13][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33]

In compact form:

- set the **review cadence** by how fast the package can visibly miss,
- set the **reroute horizon** by how long it is still responsible to wait before adding or revising the package,
- and set the **durability window** by the slowest ordinary reversal channel that still needs to be faced before softer language counts more than provisional.[S05][S06][S20][S31][S32][S33]

## The three different timing objects

### 1. Review cadence

This is how often the case should be re-read while the current package or posture is still live.
A review cadence is for **seeing misses early enough to matter**.
It should usually be tied to the tempo of claiming, take-up, retention, price response, exclusion, enforcement drift, or fiscal delivery—not to the slower tempo of polished annual commentary.[S07][S17][S21][S22][S23][S31][S36][S39][S40]

### 2. Reroute horizon

This is how long the archive is willing to wait before concluding that the live package is missing badly enough to justify revision.
It is not the same as the review cadence.
A case may be reviewed often, yet still be given a little time before a full reroute, especially where some delivery lag is normal.
But the archive should not let predictable miss channels hide behind ceremonial patience.[S04][S07][S12][S21][S22][S23][S31][S36]

### 3. Durability window

This is the time burden for softer language: ratchet, de-escalation, provisional near-pass hardening, or near-acceptable certification.
The archive should ask whether the case has survived enough ordinary turnover, repricing, and anti-capture stress to count as more than one benign interval.[S05][S06][S20][S31][S32][S33]

## The standing selection rule

Choose all three timing objects in this order.

### 1. Start from the fastest plausible miss

Ask which failure could show up first if the current package is landing badly:

- claimant drop-off,
- capitalisation into prices,
- retention collapse,
- entrant exclusion,
- group skew,
- enforcement leakage,
- or immediate rail strain.

That channel sets the **outer bound** for the review cadence.
A case should be reviewed before that miss can become socially normal or politically sunk.[S07][S17][S21][S22][S23][S31][S36][S39][S40]

### 2. Then ask what revision still needs time to show honestly

Some packages need a short interval for claiming, uptake, withholding, or delivery to become legible.
The archive should allow enough time to see whether a package is merely early or actually wrong, but not so much time that avoidable loss becomes the new floor.
That interval sets the **reroute horizon**.[S04][S07][S12][S21][S22][S23][S31]

### 3. Then ask what ordinary reversal channel has not yet been faced

For softer certification, the question is not just whether the package looks better now.
It is whether the case has lived through the ordinary kinds of pressure that typically rebuild hierarchy:

- budget retrenchment,
- coalition or governing turnover,
- asset / credit repricing,
- capture pressure,
- enforcement fade,
- and entrant-side squeeze.

The slowest of those still-unfaced ordinary channels should govern the **durability window**.[S05][S06][S13][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33]

### 4. Tighten cadence when evidence is operationally faster than statistics

When administrative, registry, claiming, payment, eviction, audit, or retention evidence moves faster than official wealth series, the archive should use the faster evidence for review and reroute.
It should not wait for the prettiest annual table when the live package is already obviously missing on the ground.[S04][S07][S17][S21][S22][S23][S31][S36][S39]

### 5. Slow certification more than correction

The archive should usually move **faster to correct** than to certify.
Thin proof can still justify lower-regret repair, anti-washout closure, or anti-capture hardening.
It should count for much less when the question is whether to soften the verdict or speak as if a society has become near-safe.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## Default timing shapes by watch posture

These are shape rules, not universal month counts.
They tell the archive what kind of timing burden belongs to each posture.

### A. Build posture

For **build**, review on the tempo at which ordinary people can actually try to claim, keep, or be excluded from the package.
The reroute horizon should usually be one short operational window beyond that, long enough to distinguish early friction from structural miss.
The durability window is not yet the main question.[S04][S07][S17][S21][S22][S39][S40]

### B. Reroute posture

For **reroute**, review on the tempo of the fastest live miss channel and keep the reroute horizon short.
If passage, retention, price response, skew, or capture is already visibly diverging across the first real windows, the archive should revise by the smallest missing function rather than waiting for a ceremonial annual verdict.[S07][S10][S17][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S36]

### C. Ratchet posture

For **ratchet**, review on the tempo at which gains can still leak back out: take-up decay, resale drift, benefit loss, re-privatization, weakened funding, or capture of the new value.
The durability window should include at least one ordinary renewal, budget, or repricing interval that can test whether the gain is harder to reverse than before.[S05][S06][S20][S31][S32][S33]

### D. Reopen posture

For **reopen** or proposed de-escalation, the durability window should be strictest.
A softer reading should generally survive both an ordinary political-budget test and an ordinary asset / credit or entry-pressure test before the archive talks as if the case is safely beyond correction.[S05][S06][S13][S20][S31][S32][S33]

## The four main false moves this note is meant to stop

### 1. Letting the publication calendar choose the moral calendar

A case should not wait for the slowest official series when faster operational evidence already shows that the package is missing or that the floor is washing back upward.

### 2. Using one good wave to soften the verdict

One clean survey, one budget year, one calm credit interval, or one enforcement burst should not do the work of a real durability window.[S05][S06][S20][S31][S32][S33]

### 3. Using the same cadence for review, reroute, and certification

Those are different burdens.
The archive should usually review earliest, reroute next, and certify last.

### 4. Naming a watch posture with no time burden attached

A case memo should not say **build**, **reroute**, **ratchet**, or **reopen** without also naming the next review cadence and the timing burden for a harder or softer reading.

## What a thin case memo should output

After the next scoreboard and the evidence debt are chosen, add three short timing lines:

- **review cadence** — how often the case should be re-read now,
- **reroute horizon** — how long the archive will wait before revising the live package if misses continue,
- **durability window** — what ordinary turnover or pressure the softer reading still must survive.

A good timing block is narrow.
It should name the next operational check, the point at which continued miss forces revision, and the ordinary cycle that still must be faced before softer language counts more heavily.

## Bottom line

The archive should now handle timing in one stable order:

1. **review before the fastest plausible miss can settle in,**
2. **reroute once enough honest time has passed to distinguish lag from failure,**
3. **and certify only after the case survives the ordinary reversal channels that usually rebuild hierarchy.**

That keeps the archive from drifting into two opposite mistakes at once: waiting too long to correct a missing package, and speaking too softly after one flattering interval.[S05][S06][S20][S31][S32][S33]
