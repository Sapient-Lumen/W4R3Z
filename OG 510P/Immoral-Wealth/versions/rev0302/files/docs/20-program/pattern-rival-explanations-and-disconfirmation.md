# Pattern rival explanations and disconfirmation

## What this note is for

The archive can now choose a comparison set, extract a recurring pattern, state separate confidence burdens, and screen what actually travels beyond the compared cases.
What it still lacked was the thinner bridge between **confidence** and **export**:
**what is the strongest rival explanation, what evidence would actually disconfirm the claimed pattern, and what smallest next test would most change the archive's mind?**

That gap matters because cross-case work can drift in two opposite ways:
- a recurring pattern can coast forward because the archive never names the strongest rival and never says what would count against it,
- or every pattern can remain stuck at general unease because the archive never states the one next test that would really separate the claimed mechanism from its nearest competitor.[S15][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33]

This note adds a compact **rival-and-disconfirmation discipline** for multi-case work.
It is not a new doctrine note and not a replacement for single-case evidence discipline.
It is the small rule that stops cross-case lessons from hardening merely because they sound portable, cumulative, or familiar.

## Minimum rule

After a cross-case pass states its pattern-confidence labels, it should usually add **four more lines** before speaking about portability or doctrine motion:

1. **main live rival explanation** — the strongest competing story that could explain the same recurring surface.
2. **main measurement rival** — the strongest way the pattern might be an artefact of measurement architecture, freshness mismatch, or comparison design rather than of the world.
3. **cleanest disconfirmation trigger** — the observation that would most directly count against the claimed pattern or mechanism.
4. **next discriminating proof burden** — the smallest evidence that would most separate the pattern story from its nearest rival.

Do not let these collapse into one mood of caution.
The archive should be able to say:
**moderate confidence that the pattern is real, but the main live rival is policy-bundle confounding, the main measurement rival is freshness mismatch, and the cleanest disconfirmation trigger would be the same cases surviving comparison after recertified timing and rail controls.**

## What counts as a real rival explanation

A real rival explanation is not just a negation sentence.
It must be a **plausible different story** that could explain why the cases look similar.
Ordinarily a rival earns the label only if it changes one of these:
- what deeper mechanism is doing the work,
- what package lesson should be exported,
- what rails are actually necessary,
- or whether the pattern belongs to the compared cases at all.[S04][S15][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33]

Good rivals are therefore usually things like:
- **selection or portfolio bias** — the archive chose unusually vivid cases rather than a fair case family,
- **measurement architecture bias** — the pattern is mostly coming from household masking, top-tail undercapture, or freshness mismatch,
- **bundle confounding** — the apparent recurring win or failure is really riding a different package component than the one being credited,
- **rail dependence** — the pattern only holds where visibility, delivery, or enforcement rails are stronger,
- **phase mismatch** — one case is still in build posture while another is already in reroute or ratchet territory,
- **local institutional blocker** — the shared result is actually being driven by one local veto point that does not travel.

## What usually counts as a measurement rival

Cross-case work should normally name one measurement rival separately because comparison can look much cleaner than it really is.
The main measurement rival is often one of these:
- freshness mismatch across memos,
- mismatched claim levels,
- different visibility of top-tail ownership or controller identity,
- group disaggregation that is present in one case and absent in another,
- or a flattering aggregate that hides person-level access, carrying costs, or washout.[S17][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33]

A comparison that survives its main measurement rival is much more useful than one that simply sounds robust.

## What a clean disconfirmation trigger looks like

A disconfirmation trigger should be specific enough that future case work could really trip it.
It should usually say what observation would count **against** the claimed pattern, not just what more evidence would be nice to have.

Good triggers often look like:
- the same apparent pattern disappears once stale memos are recertified,
- the pattern reverses when the cases are matched on rail strength,
- the same package succeeds when the supposed blocker is absent,
- the same washout disappears after a named rail is added,
- or the claimed mechanism no longer predicts outcome once a stronger rival is controlled for.

Bad triggers are things like:
- "more research is needed,"
- "future work may complicate this,"
- or any sentence that lets the archive sound provisional without naming what would really change the call.

## Export caps when rival discipline is missing

The archive should cap cross-case export when this step is skipped.
Ordinarily:
- no pattern should be treated as a **bridge candidate** without a named main live rival,
- no portability call should be treated as more than **tentative** without a named main measurement rival,
- and no doctrine-pressure claim should outrun a clearly stated disconfirmation trigger.

That does **not** mean every comparison must become a long adversarial memo.
It only means the archive should do the minimum honest work needed to stop pattern language from coasting forward under its own smoothness.

## The smallest good output

A retained cross-case pass usually needs only these four extra lines:

1. **main live rival explanation**
2. **main measurement rival**
3. **cleanest disconfirmation trigger**
4. **next discriminating proof burden**

If those lines start expanding into a full secondary essay, the comparison is probably trying to do too much at once.
Usually the right fix is to narrow the pattern claim or lower the export posture, not to turn the bridge note into another doctrine memo.

## The main false moves this note is meant to stop

### 1. Letting recurrence substitute for discrimination

A pattern that keeps reappearing is still weak if the archive cannot say what the strongest rival is.
Repeated mention is not the same as tested explanation.

### 2. Letting portability ride on clean prose

A lesson may sound portable because the compared cases are described elegantly.
If the main rail-dependent rival is never named, that elegance should count for very little.

### 3. Letting doctrine pressure outrun disconfirmation

A comparison can be strong enough to warrant watchfulness and still too weak to move doctrine.
That is exactly when the archive should name a clean disconfirmation trigger and stop there.

### 4. Treating "uncertainty" as a substitute for the main next test

General caution is not enough.
The archive should prefer one named discriminating next test to a vague paragraph of hedging.

## Fast use rule

When a cross-case note extracts a real pattern:

1. **State the four pattern-confidence labels.**
2. **Name the strongest live rival explanation.**
3. **Name the strongest measurement rival.**
4. **Name the cleanest disconfirmation trigger.**
5. **Name the smallest next discriminating proof burden.**
6. **Only then choose the export posture and portability tier.**

## Bottom line

The archive now has a compact answer to another question that appears **after** a pattern has been extracted but **before** it should travel:
**what is the strongest rival explanation, what measurement rival could still be fooling us, what would actually count against the pattern, and what next test would most change the archive's mind?**
Name those four things directly, then let portability and doctrine motion rise only as far as that rival-and-disconfirmation burden honestly allows.
