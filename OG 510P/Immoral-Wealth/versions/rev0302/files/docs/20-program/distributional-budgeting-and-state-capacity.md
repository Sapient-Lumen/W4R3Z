# Distributional budgeting and state capacity

## What this note is for

This note turns one of the archive's quiet background requirements into a **full stable budgeting and state-capacity stack**.
A society can announce good goals, pass good statutes, and still rebuild concentration through the budget if scoring arrives too late, hidden spending stays off-ledger, implementation capacity is assumed rather than funded, or ex post failure is treated as a side issue.
So the archive treats **distributional budgeting and state capacity** as part of wealth-distribution infrastructure rather than as a technical appendix for finance ministries.[S13][S19][S31][S34][S36][S37]

## Minimum operating rule

Before a major wealth-relevant package is locked, governments should require a **wealth-aware distributional budget read** that is early enough to change design, broad enough to catch hidden reversals, and concrete enough to shape staffing, delivery, enforcement, and later repair.[S31]

A serious budgeting discipline should usually do **seven things at once**:

1. score **packages rather than isolated instruments**, because tax, housing, labor, public-wealth, and anti-capture effects interact,
2. score **wealth formation, floor integrity, closure, public-value capture, and durability**, not only annual disposable-income effects,[S13][S19][S31][S34][S35][S36][S37]
3. run **early enough to reshape design** rather than merely decorate a near-final package,[S31]
4. use linked administrative and program data that are **good enough to see who actually gets through**, not just who is formally eligible,[S27][S28][S29][S30][S31]
5. budget for **delivery, enforcement, appeals, auditing, and maintenance capacity** instead of assuming the machinery will somehow appear,
6. treat **tax expenditures, guarantees, concessional finance, and publicly created uplift** as distributionally decisive budget choices rather than neutral background settings,[S31][S34][S35][S36][S37]
7. and create an **ex post correction loop** so packages are revised when take-up failure, capitalization, exclusion, or avoidance defeats the forecast.[S21][S22][S31][S36][S37]

A package should count less as a success when one table looks progressive but the wider budgeting record shows that footholds remain weak, public value is leaking upward, or the delivery state was never funded to make the promise real.

## The main false passes

### 1. A package looks progressive on current income, but weakens wealth formation

A country should count less budgeting success when the headline score shows better annual cash flow while the same package leaves asset entry, savings room, debt stress, secure tenure, or early footholds largely unchanged.
The archive does not treat disposable-income tables as enough when the question is durable wealth broadening.[S13][S19][S31]

### 2. Eligibility is broad on paper, but the claiming machine is thin in practice

A society should count less success when the budget announces broad support but underfunds staffing, outreach, appeals, adjudication, portability, renewal, or error correction.
That pattern can turn apparently universal design into selective non-take-up or slow attrition.[S27][S28][S29][S30][S31]

### 3. Public investment is scored on the spending side, but not on the uplift side

The archive should count less seriousness when infrastructure, rezoning, corridor investment, procurement, or place-making decisions are budgeted as public outlays while the land or asset-value gains they create are barely measured and weakly recaptured.[S34][S35]

### 4. Hidden spending stays off the main distributional page

A country should count less progress when deductions, exclusions, concessional rates, guarantees, subsidized credit, or retirement-saving relief are treated as technical background rather than as real distributive choices with owner-skewed or price-capitalizing effects.[S31][S36][S37]

### 5. Capacity is invoked rhetorically, but not budgeted concretely

The archive should count less seriousness when governments say a reform depends on enforcement, valuation, registry upkeep, casework, or litigation capacity but do not fund the teams, systems, and maintenance windows required to carry that work.
Unfunded capacity claims are often just a slower way of saying that delivery will be partial.[S23][S31]

### 6. Forecasts are published once, then never confronted with reality

A society should count less budgeting success when ex ante tables are treated as self-validating and no routine ex post review asks whether a package capitalized into prices, missed excluded groups, shifted burdens within households, or was arbitraged by sophisticated actors.[S21][S22][S31][S36][S37]

### 7. The package is scored in fragments, so interacting reversals disappear

The archive should count less seriousness when tax, housing, labor, public-wealth, and anti-capture components are each scored narrowly enough to look fine in isolation while their combined effect still rebuilds scarcity, upward capitalization, or delivery overload.
Budget discipline counts less when it cannot read the package as one interacting wealth order.[S13][S19][S31][S34][S36][S37]

## The standing budgeting and state-capacity stack

### 1. Early ex ante package scoring

Core moves:

- run distributional analysis before political lock-in,
- test multiple design variants rather than a single preferred package,
- score packages rather than isolated instruments,
- and include likely incidence, capitalization, and delivery-failure channels rather than assuming clean statutory transmission.[S31][S36][S37]

The archive wants budgeting to shape design early, not merely describe it late.
A late score can still be informative, but it should count less once coalition bargains, agency timetables, and administrative assumptions have already hardened.

### 2. Wealth-aware linked data and administrative visibility

Core moves:

- connect the administrative records needed to see wealth-relevant outcomes,
- follow households and persons through delivery, not only formal eligibility,
- make disaggregation good enough to detect group, cohort, household, and place-based closure where law and data permit,
- and build readback capacity strong enough to see who actually receives, retains, loses, or never reaches the promised gain.[S13][S27][S28][S29][S30][S31]

The point is not maximal data collection for its own sake.
It is to stop governments from claiming distributional success with instruments they cannot actually observe in operation.

### 3. Delivery, enforcement, and maintenance budgeting

Core moves:

- budget explicitly for staff, outreach, case handling, appeals, audits, registry upkeep, valuation support, compliance work, and routine maintenance,
- identify which agencies or public bodies actually carry each reform lane,
- and treat maintenance, renewal, and repair as part of the policy rather than as optional overhead.[S23][S31]

The archive prefers a smaller deliverable package over a larger package whose operating machinery is never funded.
A promise that requires invisible staff, invisible systems, or invisible litigation capacity should be discounted before it is celebrated.

### 4. Public-value capture and hidden-spending scoring

Core moves:

- score tax expenditures like spending,
- score guarantees and concessional finance like real state support,
- score land or asset uplift generated by public action for who receives it and how much is recaptured for common use,
- and keep hidden support on the same distributional page as visible outlays.[S31][S34][S35][S36][S37]

This is where budgeting meets the archive's concern with the **hidden wealth state**.
A budget cannot count as distributionally serious while leaving major owner-skewed support streams in the political blind spot.

### 5. Public distributional statements with uncertainty and failure channels

Core moves:

- publish compact statements of who is expected to gain and lose,
- identify the main uncertainty ranges,
- name the main failure channels such as non-take-up, within-household masking, price pass-through, rent capitalization, or avoidance,
- and make it easy to compare the budget story with later observed incidence.[S27][S28][S29][S30][S31][S36][S37]

The archive wants public honesty about where a package might fail, not polished tables that imply false precision.
That honesty is part of democratic legitimacy, not a concession to critics.

### 6. Ex post review, repair, and automatic revision triggers

Core moves:

- revisit major packages after implementation,
- compare observed outcomes with the original score,
- attach automatic review or redesign triggers when the package misses entrants, fuels revaluation, or mainly enriches incumbents,
- and treat forecast failure as a design problem to be repaired rather than as a communications problem to be managed.[S21][S22][S31][S36][S37]

A policy that modeled well but washed out in practice should count as a repair obligation, not as a success to be narrated.

### 7. Package-level sequencing and anti-backloading discipline

Core moves:

- budget earlier delivery for floor repair, secure tenure, and first footholds,
- refuse packages that push present exposure onto households while backloading most of the gain,
- ask whether the budget ordering strengthens or weakens later reform capacity,
- and judge whether the state is asking ordinary people to front the risk while public repair remains deferred.[S17][S18][S19][S31]

This is where distributional budgeting meets the archive's wider rule against **promissory offsets and backloaded justice**.
The archive should count less seriousness when the budget claims future egalitarian payoff while leaving current exposure, current gatekeeping, and current carrying costs mostly intact.

## Fast operating rule

Use this note in four steps:

1. **Ask whether the package is being scored as one interacting wealth order.**  
   Do not stop at a disposable-income table if the package also changes housing scarcity, tax expenditures, public-value capture, delivery friction, or enforcement load.[S13][S19][S31][S34][S36][S37]
2. **Ask whether the score arrives early enough to change the package.**  
   Late modelling can still inform debate, but it counts less once the real design is already politically locked.[S31]
3. **Ask whether the state funded the machinery, not just the headline.**  
   Check staffing, outreach, adjudication, auditing, registry upkeep, valuation support, enforcement, and later repair rather than assuming the operating capacity will somehow appear.[S23][S27][S28][S29][S30][S31]
4. **Ask whether the budget can be read back against reality.**  
   A package counts less when failure channels are hidden, ex post review is weak, or there is no automatic trigger to repair capitalization, exclusion, or washout once observed incidence diverges from the forecast.[S21][S22][S31][S36][S37]

Default shortcut:

- **early package scoring + hidden-spending visibility + funded delivery/enforcement + ex post repair triggers** -> count as a stronger budgeting lane inside the wealth program;
- **late decorative scoring + off-ledger subsidy support + unfunded delivery + no repair loop** -> assume the budget process can still quietly undo egalitarian law;
- **mixed case** -> weight the judgment by whether the budget reads wealth formation, floor survival, and public-value leakage rather than just one year of disposable income.

## Non-substitution rule

Do **not** let the following excuse weak budgeting discipline:

- one flattering current-income table,
- formal eligibility without funded claiming machinery,
- hidden tax expenditures or guarantees kept off the main distributional page,
- public investment scored without public-uplift capture,
- sophisticated microsimulation without household-side delivery visibility,
- or review promises without automatic repair triggers.

Those facts may matter once the budget can genuinely see the package, fund the machinery, and correct forecast failure.
They do **not** by themselves clear the budgeting lane.

## Program implication

Once this stack is read clearly, budgeting stops looking like a downstream accounting exercise and starts looking like one of the archive's main **anti-washout rails**.
That changes priority.
Early distributional scoring, hidden-spending visibility, delivery budgeting, and ex post repair often need to move **earlier** than governments prefer, because taxes, housing reform, social inheritance, and labor gains are all easier to narrate than to preserve once the fiscal machinery is left thin.
That is why this note now sits directly under measurement, subsidy hygiene, housing, implementation credibility, public-value capture, and mode selection.

## Bottom line

The archive should now read distributional budgeting and state capacity in this order:

1. **Ask whether the package is being scored as one interacting wealth order rather than as isolated instruments.**
2. **Ask whether the score reaches wealth formation, floor integrity, closure, public-value capture, and durability rather than only one-year income effects.**
3. **Ask whether delivery, enforcement, and maintenance capacity are funded concretely enough to make the promise real.**
4. **Ask whether hidden spending and publicly created uplift sit on the same page as visible outlays.**
5. **Ask whether forecast failure triggers real repair once capitalization, exclusion, or washout appear.**

The archive's budgeting judgment is therefore not: **model the package well** or **fund the agencies well** or **publish a fairness statement** in isolation.
It is: **treat those as one governing lane, because otherwise a nominally progressive package is still easy to flatter on paper, underfund in practice, and quietly reverse through the budget itself.**
