# Hardening gains into ratchets

## What this note is for

This note gives the archive a **thin positive rule for packages that are actually landing**.
The archive already has a way to choose an opening package, monitor it, reroute it when it is missing, and reopen correction when softer verdicts decay.
What was still missing was the compact bridge on the better path:
**when a launched package is genuinely improving entrant passage, floor integrity, or top-tail discipline, what should be hardened next so the gain becomes harder to reverse than before?**

The archive's answer is that early success should not be treated as a self-ratifying verdict.
A serious case should usually convert early gains into six harder forms:
1. **automatic and low-friction delivery** rather than discretionary or expert-heavy claiming,
2. **person-level, portable footholds** rather than household-masked or place-trapped gains,
3. **closed washout channels** rather than visible broadening that can still be capitalised away,
4. **durable fiscal or public/common support** rather than one soft budget window,
5. **visibility, enforcement, and anti-capture hardening** rather than hopeful compliance,
6. and **reviewable legal or institutional defaults** that survive calmer politics better than the original package did.[S04][S07][S12][S15][S17][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S27][S28][S29][S30][S31][S32][S33][S36][S39][S40][S54]

## Minimum operating rule

A serious gain-hardening discipline should usually do **six things at once**:

1. **Name the gain that is real enough to defend.** Do not talk about ratchets before there is actual entrant passage, retention, or closure worth protecting.[S17][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S31]
2. **Ask what would reverse fastest if politics softened tomorrow.** Harden the most likely reversal channel first rather than the most glamorous reform.[S07][S15][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33][S36]
3. **Convert receipt into default access where possible.** A foothold counts more when it becomes automatic, low-friction, and ordinary to keep.[S21][S22][S39][S40]
4. **Convert gains into person-level, portable, defendable claims.** Household masking, discretionary administration, or place-locking should count against ratchet status.[S18][S19][S27][S28][S29][S30][S41][S43]
5. **Harden closure, visibility, and anti-capture around the gain.** A good package without these can still be unwound by rent, opacity, or sabotage.[S07][S15][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33][S36]
6. **Require a durability window before softer certification.** Early hardening matters, but it is still not the same thing as a fully earned pass.[S05][S06][S15][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33]

So the standing rule is:
**when a package is landing, harden the gain into defaults, person-level footholds, closed washout channels, funded rails, and anti-capture protections before speaking as if the case has settled.**

## The six main false passes

### 1. A good rollout is mistaken for a ratchet

A package counts less as a ratchet when it is merely having a good quarter, a friendly budget cycle, or a calm political moment.
Improvement is real, but it has not yet been converted into a form that is materially harder to reverse than before.[S05][S06][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33]

### 2. Delivery remains discretionary or expert-heavy

A gain counts less as a ratchet when people still need repeated forms, bespoke advocacy, unstable documentation, or favorable caseworker discretion to keep what they received.[S21][S22][S39][S40]
A package should count more once ordinary claiming becomes simpler, more automatic, and less punishing.

### 3. Receipt remains household-masked, joint, or non-portable

A gain counts less as a ratchet when it arrives mainly as household wealth, employer-bound entitlement, place-bound privilege, or a claim that is easy to forfeit during separation, unemployment, mobility, or administrative churn.[S18][S19][S27][S28][S29][S30][S41][S43]
The archive wants gains that travel with the person and remain defendable under ordinary life changes.

### 4. The washout channel stays open

A gain counts less as a ratchet when the main upward escalator is still live.
Housing capitalisation, land scarcity, debt drag, fees, monopoly tolls, or quiet carrying costs can erase visible progress while the program still looks successful on paper.[S07][S10][S17][S20][S31][S34][S35][S36][S42]

### 5. Funding and public/common support remain too soft

A gain counts less as a ratchet when it depends on one temporary appropriation, one favorable minister, or one underspecified public vehicle.
The archive counts more durability when the gain is anchored to visible funding, public/common counterweight, or institutional defaults that can survive ordinary politics better than a one-cycle patch.[S02][S12][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33]

### 6. Visibility and anti-capture hardening are deferred until later

A gain counts less as a ratchet when controller visibility, enforcement, procurement integrity, appeals, audit, or anti-capture firewalls are treated as later embellishments.
Where concentrated wealth can still sabotage or arbitrage the package, the gain has not yet been made safe enough for softer language.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

## The standing gain-hardening stack

### 1. Convert successful passage into low-friction defaults

When a package is reaching the right entrants, the first hardening question is whether that passage can be made easier to keep.
Usually that means some combination of:
- automatic enrollment or pre-filled routes where appropriate,
- longer certification or renewal windows,
- simpler proofs,
- fewer interruption points,
- and stable support channels for appeals, correction, and renewal.[S21][S22][S39][S40]

The archive counts more ratchet force when the gain no longer depends on repeated feats of navigation.

### 2. Convert receipt into person-level, portable footholds

When a package is creating real footholds, the next hardening question is whether the foothold belongs clearly enough to the person who needs it.
Usually that means:
- person-level title or receipt where feasible,
- portability across jobs, homes, jurisdictions, or family transitions,
- non-forfeiture or low-forfeiture design,
- and cheaper proof and defense of the claim itself.[S18][S19][S27][S28][S29][S30][S41][S43]

This is how a gain stops being a temporarily favorable arrangement and starts becoming a more stable part of ordinary citizenship or economic standing.

### 3. Close the live washout channel around the gain

No gain should be called ratcheted while the main reabsorption path remains open.
So when a package is landing, the archive should ask what would quietly take the gain back first:
- housing and land capitalisation,
- debt service and refinancing pressure,
- fees and toll extraction,
- monopoly markups,
- or other routine carrying-cost erosion.[S07][S10][S17][S20][S31][S34][S35][S36][S42]

Default hardening move:
- add the smallest closure rule that protects the new foothold from being priced away, charged away, or refinanced away.

### 4. Anchor the gain to durable fiscal or public/common rails

A gain counts more as a ratchet when it is supported by machinery that can survive a colder political season.
That usually means:
- visible and recurring funding,
- public or common asset capture where appropriate,
- explicit budget lines for delivery, maintenance, and enforcement,
- and less dependence on one-off grace, one-off subsidy, or off-books optimism.[S02][S12][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33]

The archive does not require every gain to become a giant permanent program immediately.
But it does require a clear move away from pure transience.

### 5. Harden visibility, enforcement, and anti-capture around the gain

When a gain is worth defending, the archive should also harden the machinery that stops dilution and sabotage.
That usually means:
- better controller or beneficial-ownership visibility where top-tail discipline matters,
- registry, audit, and appeals quality,
- anti-procurement-capture and anti-tailoring firewalls,
- and simpler rules where complexity is enabling arbitrage.[S15][S23][S31][S32][S33]

A package that broadens access but leaves sabotage channels wide open has improved the case without yet making the improvement durable.

### 6. Write the ratchet note before claiming softer settlement

When a package is landing, the case memo should usually add one short **ratchet note** before speaking in softer verdict language.
That note can stay compact:
- **gain now landing**,
- **main reversal risk**,
- **hardening move now required**,
- **what is still too soft to certify**,
- **and what durability window would make the softer reading more credible**.

That keeps the archive from confusing encouraging implementation with already-earned settlement.

## Fast operating rule

Use this note in five steps whenever a launched package is **landing well enough that the next question is how to keep the gain**:

1. **Name the gain worth defending.**  
   Do not talk about ratchets in the abstract.
2. **Pick the likeliest reversal channel.**  
   Ask what would take the gain back first: friction, non-portability, housing, debt, opacity, or sabotage.
3. **Add the smallest hardening move that changes that answer.**  
   Prefer defaults, person-level title, closure, and rail hardening before rhetorical expansion.
4. **Keep the verdict softer only provisionally.**  
   A working package earns harder institutional form before it earns relaxed moral language.
5. **Reset the watch window.**  
   After the hardening move, keep monitoring passage, retention, closure, and capture rather than assuming the gain is now self-sustaining.

Default shortcut:
- **landing but friction-heavy** -> harden automaticity and claims support;
- **landing but household-masked or place-bound** -> harden person-level title, portability, and non-forfeiture;
- **landing but washing out** -> harden the closure lane first;
- **landing but politically soft** -> harden funding, legal defaults, and public/common counterweight;
- **landing but sabotage-prone** -> harden visibility, enforcement, and anti-capture first.[S07][S12][S15][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S27][S28][S29][S30][S31][S32][S33][S36][S39][S40]

## Handoff to case work

A future case memo does not need a new essay for this note.
If a package is already underway and is landing better than expected, the memo can usually add one short line in the final block:
- what gain is now real enough to defend,
- what ratchet move is now due,
- and what still blocks softer certification.

That keeps the archive thin.
The point of this note is not to widen case work.
It is to stop early success from being narrated either as closure or as a reason to skip the institutional hardening that makes success survive. When several hardening moves look plausible at once, future case work can use [`package-choice-and-tie-break-rules.md`](package-choice-and-tie-break-rules.md) to choose the move that most cleanly protects the gain with the least new proof debt.

## Bottom line

The archive now has a cleaner answer to the positive mirror image of reroute logic:
**what should happen when the package is working?**
The answer is not triumphalism.
It is to harden the gain into lower-friction claiming, person-level and portable footholds, closed washout channels, durable fiscal and public/common support, and stronger visibility and anti-capture rails.
That is how temporary improvement begins to turn into a settlement that is actually harder to unwind than before.[S04][S07][S12][S15][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S31][S32][S33][S36][S39][S40]
