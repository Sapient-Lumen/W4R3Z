# Ideal wealth distribution bands

## What this note is for

This note is the archive's **number-side reading rule** for the question, **what would an ideal wealth distribution look like in share terms, how exact should those shares be, and what numerical warnings should make a case fail early?**
It keeps the archive from drifting into either of two mistakes: pretending the answer is a single sacred tuple, or pretending the numbers do not matter because the real questions are elsewhere.[S02][S03][S16][S18][S19][S20][S23][S26]

The archive therefore keeps a **default attractor** and a **small family of rough working bands**.
Those four-part bands are read as shares of **total private wealth**; the archive's public/social wealth requirement is a separate standing surface, not a fifth bucket inside the tuple.[S02][S03][S21][S22]
For ordinary cross-country comparison, the archive's default lens is **household-based private net wealth shares**; but those household totals only count morally after a **person-level title, control, and exit screen** so the number-side reading does not silently bless intra-household dependency as broad ownership.[S13][S27][S28][S29][S30]
But it does **not** treat those bands as self-sufficient moral grades.
A country does not count as near-ideal just because the shares look tidy while the bottom half still lacks a real floor, early footholds still arrive too late, the top tail still governs, or broadening is still being washed out by leverage, housing capitalization, opacity, or reconcentration pressure.[S07][S13][S16][S18][S19][S20][S23][S31][S36]

## Minimum reading rule

Read the bands as a **disciplining screen** for judgment, not as a complete theory of justice.
A society can sit somewhat off-center and still count as near-acceptable when the deviation is clearly earned by strong public/social counterweight, unusually real lower-half thickening, or another companion strength the archive already recognizes.
A society can also fail **inside** the bands when the surrounding constitutional surfaces are broken badly enough that the apparent numerical success is flattering rather than real.[S02][S16][S18][S19][S21][S22][S23]

The practical rule is simple:
**the closer a case is to the preferred shares, the more seriously the archive should ask what those shares are actually doing; and the farther a case is from the preferred shares, the more seriously the archive should ask what companion strengths could possibly justify the deviation.**
Numbers discipline the reading.
They never replace it.[S02][S13][S16][S20]

## The six standing band-reading surfaces

### 1. Bottom 50% share

The archive's default target for the **bottom 50%** remains **20%** of household-based private net wealth, with a rough acceptable band of **15-25%**.[S02][S03][S13]
This is the number-side expression of a deeper rule: mass adulthood should not normally begin from zero-or-negative wealth, chronic assetlessness, or one bad shock away from collapse.
A bottom-half share near 20% is not magic, but it is a useful quantitative sign that the lower half may no longer be living almost entirely outside ownership.[S16][S17][S25]

**Warning range:** a **bottom-50 share below 10%** is a strong sign that mass assetlessness is still structurally normal, even if some middle or public numbers look better.[S02][S16]
The archive should also discount bottom-half gains when they are mostly nominal housing revaluation, thin pension claims, or highly levered balances that entrants cannot realistically reproduce.[S07][S13][S16][S24][S25]

### 2. Middle 40% share

The default target for the **middle 40%** remains **50%**, with a rough acceptable band of **45-55%**.[S02][S03]
This is the archive's numerical shorthand for a **socially real middle owner stratum**: large enough to keep society from splitting into a narrow owner elite and a much larger renter-debtor mass, yet not so swollen by classification tricks that lower-half exclusion is being hidden inside household averages.[S16][S20]

A middle share should count less when it is doing too much of its work through household masking, age averaging, or ownership that exists only on paper while operative control and usable liquidity stay elsewhere.
The archive therefore treats middle thickness as a real good only when it coexists with person-level adequacy below and bounded top-tail command above.[S13][S17][S20][S26]

### 3. Next 9% share

The archive keeps the **next 9%** around **20%**, with a rough acceptable band of **15-25%**.[S02][S03]
This slice matters because the archive does **not** aim at flat sameness.
It leaves room for substantial non-oligarchic affluence, entrepreneurial accumulation, and a genuinely plural upper-middle / upper-professional owner class.
The point is not to abolish successful households.
The point is to prevent success in this slice from serving as a relay station into dynastic rule or a sheltered feeder belt into the ultra-top.[S03][S20][S23]

This share should be read with care.
A country can tolerate a somewhat larger next-9 slice only if the lower half is already clearly above a real floor and the top tail is staying constitutionally bounded.
If the next 9 is thriving while the bottom half stays locked out and the top 1 keeps converting wealth into rule, the apparently healthy middle-upper band is not a reassuring sign but part of the closure pattern.[S16][S18][S19][S20][S23]

### 4. Top 1% share

The archive's default target for the **top 1%** remains **10%**, with a rough acceptable band of **8-15%**.[S02][S03]
This should be read as a **bounding rule**, not as a demand that the top 1 cease to be rich.
They can remain rich.
The archive's narrower claim is that they should not remain rich enough to function as a semi-permanent governing estate across finance, politics, media, major land, agenda-setting institutions, or crisis design.[S20][S23][S26]

**Warning range:** a **top-1 share above 20%** is a strong sign that the top tail is still constitutionally too large, even before every companion rule is consulted.[S02][S03]
A society can also fail below that warning if concentrated control, stewardship bottlenecks, lobbying power, procurement dependence, or rescue privilege are still severe.
So the warning range is a strong signal, not the whole test.[S20][S23][S26]

### 5. Top 0.1% concentration inside the top tail

The archive keeps a working target of **3% or less** for the **top 0.1%** inside total private wealth, and treats **above 5%** as a strong warning that ultra-concentrated command is still live.[S02][S03]
This is the archive's numerical guardrail against letting a society pass merely because the top 1 softened a little while a much smaller ultra-top still holds estate-like command capacity.

The archive should be especially suspicious here when the top 0.1% controls opaque private vehicles, private-company equity, concentrated land, media platforms, political financing channels, or large pools of intermediated capital without meaningful counter-governance from below.
At that point, the issue is no longer just rich people owning a lot.
It is the reappearance of a near-governing wealth stratum.[S20][S23][S26]

### 6. Public/social wealth position

The archive's target is **clearly positive net public wealth** and a visibly growing **public/social wealth pool**, not weak or persistently negative public balance sheets that leave private concentration as the only serious buffer, allocator, or rescuer in the system.[S02][S21][S22]
The number-side reading is therefore incomplete if it only stares at household shares.
A society can require less private wealth for ordinary security when common systems are strong, universal enough, and actually usable.
That can justify some movement inside the acceptable-envelope logic.
But it does not justify leaving households near the bottom effectively assetless.[S18][S19][S21][S22]

**Warning range:** persistently **negative net public wealth** is a strong sign that the society is carrying too little public/social counterweight and may be forcing too much security work back onto private balance sheets.[S02][S21]
The archive should treat this as especially serious when private concentration simultaneously remains high, because the combination leaves concentrated private wealth in an even stronger governing position.

## The six main false passes

### 1. The shares look good, but the bottom half still lacks a real foothold

A country should not pass numerically when the lower half still lacks resilient net wealth, liquid buffers, secure tenure, low-clawback support design, defensible claims, or manageable debt burdens.
A nicer share vector is not enough if the floor is still too weak to be lived from.[S16][S17][S24][S25]
See [`minimum-asset-floor.md`](minimum-asset-floor.md), [`protected-footholds-and-anti-spend-down-traps.md`](protected-footholds-and-anti-spend-down-traps.md), and [`stress-tested-footholds-and-ordinary-shock-survivability.md`](stress-tested-footholds-and-ordinary-shock-survivability.md).

### 2. The shares look good, but person-level control is still too thin

A country should not pass when wealth looks broad at the household or pension-fund level while one adult, one manager class, or one intermediary layer still controls the operative claims.
The archive counts wealth morally only when ordinary people can actually keep, use, move, and understand the footholds being counted for them.[S17][S20][S26]
See [`individual-ownership-and-intra-household-power.md`](individual-ownership-and-intra-household-power.md), [`intermediated-ownership-and-stewardship.md`](intermediated-ownership-and-stewardship.md), and [`ordinary-comprehensibility-and-low-expertise-operability.md`](ordinary-comprehensibility-and-low-expertise-operability.md).

### 3. The shares look good, but circulation is still inherited and late

A country should not pass when adult asset position still depends too heavily on parental housing position, late inheritance, family deposits, or exclusion from high-opportunity places.
A soft-looking distribution that still sorts people too strongly by origin is not near-ideal, even if the aggregate shares are close to the center.[S05][S18][S19][S34]
See [`intergenerational-circulation.md`](intergenerational-circulation.md), [`life-course-timing-and-early-footholds.md`](life-course-timing-and-early-footholds.md), and [`spatial-concentration-and-place-based-closure.md`](spatial-concentration-and-place-based-closure.md).

### 4. The shares look good, but the top tail still converts wealth into rule

A country should not pass when the top tail still dominates media, politics, major firms, rescue design, lobbying, land, or agenda-setting institutions even if its raw share softened somewhat.
Numerical improvement counts less when large fortunes remain constitutionally privileged actors rather than merely very rich households.[S20][S23][S26]
See [`wealth-to-rule-conversion.md`](wealth-to-rule-conversion.md), [`asset-composition-and-control.md`](asset-composition-and-control.md), and [`anti-averaging-and-constitutional-vetoes.md`](anti-averaging-and-constitutional-vetoes.md).

### 5. The shares look good, but the public counterweight is too weak or too decorative

A country should not pass when public/social wealth exists largely as a macro accounting fact while ordinary households still must self-insure heavily against care needs, housing stress, income breaks, or old-age insecurity.
Common wealth counts most when it actually lowers required private exposure.
It does not count much when households still need large private war chests just to survive normal life.[S18][S19][S21][S22]
See [`social-floor-and-private-wealth.md`](social-floor-and-private-wealth.md) and [`forced-self-insurance-and-private-buffer-coercion.md`](forced-self-insurance-and-private-buffer-coercion.md).

### 6. The shares look good, but the route is temporary, flattering, or self-eroding

A country should not pass when better-looking shares are being propped up by housing-price inflation, subsidized leverage, thinly understood pension promises, repeated public downside absorption, hidden subsidies, or rapid reconcentration pressure.
The archive does not grant near-ideal status to a share pattern that looks good only in the current snapshot and then quietly washes upward again.[S07][S13][S23][S31][S32][S33][S36]
See [`carrying-costs-and-self-eroding-footholds.md`](carrying-costs-and-self-eroding-footholds.md), [`upside-down-subsidies-and-the-hidden-wealth-state.md`](upside-down-subsidies-and-the-hidden-wealth-state.md), and [`reconcentration-pressure.md`](reconcentration-pressure.md).

## Fast use rule

Use this note in five steps:

1. **Start from the default attractor.**  
   Begin with **20 / 50 / 20 / 10** plus the public/social-wealth requirement.
2. **Check the rough bands and warnings.**  
   Read **15-25 / 45-55 / 15-25 / 8-15**, then ask whether the case is also tripping warnings such as **bottom 50 below 10**, **top 1 above 20**, **top 0.1 above 5**, or **persistently negative public wealth**.
3. **Ask what the numbers are actually securing.**  
   Is the lower half clearing a real floor, is the middle socially thick, is the upper non-elite slice plural rather than oligarchic, is the top tail bounded, and is there a real common counterweight?
4. **Reject the case if a false pass is live.**  
   Do not give numerical credit for person-level masking, late inheritance, housing-capitalization tricks, decorative public wealth, or active money-into-rule conversion.
5. **Escalate when the case is close or flattering.**  
   Use [`acceptable-envelopes-and-substitution-limits.md`](acceptable-envelopes-and-substitution-limits.md) and [`target-selection-rules.md`](target-selection-rules.md) when the case depends on companion judgments rather than simple band comparison.

Quick shorthand:

- **20 / 50 / 20 / 10 with no major warnings and no active false pass** -> treat as the archive's cleanest numerical center;
- **nearby shares with a strong common-floor or lower-half-thickening story** -> test through the acceptable-envelope rule rather than failing automatically;
- **nice-looking shares with live floor failure, inherited closure, oligarchic control, decorative public wealth, or reconcentration pressure** -> refuse the near-pass.

## Program implication

Once this note is read clearly, the archive should become harder to misread in two opposite directions.
It should no longer sound as if one elegant share tuple solves the whole moral problem.
But it also should no longer sound as if the archive is indifferent to the numeric shape of ownership so long as some qualitative story sounds good.
The archive has a real numerical center because it wants a broad lower half, a thick middle, a bounded top tail, and real common counterweight.
It just refuses to let those numbers float free of their companion conditions.[S02][S16][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23]

That matters for policy design.
A country should not chase a beautiful ratio mechanically.
It should ask how taxes, social inheritance, housing reform, public wealth building, anti-monopoly policy, claim protection, debt repair, and political firewalls move the society toward a wealth order that has actually **earned** any nice-looking share pattern it reports.
Conversely, a country that improves the lower-half floor, thickens the middle, and reduces top-tail command in durable ways should get moral credit even before it reaches the clean center exactly.
The point of the numbers is not numerology.
The point is to keep the archive honest about what structural progress should look like.

## Bottom line

The archive's current number-side center remains, read first through **household-based private net wealth shares** and then through the archive's person-level control screen:

- **Bottom 50%:** target **20%**, rough acceptable band **15-25%**
- **Middle 40%:** target **50%**, rough acceptable band **45-55%**
- **Next 9%:** target **20%**, rough acceptable band **15-25%**
- **Top 1%:** target **10%**, rough acceptable band **8-15%**
- **Top 0.1%:** working target **3% or less**
- **Public/social wealth:** **clearly positive** and materially real

The archive's main warning signals remain:

- **bottom 50 below 10%**,
- **top 1 above 20%**,
- **top 0.1 above 5%**,
- and **persistently negative public wealth**.

But these warnings are not the whole judgment.
The archive's final question is always whether the wealth order is becoming one of **broad secure footholds, plural middle ownership, bounded top command, open circulation, and real democratic counterweight** rather than one of prettier arithmetic covering the same underlying domination.[S02][S16][S18][S19][S20][S21][S22][S23]
