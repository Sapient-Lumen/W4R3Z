# Asymmetric tradeoffs and no-regret priorities

## What this note is for

This note turns one of the archive's most practical judgment calls into a **stable tradeoff rule**.
It answers a narrower question than the full target procedure in [`target-selection-rules.md`](target-selection-rules.md) and the fuller failure screen in [`anti-averaging-and-constitutional-vetoes.md`](anti-averaging-and-constitutional-vetoes.md):
**when a reform package improves one thing and worsens another, which losses are tolerable, which are presumptively acceptable, and which should count as disqualifying trades rather than normal policy friction?**

The archive does not treat all tradeoffs symmetrically.
Some losses are part of real correction because they fall mainly on rents, opacity, windfalls, excessive leverage, or concentrated veto power.
Other losses are constitutionally worse because they buy one visible gain by deepening another breach the archive already treats as central: floor weakness, later-life dependence, entry closure, illegibility, public-loss transfer, or wealth-to-rule conversion.[S04][S05][S07][S13][S15][S19][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33][S36][S37]

## Minimum reading rule

Read mixed packages **asymmetrically and in order**.
The archive should give the strongest presumption to reforms that improve one constitutional surface without materially worsening another.
It should usually accept bounded short-run losses when they fall mainly on rents, incumbent windfalls, opacity, excessive leverage, or convenience enjoyed by concentrated actors.
It should reject or heavily downgrade packages that improve a headline metric only by worsening a veto surface, weakening the bottom-half floor, worsening entry timing, or making the system less governable.[S04][S07][S13][S15][S19][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33][S36][S37]

The compact intuition is:
**not every loss counts the same, and not every gain deserves the same moral price.**
A better-looking average should not be purchased by a deeper constitutional breach somewhere else.

## The six main false passes

### 1. One visible gain is mistaken for a net improvement even though another constitutional surface gets worse

A package counts for less when it improves one metric but worsens floor integrity, early footholds, legibility, anti-capture, or loss-bearing symmetry.
The archive should not score those cases as ordinary tradeoffs and move on.[S15][S19][S23][S31][S32][S33]

### 2. Incumbent revaluation is mistaken for broadening

A policy should not count as wealth broadening when it mainly raises incumbent housing, land, or asset values while later entrants face worse affordability, thinner tenure security, or greater dependence on parental transfers and debt.[S07][S13][S19][S36]

### 3. Debt-fragile inclusion is mistaken for acceptable access

Broader participation should count for less when households enter mainly through thin equity, higher servicing strain, refinancing dependence, or other debt structures that make the gain easy to unwind under ordinary stress.[S17][S24][S25][S42]

### 4. Administrative ease is mistaken for a neutral reason to weaken visibility or anti-capture controls

A package should count for less when it buys speed or simplicity by making ownership, lobbying, transfers, procurement, or concentrated-controller channels harder to see and govern.[S15][S23][S31]

### 5. Crisis stabilisation is mistaken for fair repair

Emergency support should count for less when it protects large private balance sheets or incumbent asset prices without meaningful burden sharing, recovery, or repair, even if it softens the immediate snapshot.[S31][S32][S33]

### 6. Later compensation is mistaken for an acceptable substitute for present breach

The archive should count less success when a package justifies present floor weakness, late access, weak person-level security, or unequal exposure by pointing to later transfers, future growth, or eventual inheritance.[S05][S19][S27][S28][S29][S30]

## The six standing priority surfaces

### 1. Floor strengthening without new punishment near the bottom

The archive should strongly favor reforms that widen liquid resilience, secure tenure, holdable claims, and protected minima without pushing new risk, complexity, or depletion pressure onto households near the floor.[S16][S17][S21][S22][S24][S25]

### 2. Entry reopening and earlier footholds

Reforms should count more when they make first secure entry easier for people without wealthy parents, and count less when they mainly flatter incumbent holders or postpone access into later life.[S05][S13][S19]

### 3. Person-level security, portability, and ordinary operability

The archive should favor packages that make claims easier to hold, move with, understand, and defend in ordinary life rather than tying them more tightly to one employer, administrator, household controller, or expert maze.[S28][S29][S30][S40][S41][S44][S45]

### 4. Legibility, auditability, and anti-capture capacity

A package counts more when it leaves ownership, transfers, controllers, and political influence more visible and more governable after the reform than before.[S15][S23][S31]

### 5. Common-value return and anti-rent discipline

The archive should prefer reforms that recapture publicly created value, reduce land or monopoly tolls, and weaken incumbent windfall channels rather than capitalising more public action into private fortunes.[S20][S34][S35][S36]

### 6. Loss-bearing symmetry and public-balance-sheet protection

A package counts more when downside is borne more proportionately by those who enjoyed concentrated upside, and counts less when household insecurity or public balance sheets are used to preserve private concentration.[S31][S32][S33]

## The three standing trade classes

### 1. No-regret improvements

Give the strongest presumption to packages that improve one or more priority surfaces without materially worsening the others.
Typical cases include cleaning upside-down subsidies, improving registries and beneficial-ownership visibility, building protected liquid buffers, strengthening public/social wealth, and recapturing collectively created uplift.[S21][S22][S23][S31][S34][S35][S36][S37]

### 2. Hard but acceptable correction costs

Some reforms impose real short-run losses: lower incumbent house-price growth, smaller tax shelter value, weaker monopoly rents, tighter reporting obligations, or less generous asset-price defense in crises.
The archive should usually treat those losses as acceptable when four conditions hold together:

1. the package is directly reducing a real constitutional breach,
2. the burden falls mainly on rents, opacity, windfalls, excessive leverage, or concentrated veto power,
3. the trade is time-bounded and institutionally specified rather than left to drift,
4. and the package leaves the system more governable and less hierarchy-forming than before.[S04][S06][S15][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33][S36]

### 3. Forbidden trades

The archive should reject or redesign packages that buy visible gains by worsening a veto family or floor guardrail.
The most important forbidden trades are:

1. **do not buy broader ownership through heavier debt fragility,**
2. **do not buy better-looking shares through incumbent revaluation that worsens entry,**
3. **do not buy speed or simplicity through weaker legibility or anti-capture controls,**
4. **do not buy stability through untargeted defense of concentrated private upside,**
5. **do not buy a softer aggregate picture through timing, household, cohort, or group masking.**[S05][S13][S15][S19][S23][S24][S25][S31][S32][S33][S36]

## Fast package screen

Use the rule in this order:

1. **Name the breach being reduced.** If that answer is vague, the package is probably too atmospheric.
2. **Check vetoes and floor guardrails first.** If the package worsens floor security, rule conversion, closure/timing, group inclusion, or legibility, downgrade it before admiring the headline gain.
3. **Ask whether gains are entrant-sided or incumbent-sided.** If incumbent wealth rises while access closes, the package is probably counterfeit.[S13][S19][S36]
4. **Ask who bears the transition cost.** Favor packages that load cost onto rents, opacity, windfalls, and concentrated power rather than bottom-half security or the public balance sheet.[S31][S32][S33]
5. **Ask whether repair and ratchets are strengthened.** Count more when the package leaves stronger institutions, cleaner incentives, and less room for later reconcentration or capture.[S20][S31]
6. **If the package is emergency relief, check repair debt.** Crisis derogations should be temporary, transparent, burden-shared where concentrated actors benefit, and paired with explicit repair duty rather than institutional amnesia.[S31][S32][S33]

Default shortcut:

- **improves one surface and worsens none** -> strongly favored;
- **improves one surface and imposes bounded losses on rents or elite convenience** -> usually acceptable;
- **improves one surface by worsening another constitutional surface** -> reject, redesign, or downgrade heavily.

## Program handoff

This note should now make the archive stricter in four places.

First, it should harden the veto and measurement notes against flattering packages that hide one worsening surface behind one improved average.
See [`anti-averaging-and-constitutional-vetoes.md`](anti-averaging-and-constitutional-vetoes.md) and [`../20-program/measurement-and-scoreboard.md`](../20-program/measurement-and-scoreboard.md).

Second, it should push the housing, subsidy, and public-value notes toward entrant-sided and common-value-return judgments rather than incumbent-rich success stories.
See [`publicly-created-value-and-common-asset-capture.md`](publicly-created-value-and-common-asset-capture.md), [`upside-down-subsidies-and-the-hidden-wealth-state.md`](upside-down-subsidies-and-the-hidden-wealth-state.md), and [`../20-program/housing-land-and-anti-monopoly.md`](../20-program/housing-land-and-anti-monopoly.md).

Third, it should make the floor companions harsher on debt-heavy, maze-dependent, or backloaded packages that pretend to broaden security while raising fragility.
See [`minimum-asset-floor.md`](minimum-asset-floor.md), [`encumbrance-and-debt-quality.md`](encumbrance-and-debt-quality.md), and [`promissory-offsets-and-backloaded-justice.md`](promissory-offsets-and-backloaded-justice.md).

Fourth, it should prevent crisis policy from being treated as neutral merely because it was stabilising.
See [`../20-program/emergency-exceptions-sunsets-and-repair-duties.md`](../20-program/emergency-exceptions-sunsets-and-repair-duties.md) and [`downside-discipline-and-loss-bearing.md`](downside-discipline-and-loss-bearing.md).

## Bottom line

The archive should now read tradeoffs in this order:

1. **favor no-regret gains first,**
2. **accept bounded correction costs when they fall mainly on rents, opacity, windfalls, excessive leverage, or concentrated veto power,**
3. **reject packages that buy one visible gain by worsening another constitutional surface,**
4. **treat emergency departures as repair debt rather than neutral exceptions,**
5. **and refuse to let flattering averages launder forbidden trades.**

The point is not to demand painless reform.
It is to stop treating every mixed package as morally symmetrical when some trades correct hierarchy and others quietly deepen it.[S04][S07][S15][S19][S20][S23][S31][S32][S33][S36][S37]
