# Non-extractive wealth formation

## What this note is for

This note turns one of the archive's core path-quality claims into a **stable source-quality rule**.
It answers a narrower question than the fuller target procedure in [`target-selection-rules.md`](target-selection-rules.md):
**when should broader or rising wealth count less because the main gains are being generated through scarcity rents, monopoly markups, fee extraction, incumbent revaluation, or other bottleneck channels rather than through wider open access, saving, productive renewal, or common-wealth building?**

The archive does **not** judge a wealth order only by a stock snapshot.
It also asks **how the stock is being made**.
The same-looking balance-sheet improvement can reflect very different moral processes:
- broader saving out of decent earnings,
- earlier entry into secure housing or diversified long-run claims,
- larger social inheritance or public/social wealth,
- or incumbent gains that mainly come from housing scarcity, monopoly power, captive fees, and publicly created value capitalising into already-held assets.[S07][S10][S13][S16][S20][S34][S35]

That means this note is not a sixth separate doctrine so much as a **cross-cutting reading rule**.
It tells the archive when to discount wealth gains because they are being built in ways that make the next entrant thinner, later, or more dependent even while incumbent balance sheets look better.[S07][S10][S13][S16][S20][S34][S35][S36][S37]

## Minimum reading rule

Read wealth formation through an **entrant-side and bottleneck-side test**, not only through an incumbent-side stock test.
A society should count as more just only when a meaningful share of new durable wealth is being built through channels that widen secure access, strengthen ordinary bargaining power, or enlarge common/public wealth rather than mainly through price inflation on scarce assets, rising markups, escalating fees, or publicly supported windfalls that reward already-positioned owners.[S07][S10][S13][S16][S20][S34][S35][S36][S37]

The archive's compact intuition is:
**wealth should count less when it is broad in appearance but extractive in formation.**

This note therefore reads wealth growth more harshly when the apparent gains depend mainly on:
- incumbent housing revaluation while entry gets harder,
- rents, markups, or fees that absorb wage or transfer gains,
- subsidies and tax privileges that capitalise into land values or incumbent portfolios,
- profits protected by restricted entry or weak competition,
- or public intervention that raises private asset values without widening secure access.[S07][S10][S13][S20][S34][S35][S36][S37]

## The six main false passes

### 1. Revaluation is mistaken for broadening

A society should count less improvement when wealth grows mainly because already-held assets become more expensive rather than because more people gain secure entry, real saving capacity, or durable claims.[S07][S13][S16]

### 2. Housing appreciation is mistaken for housing justice

A society should count less progress when incumbent owners become richer through land scarcity or supply constraint while young adults, renters, and lower-wealth entrants face later access, higher debt, or exclusion from high-opportunity places.[S07][S13][S19][S34][S35]

### 3. Higher profits are mistaken for socially good wealth formation

Persistent profits are not automatically illegitimate, but the archive should count less progress when rising capital income comes mainly from weak competition, restricted entry, or bottleneck control rather than productive renewal that broadens opportunity and investment.[S10][S20]

### 4. Fee-heavy intermediation is mistaken for ordinary wealth building

A society should count less improvement when a large share of household financial effort is quietly transferred upward through management fees, financing spreads, penalty structures, or other intermediation tolls that reduce net accumulation for ordinary holders.[S16][S37]

### 5. Public support is mistaken for broad wealth creation when it mainly capitalises upward

A society should count less progress when tax relief, cheap credit, rezoning gains, infrastructure uplift, or other public action mainly raises land values, provider prices, or incumbent portfolios rather than widening secure entry or building common wealth.[S34][S35][S36][S37]

### 6. Better shares are mistaken for entrant-friendly path quality

A society should count less success when today’s middle or lower-middle wealth gains are being bought at the cost of worse access, heavier debt, or thinner future footholds for the next cohort.[S07][S13][S18][S19]

## The six standing source-quality surfaces

### 1. Entry versus incumbent revaluation

The archive should ask whether the main gains are coming from **new secure entry** or from higher valuations on assets already held by incumbents.
Gains should count more when new owners, new savers, and younger adults are entering durable claims on reasonable terms.
They should count less when incumbent paper gains rise while entry ratios worsen.[S07][S13][S19]

### 2. Rent, markup, and bottleneck dependence

The archive should ask how much wealth formation depends on scarcity rents, monopoly markups, landlord power, or other bottlenecks that require others to pay more simply to participate in ordinary life.
Wealth built through durable contribution and open rivalry should count more than wealth built through restricted access and weak competitive discipline.[S10][S20]

### 3. Fee drag and intermediation tolls

The archive should ask how much of ordinary saving or portfolio exposure survives after the fees, spreads, and tolls charged by the intermediating system.
Gross account access flatters reality when the net wealth path is quietly thinned by routine extraction from below.[S16][S37]

### 4. Publicly created value versus private windfall capture

The archive should ask whether land-value uplifts and similar gains created by infrastructure, planning decisions, or public coordination are being partly recaptured into common wealth or mainly left to harden into private windfalls.
Where public action creates the value, purely private capture should count less as morally clean wealth formation.[S34][S35]

### 5. Productive renewal versus defended position

The archive should ask whether capital gains and profits are tied to investment, diffusion, entry, and renewal or whether they mainly reflect defended market position, strategic withholding, or balance-sheet enrichment without corresponding widening of opportunity.[S10][S20]

### 6. Entrant-side incidence of the next durable gains

The archive should ask where the **next durable units of wealth** are landing after capitalisation and bottleneck effects are counted.
If the next gains mainly deepen incumbent position while entrants remain later, thinner, or more indebted, the path should be read as morally weaker even if one static distribution table improves.[S07][S13][S18][S19][S34][S35][S36][S37]

## Fast use rule

Use this note in four steps:

1. **Ask how the gain is being made.**  
   Is the wealth increase coming from broader access, stronger saving power, productive renewal, or common-wealth building — or mainly from scarcity, markups, fees, and revaluation?
2. **Read from the entrant side.**  
   Ask whether younger, poorer, or newly entering households are gaining easier access or facing harder access.
3. **Discount extractive broadening.**  
   Count less improvement when incumbent gains depend on price inflation, bottleneck power, or public action that capitalises upward.
4. **Prefer open-access formation.**  
   Count more progress when wage gains, low-fee accumulation, secure housing entry, cooperative or pension claims, public/social wealth, and early footholds widen together.[S07][S10][S13][S16][S20][S34][S35][S36][S37]

Default shortcut:

- **entry-widening, low-extraction gains** -> read the case more favorably because wealth is broadening through open access rather than bottleneck power;
- **incumbent revaluation, rising fees, or markup-heavy gains** -> read the case more harshly because the wealth order is still reproducing dependence from below;
- **mixed case** -> ask whether the next cohort is actually entering more safely or merely facing a richer incumbent class.

## Program implication

Once this source-quality surface is read clearly, two policy mistakes become easier to avoid.
First, the archive should stop treating all wealth gains as equally good once they appear on balance sheets.
Second, it should stop confusing incumbent enrichment with broad entry.

That means a serious anti-inequality program should usually prefer:
- wage growth with bargaining power over reliance on asset booms,
- secure and abundant housing entry over scarcity-supported appreciation,
- competition and anti-monopoly work where markups and protected profits dominate,
- low-fee saving and ownership channels over toll-heavy intermediation,
- and public-value recapture plus common-wealth building where the public helped create the gain.[S07][S10][S20][S34][S35][S36][S37]

## Bottom line

The archive should now read non-extractive wealth formation in this order:

1. **Ask whether new wealth is being built through wider entry or through incumbent revaluation.**
2. **Discount gains that depend mainly on rents, markups, fees, scarcity, or upward capitalisation of public action.**
3. **Read every flattering wealth gain from the entrant side as well as the incumbent side.**
4. **Count open-access, low-extraction accumulation as morally better than bottleneck accumulation.**
5. **Refuse to let richer incumbents stand in for a better wealth order.**
