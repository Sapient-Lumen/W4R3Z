# Immoral-Wealth — rev0302

## Can adulthood begin without a family balance sheet?

Imagine two young adults who can meet the same monthly housing payment. One has parents who can provide a deposit or guarantee; the other does not. Equal present earnings do not give them equal entry. A society can report broader ownership while leaving this threshold largely intact.

That illustrative contrast is the way into rev0302. Its [opening account](files/START_HERE.md) makes an existing concern explicit: early access is not fully open when ordinary entrants still depend on parental deposits, guarantees, inherited housing position or comparable family backing. The edition calls this **family-gated** entry. The question is about how an institution distributes access, not simply how much wealth its current owners hold.

Read the [main Immoral-Wealth guide](../../README.md) for the wider argument about independence and wealth becoming governing power. This entrance follows one narrower thread: **when security arrives, what makes entry possible, and whether the resulting claim really belongs to the person.**

## Timing and family dependence are distinct but connected tests

The [timing essay](files/docs/10-framework/life-course-timing-and-early-footholds.md) asks whether a first durable foothold arrives soon enough to change adult choices. Secure housing, a liquid buffer or a usable capital claim can affect where someone lives, whether they can change work, and which risks they can afford. Later support remains valuable, but cannot simply undo choices already forced under scarcity.

The [circulation essay](files/docs/10-framework/intergenerational-circulation.md) asks a different question: how strongly does parental position determine who can get that foothold? Early ownership obtained mainly through affluent parents may pass the timing test for those recipients while leaving intergenerational access closed. Conversely, a broadly available route that arrives only after decades of insecurity may reduce family sorting without solving the timing problem.

This is why a flattering national snapshot is insufficient. Older owners may be secure while the next cohort needs more debt, more family help or longer waiting to reach comparable security. The work asks readers to examine entrants and cohorts, not infer openness from the comfort of people already inside.

“Foothold” is deliberately broader than buying a home. The circulation essay includes secure renting, fair credit, pension rights, liquid reserves and institutions that let wages become savings. The issue is usable independence, not a requirement that everyone acquire the same asset.

## Non-family routes must do more than create an account

The [social-inheritance proposal](files/docs/20-program/social-inheritance-public-wealth-and-baby-bonds.md) combines an early starter claim with continuing public or social wealth. Its preferred design is near-universal, with more support for people starting further behind. It also insists on direct person-level claims, portability and protection against forfeiture through ordinary transitions.

Those requirements explain why a baby-bond announcement is not the endpoint:

- A claim can exist too late, be too small or remain too restricted to affect early-adult choices.
- A household may receive a benefit while the intended person lacks control or credible exit.
- A grant may be absorbed into housing prices, tuition, fees or lender charges.
- Holding modest wealth may trigger loss of essential support or forced depletion.

The archive therefore joins starter capital to housing and fee reform, an adequate social floor and durable common assets. It does not propose replacing public protection with one private account. Nor does creating a public fund establish that access is fair: the [policy portfolio](files/docs/20-program/ideal-distribution-and-policy-portfolio.md) requires rule-bound access and governance rather than a new dependence on patrons or discretionary gatekeepers.

These are proposed institutional conditions. Whether a particular package achieves them still requires evidence about who gains entry, who retains control and what happens after ordinary shocks or changes of job, household or place.

## A diagnosis, not a verdict on every family gift

The central criticism is that ordinary adult independence should not require wealthy relatives. That does not by itself settle the moral boundary of every gift, modest inheritance or act of family care. A reader can accept that non-family routes are necessary while still asking how to protect legitimate family provision and which interventions fairly distribute their costs.

The [objections essay](files/docs/20-program/counterarguments-and-failure-modes.md) discusses incentives, administrative limits, exemptions for modest transfers, political legitimacy and capture. It asks critics to identify the actual instrument at issue rather than use the hardest tax problem to dismiss every route into broader security. Its own defense also needs scrutiny: a disappointing result must not become permanently immune to criticism merely because another companion reform can always be named. This is an evaluative question for the program, not a reported failure of a tested package.

## What changed in this edition

The [rev0302 changelog](files/CHANGELOG.md) says the doctrine is unchanged. The revision strengthens the short entry points and their verdict vocabulary so family-backed thresholds are not lost when the fuller argument is compressed.

Within the archive's proposed assessment, “family-gated” names a live reason to withhold the archive's near-ideal / acceptable verdict, not a finding that every other improvement is worthless. Its wider criteria also examine usable security, durability, concentrated control and public counterweights. Passing the family-entry test would therefore be necessary to its account of open circulation, not sufficient to certify an entire wealth order.

This is a useful edition for studying how a long argument survives summary. The additional label matters only if it directs attention back to the mechanism: who can get started without inherited backing, in time to shape adult life, with a claim they can actually use and keep?
