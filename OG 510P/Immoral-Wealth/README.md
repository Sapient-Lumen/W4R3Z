# Immoral-Wealth

## What wealth lets a person refuse

A person can own something and still have little freedom. A house may consume every spare dollar in insurance, utilities and repairs. A pension may be real but unusable at the moment someone needs to leave a job or household. A young adult may earn enough to meet the monthly payments yet be unable to enter housing without a parental deposit or guarantee.

Immoral-Wealth begins with this difference between possessing an asset and possessing independence. It asks what ordinary people can actually keep, use, defend or leave with. At the other end of the distribution, it asks when wealth ceases to be a large personal holding and becomes the power to set other people's terms.

The [core argument](versions/rev0302/files/docs/10-framework/immoral-wealth-inequality.md) does not demand identical fortunes. It allows unequal rewards, enterprise and substantial affluence. Its moral boundary is crossed when wealth becomes a durable system of dependence below, command above and inherited closure between generations. The later [charter](versions/rev0381/files/Immoral-Wealth-rev0381/docs/00-meta/charter.md) makes the paired ambition especially clear: ordinary independence should be widely available; durable private governing power should not.

That is why the work is about more than income. A flow of earnings matters, but assets and liabilities shape the ability to survive an interruption, move, retrain, begin a business or refuse a bad offer. Some assets also carry votes, control of land or infrastructure, influence over institutions, and claims on other people's future work. A distribution of wealth is partly a distribution of options and authority.

## Ownership that can be kept

The [carrying-cost essay](versions/rev0104/files/docs/10-framework/carrying-costs-and-self-eroding-footholds.md) is a particularly useful way into the project. Its question is not merely whether someone can buy a foothold, but whether ordinary life lets them retain it.

It distinguishes three things easily collapsed in a headline balance sheet: debt, sudden shocks and continuous erosion. A mortgage-free home can still be expensive to hold. A small account can lose much of its value to recurring charges. Repairs postponed to make ownership affordable may consume the asset's condition rather than preserve security.

The argument is not that taxes, maintenance or fees are inherently illegitimate. It is that ownership does different moral work when these costs leave room to save and choose than when they consume the entire margin. Acquisition is only the beginning of the test.

This also keeps the work from treating homeownership as the only acceptable destination. A later [German renter-security case](versions/rev0381/files/Immoral-Wealth-rev0381/cases/germany-renter-security-rev0306-case.md) deliberately asks whether secure, affordable renting and room to accumulate other assets can supply a genuine foothold. Its answer is conditional, not a certified verdict on Germany. The important correction is conceptual: tenure should be judged by security, access and exit, not by the prestige of its label.

## Adulthood before inheritance

The [timing](versions/rev0104/files/docs/10-framework/life-course-timing-and-early-footholds.md) and [circulation](versions/rev0104/files/docs/10-framework/intergenerational-circulation.md) essays ask when a foothold arrives and whose resources make entry possible. Wealth received late in life cannot fully replace the options missing when someone chose education, work, housing or care under pressure.

The rev0302 snapshot makes one form of closure unusually explicit: parental deposits, guarantees, inherited housing position and other family backing at the threshold of entry. A market can look open to everyone while its practical admission ticket remains a family balance sheet. Equal current earnings do not erase that difference.

The proposed response is not simply to wait for inheritance taxes to redistribute later. It is to build non-family routes into early security: starter capital, safe credit, secure housing, common provision and earnings that leave room to save. These measures must also resist capture. A grant that immediately becomes a higher purchase price, rent or lender fee has not necessarily made entry more independent.

The later [Child Trust Fund case](versions/rev0381/files/Immoral-Wealth-rev0381/cases/uk-child-trust-fund-rev0307-case.md) adds another difficulty. An account can exist without the intended young adult finding or claiming it. The archive therefore asks for notification, accessible tracing, usable balances and direct control, not just account creation. It is a provisional historical case, but a strong example of the underlying idea: a promised asset must become a usable claim.

## A proposed distribution rather than a magic ratio

The default measurement lens is household-based private net wealth, followed by a person-level check of title, control and exit. Within that lens, the archive offers a recognizable numerical center:

- Bottom 50 percent: 20 percent of private wealth.
- Middle 40 percent: 50 percent.
- Next 9 percent: 20 percent.
- Top 1 percent: 10 percent.

Its [distribution essay](versions/rev0302/files/docs/10-framework/ideal-wealth-distribution-bands.md) treats this as a working family with ranges, not an exact compulsory allocation. Public and social wealth is a separate requirement, not another slice hidden inside those four shares.

These numbers are normative proposals. Descriptive inequality research does not prove that these exact percentages are morally correct. The later archive says so directly and acknowledges the danger of making its own target look externally established through repeated citations.

Even on its own terms, reaching the numbers would not settle the question. A household total may conceal an adult without title or safe exit. Pension beneficiaries may hold financial claims while a narrow managerial group controls the votes. Rising house values may enrich existing owners while closing entry to others. National improvement may leave particular groups excluded.

The work consequently refuses to let a good average compensate for a decisive failure of independence or control. This makes the framework more demanding than a target chart, although it also leaves a difficult question: how should imperfect reforms be compared when several urgent goods compete?

## Why no single instrument can carry the program

The [policy portfolio](versions/rev0302/files/docs/20-program/ideal-distribution-and-policy-portfolio.md) combines measures because isolated gains can be undone elsewhere.

A stronger social floor reduces the private savings needed to survive ordinary illness, care or earnings interruption. Better wages and bargaining power create income that can become wealth. Earlier person-level claims reduce dependence on wealthy relatives. Housing, land and competition reforms aim to stop those gains being absorbed by scarcity and tolls. Capital-income, inheritance and gift reforms interrupt dynastic accumulation. Public assets and anti-capture institutions provide a counterweight to private power.

The [tax argument](versions/rev0302/files/docs/20-program/tax-capital-gains-inheritance-and-wealth.md) is therefore more specific than a demand for one wealth tax. It asks what base can be seen and taxed, how gifts and inheritances interact, whether trusts and other structures obscure control, and which instruments fit administrative capacity. It also asks whether hidden housing or retirement subsidies refill incumbent wealth while the visible tax schedule appears progressive.

The [social-inheritance argument](versions/rev0302/files/docs/20-program/social-inheritance-public-wealth-and-baby-bonds.md) is equally conditional. A starter claim should arrive early, belong to the person, survive ordinary transitions and avoid being clawed back by support rules. It should supplement an adequate floor, not become an excuse to leave people alone with a small account and large risks.

Nor is public ownership automatically the answer. The archive worries about patronage, opaque managers and discretionary access. Replacing a private gatekeeper with an unaccountable public one would fail its own test.

## What the argument still owes its critics

The [objections essay](versions/rev0302/files/docs/20-program/counterarguments-and-failure-modes.md) takes productive capacity, administrative visibility, mobility, political legitimacy and capture seriously without treating every difficulty as a veto on reform. Its useful discipline is to name the particular instrument and mechanism: a difficulty valuing offshore holdings does not automatically defeat a different proposal about land, inheritance or accessible claims.

That defense also needs limits. If every disappointing outcome is attributed to a missing companion reform, the favored package can become impossible to disconfirm. A serious assessment must say what would count against the package itself. Likewise, refusing to average away domination does not yet settle how to compare incomplete reforms when their costs, timing and benefits conflict. The exact distribution bands, the boundary between legitimate family provision and dynastic closure, and the durability of public counterpower remain questions for argument and evidence rather than conclusions guaranteed by the framework's vocabulary.

## A development history worth reading

The supplied snapshots preserve distinct stages of this argument.

- [Rev0019](versions/rev0019/README.md) already contains the floor, inheritance, timing, person-level ownership and reconcentration concerns. Its immediate addition examines hidden wealth-building subsidies and who actually benefits from them.
- [Rev0104](versions/rev0104/README.md) sharpens carrying costs into a stable test of whether ownership can be held. It reorganizes an existing doctrine rather than claiming a new discovery.
- [Rev0302](versions/rev0302/files/START_HERE.md) makes family-backed entry explicit in the short criteria for judging a wealth order. Its own history says the doctrine is unchanged; the change makes an existing concern harder to overlook.
- [Rev0381](versions/rev0381/files/Immoral-Wealth-rev0381/START_HERE.md) contains a much larger set of institutional cases, evidence records and self-audits. The latest case work connects Medicare Advantage enrollment and incentives to claims, remedies and practical exit. It remains a test of the wider mission, not a replacement for it.

The later [mission audit](versions/rev0381/files/Immoral-Wealth-rev0381/reports/mission-heart-truth-surface-and-semantic-evidence-reset-rev0371.md) is important reading. It criticizes the archive's own numerical overconfidence, lack of independent review, thin outcome evidence and tendency to accumulate machinery faster than completed answers. That self-criticism belongs alongside the philosophical achievement.

At the preserved rev0381 boundary, the [maturity ledger](versions/rev0381/files/Immoral-Wealth-rev0381/cases/CASE_MATURITY_LEDGER.json) reports no certified-current cases. Source associations are not independent verification, and a complete-looking record is not a proved conclusion. Archived legal and economic claims also need fresh primary checking before they are used as statements about the present.

The work is nevertheless more than its unfinished certification system. It offers a sustained way to ask whether an institution gives people usable independence or merely records assets in their names. Its most serious challenge is to demonstrate, through bounded and contested cases, which proposed institutions actually achieve that aim.
