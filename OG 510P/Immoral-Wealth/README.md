# Immoral-Wealth

## More than a picture of unequal shares

Immoral-Wealth asks when differences in assets become an order of domination and closure. Its concern is not only what fraction belongs to whom, but whether ordinary people have durable independence and whether concentrated wealth can govern their opportunities or public institutions.

The distinction between wealth and income matters throughout. Assets, debt, inheritance, land, ownership and public balance sheets affect what a person can withstand, refuse or begin. A reform can improve a headline distribution while leaving dependence largely intact.

## Read the targets as proposals

The archive’s distribution targets are normative working choices with supporting arguments and conditions. They are not discovered constants or a personal investment prescription. Its stronger question is whether a proposed order preserves an asset floor, limits private governing power and resists rapid reconcentration.

The later rev0381 delivery retains a rev0380 heading before its appended update. Known checksum-list self-reference differences in earlier snapshots are also preserved. Those archival details belong below the reading entrance, but they matter when comparing exact claims across versions.

Read beside [Moral Taxation](../Moral-Taxation/README.md), which asks how to choose an instrument and burden bearer, and [The Good](../The-Good/README.md), which supplies a neighboring account of sufficiency and protected floors. The three works can inform and challenge one another without becoming a single policy manual.
