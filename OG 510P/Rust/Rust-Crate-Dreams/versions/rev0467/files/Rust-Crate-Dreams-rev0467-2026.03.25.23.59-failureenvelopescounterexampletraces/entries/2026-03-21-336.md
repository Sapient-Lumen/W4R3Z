# Entry 336 — Async Dyn Transition Kit deepened for recipe identity, tooling interop, and native-readiness truth

This pass returned to **P-0458 Async Dyn Transition Kit** instead of adding another async bridge macro, another executor abstraction, or another generic trait-refactoring crate.

## Why this lane earned a serious pass now

The archive already had the right instinct for **P-0458** and a useful early fixture pack.
What it still lacked was the same product-shape discipline recent frontiers received: a `0.1` build sketch, a lane-boundary memo, and a clearer split between **recipe identity**, **tooling interop**, and **native-readiness posture**.

Current substrate makes that split unusually concrete:

- Rust's 2026 flagships still list **stabilize `async fn in dyn trait`** inside **Just Add Async**;
- the async-fundamentals explainer and its allocation chapter make it clear that dyn async dispatch is about **object surface** and **allocation authority**, not just syntax sugar;
- `async-trait` remains the common type-erasure path for dyn support today;
- `trait-variant` is a send-split specialization path, not native dyn support by itself;
- `dynosaur` and `dynify` prove that distinct bridge families already exist and should not be blurred together;
- `mockall` shows that macro ordering and canonical import rules are real downstream compatibility constraints.

That means a worthy crate here should not merely say “supports async traits” or “future native support will solve this.”
It should tell another maintainer **which recipe exists today, what it promises publicly, what tooling constraints come with it, and whether native arrival is expected to preserve or reopen the review**.

## What moved this time

This pass elevated five review objects into first-class status for **P-0458**:

1. **recipe identity** — `async-trait`, `trait-variant`, `dynosaur`, `dynify`, local adapter, or mixed family;
2. **object surface** — whether dyn dispatch, generated wrappers, or static-only posture are actually part of the public promise;
3. **allocation posture** — boxed futures, caller-owned buffers, wrapper-owned storage, or honest unknowns;
4. **tooling interop** — mockability, macro ordering, canonical imports, and comparable downstream ergonomics;
5. **native readiness** — whether future native `async fn in dyn Trait` is expected to preserve the current promise or trigger a fresh surface review.

## Main judgment after the pass

A worthy async-dyn transition contribution for Rust should now provide more than a proc macro, more than a migration blog post, and more than a vague promise that native support is coming.
It should also provide:

- one explicit **dispatch-recipe receipt**,
- one explicit **allocation profile**,
- one explicit **tooling-interop report**,
- one explicit **native-readiness receipt**,
- one explicit **object-surface diff**,
- and one explicit **migration receipt**.

That is the smallest support surface that gives another team something reviewable instead of folklore.
