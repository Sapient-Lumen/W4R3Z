# Entry 466 — 2026-03-25

## Theme
**assurance-case / claim-traceability / witness-chain pass**

## Why this pass exists

The archive already had strong layers for:
- evidence bundles,
- conformance tiers,
- interchange profiles,
- policy packs,
- review packets,
- renewal programs,
- and delta/carry-forward handling.

What it was still missing was a sharper answer to:

**why should another team believe a claim, and how can they trace that belief back to named witnesses, warrants, exceptions, and supersession history without reconstructing the whole story by hand?**

This pass answers with:
- one shared **claim catalog**,
- one portable **assurance case** that links claims to witnesses and warrants,
- one **witness bundle** that keeps raw basis distinct from reviewed claims,
- one **challenge register** for explicit open objections and unresolved hazards,
- one **non-claim note** for things the crate refuses to imply,
- and one **supersession bridge** so old cases are replaced rather than forgotten.

## Main judgment

### 1. The next missing layer is not more verdict vocabulary; it is traceable justification

The archive already knew how a worthy crate might emit receipts, support tiers, policy verdicts, review packets, renewal ledgers, and delta bundles.
What it still lacked was a durable **argument structure** tying those outputs together.

A worthy crate should increasingly provide downstream teams with:
- **what is being claimed**,
- **what evidence witnesses that claim**,
- **what warrant turns that evidence into the claim**,
- **what exceptions or gaps weaken it**,
- **which claims are inherited versus freshly established**,
- and **which changes supersede the old case**.

That is a stronger answer to “what should the crate provide other people?” than one more local report or even one more policy decision.

### 2. The strongest assurance-first lane is the decision front door, with the support-envelope ring right behind it

For the assurance-case lens, the strongest immediate build slice is now:
1. **P-0509 + P-0536 + minimal P-0535**
2. **P-0472 + P-0484**
3. **P-0431 + P-0496 + P-0125**
4. **P-0486**
5. **P-0537**
6. **P-0538**

This does **not** overthrow the archive’s broad frontier.
It changes the next practical question.

The decision front door rises because that is where another team actually needs:
- a bounded recommendation claim,
- explicit decisive witnesses,
- stated warrants,
- named exceptions,
- and inherited-versus-fresh reasoning.

The support-envelope ring remains essential because it is the best supplier of witness-grade docs/build/API/target facts.
The continuity ring follows because it is where inherited claims are preserved, downgraded, or reset when routes, advisories, mirrors, or boundaries change.

### 3. A worthy crate should ship a reviewable case, not just a packet

A stronger crate promise is not only:
- “here is the packet,”
- “here is the verdict,”
- or “here is the rerun plan.”

It is also:
- **here is the claim this packet supports**,
- **here are the witnesses that matter**,
- **here is the warrant we are using**,
- **here are the unresolved challenges**,
- **here is what we explicitly do not claim**,
- and **here is how this case supersedes or inherits from the previous one**.

That matters especially where support claims are subtle or expensive:
- docs.rs target/default-target changes,
- debugger and OS/version variance,
- build-dir and build-analysis substrate movement,
- public/private boundary drift,
- alternate-registry and mirror divergence,
- and safety-critical or long-lived adoption.

### 4. The archive itself now deserves a machine-readable assurance memory file

The repo already had `archive-state.json`, `suite-topologies.json`, `conformance-kits.json`, `interchange-profiles.json`, `policy-packs.json`, `review-packets.json`, `renewal-programs.json`, and `delta-programs.json`.
This pass adds **`assurance-cases.json`** so later human/LLM passes can see:
- the current claim vocabulary,
- the default artifact family for traceable justification,
- which lanes are strongest under the assurance lens,
- and which lane should define shared witness / warrant / challenge semantics first.

This is repo hygiene, not a crate proposal.

## Practical lesson from this pass

A worthy Rust crate contribution should often provide other people with:
- one **claim catalog**,
- one portable **assurance case**,
- one **witness bundle**,
- one explicit **warrant vocabulary**,
- one **challenge register**,
- one short **assurance summary**,
- one **non-claim note**,
- and one clear **supersession / inheritance bridge**.

That is how a crate gives another team something they can not only run and review, but actually **believe, contest, and carry forward**.
