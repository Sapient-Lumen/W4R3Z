# Entry 390 — MC/DC coverage deepened around profile compatibility, campaign policy, and manual-review debt for P-0433

This pass deepened **P-0433 MC/DC Coverage Workbench Kit** again, but in a narrower, more review-critical direction: **profile compatibility, campaign policy, and manual-review debt**.

## Why this lane earned another pass now

Fresh official and primary-source signals line up unusually well:

- the Rust 2026 flagship themes still explicitly include **implement MC/DC coverage support** in the Safety-Critical Rust milestone set;
- the rustc coverage book still documents a real source-based workflow, but it says including doctests requires unstable `--persist-doctests`, still points at the known doctest source-line issue, and keeps `-Z coverage-options=<options>` unstable;
- the rustc codegen-options book still warns that while `-C instrument-coverage` is stable, the profile data format may change and may not work with coverage tools other than those built and shipped with the compiler;
- `cargo-llvm-cov` still exposes `--mcdc` and doctest support as unstable;
- LLVM’s current source-based coverage and instrumentation-profile docs say raw profiles have **no backward or forward compatibility guarantees**, while indexed formats are backward-compatible but not forward-compatible;
- Rust’s current branch-coverage limitations issue still lists `match` arms / or-patterns, `?`, `.await`, and macro-introduced branches as unsupported or caveat-heavy;
- and the current MC/DC-for-Rust research line still argues that Rust-specific constructs need language-aware handling rather than generic C-style assumptions.

Together these sharpen one conclusion: a worthy crate here is not just “collect MC/DC-looking evidence”.
It is a crate that helps other people inspect **whether the profile inputs are safe to merge/trend, what construct-policy bar the campaign actually adopted, and what manual-review debt still remains even after the runner succeeded**.

## Main judgment after the pass

The strongest near-term contribution in this lane is still a **stable support-contract layer** above coverage runners, LLVM reports, and future upstream MC/DC maturation.
But that support contract now needs three additional review objects:

11. **profile-compatibility receipt** — whether the raw/indexed profile artifacts, producers, and merge basis are safe for the intended comparison or retention story;
12. **campaign-policy receipt** — what support classes, construct families, and execution requirements the campaign explicitly required, tolerated with caveats, or excluded by policy;
13. **manual-review-debt report** — which decisions, construct families, or scope gaps still need human review or added evidence before the bundle can support a stronger assurance claim.

## Why these objects matter

Current substrate is now concrete enough that the archive should stop flattening MC/DC-facing evidence:

- two bundles can both say “MC/DC run succeeded” while one depends on raw profiles whose producer/tool version makes long-lived trend claims unsafe;
- a construct-support matrix can show `match_arm` or `question_mark` as unsupported without saying whether the campaign excluded them by policy, tolerated them with caveats, or now owes a manual-review debt item;
- a host-only preview run can be valuable exploratory evidence while still leaving unresolved debt for on-target execution, macro-generated spans, or unsupported control-flow families;
- and a green summary can still hide a growing queue of unresolved review obligations.

## New archive-level stance

For safety-critical coverage work, the archive should now prefer this layering:

1. **P-0433** for decision authority, construct support, campaign scope, independence evidence, caveat basis, comparison basis, qualification basis, profile compatibility, campaign policy, manual-review debt, and lineage;
2. runner/workflow tools such as `cargo-llvm-cov` for practical execution substrate;
3. rustc/LLVM coverage for instrumentation, profile formats, and report generation substrate;
4. broader verification-campaign or assurance-case lanes only after the MC/DC support bundle stays honest on its own.

That keeps the portfolio from minting another crate that merely says “MC/DC evidence exists” without clarifying whether the profile inputs were durable, whether unsupported constructs were explicitly accepted or deferred, or what review debt still blocks a stronger claim.

## What should count as a worthy crate contribution after this pass

A worthy MC/DC-facing contribution should now be able to answer all of these cleanly:

1. **What decision inventory was authoritative?**
2. **Which construct classes were supported, unsupported, or excluded?**
3. **What exact campaign scope produced the evidence?**
4. **Which conditions have witnessed independence pairs?**
5. **What caveat basis constrained the result?**
6. **Are two bundles honestly comparable?**
7. **What qualification story is actually being told?**
8. **Are the profile inputs safe for the intended merge / retention / trend claim?**
9. **What policy bar was the campaign actually enforcing?**
10. **What manual-review debt still remains?**
11. **What concrete run lineage backs the verdict?**

If a candidate crate cannot answer those questions, the archive should usually treat it as coverage folklore rather than a serious support contract.
