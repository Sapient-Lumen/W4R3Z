# Entry 273 — crate upgrade support sharpened around expiring deviations and compact warning registers

This pass came from noticing that the pack could now be honest about readiness, consistency, export posture, and public surface while still having two quiet escape hatches:

1. maintainers could intentionally proceed under exception without leaving a durable record,
2. and public warning labels could still float free of severity, audience, and provenance.

## What moved this time

Two donor patterns kept surviving scrutiny:

- **Radical-Governance** made the strongest case that non-default posture should become a numbered, expiring deviation record with owner, authority, and public-summary status rather than a hallway-memory decision.
- **Goldenrule + The Election Stack** made the strongest case that warning labels should be backed by one compact register that keeps severity, audience, source refs, and surfaced-on refs explicit.

Both moves apply directly to **P-0514 Crate Upgrade Pack Kit**.

## Main planning move

The previous passes already established:

- source lineage and source heads,
- hazard arbitration and native import truth,
- fixup posture,
- package-scope truth and active follow-through state,
- pack readiness,
- review queue and cross-register consistency,
- export posture,
- and exact public surface.

This pass adds two sharper review objects above them:

1. **deviation-ledger truth** — if a team intentionally carries a freeze/export/consistency override, it becomes a numbered, expiring record with compensating controls and public-summary status,
2. **warning-register truth** — if the pack exports warning labels, one compact register says which warnings are active, blocking, public, and optionally linked to a live deviation.

## Why this matters

Without **deviation-ledger truth**, the crate can still honestly say a pack is only a `candidate_public` bundle and then quietly carry that exception forward for weeks without owner, expiry, or public summary.

Without **warning-register truth**, the crate can still publish warning labels such as `citation_head_missing` or `manual_review_required` while leaving downstream users to guess:

- whether the warning is blocking or advisory,
- whether it is maintainer-only or part of the public contract,
- where it came from,
- and whether a temporary override is carrying it forward.

## Main judgment after the pass

A worthy upgrade-support crate should now provide **more than an internally consistent migration bundle and an exact public surface**.
It should also provide:

- an **expiring deviation ledger** for the rare times a team intentionally proceeds under override,
- a **compact warning register** that says which warnings are active, blocking, public, and provenance-backed,
- and explicit links between warning labels and any deviation that temporarily carries them forward.

That makes the lane more honest in two more places where support artifacts often start bluffing: they hide exceptions in institutional memory, and they export warnings without enough structure to tell users what those warnings actually mean.
