# Entry 429 — concurrency-contract deepened around delivery order, sequence windows, and gap-visibility honesty

This pass chose to **deepen P-0538 Concurrency Contract Kit** again instead of inventing another queue wrapper, actor mailbox helper, or “best channels” comparison layer.

The sharper question was:

> once a concurrency surface can already describe memory, pressure, audience, claim, and producer-visible acceptance, how does it also say **what sequence a receiver is actually promised** and **whether missed or collapsed units are visible, countable, or silent**?

## Why this deepening pass earned a full revision

Fresh primary sources made the next missing layer concrete:

- the March 2026 Rust challenges write-up still says async complexity remains part of the current Rust pain picture;
- Tokio bounded `mpsc` explicitly says all data sent on `Sender` becomes available on `Receiver` in the same order it was sent;
- Tokio `broadcast` explicitly says all data sent becomes available on every active receiver in the same order it was sent;
- Tokio `broadcast::RecvError::Lagged` explicitly includes the number of skipped messages, and `Receiver::recv` explicitly says a lagging receiver’s cursor is moved to the oldest value still retained;
- Tokio `watch` explicitly says only the last value sent is made available and all intermediate values are dropped;
- Tokio `Notify` explicitly says it stores at most one permit and many sequential `notify_one` calls collapse to a single stored permit;
- Crossbeam `Select` explicitly says if multiple operations are ready at the same time, a random one is selected, while `new_biased` instead chooses the lowest index.

That means the next worthy move for **P-0538** is not just broader “channel behavior.”
It is to make **delivery-order** and **gap-visibility** first-class review artifacts.

## Main judgment after this pass

The top frontier still begins:

1. **P-0537 Compile Iteration Feedback Kit**
2. **P-0509 Crate Ecosystem Pathfinder & Decision-Pack Kit**
3. **P-0535 Dependency Lifecycle Transition Kit**
4. **P-0536 Crate Knowledge Pack Kit**
5. **P-0538 Concurrency Contract Kit**

But **P-0538** is stronger now because the archive no longer stops at:

- reentrancy scope,
- progress/fairness class,
- wait-cancellation semantics,
- recovery posture,
- execution-context boundaries,
- mobility / affinity,
- driver-liveness,
- delivery memory,
- backlog pressure,
- delivery audience,
- consumption claim,
- delivery acceptance,
- observation-evidence ceilings.

It now has a clearer product shape for:

- **delivery-order reports**,
- **gap-visibility reports**,
- **per-receiver FIFO versus latest-snapshot-only honesty**,
- **counted skip signals versus silent collapse honesty**,
- and portable bundle inventory that keeps order/gap truth separate from memory, pressure, audience, claim, fairness, cancellation, or acceptance/evidence.

## What should count as a worthy crate contribution in this lane now

A worthy concurrency-contract crate should provide other people:

1. **one delivery-order report** that says whether the surface promises FIFO to one receiver, FIFO per receiver, latest-snapshot-only replacement, coalesced wake semantics, or selection-level random choice;
2. **one gap-visibility report** that says whether missed units are impossible under the supported route, counted, cursor-rebased, silently collapsed, or only manually reviewable;
3. **one explicit separation between “the surface has memory” and “the surface exports a meaningful observable sequence”**;
4. **one explicit separation between “something was missed” and “the receiver can quantify or even detect what was missed”**;
5. **one portable bundle** that keeps order and gap visibility separate from memory, pressure, audience, claim, acceptance/evidence, fairness, cancellation, recovery, context, locality, and liveness.

That is closer to an ecosystem-worthy crate than another queue wrapper or “channel comparison” article because it tells adopters whether they can reason about sequence at all.
