# Entry 317 — trust-lens deepened for notification-channel coverage, watch-surface honesty, and feed-gap visibility

This pass deepened **P-0017 Trust Lens** rather than opening another adjacent advisory client, feed mirror, or registry-policy lane.

## Why this lane earned another serious pass

The archive already had sharper answers for:

- confusable-name / impersonation risk,
- signal-basis provenance,
- assumption registers,
- review debt,
- and conservative policy decisions.

What still needed a more disciplined answer was the watch question:

> “Which channel would actually tell us that this trust posture changed, and what classes of change would that channel miss?”

Current crates.io and RustSec signals now make that question concrete enough to build around.

## What moved this time

The key planning move was to stop treating “we checked trust signals” as sufficient watch truth.

This pass elevated three review objects more explicitly:

1. **notification-channel coverage** — which concrete watch surfaces were consulted, such as RustSec advisories / RSS, the crates.io Security tab, crate settings for Trusted Publishing posture, or the Rust blog;
2. **change-class coverage** — whether the recorded watch set covers known vulnerabilities, routine malicious-crate removals, higher-signal campaign incidents, publish-identity posture, or only a subset of those classes;
3. **feed-gap visibility** — whether the current watch posture leaves a decisive trust-change class with no configured route and therefore should downgrade to `manual_review_required`.

## Main judgment after the pass

A worthy trust-lens contribution should not stop at current advisories, audit imports, or identity-risk flags.
It should also provide:

- one explicit **notification-channel report**,
- one honest **change-class coverage** summary,
- and one conservative **feed-gap warning** when a team is relying on blog posts, crate pages, or publishing settings for trust changes those surfaces do not comprehensively carry.
