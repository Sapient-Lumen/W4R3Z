# Entry 339 — cross-language interop frontier mapping, archive memory anchoring, and FFI boundary deepening for P-0121

This pass deliberately **deepened P-0121 FFI Boundary & Bindings Conformance Kit** and added archive-level interop memory aids instead of opening another language-specific shipkit.

## Why this lane earned another serious pass

Fresh official signals line up unusually well:

- the 2025 State of Rust survey still shows **resource usage** and **debugging** as recurring ecosystem pain, which means crates that lower adoption friction for downstream teams still matter;
- the 2026 program-management update explicitly frames **Cross-language interop** and **Safety-critical & regulated** as application areas;
- the January 2026 safety-critical Rust write-up explicitly says teams will keep integrating Rust into C and C++ systems for years and that interface correctness, auditability, and sync matter;
- and the same post includes a field quote that the hard part is often **generating bindings and finding out what the problem was**.

That combination makes a boring conclusion more compelling, not less: one of the most worthwhile Rust contributions is still a crate that helps other people understand a foreign boundary *without reading generator folklore and glue code by hand*.

## Main judgment after the pass

The strongest near-term interop contribution is **not** another generator.
It is a **reviewable boundary contract layer** above generators and bridge families.

The archive already had four first-class review objects for **P-0121**:

1. ownership transfer,
2. unwind posture,
3. callback execution,
4. binding coverage.

This pass promoted two more objects into first-class status:

5. **layout authority** — what representation actually crosses the boundary, who is the source of truth, and how that representation is verified;
6. **error channel** — how fallibility, panic, exception, and callback failure actually cross the boundary, including payload fidelity and unexpected-failure behavior.

## Why these two objects matter

Current interop substrate is now specific enough that the archive should stop flattening it:

- `cxx` sharply separates **shared by-value types** from **opaque indirection-only types**;
- UniFFI documents explicit object-reference and callback-handle ownership mechanics, async cancellation hooks, and error/exception shaping;
- Diplomat documents explicit opacity and limited supported type/result surfaces;
- WIT + the component model make **interface contracts** and the **Canonical ABI** explicit;
- `cbindgen` is still header-generation substrate, but generated declarations are not a complete boundary contract by themselves.

A crate can therefore truthfully say “supports C++/Swift/Kotlin/Wasm bindings” while still hiding:

- whether a type is by-value, opaque-handle, serialized-buffer, or canonical-resource based;
- whether errors arrive as a flat enum, message-only exception, structured result, status code, or panic;
- and whether the strongest guarantees are actually uniform across surfaces.

## New archive-level stance

For interop-heavy work, the archive should now prefer this layering:

1. **P-0121** for shared boundary-contract truth;
2. language- or packaging-specific shipkits for delivery workflows;
3. plugin/component lanes for sandboxing, capabilities, and lifecycle;
4. language-runtime lanes for thread attachment, interpreter/runtime obligations, or packaging-specific requirements.

That keeps the portfolio broad without duplicating the same ownership/error/layout story in every interop proposal.

## What should count as a worthy crate contribution after this pass

A worthy interop contribution should now be able to answer all of these cleanly:

1. **What actually crosses the boundary?**
2. **Who is the source of truth for that representation?**
3. **How do ownership and teardown work?**
4. **How do errors, panics, or exceptions cross?**
5. **Which surfaces are truly checked, and which are only generated or packaged?**
6. **What neighboring lanes does the crate refuse to absorb?**

If a candidate cannot answer those questions, the archive should usually synthesize it into an adjacent lane rather than mint another proposal.
