# Entry 290 — trust-lens sharpened from score folklore into a reviewable trust bundle

This pass came from noticing that the archive had gotten much better at **choosing crates**, **freezing starter sets**, **describing crate health**, and **planning off-ramps** while leaving one adjacent lane too underplanned:

> how does another team review whether a dependency graph is *trustworthy enough to carry* without collapsing confusable names, live advisories, publisher identity, imported audits, and remaining human review debt into one fake score?

## What moved this time

Several live Rust/security signals changed the shape of the gap:

- crates.io now exposes a **Security** tab and **Trusted Publishing Only Mode**, so some trust signals are finally visible at the registry surface;
- the crates.io malicious-crate policy now says routine malware removals will always produce a **RustSec advisory** and can be followed via the **RustSec RSS feed**;
- real 2026 RustSec advisories show active **typosquat / impersonation campaigns** such as `tracings` and `chrono_anchor`;
- **Cargo Sherlock** shows that trust can be modeled as an assumption-bearing problem rather than just a heuristic score;
- **Cargo Scan** shows that many crates can be classified automatically, but some still carry concentrated **manual review debt** tied to dangerous effects and calling context;
- `cargo vet` remains useful audit substrate, but it is still fundamentally about matching dependencies against trusted audit records rather than publishing one joined trust posture.

That means the missing crate is no longer just “a trust score tool.”
It is a **reviewable trust bundle** above the growing pile of registry, advisory, audit, and research signals.

## Main planning move

This pass sharpens **P-0017 Trust Lens** around five first-class review objects:

1. **identity-risk truth** — which dependencies look confusable, campaign-adjacent, impersonation-prone, or otherwise likely to be mistaken for something else,
2. **signal-basis truth** — which cues come from crates.io, RustSec, trusted-publishing posture, cargo-vet imports, Cargo Scan findings, Cargo Sherlock assumptions, or local policy,
3. **assumption-register truth** — which assumptions are carrying the trust posture instead of hiding behind one magic score,
4. **review-debt truth** — what still needs human inspection even if registry/advisory signals look clean,
5. **policy-decision truth** — whether the graph is `allow`, `warn`, `manual_review_required`, or `deny`, and why.

## Why this matters

Without those five objects, the archive still permits one ordinary lie:

- a graph can have no known advisory at capture time,
- and some crates can even show Trusted Publishing or imported audit signals,
- while one dependency still looks like a typosquat of a widely used crate,
- or still carries dangerous-effect review debt,
- or still depends on unspoken trust assumptions that another team would have rejected if they had been made explicit.

That is not a cosmetic problem.
It is the difference between “we have some security-ish signals” and “another team can review what trust assumptions they are being asked to accept.”

## Main judgment after the pass

A worthy trust/risk contribution for Rust should now provide more than a score, more than a confusable-name warning, and more than a cargo-vet import.
It should provide:

- one explicit **identity-risk report**,
- one explicit **signal-basis report**,
- one explicit **assumption register**,
- one explicit **review-debt report**,
- and one explicit **policy-decision report**.

That is a better answer to the current ecosystem than either:

- “just compute a trust cost,”
- “just show trusted-publishing and RustSec state,”
- or “just import audits and hope the rest is obvious.”
