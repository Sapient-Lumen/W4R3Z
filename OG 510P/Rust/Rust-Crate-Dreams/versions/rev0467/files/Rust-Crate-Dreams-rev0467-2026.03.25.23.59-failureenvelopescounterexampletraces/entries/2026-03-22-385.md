# Entry 385 — MC/DC coverage deepened around campaign scope, comparison basis, and qualification honesty for P-0433

This pass deepened **P-0433 MC/DC Coverage Workbench Kit** again, but in a narrower, more review-critical direction: **campaign scope, comparison basis, and qualification honesty**.

## Why this lane earned another pass now

Fresh official and primary-source signals line up unusually well:

- the Rust 2026 flagship themes still explicitly include **implement MC/DC coverage support** in the Safety-Critical Rust milestone set;
- the rustc coverage book still documents a real source-based workflow, but it says including doctests requires unstable `--persist-doctests`, and it still notes a known doctest source-line issue in `llvm-cov show`;
- `cargo-llvm-cov` still exposes `--branch` / `--mcdc` and doctest support as unstable, warns that `--remap-path-prefix` is not fully compatible with doctests, and notes that `--target` mode omits proc-macro and build-script coverage from display;
- nightly rustc source now makes the coverage mode split explicit as `Block`, `Branch`, and `MCDC`, with `MCDC` defined as branch coverage plus extra instrumentation for some boolean expressions that are not directly used for branching;
- Clang/LLVM’s current source-based coverage docs now spell out that raw profiles have **no** backward or forward compatibility guarantees, that coverage mappings are not forward-compatible, and that Clang MC/DC views are bounded and can exclude expressions when hard limits are exceeded;
- and current Rust MC/DC research still shows that Rust-specific constructs such as `?`, pattern matching, and “constant” conditions need language-aware interpretation rather than generic C-like assumptions.

Together these make one conclusion sharper: a worthy crate here still is not “run MC/DC and emit a number”.
It is a crate that helps other people inspect **what campaign scope produced the result, whether two bundles are actually comparable, and what qualification basis keeps the verdict conservative**.

## Main judgment after the pass

The strongest near-term contribution in this lane is still a **stable support-contract layer** above coverage runners, LLVM reports, and future upstream MC/DC maturation.
But that support contract now needs three additional review objects:

8. **campaign-scope receipt** — which packages, targets, test families, doctest lanes, and execution environments were actually in scope;
9. **comparison-basis receipt** — whether two support bundles are honestly comparable, or whether scope/toolchain/support drift blocks a trend claim;
10. **qualification-basis receipt** — whether the campaign is exploratory, evidence-only, mixed host/target, or conservative enough for a stronger review story.

## Why these objects matter

Current substrate is now concrete enough that the archive should stop flattening MC/DC-facing comparisons:

- two runs can share the same source revision and still differ materially because one omitted doctests or external harnesses;
- two runs can both say “MC/DC” while differing in toolchain revision, coverage-mode support, or construct exclusions;
- a host-executed run can still be useful evidence without honestly serving as the same qualification story as an on-target execution;
- and merged raw/indexed profile data can be real while still being a bad basis for long-lived, like-for-like comparisons.

## New archive-level stance

For safety-critical coverage work, the archive should now prefer this layering:

1. **P-0433** for decision authority, construct support, campaign scope, independence evidence, caveat basis, comparison basis, qualification basis, and lineage;
2. runner/workflow tools such as `cargo-llvm-cov` for practical execution substrate;
3. rustc/LLVM coverage for instrumentation, profile formats, and report generation substrate;
4. broader verification-campaign or assurance-case lanes only after the MC/DC support bundle stays honest on its own.

That keeps the portfolio from minting another crate that merely says “coverage improved” without clarifying whether the observed campaigns were even in the same scope or qualification class.

## What should count as a worthy crate contribution after this pass

A worthy MC/DC-facing contribution should now be able to answer all of these cleanly:

1. **What decision inventory was authoritative?**
2. **Which construct classes were supported, unsupported, or excluded?**
3. **What exact campaign scope produced the evidence?**
4. **Which conditions have witnessed independence pairs?**
5. **What caveat basis constrained the result?**
6. **Are two bundles honestly comparable?**
7. **What qualification story is actually being told?**
8. **What concrete run lineage backs the verdict?**

If a candidate crate cannot answer those questions, the archive should usually treat it as coverage folklore rather than a serious support contract.
