# Entry 316 — cargo-global-cache-policy-gc-kit deepened for recovery obligations, cache-surface separation, and offline-cost honesty

This pass deepened **P-0480 Cargo Global Cache Policy & GC Receipt Kit** rather than opening another adjacent disk-cleaner, target-dir janitor, or remote-cache service lane.

## Why this lane earned another serious pass

The archive already had sharper answers for:

- Cargo-home inventory,
- dry-run cleanup planning,
- exemption ledgers,
- cleanup receipts,
- and mixed-toolchain caveats.

What still needed a more disciplined answer was the recovery question:

> “If we evict this entry, how does it come back, who pays the cost, and is this even the same cache surface?”

Current Cargo and Rust project signals now make that question concrete enough to build around.

## What moved this time

The key planning move was to stop treating “space can be recovered” as sufficient storage truth.

This pass elevated three review objects more explicitly:

1. **recovery-obligation truth** — whether an evicted entry is locally recreatable, must be re-downloaded, comes from an external cache/plugin route, or remains manual-review-only;
2. **cache-surface separation** — whether a plan is talking about Cargo-home global caches, final artifacts in `target/`, internal/intermediate build-dir state, or future user-wide build-cache surfaces;
3. **offline-cost honesty** — whether an otherwise valid cleanup plan becomes riskier or invalid under offline-heavy, air-gapped, or slow-link expectations.

## Main judgment after the pass

A worthy Cargo cache-governance contribution should not stop at inventory, exemptions, or “what would be deleted.”
It should also provide:

- one explicit **recovery-obligation report**,
- one honest **cache-surface receipt**,
- and one conservative **offline survivability** story when cleanup would shift cost onto redownloads, remote plugins, or later CI jobs.
