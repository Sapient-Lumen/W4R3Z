# Entry 114 — support contracts and verification campaigns

This pass kept the archive on the newer rule: when Rust already has real substrate, the sharper missing crate is often the **boring artifact that joins it into an ordinary maintainer workflow**.

New proposals:

- P-0484 Toolchain & Target Support Contract Kit
- P-0485 Verification Campaign Workbench Kit

Why these rose:

- **P-0484** rose because rustup already documents override precedence, toolchain files, profiles, components, and extra targets, while docs.rs already exposes build metadata and target configuration. The sharper gap is now a **support contract / environment receipt / drift bundle**, not another installer or CI wrapper.
- **P-0485** rose because Rust now has a meaningful spread of verification substrate — Miri, Kani, Creusot, Prusti, Flux, and BorrowSanitizer-era work — but teams still lack a compact **obligation / trust / evidence / drift artifact** that stays honest across tool semantics.

What changed in archive judgment:

- The archive now treats **toolchain and target support** as a first-class review surface distinct from both MSRV work and offline mirroring. A worthy crate contribution here may be a **support-state vocabulary**, **bootstrap hint generator**, or **drift receipt** rather than another installer.
- The archive now treats **verification campaigns** as distinct from any one proof engine. A worthy crate contribution here may be a **trust ledger**, **obligation index**, or **campaign diff bundle** rather than another solver frontend.
- The repo keeps converging on a sharper heuristic: if several official or credible tools already exist, the next missing crate may be the **artifact that lets teams explain, compare, and govern their use** of those tools.

Archive hygiene added in this pass:

- Added an amnesia resistor warning future revisions not to mistake `rust-toolchain.toml` and docs.rs metadata for a boring support workflow.
- Added an amnesia resistor warning future revisions not to mistake multiple verifiers for a boring verification campaign workflow.
