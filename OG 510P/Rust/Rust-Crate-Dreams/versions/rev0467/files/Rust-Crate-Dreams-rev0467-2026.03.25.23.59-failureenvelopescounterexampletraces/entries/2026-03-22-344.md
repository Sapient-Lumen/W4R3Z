# Entry 344 — unsafe contract auditing deepened around contract authority, interpreter boundaries, and witness fidelity for P-0120

This pass deliberately deepened **P-0120 Unsafe Contract Auditor Kit** instead of opening another verifier, another lint pack, or another generic “unsafe dashboard.”

## Why this lane earned another serious pass

Fresh official signals line up unusually well:

- the 2026 flagship goals explicitly include **normative unsafe documentation** and other safety-critical building blocks;
- the 2025h1 std-contracts goal says the standard library already has many safety conditions documented and that the direction is effectively **contract as code** via experimental attributes and tooling;
- the January 2026 safety-critical write-up says teams need reusable patterns and reviewable building blocks rather than isolated heroics;
- Rust 2024 makes some foreign-boundary obligations more explicit with `unsafe extern`;
- and the Reference still makes the hard rule unchanged: `unsafe` never licenses undefined behavior.

Together these make one conclusion stronger: a worthy ecosystem contribution here is not just “run Miri in CI.” It is a crate that helps other people understand **what the unsafe obligations are, where they came from, what evidence touches them, and where that evidence stops**.

## Main judgment after the pass

The strongest near-term unsafe contribution is **not** another proof engine.
It is a **reviewable unsafe obligation layer** above today’s witnesses and documentation substrate.

This pass promotes five P-0120 review objects into first-class status:

1. **contract authority** — what source defined the obligation: docs, imported upstream contracts, experimental attributes, or manual declaration;
2. **obligation map** — which unsafe sites and APIs exist, what classes of obligation they carry, and what evidence is linked;
3. **interpreter boundary** — what a dynamic witness could and could not observe because of FFI, platform, process, network, callback, or target limits;
4. **witness fidelity** — what a passing or failing witness actually means and what it does *not* justify;
5. **unsafe audit bundle** — the smallest portable package that another reviewer can actually consume.

## Why these objects matter

Current substrate is finally specific enough that the archive should stop flattening it:

- Miri is real, useful, and increasingly central, but it explicitly does not catch every violation and has intentionally constrained execution environments;
- the std-contracts goal makes it plausible that more unsafe obligations will eventually exist as machine-readable authority rather than only prose;
- `unsafe extern` and unsafe attributes show that not every unsafe obligation is just “memory model under interpretation”; some are symbol, ABI, or foreign-behavior obligations;
- and safety-critical Rust work is asking for reusable patterns, not vibes.

A crate can therefore truthfully say “we run Miri” while still hiding:

- whether there is any authoritative unsafe obligation inventory,
- whether FFI or symbol obligations were out of scope,
- whether Loom-style schedule exploration was being mistaken for aliasing or initialization evidence,
- and whether a report is strong, advisory, or manual-review-only.

## New archive-level stance

For unsafe-heavy work, the archive should now prefer this layering:

1. **P-0120** for unsafe obligation and witness-honesty truth,
2. **P-0485** for broader verification campaigns that combine many evidence lanes,
3. **P-0121** when the hard part is actually cross-language boundary contracts,
4. sanitizer or debugger lanes only when the primary need is operational diagnostics rather than unsafe obligation review.

That keeps the portfolio from minting another crate that merely says “unsafe checks included” without clarifying the source of authority or the boundary of the witness.

## What should count as a worthy crate contribution after this pass

A worthy unsafe-audit contribution should now be able to answer all of these cleanly:

1. **What unsafe obligations exist, and where did each one come from?**
2. **Which obligations are covered by dynamic witnesses, and which remain manual review only?**
3. **What did the witness actually observe, and what did it explicitly not observe?**
4. **Are foreign, symbol, ABI, or callback obligations being kept separate from memory-model claims?**
5. **Can another engineer compare posture across revisions without re-deriving the entire unsafe story by hand?**

If a candidate crate cannot answer those questions, the archive should usually treat it as incomplete unsafe folklore rather than a serious ecosystem contribution.
