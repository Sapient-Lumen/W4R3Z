# Entry 439 — widen the sector map, make pathfinder more buildable, and raise the receiver-value bar for what counts as worthy

This pass again chose **deepen over append**.

The archive already has more proposals than one team could plausibly build.
What it still needed was a more disciplined answer to three questions:

1. how to keep the repo open to an **extreme variety of real use cases** without letting every sector become a new top-ranked lane,
2. what a buildable **P-0509 Crate Ecosystem Pathfinder & Decision-Pack Kit** should actually ship for those audiences,
3. and what a supposedly “epic” crate must provide **other people** before the archive should promote it.

## Main judgment

Three repo moves were worth making now.

### 1. Add a wider sector atlas, but keep the frontier honest

The March 2026 challenges post explicitly says two things at once:
- many people still struggle with **crate choice / trust / best-fit decisions**, and
- in some domains and industries **there still are not crates that do what people need**.

Those are not contradictory.
They mean the archive must be able to think in two directions at once:
- **horizontal** missing crates that help many sectors at once,
- and **sector-specific** missing crates that only deserve promotion when they export a genuinely new evidence seam.

The new sector atlas therefore widens the map across:
- backend and async services,
- GUI and creative-app loops,
- embedded and edge,
- safety-critical and regulated delivery,
- Wasm and plugin ecosystems,
- mixed-language/C++ interop,
- local-first collaborative software,
- robotics / digital twins / industrial automation,
- geospatial / logistics / physical-world data,
- scientific, data, and numerics pipelines,
- reverse-engineering / observability / eBPF-adjacent operations,
- media/transcoding/timeline-heavy systems,
- and air-gapped enterprise delivery.

But the atlas does **not** flatten them into “everything is equally top-priority.”
Its main conclusion is still that the strongest repeated ecosystem needs are the same control-plane crates already near the top of the frontier:
- **P-0509 Pathfinder**,
- **P-0486 Debuggability Support**,
- **P-0538 Concurrency Contract**,
- **P-0472 Docs.rs Build Parity**,
- **P-0535 Dependency Lifecycle Transition**,
- **P-0489 Cargo Build-Dir Consumer Transition**,
- **P-0484 Toolchain & Target Support**,
- and **P-0536 Crate Knowledge Pack**.

### 2. Turn pathfinder from a ranking idea into a scenario-pack product

The archive already knew that pathfinder should emit decision packs and starter-set receipts.
What it still lacked was a more operational answer to:

> what first scenario packs should `cargo pathfinder` ship, and what do those packs give another engineer on day one?

The new scenario catalog answers that directly.
A credible `0.1` should start with a **small, named pack family**, not an open-ended “search the ecosystem” UI.

Recommended first packs:
1. `cli_baseline`
2. `async_backend_stack`
3. `desktop_gui_stack`
4. `wasm_component_plugin_stack`
5. `embedded_no_std_edge_stack`
6. `mixed_language_interop_stack`
7. `local_first_sync_stack`

Each pack should produce a compact review packet such as:
- `decision-brief.md`
- `starter-set.bundle.json`
- `candidate-elimination.receipt.json`
- `decision-timebox.receipt.json`
- `as-of-replay.report.json`
- `revisit-trigger.policy.json`

That is the difference between “a good crate-chooser idea” and “a crate another team can adopt for architecture review.”

### 3. Raise the bar for receiver-facing value

The archive already had an epic-crate contribution bar and delivery cards.
What it still needed was one clearer rule for future promotion:

> A worthy crate contribution must provide a **receiver-value packet** to at least one real downstream audience.

The new receiver-value bar makes that concrete.
A serious crate should usually help at least two of these audiences:
- the **adopter** making a choice,
- the **maintainer/support engineer** answering incidents,
- the **reviewer/approver** checking claims,
- the **operator/integrator** keeping a system healthy,
- the **tool/assistant** importing machine-usable evidence.

And it should do so with portable artifacts, not just nicer prose or a clever wrapper.

If a proposal only gives one of these:
- a ranking page,
- a generic chat shell,
- a prettier builder API,
- a broad promise without artifacts,

it probably still belongs **below** the current frontier.

## Salience consequence

This pass does **not** justify a major rerank.
The top salience board remains:

1. **P-0509 Crate Ecosystem Pathfinder & Decision-Pack Kit**
2. **P-0486 Debuggability Support Contract Kit**
3. **P-0538 Concurrency Contract Kit**
4. **P-0472 Docs.rs Build Parity & Evidence Kit**
5. **P-0535 Dependency Lifecycle Transition Kit**
6. **P-0489 Cargo Build-Dir Consumer Transition Kit**
7. **P-0484 Toolchain & Target Support Contract Kit**
8. **P-0536 Crate Knowledge Pack Kit**
9. **P-0058 Native Deps Kit**
10. **P-0046 Buildscript UX Kit**
11. **P-0537 Compile Iteration Feedback Kit**
12. **P-0125 Cargo SBOM Precursor Workbench Kit**

## Practical queue consequence

But the practical queue should now emphasize **pathfinder packaging** more sharply:

1. **P-0509** — named scenario packs and review-packet outputs
2. **P-0486** — stronger debug-support evidence packets
3. **P-0472** — docs.rs parity doctor and issue bundles
4. **P-0489** — build-dir dual-support doctors
5. **P-0536** — pinned, refreshable knowledge packs for the leading pathfinder scenarios
6. **P-0538** — scenario-lab evidence bundles for async/runtime semantics
7. **P-0535** — lifecycle/off-ramp bundles for selected foundational stacks

Interpretation:
- the archive is now saying “make **P-0509** feel like a product” before adding more speculative sector lanes;
- only after the horizontal queue gets more buildable should the repo consider promoting new sector labs;
- and when it does, the best current watchlist themes are **local-first**, **robotics/digital twins**, **geospatial provenance**, **media timing**, and **air-gapped enterprise build/support contracts**.

## What this pass says a worthy crate should provide other people

A serious crate should usually hand another person at least one of these:
- a **decision brief**,
- a **starter-set bundle**,
- a **capability/support packet**,
- a **doctor report**,
- a **replay / freshness witness**,
- a **manual-review trigger**,
- a **machine-usable knowledge pack**,
- or a **portable scenario bundle**.

That is now the stronger default test for whether a proposal belongs in the archive’s lead group.
