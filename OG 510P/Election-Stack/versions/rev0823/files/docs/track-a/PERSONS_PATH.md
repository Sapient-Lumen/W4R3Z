# Person’s path (being a voter) — Track A framing

**Track:** A (Deployable core)


This is a **human** map (not a persona catalog): the recurring experiences that make election legitimacy fragile,
and the evidence surfaces Track A uses to keep those experiences *correctable*.

## Eight common experiences (and what evidence must exist)

1) **Opacity:** “I don’t know if my vote was counted.”
   - Floor: paper ballot of record + public audit/recount surfaces (`24`, `36`, `237`).
   - Public: results objects + correction discipline (`234–236`) and dispute-ready bundles (`211`).

2) **Waiting:** “The results are contested and my life is on hold.”
   - Floor: pre-committed milestones + next-update commitments (`219`, `237`).
   - Public: effective-state resolution for corrections (`220`) so “current status” is not negotiable.

3) **Rumor overload:** “I’m seeing three different stories at once.”
   - Floor: signed PublicNotice feed + fast verification of authenticity (`194–195`, `200`, `208`).
   - Public: parity snapshots + capture notes for “who saw which bytes” (`201`, `223–224`).

4) **Process failure:** “Officials made a mistake (or I think they did).”
   - Floor: checklists + publication deadlines that make omissions provable (`145`, `187`, `216`).
   - Public: incident comms as evidence, with epistemic tags (`186`, `218–219`).

5) **Eligibility friction:** “I was told I’m not eligible / I’m missing paperwork.”
   - Floor: auditable registration/status proof surfaces (where applicable) + redaction logs for sensitive exhibits (`15`, `225`).

6) **Fear / retaliation:** “I’m afraid to vote because someone will know.”
   - Floor: paper secrecy + strict handling of sensitive materials (`189`) and best-effort privacy guidance (`39`, `49`).

7) **Coercion pressure:** “Someone is trying to control my vote.”
   - Floor: Track A does **not** promise coercion resistance for remote return; treat coercion as a hazard and publish mitigations honestly (`07`, `33`, `167`).

8) **Distrust of tools:** “I don’t trust your app / your server / your vendor.”
   - Floor: offline verification + mirrorability + independent verifiers (`177`, `188`, `193`).
   - Governance: visible monitor/witness accountability + anti-capture posture (`131–136`).

## Representation duty (explicit)

When an individual voter cannot verify, **someone must verify on their behalf**.
In Track A, that role is played by monitors/witnesses/civil society/journalists/campaigns — and their verification must itself be auditable.

The archive treats this as a governance surface, not an informal trust request:
- identity + funding/COI disclosure,
- replayable verifier reports,
- dissent and disagreement surfaces,
- capture-resistance incentives and rotation.
