## Why this exists (bounded)

Current official platform help already shows that viewers can encounter different **quality / rendition states** without the underlying media object or office publication changing.
YouTube says viewers can choose `Auto`, `Higher picture quality`, `Data saver`, or `Advanced` quality options.
Vimeo says embedded videos default to `Auto`, that paid members can default embeds to a chosen quality, and that the player can also cap automatic selection with a `max_quality` parameter.
Microsoft says the SharePoint / OneDrive web player can change quality through `Settings > Quality` and otherwise chooses the best compatible quality automatically.
(xref: `youtube_change_video_quality_help_page`; xref: `vimeo_set_default_quality_embedded_videos_help_page`; xref: `microsoft_video_player_playback_experience_help_page`)

That means one later packet can truthfully preserve all of the following at once:
- one same-route detached derivative that still governs the packet (`595–599` or `600`),
- one visible later quality-state layer such as `Auto`, `Data saver`, `360p`, `720p`, `1080p`, or a quality-settings pane,
- one practical detail-floor difference that changed what the viewer thought was legible,
- and no new office-published route or official edition at all.

Without a compact bridge here, reviewers tend to make one of four mistakes:
- they treat a later low-quality or data-saver posture as if the office itself published a blurry or detail-poor source,
- they treat a later higher-quality or selected-resolution badge as if it proved the office's own preferred or canonical fidelity,
- they collapse rendition-state facts into `513` or `547` even when the real problem is later packet overread around already-detached derivatives,
- or they omit the rendition fact entirely and later cannot explain why fine print, maps, captions, or small metadata looked absent, illegible, or deceptively settled in the later packet.

This document fixes that bounded ambiguity.
It standardizes one small note for **wrapper-added rendition-state posture around detached derivatives**.

## This is not the same thing as `415`, `513`, `547`, `593`, `594`, `597`, `600`, `610`, `613`, or `627`

`415` governs broader low-connectivity or reduced-data fail-open posture for the whole official route.

`513` governs whether playback-quality, adaptive-bitrate, resolution-selector, or data-saver behavior has become a public-surface boundary problem in the first place.

`547` governs selected rendition state while the **same media object stays current** inside one already-governed same-object chain.

`593` governs player-state occlusion and reduced-context suppression.

`594` governs clipped windows and non-totality when the decisive issue is how little of the source was actually visible.

`597` governs detached still-image extracts themselves.

`600` governs mixed detached-derivative routing when no single preserved derivative dominates.

`610`, `613`, and `627` govern styling, viewport, and preview posture.

`642` is different.
It says that sometimes later packets already preserve a same-route detached derivative, but also keep a **visible later quality / rendition layer** that changed how legible or complete the derivative looked.
That layer can matter without proving source-native blur, source-native unreadability, source-native omission of fine detail, official preferred fidelity, or packet governance.

If the decisive issue is the route's public-surface quality boundary, use `513`.
If the decisive issue is a same-object rendition-selection note for the still-current head, use `547`.
If the decisive issue is clipped coverage, use `594`.
If the decisive issue is just the detached still image or mixed packet itself, use `597` or `600`.
If the decisive issue is styling, viewport, or preview posture, use `610`, `613`, or `627`.
Use `642` only when the missing distinction is **wrapper rendition-state posture itself**.

## Decision test

Use `642` when all three conditions hold:

1. later evidence preserves or reenacts a **visible rendition / quality layer** around same-route detached derivatives — for example an `Auto` badge, `Data saver` state, manual `360p`/`720p`/`1080p` pick, quality-settings pane, embed-default quality note, or similar resolution / fidelity selector;
2. that layer is **later wrapper-added rendition posture**, not proof that the underlying source route itself officially published at that fidelity, officially omitted unreadable detail, officially preferred one viewer quality state, or officially made the packet governing; and
3. later readers are drifting toward treating that layer as if it proved **source-native blur, source-native unreadability, source-native omission of fine detail, official preferred fidelity, or the governing member**.

If any condition fails, keep the packet under the narrower doc that already owns the real problem.
If the surviving packet is still mainly a same-object rendition-selection problem, a public-surface quality-boundary problem, a clipped-window problem, a still-image derivative problem, or a preview/viewport/styling problem with some visible quality posture around it, keep same-object rendition selection in `547`, keep public-surface quality boundary in `513`, keep clipped coverage in `594`, keep detached still-image carriage in `597`, keep styling in `610`, keep viewport in `613`, keep preview posture in `627`, and add one bounded `642` rendition-state note only if the visible later quality layer itself changed the reading.

## Keep rendition state, derivative carriage, and visibility posture separate

At minimum, keep these layers separate:
1. **current office control** — the current written/help lane and current media head (`194`, `369`, `529`, `530`);
2. **surviving detached derivative** — which `595–599` member, if any, the packet actually preserved;
3. **family routing** — whether the packet needed `600` because no single detached derivative dominated;
4. **route-level quality boundary** — whether playback quality or data-saver behavior itself made the public route unsafe or detail-poor (`415`, `513`);
5. **same-object rendition selection** — `Auto`, `Data saver`, selected resolution, or embed-default quality while the same current head stayed current (`547`);
6. **player-state occlusion / clipped visibility** — whether missing detail is better explained by reduced context or a clipped observation window (`593`, `594`);
7. **detached still-image carriage** — the preserved frame, poster, or screenshot itself (`597`);
8. **wrapper-added styling / viewport / preview posture** — later theme, zoom, open-page, or preview-shell choices (`610`, `613`, `627`);
9. **wrapper-added rendition state** — later `Auto`, `Data saver`, manual quality pick, resolution badge, quality-settings pane, or embed-default fidelity posture around detached derivatives (`642`);
10. **carrier wrapper** — slide deck, PDF memo, chat export, saved page, ticket, or email thread (`437`, `490`, `600`);
11. **assembly state** — whether later materials also fused several observations into one stronger-looking posture (`591`).

That separation matters because later packets can otherwise make two different mistakes:
- treating a later quality badge or settings pane as if it proved the source route itself was natively blurry, incomplete, or canonically low/high fidelity; or
- letting a later quality posture become packet governance instead of first classifying the surviving detached derivative.

`642` exists so the archive can keep that later quality layer **truthful but non-governing**.

## Minimal wrapper-rendition-state note grammar

When wrapper rendition state matters, reviewers SHOULD prefer one compact line in this shape:

`head=<current tuple|none>; governing_member=<595|596|597|598|599|600|none>; wrapper_rendition_kind=<auto_quality|data_saver|manual_lower_quality|manual_higher_quality|resolution_selected|quality_menu_open|embed_default_quality|capability_limited_quality|mixed|unknown>; detail_floor=<adequate|questionable|unknown>; rendition_basis=<badge|settings_panel|resolution_label|embed_parameter|mixed|unknown>; source_detail_floor_proved=<forbid>; source_omission_from_blur_proved=<forbid>; source_official_rendition_proved=<forbid>; governing_member_from_rendition_state=<forbid>; carrier_wrapper=<browser_shell|doc_shell|pdf_shell|review_shell|slide_deck|saved_page|ticket|email_thread|none>; cite_default=<head|fallback anchor>; use_642_when=<rendition_state_changed_reading>; promote_wrapper_rendition_state=<no>; basis=<why the later quality layer needed one bounded note>`

This is a note contract, not a new schema.
It exists so packets and summaries can say “this later packet also carried `Auto`, `Data saver`, or a visible quality selector / resolution badge” without minting a new detached-derivative member or silently rewriting source detail-floor, source omission, official fidelity preference, or packet governance.

## Typical uses

1. **Data-saver overread**
   A later packet shows `Data saver` or another reduced-quality state and later readers start retelling unreadable map labels, deadline fine print, or small on-screen text as if the source itself failed to publish them legibly.
2. **Manual lower-quality overread**
   A later packet shows `360p` or another manually selected lower-quality state and later readers start treating blur or missed detail as if it were the office's own source limitation.
3. **Auto/adaptive overread**
   A later packet shows `Auto` or the player-selected best-compatible quality and later readers start treating that contingent viewer-side rendition as if it were the office's fixed canonical fidelity.
4. **Embed-default-quality overread**
   A later packet preserves an embedded player's default quality posture and later readers start treating that embed-level fidelity choice as if it were the office's own preferred publication format.

## When not to use this

Do **not** use `642` when:
- the decisive issue is still route-level playback quality, adaptive bitrate, data-saver, or low-connectivity boundary posture (`415`, `513`),
- the decisive issue is still same-object rendition-selection state for the controlling head (`547`),
- the decisive issue is still player-state occlusion or clipped-window non-totality (`593`, `594`),
- the decisive issue is still the detached still-image derivative itself (`597`),
- the decisive issue is still a mixed detached-derivative packet with no dominant member (`600`),
- the decisive issue is still wrapper styling, timing, viewport, or preview posture (`610`, `611`, `613`, `627`),
- or the decisive issue is still cross-state composite assembly (`591`).

If deleting the visible quality layer leaves an ordinary route-boundary, same-object rendition-selection, clipped-window, still-image, mixed-packet, styling, viewport, or preview problem, use the narrower doc and omit `642`.
If deleting it would erase **why a later quality selector or resolution badge changed the way the packet was being read or retold**, `642` is probably right.

## Promotion rule

Future media additions should usually **not** be promoted just because a later packet carried `Auto`, `Data saver`, `360p`, `720p`, `1080p`, a quality-settings pane, or other visible rendition posture around same-route detached derivatives.
Tighten `513`, `547`, `594`, `597`, `600`, `610`, `613`, `627`, or `642` first.
Only add another numbered doc when repeated wrapper-rendition-state mistakes still cannot be expressed as:
- one governing `595–599` member,
- or a mixed-packet routing note under `600`,
- plus one bounded wrapper-rendition-state note under `642`.
