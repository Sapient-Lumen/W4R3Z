# Official voter-information color and contrast surface checklist

Use this quickcheck when an election office needs a bounded way to keep official voter-information routes readable under low contrast, washed-out displays, grayscale printing, color-deficient vision, glare, or other ordinary conditions where hue alone is unreliable.

## Inventory and review scope

- Identify the critical public-answer routes most likely to encode meaning visually: landing pages, lookup forms, results cards, warning banners, maps/legends, downloadable instructions, and office-help fallbacks.
- Distinguish this from screen-reader review, keyboard-only review, small-viewport review, and document-edition review.
- Re-check routes whose meaning depends heavily on colored chips, status pills, faint buttons, low-contrast helper text, map pins, or required-field borders.

## Text readability and action visibility

- Confirm that the main answer/help lane text remains readable against its background and is not carried by faint gray-on-gray styling.
- Re-check links, buttons, and primary next-action affordances whose visibility depends on subtle contrast differences.
- Avoid making the current official answer depend on washed-out placeholder text, pale captions, or low-emphasis date labels that disappear under ordinary viewing conditions.

## Non-color cues and state differentiation

- Confirm that required, selected, urgent, corrective, successful, unavailable, and outdated states expose a visible cue beyond hue alone.
- Re-check that warnings, deadlines, and status changes still make sense if the route is viewed in grayscale or by a user who does not reliably distinguish the chosen colors.
- Keep legends, icons, tags, and state pills paired with text, patterns, labels, shapes, grouping, or another redundant visual cue when those elements control action.

## Components, graphics, and fallback

- Make sure controls, indicators, map/list states, and graphics required to understand the answer remain visually distinguishable from adjacent colors.
- Do not rely on low-contrast borders, outline-only selected cards, or color-only map markers to show the official answer path.
- Preserve a plainly visible first-party help/contact fallback when the main route becomes visually ambiguous.

## Evidence posture

- Preserve only route labels, reviewed visual-state paths, contrast/non-color review state, checked warning-selection-action cues, and last review time.
- Do not preserve individualized vision profiles, disability guesses, raw user display telemetry, or exhaustive screenshot collections when bounded policy reconstruction is sufficient.
