# Official voter-information site-signage checklist

- Inventory the site-signage lanes the office expects voters to rely on: exterior wayfinding, parking/drop-off, accessible entrance, room identification, curbside help, queue/reroute, and hours/closure notices.
- Treat site signage as a physical delivery layer for current official answers, not as a detached standalone rulebook.
- Make the governing site / entrance / room / route scope legible on temporary action-changing signs.
- Keep clear directional language and arrows on any sign that changes where a voter should go next.
- Mark the accessible entrance clearly, and place signs at inaccessible entrances directing voters to the accessible entrance when applicable.
- Use large print, visible placement, and accessible/alternate contact methods when a sign alone is not enough.
- For curbside or similar assistance signs, include a usable notification path such as a current phone number or approved assistance method.
- Keep site signs aligned with the current directory, hours, live-status, accessibility, hotline/help, alert, and print/partner surfaces covering the same answer.
- Do not let previous-election or previous-room signs remain in place where they can misroute voters.
- Replace, cover, stale-mark, or remove temporary action-changing signs when the condition or election period ends.
- Keep a bounded record of the current site-signage packet or removal policy without defaulting to exhaustive site photography.
- Recheck signage after room moves, entrance changes, accessibility remedies, queue reroutes, curbside changes, incident updates, or office-hours changes.
- Publish `last_verified_at` for the site-signage surface and review it again before each voting period it covers.
