# Official voter-information social-profile checklist

- Inventory every official social profile the public may treat as a voter-information surface, including legacy accounts that still circulate in screenshots or search results.
- Make the routing role explicit: the profile helps people recognize the office and reach current official information, but it does not become the legal rule source by itself.
- Use profile names, handles, and bios that make the office and jurisdiction legible without requiring users to infer identity from a logo alone.
- Keep the bio link on a current official-domain destination or authoritative office/help route whenever possible.
- Avoid opaque third-party link hubs when one stable official link would route voters more clearly.
- Treat pinned posts as controlled surfaces: keep them evergreen or explicitly dated/scoped, and review, replace, stale-mark, or unpin them when they stop controlling.
- Review highlights, featured media, and profile-level collections for stale date scope, stale election references, or detached media that no longer routes to the current official destination.
- Mark legacy profiles clearly and provide successor-account plus official-website recovery when handles or platforms change.
- Keep the official channel directory synchronized with profile changes so third parties can confirm which handles are current.
- Do not place critical routing facts only in inaccessible profile images, featured media, or story frames.
- Add alt text, captions, and accessible content practices for pinned or featured media, and re-test accessibility when the platform changes profile features.
- Preserve a bounded trace of handle/display-name state, bio text version, bio-link destination ref, pinned-item ref, transition state, and timestamps.
- Minimize telemetry: do not turn routine profile visits into individualized surveillance when bounded policy reconstruction is sufficient.
