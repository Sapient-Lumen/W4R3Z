# Official voter-information platform playback-scan-surface checklist

Use this checklist when an election office needs the **current answer/help lane to remain reconstructible even if already-open official media can be sped up, slowed down, scrubbed, skipped, rewound, or otherwise scanned without leaving the player**.

## Scope and boundary review

- [ ] Record which public recordings, livestream replays, watch pages, or event players were reviewed for playback-speed, skip, seek, and DVR behavior.
- [ ] Distinguish this surface from transcript panes, chapter maps, clipped excerpts, immersive-player layout changes, and detached playback.
- [ ] Keep “the player has scan controls” separate from “the office intentionally reviewed fragmentary or accelerated playback as a sufficient stand-alone route.”

## Playback-speed and seek review

- [ ] Review whether the route supports speed changes, hold-to-scan behavior, skip-forward / skip-back controls, percentage jumps, frame stepping, or seek-bar scrubbing.
- [ ] Review whether live-event DVR or catch-up controls let viewers rewind, watch delayed content, and then jump back to live.
- [ ] Review whether the real route exposes these controls differently across desktop, mobile, embedded, app, or live contexts.

## Qualifier-loss review

- [ ] Review whether critical date, scope, exception, correction, or escalation cues can be too easily outrun through ordinary scan behavior.
- [ ] Do not let the mere fact that the full recording remains available imply that a sped-up or fragmentary pass is equivalent to the full reviewed answer route.
- [ ] Treat playback convenience as navigation, not as proof that the scan path is itself the controlling current official answer.

## Live currentness and recovery review

- [ ] Review whether delayed or rewound live viewing can confuse a previously viewed slice of the event with the current controlling state.
- [ ] Keep the current written page, notice, FAQ/help entry, or named office contact practically recoverable after seeking around or returning from DVR delay.
- [ ] Treat volatile or corrected topics as requiring explicit recheck-the-current-route recovery rather than “the video is still open and I watched the part I needed.”

## Availability and variation review

- [ ] Review whether playback-speed or seek controls vary by device, player type, video type, embed settings, or rollout state.
- [ ] Do not promise universal scan-control support if some viewers will see different or missing affordances.
- [ ] Keep “scan controls available” separate from “scan controls reviewed as a sufficient stand-alone route.”

## Authority-boundary review

- [ ] Keep playback-speed, skip, seek, and rewind behavior visibly subordinate to the full recording plus the current written/help lane.
- [ ] Re-review routes where scan behavior composes with transcript panes, chapters, clips, fullscreen, or later detached/remote playback.
- [ ] Preserve a small public digest of reviewed scan contexts, recovery anchors, and last review time rather than individualized watch histories or seek logs.
