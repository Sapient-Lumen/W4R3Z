# 493 — Official voter-information platform transcript panes, searchable transcripts, and clip-jump authority-boundary discipline

**Track:** Shared / Public surfaces

This document defines a bounded control for **official voter-information routes whose already-open recording is also exposed through a player- or platform-level transcript pane, interactive transcript, searchable transcript, or downloadable/copyable transcript sidecar**:
embedded explainers,
livestream replays,
platform-hosted briefings,
audio-first recordings,
and similar official media where the player turns the recording into a second text-and-navigation surface.

It is not mainly about whether captions exist.
It is about what happens when **a transcript panel starts behaving like a portable, searchable, and clickable text edition of the recording**.

It composes with:
- `369`, which governs the office-published video / livestream / clip delivery lane itself,
- `377`, which governs official language selectors, locale fallback, and machine-translation boundaries,
- `492`, which governs browser- or platform-generated live captions, translated subtitles, and copied caption text over the already-open recording,
- `305`, which governs the office/help lane the voter should be able to recover when transcript text is incomplete, stale, mistranscribed, or insufficient,
- and `557`, which governs same-object transcript-pane open/search/highlight state after the transcript surface boundary is already understood.

The narrow rule is:
**if an official voter-information recording may also be consumed through a transcript pane, searchable interactive transcript, or downloadable/copyable transcript sidecar, the office should not let that transcript surface masquerade as a settled reviewed publication unless the office actually reviewed and stands behind that exact text edition, and should keep the current official written destination/help lane recoverable without trusting the transcript alone.**

## Why this is a distinct surface

`369` already says official recordings, clips, captions, and transcripts are a real voter-information delivery lane.
`492` already says generated live captions and translated subtitles over already-open media can become a shadow transcript.

A smaller but distinct seam remains after those controls:
- many players expose a separate transcript pane rather than only ephemeral captions,
- that pane may scroll independently, accept keyword search, and let the user jump into the recording from a text line,
- the pane may be copyable or downloadable,
- and the transcript text can start to feel like the easiest authoritative reading of the recording even when it is only derived from captions or machine transcription.

Current platform and accessibility guidance is specific enough to justify a compact control here.
W3C’s current captions guidance says some media players use caption files to provide **interactive transcripts**, that the transcript highlights phrases as they are spoken, and that users can select text in the transcript and go to that point in the video.
W3C’s current transcript guidance says interactive transcripts are a feature of the media player that uses the captions file, and says transcripts should be easy for users to find from the audio or video file.
YouTube’s current transcript guidance says viewers can open a full transcript for videos that have captions, that the transcript scrolls with the current caption text, that users can click any line to jump to that part of the video, and that some videos support keyword search inside the transcript.
YouTube’s current automatic-caption guidance says captions can be created automatically by speech recognition, that quality may vary, and that creators should always review and edit them.
Microsoft’s current transcript guidance for web-played videos says viewers can browse the transcript and jump to the content they want, search transcript keywords, and that captions/transcripts can be automatically generated, downloaded, or stored with the video and influence search.
(xref: `w3c_wai_captions_page`; xref: `w3c_wai_transcripts_page`; xref: `youtube_view_video_transcripts_help_page`; xref: `youtube_use_automatic_captioning_help_page`; xref: `microsoft_video_transcripts_and_captions_help_page`)

So the bounded question is not “are transcripts useful?”
Of course they are.
The bounded question is smaller:
**when the transcript pane becomes the easiest way to read, search, quote, or jump through an official recording, does the voter still have a clear route back to the official recording, any reviewed official transcript edition, and the current help lane when the transcript text is partial, autogenerated, corrected later, or detached from surrounding context?**

## This is not the same thing as the video lane or caption overlays

`369` asks whether the office’s **published recording lane** has enough edition, correction, transcript, and linkback discipline.

`492` asks whether **generated live captions or translated subtitles during playback** become a shadow transcript or translated publication.

`494` asks whether **chapter markers, key moments, or shareable/copyable chapter lists** turn the already-open recording into a titled jump map that begins to overstate one segment as the answer.

`499` asks whether **player-native AI summaries or answer modules** restate or interpret the already-open recording in generated language that begins to outrun the reviewed text edition or current help lane.

`515` asks whether a **publicly reachable recording still lacks ordinary transcript/caption or other ready-state derivatives because processing is not finished yet**.

`557` asks whether a same-object chain should preserve transcript-pane open/search/highlight state without promoting that pane state into the controlling head once the transcript surface boundary is already understood.

`493` asks a different question:
**once the recording is already open, does a transcript pane or transcript sidecar turn that recording into a searchable, copyable, clip-jump text surface that begins to outrun the office’s actual reviewed text edition and current written answer lane?**

A route may pass `369` and `492` and still fail `493` if:
- the video page is current, but a transcript pane lets users jump directly to a decontextualized line with none of the surrounding qualifier or correction language,
- the platform exposes a transcript derived from automatic captions while the office only reviewed the recording and a short summary,
- a keyword search inside the transcript makes one line look like the definitive rule for the voter’s situation,
- or a copied/downloaded transcript file starts circulating as though it were the office’s settled portable publication.

## Interactive transcript panes are text and navigation surfaces at once

A transcript pane is not just another caption view.
It is usually:
- persistent rather than fleeting,
- scrollable independently of the player,
- searchable,
- clickable for timestamp jumps,
- and easier to quote or forward than the media itself.

That means transcript panes can compress away the most important thing about action-changing election recordings: the line is often safe only with its surrounding context.
The sentence the voter searched for may have depended on:
- speaker identity,
- a date or election scope named earlier in the recording,
- a "for this county" or "for this election only" qualifier,
- an immediately following exception,
- or a closing instruction to use the current official page or office help lane for edge cases.

`493` does **not** require transcript panes to become impossible to misuse.
It requires the office to notice that a transcript pane is a public answer surface in its own right.

## Transcript provenance must stay explicit

The public-safe posture is simple:
**not every transcript pane reflects a reviewed official transcript edition, even when it sits next to the official recording.**

At minimum, keep these layers distinct:
1. the official recording itself,
2. any office-reviewed transcript/caption/subtitle file the office actually publishes,
3. a transcript pane derived from captions or automatic transcription inside the player/platform,
4. and the current written destination/help route the office still wants voters to use when meaning, freshness, or eligibility facts matter.

That distinction matters because current platforms can produce or revise transcript text in different ways.
YouTube says automatic captions are generated by speech recognition and may misrepresent spoken content; creators should review and edit them.
Microsoft says transcript/caption files can be automatically generated, downloaded as WebVTT, edited, replaced, and stored with the video.
(xref: `youtube_use_automatic_captioning_help_page`; xref: `microsoft_video_transcripts_and_captions_help_page`)

If the archive lets those layers collapse into one story, later observers cannot tell whether a voter relied on:
- the recording,
- a reviewed official transcript,
- an autogenerated transcript panel,
- or a later edited transcript file that no longer matches what another voter originally saw.

## Search and clip-jump behavior can overstate one line

Searchable interactive transcripts are useful precisely because they let a user jump to the one part they care about.
That is also where authority drift happens.
A transcript search for “ID,” “deadline,” “drop box,” or “curbside” can make one matching line look like the whole answer even when the recording framed that line narrowly.

So `493` should bias toward a modest discipline:
- keep recording date, jurisdiction, and still-current status visibly attached to the recording page or adjacent page context,
- keep the current written destination/help route nearby,
- and avoid presenting transcript snippets or jump targets as though the line alone settles the voter’s question.

The archive does **not** require offices to remove transcript search.
It requires them to keep transcript search subordinate to the same official routing and current-state cues that govern the recording itself.

## Copying, downloading, and search indexing make the transcript feel more settled than it may be

Transcript panes become especially risky when the user can copy or download text, or when transcript words affect discovery.
Microsoft says transcript/caption files can be downloaded, that the transcript file is stored with the video, and that videos can be found in Microsoft 365 search by words in the transcript file.
W3C’s guidance says many tools can export a plain-text transcript from captions.
(xref: `microsoft_video_transcripts_and_captions_help_page`; xref: `w3c_wai_captions_page`)

That means a transcript surface can start to behave like:
- a searchable document,
- a quotable portable artifact,
- a machine-discoverable policy text,
- or a shareable transcript edition.

So the office should not quietly imply that every transcript pane or exported file is the same thing as a reviewed official publication.
If a reviewed official transcript exists, say so and point to it.
If the transcript is convenience text derived from captions or platform generation, keep that explicit.

## Availability, permissions, and storage vary by platform

Not every voter sees the same transcript controls.
Some platforms only expose transcripts when captions exist.
Some support keyword search.
Some allow download or editing only for owners or editors.
Microsoft says transcript/caption files may inherit the same permissions and retention behavior as the video file.
YouTube says some videos support transcript search rather than all videos.
(xref: `youtube_view_video_transcripts_help_page`; xref: `microsoft_video_transcripts_and_captions_help_page`)

So the safest public posture is:
- transcript panes are optional support layers unless the office explicitly publishes that transcript as the reviewed edition,
- transcript behavior differs by platform and permission state,
- copied or downloaded transcript text is not automatically the current official portable record,
- and volatile or corrected topics should always route back to the current official written destination/help lane.

## Minimal state taxonomy

Keep at least these states separate:
- **reviewed official transcript edition published**
- **interactive transcript pane derived from reviewed caption file**
- **interactive transcript pane derived from autogenerated caption/transcription**
- **transcript search / clip-jump available**
- **copy/download/export available**
- **no transcript pane available**
- **recording or transcript superseded/corrected**

The point is not to log each user’s transcript actions.
The point is to keep the public explanation honest about which text state the office actually stands behind.

## Bounded transcript-pane trace minimum

A small public digest should make it possible to reconstruct:
- which official media routes were reviewed for transcript-pane behavior,
- whether the transcript pane was reviewed as a convenience surface or a reviewed official transcript edition,
- whether transcript search and clip-jump were in scope,
- whether copy/download/export behavior was reviewed,
- where the current official written destination/help lane lived,
- and when the route was last verified.

It should **not** require storing individualized transcript searches, click paths, or user-copied transcript fragments.

## Minimal claim-set

1. **Transcript-surface subordination claim:** transcript panes and transcript sidecars stay subordinate to the official recording, any office-reviewed transcript edition, and the current written destination/help lane.
2. **Generation-provenance claim:** the office distinguishes reviewed transcript editions from autogenerated or platform-derived transcript text.
3. **Clip-jump-context claim:** transcript search and timestamp jumps do not silently replace the surrounding scope, date, or help context needed to interpret a line safely.
4. **Portable-record honesty claim:** copied or downloaded transcript text is not automatically treated as the office’s current portable publication unless the office actually publishes and stands behind that edition.
5. **Searchability honesty claim:** if transcript text affects discovery or internal platform search, the office still treats the current written destination/help lane as controlling for action-changing questions.

## Canonical digest artifacts

Publish **small digests of transcript-surface posture**, not full transcript archives by default.

- **Interactive Transcript Surface Digest (ITSD):** digest of routes reviewed for transcript-pane / searchable-transcript behavior.
- **Transcript Edition Boundary Note (TEBN):** optional note identifying which transcript text the office actually reviewed and stands behind.
- **Transcript Jump Context Note (TJCN):** optional note identifying how transcript search/jump stays subordinate to the current official page/help lane.

## What belongs in the public transcript-surface payload

Keep the payload **small, route-aware, and explicit about transcript provenance and subordination**.

Recommended top-level fields:
- stable `surface_id`
- `jurisdiction_id` / election scope
- `transcript_surface_label`
- `delivery_role_note`
- `covered_surface_refs[]`
- `official_source_anchors[]`
- `reviewed_transcript_contexts[]`
- `official_transcript_edition_note`
- `transcript_generation_boundary_note`
- `search_and_jump_boundary_note`
- `search_index_boundary_note`
- `portable_record_boundary_note`
- `current_help_recovery_note`
- `last_verified_at`
- supersedes / superseded-by pointers

Do **not** publish by default:
- individualized transcript search queries,
- viewer-specific click paths,
- copied transcript fragments from users,
- exhaustive per-platform UI screenshots,
- or full transcript corpora when a compact public posture is sufficient.

## How this fits the family map

Transcript panes and searchable interactive transcripts are **not** a new underlying voter-question family bucket.
They are a delivery/control layer over already-existing public-answer surfaces.

Use `493` when the right official recording is already open, but a player- or platform-level transcript surface can still become a shadow portable text edition or decontextualized jump map unless transcript-pane boundaries are reviewed explicitly.
