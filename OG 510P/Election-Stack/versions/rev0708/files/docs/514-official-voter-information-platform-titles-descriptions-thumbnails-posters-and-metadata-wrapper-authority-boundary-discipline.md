# 514 — Official voter-information platform titles, descriptions, thumbnails, posters, and metadata-wrapper authority-boundary discipline

**Track:** Shared / Public surfaces

This document defines a bounded control for **mutable platform-native metadata wrappers around official voter-information media that can change what the public thinks one recording is about before or around playback**:
video titles,
video descriptions,
thumbnail or poster images,
player-page summary text,
playlist-local display titles,
and similar platform-managed metadata layers that label, frame, or preview the same official media object without changing the underlying recording itself.

It does not try to ban normal metadata editing.
It adds one narrow control:
**when official voter-information media carries mutable titles, descriptions, thumbnails, posters, or other metadata wrappers, those wrappers should stay visibly subordinate to the current written/help lane instead of quietly becoming the controlling currentness claim, scope summary, or action-changing answer.**

## Why this exists (bounded)

The archive already covers the recording lane itself (`369`), shared-link preview cards (`388`), excerpt/snippet discipline (`395`), office-curated collections (`507`), upstream discovery (`510`), portable share/export wrappers (`511`), restriction shells (`512`), and fidelity variation (`513`).
A smaller but distinct seam still remains:
**the same official recording can be relabeled, re-described, or re-thumbnailed by platform-native metadata wrappers even when the underlying media object did not change.**

Current platform guidance is specific enough to justify a compact control here.
YouTube’s current creator help says viewers usually first see a video’s thumbnail and title, and that those fields help them decide whether to watch.
Its current video-settings help says creators can edit a video’s title and description after upload.
Vimeo’s current help says creators can update a video’s title and description from the video settings page.
Its current thumbnail help says creators can manually change the thumbnail, upload a custom thumbnail, or select a frame from the video.
Microsoft’s current Clipchamp and Microsoft 365 support says the player can carry custom thumbnails, titles, and descriptions.
Its current custom-thumbnail help says uploaded videos can get autogenerated thumbnails that owners may replace by uploading an image or selecting a frame from the video.
Its current playlist help says playlist-local video metadata such as the displayed title can be edited and that changes to the source file metadata do not automatically flow into the playlist entry.
(xref: `youtube_thumbnail_title_tips_help_page`; xref: `youtube_edit_video_settings_help_page`; xref: `vimeo_update_video_title_description_help_page`; xref: `vimeo_change_video_thumbnail_help_page`; xref: `microsoft_clipchamp_video_capabilities_help_page`; xref: `microsoft_clipchamp_custom_thumbnail_help_page`; xref: `microsoft_video_playlists_onedrive_sharepoint_help_page`)

So the bounded question is not “may an office edit video metadata on a platform?”
Of course it may.
The bounded question is smaller:
**once titles, descriptions, thumbnails, posters, or playlist-local labels around official election media become mutable, do those metadata wrappers start acting like the current official answer or scope summary even though the reviewed written/help lane still controls action-changing claims?**

## This is not the same thing as the recording itself, discovery ranking, share/export portability, restriction shells, or playback quality

`369` asks whether the **recording or livestream artifact itself** stays scoped, currentness-aware, and subordinate to the current written/help lane once opened.

`388` asks whether an **off-platform shared-link preview card or cached unfurl** becomes the trusted first-contact answer.

`395` asks whether **search/AI snippets and excerpts** quote or summarize volatile official text out of context.

`507` asks whether an office-curated **channel home, playlist, showcase, or collection page** becomes a shadow FAQ/router.

`510` asks whether a **search-result page, homepage row, or browse feed** becomes the practical current-answer router before the voter even lands on the media.

`511` asks whether **copied links, timestamp links, or embed exports** carry official media into new wrappers that then act like stand-alone current-answer objects.

`512` asks whether the media resolves to a **private, unavailable, age-gated, region-blocked, or not-playable-here shell**.

`513` asks whether **the media still plays but auto/adaptive quality, data-saver, or embed-default fidelity changes how much of the answer is practically visible**.

`515` asks whether the route is **public before the ordinary playback or transcript derivatives are fully ready**, even if its metadata already looks polished.

`519` asks whether **dynamic audience metrics around that same media** start acting like proof that the route is broadly confirmed or controlling, even if the title/thumbnail/description themselves are accurate.

`520` asks whether **channel bylines, handles, badges, profile photos, creator URLs, uploader names, or profile-card source cues around that same media** start acting like the whole proof that the route is official and current, even if the title/thumbnail/description themselves are accurate.

`554` asks a narrower chain-layer question: once the wrapper family is already understood, how should later notes preserve same-object title/description/thumbnail/playlist-label drift without letting that wrapper state outrank the current head or head-first citation rule.

`514` asks a different question:
**around the same official media object, do mutable platform-native titles, descriptions, thumbnails, posters, or metadata wrappers start behaving like the controlling currentness claim, scope label, or stand-alone summary of what voters should do?**

A route may pass `369`, `388`, `395`, `507`, `510`, `511`, `512`, and `513` and still fail `514` if:
- an old explainer video gets a newer-looking title or thumbnail that makes it feel like the current election-cycle answer;
- a description field quietly carries action-changing details that were never reviewed like the current written/help lane;
- a playlist entry shows a display title that differs from the source video metadata and the drift is never checked;
- a poster image or selected frame visually suggests a polling place, ballot deadline, or event state that the underlying recording no longer supports;
- or the office reviews the recording body but never reviews the metadata wrappers voters actually see first.

## Metadata wrappers are labels around one media object, not proof that the underlying answer changed

The public-safe posture is simple:
**platform-native metadata wrappers are labels and preview layers around one media object; they are not proof that the office changed the underlying answer, updated the current election state, or superseded the current written/help lane.**

At minimum, keep these layers distinct:
1. the underlying official recording or livestream object;
2. the current written page, notice, FAQ/help entry, or named office contact that still controls action-changing next steps;
3. the platform-native metadata wrappers around the media object (title, description, thumbnail/poster, playlist-local label, and similar);
4. and any surface-specific rendering context that reuses those wrappers (search card, playlist row, share wrapper, restricted shell, or embedded player).

That distinction matters because metadata feels authoritative.
A voter may reasonably treat a confident title, a fresh-looking thumbnail, or a dense description block as the office’s current official answer even before playback begins.
If the archive lets those layers collapse, later observers cannot tell whether the public actually relied on:
- the reviewed written/help lane,
- the recording body itself,
- a mutable title or description field,
- a custom thumbnail/poster,
- or a playlist-local metadata override that drifted away from the source media.

## First-contact metadata can steer belief before playback ever starts

YouTube’s current help says viewers usually first see the thumbnail and title before deciding whether to watch.
Microsoft’s SharePoint thumbnail help says thumbnails are often the first thing people see and that thumbnails also appear in Microsoft 365 search results.
(xref: `youtube_thumbnail_title_tips_help_page`; xref: `microsoft_sharepoint_video_thumbnail_help_page`)

That means the public can form a practical answer belief before opening the official media at all:
- a thumbnail can make an old recording look election-day current;
- a title can compress a nuanced eligibility or deadline rule into an overbroad slogan;
- a description can look like the official step-by-step instruction set even if the office only reviewed the recording body;
- and a playlist row can relabel one familiar recording inside a collection without changing the source file.

For `514`, the bounded rule is not to stop offices from using descriptive labels.
It is to keep the archive honest that the **wrapper seen first** can quietly become the real public answer surface unless it is reviewed with the same boundary discipline as the recording body.

## Source-file metadata and container-local metadata can diverge

Microsoft’s playlist help is especially useful because it makes explicit that playlist-local metadata can be edited and that changing metadata of the source file does not automatically reflect in the playlist entry.
(xref: `microsoft_video_playlists_onedrive_sharepoint_help_page`)

That makes an important bounded point for `514`:
**the same underlying video can carry more than one display label depending on the container that is presenting it.**

So offices should review whether:
- the source video title/description and any playlist-local display title still tell the same truth;
- collection rows, channel shelves, and embedded wrappers inherit or override metadata in ways the office actually reviewed;
- and a corrected or superseded recording has been relabeled consistently across the source page and the places where the platform re-presents it.

## Descriptions can become shadow instructions unless the written/help lane still clearly controls

YouTube, Vimeo, and Microsoft all document editable title/description fields around uploaded videos.
(xref: `youtube_edit_video_settings_help_page`; xref: `vimeo_update_video_title_description_help_page`; xref: `microsoft_clipchamp_video_capabilities_help_page`)

That makes description fields operationally useful, but also easy to overtrust.
For `514`, offices should review whether:
- description text carries action-changing instructions that should instead live in the current written/help lane;
- the description still states date/scope/office context plainly enough not to sound timeless;
- the description links back to the controlling current route when the recording may age;
- and metadata edits are part of the office’s correction workflow rather than an untracked afterthought.

## Thumbnail and poster changes can silently rewrite perceived currentness

Vimeo and Microsoft both document choosing a thumbnail by selecting a frame from the video or uploading a custom image.
Microsoft also says uploaded videos may start with autogenerated thumbnails.
(xref: `vimeo_change_video_thumbnail_help_page`; xref: `microsoft_clipchamp_custom_thumbnail_help_page`; xref: `microsoft_sharepoint_video_thumbnail_help_page`)

So `514` should not treat thumbnails as harmless decoration.
It should make one smaller point:
**a frame choice, poster, or uploaded thumbnail can silently rewrite what the public thinks the video is about, how current it is, and whether it is the right route for a time-sensitive question.**

For detail-heavy or volatile election guidance, offices should review whether:
- the thumbnail/poster actually matches the election cycle, geography, and action being discussed;
- autogenerated thumbnails accidentally highlight stale date text, the wrong facility, or a misleading visual moment;
- updated titles/descriptions and updated thumbnails move together rather than drifting apart;
- and the current written/help lane remains easy to recover even if the thumbnail over-attracts attention.

## Minimum review posture

For public official-media routes that rely materially on platform metadata wrappers:
- review the title, description, and current thumbnail/poster as part of the same bounded release review as the recording body;
- check any collection-, playlist-, or embed-local display labels that the public is likely to encounter first;
- keep action-changing details recoverable from the current written/help lane rather than only from mutable media metadata;
- treat metadata edits as change-controlled public-surface edits when they materially affect scope, freshness, or actionability;
- and preserve bounded evidence of which wrapper states were reviewed without storing individualized watch or click histories.

## Public proof posture

Publish enough to show:
- which media object and wrapper states were reviewed,
- when those metadata wrappers were last checked,
- which current written/help lane remained controlling,
- and how container-local metadata drift (if any) was resolved.

Do not publish:
- individualized watch analytics,
- recommendation or search-personalization traces,
- internal marketing experiments,
- or raw moderation/admin histories beyond what is needed to show bounded public-surface review.
