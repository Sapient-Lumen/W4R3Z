# 494 — Official voter-information platform chapter markers, key moments, and shareable chapter-list authority-boundary discipline

**Track:** Shared / Public surfaces

This document defines a bounded control for **player- or platform-level chapter markers and chapter lists attached to already-open official voter-information media**:
manual chapter titles,
automatic chapters,
"key moments,"
tables of contents,
seek-bar chapter markers,
and shareable chapter links or copied chapter lists.

It is not trying to turn every recording into a formal publication.
It is trying to keep one practical public-risk seam from going soft:
**what happens when a platform compresses a legitimate official recording into titled segments that look more settled, portable, or search/discovery-ready than the office actually reviewed.**

## Why this exists (bounded)

The archive now already covers the official recording lane itself (`369`), generated caption overlays (`492`), and transcript panes/search (`493`).
That still leaves a smaller but distinct layer: **chapterized navigation surfaces**.
Current platforms let owners create chapters manually or automatically, show chapter titles on the seek bar or in a table of contents, let viewers jump directly to those segments, and in some cases let viewers share a single chapter or copy a whole chapter list.
YouTube says creators can add their own chapters or rely on automatic chapters and key moments, that creator-entered chapters override automatic ones, and that chapters may appear in transcripts.
Microsoft says owners can create chapters automatically or manually, viewers can search the chapter list/table of contents and jump to a section, and viewers can share a single chapter or copy the chapter list.
Google Search says some videos are tagged with Key Moments, which work like chapters in a book, and that creators may add them or Google may detect them automatically.
(xref: `youtube_video_chapters_help_page`; xref: `microsoft_clipchamp_video_chapters_help_page`; xref: `google_search_find_videos_key_moments_page`)

So the bounded question is not “are chapters useful?”
Of course they are.
The bounded question is smaller:
**when the voter is already on an official recording, do chapter titles, key moments, or copied/shareable chapter lists begin to behave like a titled answer map that outruns the office’s actual reviewed text edition and current written help lane?**

## This is not the same thing as the recording lane, transcript-pane lane, or play-next lane

`369` asks whether the office’s **published recording lane** has enough recording-date, edition, correction, and linkback discipline.

`493` asks whether a **transcript pane** turns the recording into a searchable, copyable, line-by-line text surface.

`494` asks a different question:
**once the recording is already open, do chapter markers, chapter titles, key moments, or shareable chapter lists turn that recording into a sparse titled jump map whose headings begin to look like settled official answers?**

If the distinct problem is a **portable clipped excerpt, shorter highlight, or share-a-segment link derived from the recording rather than a titled navigation map over it**, use `495`.

If the distinct problem is **autoplay, end screens, cards, or a post-play handoff into another destination rather than a titled navigation map over the current recording**, use `496`.

If the distinct problem is **viewer-controlled playback speed, scrubbing, rewinds, or seek jumps without any titled chapter map being the thing that changed authority posture**, use `505`.

A route may pass `369` and `493` and still fail `494` if:
- the recording is current, but an automatically generated chapter title makes one segment sound universal when the recording framed it narrowly,
- a chapter list flattens qualifiers like jurisdiction, election date, or voter category that were only explained inside the segment,
- a shared chapter link circulates as though the title itself is the office’s current guidance,
- or a discovery surface exposes a “key moment” that looks like the official answer even though the office never reviewed that segment label as a stand-alone publication.

If the chapter/key-moment surface itself is the authority problem, keep that boundary in `494`. If the same current recording already controls and the real missing fact is that automatic chapters or key moments were simply absent at first and only appeared later, keep the chapter-surface question in `494` but record that later-settling derivative fact under `561` rather than treating the later-available chapter layer as a fresher head, a new route, or a reviewed new edition.

## Chapter titles are navigation aids, not automatic policy headings

The public-safe posture is simple:
**chapter titles help users navigate media, but they are not automatically the office’s reviewed current text edition or portable publication.**

At minimum, keep these layers distinct:
1. the official recording itself,
2. any office-reviewed transcript, summary, FAQ, or notice the office actually publishes,
3. chapter markers or key moments attached by the platform/player,
4. and the current written destination/help route the office still wants voters to use when a segment title is too coarse, stale, or decontextualized.

That distinction matters because chapter surfaces are deliberately sparse.
A chapter title is much shorter than the segment it labels.
It may omit the caveat, jurisdiction, time limit, exception, or correction status that makes the underlying recording safe to rely on.
Automatic chapters are sharper still because the platform may have generated the label or boundary as a convenience feature rather than as reviewed official language.

## Discovery and sharing make chapter labels travel farther than the player

Chapter surfaces stop being “just player chrome” once they travel.
They can appear:
- in a player’s chapter panel or seek bar,
- in copied chapter lists,
- in a single-chapter share link,
- or on search/discovery surfaces as key moments.

That changes the risk.
A viewer may encounter a chapter title first and the surrounding recording second — or not at all.
If the chapter title says “Bring ID” or “Return your ballot here,” the title can start to feel like the settled answer even when the recording actually said “for this county,” “for this election only,” or “unless you qualify for this alternative path.”

So `494` should bias toward a modest discipline:
- keep recording date, jurisdiction, and still-current status visibly attached to the recording route,
- keep the current written destination/help route nearby,
- and do not imply that a chapter heading alone settles the voter’s question.

## Automatic chapters and key moments need provenance honesty

The archive does **not** require offices to disable chapter features.
It requires the office to keep provenance honest.

The minimum distinction is:
- **office-authored/reviewed chapter labels**,
- **platform-generated chapter labels or key moments**,
- **chapter surfaces available only on some players/discovery routes**,
- and **no chapter surface available**.

That distinction matters because current platforms explicitly mix manual and automatic chaptering.
YouTube says creators can add chapters manually or use automatic chapters and key moments, and that manual entries override automatic ones.
Google Search says key moments may come from creator-added chapters or may be detected automatically.
Microsoft says owners can generate chapters automatically, then edit them later, and viewers can search and share chapters.
(xref: `youtube_video_chapters_help_page`; xref: `google_search_find_videos_key_moments_page`; xref: `microsoft_clipchamp_video_chapters_help_page`)

If the archive lets those layers collapse into one story, later observers cannot tell whether a voter relied on:
- the recording,
- a reviewed office-written chapter label,
- an auto-generated title,
- a copied chapter list,
- or a discovery surface the office never directly authored.

## Shareable chapter links can create shadow excerpts

`471` already governs truthfulness of copy/share controls in general.
`494` adds the narrower media-specific rule:
**a shareable chapter link or copied chapter list can create a shadow excerpt surface even when no transcript was shared.**

A chapter share is more dangerous than an ordinary deep link when:
- the label reads like a settled rule,
- the title is shorter and more categorical than the segment itself,
- the segment begins mid-explanation,
- or the chapter list is copied without any visible date, jurisdiction, or correction context.

So the office should not quietly imply that a shared chapter or chapter list is equivalent to a reviewed official portable text artifact.
If a reviewed text summary or FAQ entry exists, keep it easy to find.
If not, keep the current help lane obvious.

## Minimal state taxonomy

Keep at least these states separate:
- **reviewed office-authored chapter labels published**
- **platform-generated chapters/key moments available**
- **chapter search or table of contents available**
- **single-chapter sharing or chapter-list copy available**
- **chapter surfaced in external discovery**
- **no chapter surface available**
- **recording or chapter labels superseded/corrected**

The point is not to log each user’s chapter clicks.
The point is to keep the public explanation honest about which chapter surface the office actually stands behind.

## Bounded chapter-surface trace minimum

A small public digest should make it possible to reconstruct:
- which official media routes were reviewed for chapter-marker / key-moment behavior,
- whether chapter labels were office-authored/reviewed or platform-generated,
- whether chapter search, seek-bar markers, or chapter-list sharing were in scope,
- whether external discovery/key-moment surfaces were considered,
- where the current official written destination/help lane lived,
- and when the route was last verified.

It should **not** require storing individualized seek behavior, viewer watch history, or copied chapter fragments.

## Minimal claim-set

1. **Chapter-surface subordination claim:** chapter markers, key moments, and chapter lists stay subordinate to the official recording and the current written destination/help lane.
2. **Generation-provenance claim:** the office distinguishes office-authored/reviewed chapter labels from platform-generated chapter labels or key moments.
3. **Heading-overstatement claim:** chapter titles are treated as navigation aids, not automatically as stand-alone current policy headings.
4. **Portable-excerpt honesty claim:** shared chapter links and copied chapter lists are not automatically treated as reviewed official portable publications.
5. **Discovery-boundary claim:** if chapter/key-moment labels appear in search or discovery, the current written destination/help lane still controls for action-changing questions.

## Canonical digest artifacts

Publish **small digests of chapter-surface posture**, not watch-history exports.

- **Chapter Surface Digest (CSD):** digest of routes reviewed for chapter-marker / key-moment behavior.
- **Chapter Provenance Note (CPN):** optional note identifying which chapter labels the office actually wrote, reviewed, or merely tolerated as platform convenience output.
- **Chapter Discovery Boundary Note (CDBN):** optional note identifying how chapter/key-moment discovery stays subordinate to the current official page/help lane.

## What belongs in the public chapter-surface payload

Keep the payload **small, route-aware, and explicit about chapter-title provenance and subordination**.

Recommended top-level fields:
- stable `surface_id`
- `jurisdiction_id` / election scope
- `chapter_surface_label`
- `delivery_role_note`
- `covered_surface_refs[]`
- `official_source_anchors[]`
- `reviewed_chapter_contexts[]`
- `chapter_label_provenance_note`
- `search_and_share_boundary_note`
- `discovery_boundary_note`
- `portable_excerpt_boundary_note`
- `current_help_recovery_note`
- `latest_notice_uri`
- `last_verified_at`
- `supersedes[]`
- `superseded_by[]`
