# 581 — Official voter-information platform media AI-answer remediation-authority states, owner-admin activation, and transcript-repair dependence discipline

**Track:** Shared

This document gives the recent platform-media chain layer one more compact rule.

It exists because the archive already knows how to separate:
- the underlying media object itself (`369` and `528–529`),
- player-native AI answer modules as public-answer boundaries (`499`),
- derivative-readiness / transcript-prerequisite lag (`561`),
- AI-answer eligibility / rollout gating / captured absence (`570`),
- AI-answer input-sufficiency / transcript-floor fit (`572`),
- and head-first citation / historical-leg scoping inside one same-object chain (`530`).

A smaller ambiguity still remains:
**what should the archive do when the same media object stays current, the AI-answer surface is already understood, but the remaining truth is that the visible AI layer can only be enabled, repaired, or made usable by a particular role — owner/admin toggle control, editor/owner transcript generation or repair, viewer-must-contact-owner dependence, or no published self-service path at all — and that authority path starts to sound like a new route, a new default, or a safer citation target than the controlling head?**

This document answers that narrow question.

## Why this exists (bounded)

Current official platform help already shows that the same media object can stay current while the practical question becomes **who can actually make the AI layer appear or work correctly**.
Vimeo's current Ask AI help says viewers can only use Ask AI where the Vimeo AI button is displayed, and that account owners on select Vimeo Enterprise plans can activate Ask AI on AI-eligible videos.
Vimeo's current AI settings help says team settings control access to Vimeo AI features and that an Ask AI toggle allows viewers of videos to interact with Ask AI.
Microsoft's current Clipchamp player help says Copilot depends on a transcript, that if the video lacks one you must generate it first, and that if you do not have edit permissions you should contact the video owner.
Microsoft's current transcript-management help says video owners can generate transcripts and captions, edit transcript text, replace transcript files, and download or delete them.
YouTube's current conversational-AI help says the feature appears only on select videos for signed-in users who meet age, language, country, and platform conditions, and the current published help does not describe a viewer- or publisher-facing self-enable path.
(xref: `vimeo_ai_ask_questions_about_videos_help_page`; xref: `vimeo_ai_settings_page_help_page`; xref: `microsoft_clipchamp_copilot_video_player_support_page`; xref: `microsoft_video_transcripts_and_captions_help_page`; xref: `youtube_conversational_ai_tool_help_page`)

That means one same-object chain can truthfully contain all of the following at once:
- one current controlling head,
- one AI-answer surface already governed by `499`,
- one visible-or-absent state already governed by `570`,
- one transcript/readiness or fit problem already governed by `561` or `572`,
- and one separate **remediation-authority posture** saying who could actually enable, repair, or request repair of the AI layer.

Without a compact rule here, reviewers tend to make one of five mistakes:
- they flatten owner/admin enablement into generic rollout gating,
- they flatten editor/owner transcript repair into transcript-floor or terminology-fidelity vocabulary,
- they mistake “contact the owner” or “only admins can turn this on” for a new public route,
- they overread one viewer's inability to self-remediate as proof that the object itself changed,
- or they omit remediation authority entirely and later cannot explain why one viewer could not make the same AI lane appear or recover.

This document fixes that bounded ambiguity.
It standardizes one small note for **same-object AI-answer remediation authority + head/default retention**.

## This is not the same thing as `561`, `570`, `572`, `575`, or `576`

`561` governs whether ordinary same-object derivative layers were still settling.

`570` governs whether the AI surface was visible or absent for a viewer/capture because of rollout, account, plan, region, language, client, or other gating.

`572` governs whether the AI layer cleared published minimum transcript-length, duration, or best-fit rules.

`575` governs whether the governing transcript language matched the spoken source.

`576` governs whether names, acronyms, offices, or domain terms were recognized correctly and later repaired.

`581` is different.
It says that sometimes one same-object chain should keep:
- one full-object current head or fallback anchor,
- plus one **AI-answer remediation-authority note** such as `owner_or_admin_can_enable_ai`, `editor_or_owner_can_generate_or_repair_transcript`, `viewer_must_contact_owner_or_admin`, or `no_self_service_enablement_path_disclosed`,
- while recording who could actually enable or repair the same AI layer **without** creating a new head, a new public route, or a safer citation target than the controlling head.

If the decisive issue is that the derivative layer was still settling, use `561`.
If the decisive issue is whether the surface was visible or absent for this viewer, use `570`.
If the decisive issue is whether the input cleared a published minimum floor, use `572`.
If the decisive issue is transcript language alignment, use `575`.
If the decisive issue is transcript term fidelity or repair, use `576`.
Use `581` only when the surface, readiness, and fit are already understood but the archive still needs to classify **who could enable or fix the same AI-answer lane**.

## Default rule: preserve remediation authority, but keep control with the head

Inside one `528` same-object chain, reviewers MAY keep a compact **AI-answer remediation-authority note** when all of the following hold:

1. **The underlying object is still the same.**
   Playback stayed within the same office-controlled event, recording, or published media answer.
2. **The practical difference is who can enable or repair the AI layer, not a new publication.**
   The decisive fact is owner/admin toggle control, editor/owner transcript generation or repair authority, viewer-contact-owner dependence, or the absence of any published self-service path.
3. **Treating that authority path as a new route would mislead.**
   A reader could mistake one enablement or repair lane for the current official head, a reviewed office support lane, or a safer citation target than the controlling head.
4. **A better present-tense default still exists or the chain is honestly headless.**
   `529` and `530` can still name the current head, or `533` can honestly say no public media head exists and point to the best fallback anchor instead.
5. **The remediation-authority fact still matters.**
   The archive would lose useful truth if it omitted who could actually enable, repair, or request repair of the same AI surface.

When those conditions hold, keep the head/default under `529–530`, keep any derivative-settling fact under `561`, keep any visible-or-absent gating fact under `570`, keep any floor/fit fact under `572`, keep any language-alignment fact under `575`, keep any terminology-fidelity fact under `576`, and add one `581` remediation-authority note.
Do **not** silently promote one repair or enablement path into the chain's current head.

## Minimal AI-answer remediation-authority grammar

When a same-object chain has a current head or fallback anchor plus a meaningful remediation-authority posture, reviewers SHOULD prefer one line in this shape:

`chain=<stable object label>; head=<current tuple|none>; remediation_authority_state=<owner_or_admin_can_enable_ai|editor_or_owner_can_generate_or_repair_transcript|viewer_must_contact_owner_or_admin|no_self_service_enablement_path_disclosed|remediation_authority_unclear|unknown>; remediation_authority_basis=<vimeo_ai_settings_help|vimeo_ask_ai_help|clipchamp_transcript_manage_help|clipchamp_get_started_help|youtube_help_no_self_service_path|capture_only|no_published_rule|unknown>; cite_default=<head|fallback anchor>; cite_remediation_authority_when=<claim about who could enable, repair, or request repair of the same AI layer>; promote_remediation_authority=<no>; basis=<why the authority path mattered without becoming the public default>`

This is a compact note contract, not a new schema field.
It exists so packet notes can preserve **who could actually make the same AI layer usable** without making one owner/admin setting, one transcript-management permission, or one contact-the-owner dependency sound like a new public route or a better citation target than the head.

`header_pick_order=<owner_or_admin_can_enable_ai|editor_or_owner_can_generate_or_repair_transcript|viewer_must_contact_owner_or_admin|no_self_service_enablement_path_disclosed|remediation_authority_unclear|unknown>`

`detail_pick_order=<owner_or_admin_can_enable_ai|editor_or_owner_can_generate_or_repair_transcript|viewer_must_contact_owner_or_admin|no_self_service_enablement_path_disclosed|remediation_authority_unclear|unknown>`

For packet headers, that winner order means the carried `581` token should prefer the most reconstructively specific remediation-authority posture: a disclosed owner/admin enablement path outranks a disclosed editor/owner transcript-repair path, which outranks a viewer-must-contact-owner dependency, which outranks a no-self-service-disclosed posture, which outranks unclear or unknown authority. If one extra same-doc `581` residue fact still matters after the header winner is chosen, use the first applicable token from `detail_pick_order=<...>` that is not already carried for `581`.

## When to use an AI-answer remediation-authority note

Typical uses include:

1. **Owner/admin enablement matters**
   The same head still controls, but later reviewers need to preserve that an owner/admin settings toggle controlled whether viewers could use the AI lane.
2. **Editor/owner transcript repair matters**
   The same head still controls, but later reviewers need to preserve that transcript generation, editing, replacement, or repair authority controlled whether the AI lane could work.
3. **Viewer must contact owner/admin**
   The same head still controls, but later reviewers need to preserve that the viewer could not self-remediate and had to rely on an owner/admin or editor.
4. **No self-service path is published**
   The same head still controls, but later comparison requires preserving that the product help disclosed availability conditions without giving a viewer- or publisher-facing self-enable path.

## Citation rule

By default, later notes SHOULD still cite the **current controlling head** under `530`, or the fallback anchor if the chain is headless under `533`.
A `581` remediation-authority note SHOULD be cited only when the later claim is specifically about:
- who could enable Ask AI or a comparable player-native AI lane,
- who could generate, edit, replace, or otherwise repair the governing transcript that the AI lane depended on,
- whether the viewer could only request help from an owner/admin instead of self-remediating,
- or why that enablement/repair path did **not** become a new current route, a live support lane, or a safer citation target than the head.

That means `581` preserves one honest remediation-authority exception to head-first citation without letting one owner-only toggle, one transcript-management permission set, or one support-contact dependency quietly become the archive's present-tense authority object.

## When not to use this

Do **not** use `581` when:
- the decisive issue is whether the AI surface was visible or absent for the viewer — use `570`,
- the decisive issue is that transcript or caption generation was still settling — use `561`,
- the decisive issue is whether the content cleared a published minimum floor or best-fit rule — use `572`,
- the decisive issue is transcript language match or mismatch — use `575`,
- the decisive issue is transcript term-recognition or bounded repair — use `576`,
- or the archive is trying to preserve full support conversations, internal admin workflows, or more remediation detail than bounded reconstruction requires.

If deleting the remediation-authority fact would erase **who could actually enable or fix the same AI lane**, `581` is probably the right companion.
If deleting that fact would erase only viewer gating, transcript readiness, input fit, language alignment, or transcript correction, the problem probably belongs elsewhere.

## Tie-breaker when reviewers ask “if owners/admins can turn it on, why isn't that the head?”

Ask three questions:
- does the remediation-authority note prove **who could enable or repair the AI lane** rather than **what object or route currently controls for the ordinary public now**,
- would a head-first summary become less accurate if it defaulted to one owner/admin setting or transcript-management permission instead of the controlling head,
- and can the needed reconstruction stay truthful without preserving the full support or admin workflow?

If yes, keep current control under `529–530`, preserve any derivative-ready or fit facts under `561` and `572`, preserve any visible-or-absent gating fact under `570`, preserve any transcript-language or term-fidelity fact under `575` and `576`, and record the enablement/repair authority under `581`.
Do **not** let remediation authority absorb chain control.

## Promotion rule

Future media additions should usually **not** be promoted just because the same player-native AI module depended on owner/admin settings, transcript-management permissions, or contact-the-owner repair posture.
Tighten `581` first.
Only add another numbered surface when the ambiguity is really about a new public route, a new authority object, or a new governing surface rather than about **who can enable or repair the same AI-answer lane inside an already-governed same-object media chain**.
