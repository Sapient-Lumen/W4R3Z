# Official voter-information mobile-app checklist

- Decide first whether the app earns its existence; prefer the mobile-friendly official website when that solves the task clearly.
- Inventory every public-facing voter-information app listing the office publishes, endorses, or still links to, including older listings that remain searchable in app stores.
- Make the routing role explicit: the app can help people reach current official information faster, but it does not become the legal rule source by itself.
- Use an app name, subtitle, developer name, icon, and screenshots that make the office and jurisdiction recognizable before download.
- Keep visible official website/help recovery on the store listing and inside the app for users who cannot install, do not trust the permissions, or hit a stale build.
- Keep store descriptions and screenshots current to the released version and current election scope; do not let old screenshots silently frame a past election as current.
- Review developer-name, support-contact, and website fields whenever offices, vendors, or maintenance responsibilities change.
- Keep privacy-policy links, app privacy answers, and Data safety disclosures accurate and current for the released version, including third-party SDK behavior where applicable.
- Treat accessibility claims as controlled surfaces: do not overclaim support, and keep the non-app website/help path usable for people who cannot or do not wish to use the app.
- Mark deprecated or unsupported app listings clearly and provide website or successor-app recovery instead of silent abandonment.
- Preserve a bounded trace of listing refs, app/developer-name state, disclosure refs, scope/version notes, transition state, and timestamps.
- Minimize telemetry: do not treat routine installs or app usage as individualized surveillance when bounded public-surface reconstruction is enough.
