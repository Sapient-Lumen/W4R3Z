# Official voter-information browser page-audio / read-aloud surface checklist

Use this checklist when an election office needs the **current answer/help lane to remain reconstructible even if a browser narrates the already-open official page through read aloud, listen-to-page, reader-view narration, or conversational/AI playback**.

## Scope and boundary review

- [ ] Record which public routes were reviewed for browser-integrated page-audio behavior.
- [ ] Distinguish this surface from screen-reader semantics, visual reader mode, browser-integrated summaries, translation, and OCR/text extraction.
- [ ] Keep “browser narration of the current page” separate from “reviewed official audio publication the office actually stands behind.”

## Serial-audio and authority review

- [ ] Check whether route identity, jurisdiction scope, timing cues, qualifiers, and the next official help step stay reconstructible when consumed as serial audio.
- [ ] Review whether important exceptions or routing notes arrive close enough to the operative sentence that listeners are unlikely to detach them.
- [ ] Avoid designs where page audio sounds more authoritative or more complete than the visible current official page/help lane.

## AI playback and structure review

- [ ] Review ordinary read aloud, reader-view narration, and any conversational or AI playback as distinct states.
- [ ] Check whether headings, table relationships, warnings, or grouped conditions carry meaning that becomes unsafe when flattened into audio.
- [ ] If the answer is not safely portable through narration alone, make the page say so plainly and route the listener back to the visible current official help lane.

## Background playback and recovery review

- [ ] Review whether playback can continue while the user switches tabs, browses elsewhere, or locks the screen.
- [ ] Keep source-page recovery and official help routing legible enough that the listener can get back to the controlling visible page without guesswork.
- [ ] Do not assume page-audio availability is universal across websites, browsers, languages, settings, or enterprise environments.

## Privacy and evidence review

- [ ] Preserve a small public digest of reviewed routes, narration contexts, recovery/help anchors, and last review time.
- [ ] Do not preserve generated audio files, listening histories, or word-by-word playback transcripts merely to prove the posture was reviewed.
- [ ] Keep any future idea of standardized browser narration provenance or perfect audio diff auditing in quarantine until trustworthy hooks actually exist.
