# Official voter-information platform remote-playback surface checklist

Use this checklist when an election office needs the **current answer/help lane to remain reconstructible even if already-open official media can move onto a TV, projector, or other second-screen playback target through casting, AirPlay, Chromecast, Miracast, or screen-mirroring style routes**.

## Scope and boundary review

- [ ] Record which public recordings, replays, clips, or embeds were reviewed for remote-playback and second-screen behavior.
- [ ] Distinguish this surface from same-device detached playback, from saved-media shelves, from autoplay / play-next adjacency, and from intentional outbound destination handoffs.
- [ ] Keep “the platform or OS let the current media move to another screen” separate from “the office intentionally published a standalone TV-safe official edition.”

## Remote-playback mode review

- [ ] Review whether the official recording can be cast or remoted through Chromecast, AirPlay, Miracast, screen mirroring, browser remote playback, or similar second-screen controls.
- [ ] Review whether the sending device remains the effective controller and whether the remote display exposes a queue, receiver UI, or neighboring-media state that can change what the voter sees next.
- [ ] Review whether casting or second-screen transfer can be disabled per recording, player, or embed and avoid promising universal availability if the platform or owner can turn it off.

## Split-context review

- [ ] Review which date, scope, correction, and help-route cues stay only on the sending device once playback moves to the remote screen.
- [ ] Do not let the mere fact that the video now appears on a TV or projector imply unchanged authority, freshness, or jurisdictional scope.
- [ ] Treat suggested/automatic second-screen handoff and screen-mirroring convenience as playback behavior, not as a sufficient current official answer lane.

## Recovery and current-state review

- [ ] Keep the current written page, notice, FAQ/help entry, or named office contact recoverable from the sending device or official channel after remote playback starts.
- [ ] Treat volatile or corrected topics as requiring explicit recheck-the-current-route recovery rather than “the cast session is still running.”
- [ ] Re-review routes where queue state, autoplay, or local-network device behavior can change what is shown on the remote display.

## Authority-boundary review

- [ ] Keep remote playback visibly subordinate to the full recording and the named help lane.
- [ ] Avoid treating a second-screen receiver, TV queue, or mirrored display as proof that the remote screen is the controlling official page.
- [ ] Preserve a small public digest of reviewed remote-playback modes, recovery anchors, and last review time rather than individualized cast histories, household device rosters, or network session telemetry.
