# Ethical handling

## Default stance

This archive studies failure without turning failure into spectacle. Its moral center is learning with dignity.

## People are not root causes by default

Many failures involve individual actions, but the archive should look for system design, incentives, interfaces, institutions, training, regulation, culture, and information flow. Individual naming should be accurate, sourced, necessary, and proportionate.

## Hindsight bias

Every serious incident record should include an “at-the-time view” field. A bad outcome does not prove that the decision looked obviously bad from inside the original context.

## Sensitive records

High-sensitivity records include deaths, medical harms, stigmatized groups, living researchers’ unpublished failures, private correspondence, legal exposure, national security, and survivor/family accounts.

## Author wishes and unpublished failures

The file drawer is valuable precisely because it contains work people often did not publish. That creates ethical risk. The archive should not expose private failures merely because they are interesting. For unpublished or private material, prefer aggregate patterns, consented accounts, or public-source-only treatment unless a strong public-interest case exists.

## Victims and survivors

Do not use trauma as decoration. If personal accounts are included, clarify why they are load-bearing and handle them with restraint.
