# Hazard basis and operating-experience method note

Rev0023 adds a method distinction that should be reused outside nuclear safety.

A hazard basis is the event envelope a system is designed, licensed, practiced, or mentally prepared to handle. A severe event often teaches less as a generic “failure” than as a mismatch between assumed envelope and realized stressor. The right question is not merely “what broke?” but: what stressor was considered credible, what combination was excluded, what common-mode exposure existed, and what information would have made the boundary visible before the event?

Operating experience is separate. A prior event, near-miss, or report is only memory until it becomes equipment, training, inspection, exercises, regulation, emergency plans, or changed authority. This cube should distinguish:

- event memory;
- event dissemination;
- event interpretation;
- recommendation;
- implementation;
- verification;
- recurrence or non-recurrence.

Emergency notification is a third surface. It moves urgent information during an event. It is not the same as retrospective causal learning.

Rev0023 therefore keeps three rails separate: design-basis/hazard envelope, operating-experience memory, and emergency notification/public significance.
