# Near-miss and confidential-reporting method surface

Rev0007 adds the first **source-infrastructure** records. The aim is to keep the engineering-failure lane from overfitting to famous catastrophes. Disasters are richly documented, but the denominator lives in weak signals: near-misses, unease, fatigue concerns, equipment concerns, workarounds, procedural drift, and warnings that never became accidents.

A source-infrastructure record is not an incident record. It records a system that captures or routes knowledge:

1. Who can report?
2. What can they report?
3. Is reporting mandatory, voluntary, confidential, anonymous, de-identified, protected, or public?
4. Who receives the report?
5. What triage or analysis occurs?
6. What outputs exist: alert, database, newsletter, recommendation, report, or action closure?
7. What are the exclusions and limits?
8. What must not be inferred from the system's existence?

The central distinction is **signal capture** versus **outcome evidence**. Rev0007 promotes ASRS, CHIRP, CIRAS, ATSB/REPCON, and NTSB safety recommendations as source infrastructure. It does not claim these systems are complete, equally trusted, or demonstrably effective.

Privacy rule: do not import individual report narratives until a de-identification/re-identification review exists. Publicly accessible does not mean ethically safe to recombine.

Connection to rev0006: `MKH-PAT-0002` asks how warnings lose force. Rev0007 asks what institutions are designed to keep warnings alive before catastrophe.
