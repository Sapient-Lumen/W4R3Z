# Process safety and barrier-health method note

Process-safety records should not be reduced to explosions, fires, or individual operator mistakes. A process-safety record asks what inventory or stored energy existed, what barriers were supposed to prevent release or escalation, how barrier health was measured, which leading and lagging indicators were available, how management-of-change and mechanical integrity were governed, what emergency/offsite-consequence surfaces existed, and what correction rails existed after the event.

The rev0024 rule is: metric surfaces are evidence surfaces, not truth surfaces. A low injury rate is not a process-safety guarantee. A leading indicator is not useful unless it is owned, specific, acted on, and tested against barrier degradation. A recommendation is not a lesson infused until it changes design, procedure, inspection, training, funding, accountability, or emergency practice.

Use CSB reports as primary sources where available, but do not treat CSB existence as sufficient. Pair each incident with recommendation closure and later recurrence checks.
