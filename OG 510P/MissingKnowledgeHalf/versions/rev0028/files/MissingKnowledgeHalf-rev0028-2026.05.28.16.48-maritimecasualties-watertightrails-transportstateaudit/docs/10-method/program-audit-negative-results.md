# Program-audit negative results

Some negative results are not experiments. They are **formal audits of a research program's practical promise**.

A program-audit record should not be reduced to “the field failed.” It must separate:

1. the promise or practical claim under evaluation;
2. the evaluator or committee mandate;
3. the criteria used for usefulness or success;
4. the negative or narrowing finding;
5. the work the evaluator preserved or recommended;
6. the downstream funding/practice/public-understanding consequences;
7. the historiographic uncertainty around those consequences.

Rev0005 admits `MKH-NEG-0013` as the first record of this kind. ALPAC says useful machine translation of general scientific text, under its definition, did not exist and was not in immediate prospect. The same report supports computational linguistics and translation-process improvement. That separation is the point.

`MKH-GEN-0005` is adjacent but not identical: the Lighthill Report is encoded as an anti-genealogy/evaluator scene because it is as much about research-policy lineage, response, and later historiography as about a single negative result.
