# Positive controls and surviving anomalies

The cube must not learn only from collapse. An anomaly-discipline pattern built only from corrected false alarms, foreground corrections, and replication failures teaches the wrong lesson: that extraordinary anomalies are usually errors. The stronger lesson is procedural: extraordinary claims become knowledge, correction, or boundary-narrowed learning through controls, provenance, independent scrutiny, and status history.

A surviving-anomaly positive control is a record where a surprising or extraordinary evidence surface survives substantial discipline and becomes consolidated knowledge. Its job is not to glorify the discovery. Its job is to calibrate the pattern so future sessions ask: what controls turned surprise into knowledge, and what limits still remained?

For rev0022, GW150914 is the first such control. It is paired with GWOSC and a reproducibility audit so that the positive control does not become heroic myth. Open data and public code are powerful, but exact workflow reproduction can still be bounded by private/internal datasets, calibration versions, and workflow provenance.
