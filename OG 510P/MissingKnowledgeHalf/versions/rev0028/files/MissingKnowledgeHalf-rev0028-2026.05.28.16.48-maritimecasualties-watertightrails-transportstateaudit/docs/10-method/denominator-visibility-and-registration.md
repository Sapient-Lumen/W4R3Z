# Denominator visibility and registration method — rev0008

The missing-half corpus needs more than case records. It needs surfaces that reveal whether a study, plan, protocol, result posting, correction, retraction, or critique existed at all. Rev0008 calls these **denominator visibility rails**.

A visibility rail is not a verdict. It is a linkable source object that can constrain later claims.

## Link classes

- `registry_record`: a trial or study has a public registry entry, identifier, status fields, and sometimes results.
- `preregistration_record`: a planned hypothesis/method/analysis exists before outcome observation or analysis.
- `private_or_embargoed_registration`: a plan may exist but is not yet public; this improves adoption but does not reveal the denominator.
- `registered_report_stage1`: the study was peer reviewed and accepted in principle before outcomes were known.
- `results_reporting_surface`: summary results are posted independently of journal publication.
- `formal_update_metadata`: a published item has a correction, expression of concern, retraction, reinstatement, or related notice.
- `post_publication_comment_signal`: a public critique or anomaly signal exists but still requires independent evaluation.

## Non-equivalence rules

A registry record is not completion. A completed study is not reported results. Posted results are not a peer-reviewed publication. A preregistration is not adherence. A Registered Report is not perfect execution. A retraction is not a full causal account. A PubPeer comment is not a verdict.

## Why this matters

The seed says the negative-results/file-drawer corpus must track unpublished, null, abandoned, failed, and selectively reported work. That is impossible unless the cube first models the systems that make study existence and status visible.

## Promotion rule

Rev0008 promotes infrastructure records only. It does not import individual trials, preregistrations, retractions, or comment threads. The next step is one study-lifecycle pilot, not broad scraping.
