# Cross-corpus linking

## Why links are the distinctive contribution

A list of failures is not enough. The corpus becomes valuable when records link claims, sources, replications, practice, patterns, and genealogy.

## Link families

- **Claim qualification** — negative result narrows a positive claim.
- **Replication relation** — replication record compares directly or conceptually to an original study.
- **Practice relation** — literature status connects to clinical, engineering, policy, or textbook practice.
- **Pattern recurrence** — two or more records share a failure mode.
- **Genealogy relation** — ideas, methods, or institutions transmitted the claim or its correction.
- **Anti-genealogy relation** — a person, school, field, or movement reacted against a prior idea.
- **Defeater relation** — a record undercuts another record’s evidential authority.

## Pattern promotion rule

Do not promote a pattern record solely because it is familiar or rhetorically attractive. A recurring-pattern record should cite at least two case records, and ideally three, before becoming more than candidate.

## Practice lag rule

When possible, separate:

- date of original positive claim,
- date of first serious disconfirmation,
- date of consensus shift,
- date of textbook/policy/practice update,
- persistence after update.
