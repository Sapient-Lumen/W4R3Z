# Rev0003 engineering official-reports pilot

Revision: `rev0003`

This pilot begins the corpus with three records whose source base is strong enough to test the archive's mechanics.

## Selected cases

### MKH-ENG-0002 — Mars Climate Orbiter

Why selected: official NASA report; clear interface/specification failure; strong connection to the seed’s software/engineering lane.

What it tests: whether the archive can separate a memorable public summary (“metric vs English units”) from a richer record containing AMD files, software-interface requirements, small-force modeling, communication/operations transition, anomaly detection, and a contingency maneuver.

### MKH-ENG-0007 — Ariane 5 Flight 501

Why selected: official ESA summary plus full Inquiry Board report mirror; excellent case for software reuse, hidden assumptions, common-mode redundancy, and test-envelope mismatch.

What it tests: whether the archive can carry a source-permanence warning when the detailed report is available from an institutional mirror rather than an official-hosted PDF.

### MKH-ENG-0008 — Patriot Dhahran

Why selected: official GAO report; excellent case for numeric precision, operational drift, patch timing, and high-sensitivity harm handling.

What it tests: whether the archive can handle fatalities without spectacle and without collapsing system behavior into individual blame.

## Source discipline learned

- Source records have to be first-class records, not footnotes.
- Official reports support bounded claims; they do not close history.
- A source-backed record still needs second review.
- Source permanence is a separate problem from source authority.

## Pattern discipline learned

The three cases justify preserving a pattern candidate around numeric representation assumptions. They do not justify claims about prevalence, universal software risk, or simple preventability.
