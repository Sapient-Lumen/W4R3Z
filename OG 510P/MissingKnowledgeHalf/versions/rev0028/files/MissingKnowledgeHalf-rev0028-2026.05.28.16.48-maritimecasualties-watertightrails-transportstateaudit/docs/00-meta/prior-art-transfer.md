# Prior-art transfer: what the old cubes teach this cube

This revision inspected four uploaded prior-art cubes and treated them as archive-method examples rather than content sources. The bounded lesson is not “copy their shape exactly,” but “import the smallest durable grammar that will keep a 10k+ revision corpus coherent.”

## DelayBasin lesson: reentry is a first-class artifact

DelayBasin shows that a long-run cube needs more than documents. It needs restart surfaces, receipts, assumption/followthrough ledgers, witness vocabulary, transfer ledgers, validation, and explicit quarantine. Its strongest transferable idea is that archive hygiene is not decoration: it is the mechanism by which a future pass can resume without silently changing scope.

**Adopted here:** `START_HERE.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `context-pack.json`, `REVISION-RECEIPT.json`, `ASSUMPTION-LEDGER.json`, `FOLLOWTHROUGH-QUEUE.json`, `DATACUBE-TRANSFER-LEDGER.json`, `WITNESS-VOCABULARY.json`, and `make lint`.

**Not adopted yet:** its very large specialized witness-token machinery. MissingKnowledgeHalf does not yet have enough real records to justify that complexity.

## Theory-of-Everything lesson: claims need defeat and rollback

The Theory cube demonstrates a useful discipline for speculative/high-stakes archives: claims, routes, assumptions, defeaters, dependencies, evidence severity, and rollback must be explicit. For this project, that matters because many “failed” findings later revive in narrowed form, while some positive results die slowly and persist socially.

**Adopted here:** claim status over time, evidence severity, defeater/rollback vocabulary, and a refusal to treat a source-backed claim as permanently settled.

**Not adopted yet:** any domain-specific scientific route family.

## TriKEM lesson: fail-closed warnings protect users

TriKEM repeatedly distinguishes source scaffolding from compiled/tested/validated evidence. It openly says what has not been run or proven. That stance is essential here because the seed document contains many famous examples that feel familiar enough to invite overclaiming.

**Adopted here:** all seed case rows are `seed_mention`; no factual case record is promoted; future records need evidence gates.

**Not adopted yet:** code-heavy evidence harnesses beyond basic JSON/archive lint.

## Parables lesson: corpus builders need search memory

The Parables cube is structurally different: a text-heavy corpus plus scratchpad and already-searched URLs. Its transferable insight is practical: serious collection work must remember what was searched, considered, rejected, kept, and why. Otherwise the archive repeats searches and hides selection bias.

**Adopted here:** a source-acquisition ledger and a followthrough item to add searched/used/rejected source memory once live acquisition begins.

**Not adopted yet:** a single monolithic text corpus as the primary structure; MissingKnowledgeHalf needs linked records.

## Combined transfer rule

The archive should grow by **small, receipt-backed revisions**. Each revision should name:

- what became canon,
- what remains candidate or quarantined,
- what source authority was gained or lost,
- what future work was opened,
- what was explicitly not claimed.
