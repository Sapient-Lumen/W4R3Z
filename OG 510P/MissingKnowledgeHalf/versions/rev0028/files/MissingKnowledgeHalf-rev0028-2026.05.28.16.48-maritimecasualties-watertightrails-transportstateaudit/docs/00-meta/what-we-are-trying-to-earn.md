# What we are trying to earn

Raw record count is the wrong measure.

The cube could contain 10,000 famous cases and still fail if it only becomes a searchable list of errors. The right measure is earned capability: what can a user, researcher, engineer, historian, policymaker, journalist, educator, or model do because the cube exists?

## Capability 1: case integrity

A case record should let a skeptical reader see what is sourced, what is inferred, what is contested, what is unknown, and what was not promoted. This requires claim-level source support, not just narrative familiarity.

What earns it: second-review passes, source locators, split claims, unknowns, caveats, and permanence checks.

What cheats it: summarizing famous stories from memory.

## Capability 2: denominator visibility

The visible canon overrepresents success. The cube earns denominator visibility when it can show the attempted studies, registered trials, unpublished results, abandoned pilots, incident reports, near-misses, and correction trails around a claim or system.

What earns it: audit pairs, registries, reporting infrastructures, source-infrastructure records, and direct evidence of missingness.

What cheats it: claiming file-drawer bias without a denominator surface.

## Capability 3: status-history memory

Knowledge does not have one timestamp. It has a life. A claim may be proposed, amplified, canonical, challenged, narrowed, reversed, revived, or retained in practice after evidentiary death.

What earns it: status-history arrays, practice-lag child records, guideline timelines, textbook/public-belief surfaces, and rollback triggers.

What cheats it: stamping a claim as true or false without time.

## Capability 4: practice-lag visibility

Publication is not de-adoption. Literature status and practice status must be separate.

What earns it: utilization curves, guideline histories, reimbursement/coverage changes, regulatory updates, product changes, classroom/textbook changes, and public-message changes.

What cheats it: assuming that a trial, report, or meta-analysis changed what people did.

## Capability 5: warning-governance memory

A warning matters only if it can become action. The cube earns this capability when it records who warned, what they knew, who heard, what authority existed, what proof burden applied, and what stopped or enabled action.

What earns it: near-miss reports, meeting records, investigation findings, stop-authority structures, escalation paths, and counterfactuals stated cautiously.

What cheats it: hindsight blame.

## Capability 6: correction-surface inventory

Failure memory depends on institutions. Some fields have public accident boards, registries, confidential reporting systems, systematic reviews, retraction metadata, or inquiry archives. Others do not.

What earns it: infrastructure records with capture model, outputs, limits, blind spots, and evidence of use.

What cheats it: equating infrastructure existence with effectiveness.

## Capability 7: cross-domain pattern discovery

A pattern should make recurrence visible across domains without erasing domain specificity.

What earns it: supporting records, negative controls, counterexamples, scope conditions, and rollback triggers.

What cheats it: making a slogan from three memorable anecdotes.

## Capability 8: genealogy of correction

Ideas, overclaims, methods, doubts, and reforms travel through people and institutions. The cube earns genealogy when it can show not only who influenced whom, but what moved: a claim, method, style, warning, critique, or correction norm.

What earns it: evidence-graded influence edges, scene records, correspondence, acknowledgments, advisor relations, citation networks, syllabi, prefaces, oral histories, and reaction-against edges.

What cheats it: treating all relations as influence.

## Capability 9: failure-literacy pedagogy

The cube should eventually teach how to reason with failure. It should make people less likely to repeat dead ends, overtrust early positives, ignore warnings, or mistake practice for evidence.

What earns it: lessons downstream of reviewed records and counterexamples.

What cheats it: moralizing.

## Capability 10: machine-usable epistemic training

A future model should learn from this cube that knowledge is not merely a pile of facts. It is a record of attempts, uncertainty, disagreement, correction, revival, and scope.

What earns it: stable IDs, source graphs, claim status, status histories, crosslinks, machine-readable ledgers, and explicit caveats.

What cheats it: unstructured prose that sounds wise but cannot be audited.
