# Charter

## North star

Build the linked corpus of the missing half of knowledge: the structured record of what failed, what did not replicate, what was abandoned, what broke in practice, how those lessons were or were not absorbed, and how intellectual lineages transmitted both insight and error.

The archive exists because positive-results literature, canonical histories, and polished textbooks hide the denominator. The goal is not to replace the canon with a failure canon. The goal is to make the denominator visible enough that future research, engineering, policy, teaching, and AI systems can reason with a less survivorship-biased record.

## Four interlocked corpora

1. **Negative results / file drawer** — null findings, underpowered nulls, failed methods, abandoned studies, unpublished pilots, theses, posters, confidential industry negatives, and boundary-condition findings.
2. **Failed replications** — direct, conceptual, meta-analytic, adversarial, forensic, and reanalysis disconfirmations.
3. **Engineering failures and near-misses** — accidents, disasters, incidents, unsafe designs, latent conditions, warnings, regulatory failures, practice lag, and design change.
4. **Genealogy and anti-genealogy of ideas** — advisor lineages, citation/influence networks, scenes, conferences, correspondence, translations, reactions against, and lost lineages.

The unifying claim is methodological: these are not four hobbies. They are four views of how knowledge claims meet the world, fail, adapt, persist, or travel.

## What counts as success

A successful archive lets a future user ask:

- What has already been tried and failed?
- Was it a true null, an underpowered null, a method failure, a boundary condition, or a later-superseded result?
- Which positive claim did this qualify or defeat?
- Did the literature update? Did practice update?
- Did the same failure pattern recur in another field?
- Which intellectual lineages amplified, resisted, or corrected the error?
- What source carries the claim, and how permanent is that source?

## Refusals

- No wall of shame.
- No tragedy content mill.
- No seed-case promotion without sources.
- No giant quote hoarding when a citation and summary suffice.
- No flattening contested claims into a single verdict.
- No pretending that “failure” has one meaning across medicine, engineering, mathematics, psychology, software, and intellectual history.
- No hiding practice lag: a claim can die in literature and persist in clinics, classrooms, code, policy, or product design.

## Tone

Careful, patient, source-grounded, structurally literate, ethically restrained, and willing to preserve uncertainty.
