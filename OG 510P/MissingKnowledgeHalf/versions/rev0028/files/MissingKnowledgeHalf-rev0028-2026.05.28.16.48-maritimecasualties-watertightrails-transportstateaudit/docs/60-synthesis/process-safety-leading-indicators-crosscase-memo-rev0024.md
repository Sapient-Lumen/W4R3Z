# Process-safety synthesis memo — leading indicators and barrier health

The cross-case lesson is not “chemicals are dangerous.” The useful question is: where does a low-frequency catastrophic hazard hide during ordinary operation?

West hides it in inventory, community proximity, emergency planning, and fragmented regulatory coverage. Imperial Sugar hides it in dust accumulation and a routine equipment enclosure/housekeeping world that was not treated as a major-accident system. Chevron Richmond hides it in material heterogeneity, inspection databases, turnaround screens, and recommendation-tracking gaps. Macondo hides it in offshore safety-critical-element management, BOP design/testing limits, and complex operator/contractor/regulator interfaces. BP Texas City already in the cube hides it in personal-safety metric success and process-safety weakness.

`MKH-PAT-0019` is therefore a candidate pattern about barrier-health visibility and correction infusion. It should be red-teamed immediately: find a near miss where leading indicators worked, find a case where process-safety rails were present but irrelevant, and trace whether recommendations actually changed later practice.
