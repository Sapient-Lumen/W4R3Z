# Cross-case memo: hard endpoint reversal and evidentiary route discipline

Revision: `rev0004`

Rev0004 introduces `MKH-PAT-0005`, a provisional pattern candidate supported by WHI, CAST, SELECT, and CARET.

## The pattern candidate

A claim may be supported by a narrower route: a biomarker, a surrogate endpoint, an observational association, a secondary finding, or a mechanistic story. Those routes are useful, but they are not identical to the full clinical benefit/harm portfolio. In the four promoted medical records, the hard-endpoint trial changed the status of the claim.

## Case sketches

- WHI: observational/intermediate cardioprotection expectations did not yield a viable chronic-disease prevention risk-benefit profile for the studied estrogen-plus-progestin regimen.
- CAST: ventricular arrhythmia suppression did not improve survival and the active drug arms had excess mortality/cardiac arrest.
- SELECT: selenium/vitamin E did not prevent prostate cancer; vitamin E alone later showed a statistically significant increased prostate-cancer risk.
- CARET: beta-carotene/retinol did not prevent lung cancer in high-risk participants and was halted for no benefit and harm.

## Archival rule, not moral

The lesson is not “proxies are bad.” The lesson is that the cube must separately encode:

- the claim or hypothesis;
- the prior evidentiary route;
- the endpoint actually tested;
- benefits preserved;
- harms preserved;
- population/dose/route/scope limits;
- what would change status.

That separation lets the archive avoid both triumphalist medicine and cheap debunking.

## Why it remains only a candidate

Four cases are enough to promote a pattern candidate, not enough to estimate frequency. The pattern needs negative controls: cases where a surrogate or observational route correctly predicted a hard-endpoint benefit. It also likely needs subfamilies: surrogate endpoint failure, observational confounding, nutrient-dose reversal, and global risk-benefit portfolio reversal.
