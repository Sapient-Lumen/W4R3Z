# Proof-burden inversion cross-case memo — rev0006

This memo is provisional. It argues only that four records justify a pattern candidate.

## The shape

A hazardous signal appears. It is not converted into binding action. It is instead contained, normalized, classified as non-safety-critical, treated as requiring more proof, or weakened by schedule, production, certification, or metric incentives.

## Case signals

`MKH-ENG-0003` Challenger: O-ring erosion, low-temperature concern, and contractor engineering opposition existed, but key decision-makers did not receive the warning with enough authority, and the safety program did not provide an independent landing zone.

`MKH-ENG-0009` Columbia: foam strike and debris-analysis uncertainty existed, but CAIB explicitly found that engineers were required to prove a safety-of-flight issue rather than the program proving safety.

`MKH-ENG-0005` Boeing 737 MAX: safety assessment credited assumptions about immediate pilot response to uncommanded MCAS-related trim without adequately evaluating multiple alerts and indications produced by the same failure condition.

`MKH-ENG-0014` BP Texas City: previous incidents, audits, maintenance/investment concerns, and process-safety signals existed, but personal-safety metrics and cost/production pressures obscured catastrophic process risk.

## What not to conclude

Do not conclude that every disaster is caused by production pressure. Do not conclude that managers knew catastrophe would happen. Do not conclude that every pre-event concern was actionable. Do not upgrade the pattern without stopped-work cases, false alarms, and near-miss reporting records.

## What to build next

The next good move is a positive-control record: a case where warnings successfully stopped a launch, operation, release, or deployment. The second good move is a near-miss infrastructure record, because near misses provide less survivorship-biased data about warning governance.
