# Cross-case memo: impoundment hazard legibility

Teton, Mount Polley, Brumadinho, and Fundão all force the cube to ask how much of the critical state was visible before breach. But the answer differs by case.

Teton emphasizes internal erosion and foundation/core interface conditions during first filling. Mount Polley emphasizes an undetected foundation stratum and undrained response under embankment load. Brumadinho emphasizes upstream construction, drainage/water/saturation, creep, suction loss, brittle undrained behavior, monitoring non-legibility, and consequence exposure. Fundão emphasizes material interaction between sand tailings and slimes plus unplanned construction/operation occurrences.

The shared pattern is not “dams fail because hidden things are hidden.” That would be too broad. The sharper hypothesis is that stored-material impoundment risk is only actionable when the evidence surfaces can connect material state, hydraulic state, construction history, monitoring sensitivity, consequence exposure, and governance authority before failure.

The revision also adds correction rails: ASDSO/DamFailures.org for case memory, GISTM for lifecycle governance/auditable requirements, and the Global Tailings Portal for disclosure-denominator visibility. These rails are useful, but none equals conformance, verification, or safety.
