# Signal commons cross-case memo — rev0007

A “signal commons” is a safety institution that turns weak, private, local, identity-sensitive, or reputationally risky information into shared knowledge.

The first five infrastructure records show recurring design primitives:

- reporters need a way to speak without becoming targets;
- raw reports often need de-identification before public or organizational use;
- an independent or third-party receiver can increase trust;
- expert triage matters because not every concern is equally actionable;
- outputs must exist beyond storage: alerts, newsletters, recommendations, public databases, action tracking, or organizational response;
- scope limits and exclusions must be visible, because confidential reporting is not a legal shield for everything.

This complements the proof-burden pattern from rev0006. Catastrophe records show warning failure after the fact. Signal-commons records show the institutional machinery built to preserve warnings before the fact.

Non-claim: rev0007 does not say these systems work equally well. A reporting system can exist and still fail through mistrust, underreporting, weak triage, no authority, poor feedback, legal fear, or lack of follow-through. The next evidentiary step is not more infrastructure descriptions; it is report-to-action records and negative controls.
