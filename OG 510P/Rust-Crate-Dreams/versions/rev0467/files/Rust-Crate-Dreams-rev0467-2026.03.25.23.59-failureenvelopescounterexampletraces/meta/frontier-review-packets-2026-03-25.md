## Why this lens matters now

The archive already has evidence bundles, conformance tiers, interchangeable profiles, and policy verdicts.
What was still missing was a compact answer to:

**what should a maintainer, release manager, security reviewer, or assessor actually read and approve?**

A worthy crate under this lens is stronger when it can produce:
- a compact review packet rather than a pile of raw JSON,
- an append-only signoff ledger rather than silent status mutation,
- a visible override register rather than an invisible escape hatch,
- and a named reapproval plan rather than a vague promise to “rerun later”.

## Practical interpretation
- a lane is stronger when it can turn imported evidence and policy into a **reviewable packet**;
- a lane is stronger when it can reduce reviewer burden without hiding unknowns, refusals, or exceptions;
- a lane is stronger when it makes maintenance and reassessment visible, not just the first pass;
- future reranks should ask not only “what evidence exists?” and “what verdict exists?” but also “what exact packet another human can approve, archive, revisit, and supersede?”.
