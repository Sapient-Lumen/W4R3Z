# Entry 337 — Assurance Case Workbench Kit deepened for import policy, assumption ledgers, review gates, and export projection truth

This pass returned to **P-0503 Assurance Case Workbench Kit** instead of adding another verifier, another evidence bundle, or another standards wrapper.

## Why this lane earned a serious pass now

The archive already had the right instinct for **P-0503** and a useful early fixture pack.
What it still lacked was the same product-shape discipline recent frontiers received: a `0.1` build sketch, a lane-boundary memo, and a clearer split between **import policy**, **assumptions**, **review gates**, and **export projection**.

Current substrate makes that split unusually concrete:

- Rust’s 2026 flagship themes now explicitly include **Safety-Critical Rust** with certified tooling, specifications, and evidence;
- the January 2026 safety-critical Rust post is explicit that ecosystem support thins as teams move toward higher criticality;
- the Rust Foundation’s Safety-Critical Rust Consortium is explicitly interested in guidelines, linters, libraries, static analysis tools, and formal methods;
- SACM remains an active OMG structured-assurance standard, while the broader GSN ecosystem still shows how common standards-shaped argument views are;
- WebGSN, AssurancePlatform, and CertWare prove that assurance-case tooling exists, but they do not by themselves give Rust teams one boring import/status/diff contract above Rust-native evidence bundles.

That means a worthy crate here should not merely say “exports GSN” or “can import evidence.”
It should tell another reviewer **which argument basis is in use, what evidence is admissible, what unresolved assumptions remain, why the pack is currently green/yellow/red/blocked, and what the export preserves or loses**.

## What moved this time

This pass elevated five review objects into first-class status for **P-0503**:

1. **claim-library basis** — which reusable argument/profile basis is actually in force;
2. **import policy** — which evidence classes are admissible and what freshness/trust minima apply;
3. **assumption ledger** — which unresolved assumptions remain and which top-level claims they block;
4. **review gate** — why the current pack is green/yellow/red/blocked beyond individual claim statuses;
5. **export projection** — what GSN/SACM-shaped exports preserve, redact, or cannot faithfully carry.

## Main judgment after the pass

A worthy assurance-case contribution for Rust should now provide more than a claim graph, more than a standards-shaped export, and more than a loose pile of imported evidence.
It should also provide:

- one explicit **claim-library basis**,
- one explicit **import-policy receipt**,
- one explicit **assumption ledger**,
- one explicit **review-gate report**,
- one explicit **export-projection receipt**,
- and one explicit **assurance diff**.

That is the smallest support surface that gives another team something reviewable instead of folklore.
