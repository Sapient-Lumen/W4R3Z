
# Entry 172 — async transition and BorrowSanitizer evidence became fixture-first

This pass again chose **not** to add another narrow top-level proposal.

The better move was to tighten two underbuilt proposals whose timing is now better than it looked a week ago:

- **P-0458 Async Dyn Transition Kit**
- **P-0465 BorrowSanitizer Workflow & Evidence Kit**

## Main judgment

The archive is now large enough that the highest-value work is often to improve **handoff quality** rather than proposal count.

In this pass, the strongest missing value was not another bridge crate, another sanitizer wrapper, or another debug-tool wish list.
It was the receiver-facing artifact contract that lets another maintainer understand:

- which async-dyn recipe a crate is using,
- what that recipe implies for object surface and allocation,
- what would or would not change during migration,
- which BorrowSanitizer-era finding happened,
- which FFI or unsafe boundary mattered,
- and whether comparison against Miri is strong, partial, or simply not honest.

## Why these changes were the right ones

### 1. 2026 signals now make transition artifacts more important
Fresh official signals point in the same direction:

- the 2025 State of Rust survey still reports **resource usage** and **debugging** as meaningful productivity problems,
- the 2026 Rust flagships explicitly include **Just Add Async** milestones such as `async fn in dyn trait`,
- and Rust’s tooling direction still includes stronger sanitizer support plus BorrowSanitizer-era work.

That makes workflow crates for **transition truth**, **evidence truth**, and **support truth** look stronger than more proposal count.

### 2. P-0458 needed fixture depth, not more prose
The proposal already had a good instinct.
What it lacked was the minimal artifact pack that another person could actually inspect.

The new fixture pack freezes the first useful split:

- plan,
- recipe,
- allocation profile,
- object-surface diff,
- migration receipt.

That should make later revisions less likely to collapse language milestones, bridge recipes, and runtime/executor questions into one vague “async crate”.

### 3. P-0465 needed lane honesty, not a generic sanitizer story
The archive already had **P-0434** for the broad sanitizer workflow layer.
So the sharper move here was to make **P-0465** more explicitly the BorrowSanitizer-specific lane:

- profile,
- boundary map,
- run receipt,
- provenance finding,
- optional Miri compare,
- reduction receipt.

That should make later revisions less likely to collapse general sanitizer reports, aliasing findings, and debug-support posture into one fake bucket.

### 4. The repo itself needed amnesia resistance
This pass also added explicit hygiene notes so future humans and LLMs do not make two easy mistakes:

1. mistaking bridge crates or upcoming language support for proof that migration workflows are already solved,
2. and mistaking a general sanitizer workflow for BorrowSanitizer-specific evidence or vice versa.

## New planning stance

For the next few passes, the archive should prefer:

- strengthening **P-0458** as a recipe-comparison and migration-receipt crate,
- strengthening **P-0465** as an aliasing/provenance evidence lane above moving substrate,
- keeping **P-0434**, **P-0486**, and **P-0485** as distinct neighboring layers,
- and using fresh frontier re-ranking to keep these proposals in proportion to the archive’s broader portfolio.

The archive should **not** drift back into:

- another async bridge recipe proposal,
- another vague sanitizer wrapper,
- or a story where every safety/debugging artifact is thrown into one undifferentiated evidence blob.
