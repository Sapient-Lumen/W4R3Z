# Entry 274 — crate upgrade support sharpened around revalidation windows and pack freshness/supersession

This pass came from noticing that the pack could now be honest about source lineage, readiness, consistency, export posture, deviations, and warnings while still carrying one quiet bluff:

> a pack that was once reviewable could keep reading as the active migration contract even after time passed, source heads moved, lane inputs changed, or a newer revalidated pack already existed.

## What moved this time

Three donor patterns kept surviving scrutiny:

- **Radical-Governance** made the strongest case that reviewable approval is perishable and should reopen on material change or periodic review clocks.
- **AnonSync** made the strongest case that `current`, `stale_but_usable`, `expired`, and `superseded` are genuinely different truths.
- **Overseer** made the strongest case that older failures or summaries should become explicitly superseded when a newer reviewed result exists instead of continuing to compete as if both were current.

## Main planning move

The previous passes already established:

- source lineage and source heads,
- hazard arbitration and native import truth,
- fixup posture,
- package-scope truth and active follow-through state,
- pack readiness,
- review queue and cross-register consistency,
- export posture and exact public surface,
- and explicit deviations and warning registers.

This pass adds two sharper review objects above them:

1. **revalidation-window truth** — how long a pack stays current, which material changes expire it, and whether newer current packs supersede older ones,
2. **freshness-state truth** — whether the pack in hand is `current`, `stale_but_usable`, `expired`, or `superseded`, and why.

## Why this matters

Without **revalidation-window truth**, the crate can still act as if a frozen/public pack remains the active contract even after:

- a migration-guide head changes,
- the dependency/workspace graph shifts,
- feature or recipe surfaces move,
- or the pack simply ages past its own review horizon.

Without **freshness-state truth**, the crate can still leave downstream users guessing whether an old pack is:

- still current,
- only good enough for internal/manual reference,
- invalidated by lane drift,
- or simply replaced by a newer revalidated pack.

That is not a cosmetic refinement.
It is the difference between an archive that remembers a review once happened and an archive that can still say whether that review is the one you should trust now.

## Main judgment after the pass

A worthy upgrade-support crate should now provide **more than one internally coherent migration bundle**.
It should also provide:

- an explicit **revalidation window** that declares how the pack ages,
- a compact **freshness state** that says whether the pack is current, stale, expired, or superseded,
- and one honest way to retire older public summaries once a newer revalidated pack exists.

That keeps the lane from quietly turning old review work into evergreen authority.
