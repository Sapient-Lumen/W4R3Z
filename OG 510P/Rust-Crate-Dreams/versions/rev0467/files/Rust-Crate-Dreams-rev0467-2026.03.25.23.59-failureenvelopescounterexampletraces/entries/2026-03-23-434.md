# Entry 434 — territory map rerank separates control-plane epics, adoption amplifiers, sector labs, and archive-operator hygiene

This pass again chose **not** to add another narrow top-level proposal.

The better move was to preserve the archive's breadth **without** flattening everything into one ladder.
The archive is now wide enough that it needs a more explicit territory map:

1. **control-plane epics** — cross-ecosystem crates that cut repeated decision cost or publish support-contract truth,
2. **adoption amplifiers** — crates that make difficult language/toolchain capabilities usable and reviewable,
3. **sector labs / interop workbenches** — standard- and industry-facing kits with strong evidence value,
4. **moonshots / pack-family bets** — outside-the-box substrate ideas that could become major layers later.

## Why this rerank mattered

### 1. Variety needed a better shape than one endless ranking
The archive now covers everything from Cargo build internals to medical, industrial, geospatial, identity, media, and embedded interop workbenches.
That breadth is good.
But the archive needed a way to preserve **extreme variety** without pretending that every idea is equally urgent.
The new territory-map split keeps the long tail visible while still protecting the most ecosystem-shaping lanes.

### 2. Debuggability now deserves promotion into the control-plane frontier
Fresh official signals strengthened debugging as a first-class ecosystem problem:

- the 2025 State of Rust survey still lists debugging among the main productivity problems,
- the compiler team launched a dedicated debugging survey in February 2026,
- and the survey post is explicit that Rust still lacks consistently excellent debugger support across debuggers, operating systems, async code, expression evaluation, and visualizers.

That makes **P-0486 Debuggability Support Contract Kit** look more like a cross-ecosystem control plane and less like a side lane.

### 3. The strongest “epic crate” pattern is still support-contract infrastructure
Fresh official goals continue to cluster around:

- faster / more explainable builds,
- async ergonomics and runtime clarity,
- secure supply-chain controls,
- safety-critical evidence,
- and build/test/toolchain building blocks.

That still points away from narrow wrappers and toward crates that help people **choose**, **trust**, **prove**, **debug**, **document**, **compare**, and **migrate**.

### 4. docs.rs and registry surfaces are better, but not yet decision authority
crates.io now exposes a Security tab and RustSec-backed signals, while docs.rs now documents its target-sensitive and constrained build model more concretely and hosts rustdoc JSON.
Those are meaningful improvements.
They still do not answer:

- which crate family should a team choose,
- which target/build/runtime/symbol/debug surface is actually supported,
- or which downstream claim another team can safely make.

That keeps **P-0509 / P-0535 / P-0536 / P-0486 / P-0484** in the top frontier.

### 5. The archive itself needed stronger anti-amnesia operations
The March 2026 Rust challenges post now includes a retraction note about an earlier LLM-written draft and explicitly reflects community discomfort with generic, substance-light LLM prose.
That is a live reminder that this archive should prefer:

- specific source-grounded claims,
- clear banding and ranking,
- concrete artifact packages,
- and concise explanations with receipts.

## New planning stance

For the next few passes, the archive should prefer:

- deepening the **control-plane frontier** with more durable receipts, reports, doctor checks, and portable bundles,
- using the **adoption-amplifier** band for crates that make difficult language / runtime / toolchain capabilities reviewable rather than magical,
- keeping **sector labs** evidence-first and standards-facing instead of wrapper-first,
- and treating **moonshots** as pack-family / substrate experiments until they prove a reusable seam.

The archive should **not** drift back into:

- another “LLM for crates” shell without material-basis receipts,
- another protocol/client/parser bridge that stops at feature coverage,
- another score-only ranking crate,
- another runtime/helper wrapper that flattens semantics,
- or another support story built from docs.rs / crates.io / popularity surfaces alone.
