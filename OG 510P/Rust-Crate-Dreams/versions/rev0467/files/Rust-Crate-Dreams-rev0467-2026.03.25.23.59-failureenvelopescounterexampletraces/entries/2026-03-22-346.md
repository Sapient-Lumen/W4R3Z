# Entry 346 — unsafe-field invariant planning deepened around field authority, mutation lanes, constructor trust, and witness coverage for P-0460

This pass deepened **P-0460 Unsafe Field Invariant Ledger Kit** instead of adding another generic unsafe checker, another proof-shaped aspiration, or another “runs Miri” badge.

## Why this lane earned another pass now

Fresh official Rust signals line up unusually well:

- the unsafe-fields goal says fields that carry library safety invariants should be marked explicitly, and uses that could violate those invariants should require `unsafe` context;
- the std-contracts goal frames contracts as machine-readable preconditions, postconditions, and invariants for functions and data structures;
- the 2026 flagship goals elevate **normative unsafe documentation** as part of the safety-critical building-block story;
- and the January 2026 safety-critical write-up keeps stressing shared evidence, requirements, and practical collaboration instead of one magic verifier.

Together these make one conclusion stronger: a worthy ecosystem contribution here is not just “inventory unsafe blocks.” It is a crate that helps other people inspect **which fields actually carry soundness-relevant invariants, who is allowed to mutate them, which constructors are trusted to establish them, what evidence touched them, and how that changed over time**.

## Main judgment after the pass

The strongest near-term contribution in this lane is **not** another broad unsafe dashboard.
It is a **field-invariant contract layer** above comments, privacy tricks, tests, Miri, future unsafe-field syntax, and future contract imports.

This pass promotes six P-0460 review objects into first-class status:

1. **field authority receipt** — where an invariant claim came from and who owns it;
2. **mutation-lane report** — which safe/unsafe/generated paths can mutate the field;
3. **trusted-constructor receipt** — which creation or reconstitution paths are allowed to establish the invariant;
4. **invariant-witness report** — what tests, Miri, contracts, fuzzing, or manual review actually covered;
5. **field-contract drift diff** — what widened, narrowed, appeared, or disappeared across revisions;
6. **unsafe-field bundle manifest** — the portable review bundle joining those facts.

## Why these objects matter

Current unsafe-review substrate is finally specific enough that the archive should stop flattening it:

- a crate can have detailed safety docs and still leave the **authority source** for a field invariant unclear;
- a crate can have clean `unsafe` blocks and still let a **safe helper** widen mutation scope;
- a crate can pass Miri in one scenario and still leave the invariant witness **too narrow** to justify a downstream trust claim;
- and future native unsafe-field or contract syntax may help expression, but teams still need a **receiver-facing artifact layer** that survives code review, release comparison, and audit handoff.

## New archive-level stance

For unsafe-review work, the archive should now prefer this layering:

1. **P-0460** for field-carried invariants, mutator authority, and constructor trust;
2. **P-0120** for broader unsafe-obligation inventory, authority imports, interpreter boundaries, and witness fidelity;
3. **P-0121** when the hard problem is FFI ownership/layout/unwind/error truth;
4. **P-0485** when the hard problem is comparing many verification lanes or policy-evaluating a full campaign.

That keeps the portfolio from minting another crate that merely says “unsafe reviewed” without clarifying which fields matter, who may mutate them, or what evidence really covers them.

## What should count as a worthy crate contribution after this pass

A worthy unsafe-field contribution should now be able to answer all of these cleanly:

1. **Which fields carry soundness-relevant invariants, and exactly where does that authority come from?**
2. **Which safe or unsafe paths may mutate those fields, under what preconditions?**
3. **Which constructors, parsers, or deserializers are trusted to establish the invariant?**
4. **What evidence touched the invariant, and what did that witness not cover?**
5. **Can another engineer compare the field-contract story across revisions without reverse-engineering the whole crate by hand?**

If a candidate crate cannot answer those questions, the archive should usually treat it as unsafe folklore rather than a serious field-invariant contract.
