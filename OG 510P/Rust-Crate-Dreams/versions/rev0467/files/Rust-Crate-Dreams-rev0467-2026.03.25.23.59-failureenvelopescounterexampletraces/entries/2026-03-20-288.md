# Entry 288 — crate health sharpened into a maintainer-facing contract instead of stale badge folklore

This pass came from noticing that the archive had become much better at **choosing crates**, **upgrading crates**, and **leaving crates**, while still leaving one ecosystem-wide question oddly under-specified:

> once someone depends on a crate, what compact, reviewable answer tells them whether the crate is actively supported, reactively maintained, frozen, seeking help, or quietly drifting toward abandonment?

## What moved this time

Several live Rust signals lined up in a way that changed the shape of the gap:

- the **2025 State of Rust survey results** say concern about **developer and maintainer support** ticked upward and explicitly ask companies to support crate authors and project contributors;
- crates.io now exposes more real registry-side signals such as a **Security** tab and broader **Trusted Publishing** support;
- RFC work around the **MSRV-aware resolver** explicitly says mutable package/version metadata on crates.io could let Cargo report **whether you are still supported**, not just whether a newer release exists;
- and an older crates.io issue already captured a core failure mode: the old maintenance-status field was too tied to `Cargo.toml`, so it could go stale because updating it required a new release.

That means the missing value is no longer just “a nicer maintenance badge.”

## Main planning move

The archive now treats **P-0011 Crate Health** as a sharper crate family:

1. **maintenance-window truth** — whether the crate is in active feature work, reactive bugfix/security support, critical-fix-only mode, frozen maintenance, or explicitly seeking co-maintainers,
2. **succession-map truth** — who can take over, what successor or fallback exists, and whether the crate is relying on one person with no visible backup,
3. **support-intent truth** — which promises are actually being made about MSRV policy, issue/PR responsiveness, security-contact posture, and release expectations,
4. **health-check truth** — a conservative consistency report that keeps imported registry signals, maintainer-authored declarations, and manual-review zones visibly separate.

## Why this matters

Without a joined crate-health contract, the ecosystem still permits one ordinary lie:

- a crate can look alive because it published recently,
- or look dead because it has not published recently,
- or look trustworthy because it has a security tab and trusted publishing,
- while still giving no compact answer about whether downstream users are actually supported, what horizon that support has, or who should carry the work next.

That is not cosmetic.
It is the difference between “some signals exist” and “another team can make a dependency decision without reverse-engineering maintainer intent from scattered surfaces.”

## Main judgment after the pass

A worthy crate-health contribution should now provide more than a badge, one release date, or a popularity proxy.
It should provide:

- one compact **health profile** that maintainers can update without faking a release,
- one explicit **maintenance-window report** instead of vague “active / maybe inactive” folklore,
- one **succession map** that says whether another team can reasonably depend on the crate surviving a maintainer handoff,
- and one **health-check report** that refuses to flatten registry trust signals, security signals, and maintainer support promises into one fake status.

That keeps the lane from quietly turning “visible crate metadata” into “actual support truth.”
