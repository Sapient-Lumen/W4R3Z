# Entry 416 — dependency lifecycle deepened around re-resolution honesty, selection anchors, and lock/yank survivability

This pass chose to **deepen P-0535 Dependency Lifecycle Transition Kit** instead of opening another dependency dashboard, upgrade bot, or local patch helper.

The sharper question was:

> when a team claims “this dependency is contained, temporary, or on a safe transition path,” what actually keeps that claim true on a clean resolve, another machine, or the next release branch?

## Why this deepening pass earned a full revision

Fresh official sources made the next missing layer concrete:

- the January 2026 safety-critical write-up still says teams often use crates early, then hide them behind abstraction layers, vendor them, fork them, or replace them later as criticality rises;
- the January 2026 maintenance post makes dependency updates and keeping things working over time part of ordinary maintenance rather than an exceptional event;
- Cargo still draws a hard line between the broad intent in `Cargo.toml` and the exact realized graph in `Cargo.lock`;
- `[patch]` may live in checked-in manifests, but Cargo also explicitly allows config-file and CLI patches for local-only testing;
- source replacement is only valid when the replacement is **the same source code**, which means “vendored” or “mirrored” does not itself imply fork progress or transition completion;
- Cargo still says `cargo update <spec>` performs a conservative lockfile update rather than a generic replan of architectural intent;
- Cargo still says yanking only stops future default selection and does **not** invalidate existing lockfiles or direct downloads;
- and `rust-version` can influence both `cargo add` and resolver choice, which means a lifecycle posture may depend on toolchain-floor context even when manifests look stable.

That means the next worthwhile move for **P-0535** is not only:
- lane assignment,
- seam receipts,
- override authority,
- imported-signal freshness,
- and exception reevaluation.

It is also to make the transition story's **selection anchor** and **clean re-resolution risk** reviewable.

## Main judgment after this pass

The top frontier still begins:

1. **P-0537 Compile Iteration Feedback Kit**
2. **P-0509 Crate Ecosystem Pathfinder & Decision-Pack Kit**
3. **P-0535 Dependency Lifecycle Transition Kit**
4. **P-0536 Crate Knowledge Pack Kit**
5. **P-0538 Concurrency Contract Kit**

But **P-0535** is stronger now because the archive no longer stops at:

- where a dependency is allowed,
- what seam exists,
- who can see the override route,
- whether imported context is stale,
- and whether an exception expired.

It now has a clearer product shape for:

- **selection-anchor receipts**,
- **re-resolution risk reports**,
- **lockfile-versus-policy separation**,
- **yank and fresh-resolve exposure honesty**,
- and doctor rules that reject fake “the current transition is shared and durable” stories.

## What should count as a worthy crate contribution in this lane now

A worthy dependency-lifecycle crate should provide other people:

1. **one selection-anchor receipt** that says what currently makes the claimed transition posture hold: checked-in lockfile, root manifest patch, config-only patch, source replacement, git rev pin, or other anchor;
2. **one re-resolution risk report** that says what would change on a clean resolve, a lock refresh, a fresh checkout, or a different `rust-version` context;
3. **one explicit separation between current green builds and future selectability** so yanked versions and stale lockfiles do not masquerade as durable policy;
4. **one way to say whether the route is genuinely shared** or only lives in local config, CI-only config, or manual operator practice;
5. **one doctor path** that warns when teams over-read the lockfile, vendoring, or a temporary patch as proof that the lifecycle transition is complete.

That is much closer to an ecosystem-worthy crate than another dependency graph UI.
