# Archive policy

## Purpose

Keep the archive small enough to survive and rich enough to reopen.

## Rules

1. Prefer summaries and citations over long quotation.
2. Do not keep large binary research artifacts in the long-term archive unless they are load-bearing.
3. New notes must earn their place by improving the portfolio, not merely by being interesting.
4. Every canon note must include: thesis, why it matters, mechanism, watchpoints, weakening conditions, and source anchors.
5. Quarantine is mandatory for bold moves that feel directionally promising but under-supported.
6. If two notes collapse into one another, merge them.
7. If a note loses salience, demote or delete it.
8. Prefer official, institutional, or otherwise high-signal sources for the first anchor layer.
9. Preserve broadness: the archive should span technological, political, demographic, institutional, and cultural surfaces.
10. Keep every release inspectable in under an hour.

## Status labels

- `canon` — important enough, grounded enough, and broad enough to structure further work.
- `quarantine` — worth preserving, not yet trustworthy enough to steer the canon.
- `watchlist` — not yet a note, but a recurring pressure worth monitoring.

## Release discipline

Each packaged release should include:

- top-level orientation,
- bibliography,
- method surface,
- registry,
- changelog,
- receipt,
- and a predictable zip name.
