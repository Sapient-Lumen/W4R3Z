# Research agenda

## Immediate next questions

1. **Power:** How quickly are utilities and regulators beginning to create AI-specific load classes, tariffs, or queue rules?
2. **Insurance:** When does insurance repricing start visibly feeding into bond markets, school funding, or migration patterns?
3. **Care:** Which countries are first to frame care labor and eldercare capacity as strategic national infrastructure?
4. **Biology:** Does sequence screening become truly international, or does it remain fragmented and bypassable?
5. **Provenance:** Which sectors move from optional provenance to mandatory provenance first?
6. **Airspace:** What does a realistic civil anti-drone governance stack look like below traditional air-defense thresholds?
7. **Education:** Which institutions can preserve trust in assessment without reverting to purely pre-digital pedagogy?
8. **Observability:** Which public systems first replace self-reporting or sparse inspection with persistent remote sensing as primary evidence?
9. **Trade geolocation:** In which sectors do plot-level or facility-level coordinates become a routine condition of market access?
10. **Threshold credentials:** Which sectors stop accepting document copies once wallet-based attribute proofs become practical?
11. **Recovery and exclusion:** Where do wallet-mediated proof systems generate unacceptable lockout, coercion, or over-collection risk?
12. **Object passports:** Which sectors move first from PDF compliance to product-level passport infrastructure, and where does that become a de facto trade barrier?
13. **Transaction rails:** When does eInvoicing escape tax and become shared infrastructure for customs, procurement, finance, or sustainability reporting?
14. **Title rails:** Which jurisdictions and sectors begin treating exclusive digital control over trade documents as routine commercial infrastructure rather than as a niche legal innovation?
15. **Authority credentials:** Which sectors first stop trusting emailed delegation and portal-role sprawl, and instead require reusable proofs that a human or system is authorised to act for a legal entity?
16. **Authoritative evidence exchange:** Which procedures actually retire upload-heavy application packets once source-to-source evidence retrieval becomes legally and technically available?
17. **Relay surfaces:** Which sectors first make certified access points, trust service providers, or registered intermediaries a real condition of admissibility rather than a convenience layer?
18. **Status surfaces:** Which sectors first make runtime standing checks — revocation, suspension, trusted-list validation, registry confirmation, or capability discovery — an ordinary condition of admissibility?
19. **Large-load classes:** Which jurisdictions first make self-supply, flexibility, minimum-take, or collateral obligations normal conditions for very large energy users rather than exceptional one-off deals?
20. **Travel preclearance:** Which mobility corridors first make upstream digital permission, carrier-side checks, and pre-submitted travel credentials more decisive than the traditional arrival booth?
21. **Prior authorization rails:** Which treatments and payer segments first turn electronic prior authorization from an administrative nuisance into decisive care-access infrastructure?
22. **Work permission rails:** Which labour markets first make digital work-right checks, platform onboarding controls, and posted-worker declarations more decisive than the signed contract or photocopied document set?
23. **Permit-ready models:** Which jurisdictions first make machine-checkable BIM/GIS submissions more decisive than narrative plan packets for ordinary building, retrofit, or infrastructure approvals?
24. **Payee verification:** Which payment corridors first make counterparty validation, warning logging, and sanctions-state refresh more decisive than the bare account identifier when money is sent?
25. **Resource telemetry:** Which extraction regimes first make approved meters, telemetry-capable devices, or electronic monitoring more decisive than the licence paper or annual report itself?
26. **Insurable collateral:** Which housing-finance systems first make property-level hazard, remap exposure, and insurance quality more decisive than borrower-only qualification for ordinary mortgage access?
27. **Listing permission rails:** Which cities, states, or platforms first make registration-number validity, exemption status, and platform-side legality checks more decisive than complaint-driven enforcement for short-term lodging markets?

## Portfolio-level pressure tests

- Is observability best understood as its own branch, or merely a subtype of proof?
- Do object passports belong inside proof surfaces, or do they eventually justify a distinct branch of machine-readable goods governance?
- Do transaction rails deserve their own branch, or will they collapse back into proof surfaces or gatekeeping once the portfolio matures?
- Are software certification, API governance, and access-point accreditation becoming under-recognised control surfaces across the archive?
- Does digital control of legally effective originals belong inside proof surfaces long-term, or does it eventually justify its own title-rails branch?
- Do organisational authority credentials remain a proof-surface subtype, or do they become their own branch once wallets, mandates, and firm-side agent automation converge?
- Do query surfaces deserve their own branch, or will authoritative-source retrieval collapse back into proof surfaces once wallets, attestations, and data-sharing stacks converge?
- Do relay surfaces deserve their own branch, or are access-point accreditation, qualified trust services, and registered intermediary layers better understood as a subtype of gatekeeping or transaction-rail governance?
- Do status surfaces deserve their own branch, or are revocation feeds, trusted lists, capability directories, and live registry checks better understood as implementation details of proof, query, or relay systems?
- Do allocation surfaces deserve their own branch, or are large-load service classes better understood as a subtype of gatekeeping or scarce-complement politics?
- Is travel preclearance best understood as a straightforward gatekeeper, or does upstream journey-readiness eventually become its own recurring branch once authorisation, carrier enforcement, and digital travel credentials converge?
- Do episode-specific adjudication loops such as electronic prior authorisation deserve their own branch, or are they better understood as a gatekeeper and transaction-rail subtype?
- Do permit-ready models remain a gatekeeper subtype, or does model-governed approval eventually justify its own recurring branch once building, environmental, and infrastructure permitting converge on shared machine-readable review objects?
- Do payee-validation stacks remain a gatekeeper/status/transaction subtype, or do they eventually justify a distinct financial-admissibility branch once instant payments, scam reimbursement, and counterparty discovery converge?
- Do telemetry-conditioned extraction rights deserve their own branch, or are approved meters, vessel tracking, electronic logbooks, and onboard monitoring better understood as a gatekeeper/observability subtype?
- Does insurability-conditioned collateral remain a subtype of gatekeeping and territorial repricing, or does housing finance eventually justify its own recurring branch once insurers, mortgagees, servicers, and securitisers converge on hazard-aware collateral governance?
- Do platform-enforced lodging-legality stacks remain a gatekeeper subtype, or do registration-number governance, listing disablement, and platform-side transaction blocking eventually justify their own recurring branch once they spread beyond a few housing-stressed jurisdictions?
- Which canon notes are most likely to become redundant if a stronger synthesis arrives?
