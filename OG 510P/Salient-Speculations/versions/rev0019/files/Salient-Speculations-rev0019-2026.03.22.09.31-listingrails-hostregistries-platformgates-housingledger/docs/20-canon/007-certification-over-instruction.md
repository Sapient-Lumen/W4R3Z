# 007 — Certification over instruction

**Status:** canon

## Thesis

As generative AI becomes ubiquitous and partly outside institutional control, schools and universities will shift from being mainly content-delivery systems toward being **certification, coaching, and trusted-performance systems**.
Instruction becomes cheaper; trustworthy assessment becomes dearer.

## Why it matters

This reorders the role of educational institutions.
If AI can already assist with materials, tutoring, navigation, and drafting, then the hardest function to preserve is not access to explanations but confidence about what a learner can actually do, alone or in a governed collaborative setting.

## Mechanism sketch

- OECD now describes generative AI as widely accessible and often used beyond institutional control.
- UNESCO emphasizes both the upside and the policy lag of AI in education.
- U.S. education guidance already frames AI materials, tutoring, and personalized support as practical tools.

That combination means schools cannot simply ban their way back to an older information environment.
They will instead need stronger ways to validate judgment, skill, authorship, and collaboration under AI abundance.

## What this speculation predicts

1. Assessment formats that rely on live, oral, practical, or supervised performance will grow.
2. Educational prestige will increasingly attach to trustworthiness of evaluation, not only prestige of instruction.
3. Institutions that cannot produce credible certification signals will lose standing faster than those with mediocre teaching content.

## Watchpoints

- rise of oral exams, practicum models, studio critique, and live demos
- increased use of AI tutoring alongside tighter assessment controls
- policy emphasis on AI literacy plus authentic assessment
- provenance or process logging used in student work verification

## What would weaken this

- strong evidence that ordinary written assessment remains robust under widespread AI access
- broad public demand for low-friction credentialing overwhelming concerns about authentic performance
- institutional inability to redesign assessment at meaningful scale
