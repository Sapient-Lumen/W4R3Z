# 010 — Object passports and the end of opaque goods

**Status:** canon

## Thesis

A growing share of economically important goods will need to carry a **machine-readable passport** — not just a certificate in a file cabinet, but a live, queryable record of identity, compliance, origin, repair, and end-of-life-relevant data.
The market-access primitive shifts from “this good was once certified” to “this object can be interrogated.”

## Why it matters

This is broader than sustainability reporting.
If products need persistent identifiers, accessible passport data, and interoperable traceability events, then customs, marketplaces, regulators, repairers, recyclers, and industrial buyers all begin to rely on the same underlying data surface.
That turns product identity into infrastructure.

Once goods are expected to answer questions about what they are, where they came from, who handled them, whether they comply, and what can be done with them at end of life, supply chains become less like chains of paperwork and more like permissioned information systems.

## Mechanism sketch

- The EU’s Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation establishes a digital product passport regime, applies across physical goods in general, and makes product placement conditional on a passport when delegated acts require it.
- The same regulation requires persistent identifiers, open standards, machine-readable and transferable data, and a registry pathway that lets customs authorities verify that imported products have a passport.
- The Commission’s 2025–2030 working plan turns the passport from a distant idea into a sector rollout program.
- The EU’s new toy-safety rules show the pattern spreading beyond sustainability narratives into direct safety and customs enforcement: toys will need digital product passports and customs will be able to check them for online and imported goods.
- The existing battery-passport regime makes the logic concrete even earlier: from 18 February 2027, major battery categories on the EU market must carry an electronic record with open, interoperable, searchable information linked by QR code and unique identifier, with interoperability requirements linking battery passports to other digital product passports under Union law.
- UNECE’s Digital Product Passport specification shows that a cross-border standards architecture is being built around conformity credentials and traceability events, not merely around static disclosure pages.
- The Commission is already explicitly framing the Digital Product Passport as a supervisory tool for e-commerce imports alongside AI tools.

The deeper pattern is that cheap goods-data storage, cheap scanning, and rising compliance complexity make object-level machine readability more attractive than shipment-level paperwork.
A good increasingly needs not only to satisfy a rule but to carry a usable interface to the proof that it satisfies the rule.

## What this speculation predicts

1. Market access in regulated sectors will increasingly depend on product-level or batch-level passport data rather than on disconnected PDF declarations.
2. Customs, marketplaces, and market-surveillance systems will become major verifiers of object passports, not just human inspectors of paperwork.
3. More firms will spend money on identifier, facility, access-right, and traceability-event plumbing because product-data operations become part of compliance itself.
4. Political conflict will move toward confidentiality, SME burden, foreign-supplier inclusion, interoperability governance, and whether product passports become non-tariff barriers in practice.

## Watchpoints

- delegated acts and working-plan milestones that add specific product groups to passport regimes
- customs, marketplace, or procurement systems that query passport registries or require machine-readable product identifiers
- product-passport expansion beyond batteries and toys into textiles, electronics, steel, construction products, or other high-volume sectors
- standards convergence around interoperable identifiers, access rights, conformity credentials, and traceability events
- disputes over commercially sensitive data, error correction, appeals, and whether smaller or foreign firms can comply without being squeezed out

## What would weaken this

- passport rollout staying narrow, slow, or mostly symbolic across sectors
- firms satisfying the rules with shallow static disclosures that do not materially change enforcement, repair, or trade workflows
- severe fragmentation across jurisdictions or standards bodies preventing cross-border portability
- backlash strong enough that regulators retreat from object-level data expectations in favour of traditional paperwork
