# 901 — Giga-campuses become quasi-utilities

**Status:** quarantine

## Thesis

Very large compute campuses may evolve into something like **special utility enclaves**: sites whose political economy more closely resembles heavy industrial infrastructure, ports, or company towns than ordinary commercial real estate.

## Why it is quarantined

The direction feels plausible, but the archive does not yet have enough direct evidence to treat it as canon.
What is clear is pressure toward large dedicated loads, co-located generation, special tariffs, and local bargaining. What is not yet clear is whether these arrangements will consolidate into a genuinely new governance form.

## Why preserve it anyway

If it happens, it changes local sovereignty, ratepayer politics, labor markets, land use, and emergency planning all at once.

## Watchpoints

- bespoke utility agreements or regulatory carve-outs for megascale AI sites
- compute campuses financing generation or transmission themselves
- local tax, water, or emergency-service negotiations taking on quasi-constitutional importance
- campuses behaving as dispatchable civic actors during grid stress
