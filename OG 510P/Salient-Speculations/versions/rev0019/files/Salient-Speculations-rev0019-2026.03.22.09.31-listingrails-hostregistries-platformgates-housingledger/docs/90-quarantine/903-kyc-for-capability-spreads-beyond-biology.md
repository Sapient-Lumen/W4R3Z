# 903 — KYC-for-capability spreads beyond biology

**Status:** quarantine

## Thesis

The screening logic now visible around nucleic-acid synthesis may generalize into a broader pattern: dangerous general-purpose capabilities get governed through **identity, attestation, logging, and transaction filtering** rather than only through possession bans.

## Why it is quarantined

The biological case is real enough to observe, but the cross-domain generalization is still a step beyond the evidence gathered here.
The archive should preserve the idea without yet canonizing it.

## Why preserve it anyway

If this pattern spreads, it becomes a major governance template for advanced tools: not “who owns the tool?” but “who can request which actions under what verifiable conditions?”

## Watchpoints

- screening and attestation logic appearing in adjacent high-risk toolchains
- procurement rules demanding identity-rich access logs for powerful systems
- convergence between biosecurity, export-control, cloud-security, and provenance architectures
- institutional language shifting from prohibition toward monitored permissioning
