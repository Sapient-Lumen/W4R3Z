# Principles for Admitting Speculation

## 1. Prefer bottleneck shifts over trend extrapolation

Weak speculation says: *X grows fast*.  
Better speculation says: *because X grows fast, control shifts to Y*.

The archive should prefer the second form.

## 3. Reward cross-domain leverage

A strong speculative entry should usually link at least two of the following:

- material systems;
- institutional systems;
- information systems;
- demographic systems;
- moral or status systems.

## 4. Keep the payload small

Each dossier should aim to answer only five questions:

1. What is the claim?
2. Why now?
3. What follows if true?
4. What would falsify or weaken it?
5. What should be watched next?

## 5. Separate pressure from destiny

The archive should record **pressures**, not pretend to know exact outcomes.
A useful speculation identifies directional force without claiming inevitability.

## 6. Require asymmetry

A speculation is especially archive-worthy when small signals imply large downstream reordering.

## 7. Demand falsifiers

Every serious speculation should list at least three ways it could fail:

- the enabling trend stalls;
- institutions adapt faster than expected;
- a different bottleneck becomes dominant;
- the public rejects the regime change;
- economics fail even if the technology works.

## 8. Promote only what compresses well

A broad archive becomes useless if each thesis needs a book. Promote ideas that become sharper when compressed.

## 10. Prefer state transitions over noun proliferation

A new dossier should not merely name another artifact. It should identify a state change: who can rely, who must pause, who may appeal, who pays, who receives notice, what expires, what is superseded, and what happens if the state was wrong.

Weak addition: *X becomes a label.*  
Better addition: *X changes the admissible state of Y for Z reliance purpose, and creates named duties when that state is challenged, expires, or is corrected.*

## 11. Require an abuse and burden pass

Every useful governance artifact becomes a target. Before promotion, ask how the proposed object can be forged, laundered, poisoned, strategically delayed, privacy-leaking, exclusionary, captured by incumbents, or too expensive for small actors to maintain.

The archive should now treat anti-legibility, selective disclosure, burden shifting, and small-supplier intermediation as first-order questions, not as optional ethical commentary after the mechanism is already admired.

## Principle: index broadly, annotate carefully

The archive should become machine-readable without pretending every old dossier has already earned precise metadata. It is acceptable to generate broad heuristic indexes for navigation, but promoted metadata should remain reviewed and finite.

Use three tiers:

1. **Machine index** — broad, heuristic, useful for search and triage.
2. **Reviewed front matter** — precise enough to carry graph edges and lifecycle stages.
3. **Canonical schema value** — stable enough to shape future dossiers.

Do not turn every evocative phrase into a schema value. Do not let the schema become a thesaurus.



## rev0182 principle: stress proof objects before promoting them

A new proof object should not be promoted merely because it is plausible. It should survive adversarial review.

Before promotion, ask:

1. How is it forged, laundered, poisoned, made stale, or attached to the wrong subject?
2. Who is excluded because they cannot produce the required evidence?
3. What is the minimum sufficient proof, and who prevents over-collection?
4. What happens when the issuer, resolver, registry, witness, or trust anchor fails?
5. Which downstream actor is allowed to rely on the state, and which actor pays to correct it?
6. What would make the thesis false?

This principle is especially important for passports, credentials, model cards, validation reports, recall states, AI assurance packets, due-diligence statements, recipient graphs, and appeal-stay labels.


## rev0183 principle: decision-grade or demote

A speculation should now change at least one decision surface:

- product architecture;
- procurement language;
- diligence checklist;
- audit or assurance design;
- regulatory watchlist;
- capital allocation;
- civil-rights or access strategy;
- institutional operating model.

If a dossier is merely interesting but does not change a decision, it should be a seed, a note in the transfer atlas, or a sub-state inside a lifecycle model.

## rev0183 principle: audit over confidence

The archive should not confuse a good conceptual fit with evidence. Every promoted dossier should have an evidence grade:

- E4: enforcement active;
- E3: artifact live;
- E2: signal cluster;
- E1: analogy-backed;
- E0: evocative.

A low evidence grade does not make a speculation bad. It means the dossier must be explicit about what would make it wrong.

## rev0183 principle: absence is a state

The archive has repeatedly treated proof presence as powerful. rev0183 adds the complement: absence, nonparticipation, silence, stripping, unavailability, and refusal are themselves states. They should not be collapsed into noncompliance unless the dossier explains why.
## rev0184 addition: freshness is not validity

A dossier should not use `valid`, `fresh`, `expired`, `revoked`, `stale`, `stayed`, and `superseded` as loose synonyms. These are distinct governance states. When the claim depends on proof age, specify the clock and reliance rule.

A new dossier about expiry or revalidation should normally be folded into `19-evidence-freshness-model.md` unless it adds a new enforcement surface, abuse pattern, fallback constitution, or distributional burden.

## rev0185 addition: remedy is a clocked state machine

Do not treat an appeal, complaint, dispute, grievance, correction request, explanation request, or human-review request as a generic support ticket. A remedy object should specify:

1. who has standing;
2. what notice started the clock;
3. what information is sufficient to file;
4. whether downstream reliance is stayed, restricted, reserved, or unaffected;
5. who must supply evidence;
6. who can review and reverse;
7. what outcome states exist;
8. who must receive the outcome;
9. how abuse filters are themselves appealable.

A new remedy dossier should normally be folded into `22-dispute-and-remedy-lifecycle.md` unless it creates a new enforceable surface, a new proof object, or a new abuse/distributional failure mode.


## rev0186 principle: authority is action-scoped

Do not treat identity, access, consent, role, and delegated authority as interchangeable. A future dossier in the authority family should specify:

- who the principal subject is;
- who or what is acting;
- what source grants authority;
- which action is permitted;
- which action is nondelegable;
- whether the action is joint, step-up, time-limited, matter-limited, value-limited, or channel-limited;
- how authority is presented and verified;
- how revocation propagates;
- how misuse is logged and disputed.

A dossier that only says “X will need authorization” is not enough. The archive should prefer claims that expose a new scope grammar, verification choke point, revocation path, fallback mode, or abuse telemetry surface.


## rev0187 principle: lineage is a state machine, not a halo

Do not accept “provenance attached,” “traceable,” “passport-ready,” “signed,” or “content credentialed” as sufficient. A lineage claim should say which subject was identified, which source state was captured, which transformation occurred, who held custody, which layer is authoritative, what was redacted, how the object resolves, what changed, whether replay is sufficient, and how a custody break is declared.

A provenance label without replay, route, custody, and dispute semantics is only a halo around an object. It is not yet infrastructure.

## rev0188 principle: money follows state

A speculation is stronger when it identifies how a proof-state change moves money: coverage, reserve, holdback, renewal, exclusion, defense control, indemnity, subrogation, or capital release. Do not say merely that an artifact creates liability. Say which exposure state changes and who now bears the loss.
