# Research Signal Ledger

This file records current outside signals that should shape the next several revisions. It is not a source dump. It is a pressure map: which external developments make archive claims stronger, weaker, nearer, or more politically complicated?

## A. Product biographies and proof objects are becoming real

### Digital Product Passport

The Commission's Digital Product Passport consultation frames DPPs as infrastructure for storing and sharing product sustainability, durability, environmental, instruction, and conformity information [S1484]. This strongly supports the archive's long-running claim that products acquire governed biographies.

**Implications for the archive**

- Product passports should no longer be treated as an isolated dossier family. They are a convergence point for source identity, resolver continuity, repair evidence, compliance-object forgery, selective disclosure, correction rights, and non-reliance states.
- The next productive dossier is probably not “passports matter.” It is one of the second-order problems: passport issuer succession, forged passport revocation, repair-event appendices, passport redaction profiles, or passport-to-customs reconciliation.

### Data Act

The Data Act applies from 12 September 2025 and expands rights and obligations around access to and use of data from connected products and related services [S1481].

**Implications for the archive**

- Data access rights create proof duties: who requested data, what was provided, what was withheld, whether the export was complete, and whether a third party could use it.
- The Data Act should be connected to repair evidence, small-supplier evidence brokers, and data-minimization proofs rather than kept in a standalone data-portability lane.

### Right to repair

The EU repair directive must be transposed and applied from 31 July 2026 [S1494].

**Implications for the archive**

- Repair rights need repair evidence. The likely artifacts are refusal reason codes, diagnostic-access receipts, parts-availability proofs, repair-event records, and warranty-dispute packets.
- Repair should be treated as a household-facing version of managed legibility: a right becomes real when a consumer can produce admissible evidence.

## B. Grid access is becoming a strategic allocation surface

### Interconnection queue pressure

Berkeley Lab reports roughly 10,300 active U.S. interconnection projects at the end of 2024, representing about 1,400 GW of generation and 890 GW of storage [S1495]. FERC Order No. 2023 addresses interconnection backlogs through reforms including cluster-study approaches [S1496]. IEA projects global data-centre electricity consumption to more than double to around 945 TWh by 2030 [S1487][S1497].

**Implications for the archive**

- Queue governance is no longer a background metaphor. It is a concrete industrial-policy axis.
- The archive should track grid-access evidence: readiness deposits, site-control proof, affected-system dependencies, queue-transfer rules, flexible-connection envelopes, and curtailment obligations.
- AI/data-center energy should not be framed only as “demand grows.” The bottleneck is admissible connection under technical, political, and financial constraints.

## C. AI governance is turning into packets, not just principles

### AI Act and GPAI obligations

The EU AI Act entered into force on 1 August 2024 and becomes broadly applicable on 2 August 2026 with exceptions [S1500]. The Commission says GPAI obligations entered into application on 2 August 2025, with Commission enforcement powers applying from 2 August 2026 and pre-existing GPAI models needing compliance by 2 August 2027 [S1498]. The GPAI Code of Practice includes transparency, copyright, and safety/security chapters and a model documentation form [S1499].

**Implications for the archive**

- Model governance should be expressed as packet governance: model documentation packets, deployment-context appendices, change notices, redacted annexes, evaluation lineage, and use-case suitability statements.
- The archive should connect AI governance to existing dossiers on validator services, portable validation reports, source-snapshot escrow, benchmark stewards, conformance regression alerts, and transformation-code custody.
- Open-weight model forks create a successor-map problem: which documentation travels with which derivative?

## D. Cybersecurity status systems are moving from binary truth to state-aware reliance

### NVD enrichment shift

NIST's April 2026 NVD update says CVE submissions grew 263% between 2020 and 2025 and explains a risk-based enrichment model rather than uniform enrichment [S1480].

**Implications for the archive**

- Vulnerability records should be tracked as status-bearing reliance objects: listed, enriched, deferred, exploited, vendor-disputed, VEX-not-affected, stale, superseded, or non-reliance.
- This validates the archive's move from “database as authority” to “state label as governance.”

### Incident reporting

SEC cyber disclosure rules require current disclosure of material cyber incidents, generally within four business days after materiality determination [S1501]. CIRCIA directs CISA to create reporting rules for covered cyber incidents and ransom payments [S1502]. DORA and the Cyber Resilience Act add separate operational-resilience and product-security reporting surfaces [S1483][S1482].

**Implications for the archive**

- Incident reporting should be a routing and materiality-clock thesis, not merely a legal-compliance note.
- Source-witness nonresponse matters here: third-party vendors often control the evidence needed by the reporting entity.
- Correction afterlife is central because early reports are frequently incomplete.

## E. Credentials and selective disclosure are becoming mainstream enough to support anti-legibility

### Verifiable credentials and privacy-preserving proof

W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model 2.0 reached Recommendation in May 2025 and supports selective disclosure patterns; W3C BBS cryptosuites support selective disclosure and unlinkable proofs [S1488][S1489]. EU Digital Identity Wallet materials point toward wallets available by the end of 2026 and privacy/security features including selective disclosure [S1492][S1493].

**Implications for the archive**

- Anti-legibility should be promoted from caveat to core axis. The most mature infrastructure may prove less, not more.
- Recipient-graph privacy proofs and data-minimization proofs are not privacy side quests; they are what prevent proof infrastructure from becoming surveillance infrastructure.
- Public proof-profile registries may become the next key artifact: a registry of what must be proven for a purpose, what must not be disclosed, and which cryptographic formats are accepted.

## F. Compliance objects become targets

### Provenance standards and forgery resistance

C2PA frames its standard as a way to establish the origin and edits of digital content [S1503]. Product passports, due-diligence statements, model documentation packets, repair records, and incident reports all create similar provenance questions.

**Implications for the archive**

- Compliance-object forgery should not remain a single dossier. It should become an abuse lens applied to passports, AI documentation, repair evidence, EUDR statements, grid-readiness profiles, and incident reports.
- Future dossiers should distinguish forgery, laundering, replay, stale-proof reuse, subject mismatch, issuer compromise, and resolver capture.

## Near-term research bets

1. **Repair-event records** will become the first household-facing product-biography artifact.
2. **Grid connection-readiness profiles** will become financeable and disputable project assets.
3. **Model documentation packets** will become procurement boilerplate before they become clean regulatory filings.
4. **Small-supplier evidence brokers** will emerge wherever product passport or due-diligence regimes meet fragmented supply chains.
5. **Incident-routing matrices** will become standing governance artifacts for regulated firms.
6. **Public proof-profile registries** will become necessary once selective disclosure moves from identity wallets into procurement and compliance.

## What would make the archive wrong

- Regulations remain broad but enforcement stays weak, so proof objects do not gate money, permission, or ranking.
- Buyers continue accepting generic vendor assurances rather than structured evidence.
- Public agencies provide simple enough tooling that no intermediary markets form.
- Courts and regulators reject machine-readable status labels as insufficiently contextual.
- Privacy backlash blocks reliance-graph and passport infrastructure before selective disclosure matures.


## rev0182 additional signal layer

### Selective disclosure needs sufficiency governance

W3C VC 2.0 and BBS selective-disclosure work support privacy-preserving proof [S1488][S1489][S1505], while EU Digital Identity Wallet materials point toward wallet availability and selective disclosure features [S1492][S1493]. The archive's inference is that the next bottleneck is not only proof format but public profiles that say what proof is sufficient and what disclosure is excessive.

### Product passports create resolver and afterlife risk

The ESPR framework for product passports, identifiers, data carriers, access rights, and registries [S1504], plus Commission DPP service-provider consultation [S1484], support the claim that product biography will depend on resolver governance, provider continuity, and historical state access.

### Supply-chain compromise validates graph-poisoning stress tests

CISA alerts around npm ecosystem and Axios package compromise [S1508][S1509], OWASP LLM risk categories [S1506], and MITRE ATLAS [S1507] reinforce the idea that trusted graphs and transitive relations are attack surfaces. The archive extends this from software and AI to supplier, product, recipient, recall, and identity graphs.

### Inclusion risk is now a proof-infrastructure question

World Bank ID4D materials on identity gaps and grievance redress [S1510][S1511], together with FAO / ITC work on supply-chain compliance tooling for smallholders [S1512][S1513], support the anti-legibility and informal-refuge correction: proof systems can increase trust while excluding compliant actors who lack evidence capacity.

### Recall systems hint at product-biography state propagation

Safety Gate and OECD GlobalRecalls already make recall state visible across public systems [S1514][S1515]. The speculative move is that product passports, repair records, and resale workflows may turn recall status into a routine machine-readable condition rather than a separate public alert.


## rev0183 additional signal layer

### Post-quantum cryptography turns crypto into transition-state governance

NIST's finalized FIPS 203/204/205 standards [S1516][S1517], CISA's product-category planning [S1518], and NIST NCCoE migration guidance [S1530] support a new archive branch: cryptographic agility as maintained evidence. The speculative implication is that buyers will not only ask whether a vendor supports a standard; they will ask which products, archives, signatures, trust anchors, and exceptions are in which transition state.

### Assessor capacity is a market-access bottleneck

European Commission notified-body materials [S1525], EUDAMED certificate-state publication [S1526], and the Commission's 2025 simplification proposal [S1527] strengthen a broader claim: conformance capacity and certificate afterlife are market infrastructure, not administrative background.

### Agents move delegated authority from paperwork into logs

Mainstream agent-building primitives [S1529], impersonation enforcement [S1528], and AI risk-management documentation [S1531] point toward scoped authority logs, tool-call receipts, and consumer redress pathways as a new proof layer.

### Data spaces create borders as well as flows

The Data Governance Act's data-intermediary framework [S1523] and common European data-space strategy [S1524] support a shift from portability to participation governance: who is admitted, under what profile, with which connector, purpose, audit right, and appeal.

### Content provenance needs absence semantics

C2PA provenance standards [S1521][S1522] and AI Act transparency obligations [S1519][S1520] make provenance absence salient. The archive's inference is that “no credential” will split into legacy, stripped, anonymous, unverifiable, nonparticipating, external-manifest, and suspicious states.


## rev0184 freshness-refactor signals

The evidence-freshness refactor is supported by several cross-sector signals:

- HTTP caching has mature freshness and stale-use semantics, including cache control and stale-use extensions [S1534][S1535].
- Verifiable credential specifications expose validity windows and proof timing fields [S1536][S1537].
- NVD guidance encourages last-modified update windows for local repositories rather than repeated full-dataset pulls [S1532].
- CA/Browser Forum baseline requirements are shortening public TLS certificate and validation-data clocks over a published transition schedule [S1538][S1539].

Speculative implication: more markets will move from binary validity to disclosed proof age, allowed stale-use, revalidation clocks, and exception registries.

## rev0185 remedy-lifecycle signals

The remedy-lifecycle refactor is supported by several cross-sector signals:

- The Digital Services Act creates internal complaint and out-of-court dispute pathways for platform decisions, while also recognizing misuse protections for manifestly unfounded notices or complaints [S1541][S1542][S1551][S1552].
- GDPR rights operations expose mature response clocks, extension notices, restriction-of-processing states, and data-subject request workflows [S1543][S1544].
- Credit-reporting and lending rules already require structured dispute handling and specific adverse-action explanations rather than generic score references [S1545][S1546][S1547].
- The AI Act adds complaint and explanation surfaces around high-risk AI decision-making and staged deployer/provider obligations [S1548][S1549][S1550].

Speculative implication: more sectors will discover that remedy is not an afterthought. It is a clocked, stateful infrastructure layer involving notice sufficiency, standing proof, interim-state control, reviewer capacity, evidence routing, abuse filtering, and downstream propagation.


## rev0186 authority-lifecycle research signals

- EUDI Wallet materials now explicitly frame electronic attestations as carrying attributes including rights and permissions, and define users and identification schemes in ways that include natural persons representing other natural persons or legal persons [S1553, S1555].
- The EUDI Architecture Reference Framework includes representation of minors, people with diminished legal capacity, elderly or incapacitated people, caregivers, relatives, and powers of attorney as wallet ecosystem use cases [S1554].
- NIST SP 800-63-4 refreshes the federal digital identity model around proofing, authentication, federation, assertions, security, privacy, and customer experience [S1556]. That supports the identity layer but also shows why delegated authority needs additional action-scoped semantics.
- OAuth token exchange, MCP authorization, and emerging agent-authorization drafts show that agentic systems are moving toward token, capability, and delegated-action semantics rather than simple login [S1558, S1559, S1561, S1564].
- IRS power-of-attorney operations and GOV.UK LPA guidance show mature non-AI domains where representative-of-record, scope, duties, revocation, and confidentiality already matter [S1562, S1563].


## rev0187 signal cluster: provenance becomes operational lineage

Recent and mature standards point to a common operational pattern:

- W3C PROV supplies the entity/activity/agent grammar for provenance and derivation.
- C2PA turns content origin and edits into a visible credential surface.
- OpenLineage treats jobs, datasets, run events, and design-time events as observability for data transformation.
- SLSA and in-toto make software supply-chain trust depend on recorded steps, actors, and tamper-resistant provenance.
- SPDX now spans software, security, AI, datasets, and system components.
- GS1 Digital Link and resolver standards expose the product-passport routing layer: identifiers, default links, linksets, validation, and resolver-description files.
- The European Commission's DPP consultation makes service-provider storage, management, and certification a live policy surface.

Speculative implication: the next bottleneck is not whether proof objects exist. It is whether their origin, transformation, routing, redaction, derived-use rights, replay sufficiency, and custody breaks are legible enough for reliance.
