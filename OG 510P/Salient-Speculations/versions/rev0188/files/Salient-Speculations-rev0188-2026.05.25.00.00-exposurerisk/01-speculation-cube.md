# The Speculation Cube

A compact way to generate, sort, and compare candidate speculations.


> **rev0180 operating note.** This file now has two layers. The short schema below is the cube. The long lists that follow are a primitive inventory: useful for generation, but too large and mixed-granularity to serve as the cube axes themselves. When classifying dossiers, use `05-cube-schema.md` as the controlled vocabulary and treat the old lists as examples, synonyms, and raw material.

## Controlled cube, v2

A dossier should now be classifiable using ten compact dimensions:

1. **Domain / substrate** — what underlying system is being reorganized.
2. **Bottleneck type** — what becomes newly scarce, governable, or priced.
3. **Enforcement surface** — where refusal, liability, access, funding, or ranking becomes real.
4. **Artifact type** — the reusable object, status, report, registry entry, packet, proof, or notice that travels.
5. **Lifecycle stage** — source, transform, publish, rely, dispute, stay, correct, supersede, retire.
6. **Coordination actor** — who becomes central because they operate, interpret, or arbitrate the new object.
7. **Failure mode** — how the system fails in ways that are not merely ordinary delay.
8. **Maturity / confidence** — seed, signal, artifact, enforcement surface, infrastructure, constitutional layer.
9. **Adversarial pressure** — who benefits from spoofing, withholding, poisoning, delaying, or over-reading the object.
10. **Distributional effect** — who pays, who is excluded, who needs a broker, and who gains leverage.

The promotion rule is now stricter: a candidate speculation should name not only a bottleneck but also an artifact, a state transition, an enforcement surface, an abuse path, and a plausible falsifier.

## Promotion rule

A seed should be promoted into a dossier when it has:

- one credible source base,
- one non-trivial institutional consequence,
- one clearly named bottleneck shift,
- one plausible alternative explanation,
- and one falsification path.

## rev0181 operating note: index layer

The cube now has a companion index. Use `INDEX/dossier-index.csv` and `INDEX/dossier-index.json` for navigation, but treat their inferred constellation and artifact flags as provisional. Reviewed metadata belongs in dossier YAML; heuristic metadata belongs in the index.

The distinction matters: the archive can be searchable before it is fully migrated, but it should not claim more classification certainty than it has earned.



## rev0182 stress axes

The cube now has a standing stress layer. For any candidate dossier, classify not only the domain, actor, artifact, and bottleneck, but also the attack and burden path.

### Adversarial axis

- forgery;
- laundering;
- stale-proof reuse;
- subject mismatch;
- issuer compromise;
- resolver capture;
- graph poisoning;
- nonresponse leverage;
- redaction abuse;
- trust-anchor failure.

### Burden axis

- small-supplier evidence capacity;
- low-connectivity or offline access;
- credential or identity exclusion;
- appeal cost;
- proprietary tooling dependency;
- over-disclosure pressure;
- public-good validator absence.

### Anti-legibility axis

- privacy-preserving proof;
- proof-profile sufficiency;
- local trust override;
- escrowed disclosure;
- informal fallback;
- illicit evasion.

A dossier that cannot be placed on these axes is probably still a trend note rather than a datacube-ready speculation.


## rev0183: Decision-grade layer

The cube now has a decision layer. A dossier should be readable not only as a claim but as a planning object:

```text
If this thesis is true, what should a buyer, regulator, builder, insurer, auditor, platform, investor, or public-interest actor do differently?
```

The new decision-grade fields are:

- evidence grade;
- bottleneck hardness;
- decision surface;
- next verification move;
- overconfidence risk;
- consolidation risk.

This prevents the cube from becoming a beautiful taxonomy that cannot rank its own claims.

## New transfer tests

A pattern may transfer across domains only if the target domain has:

1. a relying actor;
2. a repeatable artifact or state label;
3. an enforcement surface;
4. an adversarial or exclusion failure mode;
5. a falsifier.

If those are missing, leave the idea in `17-transfer-atlas.md` instead of promoting it.
## rev0184 operating note: model families before noun proliferation

The evidence-freshness audit showed a recurring archive failure mode: once a lens becomes productive, every adjacent noun looks like a standalone dossier. Future cube work should first ask whether the candidate belongs inside an existing model family. Current reusable model families include reliance-object lifecycle, product biography lifecycle, anti-legibility, decision-grade scoring, and now evidence freshness.

## rev0185 operating note: remedies are not support tickets

The cube now treats the appeal/correction/grievance family as a separate lifecycle with its own state vocabulary. When classifying a dossier, add remedy metadata where relevant:

- `remedy_role` — what function the dossier performs inside the lifecycle;
- `remedy_stage` — notice, intake, standing-check, admissibility, interim-state, evidence-request, review, decision, correction, propagation, closure, telemetry;
- `state_family: remedy` — when the claim depends on pending, stayed, corrected, denied, closed, or abuse-limited states.

This avoids confusing four different questions:

1. Is the underlying proof valid?
2. Is the proof fresh enough to rely on?
3. Is the proof currently disputed?
4. Is the affected party able to obtain remedy?

A dossier can be strong on the first two and still fail the fourth.


## rev0186 cube refinement: authority lifecycle axis

The cube now treats delegated authority as a lifecycle, not just an identity credential. Add the following axis when evaluating dossiers involving proxies, representatives, agents, guardians, legal persons, service accounts, AI agents, or standing proofs:

`define → grant → bind → scope → publish → present → verify → act → log → revoke → propagate → fallback → dispute → audit`

The key test is whether an actor may perform a specific action for a principal subject under a scope and whether that permission can be verified, logged, revoked, and challenged. This axis prevents several false mergers:

- identity is not authority;
- access is not authority;
- consent is not all authority;
- standing is not settlement power;
- agent identity is not tool authority;
- a credential is not a complete delegation state.


## rev0187 controlled primitive: provenance-lineage

The `provenance-lineage` refactor prevents the cube from minting a new dossier every time an artifact gets a passport, signature, content credential, data lineage field, resolver, or source attestation. Future additions should classify the relevant stage: identify, capture, bind, transform, package, sign, resolve, disclose, redact, transfer, diff, replay, verify, rely, dispute, correct, supersede, or archive.

Use `30-lineage-state-vocabulary.md` when the bottleneck is origin, custody, transformation, wrapper divergence, resolver continuity, redaction boundary, derived-use rights, replay sufficiency, or custody-break disclosure.

## rev0188 axis patch: exposure states

The cube now treats `exposure` as a state family alongside freshness, remedy, authority, and provenance. Exposure states include allocation, coverage attachment, condition precedent, trigger, notice, reservation of rights, exclusion, retention, limit erosion, reserve release, indemnity mapping, subrogation, renewal restriction, and tail closure.
