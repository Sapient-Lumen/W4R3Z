# Reliance-Object Lifecycle

The recent managed-legibility sequence is no longer best understood as isolated dossiers. It is a lifecycle model for **reliance objects**: packets, reports, scorecards, credentials, validation verdicts, due-diligence statements, source snapshots, and notices that other institutions use to make decisions.

The archive's key claim is that many future markets will not be governed only by raw information. They will be governed by the state of information objects: whether they are source-bound, transformed, published, relied on, stayed, corrected, superseded, non-reliable, or retired.

## Canonical lifecycle

### 1. Source object

A source system contains the native record: ticket, alert, parcel restriction, product component, due-diligence statement, credential, vulnerability record, calibration certificate, map geometry, or authority status.

Load-bearing questions:

- What is the native identifier?
- Is the identifier stable across migration, rename, split, merge, or deletion?
- Who is the source witness?
- What retention window exists?
- Which source wins if two sources conflict?

Related dossiers:

- authoritative source hierarchies become hidden constitutions
- source-object identity warranties become broker liability caps
- reliance-grade source attestations become a service tier
- source-snapshot escrow becomes liability-tail infrastructure

### 2. Transformation

A broker, validator, platform, auditor, or internal team converts source objects into a normalized packet or verdict.

Load-bearing questions:

- What mapping code was used?
- Which schema, lookup table, clock policy, thresholds, and runtime environment governed the output?
- Can the transformation be replayed?
- What was lost, inferred, collapsed, or left unmapped?

Related dossiers:

- transformation-code escrow becomes packet custody
- normalization-loss warranties become diligence language
- semantic-equivalence grades become migration shorthand
- translation-loss proofs become an audit surface

### 3. Publication

The packet, report, scorecard, or statement is issued into an ecosystem where others can rely on it.

Load-bearing questions:

- What reliance purposes does it support?
- What version, effective date, criteria set, and support window attach?
- Which outputs are authoritative: human rendering, structured data, signature, registry entry, or API response?

Related dossiers:

- portable validation reports become a quiet mutual-recognition surface
- report-signature trust chains become interoperability bottlenecks
- readable/structured divergence becomes a liability surface
- authoritative rendering services become evidentiary choke points

### 4. Reliance grant

The object begins gating money, access, eligibility, ranking, closure, underwriting, covenant calculation, or regulatory treatment.

Load-bearing questions:

- Who relied?
- For what purpose?
- Did the reliance right transfer to successors, lenders, reinsurers, assignees, or replacement counsel?
- What notice rights survive after the original transaction closes?

Related dossiers:

- reliance-scope matrices become procurement exhibits
- amendment-recipient registries become reliance-graph infrastructure
- historical-state views become buyer due-diligence surfaces
- legacy-status transfer attestations become closing artifacts

### 5. Recipient graph

Once reliance exists, later changes must route to the right people or systems.

Load-bearing questions:

- Which recipients deserve which class of notice?
- Does routing expose sensitive business relationships?
- What proves delivery without revealing the whole graph?
- How do expired recipients, delegated endpoints, and downstream forwarding rights behave?

Related dossiers:

- state-transition notice services become a quiet vendor market
- delivery attestations become an evidentiary service tier
- amendment-recipient registries become reliance-graph infrastructure
- recipient-graph privacy proofs become broker trust products

### 6. Dispute

A party challenges identity, source sufficiency, transformation, score, materiality, recipient scope, or reliance purpose.

Load-bearing questions:

- Who has standing?
- What evidence must be accepted?
- How does the system treat vendor silence, expired logs, inaccessible source systems, or conflicting source witnesses?
- What status attaches while the dispute is live?

Related dossiers:

- identity-match appeals become broker support queues
- complaint and appeal telemetry becomes an early-warning system
- source-witness nonresponse defaults become broker policy

### 7. Stay

A pending dispute does not always mean “delete” or “continue as normal.” It creates an interim reliance state.

Load-bearing questions:

- May automated use continue?
- Is the score excluded, provisional, or manual-review-only?
- Is eligibility suspended?
- Is money reserved?
- Who can override a stay and with what record?

Related dossiers:

- appeal-stay labels become reliance controls
- emergency-override constitutions become procurement questions
- proceed-before-convergence waivers become a standing dispute class

### 8. Correction and materiality

The dispute produces a result: verified, corrected, partially corrected, unverifiable, irrelevant, late, stale, out-of-scope, or unresolved. That result may or may not change the object's consequences.

Load-bearing questions:

- Was the correction merely hygiene, or did it change a threshold?
- Does it affect score, covenant, holdback, eligibility, or notice duty?
- Is the correction prospective only, or retroactive?
- Does it require restatement?

Related dossiers:

- split/merge correction notices become packet amendment events
- correction-materiality thresholds become packet boilerplate
- corrected-score restatements, already latent in the archive's lifecycle sequence

### 9. Non-reliance / supersession / retirement

Some prior objects become unsafe for specified purposes. They are not simply old. They are no longer admissible for certain reliance uses.

Load-bearing questions:

- Which version became non-reliable?
- For which reliance purpose and time interval?
- Who must be told?
- Are downstream decisions reopened, reserved, grandfathered, or left undisturbed?
- How long must the non-reliance history remain queryable?

Related dossiers:

- non-reliance packet states become version lifecycle controls
- regression-retirement markers become evidence hygiene
- machine-readable retirement notices become a buyer-control surface
- history-retention floors become procurement terms

- withdrawn
- non-reliance-pending
- non-reliance-final
- archive-only

## Why this model matters

The archive should now resist treating each new status as a separate revelation. The better test is whether the status fills a missing lifecycle stage or changes a major enforcement surface.

A new dossier in this family should be promoted only if it does at least one of the following:

1. introduces a missing lifecycle state;
2. identifies a new enforcement surface where existing states become consequential;
3. reveals an abuse path the lifecycle cannot yet handle;
4. preserves legitimacy by reducing over-disclosure, small-supplier burden, or appeal asymmetry;
5. imports the lifecycle into a major new domain, such as product passports, EUDR place-proof, cyber vulnerability enrichment, operational resilience, repair evidence, or grid-connection queues.
