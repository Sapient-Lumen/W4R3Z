# Preliminary-final-effective state machines become procurement calendars

## Core claim

Once consequential determinations are public, appealable, and commercially legible, the scarce object stops being the score alone and becomes the **state machine around the score**: draft, preliminary, proposed, appealed, final, effective, superseded. The institutions that matter most are the ones that can say **what a status may be used for at each state, when that state changes, and what obligations attach while the status is still contestable**.

## Why this belongs in the archive

The archive’s conditioned-place lane already runs through **institutional controls become a shadow zoning layer**, **restriction-search infrastructure becomes routine conveyancing**, **machine-readable restriction objects become transaction middleware**, **action-clearance objects become field-work middleware**, **replay-grade clearance logs become insurance evidence**, **source-snapshot escrow becomes liability-tail infrastructure**, **reliance-grade source attestations become a service tier**, **reliance-scope matrices become procurement exhibits**, **re-review trigger grammars become procurement language**, **emergency override constitutions become procurement questions**, **stale clearances split into distinct fault classes**, **replay-quality grades become underwriting inputs**, **source-drift warranties become contract language**, **override-misuse forensics becomes a standing dispute class**, **fault-class scoreboards become qualification filters**, and **scoreboard-appeal workflows become a governance service tier**. That sequence explains how a conditioned place becomes searchable, machine-checkable, replayable, rankable, and contestable. It still leaves one practical bottleneck under-described: **when is a status actually usable?**

EPA already exposes the shape of that bottleneck. ECHO’s release notes say recently reported data are treated as **draft** so regulated entities can identify possible errors before an official quarterly noncompliance status is calculated [S1178]. ECHO’s current water-search help says a permit-level CWA compliance status is generated after every quarter and then reviewed before becoming **official**, while the most recent quarter remains draft and not fully quality assured [S1179]. In other words, an important public compliance surface already depends on an explicit state machine rather than on one undifferentiated “current status.”

NRCS shows the same logic in land-affecting determinations. Its current conservation-compliance appeals page says initial determinations are issued as **Preliminary Technical Determinations** and become final after 30 days if not appealed [S1180]. That means usability is not attached only to the content of the determination; it is attached to the passage from preliminary to final.

FEMA makes the staged nature of consequential status even clearer. Its current flood-hazard-determination notices page says new or modified hazard determinations receive a **90-day appeal period** that begins after the required publications [S1181]. FEMA’s current Risk MAP project-lifecycle page says that after appeals are resolved, FEMA sends a **Letter of Final Determination**, and the new maps become effective six months later [S1182]. So one official map can be proposed, appealable, final, and effective at different moments. Those are not semantic niceties; they are operational and financial gates.

USACE shows the same state logic from the permitting side. Its current appeals pages say applicants and landowners may appeal certain jurisdictional determinations and permit decisions within 60 days, and accepted appeals can lead to site visits, appeal conferences, or remand for reconsideration [S1183]. This means the decision state is not just “issued” or “not issued.” It can be issued-yet-appealable, under review, affirmed, or remanded.

Taken together, these official materials suggest the next bottleneck above redress: **preliminary-final-effective state machines become procurement calendars**. Once a determination or score changes qualification, price, diligence, or work sequencing, another institution will increasingly ask not only *what is the status?* but *what state is that status in, when does it cut over, what can be done before the cutover, and what must be paused until effectiveness arrives?*

So this belongs in the archive because it names the institutional layer above appeals: **state-governed usability**. The actors who can label draft versus official, preliminary versus final, appealable versus effective, and then synchronize those transitions across procurement, underwriting, permitting, and field execution may increasingly matter as much as the parties who generated the underlying determination.

## Speculative consequences worth tracking

### 1. Status labels become commercially active

Draft, preliminary, appealed, final, and effective labels increasingly stop being informational footnotes and start changing whether bids can be submitted, policies bound, loans closed, or work released.

### 2. Procurement calendars begin keying off state transitions

Teams may increasingly plan around appeal-close dates, final-determination letters, official-quarter cutovers, and effectiveness windows rather than around raw publication dates.

### 3. “Use while contested” policies become boilerplate

Institutions may increasingly need explicit rules for whether a still-contestable score or determination can be used for screening, provisional pricing, conditional award, escrow holdback, or internal triage.

### 4. Transition synchronization becomes a service market

Vendors may increasingly sell monitored state transitions: draft-to-official notices, preliminary-to-final alerts, appeal-window countdowns, and effective-date synchronization across internal systems.

### 5. Status-misalignment disputes become a recurring liability class

A growing share of disputes may turn on whether a party relied on the right determination content but the wrong determination state — for example, treating preliminary as final, or final as already effective.

### 6. Dashboards start carrying usability metadata, not just values

A mature scoreboard may increasingly need fields for publication state, appealability, finality date, effective date, supersession risk, and permitted uses while a status remains unsettled.

### 7. Institutions begin comparing state-machine discipline itself

Buyers and regulators may increasingly compare providers and public systems by how clearly they label status transitions, how fast they propagate them, and how often downstream users are caught on the wrong side of a cutover.

## What could falsify or weaken the thesis

- Most users continue treating draft, preliminary, final, and effective distinctions as low-stakes formalities.
- Appeals, correction rights, and effective dates remain too slow or too rare to shape procurement or operational timing.
- Institutions simply wait for full finality rather than building explicit interim-use rules.
- Downstream systems never ingest or expose status-state metadata in a usable form.
- The main risk remains wrong content rather than wrong state, leaving state-machine discipline secondary.

## Research queue

- What is the minimum usability matrix for a contested status: screening, provisional pricing, conditional award, escrow holdback, internal triage, or full operational release?
- Who should operate the synchronization layer: source agency, dashboard operator, procurement platform, insurer, lender, or independent notice service?
- Which failure class dominates first: stale state labels, ambiguous cutover clocks, missing downstream propagation, or misuse of provisional determinations as final ones?
- Which metric becomes the first shorthand for state-machine quality: correction latency, cutover-notice latency, appeal-window visibility, effective-date propagation completeness, or provisional-use error rate?
