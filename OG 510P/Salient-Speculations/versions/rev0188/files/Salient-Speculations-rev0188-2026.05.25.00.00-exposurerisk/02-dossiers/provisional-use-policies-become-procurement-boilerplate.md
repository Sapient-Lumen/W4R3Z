# Provisional-use policies become procurement boilerplate

## Core claim

Once consequential determinations spend meaningful time in **draft, preliminary, advisory, administratively continued, appealed, or pre-effective** states, the scarce object stops being the determination alone and becomes the **policy that says what another institution may do with that state right now**. The actors that matter most are the ones that can say **which actions are allowed before full finality, which are forbidden, which are only advisory, which may continue under old authorization, and which require explicit temporary conditions or waivers**.

## Why this belongs in the archive

The archive’s conditioned-place lane already runs through **institutional controls become a shadow zoning layer**, **restriction-search infrastructure becomes routine conveyancing**, **machine-readable restriction objects become transaction middleware**, **action-clearance objects become field-work middleware**, **replay-grade clearance logs become insurance evidence**, **source-snapshot escrow becomes liability-tail infrastructure**, **reliance-grade source attestations become a service tier**, **reliance-scope matrices become procurement exhibits**, **re-review trigger grammars become procurement language**, **emergency override constitutions become procurement questions**, **stale clearances split into distinct fault classes**, **replay-quality grades become underwriting inputs**, **source-drift warranties become contract language**, **override-misuse forensics becomes a standing dispute class**, **fault-class scoreboards become qualification filters**, **scoreboard-appeal workflows become a governance service tier**, **preliminary-final-effective state machines become procurement calendars**, and **effective-date synchronization services become a workflow tier**. That sequence explains how a consequential status becomes searchable, stateful, synchronized, challengeable, and commercially time-sensitive. It still leaves one practical bottleneck under-described: **what are you actually allowed to do while the status is not fully settled?**

EPA’s current industrial-stormwater material shows one clear answer. The administratively continued 2021 MSGP remains in force for facilities that already had coverage before expiration, and those operators must continue complying with permit requirements [S1187]. But new facilities cannot obtain coverage under the administratively continued permit; instead, EPA says some may operate only under a No Action Assurance if they satisfy specified conditions, and even then EPA is explicit that the NAA does **not** confer permit coverage [S1187]. That is already a provisional-use policy: same regulatory neighborhood, different allowed actions by status class.

USACE shows the same structure in a different register. Its current Philadelphia District jurisdictional-determinations page says preliminary JDs are advisory, may not be appealed, and may be elected by a landowner or permit applicant who wants to move ahead expeditiously to obtain a Corps permit authorization [S1188]. In other words, a preliminary determination is neither useless nor fully dispositive. It supports a bounded class of action while explicitly setting aside the demand for a definitive jurisdictional answer.

NRCS adds another version of the same grammar. Its current conservation-compliance appeals page says initial determinations are issued as Preliminary Technical Determinations, become final after 30 days if not appealed, and may have appeal rights waived when a participant wants to address the concern immediately, such as by pursuing wetland mitigation, in order to expedite a Final Technical Determination [S1180]. So the system already distinguishes among waiting, contesting, and accelerating finality for the sake of specific next actions.

FEMA’s current preliminary-flood-product surfaces show the opposite side of the same logic. FEMA says preliminary flood hazard data give the public an early look at projected risk, are not final, are subject to change, and are presented as the best information available at the time [S1189] [S1190]. FEMA is also explicit that preliminary data are for review and guidance only and cannot be used to rate flood-insurance policies or enforce the federal mandatory purchase requirement [S1189]. This is not just a mapping caveat. It is a use policy attached to an interim state.

Taken together, these official materials suggest the next bottleneck above synchronization: **provisional-use policies become procurement boilerplate**. Once statuses spend real time in contestable or pre-effective states, institutions increasingly need reusable language for what counts as advisory use, conditional use, continued use, waived-appeal acceleration, grandfathered use, or prohibited use. The scarce competence becomes the ability to say **what may proceed under uncertainty without accidentally treating the provisional as final**.

So this belongs in the archive because it names the operational layer above state machines and synchronized cutovers: **interim admissibility design**. The actors who can turn a provisional state into a bounded, legible action policy may increasingly matter as much as the agencies that authored the underlying status.

## Speculative consequences worth tracking

### 1. Interim-use matrices become standard attachments

Institutions may increasingly attach explicit matrices saying whether a draft, preliminary, appealed, administratively continued, or pre-effective status supports screening, provisional pricing, conditional award, continued operations, field planning, or no action at all.

### 2. “Advisory only” becomes a commercially active label

A status that is visible but guidance-only may increasingly change internal planning, reserves, diligence sequencing, or engineering scoping while remaining unusable for binding pricing or mandatory legal consequences.

### 3. Grandfathering clauses become valuable governance objects

More money may turn on whether a party is allowed to continue under an older authorization, map, permit, or determination until a new effective date arrives.

### 4. Conditional-award workflows spread

Buyers, lenders, insurers, and permitting teams may increasingly use provisional states to issue conditional approvals that convert automatically, lapse, or reroute once finality or effectiveness is reached.

### 5. Waiver and acceleration rights gain strategic value

The ability to waive appeal rights, accept a preliminary determination for a limited purpose, or elect an interim path may increasingly become part of negotiation strategy rather than mere clerical process.

### 6. Misuse of provisional states becomes a recurring dispute class

More disputes may turn not on whether the underlying determination was wrong, but on whether someone treated advisory or conditional status as if it already carried final, binding effect.

### 7. Procurement begins asking for interim-state governance

Buyers may increasingly ask vendors and operators how they label provisional states, what actions are permitted before effectiveness, how grandfathering is tracked, and how conditional actions are later reconciled.

## What could falsify or weaken the thesis

- Most institutions simply wait for final and effective states, leaving little real use for interim-action policies.
- Provisional states remain short-lived enough that bespoke human judgment stays adequate.
- Agencies collapse more interim states into simpler final/not-final categories.
- The main failures continue to come from bad content or missed cutovers rather than from misuse of provisional states.
- Buyers and regulators avoid conditional action entirely rather than standardizing it.
