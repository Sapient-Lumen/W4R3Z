## rev0182 stressgraph note

This revision adds a counter-map across the constellations. The archive had become very good at naming proof objects, state labels, registries, and reliance controls. rev0182 adds the recurring ways those objects fail or create backlash:

- **graph poisoning** stresses identity, dependency, recipient, supplier, and model-context graphs;
- **resolver capture** stresses product passports, repair records, recall status, and registry lookup;
- **public proof-profile registries** make minimization and sufficiency operational rather than aspirational;
- **informal-market refuges** mark the point where evidence burden pushes activity out of formal channels;
- **redaction-boundary ledgers** add an anti-legibility pair for AI assurance;
- **recall-state propagation** converts product safety into a product-biography state.

Read these not as a new constellation but as a stress layer that should be applied to every managed-legibility and product-biography dossier.

## rev0180 schema-bones note

This revision adds a structural layer across the constellations rather than treating the newest dossiers as another narrow managed-legibility run. The five promoted dossiers should be read as corrective infrastructure:

- **source-witness nonresponse defaults** adds the missing evidentiary state for silence, refusal, expired retention, and unverifiable source facts;
- **recipient-graph privacy proofs** limits the archive's tendency to equate better governance with more visible graphs;
- **non-reliance packet states** completes the reliance-object lifecycle by distinguishing ordinary supersession from purpose-specific inadmissibility;
- **compliance-object forgery** adds the adversarial layer created when proof objects gate access;
- **data-minimization proofs** turns privacy-preserving sufficiency into an enforceable surface rather than an afterthought.

Together they keep the archive's center of gravity on bottleneck migration while forcing each new bottleneck through abuse, burden, privacy, and state-transition tests.

## How to use this file

When considering a new speculation, first ask:

1. Which constellation does it belong to?
2. Is it actually a new front, or only a local example of an existing pattern?
3. Does it reveal a missing sector inside a known constellation?
4. Can it be compressed as a bottleneck shift rather than a trend report?

A good next addition usually does **one** of two things:

- reveals a genuinely new constellation; or
- makes an existing constellation legible in a major new domain.
