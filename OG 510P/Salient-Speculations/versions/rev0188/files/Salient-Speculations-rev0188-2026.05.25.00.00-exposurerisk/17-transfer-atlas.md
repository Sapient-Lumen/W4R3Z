# Transfer Atlas

The datacube becomes more useful when a pattern discovered in one domain can be tested in another. This file maps recurring institutional patterns and the domains where they may transfer.

## Transfer patterns

| Source pattern | Strong current domain | Transfer target | What to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Status afterlife | vulnerability records, certificates, product recalls | AI model cards, repair records, data-space permissions | Does old status remain relied on after correction? |
| Resolver capture | product passports, content credentials | data spaces, identity wallets, software registries | Who controls the pointer between subject and current state? |
| Queue position as asset | grid interconnection, notified-body review | permits, immigration, grants, compute allocation | Can queue state be financed, traded, gamed, or appealed? |
| Selective disclosure sufficiency | identity wallets, credentials | procurement, data spaces, media provenance, recipient graphs | Who defines enough proof and forbidden over-collection? |
| Source-witness nonresponse | packet appeals, incident reporting | AI audits, repair disputes, medical-device certificates | What happens when the only actor with evidence is silent? |
| Public profile registries | VC verifier profiles, conformity profiles | data spaces, AI provenance labels, repair rights | Are verifier demands standardized before they become coercive? |
| Forged admissibility | passports, certificates, attestations | crypto readiness, AI documentation, data-space participation | What prevents a false proof from unlocking access? |
| File-readiness scores | notified-body assessment | permits, grants, litigation, insurance claims | Does evidence quality become a pre-market asset? |
| Nonparticipation labels | media provenance | data spaces, wallets, ESG evidence, repair histories | When is absence neutral, suspicious, privacy-preserving, or disqualifying? |
| Authority logs | AI agents, delegated representation | care proxies, enterprise bots, public-benefit representatives | What binds action to delegated scope and redress? |

## How to test a transfer

A transfer is worth a dossier only when at least three conditions hold:

1. the target domain has a relying actor;
2. the target domain has a repeatable artifact or state label;
3. absence, staleness, or fraud changes money, permission, liability, or ranking;
4. the artifact has afterlife after correction, appeal, revocation, or supersession;
5. one group is burdened by evidence requirements.

If a pattern transfers only metaphorically, keep it in this atlas instead of promoting it.

## High-value transfer candidates

- **Crypto agility → archival evidence**: old signatures and proof bundles need re-sealing after algorithms lose trust.
- **Notified-body queues → AI conformity assessment**: future AI assessor capacity may become a market-access chokepoint.
- **Content provenance absence → product-passport absence**: lack of a proof object may need typed reasons rather than automatic suspicion.
- **Agent authority logs → delegated civic representation**: AI-assisted casework may require scoped authority and replayable action receipts.
- **Data-space access profiles → small-supplier evidence brokers**: participation proofs may become trade infrastructure.
- **Recall-state propagation → vulnerability non-reliance states**: both require status to travel into downstream resale/use decisions.
