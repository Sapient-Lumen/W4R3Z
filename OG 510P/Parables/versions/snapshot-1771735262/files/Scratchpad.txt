# Scratchpad

## Feb 21, 2026
- Web search surfaced many pages that simply restated biblical parables; skipped those as “copies.”
- Shortlist candidates (not added to Parables.txt):
  - Anthony de Mello: “More Precious Than Diamonds” (wise woman gives a stone; traveler returns seeking the inner freedom that made generosity effortless).
  - Anthony de Mello: “Prayer is Fire” (inventor of fire; people build rituals and relics but forget the actual practice of making fire).
- Chosen for Parables.txt: the “Paco / Hotel Montana” forgiveness story—simple, merciful, and startlingly universal.
- Added one original kindness parable to AI-Parables.txt.

## Feb 21, 2026 (continued)
- Considered two widely-circulated “modern parables” (Bridge Builder; Tolstoy’s “Three Questions”) but held them back this round to avoid adding what felt too common.
- Found a standout: “The Rabbi’s Gift” (Francis Dorff, 1979; popularized via M. Scott Peck). It feels Jesus-adjacent without copying a Gospel parable: the twist is not *who* the Messiah is, but what happens when people treat one another as if he could be.
- Added a Jesus-like retelling to Parables.txt as “The Rabbi in the Woods.”

## Feb 21, 2026 (continued — round)
- New web sweep focused on: folk/Hasidic/Sufi/modern “parable” retellings (filtered out anything that was simply a Bible-parable recap).
- Considered but did NOT add:
  - “Grandfather’s Wooden Bowl” (strong but leans toward consequence/karma more than mercy).
  - “The Cracked Pot” (lovely, but too ubiquitous; felt less “rare keep”).
  - “Smell of Soup / Sound of Money” (clever justice tale, but the tone can read as mockery more than compassion).
  - “The Man and the Birds” (incarnation allegory; more doctrinal than parabolic).
- Kept: the “two brothers secretly carrying grain” Jerusalem legend — clean, self-forgetful love, mutual honor, no humiliation, no scapegoat.
## Feb 21, 2026 (later)
- New URLs checked included a mixed bag: some sermon commentary, some social media reposts, some genuinely old wisdom tales.
- Considered but did not keep:
  - Ohr Somayach “Blessings in Disguise” (splinter prevents voyage; ship sinks) — true, but felt more like a proverb-illustration than a parable that pierces the heart.
  - The “Cracked Pot” — tender, but extremely ubiquitous and a bit too on-the-nose for this collection.
  - Nasrudin “Duck Soup” — funny and sharp, but the moral edge leans toward ridicule.
- Kept: Desert Fathers story of Macarius and the “Gift of Grapes” — self-forgetful generosity without judgment, performed quietly, returning as shared joy.
