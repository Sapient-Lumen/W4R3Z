# Family Triage

This is a coarse family-level map for conservative review.
It is **not** a publishability verdict for any individual paper.
It only tells a future operator where release regret is likely to be low or high.

## Legacy published backbone

- The five Mathematics-era canonical wiki links are already public.
- They remain valid and are outside the new-release naming decision.

## Standalone-series families: lower-regret first review targets

These families look more like self-contained paper groups than like an actively splitting release-maintenance tail:

- `series/anonymity_series/`
- `series/anondht_state_series/`
- `series/bossfight_series/`
- `series/certified_series/`
- `series/congestion_series/`
- `series/evaluation_series/`
- `series/operational_series/`
- `series/release_and_destination/`

That does **not** mean they are publishable now.
It means they are the safest place to begin paper-level review because they are less obviously entangled with the most active decomposition zone.

## Early synthesis foundations: important, but not first public freezes

Early synthesis papers (roughly `paper0` through `paper30`) appear to define interfaces, schemas, bridges, and explanatory spine pieces.
They may be central to internal coherence while still being poor first public entrypoints.
Review them after a future turn has oriented on the standalone families.

## Late synthesis tail: defer by default

The patch notes show repeated recent extension of the late synthesis successor/reissue machinery, including papers in the `paper65` through `paper75` range.
The worked example (`paper17`) was also repeatedly updated alongside this tail.
That is a classic high-churn zone and should be treated as default-hold territory unless a future turn records a clear stabilization reason.

## Crosswalk and index notes

Crosswalk, index, and explanation notes may be essential for maintainers without being the right public release units.
Review them for orientation, citation discipline, and dependency understanding before treating them as publication candidates.
