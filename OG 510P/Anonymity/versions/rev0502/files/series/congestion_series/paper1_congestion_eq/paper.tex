\begin{abstract}
Anonymous overlays and anonymous DHT lookup compilers typically treat \emph{destination/contact traces} as the dominant metadata channel, and then equalize or hide those traces via scheduling, padding, and randomized delegation.
This paper isolates a distinct leakage tier: \emph{congestion observables} (queueing delay, RTT, loss/ECN marks, and their time series), which can amplify tiny secret-dependent biases across repeated operations.
We propose \emph{Congestion-EQ}, a history-conditioned total-variation (TV) contract that treats congestion as an explicit channel in the compiler calculus.
We derive slack-sensitivity laws in the M/M/1 setting, give a compositional contract with an accountant for repeats/resets/public randomness, and connect the contract to an Anonymous-DHT microstudy.
Additional robustness microstudies (heavy-tailed service and bursty/correlated arrivals, including a DHT-like fanout+retry burst model) are summarized in Section~\ref{sec:congeq_robust}; full plots and scripts are available on request.
This paper is the congestion-family theorem/accountant entrypoint. It owns only the congestion witness contract, its declared budget/accountant packet, and the minimal analytic evidence needed to size that packet; PSC-Q, the PSC-Q addendum, and W-Congestion-EQ remain downstream mechanism, supporting-evidence, and verifier-bundle companions rather than prerequisites.\cite{series:pscq,series:pscqaddendum,series:wcongeq}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Prior work in this series develops total-variation (TV) contracts and certificates for metadata-hiding overlay lookups, focusing on destination/contact traces and termination-time leakage \cite{series:schedcompiler,series:knapsacktransport}.
This paper adds a distinct leakage tier: \emph{congestion observables} (queueing delay, RTT, loss/ECN marks, and their time series) that can remain informative even when contact traces are equalized.

\paragraph{Series map.}
Paper~I (this paper) is the stable congestion-family entrypoint: it defines the Congestion-EQ TV contract and an explicit accountant for repetition and reset.
Paper~II, PSC-Q \cite{series:pscq}, is the mechanism companion that explains how a compiler can try to meet those budgets without driving utilization to saturation.
Paper~IIA, the PSC-Q addendum \cite{series:pscqaddendum}, is an optional supporting-evidence packet rather than part of the theorem root.
Paper~III, W-Congestion-EQ \cite{series:wcongeq}, is the downstream verifier-bundle companion for those knob choices and budgets.

\paragraph{Why congestion is different.}
Congestion is nonlinear in load, stateful, and naturally observed.
Congestion/latency side channels have long been studied in Tor and related systems, from early circuit-clogging latency probing \cite{murdoch_danezis2005,latencyleak,congestion_longpaths} to congestion-control and padding interactions \cite{defenestrator,tor324}; fully remote latency side channels \cite{snailload,zeroclicksnailload} and RTT-aware trace transduction \cite{cellshift2026} further widen the attacker surface.
Congestion-control dynamics are themselves measurable: recent tools infer deployed congestion-control algorithms from queue/RTT traces at scale \cite{nebby2024,ccanalyzer2024}, and custom congestion control in circumvention settings can be fingerprintable \cite{customcc_foci2025}.
Complementary defenses use principled traffic shaping: NetShaper provides differential-privacy (DP) guarantees while adapting shaping decisions to workload and congestion conditions \cite{netshaper2024}; Pacer provides an end-to-end systems mitigation for packet-timing side channels that explicitly respects flow control, congestion control, and loss recovery \cite{pacer2022}. 
As slack shrinks, small secret-dependent load biases can cause large shifts in delay/loss distributions (queue timing channels) \cite{bitsqueues,gong2014timing,beams2021}.
Under repetition, even tiny per-operation advantages can amplify, motivating explicit accounting (Section~\ref{sec:amplification}).

\paragraph{Anonymous-DHT context.}
Congestion-EQ is complementary to lookup-path anonymity and query privacy in DHT ecosystems (e.g., Octopus and robust-DHT query privacy; PIR-style private routing for IPFS) \cite{octopus,add_query_privacy,peer2pir}.
Most deployed routing follows Kademlia \cite{kademlia}; recent work studies lightweight obfuscation and hashing-based variants in Kademlia/IPFS ecosystems \cite{kademlia_obfuscation,ipfs_triplehash,bitswap_randomwalks}.
Structured P2P anonymity systems built atop DHT-style routing and random-walk delegation (e.g., Salsa and ShadowWalker) and their lookup leakage tradeoffs have also been analyzed \cite{salsa,shadowwalker,mittal_infoleaks2012}.
Broader ``anonymous lookup'' design space (including DHT-based relay discovery pitfalls) is surveyed in classic work \cite{morselli2006_lookup,wang_mittal_borisov2010_lookup}.
Deployed low-latency anonymity systems also maintain DHT-like directories (e.g., I2P's netDB); measurement and attack work on those directories further motivates treating congestion and directory-probing load as first-class leakage surfaces \cite{egger_i2p2013,timpanaro_netdb2015}.
Recent IPFS ecosystem studies also highlight anonymity/abuse pressures tied to provider records and gateway/pinning service surfaces \cite{ipfs_abuse2025}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item We formalize congestion observables as a separate leakage tier and define a TV contract (Congestion-EQ) that composes with the series' lookup-compiler calculus.
\item We derive analytic structure for the M/M/1 waiting-time witness and connect it to slack sensitivity, including evidence that epochs must lengthen (or flushes must be stronger) as slack shrinks.
\item We give compositional rules for multi-hop stages and for epoch/repeat accounting, with an ``odometer'' view designed for certificate binding \cite{series:wcongeq}.
\item We connect the contract to an Anonymous-DHT microstudy and provide robustness evidence beyond M/M/1 (heavy-tailed service, bursty/clustered arrivals, and binary loss/ECN witnesses including finite-buffer dependence).
\end{itemize}

\section{Model and leakage tier}
We assume an overlay lookup compiler that maps a high-level lookup request to a randomized schedule of network actions.
Prior papers in this series treat destination/contact traces as the primary channel; here we define a separate congestion channel.

\paragraph{Adjacency.}
We inherit the series' adjacency relation on secrets: typically two secrets $s,s'$ differ by one lookup target/key or by one membership bit in a declared neighboring set, and the contract must hold uniformly over that adjacency class \cite{series:schedcompiler}.
Congestion-EQ does not require a particular adjacency definition, but it does require that the deployment and certificate bind the intended adjacency semantics so that budgets are comparable across releases.

\begin{definition}[Congestion observables]
Fix an epoch and an observation function producing a random object $Z$ from runtime behavior, e.g., an RTT time series, a queueing-delay sample stream, ECN/loss marks, or summary statistics.
Given two adjacent secrets $s,s'$ and any fixed public randomness, the induced laws are $\mathcal{L}(Z\mid s)$ and $\mathcal{L}(Z\mid s')$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Congestion-EQ contract (TV form)]
A compiler instance satisfies \(\eps\)-\emph{Congestion-EQ} for a specified congestion witness family if for all adjacent secrets \(s,s'\),
\[
\TV\big(\mathcal{L}(Z\mid s),\mathcal{L}(Z\mid s')\big) \le \eps\quad (\text{per operation, per epoch}),
\]
\noindent together with an explicit accounting rule describing (i) how \(\eps\) composes under repeats, (ii) how it resets under declared flushes, and (iii) how public randomness is exposed to both adversary and verifier.
\end{definition}

\section{Amplification: small bias, big leak}\label{sec:amplification}
We use TV because (i) it upper-bounds the bias of \emph{any} (possibly randomized) test, and (ii) it composes cleanly.
Concretely, for any measurable test $\varphi(Z)\in[0,1]$,
\[
\big|\E[\varphi(Z)\mid s]-\E[\varphi(Z)\mid s']\big| \le \TV\big(\mathcal{L}(Z\mid s),\mathcal{L}(Z\mid s')\big) \le \eps,
\]
so under equal priors the best single-shot success probability is at most $(1+\eps)/2$ (standard TV characterizations; see, e.g., \cite{levin_peres_wilmer}).
Under $m$ independent repetitions (e.g., i.i.d. witnesses), TV can grow quickly: for product laws $P^{\otimes m},Q^{\otimes m}$ with $\TV(P,Q)\le \eps$, a coupling argument gives
\[\TV(P^{\otimes m},Q^{\otimes m}) \le 1-(1-\eps)^m \le m\eps,\]
so distinguishability scales roughly linearly for small $\eps$ and then saturates near 1.
This motivates explicit accounting rather than informal ``small'' claims.

\begin{remark}[Dependence and stateful queues]
Congestion witnesses are often \emph{not} independent across operations: queue state and congestion control create history dependence.
In this regime, the product-law bound above is only a heuristic; Congestion-EQ instead uses the history-conditioned epoch telescoping rule (Proposition~\ref{prop:epochcomp}) to account across repeats without assuming independence.
\end{remark}

\section{Slack sensitivity in M/M/1}
In a basic M/M/1 abstraction of the congestion witness (queueing delay), the waiting-time law changes sharply with utilization $\rho$.

\begin{lemma}[M/M/1 waiting-time witness]\label{lem:mm1}
For an M/M/1 queue with arrival rate $\lambda$, service rate $\mu$ and $\rho=\lambda/\mu<1$, the steady-state waiting time $W$ of an arrival satisfies
$\Prb[W=0]=1-\rho$ and, for $t>0$, $\Prb[W>t]=\rho e^{-(\mu-\lambda)t}$.
Thus $W$ is a point mass at $0$ plus an exponential tail with rate $\mu-\lambda=\mu(1-\rho)$.
A small fractional load shift $\lambda\mapsto (1+\eta)\lambda$ changes the exponential rate by
$\Delta(\mu-\lambda)= -\eta\mu\rho$, so the \emph{relative} change in the tail parameter scales like $\eta\rho/(1-\rho)$ and blows up as $\rho\to 1$.
See, e.g., standard queueing references \cite{kendall1953,asmussen2003}.
\end{lemma}

\section{Other congestion witnesses: threshold marks and loss/ECN}
Delay samples are not the only congestion observable.
Many transports expose binary congestion signals (loss, ECN marks, CoDel/PIE-style marking, queue-length thresholds, etc.).
Even coarse signals can leak: for M/M/1 in steady state, the queue-length tail satisfies $\Prb[L\ge T]=\rho^T$.
If a public marking rule emits \emph{mark}$=\mathbf{1}\{L\ge T\}$, then the induced witness is Bernoulli with parameter $p(\rho)=\rho^T$.
For adjacent secrets modeled as a small multiplicative load change $\rho\mapsto (1+\eta)\rho$, the single-sample TV is simply $|p(\rho)-p((1+\eta)\rho)|$.

\subsection{Finite buffers: loss probability and loss/mark streams}
Many real systems have finite buffers, and loss itself is observable.
In an M/M/1/K loss system with capacity $K$ (including service), the steady-state occupancy satisfies
$p_n \propto \rho^n$ with $\rho=\lambda/\mu$, so the blocking probability is
\[p_{\mathrm{block}}(\rho)=p_K(\rho)=\frac{(1-\rho)\,\rho^K}{1-\rho^{K+1}}\quad (\rho\neq 1),\qquad p_K(1)=\frac{1}{K+1}.\]
See standard queueing texts for M/M/1/K blocking formulas and PASTA (Poisson Arrivals See Time Averages) \cite{gross_harris,asmussen2003,wolff_pasta1982}.
By PASTA, an arriving job is blocked with this probability.
Thus a one-bit loss witness $B\in\{0,1\}$ has single-sample TV
$\TV(B)=|p_K(\rho_0)-p_K(\rho_1)|$ under adjacent hypotheses.
Fig.~\ref{fig:mm1k} shows that finite-buffer loss becomes highly sensitive as $\rho$ increases and $K$ grows.
Even if a single arrival is only weakly informative, repeated loss/mark samples can amplify advantage, motivating explicit accounting for 
\emph{loss/ECN mark streams} as a congestion witness family.

\section{Queue-TV stability, composition, and an accountant}
Congestion-EQ must support composition across \emph{time} (repeats/epochs) and across \emph{stages} (multi-hop overlays).

A principled way to select epoch length and reset/flush rules is to combine empirical "forgetting" curves (Fig.~\ref{fig:forgetting}) with conservative stability bounds for the underlying queue-state Markov process.
Perturbation theory for uniformly ergodic Markov chains provides TV-distance bounds under small kernel changes (e.g., load drift), making explicit how mixing rate controls how quickly secret-dependent state differences can persist across epochs \cite{mitrophanov2005}; see also Levin--Peres--Wilmer for standard coupling/TV mixing background \cite{levin_peres_wilmer}.
We use this as a justification for exporting epoch parameters as compiler outputs that can be audited and re-tuned.

In general, if a witness is assembled from multiple components (e.g., per-hop delay streams or end-to-end RTT), then TV admits an additive upper bound that is tight in the worst case.

\begin{proposition}[Stage composition (history-conditioned TV)]
Let $Z_1,Z_2$ be congestion witnesses for two sequential stages (e.g., two hops in a circuit) under adjacent secrets $s,s'$.
Assume $\TV(\mathcal{L}(Z_1\mid s),\mathcal{L}(Z_1\mid s'))\le \eps_1$ and, for every realized $z_1$,
\[
\TV(\mathcal{L}(Z_2\mid Z_1=z_1,s),\mathcal{L}(Z_2\mid Z_1=z_1,s'))\le \eps_2.
\]
Then for the concatenated witness $Z_{1:2}=(Z_1,Z_2)$,
\[
\TV(\mathcal{L}(Z_{1:2}\mid s),\mathcal{L}(Z_{1:2}\mid s')) \le \eps_1+\eps_2.
\]
Moreover, any measurable function of $Z_{1:2}$ (e.g., an end-to-end RTT/sojourn witness) has TV at most $\eps_1+\eps_2$ by data processing.

\begin{proof}
Write $P$ and $Q$ for the joint laws of $(Z_1,Z_2)$ under $s$ and $s'$.
By a standard telescoping/coupling argument,
\[
\TV(P,Q)\le \TV(P_{Z_1},Q_{Z_1}) + \sup_{z_1}\TV(P_{Z_2\mid Z_1=z_1},Q_{Z_2\mid Z_1=z_1}),
\]
and the assumed bounds give the claim.
Data processing gives the final statement.
\end{proof}
\end{proposition}

Congestion-EQ is intended as a \emph{contract} between compiler and deployment: the compiler exposes knobs (calendar period, dither, substitution rules, flush strength), while the deployment provisions slack.
The research archive contains a compositional rule set and an additive ``odometer'' accountant for repeats/resets.

\begin{proposition}[Epoch/repeat composition (history-conditioned TV)]\label{prop:epochcomp}
Let operations be partitioned into epochs $e=1,\dots,E$ and let $H_{e-1}$ denote the public transcript and any verifier-visible history up to the start of epoch $e$.
Assume a history-conditioned per-epoch bound: for every history value $h$,
\[
\TV\big(\mathcal{L}(Z_e\mid H_{e-1}=h, s),\;\mathcal{L}(Z_e\mid H_{e-1}=h, s')\big) \le \eps_e.
\]
Then the full witness $Z_{1:E}$ satisfies
\[
\TV(\mathcal{L}(Z_{1:E}\mid s),\mathcal{L}(Z_{1:E}\mid s')) \le \sum_{e=1}^E \eps_e.
\]
This bound does not require independence across epochs; it is designed for stateful congestion processes where the auditor conditions on published epoch boundaries and public randomness.

\begin{proof}
Iterate the telescoping argument over epochs, conditioning on $H_{e-1}$ at each step.
\end{proof}
\end{proposition}

\section{AnonDHT mapping: destination-by-congestion gap}
Anonymous DHT lookups can equalize destination/contact traces via delegation, scheduling, and termination-time hiding (series prerequisite).
However, congestion witnesses can still correlate with lookup paths, bucket choices, or subtle load differences.
This is largely orthogonal to 
lookup-privacy mechanisms that hide or obfuscate query keys and provider discovery (e.g., Octopus and robust-DHT query privacy),
as well as query-obfuscation hardening of Kademlia and PIR-style private content discovery for IPFS \cite{octopus,add_query_privacy,kademlia_obfuscation,peer2pir,ipfs_triplehash}.
Routing attacks (e.g., routing-table poisoning and eclipse/Sybil-style disruption) can also \emph{induce} secret-correlated load skews in DHT-like ecosystems, making congestion witnesses easier to exploit \cite{ipfs_totaleclipse,singh_eclipse2006,wang_sybil_bittorrent2012}.

\section{Limitations and scope}
This paper intentionally isolates a TV-tier contract.
An adversary may also use other statistics (e.g., spectral/periodicity tests) when calendars are visible; those are treated as additional tiers in the mechanism paper and in the series' spectral-tier analysis \cite{series:spectral}.
A passing Congestion-EQ packet therefore means only that the declared congestion witness family is bounded under the stated assumptions; it is not, by itself, a proof that the deployment chose good knobs or published the right certificate/receipt wrapper.\cite{series:pscq,series:wcongeq}

\section{Conclusion}
Congestion observables are a powerful and distinct metadata channel.
Congestion-EQ provides a practical TV-based contract that integrates with compiler calculus work and supports verifiable accounting.


\section{Robustness and two-hop microstudies (summary; plots on request)}\label{sec:congeq_robust}
The main text uses an $M/M/1$ slack-sensitivity calculation as the cleanest analytic ``unit test.''
In the original split archive, we also included small robustness microstudies (not shipped in this source-only bundle) to check that the phenomenon is not an artifact of memoryless assumptions.
We summarize the takeaways here.

\paragraph{Service-time robustness (M/G/1).}
With heavy-tailed service (e.g., lognormal with the same mean but larger variance), the qualitative behavior persists:
for a fixed \emph{fractional} load shift (e.g., a 1\% increase in effective arrival rate), the induced change in the waiting-time law grows rapidly as slack $1-\rho$ shrinks.
Intuitively, in the Pollaczek--Khinchine picture the waiting-time tail is controlled by moments of the service law and the gap to saturation; when $1-\rho$ is small, even tiny parameter drift produces a large change in the stationary delay distribution.

\paragraph{Arrival-process robustness (bursty/on--off).}
With bursty arrivals (two-state on/off or MMPP-style models), slack sensitivity can be \emph{worse}:
parallel fanout, clustered retries, and correlated request phases create higher short-term utilization spikes, which amplify delay differences that are invisible to mean-rate models.
Operationally, this means a congestion contract should be validated under \emph{burst} models, not only under Poisson baselines.

\paragraph{Two-hop (stage) composition.}
We stated an additive stage bound for end-to-end TVs.
Microstudies on two-hop pipelines (including clustered-burst arrivals) were consistent with the bound:
end-to-end TV stayed below the per-hop TV sum, and the bound became closer to tight when both hops operated near the slack ``knee'' and perturbations were aligned.

\paragraph{Loss/mark witnesses (finite buffers): dependence and window statistics.}
In the split archive, we also evaluated one-bit congestion witnesses such as loss and ECN marks under finite buffers (e.g., M/M/1/K).
A key practical point is that these streams are \emph{dependent}: once the buffer is full, consecutive drops can occur, and burst/run statistics become a witness on their own.
Windowed summaries (e.g., loss-count-in-window and max-run-length-in-window) can amplify faster than i.i.d. Bernoulli sanity checks suggest.
Operationally, if loss/mark streams are within scope, budget them explicitly and publish monitoring summaries that include dependence-sensitive diagnostics (not only mean loss).

\section{Takeaways}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Congestion is a witness:} RTT/queueing delay/loss/ECN marks can carry metadata even when payloads are encrypted; treat them as explicit transcript projections with budgets.
\item \textbf{Congestion-EQ exports a budget/accountant packet:} a TV-style distance on congestion traces plus the declared witness/load-envelope fields and composition rules needed to audit repeated queries and windows.
\item \textbf{Mechanisms and certificates live elsewhere:} use PSC-Q for compiler-friendly mechanisms that meet Congestion-EQ budgets; use W-Congestion-EQ for proof-carrying certificates, verifier checks, and transparency anchoring.
\item \textbf{Non-redundancy:} later papers should cite Congestion-EQ for witness definitions and budgets rather than re-defining ``delay leakage'' locally.
\end{itemize}
