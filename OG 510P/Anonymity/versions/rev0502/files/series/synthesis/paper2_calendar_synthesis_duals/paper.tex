\begin{abstract}
PSC-Q compilers choose public service calendars (slot width, renewal family, declared dither) so congestion witnesses meet a TV-tier contract.
Separately, the published ``poset-feasible padding'' papers give dual certificates for optimal padding/scheduling under total-variation privacy constraints.
This note connects the two views: PSC-Q knob selection can be phrased as a poset-feasible padding problem on a discretized time grid,
so a compiler can emit an \emph{auditable dual certificate} alongside its calendar choice.
The result is practical cross-pollination: PSC-Q becomes easier to audit (a certificate explains \emph{why} the chosen calendar is close to optimal),
and the dual-certificate machinery gains a concrete deployment target (calendar compilation under load envelopes).
\end{abstract}
\paragraph{Scope.}
This is a short bridge note: it rephrases \emph{calendar synthesis} (PSC-Q-style compilers) as an instance of \emph{poset-feasible padding} so that deployments can publish compact \emph{dual certificates} for their knob choices.

\section{Motivation: ``knobs'' should come with a proof artifact}
PSC-Q (mechanism paper) treats $(\delta,\alpha)$ and a calendar family as compiler-visible knobs.
W-Congestion-EQ (assurance paper) argues that these knobs should be receipt-visible.
What remains is a compact argument that the chosen knobs are \emph{reasonable}---not merely an arbitrary hand-tuned point.
Dual certificates are the natural proof artifact: they provide lower bounds and optimality gaps without disclosing private traces.

\section{A minimal discretized model}
We intentionally keep the model small; full details live in the published TV-certificate papers.

\paragraph{Slots and service opportunities.}
Fix a slot width $\delta>0$ and consider a horizon of $T$ slots.
A public calendar specifies which slot indices are \emph{eligible} for service (and optionally a declared dither distribution for timestamps).
PSC-Q's ``substitution'' discipline can be idealized as: in an eligible slot, if the real queue is empty, the implementation may send cover;
otherwise it serves real work.

\paragraph{Witness family.}
Let $W$ denote a congestion witness (e.g., an RTT time series summary) extracted from a window of slots.
Congestion-EQ asks for a TV-tier bound $\TV(\mathcal{L}(W\mid s),\mathcal{L}(W\mid s'))\le \eps$ under a declared load envelope and auditor conditioning.

\paragraph{Compiler objective.}
A simple objective is to minimize overhead subject to meeting the witness contract.
For calendar families this is often ``minimize eligible-slot rate'' (equivalently maximize slack) while meeting the bound for all $\rho\in[\rho_{\min},\rho_{\max}]$.

\section{Poset-feasible padding view (informal mapping)}
The poset-feasible padding papers model \emph{admissible transformations} of observable traces as a partial order (a poset) and study TV privacy under constraints.
In our discretized calendar view:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item The observable trace is the slot-indexed service pattern (and any declared timestamp perturbation).
\item ``Feasibility'' constraints are monotone: you may \emph{delay} real work within an allowed window (scheduler constraints), and you may \emph{fill} idle slots with cover, but you may not serve cover in a way that delays real work.
\item The partial order captures allowed reschedulings and cover insertions consistent with the calendar and the substitution discipline.
\end{itemize}
Under this mapping, choosing a calendar family and parameters is choosing a feasible set in which padding/rescheduling lives.

\section{Dual certificates: what the compiler can publish}
The published knapsack/transport paper shows that for TV-privacy constraints over feasible transformations, one can construct dual objects
that certify lower bounds and, in many cases, optimality of a candidate mechanism.
We do \emph{not} reprove those theorems here; we only state the interface that matters for PSC-Q.

\begin{theorem}[Certificate interface (informal)]
Assume a PSC-Q compiler instantiates a discretized feasible set (poset constraints) and solves an optimization problem
``minimize overhead subject to $\TV$-tier constraints'' on a finite witness grid.
Then there exists a dual object (a set of potentials / transport dual variables) that:
(i) lower-bounds the achievable overhead for the declared constraints, and
(ii) yields an optimality gap for the compiler's chosen knobs.
Publishing this dual object (or a commitment to it) enables third-party auditors to check the gap without access to private traces.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{What to put in a receipt.}
A minimal ``calendar compilation receipt'' can include:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item calendar family identifier + parameters (e.g., renewal family, $\delta$, dither fraction $\alpha$),
\item declared load envelope $[\rho_{\min},\rho_{\max}]$ used for compilation,
\item a witness-grid identifier (which Congestion-EQ witness summaries were constrained),
\item a dual-certificate hash (commitment) and a short verifier that checks dual feasibility and reports the optimality gap.
\end{itemize}
This fits naturally into W-Congestion-EQ style receipts and transparency anchoring.

\section{Practical cross-pollination opportunities}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{From TV-certs to PSC-Q:} use dual gaps to choose between calendar families (periodic vs renewal, different dither regimes) without ad hoc tuning.
\item \textbf{From PSC-Q to TV-certs:} use ``interval-robust compilation'' as a standard way to define the constraint set (compile for $\rho_{\max}$ and monitor drift).
\item \textbf{From duals to monitoring:} when monitors observe regime drift, they can demand a new compilation receipt; the old dual certificate pins down how much slack was assumed.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}
This note is a bridge: it does not claim that a simple discretization captures all congestion witnesses,
and it does not replace the detailed proofs in the published poset/transport papers.
The value is the \emph{interface}: treating calendar synthesis as a certificate-emitting optimization problem.
