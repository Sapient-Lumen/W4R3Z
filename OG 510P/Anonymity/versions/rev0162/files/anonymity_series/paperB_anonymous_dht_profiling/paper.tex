\title{Anonymous DHT Profiling and the Equalization Contract\\
\large Turning transcript skews into actionable odds\,--\,inflation and identification budgets}
\author{}
\date{March 4, 2026 (archive v158)}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Anonymous DHT lookups are routinely ``anonymized'' by delegation (random walks), split queries, cover traffic, and padding.
Yet practical deployments still enable \emph{profiling}: a passive observer (or a small set of relays) aggregates many lookup transcripts and infers which keys, content IDs, or destinations a user is querying.

This note makes profiling \emph{budgetable}.
We introduce an \emph{equalization contract}: a deployment promises that a chosen transcript projection (contacts, timing buckets (bucket design: Synthesis~27)\cite{series:bucketdesign}, retry patterns, congestion witnesses, \dots) is nearly key\,--\,independent.
Using the series' odds\,--\,inflation bridge, we show how such skews compose over repeated lookups into explicit multiplicative posterior bounds and into identification sizing rules.
The point is operational: an implementer can (i) decide \emph{what} to equalize, (ii) certify the contract on logs, and (iii) publish a compact ``privacy label'' that downstream consumers can reason about.
\end{abstract}


\paragraph{Scope.}
We formalize a deployable \emph{equalization contract} for a chosen transcript projection $X=\pi(T)$:
across keys/classes $k\in\mathcal K$, the law of $X$ is nearly key-independent in a max-divergence sense.
The contract is designed to feed directly into odds-inflation / MaxL endpoints and to compose over a declared repetition window.

\section{Profiling as repeated testing}
A structured overlay lookup (e.g., Kademlia\cite{kademlia}) induces an observable transcript: which peers are contacted, message sizes, timing buckets, abort/retry decisions, congestion witnesses, and any public control-plane beacons.
Even if single lookups are ``approximately anonymous,'' \emph{repeated} lookups can be identifying.
Three canonical examples:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Content systems:} IPFS-style content routing demonstrates that DHT-facing metadata enables large-scale passive monitoring and interacts with censorship/centralization pressures; for concrete measurements and privacy threats (including passive monitoring methodology), see Balduf et al.\cite{baldufIPFSMonitoring2022} and the broader comparators in the related-work map (Synthesis~7).\cite{trautweinIPFS2022,series:relatedworkmap} For deployed control-plane surfaces (delegated-routing endpoints, AutoConf, OHTTP upgrades), see Synthesis~30.\cite{series:deployedsurfaces}
\item \textbf{Anonymous lookup lineage:} attacks and information leaks in DHT-based anonymity systems motivate specialized anonymous lookups (split queries, cover, delegation) and query-privacy primitives, and more recent work explores practical obfuscation layers for Kademlia-style lookups.\cite{wangborisovLookupCCS2010,mittal_tissec,octopus,backesQueryPrivacy2012,danielQueryObfuscationSAC2025}
\item \textbf{Network-scale measurement:} real-world DHTs have been profiled at million-node scale, making transcript aggregation operationally feasible.\cite{falknerMillionDHT2007}
\end{enumerate}

For additional comparators and concrete measurement studies, see the related-work map (Synthesis~7).\cite{series:relatedworkmap}

We model the profiling adversary as seeing $n$ (possibly dependent) samples $X_1,\dots,X_n$ of a chosen projection $X$ of the full transcript, and attempting to infer a hidden index $K\in\{1,\dots,|\mathcal K|\}$ (a key, destination class, or content ID bucket).

We emphasize that these concerns persist in newer ``anonymous DHT'' proposals for other settings (e.g., blockchain overlays) when deployments expose repeatable transcript features; the equalization contract is meant to be portable across such designs.\cite{tsengNCDHT2024}

\section{The equalization contract}
\begin{definition}[Equalization target]
Fix a measurable transcript projection $X$ (examples below).
For each hidden key $k\in\mathcal K$, let $P_k$ denote the law of $X$ for a single lookup to $k$ under the deployment's policy.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Pairwise equalization]
A deployment satisfies \emph{pairwise $(\eta,\delta)$ equalization} for $X$ if for all $k,k'\in\mathcal K$ and all measurable $E$,
\[
P_k(E)\le 2^{\eta}P_{k'}(E)+\delta.
\]
This is a max-divergence style statement (an $(\eta,\delta)$ stability guarantee) restricted to the $X$-projection.
\end{definition}


\begin{remark}[Equalization is only as good as the declared projection]
The choice of $X$ is not purely mathematical: deployed control-plane surfaces (router sets, endpoint autoconfiguration, cache layers, retrieval-mode switches) can change what is observable even when the ``mechanism'' is unchanged.
In this series, the projection choice is therefore coupled to (i) an exposure normal form id (Synthesis~3) and (ii) a digest-bound state declaration that pins the relevant surfaces under change control (Synthesis~18 and Synthesis~30).\cite{series:traceifaces,series:planstateequiv,series:deployedsurfaces}
\end{remark}
\begin{lemma}[Pairwise equalization $\Rightarrow$ one-vs-mixture anonymity for $X$]
\label{lem:pairwise-to-mixture}
Fix any prior $\pi$ on $\mathcal K$ and let $P:=\sum_{j\in\mathcal K}\pi(j)P_j$ be the public marginal of $X$.
If pairwise $(\eta,\delta)$ equalization holds, then for every $k\in\mathcal K$ and every measurable event $E$,
\[
P_k(E)\le 2^{\eta}P(E)+\delta.
\]
In particular, when $\delta=0$ we have the pointwise likelihood-ratio cap $p_k(x)/p(x)\le 2^{\eta}$ for all $x$ with $p(x)>0$.
Equivalently, the realized odds inflation of the $X$-projection (Anonymity~A) is at most $\eta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Multiply the pairwise inequality by $\pi(k')$ and sum over $k'\in\mathcal K$.
The left-hand side stays $P_k(E)$; the first right-hand term becomes $2^{\eta}\sum_{k'}\pi(k')P_{k'}(E)=2^{\eta}P(E)$; and the additive term sums to $\delta$.
For $\delta=0$, apply the same argument pointwise to the singleton event $\{X=x\}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why max-divergence?]
Total variation constraints are often natural (especially when $X$ is discrete and bounded), but TV alone does not control pointwise likelihood ratios.
Max-divergence and odds-inflation are ``endpoint friendly'': they translate directly into posterior ratio bounds and into the identification sizing rules used throughout this series.\cite{series:oddsinflation,series:endpointbridge}
When a mechanism is designed/verified in TV (e.g., via transport dual certificates in the published Knapsack/Transport note\cite{series:knapsacktransport}), we treat the TV\,$\to$\,odds conversion as an imported module (Synthesis~5).
\end{remark}

\subsection{What can be equalized? (examples)}
The contract is intentionally modular: different papers ``own'' different $X$ projections (see the projection normal forms in Synthesis~3).\cite{series:traceifaces}
Examples:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Committee-contact footprints (MUCC).} $X$ is the multiset of committee IDs contacted in each epoch. (AnonDHT State~2.)
\item \textbf{Termination-time buckets.} $X$ is a deadline bucket / stop time normal form for a lookup. (Published 2026-01-26 wiki post.)
\item \textbf{Prefix-fetch witnesses.} $X$ is a prefix-length trace (or a coarsened prefix-set witness) induced by \emph{prefix fetching} (widened lookups).
Prefix-level equalization is constrained by shared-kernel termination-time lower bounds (Prefix Capacities, 2026-01-25) and by stop-time normal forms (2026-01-26); treat feasibility and ``what can actually be made key-independent as imported math, and expose only the resulting budgeted projection.\cite{series:prefixcapacities,series:stoptimeaddendum}
\item \textbf{Congestion witnesses.} $X$ is the RTT/queueing witness used by Congestion-EQ. (Congestion series.)
\item \textbf{Retry traces.} $X$ is the schedule-only retry trace $X_{\mathsf{retry}}$ exported by the schedule-only wrapper (Operational~A).\cite{series:operationalA}
\item \textbf{Receipts / certificates.} $X$ is the published claim object itself (receipt redaction bounds). (Synthesis~1; Release~A; W-Congestion-EQ.)
\end{itemize}

The key point: you do \emph{not} need to equalize the entire transcript to obtain a meaningful profiling budget.
You only need to equalize what the adversary can reliably observe and aggregate.

\section{From equalization to profiling budgets}
Lemma~\ref{lem:pairwise-to-mixture} turns pairwise $(\eta,\delta)$ equalization for a projection $X$ into a one-vs-mixture $(\eta,\delta)$ anonymity bound for $X$.
To obtain a profiling statement over repeated lookups, we then compose this one-step bound over the repetition horizon as a \emph{horizon claim} (unit+reset+accountant+replay hook) per the receipt-accounting rulebook.\cite{series:accountingrulebook,series:threatwindows}
Rather than re-derive composition here, we import the sequential $(\varepsilon,\delta)$ ``checkpoint'' calculus from the odds-inflation endpoint paper.\cite{series:oddsinflation}

\paragraph{Horizon-$n$ profiling label (imported composition).}
This paper does not re-derive horizon composition.
Apply the sequential $(\varepsilon,\delta)$ checkpoint composition theorem from \emph{Odds Inflation} (Anonymity~A) to the per-lookup one-vs-mixture inequality obtained in Lemma~\ref{lem:pairwise-to-mixture}.\cite{series:oddsinflation}
Concretely, suppose the deployment executes $n$ lookups inside the declared threat window and publishes per-lookup equalization budgets $(\eta_\ell,\delta_\ell)$ for the same projection family $X$.
If, almost surely over the declared public history/state in scope, each step satisfies the conditional inequality
\[
P_k(X_\ell\in E\mid h)\ \le\ 2^{\eta_\ell}\,P(X_\ell\in E\mid h)\ +\ \delta_\ell
\quad\text{for all keys }k\in\mathcal K\text{ and events }E,
\]
then the joint transcript $X_{1:n}$ satisfies a one-vs-mixture anonymity bound
\[
(\eta_\star,\delta_\star)\ =\ \Bigl(\sum_{\ell=1}^n \eta_\ell,\ \sum_{\ell=1}^n \delta_\ell\,2^{\sum_{j<\ell}\eta_j}\Bigr).
\]
In the homogeneous case $(\eta_\ell,\delta_\ell)=(\eta,\delta)$, this simplifies to
\[
(\eta_\star,\delta_\star)\ =\ \bigl(n\eta,\ \delta\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}2^{j\eta}\bigr).
\]
When retries/caching/adaptive routing break conditional independence, the same conditional-checkpoint formulation still applies; the receipt-facing condition is carried by the threat-window and (if needed) a digest-bound state declaration.\cite{series:threatwindows,series:state1,series:planstateequiv}

\subsection{Conditioning on published witnesses (state-conditioned equalization)}
Some deployments publish a coarse state witness $W$ (e.g., a public staleness class or rate-limit class) and certify equalization of $X$ conditional on $W$.
Formally, require that for every witness value $w$, the conditional laws $P_k^{X\mid W=w}$ satisfy pairwise $(\eta,\delta)$ equalization.
If $W$ is public and key-independent (as declared by a digest-bound \texttt{state\_decl\_id}), then controlling the worst-case conditional leakage of $X$ given $W$ controls the joint leakage of $(W,X)$; see the conditioning lemma in the plan/state equivalence note.\cite{series:planstateequiv}
In receipts, the witness can be embedded into the ENF conditioning fields or exported as an explicit Tier~0 line item; conditional certificates can be carried as Regime~II records.\cite{series:condcert}

\paragraph{Identification cap (OINL-facing).}
The endpoint bridge converts $(\eta_\star,\delta_\star)$ into an explicit identification cap.\cite{series:endpointbridge}
Let $K\sim\pi$ and let $\widehat K(X_{1:n})$ be any estimator. Then
\[
\Pr[\widehat K = K]\ \le\ 2^{\eta_\star}\,\pi_{\max} + \delta_\star,
\qquad\pi_{\max}:=\max_{k\in\mathcal K}\pi(k),
\]
and under a uniform prior on $|\mathcal K|$ keys, $\Pr[\widehat K = K]\le 2^{\eta_\star}/|\mathcal K| + \delta_\star$.

\paragraph{OINL-ready profiling label.}
For each declared projection $X$, publish (i) the per-lookup equalization budget(s) $(\eta_\ell,\delta_\ell)$, (ii) the threat-window horizon $n$ (or a time window plus a lookup-rate assumption), and (iii) the composed pair $(\eta_\star,\delta_\star)$ together with the identification cap above.
ABOM/OINL specifies how to package this claim and commit to its supporting artifacts by digest.\cite{series:abomoinl}

\paragraph{Contract object (minimal, non-redundant).}
Publish the equalization claim as a small contract object (Synthesis~0):\cite{series:contractobjects}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Projection id + schema:} a stable name for $X$ (ENF-ID backed) and a schema digest for the extractor $f(T)$.\cite{series:traceifaces}
\item \textbf{Secret scope:} what $K$ ranges over (keys, key-classes, destinations) and what eligibility/conditioning is treated as public.
\item \textbf{Budget + horizon:} $(\eta_\ell,\delta_\ell)$ and the TW declaration used to determine $n$; if state enters, include \texttt{state\_decl\_id}.\cite{series:threatwindows,series:planstateequiv}
\item \textbf{Evidence pointer:} proof objects and/or audit receipts + the ABOM digest that bundles omitted artifacts.\cite{series:abomoinl}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Multiple projections.}
Deployments often publish several equalization claims (retry trace, committee-contact footprint, congestion witness, stop-time bucket).
Treat each as a separate witness tier and compose using the series contract-object rulebook (Synthesis~0) rather than re-deriving a global guarantee here.\cite{series:contractobjects}
\section{Certifying equalization from logs (brief)}
A production workflow needs a way to certify the equalization claim.
This series provides two complementary levers:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Mechanism-first certificates.} When $X$ is generated by a compiler with a known proof object (e.g., TV transport duals, or proof-carrying padding schedules), publish the certificate (BossFight~C; the 2026-01-29 wiki post).
\item \textbf{Data-first audits.} When only logs are available, treat equalization as a two-sample problem (compare empirical $\widehat P_k$ across $k$), but correct for \emph{many-testing} across many keys and policies.
Synthesis~25 provides menu-level simultaneous guarantees for discrete projections audited from logs, and Synthesis~26 standardizes how a deployment spends a single $\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}$ over repeated audits or expanding menus.\cite{series:stataudit,series:anytimespend}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Log-audit certificates (pointer).}
When only logs are available, equalization can be audited as a finite-sample distributional claim on a chosen interface projection $X$.
Rather than duplicating statistics across papers, this archive centralizes ``menu-level'' log-audit bounds (including many-testing correction across many keys/policies, and receipt-side fields for publishing $\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}$ and sample sizes) in Synthesis~25.\cite{series:stataudit}

In brief, an auditor chooses (i) a discrete projection $X$ (bucketed time / contact-set ID / retry-pattern hash / prefix-length bin, \dots), (ii) a confidence level $\delta_{\mathrm{stat}}$, and (iii) a scope (which keys/policies are in the menu).
If the projection depends on a data-selected bucket/coarsening map, apply the selection discipline note (Synthesis~29): treat the map family as witness-indexed or separate pilot selection from frozen certification under change control.\cite{series:selectiondiscipline}
From empirical frequencies $\widehat P_k$ per class $k$, Synthesis~25 provides a conservative bound of the form
$\TV(P_k,P_{k'}) \le \TV(\widehat P_k,\widehat P_{k'}) + r_k + r_{k'}$
holding simultaneously over the declared menu.
Publishing the resulting bound as a \texttt{profiling\_evidence} object is then enough to support the equalization contract, while keeping the paper itself referee-grade and short.
For a fully wired receipt+bundle instantiation (including the prefix-fetch variants), see Synthesis~17.\cite{series:workedexample}
For a concrete coarsening instantiation (large-alphabet projection made audit-feasible by a declared partition map), see the coarsening variant receipt in Synthesis~17 (and the design rule in Synthesis~28).\cite{series:coarsening}

\section{Positioning (one paragraph)}
This note does not propose a new anonymous-lookup protocol.
It isolates a reusable \emph{publication contract}---equalization on a declared transcript projection $X$---and a reusable \emph{budget consequence}---profiling bounds via odds inflation and identification caps.
Protocol mechanisms and measurement studies live in the related-work map (Synthesis~7), which this paper treats as the canonical pointer list.\cite{series:relatedworkmap}


Active routing attacks (Sybil/eclipse) can also \emph{amplify} profiling by shaping contact sets and termination behavior; in such regimes the equalization contract may fail even if the benign-network contract is certified. Deployments should therefore treat routing-security posture as part of the declared state surface, and (when relevant) publish a separate ``under-attack'' budget or explicitly scope it out.\cite{nettoSybilIPFSDHT2025,cholezSybilIPFSARES2024}

\section{Takeaways}
The ``anonymity'' question for DHT lookups is often too blunt.
Profiling is driven by concrete, repeatable skews in a small number of observable features.
An equalization contract turns those skews into budgets, and the series' odds\,--\,inflation bridge turns budgets into the quantities operators and downstream users can reason about (posterior inflation and identification scale).

\section{Where more work is warranted (short list)}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Equalization under dependence.} The clean horizon scaling in the horizon composition bound above assumes conditional independence.
Caches, adaptive routing, and key-dependent retries create stateful dependence; bridging ``equalization contracts'' to the state-series odometers in a way that remains OINL-friendly is a high-leverage next step. For random-walk delegation, Anonymity~D gives a compact spectral-witness pathway to Regime~II per-step certificates.\cite{series:state1,series:condcert,series:spectral2budget}
\item \textbf{Which projection to equalize (cost vs.\ value).} Tier~0/1 equalization can be cheap but may leave Tier~2 failure-order traces exploitable.
A systematic ``value of coarsening'' study---how much profiling power survives when moving from Tier~2$\to$Tier~1$\to$Tier~0 for real overlays---would inform what ENF-IDs are worth certifying.\cite{series:traceifaces}
\item \textbf{Drift and manipulation of the background $Q$.} When $Q$ is measured (not specified), the measurement process itself is an attack surface.
A lightweight, notarized drift test that feeds directly into TW declarations (what window it applies to) and ABOM change control (what recertification is triggered) is still missing as a single, reusable module.\cite{series:threatwindows,series:accountingrulebook,series:abomoinl}
\end{itemize}
