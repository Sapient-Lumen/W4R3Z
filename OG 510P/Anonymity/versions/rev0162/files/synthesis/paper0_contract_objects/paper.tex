\begin{abstract}
The papers in this archive share a design goal: \emph{brief, referee-grade} anonymity arguments that can be
certified and updated without shipping a monolithic research archive.
To keep redundancy low while staying practical, we treat each mechanism paper as exporting a small \emph{contract}
in a standard format: a witness family, a metric, a budget, and an accountant describing repeats/resets.
This note proposes a concrete ``contract object'' interface for anonymous overlays and anonymous DHT lookups, and
shows how it connects the archive's main endpoints: (i) odds-inflation / pointwise-maximal-leakage budgets,
(ii) total-variation (TV) equalization contracts (including congestion witnesses), and (iii) notarized and auditable
evaluation receipts under retries and many-testing; the centralized log-audit certificate note is Synthesis~25.\cite{series:stataudit} (see the receipt-accounting and conditional-certificate notes, Synthesis~19--21).\cite{series:accountingrulebook,series:auditorreplay,series:condcert}
We give simple composition rules that justify ``multi-tier'' budgeting and clarify how to cross-pollinate insights
across papers without re-proving the same facts.
\end{abstract}
\paragraph{Scope.}
This synthesis note defines the shared \emph{interface} between mechanism proofs, endpoint metrics, and certificate/evaluation machinery.
It is intentionally lightweight: we introduce only the contract-object vocabulary needed to keep the rest of the papers short and non-redundant.

\paragraph{Audience note (scope limitation).} This series is written for deployers, reviewers, and auditors: it specifies how \emph{providers} can make anonymity claims machine-checkable and how third parties can verify those claims from public receipts. End-user verification without access to receipts (e.g., adversarially testing a live provider from the outside) is important but out of scope here; we treat it as an additional threat model and interface problem. A minimal user-facing discoverability/validation interface is sketched in Synthesis~13 as an optional adjunct.\cite{series:userverif}

\paragraph{Imports.}
Concrete mechanisms (scheduling/stop-time padding, TV toolboxes, congestion witnesses) and endpoint bridges (odds inflation / MaxL) are imported from the corresponding series papers and treated as black boxes.
Trace-interface vocabulary (tiers, exposure normal forms, committee-contact trace schema) is owned by the trace-interfaces note (Synthesis~3), while notation conventions and the minimal probabilistic model are owned by the notation sheet (Synthesis~8).\cite{series:traceifaces,series:notation}

\paragraph{Exports.}
We export (i) a contract-object schema (Def.~\ref{def:contract}), (ii) two micro-lemmas that appear in many receipts (tier additivity and union-style tail composition), and (iii) a compact conversion lemma that links odds-inflation style caps to multiplicative and TV-style bounds. Horizon-$n$ accounting across repeats, retries, and state is owned by the receipt-accounting rulebook (Synthesis~19).\cite{series:accountingrulebook}

\paragraph{Artifact policy.}
This archive is source-only; supporting artifacts and tooling are available on request.\cite{series:artifactpolicy}


\section{Where this fits in the series}
The archive has three recurring themes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Compiler papers} (how to hide metadata): scheduling/stop-time mechanisms, delegation bounds, and TV-toolboxes.
\item \textbf{Endpoint metrics} (what the guarantee should \emph{mean}): odds inflation / pointwise maximal leakage (Anonymity~A) and TV-style contracts (Congestion-EQ / Synthesis~5).
\item \textbf{Certification and change control} (how to ship and update a claim): ABOM/OINL (Anonymity~C), proof-carrying budgets (Boss Fight series), and notarized/auditable evaluation (evaluation series).

Redundancy is minimized if mechanism papers \emph{export} a contract in a shared vocabulary, and endpoint/certification papers
provide generic conversion and composition theorems.

\section{A contract-object vocabulary}
\begin{definition}[Anonymity contract object]\label{def:contract}
An \emph{anonymity contract object} for a mechanism $\mathcal M$ consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Secret scope} $S$ and \textbf{context} $\Pi$ (a prior family on $S$, plus public eligibility interfaces).
\item \textbf{Witness family} $\mathcal W$: a declared set of observables (e.g.\ stop time, contact trace, congestion witness, retry transcript), together with an exposure normal-form id / schema hash naming the projection being budgeted.\cite{series:traceifaces}
\item \textbf{Metric} $\mathsf M$ and \textbf{budget} $B$: a per-witness bound, typically one of:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Odds-inflation / PML:} $\sup_{w\in\mathcal W}\max_{s\in S}\logtwo\frac{P_s(w)}{P(w)}\le \eps$ (Anonymity~A).
  \item \textbf{Hockey-stick:} $P_s(A)\le 2^\eps P(A)+\delta$ for all events $A$ (Anonymity~A / Anonymity~B).
  \item \textbf{TV equalization:} $\TV(P_s, P)\le \tau$ (compiler/TV toolbox papers; Congestion-EQ).
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Accountant} $\mathsf A$: an explicit rule for repeats/resets (epochs, retries, cache updates), producing a cumulative budget $B_{\mathrm{tot}}$.
\item \textbf{Knob interface} $\mathsf K$: named parameters the deployer may choose (timeouts, padding distributions, committee size, calendar period), plus admissible ranges.
\item \textbf{Certificate and receipt schemas} (optional): machine-checkable claims, proofs, and monitoring artifacts (Anonymity~C; Boss Fight; evaluation series). When a receipt line item points at a replay workflow, it should include not only a human plan name but also a digest-bound \texttt{plan\_spec\_id} committing to the canonical replay-plan specification (Synthesis~12; worked example Synthesis~17).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Why this particular split]
The witness family is what prevents ``metric laundering'' (changing what is observed) and keeps proofs honest.
The accountant is what prevents ``repeat laundering'' (replaying small leaks until they become large).
Separating $\mathsf K$ from the proof lets a paper stay short: the paper proves ``if knobs satisfy these inequalities, then the contract holds'';
a deployment certificate instantiates the inequalities for its chosen knobs.
\end{remark}

\section{Two basic composition rules that show up everywhere}
\begin{proposition}[Additive composition for odds inflation under conditional independence]\label{prop:additive}
Let $S$ be the secret and let $W_1,W_2$ be two witnesses such that conditional on $S=s$,
the outputs are independent: $P_s(w_1,w_2)=P_s^{(1)}(w_1)\,P_s^{(2)}(w_2)$.
Let $P^{(j)}$ be the public marginals induced by the same prior family.
If each witness satisfies a pointwise odds-inflation cap
\[
\max_s \logtwo\frac{P_s^{(j)}(w_j)}{P^{(j)}(w_j)}\le \eps_j \quad\text{for all } w_j,
\]
then the combined witness satisfies
\[
\max_s \logtwo\frac{P_s(w_1,w_2)}{P(w_1,w_2)}\le \eps_1+\eps_2
\quad\text{for all }(w_1,w_2).
\]
Equivalently, pointwise maximal leakage adds under conditionally independent tiers.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under conditional independence, both the conditional laws and the public marginals factor.
Therefore the likelihood ratio factors, and the log-likelihood ratio is a sum.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Union-style composition for tail budgets]\label{prop:union}
Suppose for each tier $j$ we have a hockey-stick budget
$P_s(A)\le 2^{\eps_j}P(A)+\delta_j$ for all events $A$ over witness $W_j$.
Then for the joint witness $W=(W_1,\dots,W_m)$,
\[
P_s(A)\le 2^{\sum_j \eps_j}P(A)+\sum_j \delta_j
\quad\text{for all events }A \text{ over } W,
\]
whenever the witness channels are conditionally independent given $S$.
\end{proposition}

\section{A small conversion lemma that reduces redundancy}
Several papers in the archive switch between ``odds-inflation / likelihood-ratio'' caps and TV-style contracts.
The following one-line implication is a high-leverage cross-link.

\begin{proposition}[Odds-inflation cap implies multiplicative and TV bounds]\label{prop:oi_to_tv}
Fix a prior-induced public marginal $P$ and a secret-conditioned witness law $P_s$.
If for all witnesses $w$ we have the pointwise cap
\[
\logtwo\frac{P_s(w)}{P(w)} \le \eps,
\]
then for every event $A$ over the witness,
\[
P_s(A)\le 2^{\eps} P(A).
\]
Moreover, the total-variation distance satisfies the one-sided bound
\[
\TV(P_s,P)\le 1-2^{-\eps}.
\]
(For small $\eps$, this is $\TV(P_s,P)\lesssim (\ln 2)\,\eps$.)
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The event bound is immediate by summing $P_s(w)\le 2^{\eps}P(w)$ over $w\in A$.
For TV, write $\TV(P_s,P)=\tfrac12\int |f-1|\,dP$ where $f=dP_s/dP$ satisfies $0\le f\le 2^{\eps}$ and $\int f\,dP=1$.
The maximum of $\int |f-1|\,dP$ under these constraints is attained by a two-point extremal $f\in\{0,2^{\eps}\}$, yielding $\TV(P_s,P)\le 1-2^{-\eps}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Editorial rule]
If a paper proves a pointwise odds-inflation cap, it should \emph{not} re-prove event/TV consequences.
Instead, it should cite \Cref{prop:oi_to_tv} (or the endpoint paper) and state the converted parameter values.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What this buys you in practice]

These two propositions justify a common, practical editorial move across the archive:
if a paper introduces a \emph{new witness tier} (congestion, retries, state evolution),
it should not re-define ``anonymity from scratch.'' It should specify (i) the tier's witness family and (ii) how its budget
adds into the global accountant.
\end{remark}

\section{How the main certificate objects line up}

This paper is about the \emph{contract-object interface}, not a full object catalog.
To keep redundancy low, we maintain the authoritative, archive-wide object map in the top-level \texttt{SERIES\_INDEX} (PDF) and in the math-backbone crosswalk (Synthesis~24).\cite{series:mathcrosswalk}

In brief, most papers in this archive export exactly one of three receipt-facing shapes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item a \textbf{projection-level budget} (identified by \texttt{exposure\_nf\_id}) with an accountant over the declared threat window (\texttt{tw\_id});
\item a \textbf{certificate object} that commits knobs and evidence by digest (ABOM/OINL; Certified series; evaluation receipts);
\item a \textbf{guard object} that pins assumptions (state declarations, separation manifests) so drift is diffable.
\end{itemize}

Papers should cite the owning notes for these interfaces (Synthesis~3,4,8,12,18--21,22--23) rather than re-listing object fields here.

\section{Cross-pollination opportunities, stated as reusable lemmas}

In the unified archive, the following cross-links are the highest leverage (least redundancy, most reuse):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Spectral $\leftrightarrow$ calendars/dither.}
Spectral delegation bounds imply limits for periodic calendars; declared dither in PSC-Q can be analyzed as a spectral filter.
A single lemma can be reused: ``dither kernel contracts TV and attenuates low-frequency witnesses.''
\item \textbf{State evolution $\leftrightarrow$ certification.}
State-dependent anonymity turns public scores/caches into witnesses; Closed-View Auditing turns witnesses into monitored receipts.
The shared lemma is ``small per-epoch bias amplifies quadratically under repetition,'' motivating explicit accountants.
\item \textbf{Release-property ledgers $\leftrightarrow$ notarization.}
Both are anti-equivocation devices: the ledger receipt and the notarized evaluation log differ mainly in what they commit to (software release vs.\ evaluation run).
They can share an ``append-only transparency log'' interface and a standard auditor script.
\item \textbf{Audit objects are closed-view channels.}
Any published receipt (PTL checkpoints, CPPC cards, release-ledger entries) is a \emph{view} $Y=g(X)$ of some richer internal state/trace $X$.
Treat $g$ as a channel and budget it like any other witness: if $g$ contracts TV by coefficient $\eta$ (Dobrushin / SDPI), then a TV budget $\delta$ on $X$ implies a TV budget at most $\eta\delta$ on the published view.
This is the common language behind (i) closed-view auditing in the state series, (ii) receipt-redaction bounds (Synthesis~1), and (iii) ``publish what you account'' design in the evaluation and release-ledger papers.
\end{enumerate}
