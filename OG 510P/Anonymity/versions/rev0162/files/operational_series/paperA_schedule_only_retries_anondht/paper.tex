\begin{abstract}
Retries, hedges, failover, and negotiation wrappers are ubiquitous in networked retrieval.
Even when each individual attempt is secure (e.g., via an anonymous channel, an anonymous DHT lookup layer, or an oblivious relay),
the \emph{retry trace}---how many attempts were made, in what public schedule, and under which lane---is itself a high-bandwidth metadata channel that an active adversary can amplify via selective abort.
This paper formalizes \emph{Trace Privacy with Abort} (\TPA) and introduces \emph{Schedule-Only Retry Policies} (\SRP): wrapper policies whose externally visible control flow depends only on a \emph{public schedule}, not on hidden outcomes.
We prove a lifting theorem: if each attempt satisfies privacy-with-abort (\PWA) with advantage $\delta$ and the wrapper is $\varepsilon$-close to schedule-only, then wrapper-level \TPA holds with advantage at most $T\delta+\varepsilon$, where $T$ is the public slot budget.
We instantiate \SRP for committee contact in an anonymous DHT and provide a synthetic simulation showing how best-effort retries amplify advantage across epochs and how \SRP arrests this growth.
Stop-time padding and schedule optimization are treated as prior work in this series (e.g., \cite{series:schedcompiler,series:stoptimeaddendum}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Anonymous overlays and anonymous DHTs aim to hide \emph{who contacts whom} during retrieval.
However, real retrieval stacks nearly always include wrappers:
timeouts, retries, hedged requests, multi-path probing, protocol negotiation, and gateway failover \cite{deanBarrosoTailAtScale2013,primoracWhenToHedgeNSDI2021}.
Widely deployed RPC stacks make these wrappers explicit; for example, gRPC standardizes client retry and hedging behavior (including cancellation semantics) at the library level.\cite{grpcClientRetriesA6}
Even ``mundane'' connectivity algorithms explicitly recommend racing alternatives: for example, Happy Eyeballs (RFC~8305) improves responsiveness by attempting multiple address families using concurrency, i.e., a structured form of hedging/fallback \cite{rfc8305HappyEyeballs}.
These wrappers create a \emph{trace}: a sequence of (attempt, wait, abort/accept, lane choice) events.
An active adversary can \emph{selectively abort} attempts to amplify information leaked in the trace, echoing selective denial-of-service attacks in anonymity systems \cite{borisovDanezisMittalCCS2007,dasborisovSelectiveDoS2013,jansenPointBreak2019}.

More broadly, as encrypted transports hide payload contents, traffic analysis shifts to side-channel features such as timing, size, and control-flow metadata; recent work formalizes that such side-channel leakage can be information-theoretically inevitable under broad conditions.\cite{liuInevitabilitySideChannel2026}

\paragraph{Goal.}
Design wrappers that preserve anonymity (and more broadly, metadata privacy) even in the presence of aborts,
without forbidding practical performance tools like hedging and failover.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item We define \TPA, a wrapper-level indistinguishability notion where the adversary observes retry traces and can induce aborts.
  \item We define \SRP, a discipline for wrappers whose visible behavior is \emph{schedule-only}.
  \item We prove a lifting theorem: composing up to $T$ attempts that each satisfy \PWA (advantage $\delta$) under an \SRP wrapper with deviation $\varepsilon$ yields \TPA with advantage at most $T\delta+\varepsilon$.
  \item We give an \SRP instantiation for \emph{committee contact in an anonymous DHT} and supply a synthetic simulation
        showing trace-amplification over epochs and its arrest under \SRP.
\end{itemize}

This paper focuses on \emph{retries/abort traces}. We refer to earlier series papers for
stop-time padding, schedule compilation, and dual-certificate privacy accounting
(\cite{series:schedcompiler,series:prefixcapacities,series:knapsacktransport,series:stoptimeaddendum}).

\section{Model and Notation}
A \emph{base attempt protocol} $\Pi$ is invoked repeatedly by a \emph{wrapper} $W$ until $W$ decides to stop.
Each attempt emits an adversary-visible transcript and ends in an outcome in $\{\mathsf{accept},\mathsf{abort}\}$.
A \emph{trace} records the wrapper's externally visible \emph{control-flow} events: attempt start times (or slots) and lane labels.
Per-attempt transcripts (including whether a controlled recipient responds) are accounted for at the attempt layer via \PWA; \SRP constrains only the wrapper's visible control flow. We treat stop-time padding/termination-time hiding as prior work in this series (so that per-attempt completion times need not appear in the public trace).

A \emph{public schedule} $\sigma$ is a (possibly randomized) skeleton describing which slots are executed, with which lane labels, and with what timing class.
We treat $\sigma$ as public: it may be logged, audited, and used by adversaries.
The design objective is that \emph{secrets do not modulate externally visible traces beyond what $\sigma$ allows}.

\section{Security Definitions}
We model two layers.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\PWA}{PWA}: attempt-level privacy-with-abort (imported)}
An attempt protocol $\Pi$ is invoked in slots according to a public schedule $\sigma$.
We assume $\Pi$ comes with a composition-ready per-slot guarantee:
for every executed slot and every public history consistent with $\sigma$
(including adversarial abort/delay choices), the conditional distinguishing advantage between secrets is at most $\delta$.
This is the standard hypothesis used for sequential composition in Anonymity~A and is exportable as a conditional per-step certificate (Synthesis~21).\cite{series:oddsinflation,series:condcert}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\TPA}{TPA}: wrapper-level trace privacy with abort}
Fix a wrapper $W$ and base attempt protocol $\Pi$.
The adversary chooses two secrets $(s_0,s_1)$, observes the externally visible wrapper trace
(slot indices, lane labels, and externally visible start/stop events), and may induce aborts/delays by controlling a subset of recipients/relays.
Let $\Adv^{\TPA}_{W(\Pi)}$ denote the resulting distinguishing advantage (advantage conventions follow the archive's contracts).\cite{series:advantagecontracts}
We target negligible $\Adv^{\TPA}$.

The gap between \PWA and \TPA is the wrapper: even if each attempt is safe, wrapper control flow may leak.

\section{Schedule-Only Retry Policies (	exorpdfstring{\SRP}{SRP})}
\begin{definition}[\SRP (schedule-only)]
A wrapper policy $W$ is \SRP if, for every public schedule $\sigma$ and any two secrets/outcome streams,
the distribution of its externally visible trace \emph{conditioned on $\sigma$} is identical.
Equivalently: conditioned on $\sigma$, the trace is a function only of public randomness, not of secret-dependent success/failure.
\end{definition}

Operationally, \SRP is achieved by fixing a per-epoch schedule and dummy-filling remaining slots after early success.
A minimal trace-contract/compilation pipeline is shown in \cref{fig:contract}.

\paragraph{Approximate \SRP.}
Real systems deviate (bugs, misconfiguration, shared pools). We quantify deviation as a statistical distance.

\begin{definition}[\emph{Schedule-only deviation} $\varepsilon$]
Fix $\sigma$.
Let $\mathsf{Trace}_{s}$ denote the induced trace distribution when running $W(\Pi,s)$ with schedule $\sigma$.
Define
\[\varepsilon_{\sigma}(W) := \sup_{s_0,s_1}\; \tv\big(\mathsf{Trace}_{s_0},\mathsf{Trace}_{s_1}\big),\qquad \varepsilon(W):=\sup_{\sigma}\varepsilon_{\sigma}(W).\]
$W$ is perfect \SRP iff $\varepsilon(W)=0$.
\end{definition}

\subsection*{SRP as an equalization contract for the retry trace}
Fix a public schedule $\sigma$ and let $X_{\mathsf{retry}}$ denote the wrapper-visible trace (slot indices, lane labels, and any publicly logged schedule metadata).
By definition, the schedule-only deviation $\varepsilon_{\sigma}(W)$ is the worst-case total-variation separation of the $X_{\mathsf{retry}}$ laws across secrets, conditioned on $\sigma$.
Thus for any secrets $s_0,s_1$ and any measurable event $E$ over traces,
\[
\Pr[X_{\mathsf{retry}}\in E\mid s_0,\sigma]\le \Pr[X_{\mathsf{retry}}\in E\mid s_1,\sigma]+\varepsilon_{\sigma}(W),
\]
i.e., $X_{\mathsf{retry}}$ satisfies a pairwise $(0,\varepsilon_{\sigma}(W))$ equalization contract in the sense of Anonymity~B.\cite{series:profiling}
Perfect \SRP ($\varepsilon=0$) spends zero retry-trace budget; imperfect \SRP exposes an explicit additive $\delta$ term that composes over epochs via the standard accountant.\cite{series:profiling}

\paragraph{Operational note (monitoring).}
Deployments rarely inspect full traces; instead they commit to and monitor a small plan-defined feature family (conformance predicates, negotiation bits, coarse summaries).
We treat \emph{plan commitments}, transparency anchoring, and anytime-valid drift monitoring as imported modules: see Proof-Carrying Budgets (Boss Fight~C) for certificate patterns, Release receipts for change-control binding, and Advantage Contracts (Eval~2) for anytime-valid monitoring recipes and scalable drift discovery.\cite{series:proofcarryingbudgets,series:releaseproperty,series:advantagecontracts}

\section{Why \texorpdfstring{\SRP}{SRP} is Close to Necessary}
If a wrapper's schedule depends on hidden outcomes, then an adversary can often amplify leakage by forcing aborts.
This is the same qualitative mechanism as selective-DoS attacks \cite{borisovDanezisMittalCCS2007,dasborisovSelectiveDoS2013}.

\begin{proposition}[Amplification by selective abort (informal)]
Suppose $W$ retries until success and the (slot-count or retry-count) trace is adversary-visible.
If an adversary can cause aborts with probability depending on a hidden bit, then per-epoch trace distributions separate across the bit.
Across independent epochs, likelihood-ratio accumulation drives distinguishing advantage toward $1/2$.
\end{proposition}

\section{Lifting: \texorpdfstring{\PWA $+$ \SRP $\Rightarrow$ \TPA}{PWA + SRP => TPA}}
The key point is separation of concerns: if the wrapper's trace is schedule-only, then all secret dependence must flow through the attempt transcripts, which are controlled by \PWA.

\begin{theorem}[Lifting bound]
Let $\Pi$ satisfy attempt-level \PWA with advantage $\delta$ \emph{per executed attempt slot}, in the conditional/composition-ready sense (cf.\ Anonymity~A or Synthesis~21).\cite{series:oddsinflation,series:condcert}
Let $W$ be a wrapper with schedule-only deviation $\varepsilon(W)$, and suppose each epoch's public schedule contains at most $T$ attempt slots.
Then for any adversary $\mathcal{A}$,
\[\Adv^{\TPA}_{W(\Pi)}(\mathcal{A}) \le T\delta + \varepsilon(W).\]
Here $T$ counts the total number of executed attempt slots (including parallel/hedged slots if the schedule starts multiple attempts concurrently); wall-clock concurrency does not change the hybrid loss.
In particular, perfect \SRP wrappers ($\varepsilon=0$) lift \PWA to \TPA with only a linear-in-slots loss from composing $T$ attempts.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Hybridize in two steps.
First, hybrid over the at most $T$ attempts and replace each attempt transcript with the \PWA simulator; this changes the adversary's view by at most $T\delta$.
This step remains valid against adaptive abort strategies because $\delta$ is defined as a worst-case conditional bound over histories.
Second, couple the remaining executions across secrets; the only remaining difference is the wrapper trace distribution, which differs by at most $\varepsilon(W)$ in total variation.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Product-TV bound]
Let $\mathsf{View}_{0}$ and $\mathsf{View}_{1}$ be the per-epoch adversary views, and suppose
$\tv(\mathsf{View}_{0},\mathsf{View}_{1})\le \alpha$.
If epochs are independent, then for $E$ epochs,
\[\tv(\mathsf{View}_{0}^{\otimes E},\mathsf{View}_{1}^{\otimes E})\le 1-(1-\alpha)^E\le E\alpha.\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}[Multi-epoch composition]
If the system runs for $E$ independent epochs (fresh schedules, fresh coins), then
\[\Adv^{\TPA}\le 1-(1-(T\delta+\varepsilon(W)))^E\le E(T\delta+\varepsilon(W)).\]
\end{corollary}

The first inequality is a sharper ``product distance'' bound; the second is the familiar union bound.
Operationally, both highlight that even small deviations $\varepsilon$ can matter at scale.

\paragraph{Dependence across epochs (brief caveat).}
The product-TV bound assumes independent epochs.
Without independence, a standard hybrid/triangle argument still yields the safe bound
$\tv(\mathsf{View}_{0}^{(1{:}E)},\mathsf{View}_{1}^{(1{:}E)}) \le \sum_{i=1}^{E}\alpha_i$ when $\alpha_i$ bounds the per-epoch (possibly history-dependent) conditional distance.
Thus the union bound remains valid even under adaptive network conditions; anytime-valid monitoring (Eval~2) does not require independence to flag drift.


\section{How to Implement \texorpdfstring{\SRP}{SRP} (practical sketch)}
We compile a best-effort retry policy into a schedule-only wrapper:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Choose a public schedule skeleton.} Examples include fixed slot budgets, lane schedules for negotiation/failover, or public-coin randomized stop-times.
  \item \textbf{Execute attempts on schedule.} Attempt completion can be recorded internally; visible behavior continues.
  \item \textbf{Dummy fill after success.} Remaining slots run cover traffic (or indistinguishable no-op work).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Tooling note.}
In practice, wrappers are implemented in complex control-flow languages (RPC middleware, service meshes, client libraries).
Our artifact includes a small \SRP linter/repair pass and a compiler from \emph{trace contracts} (public schedule skeletons) to wrappers that dummy-fill after success;
the associated demo figures (\texttt{fig\_srp\_lint\_demo.pdf}, \texttt{fig\_srp\_compile\_demo.pdf}) illustrate how secret- or outcome-dependent branching is detected and removed.

\paragraph{Folding in negotiation wrappers.}
Negotiation wrappers ("try privacy upgrade, then fall back") can create an amplifiable \emph{lane bit} unless lane choices are fixed by a public schedule.
This applies to upgrade/fallback mechanisms such as ECH, DDR, OHTTP, and ODoH; we treat their standards-level details (and the surrounding downgrade surfaces) as background and refer to the series related-work map (Synthesis~7) for canonical citations and context.\cite{series:relatedworkmap}
Operational proposals for authenticated ECH config distribution and rotation underscore that ``upgrade then fall back'' control flow must be treated as a first-class downgrade surface (and thus should be schedule-pinned).\cite{draftSullivanSignedEchUpdates2025}
To make negotiation wrappers \SRP, treat the lane schedule as part of the public plan: select among a small set of pre-committed lane schedules using only pinned public signals (or public coins), then execute the schedule to completion with dummy fill after success.
Outcome-dependent branching (``if upgrade fails then switch lanes'') is prohibited; instead, commit to lane schedules (and their conformance predicates) as plan objects and audit them via the proof-carrying/receipt pipeline.\cite{series:proofcarryingbudgets,series:releaseproperty}

\section{Case Study: Committee Contact in an Anonymous DHT}
Anonymous DHT lookups aim to hide initiator/target while contacting a sequence (or committee) of nodes.
Recent proposals in other settings (e.g., blockchain overlays) similarly target initiator anonymity, query unlinkability, and target privacy; our wrapper-level argument applies to such designs whenever retries and negotiation are observable.\cite{tsengNCDHT2024}
Prior work identifies lookup leakage attacks and builds dedicated anonymous lookup mechanisms\cite{wangborisovLookupCCS2010,wangborisovOctopus2012}; robust designs often rely on quorum/committee contact\cite{backesQueryPrivacy2012}.

Our emphasis is orthogonal to attempt primitives: widely deployed Kademlia-family overlays (including IPFS/libp2p) use parallelism, timeouts, and retries to tolerate churn\cite{maymounkovMazieresKademlia2002}, parameterized by the number of closest peers $k_{\mathrm{kad}}$ returned/queried per step and by lookup concurrency $\alpha$ (e.g., the original Kademlia paper uses $\alpha=3$), while the IPFS Kademlia DHT specification recommends $\alpha=10$.\cite{maymounkovMazieresKademlia2002,ipfsKadDHTSpec2025}
Those wrappers are an unavoidable privacy surface: active disruption (eclipse/Sybil) can force retries\cite{singhEclipseInfocom2006,zhangEclipseInfeasible2011,ipfsContentCensorshipNDSS2024}.
Beyond overlay-level attacks, AS-level interception and BGP hijacking can censor IPFS content retrieval and provider discovery, inducing failures and retries at scale.\cite{matterTranIPFSCensorship2025}
In IPFS, the Kademlia DHT specification explicitly permits early termination in several lookup modes, and recent work \emph{reports} an active Sybil eclipse that exploits an early trigger of content-discovery termination in Kubo (the main IPFS implementation), yielding high denial rates in their evaluation.\cite{ipfsKadDHTSpec2025,ipfsActiveSybil2025}
Meanwhile, complementary work targets attempt-level leakage in these systems (e.g., query obfuscation and PIR-style private queries)\cite{danielKademliaObfuscation2025,mazmudarPeer2PIR2025}.
Lightweight identifier-obfuscation proposals for IPFS (e.g., multi-hash schemes) further emphasize that partial defenses are easy to bypass via fallback behavior unless the wrapper's external trace is schedule-only.\cite{katsantasTripleHashing2024,danielBitswapForwarding2023}

\paragraph{Committee contact wrapper.}
Consider contacting a committee of size $m$ per epoch and needing $t$ successes.
A best-effort strategy retries until it obtains $t$ successful replies.
This is efficient but leaks via the number and timing of attempts.

\paragraph{\SRP instantiation.}
Fix a public per-epoch schedule of $S$ contact slots with a public lane map (e.g., slot indices assigned to a ``primary'' lane and a ``backup'' lane).
For each slot, the wrapper invokes the attempt primitive once; the attempt primitive may internally choose which committee member to contact (e.g., via anonymous relay selection in Octopus-style lookups), so the wrapper trace reveals only slot/lane boundaries.
After $t$ successes, the wrapper continues to execute the schedule using dummies/no-ops.
The adversary observes the same schedule regardless of success/failure, yielding \TPA subject to \PWA.

\section{Related Work (selected)}
Selective disruption/DoS lowers anonymity by forcing rebuilds/retries and biasing which traces occur
\cite{bauerLowResource2007,borisovDanezisMittalCCS2007,dasborisovSelectiveDoS2013,dannerDetectDoSTor2009,jansenPointBreak2019}.
Classic overlay/DHT security work on Sybil and eclipse attacks emphasizes that adversaries can actively induce lookup failures or dominate neighborhoods, thereby creating retries as a controllable channel.\cite{singhEclipseInfocom2006,baumgartSKademlia2007}

Anonymous DHT lookup mechanisms and analyses of lookup-related information leaks motivate strong attempt primitives
\cite{salsaNambiarSOSP2006,mittalInfoLeaksTISSEC2010,wangborisovLookupCCS2010,wangborisovOctopus2012}.
Our focus is orthogonal: \emph{even with strong anonymous attempts}, wrapper retries and negotiation can reintroduce metadata leakage unless externally visible behavior is schedule-only.

At a higher level, \SRP is an instance of data-oblivious control flow: like oblivious RAM (ORAM), it enforces that an adversary-visible schedule is independent of secret-dependent outcomes.\cite{goldreichOstrovskyORAM1996}

\paragraph{Retry schedules in deployed protocols.}
Many protocols standardize externally visible retransmission and recovery schedules (e.g., QUIC loss recovery timers),
emphasizing that the retry schedule is a common and frequently tuned surface.\cite{rfc9002}

\section{Limitations (brief)}
\label{sec:limitations}
\SRP protects only the \emph{externally visible retry trace}.
It does not, by itself, hide total volume/rate, long-lived identifiers, or other side channels in the attempt primitive.
Nor does it prevent denial-of-service; rather, it prevents an attacker from turning induced failures into an amplifiable \emph{trace} signal.
Finally, \SRP assumes that schedule selection is a public, auditable function of pinned public inputs (or public coins); if a deployment makes schedule choice depend on secret state, it reintroduces the very channel \SRP is meant to close.

\section{Conclusion}
Retries are not ``just engineering'': they are a first-class privacy surface.
\SRP offers a clean discipline for abort-safe wrappers and a path to compositional theorems.
Operationally, SRP works best when the schedule skeleton and conformance predicates are committed as plan objects and monitored for drift with anytime-valid evidence; those publication mechanics are owned by Boss Fight~C, Release receipts, and Eval~2.\cite{series:proofcarryingbudgets,series:releaseproperty,series:advantagecontracts}

\section{Takeaways}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Retries are a metadata channel:} the retry trace (slot count, lane choice, scheduling) can leak far more than a single attempt.
\item \textbf{Abort amplifies:} selective abort turns weak per-epoch trace signals into near-certain inference across repeated epochs.
\item \textbf{Schedule-only is the right discipline:} fix a public schedule and dummy-fill after early success; then wrapper-level trace privacy composes with the attempt primitive.
\end{itemize}
