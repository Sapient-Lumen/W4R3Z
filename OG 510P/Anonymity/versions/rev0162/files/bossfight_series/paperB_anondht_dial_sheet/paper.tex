\begin{abstract}
Anonymous DHT lookups are a canonical metadata problem: even if payloads are encrypted, routing structure, retries, and selective failures can identify the queried key or destination.
Building on the Boss Fight accounting lens (Paper~A), we give a practical \emph{dial sheet} for anonymous DHTs:
how to translate a maximal-leakage target into concrete design knobs such as dummy-query width, path splitting, timeout/abort semantics, and schedule equalization.
We keep the paper intentionally tight by importing padding and stop-time machinery from earlier series papers.
Instantiating the sheet on an IPFS-style Kademlia slice illustrates the central operational punchline: under modest corruption and a realistic repetition window (e.g., $Q\approx10^3$ lookups), dummy cover must approach $w/K\to 1$, so dummy-only designs economically collapse unless observation probability is driven extremely small or queries are rate-limited.
\end{abstract}

\section{Threat model and observation probability}\label{sec:pobs}
We treat the \emph{lookup target} $C$ (key, destination, or shard) as the secret and the adversary's extracted transcript feature as $Y$ (Paper~A).
In the Boss Fight, the adversary aggregates many lookups and may induce retries, delays, or selective failures.

\paragraph{Declaration (non-redundancy).}
We use the threat/window checklist of the companion note (Synthesis~4) \cite{series:threatwindows} and the tier/exposure vocabulary of the trace-interface note \cite{series:traceifaces}.
For this dial sheet we take \emph{secret} = lookup target $C$ (key/destination/shard) and treat the adversary's extracted transcript feature as $Y$ (Paper~A).
The \emph{unit} is a lookup (or a lookup attempt, depending on the declared projection) and the \emph{window} is an operational query budget $Q$ per time window with an explicit reset rule.


\paragraph{Passive upgrades vs Boss Fight (interpretation).}
Many deployed ``reader privacy'' proposals for Kademlia-derived systems primarily target passive observers; in that regime, treat this dial sheet as a conservative stress test sized for repetition and induced retries \cite{daniel2025queryobfuscation}.


\paragraph{What does $p_{\mathsf{obs}}$ mean?}
Observation probability is tier-relative and must match the declared projection: at Tier~2 (ordered contact/failure traces) $p_{\mathrm{obs}}$ is close to $1$ for compromised/queried peers, while at lower tiers or with cryptographic reader-privacy layers it can be smaller. If a deployment publishes multiple witness tiers (contact-set vs timing vs congestion), receipts should publish a tiered observation vector (one $(p_i,b_i)$ pair per tier) and combine tiers using the conservative rule in Synthesis~15.\cite{series:tieredobs}
We avoid re-tutorializing tiering here; we only require that the deployment declares which projection is being bounded and audits that the logging/monitoring pipeline matches it \cite{series:traceifaces,series:threatwindows}.


\paragraph{What is the candidate universe $K$?}
The relevant $K\triangleq|\C|$ is the number of \emph{plausible} targets under declared public side information in the declared window; see the threat/window checklist \cite{series:threatwindows} and Paper~C's certificate fields.

Many AnonDHT proposals contact \emph{committees} (shards) of size $s$ sampled from $N$ nodes, with an adversary corrupting a $\rho$ fraction.
A pessimistic sufficient condition for ``observing'' a contacted committee is that it contains at least one corrupted node.
Then the per-contact observation probability is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pobs_committee}
p_{\mathsf{obs}}
= 1- \frac{\binom{(1-\rho)N}{s}}{\binom{N}{s}}
\approx 1-(1-\rho)^s.
\end{equation}
For example, with $\rho=0.2$ and $s=10$, $p_{\mathsf{obs}}\approx 0.893$.


In Kademlia-style iterative lookups, the number of distinct queried peers $m$ can be substantial (often $\mathcal O(10)$ and sometimes much larger under failures/retries).
We treat $m$ as a knob-dependent scale parameter; representative measurements put $m$ in the $\sim 10$--$20$ range for most lookups with a long tail under failures/retries \cite{ipfs_kad_spec,cortes2024kadlimits,probelab_rfm17,trautwein2024ipfs}.

\paragraph{Caution (independence proxy).} The proxy $p_{\mathsf{obs}}=1-(1-\rho)^m$ treats each contacted peer as independently adversarial. Real overlays exhibit correlations (routing-table poisoning, selective targetting, and churn), so we use this proxy only as a conservative \emph{scale anchor} rather than a claim of tightness.

\begin{lemma}[Contacts amplify observation]\label{lem:contacts_amplify}
If each queried peer is independently adversarial with probability $\rho$ (or, more generally, the adversary observes a query whenever it hits an adversarial peer), then the probability that a lookup has \emph{any} observed contact satisfies
\[
p_{\mathsf{obs}}^{(\mathrm{lookup})}\;\ge\; 1-(1-\rho)^m,
\]
where $m$ is the number of distinct queried peers.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The lookup is unobserved only if \emph{all} $m$ queried peers are non-adversarial.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Numerical note.} In realistic repetition windows, once $p_{\mathsf{obs}}$ is not tiny and the per-lookup budget $b$ is forced into the $10^{-2}$--$10^{-3}$ bit range, the required dummy fraction approaches $1$.
\cite{series:bossfightBaddendum}

\section{A minimal AnonDHT slice with a closed-form MaxL bound}\label{sec:minislice}
To keep the paper tight, we isolate a single lookup pattern that appears (explicitly or implicitly) across many AnonDHT designs:
\emph{forced-destination with decoys} under \emph{partial visibility}.

\paragraph{Slice.}
Let the secret lookup target be $C\in\C$ with $|\C|=K$ possibilities.
A lookup instance selects a \emph{candidate set} $S\subseteq \C$ of size $w{+}1$ that contains the true target $C$ and $w$ decoys (e.g., drawn uniformly from $\C\setminus\{C\}$).
The mechanism then runs the protocol on each element of $S$ (possibly interleaving or batching queries).
The adversary's \emph{observation event} is modeled as:
with probability $p_{\mathsf{obs}}$ the transcript reveals $S$ (e.g., via corrupted committee members, prefix disclosure, or selective-failure side channels),
and with probability $1-p_{\mathsf{obs}}$ the transcript reveals no target-dependent identifier (an erasure symbol $\bot$).
This yields an explicit channel $C\mapsto Y\in \{\bot\}\cup \binom{\C}{w{+}1}$.

\begin{theorem}[Forced-destination+$w$-dummies under erasure]\label{thm:forced_erasure}
In the slice above,
\[
\MaxL(C\!\to\!Y)\;=\;\logtwo\!\Bigl((1-p_{\mathsf{obs}})+p_{\mathsf{obs}}\cdot \tfrac{K}{w{+}1}\Bigr)
=\logtwo\!\Bigl(1+p_{\mathsf{obs}}\bigl(\tfrac{K}{w{+}1}-1\bigr)\Bigr).
\]
\end{theorem}
\paragraph{Derivation.}
This is an immediate corollary of Paper~A's forced-destination-with-dummies MaxL identity and its erasure/partial-visibility rule, restated here for convenience.

\begin{corollary}[Dummy width required for a per-lookup budget]\label{cor:w_required}
If we require $\MaxL(C\!\to\!Y)\le b$ for one lookup in the slice, then necessarily
\[
w{+}1 \;\ge\; \frac{p_{\mathsf{obs}}K}{2^{b}-(1-p_{\mathsf{obs}})}\qquad (\text{when }2^{b}>(1-p_{\mathsf{obs}})).
\]
In particular, if $b=0$ then $w\ge K{-}1$ (full cover), and when $p_{\mathsf{obs}}$ is close to $1$, $w$ must be a large fraction of $K$ even for $b$ near $1$ bit.
\end{corollary}


\section{Budgeting a lookup under repetition}\label{sec:budget}
Let a single lookup produce step observations $Y_1,\dots,Y_L$ (routing hops, committee contacts, abort/timeout events).
Paper~A's accountant gives
\[
\MaxL(C\!\to\!Y_{1:L}) \;\le\; \sum_{\ell=1}^L \MaxL(C\!\to\!Y_\ell\mid Y_{<\ell}) \;\triangleq\; \Lambda_{\mathrm{lookup}}.
\]
Over $Q$ lookups in a window, the conservative Boss Fight budget is $\Lambda_{\mathrm{window}}\le Q\Lambda_{\mathrm{lookup}}$.
Thus even ``small'' per-lookup leakage becomes large at scale.

\subsection{Worked sizing example: why repetition dominates}\label{sec:worked}
The uniform-guess bound from Paper~A (and its conservative ``Boss Fight firewall'' repetition-cap corollary) implies that keeping an analyst's success probability below $\delta$ over a window of $Q$ lookups requires a \emph{total} budget $\Lambda\le \logtwo(\delta K)$ for $K$ plausible targets.
Thus the per-lookup average budget is $b=\Lambda/Q$, which quickly becomes $\ll 1$ bit.
In the minimal slice (Section~\ref{sec:minislice}), such tiny $b$ forces the dummy width $w$ to be nearly the entire candidate universe unless $p_{\mathsf{obs}}$ is extremely small.

A common pattern is ``forced destination + dummies'': contact the true shard plus $w$ dummy shards.
Paper~A gives the erasure-adjusted bound
\[
\MaxL \;=\; \logtwo\!\Big((1-p_{\mathsf{obs}})+p_{\mathsf{obs}}\cdot K/(w+1)\Big),
\]
where $K$ is the number of plausible destinations and $p_{\mathsf{obs}}$ is the probability the adversary \emph{sees} the contact.
Solving $\MaxL\le b$ yields a required dummy width; rather than restate it, we refer to Corollary~\ref{cor:w_required} (derived earlier for the same slice).

\paragraph{Approximation.}
When $b$ is tiny, $2^b-1\approx (\ln 2)\,b$.
If $p_{\mathsf{obs}}$ is not itself tiny, Corollary~\ref{cor:w_required} implies the scaling
\[
w \;\gtrsim\; \frac{p_{\mathsf{obs}}K}{p_{\mathsf{obs}}+(\ln 2)\,b}-1 \;\approx\; K\left(1-\frac{(\ln 2)\,b}{p_{\mathsf{obs}}}\right),
\]
i.e., $w$ must cover essentially the entire candidate universe.

\subsection{When can cover work?}\label{sec:coverworks}
Corollary~\ref{cor:w_required} has a sobering consequence: for large $K$, the required \emph{fraction} $w/K$ depends mainly on $(p_{\mathsf{obs}},b)$, not on $K$ itself.
Thus, ``just shrink the universe'' is not enough if the Boss Fight drives $b$ into the $10^{-2}$--$10^{-3}$ bit range.
Cover becomes practical only in regimes where (i) rate limiting (or coarse bucketing) keeps $b$ moderately large, and/or (ii) the implementation makes $p_{\mathsf{obs}}$ extremely small by shielding contacts.
Table~\ref{tab:cover_regimes} makes this explicit.

In the large-$K$ regime, ignoring the $\lceil\cdot\rceil$ rounding in Corollary~\ref{cor:w_required}, one obtains the convenient approximation
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:wreq}
  \frac{w}{K} \approx \frac{p_{\mathsf{obs}}}{2^b - 1 + p_{\mathsf{obs}}}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Implication for AnonDHT design.}
If your use case requires many lookups per window, you should plan on mechanisms that \emph{reduce observation} or \emph{hide the queried key} rather than only widening dummy sets.
In IPFS, this motivates both (a) delegated routing reader-privacy upgrades (encrypting content-routing requests in the HTTP delegated routing API) \cite{ipip0421_reader_privacy,ipfs_http_routing_v1} and (b) PIR-style approaches such as Peer2PIR \cite{mazmudar2025peer2pir}.

\section{Takeaways}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Dial the Boss Fight with two numbers:} observation probability $p_{\mathsf{obs}}$ and per-lookup MaxL budget $b$ essentially determine the dummy fraction via Eq.~\eqref{eq:wreq} and Corollary~\ref{cor:w_required}.
\item \textbf{Shrinking $K$ is not enough:} in the large-$K$ regime, $w/K$ depends mainly on $(p_{\mathsf{obs}},b)$, so ``smaller universes'' do not rescue low-latency anonymity once $b$ collapses into the $10^{-2}$--$10^{-3}$ bit range.
\item \textbf{Prefer hiding visibility over widening cover:} to keep overhead practical you must keep $b$ moderately large (rate-limit / bucket) and/or drive $p_{\mathsf{obs}}$ extremely small (shield contacts); otherwise cryptographic query privacy and reader-private routing (e.g., Peer2PIR; delegated-routing reader privacy) dominate cover work.
\item \textbf{How to cite / reuse:} use BossFight~A for budget definitions and MaxL$\leftrightarrow$PML equivalences; cite Anonymity~B for the profiling contract and Operational~A for retry discipline.
\end{itemize}
