\begin{abstract}
This note repairs a widely reused attenuation shortcut. A scalar statement that a transcript is ``observed with probability \(p\)'' does not imply the maximal-leakage transformation \(\log_2(1-p+p2^b)\). The identity requires a source-bound channel decomposition with a secret- and transcript-independent gate, or an equivalent common-noise mixture. Independence from the secret alone is insufficient: a gate can have the same firing rate for every secret while selecting exactly the revealing outputs. The revised note gives the exact theorem, an executable three-secret counterexample, the valid total-variation contraction, and the minimum evidence a relay or stub-mix receipt must carry. Scalar telemetry remains useful for monitoring but is non-authorizing by itself.
\end{abstract}

\section{The exact wrapper}\label{sec:wrapper}
Let \(Y\) be the complete declared observation generated under secret \(C\). Let \(G\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)\) be independent of the pair \((C,Y)\), and define
\[
Z=\begin{cases}Y,&G=1,\\ \bot,&G=0.\end{cases}
\]
where \(\bot\) is a secret-independent symbol. Equivalently,
\[
P_{Z\mid C=c}=(1-p)Q_0+pP_{Y\mid C=c}
\]
for one common distribution \(Q_0\) that does not depend on \(c\). The point-mass erasure is the special case \(Q_0=\delta_\bot\).

\begin{lemma}[Maximal-leakage attenuation under a common mixture]\label{lem:thin-maxl}
If \(\MaxL(C\to Y)\le b\), then
\[
\MaxL(C\to Z)\le \log_2\!\left((1-p)+p2^b\right).
\]
For a disjoint erasure symbol and exact \(b=\MaxL(C\to Y)\), equality holds.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For every output \(z\),
\[
\max_c P(Z=z\mid c)
\le (1-p)Q_0(z)+p\max_cP(Y=z\mid c).
\]
Summing over \(z\) gives at most \((1-p)+p2^b\). When the common and revealing supports are disjoint, the two contributions separate and the inequality is exact.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[TV contraction under the same common mixture]\label{lem:thin-tv}
For any two secrets \(c,c'\),
\[
\TV(P_{Z\mid c},P_{Z\mid c'})=p\,\TV(P_{Y\mid c},P_{Y\mid c'}).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The common component cancels, leaving \(p(P_{Y\mid c}-P_{Y\mid c'})\).
\end{proof}

The often quoted first-order approximation ``attenuated leakage is about \(pb\)'' is only a small-\(b\) expansion of Lemma~\ref{lem:thin-maxl} after the wrapper has been proved. It is not a way to infer the wrapper.

\section{Why equal observation rates are insufficient}\label{sec:counterexample}
Let \(C\in\{0,1,2\}\). Given \(c\), choose uniformly one of the two pairs containing \(c\). Orient the pairs so that \(0\) wins \(\{0,1\}\), \(1\) wins \(\{1,2\}\), and \(2\) wins \(\{0,2\}\). Reveal the pair iff the true secret is its winner; otherwise output \(\bot\).

The reveal gate fires with probability \(1/2\) for every secret, so \(G\) is marginally independent of \(C\). Nevertheless it is coupled to \((C,Y)\). The output column maxima sum to \(2\), giving
\[
\MaxL(C\to Z)=1\ \text{bit}.
\]
Treating \(p=1/2\), \(K=3\), and candidate-set width two as an independent erasure instead gives \(\log_2(1.25)=0.321928\) bits. Thus marginal gate independence from \(C\), equal per-secret reveal rates, and an auditable scalar \(p\) all fail to justify the attenuation identity.

Partial fields, noisy timestamps, multiple vantages, and correlated relay compromise do not ``reduce to'' independent thinning merely because each can be assigned a frequency. They require either their own common-mixture witness or a direct joint-channel bound.

\section{What a relay or stub-mix claim must prove}\label{sec:relay}
A relay layer may alter who links a client to a contact set, but the relevant output includes relay identity, ingress/egress timing, retry and fallback behavior, errors, and visible stopping. A receipt that claims Lemma~\ref{lem:thin-maxl} must bind:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item the complete pre-wrapper channel \(Y\) and post-wrapper observation \(Z\);
\item the common component \(Q_0\) or erasure symbol;
\item evidence that the gate parameter is secret- and transcript-independent over the declared horizon;
\item correlation across relays, rounds, tiers, retries, and fallback decisions;
\item source/configuration and evidence digests plus a drift rule;
\item either the resulting channel-level MaxL bound or all inputs needed to replay it.
\end{enumerate}

A monitored compromise or observation rate may accompany this witness as a health metric. It cannot replace the channel witness. If the gate is stateful or adaptive, the proof must be conditional on every reachable pre-round history.

\section{Receipt-facing interface}\label{sec:receipt}
The observation field should identify a mode, not just a number:
\begin{verbatim}
obs_model:
  mode: independent_erasure | common_mixture | direct_joint_bound
  observer_projection_id
  channel_witness_id
  channel_witness_sha256
  source_config_sha256
  gate_parameter: optional
  assumption_evidence[]
  adaptive_history_rule
  drift_invalidators[]
\end{verbatim}
A scalar \texttt{gate\_parameter} is meaningful only inside a recognized, source-bound mode. If the witness is absent, the receipt records \texttt{publication\_eligible=false}. \section{Takeaways}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item The attenuation formula is a channel theorem, not a telemetry conversion.
\item The gate must be independent of the pair \((C,Y)\), or the channel must admit an equivalent common-mixture decomposition.
\item Equal observation rates can coexist with much larger leakage.
\item Relay and stub-mix receipts must bind the complete observer channel, including correlation, retries, fallback, and stopping.
\item Missing channel evidence is publication-blocking, not a caveat to attach after the arithmetic.
\end{itemize}
