\begin{abstract}
Brief referee-grade papers often do exactly what this series is trying to do: publish the smallest auditable sentence and promise the fuller support bundle on request. Once answer publication profiles exist, the remaining drift point is no longer the emitted answer itself but the handoff policy for omitted support. One note may say ``ask for the archive'' and hand over a sprawling directory; another may send only a carrier slot and still another may forget the validator or the terminal-owner slice. This note gives that public-on-request handoff one home. It defines a compact challenge answer disclosure packet: one audience/question map from the public inline answer form to the exact requestable audit slice and validation paths that should be handed over when a reader asks for the deeper bundle omitted from a space-limited paper.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why answer disclosure packets deserve their own note}
Publication profiles already solve the inline side of the problem, and disclosure packets solve the exact requestable handoff.
They say which emitted answer card should be printed by default, which larger inline-provenance mode is allowed when space loosens, and which exact-location or full-audit objects later notes should cite when the terse form is no longer enough.
But a brief paper that explicitly omits the deeper artifacts still faces one more maintained question: if the referee asks for the omitted support, which exact archive slice should be handed over?
That is not identical to ``send the whole archive.''
A good handoff should preserve the public answer form, preserve the exact-location and full-audit escalation objects, include the narrow terminal-owner paths those objects rely on, and include enough manifest/validator support that the recipient can identify the maintained bundle cut rather than trust an informal email attachment.
If that handoff remains implicit, different papers will send different bundles for the same audience/question answer.
So the archive benefits from one thin disclosure object whose only job is to stabilize the public-on-request handoff. If a later paper needs to say exactly which maintained packet/menu cut sits behind its public ``supporting artifacts are available on request" sentence, that paper-facing aggregation belongs in Synthesis~56 rather than here; reusable evidence-class names for the omitted-support family behind the packet likewise belong in Synthesis~57 rather than in the packet itself; and exact completion criteria for when that promised packet cut has been fully satisfied belong in Synthesis~58 rather than in the packet itself.\cite{series:publicrequestcontracts,series:requestableevidenceclasses,series:requestfulfillmentcertificates}

\begin{definition}[Challenge answer disclosure packet]
Fix one concrete base$\to$successor comparison, one audience key $a$, and one downstream question key $q$.
A \emph{challenge answer disclosure packet} is a tuple
\[
D(a,q)=(a,q,P,C,E_{\mathrm{pub}},E_{\mathrm{exp}},K,T,\Gamma,\Lambda)
\]
containing one imported answer-publication-profile key $P$, one imported answer-citation-docket key $C$, one public-inline entry $E_{\mathrm{pub}}$, one expanded-inline entry $E_{\mathrm{exp}}$, one exact-location entry $K$, one full-audit entry $T$, an ordered requestable-archive path list $\Gamma$, and a validation-companion path list $\Lambda$.
The inline and escalation entries must agree with the imported publication profile, while the requestable-archive list names the exact artifact slice later notes should hand over when deeper support is requested.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Public-anchor rule]
A challenge answer disclosure packet satisfies the \emph{public-anchor rule} if its public-inline entry agrees exactly with the imported publication profile's default-inline entry and its expanded-inline entry agrees exactly with the imported profile's expanded-inline entry for the same emitted answer card.
So the disclosure packet does not invent a new public sentence or a new visible-provenance mode; it inherits the already-declared publication forms.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Requestable archive rule]
A challenge answer disclosure packet satisfies the \emph{requestable-archive rule} if every path in its requestable-archive list belongs to the maintained support manifest and the list is sufficient to recover: the imported publication profile, the companion citation docket, the exact carrier slot, the full audit trail, the supporting late-stage answer ledgers, and every narrow terminal-owner path named by that audit trail.
So the packet is neither a vague promise to share ``the archive'' nor an underspecified citation stub.
It is one maintained request slice.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Validation-companion rule]
A challenge answer disclosure packet satisfies the \emph{validation-companion rule} if its validation-companion list contains the support manifest, the artifact inventory, the current validation report, and the validator entry point needed to recheck the maintained bundle cut that the requestable archive paths belong to.
So the on-request handoff preserves not only evidence paths but also the bundle identity and recheck hook.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Disclosure packets stabilize public-on-request handoffs without changing answer ownership]
Assume every challenge answer disclosure packet satisfies the public-anchor rule, the requestable-archive rule, and the validation-companion rule.
Then a later note may cite one disclosure packet as the public-on-request handoff policy for one audience/question pair without changing the ownership of the emitted answer, exact carrier location, full audit walk, or validator semantics.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The public-anchor rule keeps the disclosure packet on the publication side of the series: it inherits the same emitted answer card and the same already-declared inline forms.
The requestable-archive rule prevents the packet from degenerating into an informal promise to share some unspecified directory later; it must name a manifest-listed slice sufficient to recover the exact-location and full-audit objects together with the narrow terminal-owner paths they rely on.
The validation-companion rule then prevents the handoff from dropping bundle identity or the recheck hook.
So the disclosure packet changes only packaging: it standardizes what to hand over when deeper support is requested, but it does not alter answer semantics, carrier ownership, or the audit walk itself.\qedhere
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why this helps the series.}
The series already had a stable path for public wording and a stable path for deep audit.
What it lacked was a stable bridge between those two under explicit space pressure.
A disclosure packet is that bridge: it lets a terse paper remain terse, but it also fixes what exactly ``ask for the archive'' means for one audience/question answer. The worked question-routing catalog now mirrors that disclosure-packet key together with a route-side disclosure-packet support-surface / manifest-cut / inventory-cut basis beside the emitted-answer and publication-budget bindings, so later terse notes can cite one maintained route object for both the visible sentence and the exact on-request archive slice that the frozen bundle binds.
But once the live question has already reached the checked-answer stop, later terse papers should usually cite the answer-side terminal citation normal form, then stop at Synthesis~73 for the maintained request-side terminal stop, drop to the worked publication-boundary card in Synthesis~17 when one concrete checked-answer/request-tail seam is wanted, and widen to the paired terminal discipline and paired cutover rule in Synthesis~74--75 only when the live issue is the abstract paired-tail discipline or abstract stop/widen/reopen judgment rather than that concrete seam; the packet reopens only when the exact answer-side requestable archive slice is itself the issue.\cite{series:challengeanswerreviewmenus,series:publicrequestresponsepacketrefreshresponsepacketclosureverdicts,series:pairedterminalcitationdiscipline,series:pairedterminalcutoverrules,series:workedexample}
That makes the omission policy auditable rather than merely polite.
