\title{Paired Terminal Cutover Rules for Stable Review Spines\\
\large When Later Terse Papers Must Stop, Widen, or Reopen}
\author{}

\begin{abstract}
Once the answer-side and request-side terminal citation normal forms are both available, a later terse paper still faces one remaining judgment question.
Is the answer-side stop already enough, must the paper widen to the adjacent request-side capstone, or has some named reopen condition fired so that neither terminal shortcut is honest anymore?
This note gives that cutover rule one home.
It adds no new owner.
It only states how later terse papers should choose among the already-owned answer-side terminal form, the already-owned request-side terminal form, and the named reopening owners that take control again when either shortcut has failed.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Non-redundancy note.}

This note owns only the cutover rule telling later terse papers when to stop at the answer-side terminal form, when to widen to the adjacent request-side terminal form, and when to fail closed and reopen a named owner instead.

\paragraph{Later-terse-paper citation posture.}
Later terse papers should cite Synthesis~75 only when the live issue is the cutover judgment itself: whether the checked-answer terminal form already suffices, whether the question has genuinely crossed into the adjacent request-side branch while the shipped closure basis still stands, or whether one of the named reopen conditions has regained control so the branch must fail closed.
They should stop at Synthesis~54 or Synthesis~73 when only one terminal form is actually live, stop at Synthesis~74 when the point is merely that the answer-side and request-side terminal forms may travel together as one paired discipline, stop at Synthesis~55 and then the first sufficient worked cut mirrored by Synthesis~17 when the live issue is still which adjacent owner bucket, bounded packet, or visible-refresh owner is live after leaving the answer-side archive cut, drop to the worked publication-boundary card in Synthesis~17 when one concrete checked-answer / request-tail stop-versus-widen seam is wanted rather than another abstract cutover summary, stop at Synthesis~55 when the point is the wider theorem-to-review bridge rather than this final cutover choice, stop at Synthesis~17 when the concrete maintained route rather than the abstract rule is the live issue, and fail closed to Synthesis~76 when the live issue has widened back above the publication boundary into theorem/worked/release/notary/claim/release stop selection rather than a final post-publication cutover choice.

\paragraph{Scope.}
This is not a new review menu, not a new source-only packet, not a new closure verdict, not a new bridge stage, and not a theorem-to-publication route.
It is a small routing rule for later terse papers operating under space pressure.

\section{Why the cutover deserves its own note}
Synthesis~74 already says that one answer-side stop and one adjacent request-side capstone may be cited together as a paired discipline.
But a later terse paper still needs one additional decision rule.
Sometimes the checked answer, its omitted-support cut, and the review-menu handoff are still the whole live question.
Sometimes the live question has really crossed into the adjacent source-only branch, so the paper should widen to the request-side capstone.
And sometimes the live question has reopened a named middle owner --- for example exact-location support, full-audit support, reveal-order support, or a visible-refresh branch whose shipped closure basis no longer holds.
Without one cutover rule, later terse notes still improvise that choice in prose.
So the archive benefits from one thin note that says exactly when later terse papers may stop, when they may widen, and when they must reopen. When a later paper needs that judgment as one auditable concrete seam rather than as an abstract three-question rule, it should then drop to the worked publication-boundary card in Synthesis~17 instead of restating the cutover logic in wrapper prose.

\begin{definition}[Answer-side sufficiency test]
Fix one audience/question branch whose checked-answer path already exports an answer-side terminal citation normal form in the sense of Synthesis~54.
A later terse paper satisfies the \emph{answer-side sufficiency test} if its live claim is still exhausted by that answer-side terminal form and does not require reopening any answer-side owner that Synthesis~54 explicitly leaves imported.
In the worked review vocabulary, that means the live claim is \emph{not} an \textsf{exact-location}, \textsf{full-audit}, or \textsf{reveal-order} dispute, and it has not yet widened beyond the answer-side archive cut into the adjacent source-only branch.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Request-side widening test]
Fix the adjacent source-only branch whose visible-refresh successor path already exports a request-side terminal citation normal form in the sense of Synthesis~73.
A later terse paper satisfies the \emph{request-side widening test} if the live claim has genuinely left the answer-side archive cut and now concerns the adjacent source-only branch, while the shipped visible-refresh branch still remains within the closure basis certified by the request-side terminal form. This test presupposes that the live issue has already passed the earlier adjacent owner-bucket choice: if the real question is still which paper-level promise/notice owner, bounded packet/reuse owner, or visible-refresh wording owner is live, then the cutover rule has not yet been reached and the paper should stop at that earlier owner instead.
In the worked example, that means the live question has widened from the answer-side stop into the paper-level public-request branch, while all three named request-side reopen triggers remain false:
\begin{quote}\small
\path{followup_taxonomy_expands}\par
\path{packet_reuse_reopens}\par
\path{visible_refresh_sentence_rebinds_to_new_delta_family}
\end{quote}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Fail-closed reopen rule]
A paired terminal citation discipline satisfies the \emph{fail-closed reopen rule} if a later terse paper must reopen the first named owner that has regained control whenever either terminal shortcut is no longer honest.
So:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item if the answer-side sufficiency test fails because the live claim has become \textsf{exact-location}, \textsf{full-audit}, or \textsf{reveal-order}, reopen the imported answer-side docket, audit, or escalation owner named by Synthesis~54; and
    \item if the request-side widening test fails because a named request-side reopen trigger has fired, reopen the request-side owner named by the closure verdict's reopen-owner map in the sense of Synthesis~73.
\end{itemize}
This rule is deliberately fail-closed: when in doubt, later terse papers must reopen the named owner rather than pretending that the terminal shortcut still covers the live question.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Later terse papers may apply the paired terminal cutover rule]
Assume one branch already has a valid answer-side terminal citation normal form, a valid adjacent request-side terminal citation normal form, and a valid paired terminal citation discipline in the sense of Synthesis~74.
Then a later terse paper may apply the paired terminal cutover rule as follows:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item stop at the answer-side terminal form whenever the answer-side sufficiency test holds;
    \item widen to the adjacent request-side terminal form whenever the answer-side sufficiency test fails only because the live question has genuinely left the answer-side archive cut and the request-side widening test still holds; and
    \item otherwise fail closed and reopen the first named owner that has regained control.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Synthesis~54 already says when the answer-side terminal form may travel without reopening its imported middle owners.
Synthesis~73 already says when the request-side terminal form may travel without reopening its imported middle owners, and it already records the named request-side reopen triggers and reopen-owner map.
Synthesis~74 already says that the two terminal forms may travel together as a paired discipline for one adjacent branch.
So the only remaining decision is which member of the pair is still honest for the live question.
If the live question is still exhausted by the checked answer, its omitted-support cut, and the review-menu handoff, the answer-side terminal form remains sufficient.
If the live question has genuinely crossed into the adjacent source-only branch but no request-side reopen trigger has fired, the request-side terminal form is the next honest stop.
If either side has reopened one of its named middle owners, a terminal shortcut would suppress the now-live owner, so the paper must fail closed and reopen that owner instead.\qedhere
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Three-question cutover ladder.}
For later terse papers the lemma above should usually be applied in one fixed order rather than as free prose.
Ask exactly three questions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item Is the live issue still exhausted by the checked answer, its omitted-support cut, and the review-menu handoff? If yes, stop at the answer-side terminal form.
    \item If not, has the live issue genuinely crossed the answer-side archive cut into the one adjacent source-only branch \emph{and} does the shipped request-side closure still stand? If yes, widen exactly once to the adjacent request-side terminal form.
    \item If not, which named reopen condition has already fired and which imported owner regains authority first? Reopen that owner and stop pretending that a terminal shortcut is still honest.
\end{enumerate}
This note owns only that test order.
It does not re-own the answer-side stop, the request-side capstone, or the reopened middle owners.

\paragraph{One tiny first-reopen-owner drill.}
If the live question becomes \emph{``where exactly is the sentence supported?''}, the cutover rule fails answer-side sufficiency and Table~\ref{tab:first-reopen-owner-card} sends the paper back to the imported citation-docket owner rather than to the request-side branch. If the live question becomes \emph{``in what order would more support be revealed?''}, the same table instead reopens the imported escalation ladder. If the live question stays request-side but \nolinkurl{packet_reuse_reopens} fires, the table routes the note to the exact request-side owner named by the shipped \nolinkurl{reopen_owner_map}. If the live issue is simply \emph{``why are these still the same paired terminals?''}, the note must widen to Synthesis~74 (and, if needed, to Synthesis~55 or Synthesis~17) before applying the cutover rule again.

\paragraph{One tiny yes/no drill.}
Suppose a referee first asks only whether the shipped compact-diff summary still answers the public claim. Q1 is yes, so a later terse paper stops at the answer-side terminal form. Suppose the next question instead asks only which notice-keyed response packet answers the widened follow-up. Then the cutover rule is still too late: the note must stop at the first adjacent owner bucket or bounded packet cut mirrored by Synthesis~17 before Q2 can succeed. Suppose instead the next question asks for the adjacent visible-refresh branch while the shipped closure verdict still certifies \nolinkurl{closed_finite_basis} over \nolinkurl{public_paths_only}. Then Q1 is no but Q2 is yes, so the paper widens exactly once to the request-side terminal form. Suppose instead that \nolinkurl{packet_reuse_reopens} has fired. Then Q2 is no and Q3 forces reopening the named request-side owner rather than citing either shortcut.

\begin{remark}[What the cutover rule buys]
The cutover rule reduces duplication without blurring ownership.
A terse later paper can now say, in one disciplined move, ``stop at the checked-answer terminal form unless the live question has become exact-location/full-audit/reveal-order or has genuinely crossed into the adjacent source-only branch; if it has crossed, widen only while the visible-refresh closure still stands; otherwise reopen the named owner.''
That is a sharper and safer reuse pattern than silently restitching the middle of the branch each time.
\end{remark}
