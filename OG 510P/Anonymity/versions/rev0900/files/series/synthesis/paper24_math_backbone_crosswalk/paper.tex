\begin{abstract}
This note is a non-redundancy aid.
The One-True-Archive relies on five already-published mathematics notes (canonical wiki links) for core mechanism design and lower bounds.
Downstream papers should not repeatedly re-explain that machinery.
We therefore record a compact crosswalk: what each backbone note exports as an interface, which series papers depend on it, and how to chain the exports into receipt-grade line items and endpoint sizing.
This is not a survey and does not introduce new primitives.
In particular, it distinguishes the theorem root, the receipt-facing landing zone, and the maintained replay/release stop that should be consulted once a certified claim changes.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Legacy-front-end posture.}
The five canonical wiki heads are not equally good \emph{front ends} for the new Anonymity series.
\texttt{wiki:scheduling} and \texttt{wiki:spectral} remain the two strongest public theorem roots and should stay the default old heads when those lines are live; for \texttt{wiki:spectral}, downstream uses must cite the corrected observation-scoped witness: exact expected fixed-kernel delegate-only $\chi^2$ leakage under the declared stationary prior uses the t-step Frobenius identity, common-stationary time-inhomogeneous schedules use the ordered-product analogue, hidden randomized lengths and hidden selectors use mixture witnesses only when the selector is independent of the initiator/path, while visible hopcount or visible selector identity uses the enlarged/revealed observation channel; adaptive or initiator-correlated selectors require the full observation channel or an evaluator-certified selection law. Downstream uses may use one-step singular-value powers only under normal/reversible side conditions, and must convert expected $\chi^2$ to a tail/pointwise receipt bound before receipt use (Markov, Cantelli when delegate-level variance is certified, exact weighted evaluator quantile, or stronger). A tail/quantile bound is first a high-probability realized PML / posterior-mass envelope; it should be advertised as channel-level MaxL only after a pointwise cap or the endpoint-bridge failure-mass fallback is applied. If the declared prior is not the stationary prior, or if the observer sees hopcount, stop time, latency, selector identity, randomized schedule choice, retry mode, or richer transcript metadata, the witness must be prior-aware and observation-channel-aware (or evaluator-certified) before this receipt-side conversion is applied; the PML/MaxL conversion uses the declared reference-prior minimum (serialized as literal fields \nolinkurl{prior_min}/\nolinkurl{reference_min}), not an unrelated stationary \texttt{pi\_min}. Joint observation scopes are a separate enlargement: individual side-channel or delegate witnesses do not compose by reuse, so a jointly visible transcript needs the product/full observation channel or an evaluator-certified composition bound. Adaptive transcript scopes are likewise full observation channels: marginal per-round checks or initial-prior witnesses do not compose by reuse when later fields are selected from earlier visible history, unless a uniform history-conditioned adaptive-composition certificate is present. Stochastic randomized-SVD/Hutchinson estimates are estimate-only unless a one-sided upper-confidence or deterministic-margin estimator certificate turns them into an upper bound. Success-only, abort-filtered, or retry-censored observations require \nolinkurl{conditioning_scope}/\nolinkurl{censoring_policy}: include the success/abort status or recompute the conditional channel under the conditioned prior. Hidden-mixture weights learned from finite calibration logs require \nolinkurl{mixture_law_source}, \nolinkurl{calibration_sample_count}, observed counts, \nolinkurl{weight_confidence}, \nolinkurl{weight_confidence_set}, and \nolinkurl{weight_uncertainty_policy}; a plug-in frequency vector is estimate-only unless the law is protocol-known or evaluator-certified. Learned or stale kernels, graph/topology snapshots, routing weights, observation channels, or stationary distributions likewise require \nolinkurl{kernel_source}, \nolinkurl{model_scope}, \nolinkurl{model_confidence}, \nolinkurl{kernel_uncertainty_set} or \nolinkurl{stationary_residual}, and \nolinkurl{model_uncertainty_policy}; a plug-in kernel is estimate-only.
\texttt{wiki:prefixcap} and \texttt{wiki:stopadd} remain valid canonical public links, but later Anonymity papers should treat them as narrower exact companions rather than as the first paper a new reader must open.
\texttt{wiki:knapsack} remains canonically citable because it is already public, but the new series should route around it by default: the paper is not self-contained enough to serve as the preferred current front end, so if poset-feasible padding becomes central the cleaner move is to write a fresh self-contained Anonymity paper and use the legacy note only as a historical / theorem root.
The frozen Many-Testing file under \nolinkurl{published/optional_many_testing_lower_bounds/paper.tex} is not a current public head at all; if that line becomes central, write a fresh Anonymity paper rather than inventing a new public target for the frozen file.

\paragraph{Four stable audit stops.}
A terse downstream paper usually needs to answer only four questions: which theorem or lower bound justifies the claim, where that claim lands on the public receipt surface, which maintained replay/release object becomes the next audit stop if that line item changes, and what the smallest maintained referee handoff should be if the question turns into review rather than mechanism explanation. This crosswalk owns the first question and the math-side landing of the second. Synthesis~17 gives one concrete instantiation of the second, Synthesis~32 owns the release-local stage cuts for the third, and Synthesis~55 owns the cross-series bridge to the fourth.\cite{series:workedexample,series:releasespine,series:seriesspines}

\paragraph{Later-terse-paper citation posture.}
Later terse papers should cite the relevant legacy \texttt{wiki:*} entry when the live issue is the theorem, lower bound, or mechanism itself, but they should not pretend that every old public head is equally good present-tense exposition: prefer \texttt{wiki:scheduling} or \texttt{wiki:spectral} as true theorem roots when those are the live lines, use \texttt{wiki:prefixcap} or \texttt{wiki:stopadd} only for narrower exact facts, and route around \texttt{wiki:knapsack} unless the historical root itself is what matters. Cite Synthesis~17 only when a concrete end-to-end instantiation or owner-map artifact is itself the live object; widen to Synthesis~32 or Synthesis~55 once the question is already about replay-local stage cuts, successor maintenance, or referee handoffs rather than the mathematics export boundary; and widen to Synthesis~76 only when the question has widened again into theorem/worked/release/notary/claim/release stop selection rather than theorem-root routing, one maintained bridge sentence, or one concrete worked cut. That division is the main reason this note is better treated as a maintained crosswalk than as a first-wave theorem, certificate, or release unit.\cite{series:workedexample,series:releasespine,series:seriesspines,series:theoremtopublicationspine}

\paragraph{Tiny routing drill.}
Suppose a later short paper needs only one sentence supporting a delegation claim of the form \emph{``this anonymous-DHT line item rests on a spectral random-walk witness under the declared state/separation guard.''} The honest compact route is: cite \texttt{wiki:spectral} for the theorem root, cite Anonymity~D for the exported per-step witness packet, and say explicitly whether the witness mode is conservative \texttt{spectral-uniform} or tail/pointwise \texttt{chi2-general} derived from a direct t-step, hidden-length, hidden-selector, visible-hopcount, visible-selector, or common-stationary ordered-product witness under the declared stationary prior, or from a prior-aware observation-channel/evaluator witness for a non-stationary or side-information scope, and whether the exported endpoint is a PML envelope or channel-level MaxL with failure-mass handling. Widen to Synthesis~19 or Synthesis~5 only if the live question has become horizon composition or endpoint sizing, and to Synthesis~55 only when the question has moved again from mathematical export routing to replay / release / referee handoff. The exported card should also record \texttt{prior\_scope}, \texttt{observation\_scope}, any \texttt{selector\_scope}, the declared literal fields \nolinkurl{prior_min}/\nolinkurl{reference_min}, and, for selector mixtures, either selector independence or the full \texttt{selection\_law}; changing any of those scopes reopens the spectral witness rather than merely reusing the previous receipt conversion, except when a richer witness is deliberately post-processed to a coarser observation with the coarsening map recorded. If the observer sees a joint observation assembled from several initiator-dependent components, cite the product/full observation channel or a composition certificate; if the observer sees a policy-adaptive or history-visible transcript, cite the full transcript channel or a uniform history-conditioned adaptive-composition certificate rather than marginal round witnesses; if the number came from stochastic trace/SVD estimation, cite the one-sided estimator certificate, keep estimator confidence separate from \nolinkurl{privacy_delta}, and mark it estimate-only unless that accounting is explicit. If the displayed certificate was selected from many stochastic candidates, also require \nolinkurl{candidate_family}, \nolinkurl{selection_rule}, and \nolinkurl{alpha_spending}/family-confidence accounting. If the selector or hopcount law was estimated from calibration counts, also require \nolinkurl{mixture_law_source}, \nolinkurl{weight_confidence}, and \nolinkurl{weight_uncertainty_policy}; plug-in weights alone reopen the witness. If the kernel, routing weights, stationary distribution, or observation channel was estimated from a snapshot or finite logs, also require \nolinkurl{kernel_source}, \nolinkurl{model_confidence}, and \nolinkurl{model_uncertainty_policy}; a plug-in kernel alone reopens the witness. If the exported number was rounded, truncated, or serialized from finite-precision arithmetic, also require \nolinkurl{numeric_error_policy}, \nolinkurl{rounding_direction}, and \nolinkurl{safety_margin} or a certified upper endpoint; display-rounded numeric values alone reopen the witness.

\section{Minimal pipeline sketch (how the pieces compose)}
This section is intentionally short: it is here so downstream papers can cite a single paragraph rather than re-stitching the same chain.

\paragraph{Delegation slice (spectral $\Rightarrow$ endpoint).}
A spectral witness bound from 2026-01-23 yields a per-step leakage bound (Anonymity~D) that can be published as a receipt line item bound $b$ in bits, either as a high-probability realized PML / posterior-mass envelope or, with a pointwise cap or failure-mass fallback, as a channel-level MaxL budget, possibly conditional on a state/separation guard. After the spectral identity repair, an exact theorem-side $\chi^2$ witness should be read as scoped to the declared observation channel: a delegate-only directed-kernel witness is the expected t-step Frobenius value $\|\mathcal D^t\|_F^2-1$, a common-stationary time-inhomogeneous walk uses the ordered-product analogue $\|\mathcal D_1\cdots\mathcal D_t\|_F^2-1$, hidden randomized length or hidden selector choice uses the corresponding mixture witness only under selector independence, adaptive or initiator-correlated selectors require the full observation channel/selection law, and visible hopcount or visible selector identity uses the enlarged/revealed observation channel; the one-step power sum $\sum_{i\ge2}\sigma_i(\mathcal D)^{2t}$ is only a normal/reversible shortcut/specialization. Before the witness becomes a receipt-side \texttt{chi2-general} card, the expected value must be converted to a tail/pointwise bound (for example by Markov $E_2/\delta$, by Cantelli when delegate-level variance is certified, by exact weighted quantile when delegate-level values are enumerated, or by an evaluator-certified pointwise/quantile certificate). A tail or quantile witness gives PML/posterior-mass semantics on the good event; channel-level MaxL requires a pointwise witness or the endpoint-bridge failure-mass fallback. If the declared prior scope is non-stationary, if the declared reference-prior minimum \nolinkurl{prior_min}/\nolinkurl{reference_min} is missing or mismatched, or if \texttt{observation\_scope} exposes hopcount, stop time, latency, or richer transcript metadata beyond the delegate, the theorem-side object must be prior-aware/observation-channel-aware, with zero-marginal observations trimmed to positive-marginal support before the Frobenius inverse is applied, before any of these conversions are used; a hidden-hopcount or hidden-selector witness cannot be reused for a visible-hopcount or visible-selector receipt, and an independent hidden-selector mixture cannot be used for an adaptive/path-dependent/initiator-correlated selector without materializing the full observation channel. tail/quantile PML evidence is endpoint-kind specific: rich-channel quantile evidence is not automatically a target-scope quantile after coarsening and must not be copied unchanged; it must be recomputed on the target channel or paired with a coarsening-contamination bound. pairwise/session claims require pairwise law and support evidence because average posterior $\chi^2$ alone is not support-safe. A richer observation-channel witness may safely upper-bound a coarser scope only when the post-processing/coarsening map is recorded; the reverse direction is fail-closed. Separation distance is not a posterior-spike or MaxL shortcut; upper posterior-mass/PML claims need max-relative-density, pointwise, or tail $\chi^2$ conversion evidence, with the declared reference-prior minimum used in that conversion. True-odds claims additionally require one-vs-complement likelihood-ratio evidence or Anonymity~A's guarded prior-cap conversion. Joint observation scopes must be certified by the product/full observation channel or evaluator composition bound rather than individual certificates; adaptive transcript scopes must be certified by the full transcript channel or a uniform history-conditioned adaptive-composition certificate rather than marginal per-round witnesses; and stochastic estimator outputs remain estimate-only until paired with a one-sided upper-bound certificate, and estimator confidence is separate from the \nolinkurl{privacy_delta} used for tail/PML/MaxL conversion; a single advertised failure-mass endpoint needs a stated union-bound or stronger evaluator policy. If the stochastic bound was selected from multiple candidate horizons, schedules, scopes, seeds, or estimator variants, the card must carry \nolinkurl{candidate_family}, \nolinkurl{selection_rule}, and \nolinkurl{alpha_spending} so the family-level confidence is visible. Success-only or abort/retry-censored traces must declare \texttt{conditioning\_scope}/\texttt{censoring\_policy}; include success/abort status in the observation channel or recompute the conditional channel under the conditioned prior. Calibration-estimated mixture laws must likewise declare \nolinkurl{mixture_law_source}, \nolinkurl{weight_confidence}, and a weight-set / empirical-law certificate; a plug-in hidden-mixture law is estimate-only. Learned or stale kernel/channel models must declare \nolinkurl{kernel_source}, \nolinkurl{model_scope}, \nolinkurl{model_confidence}, \nolinkurl{kernel_uncertainty_set} or \nolinkurl{stationary_residual}, and \nolinkurl{model_uncertainty_policy}; a plug-in kernel is estimate-only until the bound holds over that uncertainty set or carries evaluator certification. Numeric upper-bound fields are likewise receipt-scoped: rounded-down, truncated, or tolerance-unqualified \texttt{chi2-general}, PML, or MaxL numbers remain estimate-only unless \nolinkurl{numeric_error_policy}, \nolinkurl{rounding_direction}, and \nolinkurl{safety_margin} or a certified upper endpoint make the serialized value conservative.
For a horizon $n$ (or a declared retry window), compose using the checkpoint accountant (Anonymity~A / Synthesis~19) and translate to endpoint sizing using the bridge rule (Synthesis~5).\cite{series:spectral2budget,series:oddsinflation,series:receiptacct,series:endpointbridge}

\paragraph{Stop-time padding slice (TV $\Rightarrow$ receipt-grade evidence).}
A stop-time padding mechanism (2026-01-22/26) yields a TV-style guarantee on a declared termination-time witness family.
If the deployment uses feasibility constraints (calendar/poset), 2026-01-29 provides an optimization and a dual certificate that can be committed via ABOM/OINL and replayed (proof-carrying budgets).
Receipt-facing objects are: the exposure normal-form id (Synthesis~3), the threat-window id (Synthesis~4), and the replay hook binding the certificate plan spec (Synthesis~12/18/20).\cite{series:traceifaces,series:threatwindows,series:receiptitems,series:planstateequiv,series:auditorreplay,wiki:scheduling,wiki:stopadd,wiki:knapsack}

\paragraph{Backbone $\to$ receipt $\to$ release spine.}
For later maintenance, the import chain should stay explicit: backbone mathematics justifies a witness family or bound; the synthesis layer turns that export into a receipt line item and replay promise; the worked example's owner map says \emph{which note owns which field}; the worked example's release spine says \emph{how a published claim should evolve across releases}; and Synthesis~31 owns the final downstream sentence-normalization suffix once the task becomes stable public wording rather than new evidence. That separation is the main anti-redundancy gain: papers can cite the backbone for the theorem, the synthesis layer for the receipt interface, Synthesis~55 for the maintained theorem-to-review bridge, the release spine for successor-maintenance order, and Synthesis~31 only when they need reusable public wording that still resolves leftward to the narrower verdicts.\cite{series:receiptitems,series:planstateequiv,series:workedexample,series:downstreamnormalforms,series:seriesspines}

\section{Research prompts (where more work seems warranted)}
The backbone notes provide strong local results; several cross-paper ``glue'' problems remain worthwhile:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{End-to-end composition across heterogeneous witnesses.} Provide a single receipt-grade theorem that composes a delegation bound (spectral) with a stop-time padding bound (TV) under one threat-window declaration.
\item \textbf{Directed/weighted overlays in deployment.} The spectral note treats directed overlays; the series would benefit from a deployment-facing canonicalization of ``what graph object is committed'' when the overlay is dynamic.
\item \textbf{Certificate compression and witness minimization.} Dual certificates can be large; connecting the routing-signature compression work to padding/TV certificates is a practical next step.\cite{series:routingcompress}
\item \textbf{Separation/failure modeling.} For separation-qualified primary paths, publishing a credible $\rho$ remains operationally hard; the probabilistic separation note (Synthesis~23) lists evidence shapes, but calibrating them against modern reconnaissance and compromise rates is an open deployment discipline problem.\cite{series:probsep}
\end{itemize}
