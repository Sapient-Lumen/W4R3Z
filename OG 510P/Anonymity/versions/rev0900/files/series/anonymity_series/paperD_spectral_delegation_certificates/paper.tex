\large From mixing certificates to auditable per-step budgets}

\begin{abstract}
Random-walk delegation is a standard building block for ``anonymous'' DHT lookups:
a querier hands work to a relay chosen via a short walk on an overlay, and the relay performs the visible step.
The corrected published spectral anonymity note gives sharp distinguishing bounds for delegation in (possibly directed) overlays.
This paper extracts only the \emph{spectral-witness $\Rightarrow$ auditable per-step budget} corollary: a compact witness (e.g.\ a conservative second-singular-value bound, or a tail/pointwise $\chi^2$ bound derived from a direct t-step witness)
can be turned into a checked per-step realized posterior-mass inflation / PML envelope; when a receipt needs channel-level MaxL, the tail failure mass is folded in through the endpoint bridge before the value is advertised as MaxL bits. Jointly visible observations require the product/full observation channel or an evaluator composition certificate, adaptive/history-visible transcripts require the full transcript channel or a uniform history-conditioned adaptive-composition certificate, stochastic estimates require one-sided estimator certification plus separate estimator-confidence/privacy-tail and many-candidate selection accounting before they become bounds, success-only/abort-filtered traces require explicit conditioning scope plus a conditioned prior, truncated hidden-support witnesses require explicit tail-mass/remainder accounting, learned or stale kernels/channels require explicit model-source uncertainty certification, and serialized numeric upper-bound fields require conservative rounding/error-policy metadata.\cite{series:endpointbridge}
The paper stops there.  Notarized evaluation bundles, ABOM/OINL claim surfaces, and release-lineage / signed-receipt packaging belong to companion papers.\cite{series:notcert,series:abomoinl,series:releaseA}
Mechanism papers should cite this note (and the published spectral note) rather than re-deriving spectral-to-budget translations.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Backbone erratum posture.}
The May 2026 archive repair corrects that legacy note's exact expected-$\chi^2$ identity for non-normal kernels: the fixed-kernel theorem-side quantity is $\|\mathcal D^t\|_F^2-1$ (equivalently, the non-stationary singular spectrum of $\mathcal D^t$), and the common-stationary time-inhomogeneous theorem-side quantity is the Frobenius norm of the ordered discriminant product $\mathcal D_1\cdots\mathcal D_t$ with the stationary direction removed. The expression $\sum_{i\ge2}\sigma_i(\mathcal D)^{2t}$ is exact only under normal/reversible hypotheses. This paper's \texttt{spectral-uniform} mode uses the conservative contraction bound plus Markov's inequality, so its expected-to-tail arithmetic remains valid. The \texttt{chi2-general} mode must carry a tail/pointwise $\chi^2$ bound for the declared \texttt{prior\_scope} and \texttt{observation\_scope}: the declared t-step delegate-only kernel, visible-hopcount observation channel, hidden-length mixture, hidden selector mixture, visible selector channel, richer transcript channel, or common-stationary ordered product, derived from direct Frobenius/posterior evidence, a Markov/Cantelli tail conversion, an exact weighted evaluator quantile, or another evaluator certificate; a raw expected-Frobenius value is not itself a receipt-side \texttt{chi2\_bound}. A high-probability tail/quantile bound yields a realized posterior-mass inflation / PML envelope first.  It becomes a channel-level MaxL budget only if the bound is pointwise, or if the receipt applies the failure-mass fallback from the endpoint bridge. If \texttt{prior\_scope} is not the stationary prior used by the spectral theorem, the witness must be prior-aware (or evaluator-certified) rather than a stationary-prior shortcut reused by analogy; likewise, if \texttt{observation\_scope} exposes visible-hopcount, stop-time, latency, selector identity, randomized schedule choice, retry mode, or transcript metadata, the witness must be an observation-channel certificate for that scope rather than a hidden-hopcount/delegate-only shortcut. A certificate for a richer observation may be reused for a coarser scope only when the receipt declares the post-processing/coarsening map; a coarser hidden-hopcount, hidden selector, or delegate-only witness cannot be promoted to a richer visible selector or visible-transcript scope. A rich-channel quantile is not automatically a target-scope quantile after coarsening; reuse must recompute the target bound or include a coarsening-contamination certificate. Pairwise/session claims also need pairwise law and support evidence, because average posterior $\chi^2$ is not by itself a support-safe linked-session certificate. A serialized \texttt{separation\_distance} field is a lower-coverage diagnostic, not a posterior-spike or MaxL cap; upper posterior-mass/PML claims require max-relative-density, pointwise, tail-converted, or stronger evidence, while true-odds claims additionally require one-vs-complement likelihood-ratio evidence or the guarded prior-dependent conversion from Anonymity~A. Observation-channel receipts with declared transcript alphabets use \texttt{support\_policy=positive-marginal}: zero-marginal observations are trimmed before any $m^{-1/2}$ Frobenius or posterior calculation and cannot be inverted into evidence. If multiple initiator-dependent observations are jointly visible, the receipt must set \nolinkurl{joint_observation_scope} and use the product/full observation channel or an evaluator composition certificate rather than the maximum of individual witnesses. If later transcript fields, retries, route choices, or telemetry are selected after earlier visible observations, the receipt must set \nolinkurl{transcript_scope} and either materialize the full transcript channel or cite a \nolinkurl{adaptive_composition} certificate that is uniform over history-conditioned priors, positive support, endpoint kind, and failure-budget allocation; marginal/initial-prior per-round witnesses are not transcript certificates. Randomized SVD/Hutchinson outputs are estimate-only unless the receipt carries \nolinkurl{estimator_certification} with a one-sided UCB/deterministic-margin policy and the final certified upper bound.  The estimator-confidence parameter is separate from the privacy-tail parameter: receipts should serialize \nolinkurl{estimator_confidence} or \nolinkurl{estimator_alpha} and \nolinkurl{privacy_delta}, and if a single failure-mass endpoint is advertised, state the union-bound or stronger evaluator policy used to combine them. If the certified value was selected after evaluating many stochastic candidate horizons, schedules, seeds, transcript scopes, or estimator variants, the receipt must also serialize \nolinkurl{candidate_family}, \nolinkurl{selection_rule}, and \nolinkurl{alpha_spending}/family confidence; a per-candidate confidence is not a selected-certificate confidence unless simultaneous validity or an always-valid/evaluator-certified selection rule is stated. Success-only, abort-filtered, retry-censored, or post-selected logs must carry \nolinkurl{conditioning_scope} and \nolinkurl{censoring_policy}: either success/abort status is included in the observation channel, or the witness is recomputed on the conditional channel under the conditioned prior and its own \texttt{prior\_min}/\texttt{reference\_min}. If a hidden-length, hidden-selector, retry, schedule, or transcript witness was computed on a finite retained prefix of a larger support, the receipt must carry \nolinkurl{truncation_tail_mass} plus \nolinkurl{remainder_chi2_cap} or a worst-case reference-minimum fallback; a renormalized prefix value alone is estimate-only for the full hidden mixture. If hidden-hopcount, hidden-selector, retry, or schedule-mixture weights are estimated from calibration data rather than fixed by protocol, the receipt must also carry \nolinkurl{mixture_law_source}, \nolinkurl{calibration_sample_count}, \nolinkurl{observed_selector_counts} or hopcount counts, \nolinkurl{weight_confidence}, \nolinkurl{weight_confidence_set}, and \nolinkurl{weight_uncertainty_policy}; the serialized bound is evaluated over that certified weight set, while a plug-in frequency vector is estimate-only. If the kernel, graph/topology digest, routing weights, stationary distribution, or observation channel is learned, stale, or approximate, the receipt must also carry \nolinkurl{kernel_source}, \nolinkurl{model_scope}, \nolinkurl{model_confidence}, \nolinkurl{kernel_uncertainty_set} or \nolinkurl{stationary_residual}, and \nolinkurl{model_uncertainty_policy}; a plug-in kernel is estimate-only unless the model uncertainty set or evaluator certificate is accounted for. Numeric exports are also scoped: if a computed \texttt{chi2\_bound}, PML-bit field, or MaxL-bit field is rounded, truncated, or serialized from finite-precision arithmetic, the card must carry \nolinkurl{numeric_error_policy}, \nolinkurl{rounding_direction}, precision metadata, and either \nolinkurl{safety_margin} or \nolinkurl{certified_upper_endpoint}; rounded-down or tolerance-unqualified values remain estimate-only.

\section{Delegation model and spectral witness}
\label{sec:model}

We adopt the delegation setting of the published spectral anonymity paper.\cite{series:spectral}
A start node $S$ (the querier, or the querier's entry relay) initiates a length-$t$ delegation walk on an $n$-node overlay.
The observed delegate $D$ is the terminal node of the walk (or, more generally, the node that performs a visible lookup step).

The corrected published note defines a discriminant operator for possibly directed kernels, gives the exact t-step Frobenius identity for expected posterior $\chi^2$, and shows that a conservative contraction rate is controlled by a
second singular value $\sigma_2\in[0,1)$ of that operator (or a conservative certificate upper-bounding it).\cite{series:spectral}
For \texttt{spectral-uniform}, the \emph{witness} is simply: the overlay size $n$, the walk length $t$, the kernel identifier (a digest), kernel/model-source certification when that digest is learned or stale, and an auditor-checkable bound $\sigma_2$ under the declared stationary prior. For \texttt{chi2-general}, the digest may instead name the declared t-step kernel, common-stationary schedule, hidden selector mixture, visible selector channel, visible-hopcount or hidden-hopcount observation channel, richer transcript channel, adaptive transcript channel, or prior-aware evaluator scope, but the serialized \texttt{chi2\_bound} must be the tail/pointwise value consumed by \cref{prop:chi2-to-maxl}, not merely the expected Frobenius witness unless the witness also records the tail conversion used.

\begin{remark}[Why a spectral witness fits receipts]
A receipt cannot enumerate a huge conditioning space (all possible route histories), but it \emph{can} commit to a small, digest-bound kernel and a small numerical witness.
Regime~II conditional certificates (Synthesis~21) are designed to carry exactly that shape: a conditioning interface identifier plus a compact evidence object.\cite{series:condcert}
\end{remark}


\section{A conservative spectral-to-budget translation}
\label{sec:translation}

The corrected published spectral note provides (among stronger t-step Frobenius/TV results) a conservative bound on expected $\chi^2$ leakage of the posterior on $S$ given $D$.\cite{series:spectral}
We record the corollary that survives the non-normal identity repair unchanged and yields an auditable realized posterior-mass inflation / PML envelope in bits with a tail parameter $\delta$.  A channel-level MaxL value requires the extra failure-mass conversion below.

\begin{proposition}[Spectral contraction $\Rightarrow$ a high-probability per-step PML envelope (uniform prior)]
\label{prop:spectral-to-maxl}
Assume the stationary prior $\pi$ is uniform on $n$ nodes.
Let $\sigma_2$ be an upper bound on the second singular value of the discriminant operator for the delegation kernel (as defined in the published spectral note).\cite{series:spectral}
Let $\mu_D(\cdot)\defeq \PP[S=\cdot\mid D]$ be the posterior on $S$ given the observed delegate $D$ and let
$\ell(D)\defeq \log_2\max_s \mu_D(s)/\pi(s)$ be the realized posterior-mass inflation / PML.
Then for any $\delta\in(0,1)$, with probability at least $1-\delta$ over $D\sim\pi$,
\[
\ell(D)
\;\le\;
\log_2\!\Big(1+\sqrt{n(n-1)}\,\sigma_2^{t}/\sqrt{\delta}\Big)\ \text{bits}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The corrected published spectral note gives
$\EE_{D\sim\pi}\!\left[\chi^2(\mu_D\|\pi)\right]\le (n-1)\sigma_2^{2t}$.\cite{series:spectral}
Markov's inequality yields $\chi^2(\mu_D\|\pi)\le (n-1)\sigma_2^{2t}/\delta$ with probability at least $1-\delta$.
For uniform $\pi$, $\chi^2(\mu\|\pi)=n\sum_s(\mu(s)-1/n)^2$, so
$\|\mu-\pi\|_2 = \sqrt{\chi^2(\mu\|\pi)/n}$ and $\|\mu-\pi\|_\infty\le \|\mu-\pi\|_2$.
Thus $\max_s \mu(s)\le 1/n+\sqrt{\chi^2(\mu\|\pi)/n}$, implying
$\max_s \mu(s)/\pi(s)\le 1+\sqrt{n\,\chi^2(\mu\|\pi)}$.
Taking $\log_2$ gives the stated high-probability PML envelope.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What this bound is (and is not)]
This corollary is intentionally conservative and assumes a uniform stationary prior.
It is nevertheless useful as a receipt-side \emph{auditable} good-event cap: the parameters $(n,t,\sigma_2,\delta)$ are small, and the auditor can recompute the right-hand side.  It is not a channel-level MaxL budget unless the receipt either sets $\delta=0$ through a pointwise certificate or applies the failure-mass fallback in \cref{prop:chi2-to-maxl}.
Sharper and more general statements (non-uniform priors, directed kernels, optimal tests, direct t-step Frobenius witnesses, and prior-aware observation-channel witnesses) live in the corrected published spectral note.\cite{series:spectral}
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[From a $\chi^2$ witness to PML and MaxL caps (non-uniform priors)]
\label{prop:chi2-to-maxl}
Let $\rho$ be the declared reference prior on $S$ with $\rho_{\min}\defeq \min_s \rho(s)>0$.
For any posterior $\mu$ on $S$,
\[
\max_s \frac{\mu(s)}{\rho(s)}\;\le\;1+\sqrt{\chi^2(\mu\|\rho)/\rho_{\min}}.
\]
Consequently, if an evidence object implies that with probability at least $1-\delta$ (over $D$) we have $\chi^2(\mu_D\|\rho)\le B$, then with the same probability the realized posterior-mass inflation / PML obeys
\[
b_{\mathrm{PML}}(B)=\log_2\min\Big\{1+\sqrt{B/\rho_{\min}},\,1/\rho_{\min}\Big\}.
\]
If the same receipt claims channel-level MaxL rather than a high-probability PML envelope, the endpoint-bridge moment identity and the universal fallback $\max_s\mu_D(s)/\rho(s)\le 1/\rho_{\min}$ give
\[
\MaxL(S\!\to\!D)
\le
\log_2\!\Big((1-\delta)2^{b_{\mathrm{PML}}(B)}+\delta/\rho_{\min}\Big).
\]
For a pointwise $\chi^2$ cap, set $\delta=0$.  In stationary-uniform special cases $\rho_{\min}$ may be serialized as $\pi_{\min}$ or $1/n$; otherwise the card must carry the actual declared reference-prior minimum, because an unrelated stationary $\pi_{\min}$ can understate the posterior-mass/PML cap.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By definition, $\chi^2(\mu\|\rho)=\sum_s (\mu(s)-\rho(s))^2/\rho(s)$, so for each $s$,
$(\mu(s)-\rho(s))^2/\rho(s)\le \chi^2(\mu\|\rho)$ and hence
$|\mu(s)-\rho(s)|\le \sqrt{\rho(s)\,\chi^2(\mu\|\rho)}$.
Therefore
$\mu(s)/\rho(s)\le 1+\sqrt{\chi^2(\mu\|\rho)/\rho(s)}\le 1+\sqrt{\chi^2(\mu\|\rho)/\rho_{\min}}$.
Take $\log_2$ for the PML envelope.  For MaxL, use the endpoint bridge $\MaxL(S\!\to\!D)=\log_2\EE[2^{\ell(D)}]$, the good-event cap $2^{\ell(D)}\le 2^{b_{\mathrm{PML}}(B)}$, and the failure-event cap $2^{\ell(D)}\le 1/\rho_{\min}$.
\end{proof}


\section{Handoff to certificates and release objects}
\label{sec:receipt-use}

A deployment that uses delegation to hide a querier's identity (or entry relay) can publish a Regime~II per-step conditional certificate whose evidence object is:

\begin{quote}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}l@{}}
\path|kernel_digest, n, t, sigma2_upper, delta|\\
\path|or|\\
\path|kernel_or_schedule_digest, prior_scope, chi2_bound,|\\
\path|  prior_min/reference_min, delta.|
\end{tabular}
\end{quote}

The replay plan commits to the kernel or schedule digest and the numerical witness.
An auditor recomputes the per-step cap $b_t$ from \cref{prop:spectral-to-maxl} or \cref{prop:chi2-to-maxl} and records only that checked conditional budget for the step.
Adaptive horizon accounting then composes the checked per-step caps additively (Synthesis~19), without assuming independence.\cite{series:accountingrulebook,series:condcert}

\paragraph{Earliest sufficient stop.}
For a conservative audit spine, the delegation argument can stop after three steps:
(1) cite the published spectral mathematics note for the kernel family;\cite{series:spectral}
(2) attach the compact witness from this paper and recompute the per-step budget cap; and
(3) hand that checked cap either to the notarized evaluation bundle (Eval~1) or to the ABOM/OINL + Release~A publication surface.\cite{series:notcert,series:abomoinl,series:releaseA}
Nothing in this paper requires re-explaining those later layers.

\paragraph{Serialization rule.}
To keep certificate-facing companions from re-specifying this translation, the replay object should use exactly one of the following two minimal modes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{\texttt{spectral-uniform}:} \texttt{kernel\_digest, n, t, sigma2\_upper, delta} with the auditor recomputing
\[
 b_{\mathrm{PML}} = \log_2\!\Big(1+\sqrt{n(n-1)}\,\sigma_2^t/\sqrt{\delta}\Big)
\]
as the high-probability PML envelope from \cref{prop:spectral-to-maxl}, and applying the failure-mass fallback from \cref{prop:chi2-to-maxl} before serializing \texttt{derived\_maxl\_bits};
\item \textbf{\texttt{chi2-general}:} \texttt{\{ kernel\_or\_schedule\_digest, prior\_scope, observation\_scope, chi2\_bound, prior\_min/reference\_min, delta \}} with the auditor recomputing
\[
 b_{\mathrm{PML}} = \log_2\min\Big\{1+\sqrt{\chi^2\_\text{bound}/\rho_{\min}},\,1/\rho_{\min}\Big\}
\]
from \cref{prop:chi2-to-maxl}, then recomputing \texttt{derived\_maxl\_bits} only if the witness is pointwise or the failure-mass fallback is applied. Here $\rho_{\min}$ is the minimum mass of the declared reference prior (serialized as \texttt{prior\_min} or \texttt{reference\_min}); \texttt{chi2\_bound} is a true tail/pointwise bound for the declared t-step posterior leakage and prior/observation scope, where \texttt{observation\_scope} distinguishes delegate-only, hidden-hopcount, visible-hopcount, stop-time, latency, and richer transcript channels; richer-to-coarser reuse must carry a declared post-processing map, while coarser-to-richer reuse is forbidden. A direct fixed-kernel Frobenius/posterior witness, hidden-length mixture witness, visible-hopcount/revealed-length witness, richer observation-channel witness, common-stationary ordered-product witness, or prior-aware evaluator witness supplies an expected value $E_2$; before serialization it must be converted to a receipt-side bound (for example \texttt{chi2\_bound}$=E_2/\delta$ with \texttt{tail\_source=markov}, a mean/variance Cantelli cap with \texttt{tail\_source=cantelli}, an exact weighted quantile with \texttt{tail\_source=evaluator\_quantile}, or an evaluator-certified pointwise cap). It is not $\sum_{i\ge2}\sigma_i(\mathcal D)^{2t}$ unless the normal/reversible side condition is also certified, and it is not a raw expectation unless the receipt layer explicitly asks for expectation rather than a PML/MaxL budget.
\end{enumerate}
Any richer theorem-side object (directed-kernel t-step Frobenius witness, hidden-hopcount mixture, visible-hopcount/revealed-length channel, common-stationary time-varying schedule digest, class-prior certificate) should be reduced to one of these two replay modes before it reaches the receipt layer; for \texttt{chi2-general}, that reduction includes recording whether the tail/pointwise bound came from Markov, Cantelli with certified delegate-level variance, an exact weighted evaluator quantile, or a stronger analytic certificate, whether the prior scope was stationary or prior-aware, the declared reference-prior minimum used in the PML conversion, whether the selector was independent or required a full selection-law/observation-channel witness, whether the observation scope was delegate-only or metadata-visible, whether any post-processing/coarsening map justified richer-to-coarser reuse, and whether the exported endpoint is a high-probability PML envelope or a channel-level MaxL value with failure-mass fallback.
That keeps this paper small while still making the exported witness object explicit enough for Regime~II certificates and Eval~1 bundles to reuse without reinterpretation.\cite{series:condcert,series:notcert}

\paragraph{Arithmetic drill.}
The arithmetic is intentionally mechanical: $\sqrt{1024\cdot 1023}\approx 1023.50$, $0.25^8=1/65536$, and so the inner factor is about $0.1562$; hence $b_t=\log_2(1+0.1562)\approx 0.209$ bits. An auditor does not need the walk transcript or a deployment diary here---only the pinned kernel digest, the declared witness, and the deterministic recomputation rule from \cref{prop:spectral-to-maxl}.

\paragraph{Minimal fail-closed reuse rule.}
A later terse paper may reuse the minimal spectral-witness card only while five things remain fixed: the maintained carrier/replay route (the same \texttt{primary\_path\_declaration} row together with the same \texttt{eval-primary-v1} / plan-spec digest pair when the worked bundle is being cited), the \texttt{kernel\_digest}, the \texttt{witness\_mode}, the numerical witness tuple used in the recomputation, and the immediate downstream handoff target (checked cap into Anonymity~B's declared profiling card versus checked cap into Eval~1 or ABOM/OINL$+\,$Release~A). If the kernel schedule changes, the worked carrier row changes, the replay promise changes, the witness mode changes, the prior scope changes, the observation scope changes, hopcount or stop-time metadata becomes visible, multiple side-channel components become jointly visible without a product/full observation-channel witness, a history-visible/adaptive transcript lacks a full transcript-channel or \nolinkurl{adaptive_composition} certificate, a \texttt{chi2-general} witness was computed from one-step singular powers without a normal/reversible certificate, a raw expected-Frobenius value is used as \texttt{chi2\_bound} without a recorded tail/pointwise conversion (Markov, Cantelli, exact weighted evaluator quantile, or stronger), a non-stationary prior scope lacks a prior-aware witness, the serialized \texttt{prior\_min}/\texttt{reference\_min} does not match the declared reference prior, an adaptive/path-dependent/initiator-correlated selector lacks a \texttt{selector\_independence} or \texttt{selection\_law} certificate, a stochastic Hutchinson/randomized-SVD value lacks one-sided \nolinkurl{estimator_certification}, a visible-hopcount or richer-transcript scope lacks an observation-channel witness, a claimed coarser observation reuse lacks a post-processing map from the richer witness, or the question becomes the enclosing packet / lineage surface rather than the checked cap itself, fail closed and reopen either this note, the published Mathematics spectral note, or the downstream owner that now carries the live issue.\cite{series:spectral,series:workedexample,series:profiling,series:notcert,series:abomoinl,series:releaseA}

This paper does not add new public-facing fields.
The evidence object lives behind the existing replay hook (plan identifier + plan-spec digest), while Eval~1 owns the notarized evaluator bundle and Release~A owns the release-facing lineage / signed-receipt layer.\cite{series:auditorreplay,series:notcert,series:releaseA}

\paragraph{Prior-scoped budgets.}
If the operator budgets ``user within a class'' and the declared prior is the stationary distribution restricted by the theorem/evaluator scope, keep the outward claim surface unchanged: include a prior certificate parameter such as $\rho_{\min}$ (equal to $\pi_{\min}$ only when the stationary prior is the declared reference prior) and use \cref{prop:chi2-to-maxl} to translate any tail/pointwise $\chi^2$ witness into a PML envelope and, if needed, a channel-level MaxL cap with the failure-mass fallback. If the class prior is not stationary for the declared kernel, the theorem-side object must instead be a prior-aware posterior/Frobenius witness or an evaluator-certified bound for that exact \texttt{prior\_scope}; the stationary spectral witness cannot be reused by analogy. The corrected published spectral note owns those theorem-side distinctions; this paper only packages the final auditor-side tail/pointwise conversion.\cite{series:spectral}

\paragraph{A tiny class-budget drill.}
Take \texttt{prior\_scope=destination-class}, a tail/pointwise $\texttt{chi2\_bound}=4\times 10^{-3}$, and declared \texttt{prior\_min} $=\rho_{\min}=0.05$. Then
\[
 b_t = \log_2\!\Big(1+\sqrt{0.004/0.05}\Big)=\log_2(1+\sqrt{0.08})\approx \log_2(1.2828) \approx 0.360\text{ bits}.
\]
So the public sentence can stay compact: \emph{``the class-scoped delegation witness checks to at most about $0.36$ bits per step and hands only that checked class cap onward.''} If \texttt{prior\_scope} or the declared \texttt{prior\_min}/$\rho_{\min}$ changes, the outward wrapper may still look similar, but the checked cap no longer comes from the same witness card and a later terse paper should fail closed back to this note.

\section{Research warranted}
The most useful refinements are interface refinements, not longer papers:
(i) non-uniform priors and class-based priors (so the budget targets ``user within a set'');
(ii) directed overlays and time-varying kernels (so the kernel digest is a schedule and any theorem-side exact expected-$\chi^2$ witness is a t-step Frobenius/schedule witness rather than a one-step singular-power shortcut, then is converted to a tail/pointwise receipt bound before MaxL use); and
(iii) a tiny standardized field vocabulary for spectral witnesses so the same witness can flow unchanged through Eval~1 and Release~A.
Both (i) and (ii) are treated in the corrected published spectral note at the level of optimal distinguishing bounds and t-step leakage identities;\cite{series:spectral}
the remaining work is to package those bounds into compact, auditor-friendly evidence objects rather than to enlarge the theorem surface.
