\begin{abstract}
Anonymous DHT lookups expose destination-indexed contact, timing, retry, and failure transcripts even when payloads are encrypted.
This revision withdraws the earlier claim that a scalar observation probability can be compiled directly into a maximal-leakage dial sheet.
The formula
\(\log_2((1-p)+pK/(w+1))\)
is exact for a proved independent all-or-nothing erasure channel; it is not a consequence of a compromise fraction, a contact count, or equal per-secret reveal rates.
A three-secret cyclic-reveal construction has reveal probability \(p=1/2\) for every secret, yet leaks one full bit rather than the \(0.321928\) bits reported by the scalar erasure model.
Likewise, \(1-(1-\rho)^m\) is exact only under independent compromise events; the same contact marginals permit any-hit probabilities throughout \([\rho,\min(1,m\rho)]\).
The repaired dial sheet therefore begins with a source-bound observation-channel witness or a direct channel-level leakage bound. The retained Kademlia and dummy-width numbers are sensitivity analyses for an explicit control model, not deployment certificates.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Scope.}
This note states the minimum channel evidence required before a Boss-Fight budget may be compiled into anonymous-DHT knobs. It does not certify IPFS, Kademlia, a delegated-routing service, or any deployed observer model.

\paragraph{Imports and exports.}
Boss Fight~A owns maximal-leakage accounting and repeated-use composition. The threat/window and trace-interface notes own the declared secret, public side information, projection, observer, unit, horizon, and reset rule. This note exports one corrected model-extraction gate and one exact, deliberately narrow control model.\cite{series:bossfightA,series:threatwindows,series:traceifaces}

The counterexample and algebra needed to assess this revision are in the source. No unavailable trace, code bundle, or deployment measurement is treated as a certificate. The companion addendum may retain tables only as control-model regression evidence.\cite{series:bossfightBaddendum}

\section{The missing object is a channel, not a scalar}\label{sec:channel}
Let \(C\in\C\) be the protected lookup target and let \(Y\in\Y\) be the complete declared adversary observation, including any visible stop, abort, retry, timing, or failure behavior. For a finite channel,
\[
2^{\MaxL(C\to Y)}=\sum_{y\in\Y}\max_{c\in\supp(C)}\Prb[Y=y\mid C=c].
\]
After an adaptive history \(H=h\), the maximization is over \(\supp(C\mid H=h)\), not over secrets already ruled out by the history.

A monitored quantity such as
\[
p_{\mathsf{obs}}(c)=\Prb[Y\neq\bot\mid C=c]
\]
constrains only the total non-erasure mass. It does not specify which transcript appears on that mass. Even equality of \(p_{\mathsf{obs}}(c)\) across all secrets does not identify an erasure channel.

\begin{definition}[Independent all-or-nothing erasure wrapper]\label{def:erasure}
A channel has the required wrapper when there is a Bernoulli gate \(G\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)\), independent of the pair \((C,V)\), such that
\[
Y=\begin{cases}
\bot,&G=0,\\
V,&G=1,
\end{cases}
\]
and \(\bot\) is outside the support of \(V\). Equivalently,
\[
W=(1-p)E+pV,
\]
where \(E\) is a secret-independent point mass and the mixture weight is secret- and transcript-independent.
\end{definition}

A receipt may use an erasure attenuation identity only when it binds either (i) a witness proving Definition~\ref{def:erasure} for the complete declared observation, or (ii) a direct channel-level bound that already accounts for the actual reveal rule. A scalar reveal frequency, compromise fraction, or tier vector is not such a witness.

\section{Equal observation rates can still reveal the secret}\label{sec:counterexample}
\begin{theorem}[Cyclic reveal defeats scalar attenuation]\label{thm:cyclic}
Let \(\C=\{0,1,2\}\). For secret \(c\), choose uniformly one of the two unordered pairs containing \(c\). Orient the three pairs so that \(0\) wins \(\{0,1\}\), \(1\) wins \(\{1,2\}\), and \(2\) wins \(\{0,2\}\). Reveal the selected pair iff \(c\) is its winner; otherwise output \(\bot\).
Then every secret has reveal probability \(1/2\), but
\[
\MaxL(C\to Y)=1\ \bits.
\]
Under a uniform prior, the optimal exact-guess probability is \(2/3\).
By contrast, substituting \(K=3\), \(w=1\), and \(p=1/2\) into the independent-erasure formula gives
\[
\logtwo\!\left(\tfrac12+\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac32\right)
=\logtwo(1.25)
=0.321928\ \bits
\]
and an exact-guess probability of only \(5/12\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each secret, exactly one of its two candidate pairs is revealed, hence the reveal rate is \(1/2\). The output alphabet is \(\{\bot,\{0,1\},\{1,2\},\{0,2\}\}\). The column maximum is \(1/2\) for \(\bot\) and \(1/2\) for each revealed pair, so the sum of column maxima is \(2\). Under the uniform prior, optimal exact guessing is \(\frac13\sum_y\max_c\Prb[Y=y\mid C=c]=2/3\). The independent-erasure comparison instead has column-max sum \(1/2+3(1/4)=5/4\).
\end{proof}

The defect is structural: the reveal decision in Theorem~\ref{thm:cyclic} depends on the secret and the candidate pair. No amount of scalar calibration can recover the missing joint law.

\section{Contact marginals do not determine any-hit observation}\label{sec:contacts}
Let \(A_j\) be the event that contacted peer \(j\) is adversarial or otherwise exposes the declared feature.

\begin{lemma}[Independence equality and sharp marginal range]\label{lem:contacts}
If the \(A_j\) are independent and \(\Prb(A_j)=\rho\), then
\[
\Prb\!\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{m} A_j\right)=1-(1-\rho)^m.
\]
If only the equal marginals \(\Prb(A_j)=\rho\) are known, then the sharp general range is
\[
\rho\le \Prb\!\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{m} A_j\right)\le\min(1,m\rho).
\]
With only upper bounds \(\Prb(A_j)\le\rho\), even the lower endpoint may be zero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The equality follows by multiplying the independent complement probabilities. The lower bound follows because the union contains each \(A_j\); equality is attained when all \(A_j\) coincide. The upper bound is the union bound and is attained by disjoint events when \(m\rho\le1\), or by equal-measure events whose union covers the probability space when \(m\rho\ge1\).
\end{proof}

For \(\rho=0.1\) and \(m=10\), independence gives \(0.6513215599\), while perfectly correlated contacts give \(0.1\). Therefore \((\rho,m)\) neither determines an observation probability nor supplies a channel. Target-dependent routing, poisoning, retries, and churn may additionally invalidate the marginals themselves.

A uniformly sampled committee of size \(s\) from a fixed population of \(N\) nodes with exactly \(\rho N\) corrupt nodes has the narrow control-model probability
\[
p_{\mathsf{hit}}
=1-\frac{\binom{(1-\rho)N}{s}}{\binom{N}{s}}.
\]
That expression describes the sampling model only. It does not prove that a corrupt member sees the complete declared transcript, that the hit event is independent of \((C,V)\), or that repeated committee samples are independent.

\section{Exact dummy-width identity inside the proved control model}\label{sec:control}
Let \(|\C|=K\). Given target \(C=c\), sample \(w\) decoys uniformly without replacement from \(\C\setminus\{c\}\), producing a candidate set \(S\) of size \(w+1\). Suppose the complete declared observation is wrapped by Definition~\ref{def:erasure}: with probability \(p\) it reveals \(S\), and otherwise it outputs \(\bot\).

\begin{theorem}[Forced destination plus dummies under independent erasure]\label{thm:forced}
For the control model above,
\[
\MaxL(C\to Y)
=\logtwo\!\left((1-p)+p\frac{K}{w+1}\right).
\]
Consequently, a per-lookup cap \(b\) requires
\[
w+1\ge \frac{pK}{2^b-(1-p)},
\qquad 2^b>1-p.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The erasure column contributes \(1-p\). Every candidate set has conditional probability \(p/\binom{K-1}{w}\) for each secret it contains, and there are \(\binom{K}{w+1}\) such columns. Hence
\[
2^{\MaxL}
=(1-p)+\binom{K}{w+1}\frac{p}{\binom{K-1}{w}}
=(1-p)+p\frac{K}{w+1}.
\]
The width inequality is the exact inversion.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:cyclic} proves that the same \((K,w,p)\) values do not imply Theorem~\ref{thm:forced} when the reveal rule is secret- or transcript-dependent.

\section{Repeated use: allocation is not a measurement}\label{sec:repetition}
Suppose a uniform-prior exact-guess target for a window of \(Q\) lookups is \(\delta\). A sufficient total MaxL cap is
\[
B\le\logtwo(\delta K).
\]
Writing \(b=B/Q\) is an \emph{equal-share design allocation}; it is not evidence that realized per-round leakage averages to \(b\). A valid adaptive composition argument needs a conditional bound \(b_t(h)\) for every reachable pre-round history and must establish
\[
\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_t(H_{<t})\le B
\]
for every allowed run, including the information carried by stopping, aborting, and retrying.

A sum of pointwise leakage values computed after outputs are visible is a retrospective odometer. It cannot prevent overshoot because the triggering output has already been released. A preventive filter needs, before release, an outcome-uniform envelope
\[
e_t(h)\ge \sup_y \PML(C\to y\mid H_{<t}=h)
\]
and may release round \(t\) only if certified spend plus \(e_t(h)\) remains within the cap. The release/abort decision and observable sequence length are part of the channel.

\paragraph{Worked control-model sensitivity.}
For \(K=2^{20}\), \(Q=1000\), and \(\delta=10^{-3}\),
\[
B=\logtwo(\delta K)=10.0342157153\ \bits,
\qquad b=B/Q=0.0100342157\ \bits.
\]
Under the explicit independent-erasure control model with \(p=0.5\), Theorem~\ref{thm:forced} gives the minimum integer width
\[
w=1{,}034{,}140,
\qquad w/K\approx0.986232.
\]
This number is a useful regression target for the control model. It is not a deployed anonymity claim.

\section{What an authorizing knob card must bind}\label{sec:card}
A channel-bound card must include at least:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item the secret, public side information, complete observer projection, unit, horizon, and reset rule;
\item a source digest for the implementation and configuration that induce the channel;
\item either a complete conditional channel or a theorem/witness whose assumptions are machine-checkably matched to that implementation;
\item the handling of contact correlation, adaptive history, retries, stopping, and abort visibility;
\item a channel-level leakage bound and its composition rule;
\item the knob vector and a fail-closed rule for source, model, or parameter drift.
\end{enumerate}

Changing a monitored reveal frequency from \(0.50\) to \(0.60\) is not a same-card arithmetic refresh unless the source-bound witness proves that both deployments remain in the same channel family. Likewise, changing retry or stop behavior changes the channel even if \(K\), \(Q\), and the dummy count are unchanged.

\section{Kademlia/IPFS parameters are not observer parameters}\label{sec:ipfs}
A Kademlia specification can define routing-table, concurrency, termination, and provider-routing behavior. Those parameters help enumerate possible contacts, but do not by themselves define who observes which transcript or how observations depend on the target. An implementation study can estimate contact-count tails without proving independent compromise, an erasure wrapper, or a channel-level leakage bound. Thus any IPFS-style substitution of \(m\), \(\rho\), or \(p_{\mathsf{obs}}\) into Theorem~\ref{thm:forced} is a non-certifying sensitivity scenario unless a separate observer-model extraction supplies the missing joint law.\cite{ipfs_kad_spec,cortes2024kadlimits}

Cryptographic reader-private routing or private retrieval may reduce the actual channel, but that claim requires its own end-to-end transcript definition and evidence; it cannot be inferred from the name of the primitive alone.\cite{mazmudar2025peer2pir}

\section{Takeaways}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item A reveal rate is not a channel. Equal per-secret observation probabilities can coexist with perfect or near-perfect identification.
\item \(1-(1-\rho)^m\) is an independence equality, not a generally conservative contact bound.
\item Dummy-width arithmetic is exact only after the independent-erasure wrapper or another valid channel-level bound is proved.
\item \(B/Q\) is a design allocation, not an empirical average certificate; a retrospective leakage sum is not a preventive filter.
\item Until the deployment channel is source-bound, the retained tables and card are regression evidence only and must fail closed for publication.
\end{itemize}
