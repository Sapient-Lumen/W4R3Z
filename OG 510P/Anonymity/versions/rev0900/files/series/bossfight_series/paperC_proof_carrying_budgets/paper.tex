\title{Proof-Carrying Arithmetic under Source-Bound Observation Models\\
\large Why a valid budget witness is not yet an anonymity certificate}
\author{}
\date{June 18, 2026 (archive rev0900)}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A compact verifier can replay feasibility, primal and dual objectives, interval arithmetic, and a published budget inequality without trusting the mechanism compiler. That is useful, but the old proof-carrying-budget story omitted its most important precondition: the verifier must be bound to the actual observation model.
A perfectly correct checker can otherwise certify the arithmetic of a false channel.
Using the cyclic-reveal construction from Boss Fight~B, we exhibit a bundle whose independent-erasure calculation passes exactly at \(0.321928\) bits while the implemented channel leaks one full bit.
No arithmetic checker that receives only the old scalar inputs can distinguish those worlds.
The repaired interface therefore places a model-binding gate before primal/dual verification and returns the narrower verdict \emph{arithmetic valid under model digest \(d\)}. It requires source and configuration digests, a complete observer projection, a channel witness type, assumption evidence, adaptive-history and stopping semantics, and deterministic arithmetic. A missing or drifting binding is a hard failure, not a warning.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Scope.}
Boss Fight~A owns the leakage accountant; Boss Fight~B owns the repaired observation-channel boundary and dummy-width control model. This note owns only the replayable checker contract once those inputs are source-bound. It does not infer a channel from telemetry, validate a deployment by itself, notarize evaluation attempts, or authorize publication.\cite{series:bossfighta,series:bossfightb,series:evalnotary,series:abomoinl,series:releaseproperty}

The semantic counterexample and checker boundary are contained in the source. No unavailable solver log, trace, or auxiliary packet is treated as evidence.

\section{Two kinds of soundness}\label{sec:soundness}
Let \(M\) denote a declared model, \(\pi\) a concrete plan, \(\lambda\) a dual witness, \(B\) a claimed budget, and \(V\) a deterministic checker.

\begin{definition}[Arithmetic soundness]
A checker is arithmetically sound for a declared model \(M\) when acceptance proves the encoded feasibility and objective inequalities, for example
\[
\mathrm{Primal}_M(\pi)\le \mathrm{Dual}_M(\lambda)\le B,
\]
under the pinned arithmetic and serialization rules.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Model binding]
A bundle is model-bound when the object checked as \(M\) is cryptographically and semantically tied to the implementation and configuration that generate the complete declared adversary observation. The binding includes the secret and public side information, observer, extraction map, unit, horizon, reset rule, adaptive history, stopping/abort behavior, channel-witness type, and the source/evidence digests that discharge its assumptions.
\end{definition}

Arithmetic soundness is conditional. It establishes a theorem about \(M\), not the proposition that a deployment actually implements \(M\).

\begin{theorem}[Unbound arithmetic cannot certify deployment privacy]\label{thm:impossibility}
Suppose a checker receives only a declared model bundle \(x\) and not a binding between \(x\) and the deployed observation channel. If two deployments present the same \(x\) but induce channels with different leakage, then the checker produces the same verdict for both and therefore cannot establish the deployment-level leakage claim.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The checker is a function of its input bytes. Equal inputs produce equal outputs. If the deployment-level property differs while the input is identical, acceptance cannot imply that property.
\end{proof}

This elementary limitation is the central trust boundary. More schemas or signatures around the same unbound scalar do not repair it.

\section{A fully passing wrong-world certificate}\label{sec:ghost}
Consider the old forced-destination control model with \(K=3\), one dummy \(w=1\), and a declared reveal probability \(p=1/2\). The arithmetic checker evaluates
\[
B_{\mathsf{declared}}
=\log_2\!\left((1-p)+p\frac{K}{w+1}\right)
=\log_2(1.25)
=0.321928094887\ \text{bits}.
\]
It can exactly verify the candidate-set combinatorics, the independent-erasure identity, the budget comparison, and every digest in the declared bundle.

Now implement the cyclic-reveal channel of Boss Fight~B: for each of three secrets, choose one of two candidate pairs uniformly, but reveal the pair only when the secret wins a fixed pair orientation. Every secret is revealed with probability \(1/2\), so all scalar telemetry in the old bundle still matches. Yet the actual column-max sum is \(2\), hence
\[
\MaxL(C\to Y)=1\ \text{bit}.
\]
Under a uniform prior, actual exact guessing is \(2/3\), while the declared independent-erasure model predicts \(5/12\).

The checker did not malfunction. It proved the wrong conditional statement because the gate-independence assumption was neither encoded nor bound to the implementation.

\section{The repaired bundle}\label{sec:bundle}
A proof-carrying object has three layers, in this order.

\subsection{Layer 1: model-binding record}
The mandatory record contains:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{semantic identity:} protected secret, prior/support convention, public side information, observer, complete output projection, and extraction version;
\item \textbf{operational scope:} unit, horizon, reset rule, adaptive-history semantics, retry policy, and visible stop/abort/length behavior;
\item \textbf{implementation binding:} source, build, configuration, dependency, and deployment-manifest digests;
\item \textbf{channel witness type:} enumerated, independent-erasure, dominating, or theorem-backed;
\item \textbf{assumption evidence:} the object and checker establishing every family precondition, including independence and support conditions;
\item \textbf{drift policy:} which byte, configuration, observer, or evidence changes invalidate the record.
\end{enumerate}

For the independent-erasure witness, the record must establish a Bernoulli gate \(G\) independent of the pair \((C,V)\), a secret-independent erasure output, and coverage of the complete declared transcript. A monitored reveal frequency is not assumption evidence.

\subsection{Layer 2: plan and mathematical witness}
The plan \(\pi\) is the concrete mechanism object being evaluated. A dual or other proof witness \(\lambda\) may upper-bound its declared objective. The schemas must identify all dimensions, index sets, rational constants, feasibility constraints, and canonical ordering. Search traces and solver logs may help debugging, but do not replace a replayable witness.

\subsection{Layer 3: deterministic evaluator}
The evaluator binds its source digest and arithmetic contract. Exact integer/rational arithmetic is preferred. When transcendental endpoints are unavoidable, the bundle uses outward-rounded intervals with pinned precision, algorithms, and serialization. Acceptance of
\[
[p^-,p^+]\le[d^-,d^+]\le[B^-,B^+]
\]
requires \(p^+\le d^-\) and \(d^+\le B^-\). Host floating point plus an informal tolerance is a smoke test, not a proof witness.

\section{Fail-closed verifier interface}\label{sec:verifier}
\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Model-bound budget verification}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Procedure{Verify}{$R_M,\mathcal P,\mathcal D,\pi,\lambda,B$}
  \State \textbf{check} canonical serialization and all pinned digests
  \State \textbf{check} source/build/configuration match $R_M$
  \State \textbf{check} observer projection, horizon, retries, stopping, and reset match $R_M$
  \State \textbf{check} channel-witness type and every assumption-evidence result
  \State \textbf{check} $\pi\in\mathcal P$ and $\lambda\in\mathcal D$
  \State $p\gets\mathrm{PrimalValue}_{R_M}(\pi)$
  \State $d\gets\mathrm{DualValue}_{R_M}(\lambda)$
  \State \textbf{check} $p\le d\le B$ under pinned exact/interval arithmetic
  \State $d_M\gets\mathrm{digest}(R_M)$
  \State \Return \textsf{VALID-UNDER-MODEL}$(d_M)$
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The checker must not return a bare \textsf{private}, \textsf{anonymous}, or \textsf{certified} verdict. Its output includes the model digest and a nonclaim stating that deployment validity additionally depends on the correctness and completeness of the model-binding evidence.

\paragraph{Missing evidence.}
An absent channel witness, unrecognized witness type, stale source hash, unmatched observer projection, or unproved independence condition is a hard failure. ``Arithmetic passed with model warning'' is not an acceptable publication state because downstream systems routinely drop warnings.

\paragraph{Changed inputs.}
A change to source, configuration, public side information, output projection, retry schedule, stop rule, or evidence dataset invalidates the bundle unless the drift policy explicitly proves semantic equivalence. Recomputing only the final number does not refresh the certificate.

\section{Adaptive and runtime boundaries}\label{sec:adaptive}
For an adaptive sequence, a valid per-round bound is conditional on every reachable pre-round history and uses the remaining support \(\supp(C\mid H=h)\). A fixed unconditional row cannot be silently reused after the transcript has ruled out secrets.

A retrospective pointwise-leakage sum is an odometer: it reports spend after an output was released. It cannot prevent a triggering output from exceeding the cap. A prospective filter needs an outcome-uniform pre-release envelope and must include the release/abort decision and observable length in the channel. The verifier therefore distinguishes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \texttt{retrospective-odometer}: non-preventive accounting of released outputs;
\item \texttt{prospective-filter}: a source-bound pre-release envelope valid for every possible next output;
\item \texttt{fixed-horizon-allocation}: a design allocation such as \(B/Q\), not a measured average guarantee.
\end{itemize}

\section{Minimal machine-facing record}\label{sec:schema}
A compact record may be represented as follows:
\begin{verbatim}
model_binding:
  model_id
  model_sha256
  source_build_config_sha256
  secret_and_public_side_information
  observer_projection_and_extractor_sha256
  unit_horizon_reset_retry_stop_semantics
  channel_witness_type
  channel_witness_sha256
  assumption_evidence[]
plan:
  schema_id
  plan_sha256
witness:
  schema_id
  witness_sha256
evaluator:
  source_sha256
  arithmetic_mode
  canonical_input_order
claim:
  budget_value_or_interval
  verdict = arithmetic_valid_under_model
  publication_authorized = false
\end{verbatim}

The record is intentionally smaller than the former prose-heavy packet. Its purpose is to make the actual trust boundary executable, not to manufacture a new registry family.

\section{Audit checklist}\label{sec:audit}
A hostile reviewer should attempt at least the following:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item substitute the cyclic-reveal implementation while keeping the same scalar \(p\), and confirm rejection at the model-binding gate;
\item alter retry, abort, or stop behavior without changing the plan ID, and confirm digest or semantic mismatch;
\item delete one independence-evidence object, and confirm hard failure;
\item change public side information so that the conditional support shrinks, and confirm the conditional evaluator is rerun;
\item replay the arithmetic on a different platform and confirm byte-identical canonical inputs and enclosing intervals;
\item verify that the final verdict names the model digest and never collapses to an unqualified privacy claim.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and correct reuse}\label{sec:limits}
A model-bound verifier still cannot prove that the declared observer is complete, that source review found every side channel, or that evidence collected under audit matches live deployment. Those are separate empirical and adversarial-review obligations. The improvement here is narrower but real: the checker no longer disguises those obligations as a solved arithmetic problem.

A later paper may reuse a passing bundle only when the model-binding digest, implementation binding, channel witness, plan, evaluator, and budget claim are all unchanged or covered by a proved equivalence. A refreshed outer package signature does not repair an invalid inner model; conversely, a valid inner arithmetic object does not authorize its outer publication.

\section{Takeaways}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Proof-carrying arithmetic is valuable, but only conditionally on a source-bound observation model.
\item A perfect checker can certify a false scalar attenuation model; the cyclic-reveal example makes the gap executable.
\item Model binding precedes primal/dual checking and must fail closed on missing assumptions or drift.
\item The honest verdict is ``arithmetic valid under model digest,'' not ``deployment is anonymous.''
\item The repaired interface is smaller than the former packet and places effort on the substantive trust boundary rather than additional release bureaucracy.
\end{itemize}
