\begin{abstract}
Marginally uniform committee contact (MUCC) equalizes every one-committee contact bit across destination keys and committee labels.  It is therefore a useful first-moment load/equalization condition, but it is \emph{not} contact-set privacy.  We prove both separations explicitly: a contact-set law can be destination-independent without being label-uniform, and exact MUCC can coexist with perfect destination recovery from higher-order correlations.  In the public $(M,d,t,\alpha,\beta)=(256,8,4,0.8,0.05)$ example, an orbit-coded fixed-size mechanism contacts exactly $250$ committees, has exact MUCC marginals $250/256$, exceeds $0.9628$ success under exact independent liveness, yet has disjoint conditional supports and total-variation distance one between two destination laws.

What survives is a conditional cost theorem.  Under a declared MUCC marginal row and an exact/upper live-yield envelope, successful lookup imposes a universal expected-contact floor; stronger success guarantees require separately named joint-contact and liveness assumptions.  We retain the necessity/sufficiency ladder, the global-marginal-diameter repair for approximate rows, the separation of outer destination parameters $(d,t)$ from inner quorum parameters $(n,h,q,\rho)$, and the abort-safe total-contact floor.  None of these rows is anonymity evidence by itself.  A destination-privacy claim must additionally bind the joint law of the declared witness---for example by total variation, differential privacy, or another explicit transcript-level criterion---and must be re-established for each concrete protocol rather than inferred from a generic DHT analogy.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Necessity, not success certification.}
The $152$ row above is a necessary universal Markov floor in expectation, not a success certificate for the $0.95$ target.  Under the optimistic i.i.d. binomial model with $(M,d,t,\alpha,\beta)=(256,8,4,0.8,0.05)$, spending exactly $k_0=152$ gives $p=152/256$ and $p\alpha=0.475$, so $\Pr[\mathrm{Binomial}(8,0.475)\ge4]\approx0.581$, not $0.95$.  The finite-population thinned-hypergeometric calculation at $k_0=152$ is essentially the same ($\approx0.582$).  Both named optimistic joint-contact models first cross $0.95$ at $k_0=228$, with predecessor $k_0=227$ still below target.  That $228$ row is not a theorem under MUCC marginals alone: the sharp worst-case threshold under MUCC marginals plus exact independent liveness is $k_0=250$, while $k_0=249$ remains below target.  If liveness may be arbitrarily correlated and $\alpha=0.8$ is only a lower mean-yield row, even contacting all $256$ committees certifies only $0.68$; the corresponding unclipped $0.95$ mean-only requirement is $310>M$.  Thus every sufficiency sentence must name its joint-contact and liveness assumptions rather than promote a planning-tail number into a MUCC-wide certificate.

\begin{proposition}[MUCC alpha-direction non-substitution guard]
\label{prop:mucc-alpha-direction-guard}
The live-yield row has opposite evidentiary direction in necessity and sufficiency arguments.  The universal impossibility floor uses an \emph{exact yield or upper-yield envelope} to upper-bound $\E[Z]$; a success guarantee uses an \emph{exact yield or lower-yield bound} to lower-bound $\E[Z]$ or a tail probability.  An upper envelope therefore cannot be spent as a sufficiency input, and a lower bound cannot be spent as an impossibility input.  A single numerical $\alpha$ may support both directions only when the card justifies it as exact.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Nested-quorum parameter non-substitution guard]
\label{prop:mucc-nested-quorum-guard}
The outer parameters $d$ and $t$ count destination committees and required live \emph{committee responses}.  If one committee contains $n$ members and emits a response after an internal quorum of $h$ reachable members, then an $\alpha$ calibration must use the separate inner tuple $(n,h)$, not $(d,t)$.  A release card may identify the tuples only when it explicitly proves the model identities $n=d$ and $h=t$; matching numerals or reused symbols are not evidence.  Any derived $\alpha$ must also carry the member-reachability row $q$, any common-mode outage row $\rho$, and the exact/upper/lower evidentiary direction in which the result is used.
\end{proposition}

\section{Introduction}
A committee-contact trace can reveal a lookup destination even when payloads and routes are otherwise protected.  It is therefore tempting to call uniform-looking contact frequencies ``contact-set privacy.''  That temptation is wrong.  This paper isolates \emph{marginally-uniform committee contact (MUCC) marginals}, proves that the condition can leak the destination perfectly through correlations, and retains MUCC only as the premise of a conditional availability/contact-cost theorem.

\paragraph{Contact sets are witnesses.}
Even if payloads are protected and routes are anonymous, a partially observing adversary may learn which committees were contacted.
This ``who got touched'' footprint is a witness stream.
AnonDHT~State~1 formalizes the state-dependent amplification story~\cite{series:state1}; here we focus on the DHT-specific floor created by availability requirements.


\paragraph{Non-redundancy.}
We treat stop-time padding and schedule equalization mechanisms as imported from the series~\cite{series:stoptimeaddendum,series:schedcompiler}.
Our contribution is the \emph{availability floor} that remains even if those mechanisms are perfect.

\paragraph{Stop point.}
This note stops once a deployment has a declared committee-contact witness, the corresponding MUCC contact-floor inequalities, and the explicit joint-privacy nonclaim. At that point, only the conditional cost claim is source-complete. Exact packet wiring and theorem-root-to-review routing are intentionally delegated to the maintained worked example and series-spine bridge rather than reproduced here. Any richer endpoint semantics, evaluator notarization, runtime monitoring, or release packaging belong to the companion anonymity/state/evaluation/release notes already cited above.\cite{series:workedexample,series:seriesspines,series:oddsinflation,series:profiling,series:state3,series:evalnotary,series:release}


\section{Model and main results}
\label{sec:model}

\paragraph{Committees and destinations.}

\paragraph{Witness projection (CCT $\rightarrow$ committee contact).}
We treat a lookup as producing a committee-contact trace (CCT) as in Synthesis~3.
MUCC concerns a specific projection of that trace: the indicator (or set) of committees contacted during the lookup.
Formally, fix an exposure normal form \(\mathsf{ENF}\) whose \texttt{exposure\_nf\_id} names the projection \(g(\mathrm{CCT})\); all probabilities below are with respect to that declared \(g\).

A lookup targets a key whose responsible set is a destination committee-set $D\subseteq[M]$ of size $|D|=d$ (replication factor).
A lookup succeeds if it obtains at least $t$ \emph{live} responses from committees in $D$.
Let $\alpha\in(0,1]$ denote the live-response yield used by the contact-floor statement: in theorem-level impossibility rows it is either an exact per-contact yield or a public upper envelope on the conditional expected live yield of a contacted destination committee.
This is an \emph{outer} committee-response parameter. If a committee response itself requires a quorum of reachable members, its calibration uses a separate inner tuple $(n,h,q,\rho)$: committee size $n$, internal response quorum $h$, per-member reachability $q$, and any common-mode outage probability $\rho$.
A deployment may also publish a lower-bound liveness estimate for engineering success calculations, but then the formula below is a conservative planning row rather than a necessary impossibility floor unless the card separately justifies the upper/exact yield interpretation.
Section~\ref{sec:churn_caching} explains this split for churn/caching models.

\paragraph{MUCC marginals.}
Let $C$ be the (random) set of committees contacted over the \emph{entire} lookup (possibly across multiple rounds/subqueries).
For multi-round protocols we write $C^{(\ell)}$ for the round-$\ell$ contact set, so that $C=\bigcup_{\ell=1}^L C^{(\ell)}$.

\begin{definition}[MUCC (marginally-uniform committee contact) marginals]
\label{def:mucc}
A lookup mechanism has \emph{MUCC marginals} if for every key (hence every destination set $D$) and every committee $j\in[M]$,
\[\Pr[j\in C\mid D] = p\quad\text{(a constant independent of $D$ and $j$)}.\]
Write $k_0 := \E[|C|] = Mp$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Joint contact-set destination leakage]
\label{def:joint-contact-leakage}
For a declared destination family $\mathcal D$ and contact-set witness $C$, define
\[
\Delta_C := \sup_{D,D'\in\mathcal D}
  \operatorname{TV}\!\left(\mathcal L(C\mid D),\mathcal L(C\mid D')\right).
\]
Exact joint contact-set privacy for this witness requires $\Delta_C=0$.  A release may instead publish another explicit joint-law criterion, but a MUCC marginal row is not such a criterion.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[MUCC is marginal-only, not privacy]
MUCC constrains only the \emph{single-committee} marginals $\Pr[j\in C\mid D]$.
Equivalently, for each committee $j$, the one-bit projection $X_j:=\one\{j\in C\}$ is marginally equalized across destinations.
The \emph{joint} distribution of $C$ may remain destination-dependent, including maximally so.  This is the same local-versus-global hazard familiar from $k$-wise-independence testing: low-order projections can look random while the full distribution is globally structured.\cite{alon2007kwise}

Accordingly, MUCC is a \emph{first-moment label-equalization condition}.  It is weaker than the full equalization contracts used for profiling budgets, which bind the distribution of a declared transcript projection $X=f(T)$ (Anonymity~B).\cite{series:profiling}  The ceilings/floors below use MUCC only to control $\E[|C\cap D|]$ and $\E[|C|]$.  They are cost/availability statements, not destination-privacy statements.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Joint key-independence is not committee-label uniformity]
\label{rem:key-independent-not-mucc}
The deterministic mechanism $C=\{1\}$ has $\Delta_C=0$: its contact-set law is exactly independent of $D$.  Yet $q_1(D)=1$ and $q_j(D)=0$ for $j\ne1$, so it is not MUCC.  On a restricted destination family in which every valid $D$ contains committee $1$ and $t=1$, it can even succeed with one contact.  Thus joint destination privacy does not imply label-uniform MUCC without a symmetry, global marginal-diameter, or equivalent bridge.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Exact MUCC can leak the destination perfectly]
\label{prop:mucc-perfect-leakage}
There is a fixed-size mechanism at the public parameters $M=256$, $d=8$, $t=4$, and $\alpha=0.8$ such that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item $|C|=250$ always and $\Pr[j\in C\mid D]=250/256$ for every committee $j$ and each valid destination $D$ (exact MUCC);
\item $\Delta_C=1$, the optimal destination-recovery probability is one, and for a uniform binary destination $I(D;C)=1$ bit; and
\item under exact independent liveness, the success probabilities for the two destinations are $0.9628928$ and $0.9628032$, both above $0.95$.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the sharp $k_0=250$ MUCC-plus-independent-liveness sufficiency row is compatible with perfect destination leakage.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Identify committee labels with $\mathbb Z_{256}$.  Let
\[
D_0=\{0,1,\ldots,7\},\qquad D_1=\{8,9,\ldots,15\},
\]
and define omission templates
\[
A=\{0,1,2,3,4,5\},\qquad B=\{0,1,2,3,4,6\}.
\]
Draw $U$ uniformly from $\mathbb Z_{256}$.  For destination $D_0$, omit $U+A$; for $D_1$, omit $U+B$; in either case contact the complement.  Every label belongs to exactly six translates of either six-element template, so every committee is contacted with probability $250/256$ and every contact set has size $250$.

The two conditional supports are disjoint.  If $U+A=V+B$, then $B$ would be a cyclic translate of $A$, but every translate of $A$ is six consecutive residues whereas $B$ is not.  Observing $C$ therefore identifies the template orbit and hence the destination with certainty, giving $\Delta_C=1$ and the stated Bayes and mutual-information values.

For service, enumerate the $256$ shifts.  Under $D_0$, the destination-contact count $Y=|C\cap D_0|$ has multiplicities
\[
\#\{Y=2,3,4,5,6,7,8\}=\{3,2,2,2,2,2,243\};
\]
under $D_1$ the multiplicities are
\[
\#\{Y=2,3,4,5,6,7,8\}=\{2,3,2,2,2,3,242\}.
\]
A contacted destination committee responds independently with probability $0.8$, so summing $\Pr[\mathrm{Binomial}(Y,0.8)\ge4]$ over these exact counts gives $0.9628928$ and $0.9628032$.
\end{proof}

The two counterexamples establish logical independence: joint contact-set privacy does not imply label-uniform MUCC, and exact MUCC does not imply any nontrivial joint contact-set privacy.  Any system claiming both must prove both axes.

\begin{lemma}[Destination contact expectation under MUCC]
\label{lem:mucc-dest-exp}
Let $Y:=|C\cap D|$ be the number of destination committees contacted over the whole lookup.
Under MUCC, $\E[Y]=dp$.
If each contacted destination committee yields at most one live response and has conditional expected live yield at most $\alpha$, then the number of live destination responses $Z$ satisfies $\E[Z]\le\alpha dp$; under exact independent thinning with rate $\alpha$, equality holds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By linearity of expectation, $\E[Y]=\sum_{i\in D}\Pr[i\in C\mid D]=dp$.
Writing $L_i$ for the live-response indicator after destination committee $i$ is contacted, the yield-envelope assumption gives $\E[L_i\mid i\in C,D]\le\alpha$ and hence
\[
\E[Z]=\sum_{i\in D}\E[\one\{i\in C\}L_i\mid D]\le \sum_{i\in D}\alpha\Pr[i\in C\mid D]=\alpha dp.
\]
Under exact independent thinning this inequality is an equality.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Conditional availability/contact-cost bounds under MUCC}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:mucc-dest-exp}, MUCC fixes the expected number of destination contacts and, under an exact/upper live-yield row, upper-bounds the expected number of live destination responses by $\alpha dp$.
This is the only property we need for the universal ceiling below.
If $\alpha$ is merely a lower-bound liveness estimate, the same arithmetic is still a useful conservative design drill, but it must not be advertised as a theorem-level impossibility statement.

\begin{theorem}[MUCC contact-cost floor (universal, expectation form)]
\label{thm:mucc-ceiling}
Consider any lookup mechanism with MUCC marginals and an exact/upper live-yield row $\alpha$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:mucc-dest-exp}.
If the lookup succeeds with probability at least $1-\beta$ (i.e., $\Pr[Z\ge t]\ge 1-\beta$), then necessarily
\[
 d\,p\,\alpha \;\ge\; (1-\beta)\,t.
\]
Equivalently, the expected total contact satisfies
\[
 k_0 \;\ge\; \frac{M\,(1-\beta)\,t}{\alpha\,d}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
For nonnegative $Z$, Markov gives $\Pr[Z\ge t]\le \E[Z]/t$.
Rearrange.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Union-only observers]
If the observer sees only the union footprint $C$ (not per-round contacts), then Theorem~\ref{thm:mucc-ceiling} already lower bounds $\E[|C|]=Mp$.
Since total contact events satisfy $K\ge |C|$ always, the same floor applies to $\E[K]$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:mucc-stop-time-floor} isolates the common (and stricter) regime where per-round footprints are observable.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Tightness of the expectation ceiling]
Theorem~\ref{thm:mucc-ceiling} is proved via Markov's inequality and is essentially tight without additional assumptions.
Indeed, for any $\mu\in[0,t]$ there exists a nonnegative random variable $Z$ with $\E[Z]=\mu$ and $\Pr[Z\ge t]=\mu/t$ (take $Z=t$ with probability $\mu/t$ and $Z=0$ otherwise).
Thus, improving Theorem~\ref{thm:mucc-ceiling} requires adding structure (e.g., independence, sampling without replacement, or moment bounds).
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Diameter-approximate MUCC]
\label{def:diameter-approx-mucc}
Write $q_j(D):=\Pr[j\in C\mid D]$.  A lookup mechanism has \emph{$\delta$-diameter MUCC marginals} if
\[
\sup_{D,D',\,j,j'}\bigl|q_j(D)-q_{j'}(D')\bigr|\le\delta.
\]
Exact MUCC is the case $\delta=0$.  A two-sided center-radius card
$|q_j(D)-p|\le\eta$ implies $\delta\le2\eta$.  By contrast, a one-sided ceiling
$q_j(D)\le p+\eta$ alone is not an equalization contract.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[One-sided marginal ceilings do not imply cover contact]
\label{prop:one-sided-mucc-counterexample}
For the public parameters $(M,d,t,\alpha,\beta)=(256,8,4,0.8,0.05)$, there is a lookup with success probability $0.95$, exact conditional live yield $0.8$ on every contacted destination committee, and a one-sided row
$q_j(D)\le p$ for the common value $p=0.59375$, yet
\[
\E[|C|\mid D]=4.75
\]
and no nondestination committee is ever contacted.  Thus the number $Mp=152$ is not a contact floor under the one-sided row, even when its slack is called zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With probability $0.95$, choose four destination committees uniformly as the live responders, contact those four, and independently contact each of the other four destination committees with probability $1/4$; with probability $0.05$, contact nobody.  Never contact a nondestination committee.  Each destination committee is contacted with probability
$0.95(1/2+(1/2)(1/4))=0.59375$, each nondestination committee has contact probability zero, and each destination committee is live with probability $0.95/2=0.475$.  Hence its conditional live yield is $0.475/0.59375=0.8$, the lookup has exactly four live responses on the success branch, and the expected total contact is $8(0.59375)=4.75$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Marginally sharp cover floor for diameter-approximate MUCC]
\label{cor:approx-mucc}
Assume $\delta$-diameter MUCC marginals and an exact/upper live-yield row $\alpha$.
Put
\[
A:=\frac{(1-\beta)t}{\alpha d}.
\]
If $A>1$, the service target is infeasible under the yield row.  Otherwise, every destination set $D$ satisfies
\[
\E[|C|\mid D]
\;\ge\;
 dA+(M-d)\,(A-\delta)_+,
\qquad (x)_+:=\max\{0,x\}.
\]
The bound is sharp at the level of the declared marginal and mean constraints.  If a card instead publishes a two-sided center radius $|q_j(D)-p|\le\eta$, then it may substitute only $\delta\le2\eta$, giving
\[
\E[|C|\mid D]\ge dA+(M-d)(A-2\eta)_+.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Success implies $\E[Z]\ge(1-\beta)t$, while the exact/upper yield row gives
$\E[Z]\le\alpha\sum_{i\in D}q_i(D)$.  Hence
$\sum_{i\in D}q_i(D)\ge dA$, so some destination marginal is at least $A$.  The diameter contract forces every nondestination marginal to be at least $(A-\delta)_+$.  Summing the destination lower bound and the nondestination marginals proves the result.  Marginals $A$ on destinations and $(A-\delta)_+$ elsewhere attain the displayed expression and are realizable, for example, by independent Bernoulli contacts.  Thus no stronger contact lower bound follows from the declared marginal-diameter and mean constraints alone.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Public-card sensitivity.}
Here $A=0.59375$.  Exact MUCC ($\delta=0$) recovers $152$.  A two-sided radius $\eta=0.05$ permits diameter $0.10$ and lowers the marginally sharp contact floor to
$4.75+248(0.49375)=127.2$, not a one-factor ``$M\eta$'' adjustment.  Once the diameter reaches $A$, the theorem can force only the $4.75$ destination-contact expectation.  A deployment that wants an approximate claim should therefore publish a simultaneous upper bound on the declared marginal diameter, not merely a fitted center or a one-sided maximum.


The bound is intentionally assumption-light (no independence required): it uses only MUCC marginals and an exact/upper live-yield row $\alpha$.
Sharper ceilings are possible under additional structure (e.g., uniform sampling without replacement) using hypergeometric concentration inequalities~\cite{serfling1974sampling,bardenet2015withoutreplacement}, but Theorem~\ref{thm:mucc-ceiling} is already strong in large-$M$ regimes.


\subsection{A best-case tail ceiling under i.i.d.\ destination success}
The expectation ceiling above is universal and assumption-light, but it does not capture the additional slack needed when $d$ is moderate and one wants small failure probability.
To state a clean strengthening, assume an \emph{optimistic} model in which for each destination committee $i\in D$,
the event ``$i$ yields a live response'' is independent with probability $p\alpha$ (e.g., contact decisions are approximately independent across $i$ and liveness is independent thinning).

\begin{proposition}[Binomial-quantile ceiling (optimistic i.i.d.\ model)]
\label{prop:binom-quantile-ceiling}
Under the i.i.d.\ destination-success model, $Z\sim \mathrm{Binomial}(d,p\alpha)$.
Define
\[
p^\star(d,t,\beta) := \inf\{q\in[0,1] : \Pr[\mathrm{Binomial}(d,q)\ge t]\ge 1-\beta\}.
\]
If the lookup succeeds with probability at least $1-\beta$, then necessarily
\[
p\alpha \;\ge\; p^\star(d,t,\beta),
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
k_0 = Mp \;\ge\; \frac{M}{\alpha}\,p^\star(d,t,\beta).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under the i.i.d. model, $Z\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(d,p\alpha)$.
The success requirement $\Pr[Z\ge t]\ge 1-\beta$ implies that the Binomial success probability parameter $p\alpha$ must be at least the minimal value $p^\star(d,t,\beta)$ achieving that tail probability.
Rearrange to obtain the stated lower bound on $k_0=Mp$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A closed-form conservative ceiling (Hoeffding)]
\label{lem:hoeffding-ceiling}
Under the i.i.d.\ destination-success model, if
\[
p\alpha \;\ge\; \frac{t}{d} + \sqrt{\frac{\ln(1/\beta)}{2d}},
\]
then $\Pr[Z\ge t]\ge 1-\beta$.
Equivalently, a sufficient MUCC contact fraction is
\[
\frac{k_0}{M}=p \;\ge\; \frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\frac{t}{d} + \sqrt{\frac{\ln(1/\beta)}{2d}}\right).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Let $Z\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(d,p\alpha)$ and write $q=p\alpha$.
Hoeffding's inequality gives $\Pr[Z/d \le q-\epsilon]\le \exp(-2d\epsilon^2)$.
Setting $\epsilon = q - t/d$ and requiring $\exp(-2d\epsilon^2)\le\beta$ yields $\epsilon\ge\sqrt{\ln(1/\beta)/(2d)}$.
The logarithm here is natural because the tail bound is written with $\exp$; this concentration row is not a bit-budget row.
\end{proof}

Table~\ref{tab:binom-quantile} gives representative values for this named joint model.  Without a joint-contact assumption, the model-specific quantile is not a MUCC-wide guarantee; the sharp marginal-only calculation below is the correct fail-closed replacement.

\subsection{A best-case ceiling under uniform fixed-size contact (hypergeometric)}
MUCC alone does not specify how the contact set $C$ is sampled.
To sharpen ceilings under a concrete (optimistic) sampling model, assume $C$ is sampled as a \emph{uniform random subset of fixed size} $|C|=k_0$, independent of $D$.
This is a best-case MUCC instantiation: it equalizes marginals and minimizes variance among exchangeable schemes.

Under this model, $Y:=|C\cap D|$ follows a hypergeometric law $Y\sim \mathrm{Hypergeom}(M,d,k_0)$ with mean $\E[Y]=dk_0/M$.
The only assumption-light necessary condition is
\[
\Pr\!\bigl[\mathrm{Hypergeom}(M,d,k_0)\ge t\bigr]\ \ge\ 1-\beta,
\]
because $Z\le Y$ always: a lookup cannot obtain $t$ live destination responses unless it contacted at least $t$ destination committees.
This row is valid without independence, but it deliberately ignores liveness thinning and is therefore weak.

If one additionally assumes exact independent live thinning with rate $\alpha$, the correct best-case tail is the \emph{thinned hypergeometric} law
\[
Z\mid Y=y \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(y,\alpha),
\qquad
Y\sim\mathrm{Hypergeom}(M,d,k_0).
\]
A uniform fixed-size MUCC scheme meeting the success target must then satisfy
\[
\sum_{y=0}^{d} \Pr[\mathrm{Hypergeom}(M,d,k_0)=y]\,
       \Pr[\mathrm{Binomial}(y,\alpha)\ge t]
\ \ge\ 1-\beta.
\]
Equivalently, define the thinned-hypergeometric quantile
\[
k_0^{\star}(M,d,t,\alpha,\beta)
:= \inf\left\{k\in\{0,\dots,M\}: \sum_{y=0}^{d} \Pr[\mathrm{Hypergeom}(M,d,k)=y]\Pr[\mathrm{Binomial}(y,\alpha)\ge t]\ge 1-\beta\right\},
\]
and note that any uniform fixed-size MUCC scheme satisfying the exact independent-thinning target must have $k_0\ge k_0^\star$.
Closed-form conservative approximations are available via Serfling/without-replacement inequalities and binomial tail bounds.\cite{serfling1974sampling,bardenet2015withoutreplacement}

\begin{remark}[Do not certify with the $t/\alpha$ shortcut]
The tempting replacement by a rounded $t/\alpha$ destination-contact target is an expectation-planning heuristic, not a theorem-level necessary condition.
For example, when $t=1$ and $\alpha<1$, a lookup that contacts one destination committee can still succeed with positive probability, so requiring $Y\ge\lceil1/\alpha\rceil$ as an event-level precondition is false.
Release cards should either use the exact thinned-hypergeometric tail above or explicitly label any $t/\alpha$ row as a conservative planning approximation.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Sharp MUCC sufficiency ladder under weaker joint assumptions}
\label{sec:mucc-sufficiency-ladder}
The two exact tails above assume a joint law for the contact indicators.  MUCC itself fixes only the one-committee marginals and hence only the mean $\E[Y]=dp$.  The resulting robust success calculation is a small finite linear program rather than a binomial or hypergeometric tail.

\begin{proposition}[Sharp MUCC lower envelope under exact independent liveness]
\label{prop:mucc-lower-envelope}
Assume MUCC marginals and exact liveness that is independent across contacted destination committees and independent of the contact set, with live probability $\alpha$.  Put
\[
 g_h:=\Pr[\mathrm{Binomial}(h,\alpha)\ge t],\qquad h=0,\ldots,d,
\]
and, for $\mu\in[0,d]$, define the lower convex envelope
\[
 \underline g(\mu):=
 \min_{\substack{\pi_h\ge0,\;\sum_h\pi_h=1\\
                   \sum_h h\pi_h=\mu}}
       \sum_{h=0}^{d}\pi_h g_h.
\]
Then every such lookup satisfies
\[
 \Pr[Z\ge t]\ge \underline g(dp).
\]
The bound is sharp: for every feasible mean, a MUCC contact law can attain the minimizing two-point mixture for $Y$, after symmetrizing uniformly within destination and nondestination committees.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Conditional on $Y=h$, exact independent thinning gives success probability $g_h$, so $\Pr[Z\ge t]=\E[g_Y]$.  MUCC supplies the constraints $Y\in\{0,\ldots,d\}$ and $\E[Y]=dp$.  Minimizing the expectation under those constraints is a linear program with two equality constraints; an extreme optimum has support on at most two values of $h$, which is exactly the lower convex envelope.  Conversely, choose the minimizing value of $Y$ and then choose a uniformly random $Y$-subset of $D$; choose nondestination contacts with the same marginal $p$.  Every committee then has marginal $p$, so the lower envelope is attainable under MUCC.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Numerical certificate for the public card.}
For $(M,d,t,\alpha,\beta)=(256,8,4,0.8,0.05)$, the points $g_0,\ldots,g_3$ are zero and all points $g_4,\ldots,g_7$ lie above the chord from $(3,0)$ to $(8,g_8)$, where $g_8=0.9895936$.  Hence the relevant envelope is that chord.  It gives success lower bounds $0.34635776$ at $k_0=152$, $0.81641472$ at the optimistic-model threshold $k_0=228$, $0.94629888$ at $k_0=249$, and $0.95248384$ at $k_0=250$.  Therefore $250$ is the first integer expected-contact budget that guarantees the $0.95$ target under MUCC marginals plus exact independent liveness; the predecessor check at $249$ fails.

\begin{proposition}[Sharp mean-only liveness guarantee]
\label{prop:mucc-mean-only-liveness}
Suppose instead that arbitrary liveness dependence is allowed and the card justifies only the lower mean row $\E[Z]\ge\alpha dp$.  Since $Z\in\{0,\ldots,d\}$,
\[
 \Pr[Z\ge t]
 \ge
 \max\!\left\{0,\frac{\alpha dp-(t-1)}{d-t+1}\right\}.
\]
This support-and-mean bound is sharp whenever the required mean can be realized by a mixture at $Z=t-1$ and $Z=d$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Writing $s=\Pr[Z\ge t]$, the largest mean compatible with success probability $s$ is obtained by placing failure mass at $t-1$ and success mass at $d$:
$\E[Z]\le(t-1)(1-s)+ds=(t-1)+(d-t+1)s$.
Combine this upper expression with the declared lower mean and rearrange.  The two-point distribution shows sharpness.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Mean-only non-certificate.}
At full contact $p=1$ in the public card, the lower mean is $\alpha d=6.4$, so Proposition~\ref{prop:mucc-mean-only-liveness} certifies only $(6.4-3)/5=0.68$.  To force a $0.95$ guarantee from this mean-only row would require
\[
 k_0\ge \frac{M\bigl((t-1)+(d-t+1)(1-\beta)\bigr)}{\alpha d}=310,
\]
which exceeds $M=256$.  This does not say a real full-contact implementation cannot achieve $0.95$; it says the mean-only assumptions are insufficient to certify it.  A stronger dependence model or direct tail evidence is required.

\subsection{Abort-safe equalization implies a total cover-contact floor}
To hide retries/early success, anonymous lookups often fix a stop time (number of rounds/subqueries) and run dummy work when success would otherwise happen early.
Let $L$ be the fixed number of rounds, and let $C^{(\ell)}$ be the contact set in round $\ell$.

\begin{proposition}[Abort-safe cover-contact floor (splitting into rounds does not help)]
\label{prop:mucc-stop-time-floor}
Assume the observer sees per-round contacts, each round has MUCC marginals with a common per-round contact probability $p_{\mathrm{rnd}}$ (so $\E[|C^{(\ell)}|]=Mp_{\mathrm{rnd}}$), and the same exact/upper live-yield row $\alpha$ applies to each contacted destination committee.
Let $K:=\sum_{\ell=1}^L |C^{(\ell)}|$ be the total number of committee-contact events executed under abort-safe equalization (including dummies).
If the lookup succeeds with probability at least $1-\beta$, then necessarily
\[
\E[K] \;\ge\; \frac{M\,(1-\beta)\,t}{\alpha\,d}.
\]
Equivalently, more rounds can reduce per-round contact ($p_{\mathrm{rnd}}\propto 1/L$ in the best case), but cannot reduce the required \emph{total} cover-contact.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Under per-round MUCC and the exact/upper live-yield row, the expected number of live destination responses across all rounds satisfies
$\E[Z]\le\alpha\,d\,\sum_{\ell=1}^L \Pr[i\in C^{(\ell)}\mid D] = \alpha\,d\,L p_{\mathrm{rnd}}$.
Apply Markov as in Theorem~\ref{thm:mucc-ceiling}: $\Pr[Z\ge t]\le \E[Z]/t$.
Finally, $\E[K]=\sum_{\ell=1}^L \E[|C^{(\ell)}|]=L M p_{\mathrm{rnd}}$.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Publication-facing validity boundary}
A release card may reuse the MUCC floor only when the following rows are explicit and unchanged.
First, the exposure normal form must pin the raw trace projection: whether the witness is a union contact set, an ordered per-round contact sequence, or a richer contact-count transcript.
Second, the committee universe $M$, key-to-destination mapping, replication $d$, live threshold $t$, success/failure budget $\beta$, and retry/session horizon must be versioned.
Third, the $\alpha$ row must say whether it is an exact yield, an upper-yield envelope for impossibility floors, or a lower-bound engineering liveness estimate; a lower-bound-only row must not be cited as a necessary-contact theorem.  If $\alpha$ is derived from member reachability, the card must separately version the inner tuple $(n,h,q,\rho)$ and must not substitute the outer destination tuple $(d,t)$.
Fourth, any claim imported from key-independence, DP-style stability, or another richer contact-set notion must identify the label-symmetry or global marginal-relation step that actually implies the MUCC row.
Fifth, the tail row must name one rung of the assumption ladder: the universal Markov necessity floor; an exact i.i.d. or thinned-hypergeometric joint model; the sharp MUCC-marginal lower envelope under exact independent liveness; or the mean-only support bound under arbitrary dependence.  A number from one rung must not be quoted under another rung's assumptions, and any approximation must be labeled as such.
Finally, the card must list excluded channels: destination-dependent starting buckets, provider/gateway/indexer selection, lookup termination timing, padding-machine behavior, browser or application fingerprints, token issuance/redemption linkage, monitoring beacons, and any cache/republish mechanism outside the declared witness.
Changing any of these rows creates a successor MUCC card rather than a wording refresh.


\section{Implications and design notes}
\label{sec:implications}

\paragraph{What the ceiling means.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:mucc-ceiling} says that in large deployments, hiding destination from contact-set marginals can force a lookup to ``touch'' a substantial fraction of the network when availability targets are strict and the live-yield row is exact or an upper envelope.
In stress-case planning, the ceiling worsens as the declared $\alpha$ falls (churn/drops) or as $t$ rises (stronger reconstruction/availability); in release text, the card must say whether that stress-case row is theorem-bearing or only an engineering assumption.

Mainline BitTorrent's DHT is specified by BEP-5~\cite{bep5}.

\subsection{Churn and caching as \texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha}}
\label{sec:churn_caching}
Theorems~\ref{thm:mucc-ceiling} and~\ref{prop:binom-quantile-ceiling} treat $\alpha$ as an external live-response row.
For impossibility-style contact floors, that row must be exact or an upper envelope on live yield; for engineering success checks, a lower-bound liveness estimate may be used as a pessimistic design input, but it is not by itself a necessary-contact theorem.
To keep the two uses auditable, this section maps protocol assumptions (uptime, stabilization delay, cache staleness) into a conservative lower-bound liveness row for design calculations and requires the release card to say when that row is being reused as an exact/stress-case yield.

\begin{lemma}[A simple inner-quorum lower bound for $\alpha$]
\label{lem:alpha-from-churn}
Suppose each destination committee consists of $n$ members and emits one live committee response if at least $h$ of those $n$ members are reachable.
Assume (i) each member is reachable at the moment it is queried with probability at least $q$ independently across members, and (ii) reachability already includes any fixed stabilization delay $\Delta$ and any cache-staleness penalty.
Then the committee live-response probability satisfies
\[
\alpha\;\ge\;\Pr[\mathrm{Binomial}(n,q)\ge h].
\]
If member sessions have survival function $S(\Delta)=\Pr[\text{session length}\ge \Delta]$ and per-member query success probability at zero delay is at least $q_0$, then one conservative choice is $q=q_0\,S(\Delta)$.
The inner pair $(n,h)$ is distinct from the outer destination-replication/response-threshold pair $(d,t)$ used by Theorem~\ref{thm:mucc-ceiling}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Under the assumptions, the number of reachable committee members stochastically dominates a $\mathrm{Binomial}(n,q)$ random variable.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A one-line correlated-outage refinement]
If, with probability $\rho$, the destination committee experiences a common-mode outage (e.g., shared ASN/rack failure) that makes all $n$ members unreachable, and otherwise members are independently reachable with probability at least $q$, then
\[\alpha\;\ge\;(1-\rho)\,\Pr[\mathrm{Binomial}(n,q)\ge h].\]
This simple mixture model is often enough to see how quickly the MUCC planning row worsens under correlated failures.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Nested-quorum collision drill.}
Keep the public \emph{outer} tuple $(d,t)=(8,4)$, but suppose one destination committee has the \emph{inner} tuple $(n,h,q,\rho)=(16,11,0.6,0)$.  The correct independent-member lower row is
\[
\alpha_{\mathrm{inner}}\ge \Pr[\mathrm{Binomial}(16,0.6)\ge11]\approx0.3288404125.
\]
The forbidden outer-as-inner substitution instead reports
\[
\Pr[\mathrm{Binomial}(8,0.6)\ge4]=0.8263296,
\]
overstating the certified lower row by about $0.4974891875$ (a factor of about $2.51285903$).  This is not a cosmetic symbol clash: it can make an unavailable inner quorum appear healthy.  The derived row remains a lower-bound engineering input unless separate evidence justifies an exact or upper-yield interpretation.

Lemma~\ref{lem:alpha-from-churn} is intentionally coarse: correlations, adversarial suppression, and load-dependent failure can only \emph{decrease} the certified lower-bound liveness row.
Table~\ref{tab:alpha} gives a simple inline factorization example; the numerical collision drill above is the source-bound calibration check.  No separate \texttt{churn\_alpha\_table.tex} artifact is shipped or claimed.

\paragraph{Realizing MUCC marginals is not enough.}
Choosing $k_0$ committees uniformly without replacement is one way to obtain both MUCC and a destination-independent contact-set law.  Other MUCC schedulers, including Proposition~\ref{prop:mucc-perfect-leakage}, preserve exactly the same marginals while encoding the destination in correlations.  Any implementation therefore needs a joint-law argument in addition to a marginal scheduler argument.

\paragraph{Relationship to other series results.}
Schedule equalization and stop-time padding can remove timing and retry channels, but they neither create joint contact-set privacy nor evade the conditional MUCC cost floor.  Paper~1 provides the broader state-dependent lens for amplification and accounting; Anonymity~B owns distributional equalization of a declared transcript witness.



\section{Related work}
Structured overlays and DHTs include Chord, Pastry, and Kademlia~\cite{stoica2001chord,rowstron2001pastry,maymounkov2002kademlia}.
Anonymous or privacy-enhanced overlays built on structured topologies include Salsa, ShadowWalker, and AP3~\cite{nambiar2006salsa,mittal2009shadowwalker,mislove2006ap3}.
Attacks on anonymous DHT-style lookups (and the motivation for secure+anonymous lookup) were developed by Wang et al.~\cite{wang2010anonymousLookupAttacks,mittal2012informationleaks}.
Routing-table compromise as an anonymity/metadata leak channel (for storage/recipient anonymity) was quantified in an information-theoretic framework by Ray and Zhang~\cite{ray2007itframework}.
Octopus aims to provide both security and anonymity for DHT lookup via splitting and attacker identification~\cite{wang2012octopus}.

On the ``query privacy'' axis, Backes et al.~\cite{backes2012queryprivacy} study adding query privacy to robust DHTs.
More recently, Daniel et al.~\cite{daniel2025kademliaObfuscation} propose query-obfuscation methods for Kademlia-style lookups.
Aktypi and Rasmussen's Iris system targets query privacy in authenticated Chord networks while remaining compatible with non-upgraded peers~\cite{aktypi2025iris}.
These works improve \emph{content} privacy of the queried key, but (unless combined with additional mechanisms) still leave a contact-footprint witness channel: which peers/committees were contacted and in what pattern.
For deployed-scale context, BEP-5 specifies the Mainline BitTorrent DHT~\cite{bep5}.
Large-scale measurement studies highlight that Mainline DHT deployments can involve tens of millions of nodes with substantial churn~\cite{wang2013mldht,mldhtProjectPage}.
BitTorrent DHT crawling and measurement attacks further motivate treating contact footprints as a realistic observation channel~\cite{wolchok2010crawling,falkner2007profiling}.
Real-world Sybil attacks have also been observed at scale in Mainline DHT deployments, reinforcing that adversaries can both observe and manipulate contact footprints~\cite{wang2012sybil}.

Cryptographic \emph{query-privacy} mechanisms (PIR/PSI-style) can hide the lookup item from contacted peers, but they do not by themselves hide \emph{which} peers/committees are contacted; the contact-set witness channel remains.

Beyond IPFS, I2P's netDB uses a DHT-like floodfill subset for storing and locating RouterInfos and LeaseSets; entries are replicated to the three floodfills closest to a routing key and lookups are often sent in parallel to two closest ``good'' floodfills~\cite{i2p_netdb_overview}.
Notably, the operational docs also distinguish confidentiality across object types: LeaseSet lookups are end-to-end garlic encrypted (hiding content from intermediate routers), while RouterInfo lookups are not encrypted and can be snooped by the outbound endpoint of the client tunnel~\cite{i2p_netdb_overview}.

Finally, IPFS uses a Kademlia-derived DHT for peer/content discovery~\cite{ipfs_kad_dht_spec}.
This surface has been shown vulnerable to Sybil/eclipse and censorship behaviors, including provider-record censorship in NDSS'24~\cite{sridhar2024ipfs_censorship}, single-machine Sybil denial of content access~\cite{cholez2024ipfs_sybil}, follow-on active Sybil refinements~\cite{netto2025ipfs_active_sybil}, and network-level/BGP censorship models~\cite{mattertran2025ipfs_censorship}.
On the privacy side, Peer2PIR integrates PIR into IPFS routing to reduce query-content leakage~\cite{mazmudar2024peer2pir}, while Karapapas et al. document anonymity abuse patterns and gateway/pinning behavior in the wild~\cite{karapapas2025ipfs_anonymity_abuse}.
These systems motivate contact-footprint analysis, but none is treated here as an automatic instantiation of MUCC or of the theorem parameters.  Any such transfer requires the source-bound extraction checklist in Section~\ref{sec:protocol-mapping}.




\section{Protocol extraction and non-transfer checklist}\label{sec:protocol-mapping}

The theorem applies only after a concrete system supplies a source-bound model.  A reviewer must extract: (i) the observer and the exact projection producing $C$; (ii) the valid destination family $\mathcal D$; (iii) the committee universe and destination semantics $(M,d,t)$; (iv) the actual joint scheduler law $\mathcal L(C\mid D)$, not merely per-label frequencies; (v) the live-response row $\alpha$ with its evidentiary direction and any separate inner tuple $(n,h,q,\rho)$; and (vi) the fixed-stop/retry semantics.  Missing rows block transfer.

\section{Takeaways}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{MUCC is not privacy:} exact one-bit marginal equalization can coexist with disjoint joint supports, total-variation distance one, and perfect destination recovery.
\item \textbf{The useful theorem is conditional cost:} under a declared MUCC/diameter row and the correctly directed live-yield assumptions, availability forces contact expenditure; $152$, $228$, and $250$ belong to different assumption rungs.
\item \textbf{Both axes must be proved:} joint destination privacy does not imply committee-label uniformity, and label-uniform MUCC does not imply joint destination privacy.
\item \textbf{Abort-safe equalization does not erase the cost floor:} fixed-stop dummy work may hide retries, but it cannot convert marginal equalization into privacy or reduce the theorem's total-contact requirement.
\item \textbf{Protocol analogies fail closed:} IPFS, I2P, and other overlays need new source-bound witness, destination-family, joint-law, and liveness cards before this theorem transfers.
\end{itemize}
