- Reason for hold: a model-level terminology and theorem-boundary repair was required. The former Published-ready paper called `log2(pi(i|o)/pi(i)) = log2(P_i(o)/P(o))` posterior-odds inflation. That quantity is posterior-mass inflation and, after maximizing over identities, pointwise maximal leakage (PML). True one-vs-complement odds use `P_i(o)/P_{-i}(o)` and can be infinite even when PML is one bit.
- Corrected true-odds boundary: if `p=pi(i)` and `m=pi(i|o)/pi(i)`, then the true odds factor is `m(1-p)/(1-pm)` only while `pm<1`. A finite PML cap gives a finite uniform true-odds cap only with a declared prior upper bound and the no-saturation guard `2^epsilon p_max < 1`. Deterministic revelation of a uniform binary identity is the hostile control: PML is exactly one bit and true odds are infinite.
- Corrected approximate boundary: hockey-stick slack `delta` is weighted excess `E_{2^epsilon}(P_i||P)`, not in general the probability of a bad transcript. A loss-tail certificate implies a hockey-stick envelope, but the converse fails: for `P_1=(0.51,0.49)`, `P_2=(0.49,0.51)`, and their uniform mixture, the zero-bit hockey-stick slack is `0.01` while the positive-loss event has probability `0.51`.
- Tightened conversion: common-reference total variation gives the singleton bound `|P(o)-Q(o)| <= delta`; the valid ratio envelope is `(Q_min+delta)/(Q_min-delta)` for `delta<Q_min`. The old doubled `2 delta` payment was unnecessary.
