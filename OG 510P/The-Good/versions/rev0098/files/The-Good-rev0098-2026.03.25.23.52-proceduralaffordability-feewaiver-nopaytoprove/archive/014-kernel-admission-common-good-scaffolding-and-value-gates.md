# 014 — Kernel admission, common-good scaffolding, and value gates

## One-line thesis

The archive should grow by a **kernel-admission protocol** rather than by value accretion: when a candidate good appears, first classify whether it is a final good, a floor-protecting condition, a common-good facility, an indicator, or a speculative compression; only candidates that are nonderivatively important, floor-relevant, relatively nonfungible, publicly defensible, and nonredundant should enter the kernel set.

## Why this matters

The archive now has a working synthesis of priority kernels and a protocol for tragic collisions, but it still needs a disciplined rule for expansion. Without such a rule, two failures become likely. First, the kernel list can bloat until it simply restates everything attractive. Second, convenient indicators or institutional means can quietly harden into claims about what is finally good.

A theory of the good needs a growth rule: how new candidates are sorted, admitted, held at a lower rank, or quarantined.

## First question: what kind of thing is the candidate?

Before asking how important a candidate is, ask **what sort of normative role** it plays.

### 1. Final or constitutive good

Something may be good for its own sake or as part of what makes a life or common world genuinely better: friendship, dignity, meaning, truthful world-contact, freedom from domination.

### 2. Floor-protecting condition

Something may matter because without it, persons predictably fall below a minimally decent threshold: nutrition, shelter, basic care capacity, bodily security, usable time, interpretable information.

### 3. Common-good scaffolding

Some goods are best understood as shared facilities or social conditions that people owe one another as members of a common world: clean air, courts, public health systems, reliable communication channels, public education, civic contestation paths. These are not merely private consumptions added together. They are maintained in common because they serve interests that members have in common.

### 4. Indicator or proxy

Some items help track whether the good is being secured without being themselves final goods: GDP growth, a trust index, school test scores, engagement statistics, a resilience score, a composite wellbeing dashboard.

### 5. Local derivative aim

Some items are worth pursuing in a domain because they usually protect deeper goods there: shorter hospital waiting times, lower road deaths, lower lead exposure, fewer wrongful convictions.

### 6. Speculative compression

Some candidates may be bold unifying descriptions that seem illuminating but are not yet stable enough for canon. These belong in quarantine until they survive more pressure.

## Admission test for kernel status

A candidate does **not** become a kernel merely because it sounds noble. It should clear most of the following gates.

### 1. Nonderivative importance

The candidate should matter for its own sake, or so closely structure final value that treating it as merely instrumental would obviously miss something central.

### 2. Floor relevance

Its loss should predictably maim lives, agency, standing, or the conditions of minimally decent coexistence. Kernel status is stronger when absence creates serious failure rather than merely reduced elegance.

### 3. Relative nonfungibility

Losses in this domain should resist easy compensation by unrelated gains elsewhere. The more a candidate behaves like a non-substitutable threshold or leaves tragic remainder when breached, the stronger the case for kernel status.

### 4. Public defensibility under pluralism

Because kernels guide shared judgment, they should be defensible to people who disagree about religion, perfection, and the best life. This does not require neutrality about everything; it requires that the kernel not depend for its authority on one contested doctrine alone.

### 5. Cross-context robustness

The candidate should travel across multiple settings rather than only flattering one class, regime type, or cultural style. A kernel should continue to matter in intimate life, institutional design, and temporal planning, even if its expression changes.

### 6. Nonredundancy

If the candidate is already substantially covered by an existing kernel or by a common-good facility underneath that kernel, the archive should resist multiplying categories. Expansion is justified when it adds real explanatory or protective power.

### 7. Operational separability

The candidate should be trackable enough to discipline judgment without collapsing straight back into a proxy. If it can only be represented by one brittle scalar, caution is warranted.

## Decision outputs

After classification and testing, the archive should make one of five moves.

### 1. Admit as kernel

Use when the candidate is final or floor-defining, nonfungible enough to resist routine tradeoff, publicly defensible, and not already covered.

### 2. Treat as common-good scaffolding

Use when the candidate is a shared facility or social condition that protects multiple kernels. This is an important status, but it differs from saying the facility itself is the deepest unit of final value.

### 3. Keep as dashboard indicator

Use when the candidate is mainly legible evidence of deeper goods. Indicators belong in governance, but should not rule the ontology.

### 4. Keep as local derivative aim

Use when the candidate is a strong domain target that usually protects deeper goods without needing archive-wide constitutional rank.

### 5. Quarantine

Use when the candidate may be a powerful compression, but the archive cannot yet tell whether it clarifies value or merely overfits a preferred vocabulary.

## Worked examples

### 1. Relationship goods

Love, friendship, and non-isolating social belonging look like direct candidates for kernel status. They are constitutive for many lives, predictably floor-relevant under deprivation, and not cleanly replaceable by income or prestige.

### 2. Clean air

Clean air should usually be treated as common-good scaffolding with floor significance. It is not just a preference bundle; it is a shared condition required for bodily integrity, care, and intergenerational continuity.

### 3. GDP growth

GDP growth is not a kernel. At best it is a partial indicator or sometimes a means. It can rise while care, equality, truthfulness, ecological continuity, or dignity fall.

### 4. Achievement and excellence

Achievement matters deeply, but often fits better as plural flourishing above the floor or as common-good scaffolding for plural excellence than as a universal kernel. The archive should resist turning one style of aspiration into constitutional rank for all. `archive/060-aspiration-excellence-and-non-humiliating-standards.md` sharpens this by distinguishing worthwhile standards from status hierarchy, humiliation, and meritocratic laundering.

### 5. Rights institutions

Many rights-protecting institutions are best understood as common-good scaffolding or institutional expressions of deeper kernels such as dignity, non-domination, and contestability.

## Implications for archive growth

- New candidate goods should be classified before they are celebrated.
- Shared facilities should not be mistaken for scalar outputs.
- Indicators should stay answerable to the deeper goods they track.
- Kernel expansion should be slow, explicit, and burdened by a presumption of nonredundancy.
- Open questions about animals, future persons, and artificial agents should use this same gate set, while allowing for domain-specific reinterpretation.

## Guardrails

- Do not promote every morally attractive noun into a kernel.
- Do not demote public infrastructure into mere instrumentality when it is a standing condition of shared life.
- Do not let what is easy to measure masquerade as what is finally important.
- Do not use public defensibility as an excuse to erase difficult goods; use it to discipline how shared institutions invoke them.

## Compression rule for the archive

Admit a value as kernel-level only when its loss predictably damages lives or equal standing in ways that cannot be treated as ordinary exchange, and classify everything else as scaffolding, indicator, local aim, or quarantine until it earns more.
