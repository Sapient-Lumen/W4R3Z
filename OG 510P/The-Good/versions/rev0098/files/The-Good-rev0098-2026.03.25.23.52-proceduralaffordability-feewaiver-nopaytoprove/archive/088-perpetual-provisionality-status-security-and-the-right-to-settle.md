# 088 — Perpetual provisionality, status security, and the right to settle

## One-line thesis

A salient theory of **The Good** must treat durable status security and freedom from **perpetual provisionality** as morally central rather than as mere administrative timing, because lives cannot go well when housing, work, membership, care, schooling, or legal standing are held in endlessly renewable temporary form that blocks planning, attachment, refusal, and ordinary equal belonging.

## Why this matters

The archive is already strong on dwelling, movement and membership, documentary recognition, continuity, bounded emergency, and humane arrival. But it still lacks a direct canon rule against a recurrent deformation: keeping people in a standing condition of **not yet**.

That deformation appears when a shelter bed is temporary for years, a worker remains on “short-term” contracts decade after decade, a resident lives under endlessly renewed permits, a family waits through repeated recertification without a plausible route to settlement, a child grows up while housing, schooling, or papers remain pending, or a newcomer is always treated as almost-admitted but not fully of the place. In such settings people are not simply uncertain in the abstract. They are ruled through renewable dependence.

This matters because provisionality is not just a clock problem. It is a power structure. People who can be non-renewed, transferred, downgraded, or left pending learn to moderate complaint, postpone repair, avoid deep attachment, accept bad work, delay treatment, hide real family arrangements, and live tactically rather than openly. A salient theory of the good therefore needs a clearer rule: some genuinely temporary arrangements are unavoidable, but they require a specific purpose, bounded duration, reviewable justification, and real pathways toward settlement, regularization, secure tenure, or some comparably stable replacement. The good includes a **right to settle somewhere in important domains of life** rather than being managed forever as a provisional case.

## Core claims

### 1. Provisionality can itself be a mode of domination

A person need not be openly commanded at every step to be governed through uncertainty. Renewable discretion is often enough. When housing, papers, work authorization, school placement, care access, or residency remain dependent on repeated renewal by gatekeepers, people live under a standing power they must continuously appease.

### 2. Agency needs a planning horizon

Planning is part of the good. People need enough temporal security to learn, parent, decorate, invest, heal, organize, protest, grieve, study, save, and imagine a future. A life that can never extend beyond the next review date, permit expiry, contract renewal, shelter transfer, or landlord decision is morally thinner even when immediate survival is being managed.

### 3. Security of status matters across domains, not only at the border

The same structure appears in many places: insecure tenure in housing, rolling temporary contracts in work, probationary or sponsor-dependent migration statuses, foster or shelter systems organized around serial extension, benefits that require repeated innocence performance, and documentary statuses that remain unresolved long enough to make ordinary life contingent. The archive should therefore treat status security as a cross-domain design property rather than as a specialist migration issue It should also ask whether one gatekeeper controls several of these dependencies at once, because provisionality becomes more coercive when work, stay, housing, and complaint safety travel together in one sponsorship stack.

### 4. Endless temporary arrangements redistribute risk downward

Institutions often like provisionality because it preserves flexibility for those in charge. Employers keep labor disposable. Landlords keep removal easy. States delay durable membership decisions. Services ration commitment by making users repeatedly prove themselves. The uncertainty cost is then carried by the weaker party in the form of silence, self-censorship, unstable planning, and attachment held at arm’s length.

### 5. Genuinely temporary measures need pathways, not just extensions

Some statuses should indeed be temporary: emergency shelter, acute protection measures, trial supports, short transitional placements, immediate disaster response, or time-bounded forms of provisional protection. But temporary measures become morally defective when renewal becomes the substitute for building a return path, a regularization route, a secure tenure option, or another durable solution.

### 6. Settlement is compatible with mobility, plurality, and exit

This note does not glorify immobility or demand that every life be fixed in place. People may want seasonal movement, periodic change, plural homes, trial arrangements, or exit from stale commitments. The point is not to force permanence. It is to resist making insecurity the default condition under which only the powerful get to remain flexible.

### 7. Status security should not depend on exemplary obedience under dependency

A good order should not make durable standing answer mainly to gratitude, extraordinary compliance, endless document purity, sponsor approval, perfect attendance, or strategic silence while vulnerable. Where settlement or regularization is justified, it should be reachable on intelligible and humane terms rather than by moral heroics under domination.

## A working rule for the archive

### 1. Ask whether “temporary” is serving a real transition or merely managing people cheaply

When evaluating shelters, camps, visas, work contracts, probationary benefits, school placements, ward stays, or transitional programs, ask whether temporary status is tied to a concrete transition with a plausible endpoint or whether it mainly preserves institutional convenience and discretionary power.

### 2. Require visible duration, renewal standards, and review

A humane temporary arrangement should have a stated purpose, intelligible duration, renewal criteria, notification, and review routes. Indefinite extension under opaque standards should be treated as a warning sign of domination rather than as neutral caution.

### 3. Prefer conversion paths to secure tenure or ordinary standing

Where dependency, residence, contribution, attachment, or vulnerability are real, build pathways toward stable tenancy, regularized status, permanent records correction, continuing school placement, continuing care registration, or secure work terms rather than endless serial reapplication.

### 4. Count renewal burden and pending time as substantive harms

Repeated extensions, recertifications, sponsor signatures, status checks, and unresolved files are not morally trivial. They consume time, inhibit speech, weaken trust, and keep whole lives from gathering continuity. The archive should count time-in-limbo as part of the injury itself.

### 5. Treat long provisionality as a challenge to equal standing

If a person can use the library, work the job, raise the child, pay the rent, survive the shelter, or remain in the neighborhood only while being tacitly reminded that their place is not secure, the archive should read that as a degradation of equal standing even when some services are present.

## What this changes in the archive

### 1. It thickens dwelling by naming secure tenure as more than indoor continuity

`archive/029-home-dwelling-place-and-non-displacement.md` already protects dwelling against homelessness and uprooting. This note adds that dwelling is still thinner when people remain under endlessly renewable “temporary” tenure, shelter extensions, or removal-ready arrangements that never permit ordinary settlement.

### 2. It thickens livelihood by naming rolling temporariness as a labor-power asymmetry

`archive/032-livelihood-meaningful-work-and-non-servility.md` already protects decent livelihood and real exit from degrading work. This note adds that work can be unfree not only because it is dangerous or underpaid, but because serial temporary status keeps workers perpetually cautious, sponsor-dependent, or unable to plan beyond the next renewal.

### 3. It strengthens movement and membership by refusing limbo as policy success

`archive/033-movement-migration-membership-and-non-exile.md` already rejects arbitrary exile, deportability, and camp limbo. This note adds a cleaner cross-domain rule: protection without a believable path to durable standing is often containment in humanitarian or administrative dress.

### 4. It clarifies documentary recognition by focusing on durable usability rather than one-time issuance

`archive/049-recognition-before-the-law-documentary-existence-and-non-erasure.md` already protects legal existence and document continuity. This note adds that recognition also fails when nationality, residence, kinship, disability status, or other legal standing remains unresolved long enough to keep a life provisional.

### 5. It extends hospitality and continuity beyond first arrival and serial restart

`archive/065-hospitality-welcome-and-low-friction-arrival.md` already protects humane first contact, and `archive/074-continuity-non-churn-and-durable-attachment.md` already protects life against serial reset. This note adds that arrival failure and restart failure often converge in one structure: a life kept permanently not-yet-settled.

## Failure modes

- **permanent temporary**: emergency, trial, or stopgap arrangements become the stable reality.
- **renewal governance**: people remain governable through repeated reapproval rather than ordinary standing.
- **perform-to-remain life**: gratitude, silence, exceptional compliance, or sponsor pleasing become covert conditions of durability.
- **limbo by design**: institutions never decide cleanly because pendingness preserves leverage.
- **cheap flexibility for the strong**: employers, landlords, and states keep optionality by exporting uncertainty downward.
- **humanitarian containment**: food, tents, vouchers, or minimal services are used to normalize long-term non-membership.
- **documentary maybe-status**: people exist on paper only through unresolved, expiring, or cross-system-incompatible records.

## Guardrails

- Do not treat all temporary arrangements as suspect; many transitions need genuinely short-term forms.
- Do not force permanence where people need mobility, experimentation, or safe exit.
- Do not romanticize settlement into inherited hierarchy, territorial closure, or family entrapment.
- Do not confuse justified review with endless provisionality; the problem is opaque, serial, and dependency-intensifying temporariness.
- Do not make regularization or secure standing depend on impossible paperwork purity or moral heroism under precarity.
- Do not let flexibility rhetoric mean that only weaker parties must stay flexible.

## Compression rule for the archive

A good order may use temporary arrangements for real transitions, but it should not keep people governable through endless provisionality when durable standing, secure tenure, or a real path to settlement is morally due.
