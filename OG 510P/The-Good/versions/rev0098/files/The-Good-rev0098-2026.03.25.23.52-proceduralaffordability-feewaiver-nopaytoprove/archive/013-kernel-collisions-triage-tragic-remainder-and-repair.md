# 013 — Kernel collisions: triage, tragic remainder, and repair

## One-line thesis

A usable theory of the good must distinguish **ordinary tradeoffs** from **tragic collisions among protected kernels**; when collision cannot be avoided, judgment should minimize irreversible floor breaches, preserve equal standing, and carry forward visible duties of repair rather than pretending the losses were cleanly compensated.

## Why this matters

The archive now has a working synthesis of priority kernels, but it still needs a conflict protocol. Without one, kernel pluralism risks two opposite failures: paralysis when protected goods collide, or a quiet slide back into scalar optimization where every loss is treated as just another cost. A serious theory of the good needs a way to act under emergency while remaining honest that some actions leave moral remainder.

## Distinguish the kinds of conflict

### 1. Enhancement versus enhancement

Many conflicts are not tragic. They concern different ways of enlarging flourishing above protected floors: culture versus leisure spending, parks versus museums, one educational model versus another. These are real disputes, but they should not be dramatised as kernel collisions.

### 2. Floor versus enhancement

When a protected floor conflicts with an optional gain above the floor, the presumption should usually favor the floor. Convenience, prestige, speed, or aggregate uplift should not casually override bodily integrity, basic care, anti-cruelty, or non-domination protections.

### 3. Floor versus floor

The hardest cases are those in which protected goods appear to conflict: truth versus panic control, autonomy versus urgent care, liberty versus emergency quarantine, one group’s safety versus another group’s livelihood, one patient versus another under true scarcity. These are the cases that test whether the archive means what it says about non-compensable losses.

### 4. Manufactured dilemmas

Some "tragic" cases are largely products of bad institutional design. Underfunded care systems, disinformation-heavy emergency response, and brittle supply chains can force actors into unnecessary sacrifice choices. The archive should treat dilemma-prevention as part of the theory of the good, not as mere implementation detail. `archive/085-bounded-emergency-exception-drift-and-return-to-ordinary-rule.md` adds the companion rule that even when triage is real, emergency procedure should not quietly migrate into ordinary governance as a standing license for thinner rights, weaker review, or permanent exception posture.

## Working protocol for kernel collisions

### 1. Re-describe the case before ranking it

Ask whether the conflict is genuine, epistemic, or institutionally manufactured. Many apparent dilemmas weaken once facts improve, options widen, or coercive background assumptions are challenged.

### 2. Try shielding before balancing

Before trading one protected good against another, search for institutional redesign, sequencing, exemptions, redundancy, or burden-sharing that can keep both partly intact. The first task is often not to rank values but to avoid forcing a direct collision.

### 3. Give special resistance to irreversible and status-injuring harms

Where conflict remains, presumptively resist options that impose severe bodily devastation, cruelty, civic degradation, humiliation, or entrenched subordination. These losses often damage the very standing from which later compensation would have to be offered.

### 4. Refuse sacrificial concentration

Do not repeatedly solve public problems by pushing losses onto the already dependent, politically weak, or informationally excluded. A theory of the good that protects equal standing cannot treat patterned sacrifice of the same groups as acceptable cleanup.

### 5. Preserve agency and truthful disclosure as far as conditions allow

Even under emergency, affected persons should be informed, heard, and treated as participants rather than managed objects, unless immediate catastrophe makes this impossible.

### 6. Record remainder and assign repair duties

When a decision breaches a protected kernel, even necessarily, do not describe the result as fully justified and complete. Register what was lost, who bore it, why it happened, what reparative steps are owed, and what institutional redesign is required to reduce recurrence.

### 7. Keep coercive resolutions publicly answerable

When power is used to settle tragic cases, reasons should remain publicly inspectable and revisable. Emergency necessity is not a license for opaque or permanently self-authorizing rule.

## What moral remainder means here

Moral remainder is the archive’s name for the residue left when some protected claim is unavoidably wronged, burdened, or denied. It matters because theories of the good can become morally numbing when they present tragic loss as fully cancelled by aggregate gain. The right response to remainder is not sentimentalism but a disciplined package of acknowledgement, repair, compensation where possible, memorialization where appropriate, and structural prevention.

## Failure modes

- **clean-hands arithmetic**: a calculus declares all losses absorbed because the net total improved.
- **emergency laundering**: officials normalize extraordinary coercion by relabeling it as necessity.
- **hierarchical triage**: the same classes of people are repeatedly selected to absorb the loss.
- **repairless realism**: actors admit tragedy but treat acknowledgement as enough.
- **manufactured scarcity amnesia**: preventable institutional failure is forgotten once triage begins.

## Guardrails

- Do not call a case tragic until option-expansion and redesign have been seriously attempted.
- Do not infer from necessity that nothing wrong was done.
- Do not let triage rules migrate into ordinary governance as default policy.
- Do not let reparative language conceal avoidable abuse or domination.

## Compression rule for the archive

When protected goods collide, first try to prevent the collision; if that fails, choose the path that breaches the fewest and least irreversible floors, preserves equal standing as far as possible, and leaves visible duties of repair behind it.
