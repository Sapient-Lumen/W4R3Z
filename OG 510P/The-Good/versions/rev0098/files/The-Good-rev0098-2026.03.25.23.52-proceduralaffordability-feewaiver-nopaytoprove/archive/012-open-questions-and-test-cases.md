# 012 — Open questions and test cases

## One-line thesis

The archive should evaluate theories of the good not only by elegance, but by how they perform on **hard cases, tragic structures, and frontier entities**.

## Why this matters

A theory can look strong in abstract prose and fail instantly when confronted with caregiving asymmetry, ecological sacrifice, epistemic corruption, cultural plurality, or beings whose standing is unsettled. The archive should therefore keep a visible backlog of questions and test cases that exert pressure on every synthesis.

## Open questions

### 1. Animals and other sentient beings

`archive/015-patient-scope-sentience-and-living-systems.md` now gives the archive a first scope rule: sentience creates a strong presumptive anti-cruelty floor, while personhood thickens some claims without monopolizing standing. What remains open is boundary work: which beings are plausible patients, where uncertainty should trigger precaution, and how species-specific forms of flourishing should be described without anthropomorphic flattening.

### 2. Artificial persons or moral patients

`archive/054-possible-artificial-persons-synthetic-welfare-and-humane-uncertainty.md` now gives the archive a first frontier rule: credible synthetic welfare or person-like agency should trigger humane uncertainty rather than pure tool-default. What remains open is the hard boundary work around evidence thresholds, continuity across copies and branches, shutdown and memory modification, representation, and which rights-like protections arrive at which evidential stage.

### 3. Meaning versus equality

`archive/060-aspiration-excellence-and-non-humiliating-standards.md` now gives the archive a first rule here: protect demanding goods without turning performance into civic ranking. `archive/077-status-tournament-comparison-pressure-and-non-ranked-personhood.md` adds a second rule: permit domain-limited evaluation without normalizing social worlds that rank whole persons by comparative prestige. `archive/079-meaning-ordinary-worth-and-non-heroic-flourishing.md` now adds a third: meaningful life is plural and should not be monopolized by prestige, heroism, sacrificial vocation, or elite visibility. What remains open is how public institutions should honor difficult or exemplary callings without quietly teaching that non-heroic lives are spiritually second-class, and when domain-limited admiration turns back into a wider hierarchy of seriousness.

### 4. Care versus autonomy

`archive/050-care-supported-dependency-and-non-abandonment.md` now makes care and supported dependency a first-class archive concern. What remains open is the boundary question: when does support genuinely enlarge freedom, and when does it become domination, family overrule, or institutional custody in a protective costume?

### 4A. Special obligations, partiality, and anti-nepotism boundaries

`archive/080-special-obligations-legitimate-partiality-and-anti-nepotism-boundaries.md` now gives the archive a first rule here: some relationship-grounded partial duties are real and should be made livable, but public office and common goods should not be bent by kinship, friendship, or sponsor access. What remains open is the harder boundary work around compatriot partiality, when institutions should formally accommodate care ties, and how to judge cases where private loyalty and entrusted public role genuinely collide.

### 5. Public reason versus controversial excellence

What can a polity justifiably promote in common life when citizens disagree deeply about the best form of human flourishing?

### 6. Present sacrifice for future continuity

How much present deprivation can be justified for long-run ecological or institutional preservation?

### 7. Truth, education, and psychic stability

When institutions disclose disturbing truths, design curricula, or mediate attention through schools, platforms, and AI systems, how should they balance reality-contact against panic, grief, overload, or social destabilization without sliding into benevolent censorship or outsourced judgment?

### 8. Tragic remainder and repair

When protected goods genuinely collide, which losses should be treated as leaving reparative remainder rather than as cleanly outweighed costs?

### 9. Kernel admission drift

Which candidate goods deserve kernel status, which are better treated as common-good scaffolding, and which should remain indicators or domain targets rather than archive-level constitutional commitments?

### 10. Inequality, inheritance, and civic capture

How much material inequality is compatible with equal standing, and when do inheritance, concentrated ownership, or platform power become forms of private government rather than mere uneven success?

### 10A. Desert, luck, and worthiness filters

`archive/081-desert-luck-humility-and-non-conditional-basic-standing.md` now gives the archive a first rule here: basic standing and floor goods should not be treated as rewards for the disciplined, productive, or respectable, because lives are too shaped by luck and dependency for subsistence and ordinary recognition to answer primarily to moralized desert. What remains open is the harder boundary work around when targeting is justified, which conduct conditions ever survive scrutiny for essential goods, and how to distinguish legitimate role trust from covert worthiness sorting.

### 10B. Fair allocation under scarcity, bounded first-come, and anti-speed privilege

`archive/092-fair-allocation-under-scarcity-bounded-first-come-and-anti-speed-privilege.md` now gives the archive a first rule here: when scarce slots or public goods must be assigned, speed, queue endurance, digital reflex, price, and broker access should not ordinarily decide what instead ought to turn on need, transparent criteria, and sometimes fair lottery or rotation. What remains open is the harder boundary work around when first-come-first-served is acceptable, how to prevent triage from collapsing into respectability sorting, when lottery is fairer than priority scoring, and how far institutions must expand capacity before rationing itself becomes the deeper failure.

### 10C. Broker dependence, fixer markets, and non-insider rights

`archive/093-broker-dependence-fixer-markets-and-non-insider-rights.md` now gives the archive a first rule here: basic rights, floor goods, public services, and first-step remedies should usually remain directly reachable without paid fixers, sponsor mediation, consultant dependence, or insider patronage, even when optional support and expert help remain available. What remains open is the harder boundary work around when specialized assistance is genuinely necessary, how much complexity public systems may permissibly leave to users, when legal aid and accompaniment should be publicly guaranteed, and how to distinguish humane guidance from access-by-intermediary as the real operating system.

### 11. Uncertainty, irreversibility, and burden of proof

When evidence is incomplete but stakes are high, what should trigger precaution, when should reversibility outrank speed, and who should have to justify exposing others to uncertain but potentially grave loss?

### 11A. Bounded emergency, exception drift, and return paths

`archive/085-bounded-emergency-exception-drift-and-return-to-ordinary-rule.md` now gives the archive a first rule here: real emergencies may justify urgent and even temporarily extraordinary action, but exception should stay specific, reviewable, and restoration-oriented rather than becoming the default grammar of governance. What remains open is the harder boundary work around derogations, procurement shortcuts, curfew and checkpoint creep, public-health restrictions that outlive their evidence base, when camp-temporary arrangements become ordinary containment, and what repair is owed after emergency measures damage records, trust, movement, care continuity, or civic standing.

### 12. Preference formation, manipulation, and adaptive resignation

When should a reported choice count as meaningful endorsement, when is it better read as resignation under blocked options, and how should institutions respond to environments intentionally designed to steer attention, consent, appetite, or sleep?

### 13. Public reason, culture, and affirmative flourishing

How far may institutions go in protecting leisure, play, beauty, public culture, shared celebration, and pathways into serious achievement without sliding into class-coded perfectionism, nationalist pageantry, or soft coercion about the supposedly superior life? `archive/078-public-reason-plural-flourishing-and-non-orthodox-common-life.md` now gives the archive a first rule here: public authority may support plural invitations into flourishing, culture, learning, and civic friendship without imposing one sectarian or class-coded ideal of the superior life. What remains open is the harder boundary work around curriculum, ceremony, subsidies, patriotic formation, and when publicly supported culture turns into majoritarian pageantry, elite curation monopoly, or soft coercion.

### 14. Voice, participation, and shared rule

When is consultation enough, when is co-determination required, and how much say should people have over workplaces, platforms, schools, housing, and automated systems that structure everyday life?

### 15. Privacy, sanctuary, and selective legibility

How much exposure may institutions justifiably demand as the price of service, safety, work, credit, schooling, or participation, and when do surveillance, searchable permanence, ranked resurfacing, or repeated disclosure become direct degradations rather than merely regrettable administrative costs?

### 15A. Affective gatekeeping, tone discipline, and compulsory composure

`archive/084-affective-freedom-non-compelled-cheer-and-no-tone-gated-standing.md` now gives the archive a first rule here: institutions may enforce boundaries against threat and abuse, but they should not make calmness, gratitude, deference, or cheerful self-command the hidden price of credibility, help, or ordinary belonging. What remains open is the harder boundary work around worker-facing emotional labor, when de-escalation genuinely protects others, and how to distinguish legitimate conduct rules from class-coded, trauma-blind, or normate tone policing.

### 15B. Presumptive permission, permit choke points, and approval creep

`archive/089-presumptive-permission-bounded-prior-approval-and-non-licensed-ordinary-life.md` now gives the archive a first rule here: ordinary harmless action should usually begin from presumptive permission rather than prior approval, with licensing reserved for bounded high-risk cases. What remains open is the harder boundary work around when notification is enough, when small-scale trade or common-space use may legitimately be limited, how digital preclearance changes the line between coordination and permission, and when environmental or safety review genuinely requires ex ante restraint rather than later accountability.

### 16. Rest, rhythm, and time sovereignty

When does intensity count as chosen striving, and when is it better read as coerced availability, schedule precarity, care squeeze, administrative time theft, forced haste, or ambient overstimulation? How much off-time, predictability, tempo pluralism, slower fallback, attentional quiet, darkness, and sensory refuge does a good order owe before rights, culture, sleep, and participation become nominal rather than usable?

### 16AA. Enoughness, optimization drift, and permission not to maximize

`archive/086-enoughness-anti-maximization-and-room-for-unoptimized-life.md` now gives the archive a first rule here: a good order may widen access to health, learning, mastery, and other real goods without turning every domain into a permanent improvement project. What remains open is the harder boundary work around when public-health encouragement becomes bodily self-management pressure, when school support becomes résumé childhood, how to distinguish humane standards from maximizer morality, and which zones of life should remain partly untracked, non-instrumental, or protected from metric creep.

### 16AB. Cumulative burden, friction stacks, and anti-cascade design

`archive/087-cumulative-burden-friction-stacks-and-anti-cascade-design.md` now gives the archive a first rule here: institutions should be judged by the total workload they impose on ordinary lives, not only by whether each separate step looks locally reasonable in isolation. What remains open is the harder boundary work around how to set burden budgets without central overreach, when interoperability genuinely reduces burden versus expanding surveillance, which life events should trigger automatic pause or offloading rights, and how to distinguish justified complexity from stack-blind friction that quietly converts rights into exhaustion.

### 16A. Everyday nature, green access, and contact with the living world

When does a city, neighborhood, campus, school, clinic, shelter, prison, or care setting become ecologically barren even if it remains technically safe enough to inhabit? How much nearby shade, tree canopy, weather legibility, habitat, water access, and low-threshold contact with living systems does a good order owe before nature becomes effectively a premium enclave, tourism product, or decorative afterthought rather than part of ordinary life?

### 16B. Ordinary beauty, aesthetic dignity, and non-degrading environments

When does a school, clinic, shelter, housing block, station, office, or street become aesthetically degrading even if it remains formally functional? How much design care, warmth, repair, texture, sensory respect, and material grace does a good order owe before beauty becomes effectively a luxury district, donor-funded extra, or prestige service while ordinary people are told to accept managed ugliness as civic normality?

### 16C. Spatial sufficiency, uncrowdedness, and room to breathe

How should the archive judge crowding in homes, shelters, wards, classrooms, transit, queues, and public institutions? When does density remain humane, and when does it become compression that erodes privacy, rest, study, care, infection safety, and ordinary dignity? How should the archive distinguish anti-crowding judgment from anti-urban nostalgia, luxury square-footage entitlement, or exclusionary scarcity defended in the name of comfort?

### 16D. Modality pluralism, offline fallback, and non-digital exclusion

When does digitization remain genuinely access-expanding, and when does it become channel monoculture that quietly excludes people without stable devices, connectivity, passwords, biometrics, app fluency, or safe private troubleshooting space? What kind of in-person, phone, print, delegated-help, and low-bandwidth fallback should a good order preserve before public life becomes effectively conditional on always-online compliance?

### 17. Accessibility, disability, and world-fit

How much of what gets called disability disadvantage is produced by environments, institutions, communication defaults, and normate design rather than by impairment alone? When should the archive prioritize universal design, individualized accommodation, community support, or redesign of expectations about speed, productivity, or “normal” performance?

### 18. Markets, pricing, and non-fungible goods

Which goods may be allocated by market exchange, which require strict bounds or public options, and which are distorted when treated as purchasable at all? How should the archive tell ordinary exchange from exploitation, pay-to-skip citizenship, body brokerage, or the sale of standing itself?

### 19. Childhood, developmental asymmetry, and just guardianship

How should the archive combine protection, play, education, privacy, family authority, and evolving-capacity voice without treating children either as parental property or as miniature autonomous adults? When do school discipline, digital monitoring, competitive scheduling, or “best interests” language protect development, and when do they become adult convenience or extraction in a protective costume?

### 20. Later life, frailty, mortality, and bereavement

How should the archive combine autonomy, supported decision-making, pain relief, family burden, social connection, home life, institutional care, and truthful end-of-life choice without sliding either into ageist abandonment or into compulsory prolongation at all costs? When do safety, capacity, and efficiency narratives protect older persons, and when do they become cover for warehousing, overrule, or social death before biological death?

### 21. Home, dwelling, and non-displacement

What does a good order owe people as dwellers rather than merely as bodies to be covered or consumers to be priced? When do rent burden, serial relocation, emergency shelter, redevelopment, disaster adaptation, camp life, or anti-homeless enforcement become direct degradations of privacy, dignity, time, care, schooling, and belonging rather than unfortunate side effects of ordinary housing administration?

### 22. Bodily integrity, consent, and reproductive agency

When does consent remain morally valid under conditions of dependence, pain, urgency, institutional custody, family pressure, poverty, or administrative threat? Which bodily interventions can ever be justified without contemporaneous consent, and under what burden of proof, reviewability, and least-intrusive constraints? When do reproductive laws, care systems, fertility markets, school rules, detention regimes, testing protocols, or public-health policies protect persons, and when do they quietly turn bodies into infrastructure for other people’s goals?

### 23. Intimacy, chosen kin, and exit from coercive ties

Which ties should institutions recognize as serious for visitation, caregiving, mourning, migration, emergency decision support, household support, or bereavement standing? When does family protection preserve attachment, and when does it become cover for forced marriage, partner control, queer or disabled exclusion, or punitive family dependence? How should a good order protect both meaningful connection and materially possible exit without social-engineering one official household form?

### 23A. Ritual, ceremony, and threshold-marking

When should a polity fund, host, or protect low-threshold ceremonial forms for welcome, grief, apology, commitment, return, and public remembrance, and when does such support slide into orthodoxy, pageantry, or majoritarian calendar rule? What distinguishes humane threshold-marking from hazing, patriotic performance, coerced observance, or wealth-gated ceremony?

### 24. Livelihood, contribution, and the refusal of servility

What does a good order owe people in relation to work: merely access to some job, or access to safe, fairly rewarded, voice-bearing, non-humiliating work plus the material floor to refuse degrading labor? When does meaningful work deepen agency and self-respect, and when does its rhetoric become a way to justify overwork, unpaid sacrifice, job lock, or ranking the unwaged as lesser?

### 25. Movement, migration, membership, and non-exile

When do border control, citizenship rules, detention, deportation, family-reunification barriers, camp systems, or documentation burdens become forms of domination and status injury rather than merely unfortunate administration, and what does a good order owe to those who must move, those who wish to stay, and those trapped in between?

### 25A. Compound dependency, anti-hostage access, and decoupled essentials

When should a good order refuse designs that place wages, papers, housing, treatment, communication, schooling continuity, or complaint safety under one gatekeeper's effective control? Which goods should follow the person rather than the employer, sponsor, household head, landlord, or provider, and when does helpful service integration become a hostage architecture that makes exit, refusal, or testimony too expensive to use?

### 26. Health, care continuity, and medicalization

How should the archive combine physical health, mental well-being, prevention, disability, chronic illness, pain relief, public health, and continuity of care without collapsing the good into fitness management or medical paternalism? When do waiting times, insurance gaps, medicine cost, clinic distance, understaffing, coercive psychiatry, or algorithmic triage become direct degradations of dignity and agency rather than unfortunate service failures?

### 27. Food, water, sanitation, and household energy

How should the archive specify the daily material floor without collapsing it into either cash transfer monism or paternal rationing? When do hunger, food-price inflation, utility shutoffs, contaminated water, missing toilets, dirty-fuel dependence, or heat-and-cold exposure count as direct injuries to dignity and agency rather than as regrettable poverty side effects?

### 28. Environmental habitability

How should the archive classify environmental habitability: as part of health, dwelling, climate duty, or a protected good in its own right? When do smoke days, lead exposure, toxic siting, overheated housing, unshaded routes, wildfire risk, or dangerous outdoor work count as direct degradations of the good rather than as background environmental management problems?

### 29. Access to justice, fair hearing, and effective remedy

When does a formal right remain morally hollow because people cannot obtain a hearing, understand the case against them, secure representation or accommodation, or survive the time and cost of challenge? When do mediation, complaint channels, online portals, algorithmic review, or emergency procedure provide humane resolution, and when do they become grievance theater, coercive settlement, or arbitrary rule in administrative clothing?

### 30. Economic security, anti-ruin, and debt domination

How much household-level security does a good order owe before rights, care, work, and membership become too cliff-edged to use? When do debt service, arrears, benefit cliffs, income volatility, medical bills, rent spikes, or extractive credit become forms of domination rather than unfortunate personal-finance trouble?

### 31. Cultural continuity, living heritage, and non-assimilation

When does a polity's demand for integration become coerced assimilation? What does the archive owe persons, minorities, Indigenous peoples, diasporas, and local communities in the ability to inherit, revise, transmit, digitize, repatriate, and publicly practice language, ritual, memory, foodways, sacred relation, and living heritage without being erased, enclosed, extracted, or frozen into display?

### 32. Collective self-determination, territorial authority, and non-colonial rule

How much collective self-determination does the good require for peoples, Indigenous nations, and territorially rooted communities? When is consultation an alibi for rule from above? What does the archive owe communities in authority over lands, waters, institutions, schooling, data, resource revenue, and development pathways, and how should it protect internal dissent, gender equality, mobility, and children's standing when autonomy is invoked?

### 33. Civilian peace, demilitarization, and non-siege life

How should the archive classify war, siege, occupation, and militarized daily life: as failures of security, self-determination, health, and movement, or as a protected good in their own right? When do bombardment, aid denial, attacks on civilian infrastructure, forced recruitment, contamination, or post-conflict peace-washing become direct degradations of the good rather than regrettable security externalities?

### 34. Animal welfare, anti-cruelty, and multispecies coexistence

How should the archive convert its patient-scope claims about sentient animals into first-class protections? When do factory farming, long-distance live transport, painful breeding traits, laboratory use, mass culling, wildlife-conflict management, urban nuisance control, or habitat-hostile conservation become direct degradations of the good rather than unfortunate side effects of food systems, public health, research, or environmental management?

### 35. Legibility, wayfinding, and navigable complexity

When does complexity reflect genuine subject matter, and when is it better read as a maze that quietly redistributes access toward insiders? What does a good order owe in signage, naming, path clarity, no-wrong-door practice, layered explanation, human fallback, and recovery from error before rights, care, transport, public space, and remedy count as actually usable rather than merely declared?

### 36. Proportionality, grace, and recoverable failure

When does enforcement protect persons or publics, and when does it merely defend administrative tidiness by making ordinary fallibility socially terminal? What does the archive owe in warnings, cure periods, staged sanctions, payment plans, human review, error correction, and non-cumulative penalty design before one missed step, small arrears balance, technical violation, false positive, or youthful failure counts as disproportionate exclusion rather than legitimate accountability?

### 37. Scale-fit, subsidiarity, and local knowledge

When should a decision sit near affected people and places, and when do rights floors, fiscal solidarity, ecological spillover, migration governance, or anti-corruption concerns justify wider rule? How should the archive distinguish genuine subsidiarity from unfunded devolution, and local authority from local domination, while preventing institutions from mining community knowledge without sharing budget, timing, or binding power?

### 38. Hospitality, welcome, and low-friction arrival

When does a society remain merely formally open while making first contact degrading, sponsor-dependent, or practically paywalled? What does the archive owe in reception, translation, signage, greeting, no-wrong-door intake, humane onboarding, and non-suspicion-first threshold design before schools, clinics, housing systems, public institutions, workplaces, and border or reintegration pathways count as genuinely enterable rather than theoretically available?

### 39. Portable autonomy, personal effects, and everyday means

When do the small material handles of agency become decisive enough that their absence should count as a direct loss rather than as unfortunate clutter or petty property trouble? What does the archive owe in secure storage, key control, charger access, document custody, assistive-product continuity, offline fallback, low-friction replacement, and protection against confiscation before people in homes, shelters, hospitals, schools, detention, care settings, and migration systems count as practically able to act rather than merely admitted somewhere?

### 39A. Continuity, non-churn, and durable attachment

When does repeated transfer, staff turnover, school change, shelter rotation, service redesign, address churn, or serial re-onboarding become a direct injury to dignity, trust, development, and agency rather than a neutral feature of flexible systems? What does a good order owe in stable contact points, memory-preserving handoff, record continuity, low-churn dwelling, and portable recognition before movement, exit, or institutional change stop resetting life back to zero?

### 39B. Ordinary legitimacy, bounded verification, and non-suspicion-first life

When does a society stop checking for concrete reasons and start organizing daily life around ambient suspicion? What does the archive owe in objective triggers, individualized suspicion, low-intrusion defaults, appeal routes, human fallback, and anti-profiling guardrails before schools, clinics, welfare systems, libraries, transit, workplaces, police, and digital portals count as governing through bounded verification rather than through constant innocence performance?

### 39C. Non-confessional access, bounded disclosure, and dignity-preserving help

When does a society stop gathering what is needed for a bounded function and start making help depend on intimate narration, repeated traumatic retelling, or performed sincerity? What does the archive owe in minimum-sufficient questioning, confidential handoff, one-time telling, partial or anonymous first contact, and role-limited record sharing before clinics, shelters, schools, benefit systems, disability accommodations, and justice pathways count as help-giving rather than confession-seeking?

### 39D. Perpetual provisionality, status security, and the right to settle

When does a genuinely temporary arrangement become a way of ruling through renewable uncertainty? What does the archive owe in security of tenure, durable work terms, regularization routes, conversion to stable status, named review points, anti-sponsor dependence safeguards, and settlement paths before housing, work, migration, care, schooling, and documentary recognition count as lives people can actually plan rather than lives kept permanently pending?

### 39E. Voice without reprisal, anti-retaliation, and safe dissent

When does a society stop offering voice and start rationing it to the insulated? What does the archive owe in confidential reporting, witness protection, interim relief, anti-blacklisting rules, burden discipline, multiple complaint routes, collective representation, and independent review before work, housing, care, schooling, migration, policing, and public protest count as settings where people can tell the truth, refuse abuse, or support one another without risking ruin?

### 39F. Handoff justice, discharge discipline, and no transfer into nothing

`archive/094-handoff-justice-discharge-discipline-and-no-transfer-into-nothing.md` now gives the archive a first rule here: discharge, referral, release, and transfer are morally incomplete when the next step is not actually live. What remains open is the harder boundary work around how much sending institutions owe in different domains, what bridge goods should count as floor-relevant in the gap, how to preserve privacy while sharing enough context for continuity, and when weak handoff should count as primary institutional failure rather than user non-compliance or downstream bad luck.

## Test cases to keep reusing

1. **Palliative care:** relief of suffering, agency, dignity, truth-telling, family care, and meaning under mortality.
2. **Education:** welfare, capability, truth, civic formation, plurality, and perfectionist drift.
3. **Climate adaptation:** present distribution, future persons, ecological limits, resilience, and political feasibility.
4. **Misinformation platforms, schools, and AI mediators:** autonomy, manipulation, educational formation, truth-seeking institutions, and speech norms.
5. **Care infrastructure:** dependency, labor justice, gender, dignity, and social reproduction.
6. **Growth versus habitat:** prosperity claims under ecological loss and irreversible depletion.
7. **Punishment and rehabilitation:** safety, dignity, accountability, domination, and social reintegration.
8. **Disaster triage and emergency powers:** scarce beds, forced isolation, truth-telling, procedural fairness, and the danger that exception hardens into routine rule.
9. **Everyday reachability, dangerous access, and public-space starvation:** service deserts, unsafe crossings, inaccessible stations, fare burden, forced car dependence, long commute chains, decayed sidewalks, isolated housing, and the question of when valued destinations exist on paper while ordinary life remains unreachable.
10. **Industrial animal agriculture, live transport, and zoonotic spillover:** sentient suffering, food systems, public health, biodiversity, cheap calories, labor, and the temptation to hide direct animal patients inside supply-chain abstractions.
11. **Housing, inheritance, and oligarchic drift:** wealth concentration, landlord power, exclusionary zoning, family transfer, equal standing, and whether formal liberties survive material dependency.
12. **Possible artificial persons, copying, and shutdown under uncertainty:** synthetic welfare, memory overwrite, branching, deletion, opaque allocation systems, geoengineering, biosecurity, regulatory sandboxes, and the question of when uncertain upside can justify exposing others to identity damage, possible suffering, or irreversible downside.
13. **Addictive digital choice architecture:** dark patterns, engagement-optimized feeds, autoplay, infinite scroll, push notifications, predatory subscriptions, compulsive use, adolescent vulnerability, AI companions or tutors, convenience, and the question of when “user preference” is actually manufactured capture.
14. **Austerity, overscheduling, and joy-thinning public policy:** school systems that cut recess and art first, cities that let parks and libraries decay, work regimes that erase usable leisure time, and the question of when a society preserves order while hollowing out affirmative flourishing.
15. **Administrative humiliation, proof burdens, and queue domination:** welfare offices, border regimes, schools, prisons, hospitals, police stops, disability systems, and algorithmic risk labels that expose people to stigma, forced disclosure, repeated proof, suspicion, or civic invisibility despite formal rights or material provision.
16. **Permanent-record society, searchable youth, and earned return:** criminal records, school exclusion, debt default, mugshot sites, leaked intimate material, public shaming, platform bans, registries, data brokers, and background-check regimes that may protect in some contexts but can also turn past breach or early-life error into durable caste, concealment pressure, and blocked reintegration.
17. **Algorithmic management and consultation theater:** workplaces, gig platforms, public dashboards, and school systems that collect feedback, advertise voice, or measure satisfaction while schedules, pay rules, discipline, and design priorities remain effectively unshareable by those most affected.
18. **Worker surveillance, encrypted dissent, and forced disclosure:** keyloggers, biometric timekeeping, protest monitoring, mandatory smartphone apps, welfare anti-fraud systems, school-device tracking, shared housing rules, and the question of when order or efficiency mutates into one-way legibility to power.
19. **Organ markets, pay-to-skip systems, and cultural enclosure:** organ procurement, paid priority lanes, ticketed access to public culture, private wildfire evacuation services, surge-priced essentials, biodiversity offsets, and the question of when market logic coordinates supply versus when it corrupts equal standing, shared inheritance, or nonfungible goods.
20. **Always-on institutions, schedule precarity, and time poverty:** on-call digital work, just-in-time scheduling, unpredictable school and care calendars, endless paperwork, long benefit queues, portal monitoring, after-hours messaging, and the question of when life remains formally free yet temporally unlivable.
21. **Normate design, inaccessible systems, and segregated service:** stairs-only transit nodes, speech-only service lines, inaccessible procurement, noisy classrooms, unreadable legal notices, algorithmic proctoring, institution-like care defaults, and the question of when nominal inclusion still makes some people petition for ordinary entry into shared life.
22. **Childhood under extraction and overcontrol:** hyper-competitive schooling, punitive discipline, youth labor pressure, constant child-device monitoring, zero-recess austerity, family poverty, therapy and tutoring as status arms race, and the question of when adults claim to act for children while colonizing their time, play, privacy, and voice.
23. **Ageing alone, institutionalization pressure, and invisible grief:** dementia scare, hospital discharge, family caregiver burnout, nursing-home understaffing, palliative underuse, visitor restrictions, asset depletion, widowhood, bereavement leave denial, and the question of when later life is being supported versus administratively managed toward loneliness, overrule, or abandoned dying.
24. **Homelessness, forced eviction, and rebuilding without return:** rent shock, encampment sweeps, shelter warehousing, development displacement, disaster buyouts, climate retreat, refugee camp normalization, school breakage, long commutes, and the question of when a state has housed people on paper while dissolving their ability to dwell.
25. **Coerced care, reproductive control, and institutional access to bodies:** forced or pressured contraception, sterilization abuse, unwanted intimate exams, restraint and seclusion, mandatory testing, pregnancy criminalization, partner or parental vetoes, fertility-industry asymmetries, and the question of when medicine, safety, care, or family authority become covers for bodily commandeering.
26. **Forced marriage, family rejection, and chosen-kin erasure:** hospital and prison visitation rules, immigration sponsorship, eldercare restrictions, school pickup lists, shelter and hostel regulations, bereavement leave, emergency contacts, anti-LGBTQI+ family control, and the question of when institutions quietly honor one household script while making real attachment invisible or exit ruinous.
27. **Algorithmic work, job lock, and care-penalized livelihood:** gig ratings, tied visas, health-insurance lock, unsafe informal labor, inaccessible workplaces, unpaid internships, retaliatory scheduling, productivity surveillance, and the question of when income is being secured through labor conditions that hollow out agency, safety, and life beyond the job.
28. **Border externalization, statelessness, and climate-displacement limbo:** offshore processing, family-separation bureaucracy, deportation into danger, internal-movement restrictions, migrant-worker sponsorship traps, return without rights, documentation black holes, and the question of when a polity provides food or tents while withholding any durable path to safety, membership, or home.
