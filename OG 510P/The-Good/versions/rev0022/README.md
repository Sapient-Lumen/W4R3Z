# The-Good — rev0022

## Reading orientation

**A place to live is not secure if someone can arbitrarily expel you from it.** This edition brings movement and membership into The Good's account of dwelling, attachment and agency. Its [movement chapter](files/The-Good-rev0022-2026.03.22.04.02-movementmembership-nonexile-returnpaths-wayfarer/archive/033-movement-migration-membership-and-non-exile.md) asks the reader to stop assuming a securely documented insider. For someone facing statelessness, separation or indefinite provisional status, the problem is not merely how many benefits life contains after admission: it is whether that life can acquire a stable place at all.

The chapter holds rootedness and mobility together. A person may need to escape danger, join those they care for or return to a place that should remain theirs. The same person may need continuity, language, neighborhood and freedom from compulsory uprooting. Neither confinement to a birthplace nor perpetual movement is its ideal. The proposed protection concerns a just possibility to stay, leave, arrive, return and belong.

Status is therefore more than a document that sits beside other goods. In the chapter's examples, insecure status can make employment, care and participation difficult to contest. A formally available complaint may be unusable when speaking risks expulsion. That connection explains why the work reads membership across several protected goods instead of treating border throughput or admission totals as a sufficient measure of success.

Its proposed response combines safety with intelligible, reviewable routes: reasons for decisions, opportunities to respond, family unity and protection against indefinite limbo. Protection cannot always wait for the perfect documents that safety would first make possible. These are moral commitments, not one exhaustive answer about open or closed borders; the chapter explicitly keeps receiving communities' burdens and genuine security questions in view.
