# 007 — Future good: sustainability, option value, and no prosperity by liquidation

## One-line thesis

A theory of the good is defective if it allows the present to count as flourishing while it **liquidates the ecological, social, and institutional conditions** that future flourishing depends on.

## Why this matters

Many practical orders discount the future so aggressively that extraction looks like success. But a life-supporting climate, functional ecosystems, durable institutions, social trust, and intergenerational capability are not background scenery. They are part of the good itself, because the good is temporally extended.

## Working components of future good

### 1. Sustainability of enabling systems

The economic, natural, and social systems required for future well-being should be treated as morally load-bearing. Present welfare purchased by destroying those systems is morally self-undermining.

### 2. Option value

Future persons and future communities benefit when we preserve room for action, adaptation, and revision. Not every valuable good is a realized outcome; some are protected possibilities.

### 3. Resilience and repair capacity

A good society is not one that never suffers shocks, but one that can absorb, learn, and rebuild without sacrificing the vulnerable first.

### 4. Intergenerational reciprocity

The archive should treat future persons as more than a discount residue. We inherit institutions, infrastructure, culture, and ecology from the dead; reciprocity runs forward as well as backward.

## Structural claims

### 1. The future is not just another stakeholder bucket

Future concerns are partly special because many future persons cannot represent themselves, bargain, or vote. That weakens the legitimacy of treating their interests as if they were just one present constituency among others.

### 2. Ecological integrity is not merely instrumental in the thin sense

Climate stability, biodiversity, clean air, water, and habitable environments are preconditions for vast ranges of other goods. Their status in a theory of the good is therefore unusually weighty.

### 3. Time can change what looks efficient

A policy that appears optimal on a short horizon may be catastrophic once path dependence, irreversibility, and cumulative risk are recognized.

## Failure modes

- **prosperity by liquidation**: present gains are booked while foundations are depleted.
- **discounted dependency**: future persons count only rhetorically.
- **resilience for the already secure**: systems are hardened by exporting risk downward.
- **option destruction**: irreversible commitments foreclose better futures for convenience.

## Guardrails

- Do not let near-term utility swamp irreversibility.
- Do not treat climate and ecological constraints as externalities to moral theory.
- Do not confuse adaptation for the wealthy with shared resilience.
- Do not speak of future well-being while exhausting the systems it requires.

## Compression rule for the archive

No theory of the good is credible if it counts the present as successful while burning the ladder the future must stand on.
