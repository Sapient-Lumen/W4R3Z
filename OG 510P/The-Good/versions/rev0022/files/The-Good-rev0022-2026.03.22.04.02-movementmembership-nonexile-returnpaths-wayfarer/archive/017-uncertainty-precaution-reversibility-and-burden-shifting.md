# 017 — Uncertainty, precaution, reversibility, and burden-shifting

## One-line thesis

A salient theory of **The Good** needs an **uncertainty rule** in addition to a list of goods, a collision protocol, a scope rule, and a distribution rule: when stakes are serious and uncertainty is deep, prefer actions that are **reversible, option-preserving, information-improving, and floor-protecting**, and place a heavier burden of justification on proposals that could impose **grave or irreversible harm** on direct moral patients or life-supporting systems.

## Why this matters

The archive already says what goods matter, who counts, how collisions should be handled, and how protected goods should be distributed. But many live cases arrive wrapped in uncertainty.

Sometimes the uncertainty is empirical: we do not know how likely a harm is.
Sometimes it is scope-related: we do not know whether a being is a moral patient, or how thick its claims are.
Sometimes it is value-theoretic: we do not know which kernel should dominate, or whether a controversial perfectionist ideal should count at all.
Sometimes it is systemic: we do not know how interventions will cascade once institutions, markets, or ecosystems react.

Without a rule for uncertainty, the archive risks two equal and opposite failures.

First, actors can hide behind uncertainty to postpone protection until damage is already done.
Second, actors can use uncertainty language as a theatrical excuse for permanent paralysis, paternal closure, or status-quo preference.

The archive needs a middle path: **uncertainty discipline without either recklessness or freeze**.

## Four kinds of uncertainty the archive should distinguish

### 1. Empirical uncertainty

How likely is the harm, benefit, or causal pathway?

### 2. Moral-patient uncertainty

Who is directly at stake, and with what level of standing, vulnerability, or welfare significance?

### 3. Value-ranking uncertainty

How should competing kernels or protected claims be weighed in this case?

### 4. Institutional and pathway uncertainty

How likely is a proposal to lock in domination, ecological degradation, or brittle dependence once adopted at scale?

These often combine. The archive should name which kind is doing the work instead of using “uncertainty” as one blur word.

## Working uncertainty rule for the archive

### 1. Do not let uncertainty excuse serious or irreversible harm

When there is credible reason to suspect a grave or irreversible breach of protected floors, lack of complete certainty is not by itself a license to wait for disaster-grade proof.

### 2. Prefer reversible over irreversible pathways when moral stakes are comparable

If two options could serve similar aims, the archive should usually prefer the one that can be revised, stopped, sunsetted, or unwound with less remainder if the model is wrong.

### 3. Preserve option value and learning capacity

Good policy under uncertainty should not only chase immediate gains. It should preserve the ability to gather evidence, compare pathways, maintain institutional memory, and change course.

### 4. Use shielding, modularity, and staged deployment before full commitment

Where possible, prefer trials, bounded rollouts, circuit breakers, independent oversight, and fail-soft design over one-shot system-wide commitments.

### 5. Shift the burden of justification toward the imposer of potentially irreversible harm

When an actor proposes a course that could lock in cruelty, domination, mass exposure, ecosystem loss, or the destruction of future option space, the justificatory burden should not fall mainly on the vulnerable to prove the harm beyond doubt. The stronger burden should fall on the actor asking others to bear the irreversible risk.

### 6. Apply precaution symmetrically to action and inaction

The archive should not confuse precaution with blanket conservatism. Sometimes the irreversible danger lies in failing to act, delaying repair, or entrenching current harms. The question is not “act or don’t act?” in the abstract, but **which side of the choice is more exposed to serious irreversible loss, and which path preserves the most just revisability?**

### 7. Give extra protection where uncertainty is borne by those already burdened

Uncertainty magnifies injustice when the downside is dumped onto those already below the floor, politically weak, dependent, colonized, disabled, displaced, or otherwise exposed to decisions they do not meaningfully control.

## What this note is not saying

### 1. Not paralysis

The archive is not saying that uncertainty blocks action until consensus appears.

### 2. Not status-quo bias

Current institutions can be irreversible harms in motion. “Wait and see” is often a substantive choice, not neutrality.

### 3. Not anti-innovation by default

Experimentation can be justified when it is bounded, monitored, reversible, and not offloaded onto the powerless.

### 4. Not a hidden scalar expected-value theory

This note does not turn the archive into a single probabilistic calculus. It gives practical discipline where probabilities are thin, values are plural, and outcomes may be incommensurable or tragic.

## Pressure cases

### 1. Possible artificial persons or moral patients

When system capabilities or signs of agency rise but metaphysical confidence lags, what protections should arrive before certainty?

### 2. Novel high-impact technologies

How should polities govern frontier systems whose benefits may be large but whose failure modes are not yet well mapped?

### 3. Geoengineering and planetary-scale interventions

When an intervention promises climate relief but may create transboundary, hard-to-reverse harms, what counts as responsible burden of proof?

### 4. Zoonotic spillover and disease control

When urgency is real but evidence is incomplete, how should authorities weigh precaution, culling, liberty restriction, anti-cruelty, and public trust?

### 5. Automated public decision systems

What level of opacity, bias risk, or lock-in is acceptable when allocation systems mediate welfare, policing, housing, or border decisions?

## Implications for the existing archive

- `archive/010-priority-kernels-a-pluralist-working-synthesis.md` should treat uncertainty discipline as a standing action rule whenever kernel breaches are plausible but evidence is incomplete.
- `archive/013-kernel-collisions-triage-tragic-remainder-and-repair.md` should be read alongside a bias toward reversibility, shielding, and repairable pathways where feasible.
- `archive/015-patient-scope-sentience-and-living-systems.md` should use precaution when patient status is credible but not settled.
- `archive/016-distribution-sufficiency-priority-and-anti-oligarchy.md` should ask who bears downside uncertainty and whether the already burdened are being used as testing ground.
- `archive/008-measurement-dashboard-judgment-thresholds-and-policy-translation.md` should require visible reporting on irreversibility, sunset conditions, evidence quality, and who bears uncertain downside.
- `archive/012-open-questions-and-test-cases.md` should keep high-uncertainty, high-stakes cases visible rather than letting them live only in speculative footnotes.

## Guardrails

- Do not invoke precaution so vaguely that any disliked innovation can be blocked by rhetoric alone.
- Do not invoke uncertainty so aggressively that every protection must wait for impossible proof.
- Do not let reversible sandboxing become a euphemism for dumping experimental risk onto the powerless.
- Do not forget that epistemic opacity can itself be a form of domination.
- Do not let “burden of proof” language erase public reasons, oversight, or democratic contestability.

## Compression rule for the archive

Under deep uncertainty, protect floors early, prefer reversible pathways, preserve learning and option value, and make those who would impose irreversible risk carry the heavier justificatory burden.
