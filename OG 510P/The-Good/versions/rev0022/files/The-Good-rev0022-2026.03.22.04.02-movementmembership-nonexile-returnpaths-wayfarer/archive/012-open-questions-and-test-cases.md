# 012 — Open questions and test cases

## One-line thesis

The archive should evaluate theories of the good not only by elegance, but by how they perform on **hard cases, tragic structures, and frontier entities**.

## Why this matters

A theory can look strong in abstract prose and fail instantly when confronted with caregiving asymmetry, ecological sacrifice, epistemic corruption, cultural plurality, or beings whose standing is unsettled. The archive should therefore keep a visible backlog of questions and test cases that exert pressure on every synthesis.

## Open questions

### 1. Animals and other sentient beings

`archive/015-patient-scope-sentience-and-living-systems.md` now gives the archive a first scope rule: sentience creates a strong presumptive anti-cruelty floor, while personhood thickens some claims without monopolizing standing. What remains open is boundary work: which beings are plausible patients, where uncertainty should trigger precaution, and how species-specific forms of flourishing should be described without anthropomorphic flattening.

### 2. Artificial persons or moral patients

If future systems possess agency, vulnerability, preferences, or some capacity for meaning, how should kernel protections extend or change?

### 3. Meaning versus equality

How should institutions respond when some meaning-bearing projects are positional, elite, or exclusionary?

### 4. Care versus autonomy

When does support enlarge freedom, and when does it become domination in a protective costume?

### 5. Public reason versus controversial excellence

What can a polity justifiably promote in common life when citizens disagree deeply about the best form of human flourishing?

### 6. Present sacrifice for future continuity

How much present deprivation can be justified for long-run ecological or institutional preservation?

### 7. Truth versus psychic stability

When institutions disclose disturbing truths, how should they balance reality-contact against panic, grief, or social destabilization?

### 8. Tragic remainder and repair

When protected goods genuinely collide, which losses should be treated as leaving reparative remainder rather than as cleanly outweighed costs?

### 9. Kernel admission drift

Which candidate goods deserve kernel status, which are better treated as common-good scaffolding, and which should remain indicators or domain targets rather than archive-level constitutional commitments?

### 10. Inequality, inheritance, and civic capture

How much material inequality is compatible with equal standing, and when do inheritance, concentrated ownership, or platform power become forms of private government rather than mere uneven success?

### 11. Uncertainty, irreversibility, and burden of proof

When evidence is incomplete but stakes are high, what should trigger precaution, when should reversibility outrank speed, and who should have to justify exposing others to uncertain but potentially grave loss?

### 12. Preference formation, manipulation, and adaptive resignation

When should a reported choice count as meaningful endorsement, when is it better read as resignation under blocked options, and how should institutions respond to environments intentionally designed to steer attention, consent, or appetite?

### 13. Public reason, culture, and affirmative flourishing

How far may institutions go in protecting leisure, play, beauty, public culture, and shared celebration without sliding into class-coded perfectionism, nationalist pageantry, or soft coercion about the supposedly superior life?

### 14. Voice, participation, and shared rule

When is consultation enough, when is co-determination required, and how much say should people have over workplaces, platforms, schools, housing, and automated systems that structure everyday life?

### 15. Privacy, sanctuary, and selective legibility

How much exposure may institutions justifiably demand as the price of service, safety, work, credit, schooling, or participation, and when do surveillance, searchable permanence, or repeated disclosure become direct degradations rather than merely regrettable administrative costs?

### 16. Rest, rhythm, and time sovereignty

When does intensity count as chosen striving, and when is it better read as coerced availability, schedule precarity, care squeeze, or administrative time theft? How much off-time, predictability, and freedom from interruption does a good order owe before rights, culture, and participation become nominal rather than usable?

### 17. Accessibility, disability, and world-fit

How much of what gets called disability disadvantage is produced by environments, institutions, communication defaults, and normate design rather than by impairment alone? When should the archive prioritize universal design, individualized accommodation, community support, or redesign of expectations about speed, productivity, or “normal” performance?

### 18. Markets, pricing, and non-fungible goods

Which goods may be allocated by market exchange, which require strict bounds or public options, and which are distorted when treated as purchasable at all? How should the archive tell ordinary exchange from exploitation, pay-to-skip citizenship, body brokerage, or the sale of standing itself?

### 19. Childhood, developmental asymmetry, and just guardianship

How should the archive combine protection, play, education, privacy, family authority, and evolving-capacity voice without treating children either as parental property or as miniature autonomous adults? When do school discipline, digital monitoring, competitive scheduling, or “best interests” language protect development, and when do they become adult convenience or extraction in a protective costume?

### 20. Later life, frailty, mortality, and bereavement

How should the archive combine autonomy, supported decision-making, pain relief, family burden, social connection, home life, institutional care, and truthful end-of-life choice without sliding either into ageist abandonment or into compulsory prolongation at all costs? When do safety, capacity, and efficiency narratives protect older persons, and when do they become cover for warehousing, overrule, or social death before biological death?

### 21. Home, dwelling, and non-displacement

What does a good order owe people as dwellers rather than merely as bodies to be covered or consumers to be priced? When do rent burden, serial relocation, emergency shelter, redevelopment, disaster adaptation, camp life, or anti-homeless enforcement become direct degradations of privacy, dignity, time, care, schooling, and belonging rather than unfortunate side effects of ordinary housing administration?

### 22. Bodily integrity, consent, and reproductive agency

When does consent remain morally valid under conditions of dependence, pain, urgency, institutional custody, family pressure, poverty, or administrative threat? Which bodily interventions can ever be justified without contemporaneous consent, and under what burden of proof, reviewability, and least-intrusive constraints? When do reproductive laws, care systems, fertility markets, school rules, detention regimes, testing protocols, or public-health policies protect persons, and when do they quietly turn bodies into infrastructure for other people’s goals?

### 23. Intimacy, chosen kin, and exit from coercive ties

Which ties should institutions recognize as serious for visitation, caregiving, mourning, migration, emergency decision support, household support, or bereavement standing? When does family protection preserve attachment, and when does it become cover for forced marriage, partner control, queer or disabled exclusion, or punitive family dependence? How should a good order protect both meaningful connection and materially possible exit without social-engineering one official household form?

### 24. Livelihood, contribution, and the refusal of servility

What does a good order owe people in relation to work: merely access to some job, or access to safe, fairly rewarded, voice-bearing, non-humiliating work plus the material floor to refuse degrading labor? When does meaningful work deepen agency and self-respect, and when does its rhetoric become a way to justify overwork, unpaid sacrifice, job lock, or ranking the unwaged as lesser?

### 25. Movement, migration, membership, and non-exile

When do border control, citizenship rules, detention, deportation, family-reunification barriers, camp systems, or documentation burdens become forms of domination and status injury rather than merely unfortunate administration, and what does a good order owe to those who must move, those who wish to stay, and those trapped in between?

## Test cases to keep reusing

1. **Palliative care:** relief of suffering, agency, dignity, truth-telling, family care, and meaning under mortality.
2. **Education:** welfare, capability, truth, civic formation, plurality, and perfectionist drift.
3. **Climate adaptation:** present distribution, future persons, ecological limits, resilience, and political feasibility.
4. **Misinformation platforms:** autonomy, manipulation, truth-seeking institutions, and speech norms.
5. **Care infrastructure:** dependency, labor justice, gender, dignity, and social reproduction.
6. **Growth versus habitat:** prosperity claims under ecological loss and irreversible depletion.
7. **Punishment and rehabilitation:** safety, dignity, accountability, domination, and social reintegration.
8. **Disaster triage and emergency powers:** scarce beds, forced isolation, truth-telling, procedural fairness, and the danger that exception hardens into routine rule.
9. **Built environment and common facilities:** clean air, safe transit, libraries, broadband, public space, and the problem of deciding whether these are kernels, scaffolding, or indicators of deeper goods.
10. **Industrial animal agriculture and zoonotic spillover:** sentient suffering, food systems, public health, biodiversity, cheap calories, labor, and the temptation to hide moral patients inside supply-chain abstractions.
11. **Housing, inheritance, and oligarchic drift:** wealth concentration, landlord power, exclusionary zoning, family transfer, equal standing, and whether formal liberties survive material dependency.
12. **Frontier systems and hard-to-reverse interventions:** possible artificial persons, opaque allocation systems, geoengineering, biosecurity, regulatory sandboxes, and the question of when uncertain upside can justify exposing others to irreversible downside.
13. **Addictive digital choice architecture:** dark patterns, engagement-optimized feeds, predatory subscriptions, compulsive use, adolescent vulnerability, convenience, and the question of when “user preference” is actually manufactured capture.
14. **Austerity, overscheduling, and joy-thinning public policy:** school systems that cut recess and art first, cities that let parks and libraries decay, work regimes that erase usable leisure time, and the question of when a society preserves order while hollowing out affirmative flourishing.
15. **Administrative humiliation and caste-like sorting:** welfare offices, border regimes, schools, prisons, hospitals, police stops, and algorithmic risk labels that expose people to stigma, forced disclosure, suspicion, or civic invisibility despite formal rights or material provision.
16. **Permanent-record society and earned return:** criminal records, school exclusion, debt default, public shaming, platform bans, registries, and background-check regimes that may protect in some contexts but can also turn past breach into durable caste, concealment pressure, and blocked reintegration.
17. **Algorithmic management and consultation theater:** workplaces, gig platforms, public dashboards, and school systems that collect feedback, advertise voice, or measure satisfaction while schedules, pay rules, discipline, and design priorities remain effectively unshareable by those most affected.
18. **Worker surveillance, encrypted dissent, and forced disclosure:** keyloggers, biometric timekeeping, protest monitoring, mandatory smartphone apps, welfare anti-fraud systems, school-device tracking, shared housing rules, and the question of when order or efficiency mutates into one-way legibility to power.
19. **Organ markets, pay-to-skip systems, and cultural enclosure:** organ procurement, paid priority lanes, ticketed access to public culture, private wildfire evacuation services, surge-priced essentials, biodiversity offsets, and the question of when market logic coordinates supply versus when it corrupts equal standing, shared inheritance, or nonfungible goods.
20. **Always-on institutions, schedule precarity, and time poverty:** on-call digital work, just-in-time scheduling, unpredictable school and care calendars, endless paperwork, long benefit queues, after-hours messaging, and the question of when life remains formally free yet temporally unlivable.
21. **Normate design, inaccessible systems, and segregated service:** stairs-only transit nodes, speech-only service lines, inaccessible procurement, noisy classrooms, unreadable legal notices, algorithmic proctoring, institution-like care defaults, and the question of when nominal inclusion still makes some people petition for ordinary entry into shared life.
22. **Childhood under extraction and overcontrol:** hyper-competitive schooling, punitive discipline, youth labor pressure, constant child-device monitoring, zero-recess austerity, family poverty, therapy and tutoring as status arms race, and the question of when adults claim to act for children while colonizing their time, play, privacy, and voice.
23. **Ageing alone, institutionalization pressure, and invisible grief:** dementia scare, hospital discharge, family caregiver burnout, nursing-home understaffing, palliative underuse, visitor restrictions, asset depletion, widowhood, bereavement leave denial, and the question of when later life is being supported versus administratively managed toward loneliness, overrule, or abandoned dying.
24. **Homelessness, forced eviction, and rebuilding without return:** rent shock, encampment sweeps, shelter warehousing, development displacement, disaster buyouts, climate retreat, refugee camp normalization, school breakage, long commutes, and the question of when a state has housed people on paper while dissolving their ability to dwell.
25. **Coerced care, reproductive control, and institutional access to bodies:** forced or pressured contraception, sterilization abuse, unwanted intimate exams, restraint and seclusion, mandatory testing, pregnancy criminalization, partner or parental vetoes, fertility-industry asymmetries, and the question of when medicine, safety, care, or family authority become covers for bodily commandeering.
26. **Forced marriage, family rejection, and chosen-kin erasure:** hospital and prison visitation rules, immigration sponsorship, eldercare restrictions, school pickup lists, shelter and hostel regulations, bereavement leave, emergency contacts, anti-LGBTQI+ family control, and the question of when institutions quietly honor one household script while making real attachment invisible or exit ruinous.
27. **Algorithmic work, job lock, and care-penalized livelihood:** gig ratings, tied visas, health-insurance lock, unsafe informal labor, inaccessible workplaces, unpaid internships, retaliatory scheduling, productivity surveillance, and the question of when income is being secured through labor conditions that hollow out agency, safety, and life beyond the job.
28. **Border externalization, statelessness, and climate-displacement limbo:** offshore processing, family-separation bureaucracy, deportation into danger, internal-movement restrictions, migrant-worker sponsorship traps, return without rights, documentation black holes, and the question of when a polity provides food or tents while withholding any durable path to safety, membership, or home.

## Compression rule for the archive

A theory of the good earns authority by surviving difficult cases, not by remaining unexposed to them.
