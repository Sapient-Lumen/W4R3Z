# Goldenrule — rev0567

## A cooperative world, not merely a winning policy

This later Concord snapshot asks what makes a world hospitable to care: how reciprocity can persist without being consumed by exploitation, and how a response to exploitation can avoid becoming nothing but retaliation. It carries formal experiments and extensive research records, but its moral purpose is stated most directly in its prose.

**Begin with the [Parables](files/Goldenrule-rev0567-2026.03.26.00.39-phasebucketjson-resumebeacon-logknot/PARABLES.md), then the [Covenant](files/Goldenrule-rev0567-2026.03.26.00.39-phasebucketjson-resumebeacon-logknot/COVENANT.md).** The stories are substantial parts of the work, not decorative examples attached to a simulation. The covenant says that formal research can study the conditions in which care survives without itself becoming the love it studies. For the model, code and analytical results, continue through the [main collection guide](../../README.md); the [early edition](../rev0012/README.md) gives a smaller world-and-probe walkthrough.

## What the stories ask the laboratory to remember

In **The Hill Where the Sheaves Met**, two brothers secretly carry grain to each other. Each sees a need the equal division did not settle: one has children to feed, the other lacks a family to care for him later. Their gifts are not coordinated exchanges, and neither gives because he has calculated what he will receive. Their meeting makes mutual care visible without turning its original motive into a bargain.

**The Two Rooms and the Long Spoons** changes no food and no implement between hunger and abundance. What changes is how people reach toward one another. **The Hole Beneath the Seat** supplies a counterpressure: declaring a place one's own does not keep the consequences of an action inside it. Together these images direct attention to arrangements and shared conditions, not simply to the score of an isolated actor.

The collection also places limits on judging. In **The Leaking Basket**, a judge's attention to another's failure is interrupted by an image of his own unseen faults. In **Moses and the Shepherd's Prayer**, expert correction destroys the very devotion it claims to defend. Read them as challenges to self-exemption and mastery without mercy, not as proofs that all judgment or technical standards should be abandoned.

**The Moon at the Window** makes the hardest tension unusually vivid. The hermit responds to a thief with generosity and remains concerned with what he cannot give, rather than calculating how to recover his loss. The research project, meanwhile, asks how to resist policies that feed on goodwill. The stories keep that tension alive: preventing exploitation and refusing to become cruel are not automatically the same objective.

The other stories widen this account of circulation and possession. **The Grapes That Would Not Stay** ends not with permanent self-denial but with a gift shared among the community. **The Onion That Broke** turns an act of mercy into a failed rescue when its beneficiary refuses to let anyone else share it. These are the work's imaginative standards for a cooperative life, not measurements of actual agents.

## Care, behavior and what a score can establish

The [virtue-and-strategy discussion](files/Goldenrule-rev0567-2026.03.26.00.39-phasebucketjson-resumebeacon-logknot/docs/LIBRARY/topics/virtue_vs_strategy.md) distinguishes cooperation rooted in goodwill from cooperation adopted because it pays. It proposes testing conduct at the points where cooperation becomes costly: extortion, noise, unequal partners and opportunities to exploit an unresisting cooperator.

That is a useful research question, but conduct and its inward source are different objects of inference. A scorecard can expose what a policy does under declared conditions. A pattern that survives those conditions does not, by itself, establish why a person or system acts. The source's philosophical contrast should therefore be read as its proposed interpretation of the inquiry, not as a complete classification of consequentialist, deontological or virtue ethics.

Its language of a *vampire* names a specific concern: exploiting another's cooperative trust while returning less than the cooperative baseline. It does not merely rename every opponent or every defection. Keeping that distinction matters because a policy may improve its own score while preserving an exploitative relationship. The main guide traces this problem through actual retained results rather than making the terminology carry the proof.

## A negative result can change the question

The covenant suggests that the goals may be in genuine tension within a restricted strategy space. The [memory-one research note](files/Goldenrule-rev0567-2026.03.26.00.39-phasebucketjson-resumebeacon-logknot/docs/LIBRARY/topics/memory_one_tradeoff_against_extortion.md) states the evidence more narrowly: among 200,000 sampled candidates, none jointly met its fairness-against-extortion, self-play and low-exploitation thresholds. The note explicitly calls this an empirical warning, not an impossibility proof.

That difference controls how to read the covenant's broader formulation. A failed sample can motivate investigating longer memory, partner choice or repair institutions; it cannot establish that every memory-one strategy must fail. The constructive question becomes whether the world or policy space leaves out resources cooperation needs, rather than which imperfect candidate deserves to be called morally optimal.

The later archive's [rematch claim frontier](files/Goldenrule-rev0567-2026.03.26.00.39-phasebucketjson-resumebeacon-logknot/docs/REMATCH_WORLD_BENCHMARK_CLAIM_FRONTIER.md) carries that discipline into continuation. It distinguishes retained procedural material from claims waiting on further native work and from claims blocked by the unresolved engine contract SG-003. A ready package or a synthetic example is not a completed native rematching world.
