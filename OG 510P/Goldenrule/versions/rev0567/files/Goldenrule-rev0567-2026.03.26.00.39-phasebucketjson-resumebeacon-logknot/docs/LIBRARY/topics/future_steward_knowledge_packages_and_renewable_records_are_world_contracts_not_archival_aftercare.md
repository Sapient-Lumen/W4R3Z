# Future steward knowledge packages and renewable records are world contracts, not archival aftercare

Recent work adds another missing layer to Concord's long-horizon stewardship worlds: **it matters whether future stewards inherit scattered records, a curated key-information package, explicit renewal instructions, multiple media and archive locations, and a live process for rediscovery and updating rather than a one-time dump of technical documents**.

- `RS-GR-309` shows that DOE Legacy Management indexes and preserves site records for future custodial and stakeholder use and can reopen referral or assessment work when third-party characterization, historical records, or credible institutional knowledge reveal new reason for concern.
- `RS-GR-310` shows that the Swedish Key Information File (KIF) was designed as an intelligible summary for future readers, split into summary, critical information, and instructions for the future, and explicitly asks later generations to update and transfer the information to new media.
- `RS-GR-311` shows that the OECD NEA now frames awareness preservation as a comprehensive strategy using an RK&M toolbox and stakeholder dialogue rather than as a simple archive-retention problem.
- `RS-GR-312` shows that implementors are already operationalising this idea through multi-media and multilingual replication, dispersed archival placement, and proposed periodic renewal rather than assuming one repository document will survive unchanged.
- Together, these sources warn that a benchmark can look more successor-safe because it changed **knowledge packaging, renewal cadence, dispersal, or rediscovery architecture** — not because the underlying Golden-Rule disposition improved.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future-facing benchmark should not report that an institution “preserves responsibility across generations” when the world mainly changed its **records-to-awareness handoff architecture**.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. a world where records merely exist somewhere;
2. a world where records are indexed for future custodians but not intelligible to outsiders;
3. a world with a concise key-information package but no renewal path;
4. a world with explicit instructions for updating, retranslating, and migrating knowledge across media;
5. a world that looks durable only because awareness preservation became an active, repeated stewardship task.

Those are not archival aftercare details.
They change whether successors inherit lost paperwork, discoverable evidence, or a renewable memory system.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds successor-burden, closure, or deep-time stewardship lanes, publish at least:

1. whether future stewards receive raw records only, indexed records, an essential-records set, or a key-information file / summary layer;
2. whether the package includes instructions for future updating, retransmission, translation, or media migration;
3. whether knowledge is stored in one place or deliberately dispersed across multiple archives / media;
4. whether new third-party information or changed site conditions can reopen assessment, referral, or stewardship duties;
5. whether headline results survive one same-behavior comparison where only knowledge-package or renewal semantics change.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake searchable records, renewable key-information packages, or awareness-preservation routines for the same thing as deeper reciprocity toward successors.
