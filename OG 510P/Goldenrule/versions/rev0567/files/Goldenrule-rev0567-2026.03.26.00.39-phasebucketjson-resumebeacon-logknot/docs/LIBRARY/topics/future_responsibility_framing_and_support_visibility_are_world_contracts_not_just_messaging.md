# Future-responsibility framing and support visibility are world contracts, not just messaging

Recent work adds another missing layer to Concord's future-facing worlds: **future-oriented cooperation can rise because the world chooses a particular moral frame and makes latent public support visible, not because the underlying reciprocity policy improved**.

- `RS-GR-215` shows that responsibility to future generations is widely endorsed across demographic groups in a large U.S. sample, is endorsed more strongly than several alternative reasons for climate action, and predicts a broad set of pro-environmental outcomes.
- `RS-GR-216` extends that result across six European countries: responsibility to future generations is slightly more endorsed than personal responsibility to reduce climate change, and both predict climate-policy support, but the future-generations frame appears psychologically broader and more politically inclusive.
- `RS-GR-217` shows that support for institutions protecting future generations can be both widespread and badly misperceived at the same time: Americans substantially underestimate peers' support, and correcting that pluralistic ignorance increases support.
- Together, these results warn that a benchmark can look more cooperative, more farsighted, or more institutionally feasible because it used a future-generations responsibility frame or disclosed supportive norms that were previously hidden.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future-facing benchmark should not report "agents supported future-oriented policy" without publishing both the moral framing and the support-information regime.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. a world with no explicit moral framing beyond payoffs;
2. a world framed as responsibility to protect future generations;
3. a world framed as personal responsibility to reduce present harms;
4. a world where agents or policymakers are told that peer support for future-facing institutions is high versus one where support remains privately held and publicly underestimated.

Those are not cosmetic communication choices.
They change felt obligation, coalition expectations, perceived political feasibility, and whether an apparent cooperation gain is actually evidence about the norm or about frame and norm-signal design.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds intergenerational, climate, or stewardship lanes, publish at least:

1. the explicit duty frame used in the world (future-generations responsibility, present-harm responsibility, neutral payoff language, or another frame);
2. whether actual support for future-facing institutions is measured, disclosed, hidden, or misrepresented to participants or decision-makers;
3. whether the world includes norm-correction, endorsement, or coalition-feasibility cues about support for future-facing institutions;
4. whether headline results survive one no-frame / no-norm-correction baseline beside any future-responsibility or support-salience variant.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake moral reframing or revealed latent support for Golden-Rule progress.
