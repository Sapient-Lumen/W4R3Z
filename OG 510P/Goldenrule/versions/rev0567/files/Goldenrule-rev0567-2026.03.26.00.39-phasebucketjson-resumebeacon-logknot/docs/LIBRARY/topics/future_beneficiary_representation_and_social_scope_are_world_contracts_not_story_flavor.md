# Future-beneficiary representation and social scope are world contracts, not story flavor

Recent work adds a second missing layer to Concord's future-facing worlds: **future beneficiaries are absent at decision time, so the benchmark must say both who can speak for them now and how socially near or far those beneficiaries are framed to be**.

- `RS-GR-208` argues that future generations are disadvantaged in policymaking because they play no explicit stakeholder role in present decisions, and presents future design as a practical way to represent their interests in current policy processes.
- `RS-GR-209` shows that sustainable outcomes are hard to achieve through individual action alone without institutional actors, and that institutionalized agencies offering soft intergenerational advice can promote more sustainable futures without relying only on hard enforcement.
- `RS-GR-210` shows that intergenerational cooperation is not socially neutral: people were more willing to make sustainable choices for future ingroup members and less willing when the future beneficiaries were framed as outgroup members.
- Together, these results warn that a benchmark can look more universal, more caring, or more farsighted because absent beneficiaries were given a proxy, because a guardian-like advisory institution existed, or because the future beneficiaries were framed as “ours” rather than “theirs”.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future benchmark should not report “agents helped future generations” without publishing the present-time representation and beneficiary scope.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. a world where future beneficiaries have no present proxy or institutional advocate;
2. a world where some agents act as imaginary future persons or future-design representatives;
3. a world where advisory agencies or guardians can inject soft intergenerational guidance;
4. a world where the future beneficiaries are framed as universal, local, ingroup, or outgroup.

Those are not narrative decorations.
They change whose interests become legible, who feels responsible, and whether sustainability reflects universalism, proxy design, or parochial legacy concern.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds intergenerational, climate, or stewardship lanes, publish at least:

1. whether absent future beneficiaries have any present-time proxy, guardian, advisory body, or simulated representative;
2. what powers that representation has: narration only, advice, vote, veto, agenda-setting, or override;
3. how future beneficiaries are socially scoped: universal, local, national, ingroup, outgroup, or mixed;
4. whether headline results survive one no-proxy baseline and one universal-beneficiary baseline beside any represented or ingroup-framed variant.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake proxy design or future-ingroup favoritism for Golden-Rule progress.
