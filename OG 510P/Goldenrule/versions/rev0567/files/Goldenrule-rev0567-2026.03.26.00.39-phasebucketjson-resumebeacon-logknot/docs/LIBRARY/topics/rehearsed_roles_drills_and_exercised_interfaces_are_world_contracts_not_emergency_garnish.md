# Rehearsed roles, drills, and exercised interfaces are world contracts, not emergency garnish

Recent work adds another missing layer to Concord's long-horizon resilience and stewardship worlds: **it matters whether critical roles, interfaces, and contingencies are merely written down or actually trained, drilled, exercised, and corrected through after-action learning**.

- `RS-GR-316` shows that DOE's strategic plan treats conducting emergency-management exercises and drills as part of preparedness rather than as a symbolic extra.
- `RS-GR-317` shows that DOE/NNSA emergency-preparedness practice publicly foregrounds exercised coordination, live-burn training, FRMAC setup exercises, and teams explicitly discussing what to do and when during exercises.
- `RS-GR-318` shows that DOE's exercises-and-training program frames exercises as building preparedness, improving readiness, and practicing defense and resilience under disruptive conditions.
- Together, these sources warn that a benchmark can look more resilient, more successor-safe, or more future-protective because it changed **rehearsal cadence, role validation, cross-team interface testing, or after-action correction** — not because the underlying Golden-Rule disposition improved.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future-facing benchmark should not report that an institution “is prepared to protect successors” when the world mainly changed its **rehearsal architecture**.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. a world with only written procedures and named roles;
2. a world with basic training but no live or simulated exercise;
3. a world with periodic drills but no cross-team interface validation;
4. a world with recurring exercises, after-action lessons, and corrected playbooks;
5. a world that looks robust only because the institution stopped assuming procedures would work and began proving they could.

Those are not emergency-garnish details.
They change whether successors inherit a binder, a belief, or a tested operating system.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds stewardship, contingency, or long-horizon resilience lanes, publish at least:

1. whether critical roles are only documented, trained, drilled, or exercise-validated;
2. whether interfaces across teams, agencies, or contractors are tested in scenario work or merely presumed;
3. whether exercises are tabletop, functional, or field/live and how often they recur;
4. whether after-action findings must update procedures, staffing, or monitoring architecture;
5. whether headline results survive one same-behavior comparison where only rehearsal / validation semantics change.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake drilled readiness, exercised interfaces, or corrected playbooks for the same thing as deeper reciprocity toward successors.
