# Request visibility and help-seeking recognition are world contracts, not UI garnish

Recent work adds a second missing layer to Concord's future helping and mutual-aid lanes: **the audience and status of a help request are not harmless presentation details**.

- `RS-GR-200` studies support-request visibility directly and warns that request visibility involves trade-offs rather than a one-way march toward more participation.
- `RS-GR-201` shows that support-request visibility and recognition for making requests shape the perceived social costs of seeking help; recognition from helpers can reduce those social-cost concerns.
- `RS-GR-202` adds that a peer-recognition system broadly used across subgroups can strengthen employees' perception of a help-seeking norm, while partial or subgroup-specific adoption can instead reinforce a norm of **not** asking.
- Together, these results warn that a benchmark can look more caring, more candid, or more cooperative because **requests were made publicly legible or socially rewarded**, not because the underlying reciprocity policy itself improved.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future benchmark should not collapse all request channels into one generic “communication enabled” toggle.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. private requests seen only by the potential helper;
2. public requests seen by peers, bystanders, or the whole group;
3. requests that earn recognition, validation, or credit for asking;
4. worlds where only some subgroups or roles are socially licensed to ask.

Those choices do not merely change interface flavor.
They change stigma, norm perception, audience pressure, and whether asking itself counts as a cooperative act.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds support-request channels, publish at least:

1. who can see a request (helper only, local peers, whole group, or platform);
2. whether making a request earns recognition, reputation effects, or other social credit;
3. whether request visibility and recognition are symmetric across roles or subgroups;
4. whether headline results survive a private-request baseline as well as a public / recognized-request variant.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake audience design or request recognition for Golden-Rule progress.
