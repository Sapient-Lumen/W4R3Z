# Universalistic cooperation across group boundaries depends on competition and mobility contracts

Recent intergroup reciprocity work adds a compact warning for any Concord lane meant to represent Golden-Rule-like universalism rather than merely strong local cooperation.

- `RS-GR-148` shows that universalistic cooperation across group boundaries is not always reputationally rewarded: under intergroup competition, ingroup-favoring actors can be evaluated more positively than universalists, while in non-competitive conditions universalists may do only about as well.
- `RS-GR-149` shows that even a small number of agents with free mobility across group boundaries can selectively enforce intergroup cooperation, whereas restricting enforcement to ingroup interactions pushes behavior back toward group-exclusive cooperation.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future “Golden Rule” lane should not assume that helping outsiders is judged the same way everywhere.

Once groups exist, universalistic cooperation depends on at least two more institutional choices:

1. whether groups are in material competition,
2. and whether agents can cross boundaries to observe, reward, punish, or rematch with outgroup members.

Those choices can flip the local incentive of universalistic action.
A benchmark may look morally broader or narrower because outgroup enforcement was possible, impossible, or reputationally costly within the ingroup—not because the policy itself was intrinsically more universal.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds a group-boundary or intergroup reciprocity lane, publish at least:

1. whether intergroup competition is absent, mild, or strong,
2. whether agents can observe, reward, punish, or rematch across group boundaries,
3. whether universalistic cooperation toward outgroups is scored positively, neutrally, or negatively inside the ingroup,
4. whether only a minority of mobile agents can trigger cross-group enforcement,
5. one no-cross-boundary-enforcement baseline before generalizing from intergroup cooperation gains.

Without that compact contract, future sessions can mistake mobility-enabled enforcement or competition-sheltered parochialism for stable universal reciprocity.
