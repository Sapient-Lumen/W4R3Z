# Delegated coordination and coalition stages are world contracts, not preplay conveniences

Recent work adds a missing layer to Concord's future reciprocity lanes: **who gets to speak, coordinate, or pre-commit on behalf of others is part of the institution**.

- `RS-GR-192` shows that communication among only some group members can still raise cooperation relative to a no-communication baseline, even for members who did not themselves participate.
- `RS-GR-193` adds that introducing a coalition-formation stage before contribution can sort agents by preferences and raise public-good provision.
- `RS-GR-194` further shows that leader-selection mechanisms change outcomes nontrivially: purely voluntary leadership performs worst, while constrained or higher-barrier selection can make leadership signals more credible.
- Together, these results warn that a benchmark can look more cooperative because coordination rights were delegated or staged, not because the underlying reciprocity norm improved.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future benchmark should not treat delegated coordination as harmless preplay flavor.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. all agents acting simultaneously without any representative stage;
2. some members communicating while others only observe the fact of communication;
3. agents first forming coalitions before choosing contribution or helping actions;
4. a world where leadership exists, but access to leadership is open, filtered, randomly constrained, or otherwise gated.

Those choices do not merely change convenience.
They change who can create common knowledge, who can sort with whom, and whether cooperative gains arise from speech rights, coalition sorting, or leader-signal credibility.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds communication, coalition, or delegated-coordination worlds, publish at least:

1. who may communicate or bargain before action, and whether nonparticipants know that coordination occurred;
2. whether there is an explicit coalition-formation or representative-selection stage before the substantive move;
3. how leaders or representatives are selected, including whether multiple leaders, no leader, or filtered candidates are possible;
4. whether headline gains survive an all-participants-communicate baseline and a no-delegation/no-coalition companion baseline.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake coordination architecture for Golden-Rule progress.
