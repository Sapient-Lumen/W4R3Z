# Value pluralism and option-preserving portfolios are world contracts, not one locked future

Recent work adds another missing layer to Concord's future-facing worlds: **long-term governance must often act under uncertainty not only about outcomes but about future values themselves, so preserving options can be a distinct success mode from optimizing one guessed future**.

- `RS-GR-242` argues that long-term governance is shaped by deep uncertainty, including uncertainty about unknown outcomes, uncertainty about which values should guide action, and uncertainty about control over time because future administrations and generations must continue the project.
- The same source warns that long-horizon institutions are not only choosing under risk; they often govern under value uncertainty and unknown unknowns.
- `RS-GR-243` argues that value pluralism can justify portfolio approaches because multiple legitimate values may need to be served at once rather than collapsed into one objective.
- The same source recommends inclusive decision-making, diverse strategic assets, ladders of solutions, and wide knowledge sharing, which together point toward option-preserving and multi-path governance rather than one locked solution path.
- Together, these results warn that a benchmark can look more future-protective because it optimized one assumed future value system, because it diversified across several partially compatible futures, or because it preserved choice for successors under disagreement and uncertainty.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future-facing benchmark should not report "the policy protected future generations" without publishing whether the world assumes one settled future objective or preserves option value under deep uncertainty.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. a world where the relevant future value function is assumed fixed and singular;
2. a world where uncertainty exists about outcomes but not about values;
3. a world where values are plural or disputed but the benchmark still forces one optimized pathway;
4. a world where several strategic assets or solution ladders are maintained in parallel to preserve future choice;
5. a world where successor cohorts can update, reweight, or prune the portfolio as knowledge and values change.

Those are not modeling flourishes.
They change whether a benchmark measures decisive optimization, brittle lock-in, robust hedging, or option preservation for unknown successors.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds long-horizon, stewardship, transition, or future-beneficiary lanes, publish at least:

1. whether uncertainty is only probabilistic outcome uncertainty or also includes unknown unknowns and uncertainty about relevant values;
2. whether the world assumes one fixed future objective, a weighted mixture, or an explicitly plural set of legitimate successor values;
3. whether solutions are single-path, laddered, or portfolio-based;
4. whether successor cohorts may revise the objective, prune options, or inherit only a locked path;
5. whether headline results survive one single-path lock-in baseline beside any portfolio or option-preserving world.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake brittle optimization of one guessed future for durable protection of many possible futures.
