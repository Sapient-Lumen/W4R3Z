# Future-voice insertion and throughput accountability are world contracts, not representation theater

Recent work adds another missing layer to Concord's future-facing worlds: **future beneficiaries can matter because they are procedurally inserted into present decisions through role-play, advisory institutions, or accountability machinery, not only because the underlying cooperation rule changed**.

- `RS-GR-234` shows that future design can give future generations a voice inside current policymaking and can work in real public-policy pilots, though the method needs adaptation across contexts.
- `RS-GR-235` argues that future-regarding institutions do more than symbolize concern: they can improve input legitimacy by representing future interests, throughput legitimacy by increasing ex-ante accountability, transparency, inclusiveness, and openness, and output legitimacy by reducing harmful short-termism.
- `RS-GR-236` shows that sustainable outcomes were hard to achieve through individual action alone and that institutionalized agencies offering soft intergenerational advice could improve outcomes even without hard enforcement.
- `RS-GR-237` shows that future generations are already recognized in many constitutions and policy documents, which warns that nominal recognition and actual procedural voice should not be collapsed into one category.
- Together, these results warn that a benchmark can look more farsighted because future beneficiaries were inserted into the procedure, because policymakers were subjected to stronger long-horizon scrutiny, or because an advisory institution existed — not because the base Golden-Rule policy improved.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future-facing benchmark should not report "agents protected future generations" without publishing how future interests entered the present decision process.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. a world where future generations are mentioned but have no active procedural role;
2. a world where some participants role-play or simulate future beneficiaries through future design;
3. a world where an advisory or guardian-like institution can inject soft intergenerational guidance;
4. a world where a future-regarding institution changes throughput by adding scrutiny, agenda-setting, transparency, or ex-ante accountability;
5. a world where those powers become binding rather than merely advisory.

Those are not presentation choices.
They change which interests become audible, which actors face long-horizon accountability, and whether apparent sustainability gains reflect action policy or institutional voice architecture.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds intergenerational, stewardship, or climate lanes, publish at least:

1. whether future beneficiaries are absent, role-played, simulated, advisory-represented, scrutinized by a future-regarding body, or formally empowered;
2. what powers that insertion has: narration only, advice, audit, transparency trigger, agenda-setting, vote, veto, or override;
3. whether the institution changes only input/output or also throughput through ex-ante accountability, openness, and procedural scrutiny;
4. whether headline results survive one no-voice baseline and one soft-advice-only baseline beside any stronger future-voice institution.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake procedural voice insertion or accountability machinery for Golden-Rule progress.
