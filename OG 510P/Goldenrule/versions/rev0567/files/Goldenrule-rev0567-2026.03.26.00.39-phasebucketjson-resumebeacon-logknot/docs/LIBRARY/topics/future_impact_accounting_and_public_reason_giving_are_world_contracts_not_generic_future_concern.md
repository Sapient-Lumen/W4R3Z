# Future-impact accounting and public reason-giving are world contracts, not generic future concern

Recent work adds another missing layer to Concord's future-facing worlds: **it matters whether future consequences are merely invoked rhetorically or are forced into the decision record through explicit impact checks, comparable trade-off surfaces, mandatory response paths, and recurring scrutiny**.

- `RS-GR-258` shows that the EU's 2026 Intergenerational Fairness Strategy treats a youth check, foresight tools, an intergenerational fairness index, and a scheduled progress report as concrete machinery for future-regarding policymaking rather than as decorative language.
- `RS-GR-259` shows that the JRC's Futures Balance tool is explicitly designed to make intergenerational fairness and long-term trade-offs visible and comparable across time and dimensions.
- `RS-GR-260` shows that the Welsh model embeds monitoring, assessment, periodic examination, and five-year reporting into the institution itself rather than leaving future-regard to informal goodwill.
- `RS-GR-261` shows that even with a future-generations act in place, bodies can still give little explicit consideration to it and fail to understand or evaluate impact well enough for system-wide change.
- `RS-GR-262` shows that regulatory impact assessment should consider future developments and not only immediate effects.
- Together, these sources warn that a benchmark can look more future-protective because it introduced a future check, a trade-off table, a written-response route, a progress report, or a recurring audit surface — not because the underlying reciprocity norm improved.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future-facing benchmark should not report "future generations were considered" without publishing whether the future was actually made legible inside the decision process.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. a world where future beneficiaries are mentioned in narrative only;
2. a world where future impacts are scored in an ex-ante template but never compared publicly;
3. a world where trade-offs are made explicit and comparable across time, sectors, or groups;
4. a world where recommendations or warnings require written response, debate, or follow-up review;
5. a world where the same decision is revisited through recurring audit, monitoring, or progress-report cycles.

Those are not reporting details.
They change whether apparent future-regard comes from a stronger action norm or from stronger accounting, justification, and scrutiny machinery.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds future-facing governance, stewardship, or policy-evaluation lanes, publish at least:

1. whether a future-impact check exists at all, and whether it is optional, advisory, mandatory, or binding;
2. which future-facing dimensions are scored or narrated: burdens, benefits, reversibility, option value, distribution across generations, or another set;
3. whether trade-offs are made explicit and comparable across time, groups, or policy paths;
4. whether future-facing findings trigger public reasons, written responses, debate, escalation, or later review;
5. whether headline results survive one same-choice comparison where future-impact accounting or reason-giving is removed while the action set stays fixed.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake accounting quality, public justification, or audit cadence for the same thing as generic future-regarding virtue.
