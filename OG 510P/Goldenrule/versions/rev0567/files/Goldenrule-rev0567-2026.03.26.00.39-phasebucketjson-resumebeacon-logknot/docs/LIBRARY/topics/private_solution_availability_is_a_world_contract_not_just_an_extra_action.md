# Private solution availability is a world contract, not just an extra action

Recent work adds a missing layer to Concord's future collective-action and solidarity lanes: **a world with private escape routes is not the same institution as a world where the only serious route is shared provision**.

- `RS-GR-195` shows across 34 nations that when both public and private solutions exist, higher-wealth participants choose the private solution much more often, support for public solutions falls, and wealth inequality rises; the paper labels this the **private solution trap**.
- The same paper identifies two recurring escape routes from that trap: **early contributions to the public solution** and **conditional cooperation**.
- `RS-GR-196` adds that in a dilemma with both collective and individual solutions, high-trust contexts push participants toward the collective solution while low-trust contexts push them toward the individual solution, with lower overall efficiency in the low-trust case.
- `RS-GR-198` further shows that self-reliance can crowd out cooperation and increase wealth inequality, especially when some actors can solve the shared problem on their own more easily than others.
- Together, these results warn that a benchmark can look less cooperative, more unequal, or more individually rational because the world quietly offered **private protection or self-reliance**, not because the underlying reciprocity policy became worse.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future benchmark should not treat private solutions as harmless action-space enrichment.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. a world where only collective provision can solve the problem;
2. a world where agents may also buy, build, or invest in private protection;
3. a world where private solutions are accessible mainly to already-advantaged agents;
4. a world where trust, expectations, or prior coordination determine whether the public route still looks viable.

Those choices do not merely add optional moves.
They change dependence on others, the appeal of conditional cooperation, and whether inequality is damped by common provision or amplified by unequal access to self-protection.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds collective-risk, public-goods, or solidarity lanes with private alternatives, publish at least:

1. whether private solutions exist and whether they fully solve, partially solve, or merely soften the shared problem;
2. the relative cost and efficacy of the public and private routes;
3. who can access private protection, and whether access is equal, merit-linked, wealth-linked, or history-linked;
4. whether headline results survive a no-private-solution companion baseline.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake self-protection architecture for Golden-Rule progress or decline.
