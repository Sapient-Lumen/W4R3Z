# Rematch Role Assignment Is a First-Class Contract

The archive now has enough outside literature and local pressure to make one more rematch-world warning explicit:

> In restart / rematch worlds, **who comes back as whom** is part of the scientific world contract.

## Why this became important

The local archive already treats role asymmetry as scientifically real: a Golden-Rule-like strategy should survive not only extortion and noise, but also **role asymmetry**.

The restart-game literature sharpens that into an implementor obligation:

- Berker & Conitzer (`RS-GR-009`) frame restart worlds as a response to the anonymous-pool failure mode where agents can defect and immediately seek a new partner.
- Fleischmann, Fragkia, and Berker (`RS-GR-012`) extend that setting beyond symmetric games with symmetric strategies, and explicitly note that once strategies are asymmetric, rematching raises the question of **which strategy or role a player receives when paired again**.
- Leung & Turrini (`RS-GR-005`) also point toward richer partner-selection dynamics once opting out is learnable rather than fixed.

That means a future Concord rematch world cannot treat role assignment as an invisible engine default.

## What the inheritor should specify

At minimum, the world contract should say:

1. whether strategies are interpreted symmetrically or by role,
2. whether rematching preserves a player's role or allows role swapping,
3. whether any reputation / partner-history state travels across rematches,
4. whether pool entry is anonymous or history-aware,
5. and whether benchmark claims must be rerun under a role-swapped companion configuration.

## Why this matters for Golden-Rule work

A policy can look fair in one seat and exploitative in the other.
That is especially dangerous here because the program already wants to evaluate:

- exit versus stay,
- direct versus indirect reciprocity,
- universalisation / role-reversal intuitions,
- and asymmetric bad-information settings.

If rematch roles are underspecified, the archive could accidentally confuse:

- a property of the policy,
- with a property of the seat assignment rule.

## Conservative handoff rule

Until the real rematch world is specified, use this conservative posture:

1. keep one explicit fixed-role benchmark,
2. keep one explicit role-swapped companion benchmark,
3. and treat claims that change materially across those two runs as provisional rather than general.

## Practical next step

The next engine-supported leave/rematch world should expose role policy as data, not prose.
That keeps the eventual partner-choice tranche aligned with the archive's broader Golden-Rule requirement that reciprocity survive role reversal rather than merely one convenient seating arrangement.
