# Shared shock structure and risk pooling are world contracts, not payoff noise

Recent work adds a missing layer to Concord's future cooperation and mutual-aid lanes: **risk does not just change payoffs; it changes the social institution**.

- `RS-GR-176` shows that adding stochastic risk can raise cooperation in laboratory social dilemmas, but the paper finds that this rise is **not well explained by informal risk sharing** itself.
- The paper further reports that both independent and correlated risk can prevent the usual decay in cooperation, which means a benchmark can look morally improved when the real change is how uncertainty reframes the game.
- `RS-GR-177` adds that collective versus individual shocks can change intra-community cooperation in field-style public-goods settings.
- That means a world with shared exposure, common fate, or collective loss risk is not equivalent to a world with purely idiosyncratic bad luck.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future benchmark should not treat risk structure as generic background noise.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. deterministic payoffs with no shock exposure;
2. idiosyncratic shocks that hit agents separately;
3. correlated or group-level shocks that create common fate;
4. worlds that also allow side-transfers, pooling, or post-shock aid.

Those differences do not merely change variance.
They change whether cooperation looks like shared-risk response, common-fate identity, informal insurance, or actual reciprocity policy.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds stochastic public-goods, helping, or mutual-aid worlds, publish at least:

1. whether shocks are individual, correlated, or fully collective;
2. when losses are realized relative to contribution and observation stages;
3. whether side-transfers, pooling, or aid claims are allowed before or after shocks;
4. whether measured cooperation is separated from payout smoothing and shock buffering.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake risk-structure effects for Golden-Rule progress.
