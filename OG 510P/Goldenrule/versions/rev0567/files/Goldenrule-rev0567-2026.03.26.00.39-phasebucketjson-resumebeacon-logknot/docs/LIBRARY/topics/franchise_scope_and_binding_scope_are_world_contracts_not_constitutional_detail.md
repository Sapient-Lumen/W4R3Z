# Franchise scope and binding scope are world contracts, not constitutional detail

Recent work adds a second missing governance layer to Concord's future institution-design lanes: **who gets to vote, and who is bound by the resulting rule, are not harmless constitutional footnotes**.

- `RS-GR-205` studies complete and incomplete punishment institutions and shows that incomplete institutions — where only a subgroup binds itself, or subgroups bind themselves separately — behave differently from complete whole-group institutions. Demand depends not only on the benefit of cooperation, but also on subgroup coordination and the cooperativeness of participants.
- `RS-GR-204` adds that voting scope matters even inside democratic sanction worlds: individual voting and group voting do not have identical effects across mechanisms.
- Together, these results warn that a benchmark can look more cooperative or more governable because only some agents vote, only some agents are bound, or subgroup coordination quietly does the hard work.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future benchmark should not report “the group chose punishment” or “the institution was adopted” without publishing the constitutional scope.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. all affected agents voting and all affected agents being bound;
2. a subgroup voting to bind only itself;
3. multiple subgroups adopting separate institutions in parallel;
4. outsiders remaining unbound while still benefiting from others' coordination.

Those are not legal fine print.
They change free-rider exposure, coordination burden, and whether the benchmark is measuring a rule or a franchise design.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds institution choice, publish at least:

1. the electorate: who is allowed to vote or otherwise authorize the rule;
2. the decision rule: unanimity, majority, plurality, weighted vote, or representative choice;
3. the binding scope: who is actually governed, punished, or protected by the chosen rule;
4. whether headline results survive a complete all-members baseline beside any partial-franchise or subgroup-binding variant.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake constitutional scope or subgroup coordination for Golden-Rule progress.
