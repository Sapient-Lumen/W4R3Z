# Legacy motive type and action visibility are world contracts, not one future-care knob

Recent work adds another missing layer to Concord's future-facing worlds: **legacy motivation is not one undifferentiated force, and its effects depend on what kind of legacy is activated, whether the action is visible, and whether the effect lasts beyond the immediate prompt**.

- `RS-GR-218` shows that legacy motives separate into at least two meaningful types: impact-oriented legacy concern (wanting to help future people) and reputation-focused legacy concern (wanting to be remembered well). Impact legacy motives relate more strongly and more consistently to pro-environmental engagement, while reputation legacy motives are weaker and depend more on whether behavior is visible.
- `RS-GR-219` shows that an intergenerational letter-writing intervention can raise responsibility and pro-environmental intentions, and that the letter condition uniquely increased legacy motivation and donations relative to a control, but the self-reported behavior effects regressed after a week.
- Together, these results warn that a benchmark can look more future-regarding because it activated impact legacy, because it made reputation payoffs visible, or because it harvested a short-lived salience spike rather than a durable policy change.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future-facing benchmark should not report "legacy concern improved cooperation" without publishing which legacy pathway was activated and whether the induced action was publicly legible or durable.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. a world with no explicit legacy cue;
2. a world that highlights positive impact on future people;
3. a world that highlights how the present agent will be remembered;
4. a world where future-facing action is public and reputation-bearing versus private and mostly impact-bearing;
5. a world where gains are measured immediately after the prompt versus after a real delay.

Those are not one motivational slider.
They change whether the benchmark is measuring stewardship, status-seeking, or temporary prompt salience.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds legacy prompts, descendant letters, or future-self / future-child exercises, publish at least:

1. whether the intervention targets impact legacy, reputation legacy, both, or neither;
2. whether the resulting action is observable to others, reputationally scored, or effectively private;
3. whether the future recipient is one's descendants, an abstract future public, or another specified future beneficiary class;
4. whether the main claim survives one no-legacy baseline and one delayed follow-up rather than relying only on immediate post-prompt effects.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake visible status-seeking or short-lived salience for durable Golden-Rule progress.
