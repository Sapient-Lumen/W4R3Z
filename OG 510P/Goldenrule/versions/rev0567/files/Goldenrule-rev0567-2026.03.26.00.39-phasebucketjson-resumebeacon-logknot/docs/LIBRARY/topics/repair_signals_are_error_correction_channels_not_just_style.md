# Repair signals are error-correction channels, not just style

Recent indirect-reciprocity work adds a compact warning for any future Concord world that allows emotion, apology, or other expressive signals.

- `RS-GR-140` reports that emotion expressions can positively influence the evolution of cooperation in indirect reciprocity, especially when errors are frequent.
- The paper treats these signals as communicative and partly error-correcting rather than as decorative social texture.

## Why this matters for Concord

If a world allows expressive repair signals, then benchmark outcomes are no longer driven only by action policy and reputation update rules.
They are also shaped by whether agents have an extra channel for clarifying intent, softening mistaken defections, or coordinating return to cooperation.

That means a "forgiving" or "robust" policy can look better simply because the world granted it extra error-correction bandwidth.
The improvement may be real, but it belongs to the **institution-plus-policy pair**, not to the bare action policy alone.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord enables expressive repair signals, publish at least:

1. signal vocabulary / allowed acts,
2. timing rights (before action, after action, after sanction, before re-entry, etc.),
3. audience / visibility,
4. whether signals feed directly into reputation or only indirectly through partner response,
5. one no-signal baseline,
6. one abuse or overuse metric so cheap performative signaling does not hide inside a cooperation gain.

Without that compact contract, future sessions can mistake extra communicative bandwidth for a deeper reciprocity breakthrough.
