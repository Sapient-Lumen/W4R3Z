# Successor competence and capability continuity are world contracts, not record retention

Recent work adds another missing layer to Concord's long-horizon stewardship worlds: **it matters whether future stewards inherit merely records or an actually capable institution with planned succession, mission-critical knowledge preservation, targeted training, staffing depth, and enough geographic / organizational coverage to keep the work intelligible and executable over time**.

- `RS-GR-313` shows that DOE Legacy Management explicitly reviews planned retirements, tracks their impact on mission execution, plans succession and continuity across key offices, and treats mission-critical knowledge preservation, targeted training, and future staffing growth as formal objectives inside long-term stewardship.
- `RS-GR-314` shows that LM's high-performing-organization plan treats staffing levels, grade structure, technical capability needs, workforce planning, geographic distribution, and human-capital actions as part of the institution's operating architecture rather than as separable HR trivia.
- `RS-GR-315` shows that DOE's central human-capital function treats succession planning, competency development, training, and retention as mission-supporting management work rather than as optional aftercare.
- Together, these sources warn that a benchmark can look more durable, more successor-safe, or more future-protective because it changed **staffing depth, overlap, training, role redundancy, mission-critical knowledge retention, or site-proximate capability placement** — not because the underlying Golden-Rule disposition improved.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future-facing benchmark should not report that an institution “preserved responsibility across generations” when the world mainly changed its **capability-continuity architecture**.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. a world where records survive but key operators retire without overlap;
2. a world with nominal successors but thin technical capability or no targeted training;
3. a world with staffing and competency plans but weak geographic/site coverage;
4. a world with explicit mission-critical knowledge preservation plus leadership and specialist pathways;
5. a world that looks durable only because the bench, overlap, and capability map improved.

Those are not background staffing details.
They change whether successors inherit paperwork, a title, or a live capacity to govern.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds successor-burden, closure, or long-horizon stewardship lanes, publish at least:

1. whether mission-critical roles have named successors, overlap periods, or single-person failure points;
2. whether competency development and targeted training are generic, role-specific, or absent;
3. whether staffing depth and geographic distribution are matched to the sites / burdens being stewarded;
4. whether mission-critical knowledge preservation is tied to live staffing and onboarding rather than records alone;
5. whether headline results survive one same-behavior comparison where only capability continuity changes.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake staffed continuity, overlap, or trained competence for the same thing as deeper reciprocity toward successors.
