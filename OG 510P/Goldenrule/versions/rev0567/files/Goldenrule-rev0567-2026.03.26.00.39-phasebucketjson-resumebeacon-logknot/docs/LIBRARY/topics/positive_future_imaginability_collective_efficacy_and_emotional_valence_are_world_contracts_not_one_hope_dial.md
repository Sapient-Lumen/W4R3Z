# Positive future imaginability, collective efficacy, and emotional valence are world contracts, not one hope dial

Recent work adds another missing layer to Concord's future-facing worlds: **future-oriented action can rise because the world makes a better future imaginable, believable, and emotionally rewarding, not because the underlying reciprocity policy improved**.

- `RS-GR-220` shows across four countries that the ability to imagine a positive future and efficacy beliefs about social and behavioral change are both strong correlates of individual and collective biodiversity-conservation intentions.
- `RS-GR-221` shows in a large climate-advocacy megastudy that the most consistently effective intervention emphasized both collective efficacy and the emotional benefits of climate action.
- `RS-GR-222` shows that utopian versus dystopian future visions did not simply shift action directly; their effects on mitigation and adaptation intentions ran indirectly through positive emotions such as hope and happiness, and those pathways differed by action type.
- Together, these results warn that a benchmark can look more future-regarding because it supplied a vivid desirable future, because it implied that collective action can actually work, or because it bundled action with positive affect.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future-facing benchmark should not report "agents acted for the future" without publishing whether the world changed **desirability**, **possibility**, or **emotional payoff**.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. a world with no explicit future vision at all;
2. a world that supplies a desirable utopian endpoint but no believable path to get there;
3. a world that explicitly states that collective action is effective or politically feasible;
4. a world that frames action as emotionally rewarding, hopeful, or socially uplifting;
5. a world that instead uses a dystopian future and relies on reduced hope or other emotions.

Those are not one generic hope intervention.
They change whether the benchmark is measuring stewardship, perceived achievability, or affective reward from acting.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds future visions, climate stories, or stewardship prompts, publish at least:

1. whether the future scenario is positive, negative, mixed, or absent;
2. whether the scenario emphasizes mere desirability, perceived possibility, collective efficacy, or explicit political / social achievability;
3. whether emotional benefits of acting together are cued explicitly or only implied;
4. whether headline results survive one no-vision / no-efficacy baseline beside any positive-future or efficacy-rich variant.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake imagination support, efficacy signaling, or emotional uplift for Golden-Rule progress.
