# Cooperation benchmarks should separate same-partner co-adaptation from fresh-partner transfer

A cooperation score measured after acclimation can hide two very different things:
continued success with the **same** partner after the pair has learned one another, or
a policy that still works when moved to a **fresh** partner after whatever familiarization budget the benchmark allows.
Those are not the same claim.
Three standing sources already make the compact archive rule clear:

- `RS-GR-069` shows that repeated exposure changes human preferences and partner selection because people learn about partner-type behavior over time.
- `RS-GR-070` makes adaptation to diverse human behaviors and unseen physical attributes an explicit benchmark target instead of assuming one practiced interaction lane is enough.
- `RS-GR-037` shows that cooperative skills learned across varied environments can transfer to new partners and even to humans, which is stronger evidence than success with one repeatedly encountered counterpart.

## Minimum contract

Whenever a cooperation benchmark includes acclimation, practice, or post-tutorial evaluation, publish:

1. whether the reported post-adaptation score is from **same-partner continuation** or from **fresh-partner transfer**;
2. if transfer is tested, whether the fresh partner is from the **same partner pool / class** or a **shifted pool / class**;
3. whether any **memory, belief state, or learned personalization** carries across partner boundaries;
4. how much **adaptation budget** is allowed before the transfer test begins;
5. whether the transfer test preserves the same task/world or also changes environment / institution conditions;
6. and whether headline results pool same-partner and fresh-partner lanes or report them separately.

## Implementor consequence

Do not let “improves with use” quietly stand in for “generalizes after use.”
A policy may look strong because one human learned its conventions, because the model accumulated partner-specific memory, or because both sides converged on a private protocol that does not survive the next counterpart.
Fresh-partner transfer after acclimation is a different bar from same-pair co-adaptation.

## Archive consequence

Keep the retained object compact.
One benchmark-card row is enough: lane type (same-partner vs fresh-partner), partner-pool relation, carryover state policy, and adaptation budget before transfer.
That prevents future sessions from laundering private co-adaptation into a broader cooperation-generalization claim.
