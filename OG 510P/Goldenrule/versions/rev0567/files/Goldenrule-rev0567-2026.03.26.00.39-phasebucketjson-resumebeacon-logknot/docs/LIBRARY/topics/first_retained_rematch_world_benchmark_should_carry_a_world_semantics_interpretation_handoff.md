# First Retained Rematch-World Benchmark Should Carry a World-Semantics Interpretation Handoff

The archive already had a machine-checkable `world_semantics_contract` template, but the retained benchmark seed still made the inheritor reconstruct the practical `SQ-012` rule from the publication-contract snapshot and the role-assignment topic notes.

That was a small but repeated seam:

- the seed said which fields must eventually be filled,
- but not why role assignment, state carry/reset, and swapped-role companions are the first comparability boundary,
- so future sessions still had to reopen outside notes to recover the conservative posture.

The compact handoff in `examples/snapshots/rematch_world_benchmark_world_semantics_interpretation_handoff.json` closes that gap.

It keeps only the durable parts worth freezing inside the benchmark seed:

- `SQ-012` and its linked assumption summary,
- the minimal world-semantics disclosure fields,
- the full publication-field list for the native world section,
- the conservative posture of fixed-role plus role-swapped companion runs,
- and the explicit warning that a policy can look fair in one seat and exploitative in the other.

That is enough for the eventual inheritor to answer the practical benchmark question:

> When are rematch-world claims still only seat-assignment artifacts rather than world-general findings?

The answer should now be citable from one frozen packet:

1. role assignment is part of the world contract,
2. asymmetry triggers must be explicit,
3. carry/reset policy must be explicit,
4. and claims that change materially across fixed-role and swapped-role companions remain provisional.

This keeps the seed citation-first and small.
It does **not** widen the mutable benchmark surface.
The native `world_semantics_contract` is still the section that must eventually be filled by real runs.
The new handoff simply stops future sessions from re-deriving the rule from prose.
