# Own-lifetime payoff boundaries and temporal policy discounting are world contracts, not generic future concern

Recent work adds another missing layer to Concord's future-facing worlds: **it matters separately whether benefits arrive within participants' expected lifetimes, near the edge of life, or mainly after death, and whether the world lengthens personal time horizons before asking for sacrifice**.

- `RS-GR-244` shows that citizens do discount policy payoffs by timing, including whether effects land beyond their own lifespan, but also that larger later benefits can still beat smaller sooner ones, so beyond-lifetime policy support is not a simple yes/no altruism test.
- The same source warns that timing and payoff scale interact, so a benchmark can look more future-regarding because benefits were pulled forward rather than because norms changed.
- `RS-GR-245` shows that parenthood can lengthen time horizons and increase climate-policy support, and that part of the effect runs through a time-horizons mechanism rather than through issue beliefs alone.
- Together, these results warn that a benchmark can look more caring toward future generations because benefits now arrive before the decision-maker dies, because caregiver-linked time horizons lengthened, or because descendants became personally salient — not because the underlying Golden-Rule norm improved.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future-facing benchmark should not report "agents acted for future generations" without publishing when benefits arrive relative to the acting cohort's lifespan and whether the world manipulated time horizon directly.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. a world where benefits arrive clearly within the actor's own lifetime;
2. a world where benefits arrive near the edge of late life or are only partly experienced by the actor;
3. a world where benefits land mainly after death for anonymous successors;
4. a world where a child / caregiver / descendant prompt lengthens time horizon before the same decision;
5. a world where both payoff timing and family-linked future salience change at once.

Those are not framing niceties.
They change whether apparent future-regard is evidence about impartial reciprocity, personal stake extension, or the timing structure of the world itself.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds future-facing, stewardship, or long-horizon sacrifice lanes, publish at least:

1. whether payoff arrival is within-lifetime, edge-of-lifetime, mixed, or mainly posthumous for the current actor;
2. whether the world changes only payoff timing, only beneficiary identity, or both at once;
3. whether family / caregiver / descendant salience is activated before the decision;
4. whether later larger benefits are allowed to compete explicitly against sooner smaller ones;
5. whether headline results survive one matched-benefit within-lifetime baseline beside any beyond-lifetime world.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake lifespan-boundary effects and time-horizon extension for the same thing as universal future-regarding reciprocity.
