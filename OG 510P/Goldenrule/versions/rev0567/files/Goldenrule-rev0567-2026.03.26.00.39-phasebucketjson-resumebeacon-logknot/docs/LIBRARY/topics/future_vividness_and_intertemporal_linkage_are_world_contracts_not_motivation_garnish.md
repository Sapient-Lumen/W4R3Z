# Future vividness and intertemporal linkage are world contracts, not motivation garnish

Recent work adds a second missing layer to Concord's future-facing worlds: **future beneficiaries do not enter present decisions with a fixed psychological distance**.

- `RS-GR-212` shows that creating intertemporal community links across cohorts can improve coordination in an intergenerational common-resource problem, and that these links can substitute for other motivational levers in practice.
- `RS-GR-213` shows that reducing psychological distance to climate harms can increase effortful pro-environmental behavior across diverse samples, which means a future-facing world can become more action-inducing simply because harms were made to feel nearer.
- `RS-GR-214` adds an important caution: future-self perspective taking and vivid narrative immersion can increase empathy and future discussion, yet direct effects on policy support or action can be mixed when the intervention also induces distress, sadness, or low-agency doom.
- Together, these results warn that a benchmark can look more cooperative, more future-regarding, or more emotionally serious because the world built a bridge to future beneficiaries, made future harms feel proximal, or changed the vividness/agency balance of future imagination — not because the underlying reciprocity norm got stronger.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future benchmark should not report “agents cared about future generations” without publishing how psychologically near or socially linked those future beneficiaries were made.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. a world with no direct bridge to future cohorts;
2. a world with intertemporal links, lineage cues, or explicit community continuity across cohorts;
3. a world that frames future harms as distant and abstract versus near and locally consequential;
4. a world that uses vivid future-self or disaster imagination with high empathy but weak perceived agency.

Those are not mere presentation choices.
They change salience, felt responsibility, action cost tolerance, and whether a measured gain should be read as norm strength or as distance management.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds intergenerational, climate, or stewardship lanes, publish at least:

1. whether present agents have any explicit bridge to future cohorts (intertemporal links, lineage cues, future-self prompts, or institutional continuity signals);
2. whether future harms/benefits are framed as proximal or distant across time, place, and social identity;
3. whether future-imagination aids preserve agency or rely mainly on catastrophic vividness;
4. whether headline results survive one low-vividness / no-link baseline beside any psychologically proximal or intertemporally linked variant.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake proximity engineering for Golden-Rule progress.
