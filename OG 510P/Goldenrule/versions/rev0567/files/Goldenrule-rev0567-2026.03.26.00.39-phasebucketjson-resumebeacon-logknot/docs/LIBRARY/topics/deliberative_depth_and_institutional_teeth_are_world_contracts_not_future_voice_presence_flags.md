# Deliberative depth and institutional teeth are world contracts, not future-voice presence flags

Recent work adds another missing layer to Concord's future-facing worlds: **it matters separately whether future interests are present in the room, whether deliberation is actually deep, and whether any representative has formal teeth when ignored**.

- `RS-GR-240` shows that participatory governance can help with long-term environmental problems, but for long-horizon cases intensive deliberation matters more than simply representing environmental or economic interests in the room.
- The same source warns that long-term and short-term governance do not respond to the same participation recipe, so seat counts and stakeholder presence should not be treated as a generic participation scalar.
- `RS-GR-241` identifies multiple institutional proxy types for future generations and shows that they vary sharply in legal basis, policy-phase access, and formal capacity to influence government or parliament.
- The same source also warns that many proxies can voice future interests but lack watchdog capacity with teeth when ignored.
- Together, these results warn that a benchmark can look more future-regarding because deliberation became more intensive, because more actors were seated at the table, or because a future-facing body acquired formal leverage — not because the base Golden-Rule policy improved.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future-facing benchmark should not report "future generations were represented" without publishing voice presence, deliberative depth, and institutional teeth as separate world fields.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. a world where future interests are mentioned but no one is institutionally present;
2. a world where future-oriented actors are present but the process is shallow or symbolic;
3. a world where deliberation is intensive but recommendations remain non-binding;
4. a world where a proxy body can audit, escalate, or agenda-set when ignored;
5. a world where future-facing institutions can actually block, delay, or force reconsideration.

Those are not presentation details.
They change whether apparent long-horizon gains reflect deeper reasoning, altered stakeholder composition, or formal leverage over decision-makers.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds future-voice, stewardship, or long-term-governance lanes, publish at least:

1. whether future interests are absent, observer-present, stakeholder-present, proxy-represented, or institutionally guarded;
2. whether deliberation is shallow, moderate, or intensive, and what repeated exchange or justification burden exists;
3. what teeth the future-facing role has: narration only, advice, audit, agenda-setting, delay, veto, override, or none;
4. whether the institution acts only in consultation or also across drafting, review, implementation, and ex-post oversight;
5. whether headline results survive one seat-rich / low-deliberation baseline and one deliberation-rich / low-teeth baseline beside any empowered future-voice world.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake symbolic inclusion, deliberative quality, or institutional teeth for the same thing.
