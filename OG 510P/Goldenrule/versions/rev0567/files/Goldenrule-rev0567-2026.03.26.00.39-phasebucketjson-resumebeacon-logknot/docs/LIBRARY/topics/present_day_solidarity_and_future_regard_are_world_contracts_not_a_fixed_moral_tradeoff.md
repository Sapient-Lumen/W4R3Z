# Present-day solidarity and future regard are world contracts, not a fixed moral tradeoff

Recent work adds another missing layer to Concord's future-facing worlds: **care for future generations does not automatically crowd out urgent help for people alive now, but support can still depend on what immediate sacrifices the world asks current actors to bear**.

- `RS-GR-225` shows that concern for future generations predicts costly present-day prosociality and even extraordinary altruism in the form of organ-donor interest and status, challenging the assumption that future-oriented concern necessarily displaces immediate helping.
- `RS-GR-226` shows that awareness of intergenerational climate justice affects public support for deep decarbonisation specifically through willingness to tolerate greater near-term economic losses, so future-duty worlds are also sacrifice-allocation worlds.
- Together, these results warn that a benchmark can look more future-regarding or more presently prosocial because the world changed whether current sacrifice is foregrounded, who bears it, and whether present-day helping is treated as competing with or aligned to future-facing duty.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future-facing benchmark should not report "agents acted for future generations" without publishing whether the action also required immediate costly solidarity from current actors and whether current beneficiaries or current losers were made salient.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. a world where future-facing action is cheap talk or symbolic support;
2. a world where future-facing action requires immediate private sacrifice;
3. a world where future-facing action is framed as aligned with present-day helping rather than in competition with it;
4. a world where willingness is measured by real costly help, extraordinary altruism, or acceptance of near-term economic losses.

Those are not interchangeable moral readouts.
They change whether the benchmark is measuring broad moral expansiveness, willingness to bear current costs for future people, or a perceived present-versus-future tradeoff.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds future-facing stewardship, climate, or intergenerational lanes, publish at least:

1. whether the future-facing action requires immediate personal cost, household cost, community cost, or no present sacrifice at all;
2. whether current beneficiaries, current harmed groups, and future beneficiaries are all explicit or whether one side is left psychologically offstage;
3. whether the outcome is symbolic support, revealed costly helping, or willingness to accept near-term material loss;
4. whether headline results survive one matched-cost baseline where present-day and future-day beneficiaries are varied without changing the sacrifice schedule.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake cost structure or beneficiary salience for Golden-Rule progress.
