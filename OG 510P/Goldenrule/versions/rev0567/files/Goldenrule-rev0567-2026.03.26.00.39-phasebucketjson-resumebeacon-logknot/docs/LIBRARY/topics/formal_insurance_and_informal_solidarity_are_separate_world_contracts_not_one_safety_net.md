# Formal insurance and informal solidarity are separate world contracts, not one safety net

Recent work adds a second missing layer to Concord's future helping and fallback lanes: **formal protection can substitute for, complement, or morally reframe informal support**.

- `RS-GR-178` reports that private transfers fall substantially when a recipient could have avoided need by purchasing insurance.
- The paper further reports that the decline is triggered by the presence of the insurance option itself, not just by deliberate reliance on others.
- `RS-GR-179` adds that formal unemployment insurance in a real transfer network crowds out informal transfers only modestly on average, with an estimated crowd-out of at most about five cents per added dollar of UI.
- Together, those results warn against treating formal insurance as a neutral background improvement or assuming crowd-out is either complete or absent by default.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future benchmark should not collapse fallback institutions into one generic "help exists" toggle.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. no formal protection and only voluntary informal aid;
2. optional insurance that recipients could have bought ex ante;
3. automatic or public fallback that changes judgments of deservingness;
4. mixed worlds where formal protection and informal solidarity coexist only partially.

Those choices do not merely change payout floors.
They change how much others feel obliged to help, whether unmet need is judged as bad luck or failed prudence, and whether observed generosity is replacing or topping up a formal safety net.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds aid, relief, or solidarity lanes under risk, publish at least:

1. whether formal insurance or public fallback exists and who is eligible;
2. whether agents could have insured ex ante and whether observers know that;
3. whether informal transfers top up, replace, or are reduced by formal protection;
4. whether need is scored differently when formal protection was available but unused.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake insurance design or deservingness judgments for reciprocal virtue.
