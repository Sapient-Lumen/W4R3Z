# Successor-safe ceremony receipt review verdicts should collapse to explicit citation advisories

Once the archive already has a compact review verdict, the next easy failure mode is **downstream citation drift**.

A future steward can inherit a verdict that says a reviewed locator is still claim-ready or must reopen now, but still be forced to manually decide what that means for existing notes, newly written summaries, or inheritor-facing claims. That leaves the actual citation-handling decision in chat memory rather than in the archive.

The archive should therefore keep one further tiny artifact: a **citation advisory**.

For any successor-safe ceremony receipt review verdict, the advisory should:

1. cite the reviewed locator;
2. preserve the review verdict's as-of date and keep-citing versus reopen-now decision;
3. say whether the reviewed locator remains citable or is withdrawn from claim-ready use;
4. say what should happen to existing citations and what should happen to new citations; and
5. carry the regeneration sequence only when the reviewed locator has been withdrawn.

This is deliberately smaller than a full reassessment. It does not replay the package and it does not restate all of the review basis. It simply turns one freshness verdict into one downstream stewarding answer:

> **Should future notes keep citing this locator, or should they stop until the package is regenerated?**

NIST's RMF Monitor and Authorize guidance says continuous-monitoring results need to be documented in posture reports for authorizing officials and that authorization decisions are communicated with terms and conditions and made available to relevant organizational officials. A compact citation advisory translates that pressure into archive practice without widening the retained package.

The advisory should stay narrow:

- one reviewed locator;
- one source review verdict;
- one advisory decision;
- one existing-citation action;
- one new-citation action; and
- one regeneration sequence only if the reviewed locator has been withdrawn.

That is enough to keep citation handling from drifting back into oral tradition.
