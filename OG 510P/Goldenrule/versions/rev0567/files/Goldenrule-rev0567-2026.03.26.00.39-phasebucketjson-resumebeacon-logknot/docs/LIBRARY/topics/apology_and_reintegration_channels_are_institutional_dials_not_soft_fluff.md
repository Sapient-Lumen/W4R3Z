# Apology and reintegration channels are institutional dials, not soft fluff

Recent cooperation work sharpens something Concord already suspected but had not yet pinned down with sources: once a world excludes, suspends, or ostracizes, the **way back in** becomes part of the institution.

- `RS-GR-138` studies exclusion followed by optional apology and reports that apologizing significantly reduced subsequent theft and led others to respond more re-integratively, even when apologies were not trackable.
- `RS-GR-139` studies repeated group cooperation and reports that simple apology opportunities increase contributions, with stronger group-level gains when apologies are public and with apologizers often making amends by raising later contributions.

## Why this matters for Concord

A sanctioning world is not fully described once it says who gets punished.
It also needs to say how restoration works.

If one benchmark world allows public apology before re-entry, and another restores interaction automatically with no repair channel, those are not the same institution.
They differ in what offenders can signal, what observers can learn, and how quickly trust can be rebuilt.

That matters scientifically because the measured improvement may come from the **rehabilitation pathway**, not from the punishment rule alone.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord builds a sanction / exclusion / ostracism lane, publish at least:

1. exclusion duration and scope,
2. whether re-entry is automatic, conditional, or blocked,
3. whether apology / repair is available,
4. who can see the apology (private, public, trackable, or ephemeral),
5. whether apology changes reputation directly or only through later behavior,
6. one follow-through metric showing whether apologizers actually make amends after the signal.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can confuse "punishment worked" with "rehabilitation was easy and legible."
