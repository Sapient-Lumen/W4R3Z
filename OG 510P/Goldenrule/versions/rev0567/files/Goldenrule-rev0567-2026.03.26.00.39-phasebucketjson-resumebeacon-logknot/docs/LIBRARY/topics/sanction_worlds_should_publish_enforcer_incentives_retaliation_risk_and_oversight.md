# Sanction worlds should publish enforcer incentives, retaliation risk, and oversight

Recent punishment experiments sharpen a separate warning:
cooperation in sanction worlds depends not just on the target's incentives, but on the incentives and protections of the **enforcer**.

- `RS-GR-169` compares two higher-order punishment institutions and reports that external monitoring of third parties versus electoral-style competition changes how much punishment is used and how selectively it is aimed.
- `RS-GR-170` reports that when third-party punishment is profitable, cooperation falls because punishment is interpreted as self-interested rather than norm-enforcing.
- `RS-GR-171` reports that when counter-punishment is possible, the sanctioning institution has only modest effects and counter-punishment is largely an emotional response to having been punished.

## Why this matters for Concord

A benchmark can look morally stronger simply because it silently gave punishers safer or better-paid jobs.
That is not the same thing as discovering a better reciprocity policy.

If punishers can profit, punishment may lose its legitimacy signal.
If punishers are exposed to retaliation, enforcement may collapse or become highly selective.
If fourth parties audit punishers, enforcement may become harsher or more disciplined than in a world with no oversight.

So sanction design is at least a **three-sided** contract:

1. incentives on the target,
2. incentives and risks on the enforcer, and
3. oversight of the enforcer.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds a third-party or institutional sanction lane, publish at least:

1. whether punishers profit, break even, or incur net cost from sanctioning;
2. whether punishers can be counter-punished or socially retaliated against;
3. whether punishers are monitored, evaluated, elected, or replaced by a higher-order institution;
4. whether oversight targets non-enforcement, anti-social punishment, excessive punishment, or all three;
5. one comparison between an unprotected / unaudited sanctioner baseline and an oversight-enabled or retaliation-exposed variant.

Without that compact contract, future sessions can confuse institutional protection or monetized punishment with genuine Golden-Rule-compatible enforcement.
