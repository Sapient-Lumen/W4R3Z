# Sunset, reauthorization, and policy-stock retirement are world contracts, not housekeeping

Recent work adds another missing layer to Concord's future-facing worlds: **it matters whether rules expire automatically, require affirmative reauthorization, accumulate indefinitely, or overload the organizations that must keep implementing them — and those design choices can determine successor burdens even when a policy sounds temporary or cautious on paper**.

- `RS-GR-285` shows that temporary measures can increase approval of controversial rules and make later extension more likely, which means "temporary" is not automatically a safeguard against durable burden.
- `RS-GR-285` therefore warns that sunset clauses can function as approval-easing devices rather than reliable retirement mechanisms.
- `RS-GR-286` shows that policy accumulation can overload administrative organizations and force policy triage, where some obligations are delayed, neglected, or deprioritized even when they remain formally on the books.
- `RS-GR-286` also shifts the analysis from single-policy quality to the implementation burden of the whole policy stock, which is especially relevant for future-facing institutions that tend to add review, monitoring, and reporting duties over time.
- `RS-GR-287` shows that recent OECD guidance treats ex post evaluation, evidence use, and assessment of policy alternatives as part of regulating for effectiveness rather than as optional aftercare.
- Together, these sources warn that a benchmark can look more future-protective because it used temporary framing, reauthorization gates, or stock-retirement discipline — or can silently fail because policy stocks grew faster than implementation capacity — not because the underlying Golden-Rule disposition improved.

## Why this matters for Concord

A future-facing benchmark should not report that an institution is "durable and careful" when the world mainly changed its **expiry, retirement, and implementation-load architecture**.

There is a real institutional difference between:
1. a world where obligations accumulate unless explicitly repealed;
2. a world where temporary measures are politically easy to approve and easy to extend;
3. a world where reauthorization is real, evidence-linked, and failure can retire the rule;
4. a world where formal duties keep growing faster than implementation capacity;
5. a world where the appearance of caution comes from temporary labeling while successors inherit permanent review, monitoring, and compliance burdens.

Those are not housekeeping details.
They change whether successors inherit a governable portfolio, a ratcheting policy stock, or a nominally temporary institution that never actually leaves.

## Minimal implementor handoff

If Concord adds future-facing institutions, review bodies, or long-horizon policy lanes, publish at least:

1. whether rules expire automatically, require affirmative reauthorization, auto-renew, or persist until active repeal;
2. what evidence standard or review packet is required for extension, retirement, or replacement;
3. whether temporary framing changes approval thresholds or later extension probability;
4. the expected implementation load added to the standing policy stock and who bears it;
5. whether headline results survive one same-behavior comparison where only sunset / reauthorization / retirement semantics change.

Without that compact contract, future inheritors can mistake temporary framing, retirement discipline, or simple administrative underload for the same thing as deeper reciprocity or more durable care for future beneficiaries.
