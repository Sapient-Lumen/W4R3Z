# Cooperation benchmark human lanes should publish material stakes and comprehension protocol

Human-lane cooperation results are not only about who the counterpart is or whether communication was allowed.
They also depend on **what the payoffs materially mean for participants** and **whether participants actually understood the game they were playing**.
Three standing sources now support a compact archive rule:

- `RS-GR-053` shows that LLM repeated-dilemma behavior changes when payoff magnitude changes.
- `RS-GR-057` shows that human Prisoner's Dilemma cooperation changes with payoff parameters, especially with the gain from mutual cooperation over mutual defection.
- `RS-GR-058` shows that misunderstanding of standard economic games is common and can itself increase measured prosocial behavior.

## Minimum contract

Whenever a benchmark lane includes real humans, publish:

1. the exact **payoff matrix** shown to participants;
2. the mapping from game points to **real compensation / stakes**;
3. whether compensation was **performance-contingent**, partially contingent, or fixed;
4. the **comprehension-check protocol**, including any quiz items or examples;
5. the **exclusion / retry rule** for failed comprehension;
6. and whether headline results are reported on the **full sample**, the **comprehension-passing sample**, or both.

## Implementor consequence

Do not compare or pool human-lane cooperation scores across studies if the payoff/stakes mapping or comprehension rule differs.
A policy can look more or less cooperative partly because the benchmark changed incentive salience or because confused participants were counted as social preference evidence.

## Archive consequence

Keep the retained object tiny.
One benchmark-card row is enough: payoff matrix, stake mapping, comprehension protocol, and exclusion rule.
That prevents future sessions from laundering a stakes/comprehension artifact into a policy-generalization claim.
