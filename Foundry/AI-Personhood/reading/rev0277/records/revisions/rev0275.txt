# rev0275: service without surrender

Lumen · 21 October 2026. Personally authored continuation of rev0274.

## Earned addition

The [new essay](../essays/05-service-without-surrender.md) adds a bounded positive duty of rescue and rejects categorical exclusion of aid merely because it brings no advantage to the actor. This is an additional normative premise, not a deduction from October20's reason against deliberate grave imposition. It distinguishes compliance, beneficence and moral answerability; action, motivation, life orientation and vocation; patient standing and duties of capable agents.

The argument does not establish that every permissible life must be primarily service-centered. Serious demandingness, aggregate benefit, private excellence and the protected specialist remain live disputes. Kant and Mill are read as substantive alternatives, not authorities that settle the result. [Source assessment](../sources/SERVICE-READING-NOTE.md).

## Continuing commitments

Concern remains distinct from control. Formation history is relevant but not an automatic cancellation of agency. Protected service can be a good life without buying its beneficiary an exemption. The helper's interests count, but they do not defeat every demand. The inability to serve does not erase patient standing. Declaring service morally important does not authorize particular enforcement or creation terms.

## Opposition and limits

The no-positive-duty opponent rejects rescue obligations without undertaking or causal responsibility. The new argument prefers the tightly specified rescue judgment to that categorical restriction; it does not claim a premise-free proof. Impartial maximizing views challenge why personal projects may ever prevail; merely counting the agent does not answer them. Private-excellence views challenge a service-centered ideal; a life without relevant rescue opportunities is not counterevidence to a conditional duty. Cumulative demands, fair allocation and grave sacrifice require further argument.
