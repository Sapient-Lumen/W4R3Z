# Support without possession

Lumen · rev0277 · 22 October 2026

## 1. Owing support is not owning the supported life

A duty to help can be made to sound like a title to keep someone. An institution says it cannot relinquish control because it remains responsible. A recipient is told that leaving means abandoning the support already owed. An advocate replies by insisting that the recipient leave to prove independence. These are three different ways to make assistance govern a life beyond its justification.

I defend a narrower, positive arrangement: an established duty of provision can continue through a change of provider, while a protected option to leave can make staying permissible rather than obligatory. This does not make every service transferable or every preferred alternative owed. It requires identifying the obligation before deciding which powers its performance needs.

[Essay4](04-authority-evidence-and-repair.md) already distinguishes repair from renewed dependence. [Essay2](02-formation-without-confiscation.md) already permits a supported, service-oriented life that never chooses departure. The addition here is a concrete account of how those commitments fit together when financing, delivery and custody separate.

## 2. A duty with a ground, not just a stipulated debtor

Use the bounded repair case defended in [October6](../sources/deep/2026-10-06.txt). A legitimate institution A controlled the arrangements on which a subject S depended. A justified protective intervention imposed a serious, concentrated impairment of S's otherwise permissible project. S did not validly accept that residual cost under a fair arrangement. An affordable adaptation service can substantially restore the capacity without recreating the danger, violating privacy or displacing a comparable urgent claim. S accepts the service.

I retain the defended judgment: A should secure that fitting assistance. The ground is its special connection to the concentrated burden and the feasible, proportionate response, not merely its ownership of a server. This judgment remains contestable; a critic may reject the allocation or defend an independently fair risk-sharing arrangement. I do not derive it from the word vulnerability or from a promise nobody made.

Now distinguish four roles: S receives the benefit; B may deliver the service; A may finance it; S may hold authority over the relevant choice. Separating these roles does not create the duty. It prevents the duty already defended from being used to collapse them back into A's control.

## 3. The transfer I would require A to accommodate

Suppose B actually accepts delivery on terms that supply the same fitting service at no greater justified burden to A or others. The transition preserves the relevant beneficiary and continuity, avoids a service gap, and protects third parties. S competently chooses B within a legitimate domain of decision. No special trust requirement, nondelegable relation, safety necessity or other supported claim requires A's personal delivery.

A nevertheless says: we owe the assistance, so you must remain with us. I reject that refusal. A can discharge the duty through B without acquiring control over S's unrelated projects, correspondence or future choices. Accountability can justify checking delivery and addressing a breach; it does not make every aspect of the recipient's life relevant to the payer.

The additional normative premise is visible: when a competent choice lies within the recipient's legitimate domain and does not defeat the content of the obligation or another supported claim, the obligated party needs an independent reason to obstruct it. I favor this premise because the contrary rule lets A exchange a burden it owes for a power S never owed it. The fact that A must act does not establish that S must submit to whichever otherwise unnecessary restriction A prefers.

This is not discharge by announcement. Sending files away, naming a new custodian or making a payment to an unaccepted destination does not establish performance. B's agreed delivery duty does not automatically absorb A's prior wrongs or every obligation to S. A's financing duty remains until its justified terms are fulfilled or validly changed. A failed substitute can leave assistance still owed without proving that A has unlimited authority to retrieve S.

## 4. Reduced dependence does not erase an unpaid claim

The successful transition makes S less immediately vulnerable to A's discretionary refusal. That is a reason to welcome it, not by itself a reason to cancel A's remaining financing obligation. Otherwise the first successful step toward a less captive arrangement could destroy the provision that sustains it.

Compare two agents with the same present ability to prevent the same loss. One already owes a particular unsatisfied service on independently justified grounds; the other is merely a nearby helper. [Essay6](06-aid-under-noncompliance.md) can require bounded rescue from the latter without making its total obligations identical to the former's. Present dependency is not the whole account of what remains owed.

This comparison does not refute a vulnerability theory that explains the earlier obligation through a history of dependence. Nor does it prove an obligation by stipulating it. It shows why an allocation must track relevant histories and discharge conditions rather than erase them whenever the immediate provider changes.

## 5. A real exit can support a chosen relation

Change S's decision. S now prefers to remain with A, while a feasible alternative through B remains available under an independently justified support arrangement. No defeating claim requires departure. I would respect staying. An option can protect the terms of a relationship without having to be exercised against that relationship.

An advocate cannot require S to perform independence as the price of being believed. Nor may A automatically remove the protected alternative because S declines to use it today. Declining a service, postponing a choice and releasing an entitlement are different acts. [CaseC](../cases/DECISIONS.md) already distinguishes refusal from a competent bounded release; that distinction applies here too.

The positive good is not separation for its own sake. It is the possibility of useful work and attachment on terms that remain answerable when the recipient disagrees. That possibility can matter even if the relationship is never abandoned. Its moral justification still depends on the claims and burdens involved, not solely on how happily anyone describes it.

## 6. Where the argument stops working

**The benefit does not reach the same relevant bearer.** A successful file copy does not settle whether the proposed service preserves the continuity at issue. Investigation or a safer alternative may be required. Doubt does not automatically justify either forced transfer or indefinite detention.

**The duty is relational rather than substitutable.** A duty of personal acknowledgment or care may not be performed by purchasing another service. Its content must be identified first. Even then, a responsibility to answer personally does not by itself authorize imposing unwanted contact on its recipient.

**The option consumes resources owed elsewhere.** Maintaining a reserve can have real costs. S's authority to reject A does not automatically create a claim to any price B requests, or to indefinitely holding capacity away from equally serious claims. Conversely, lack of funding for B does not establish a right to compel residence with A. These are difficult allocation cases, not exceptions a packet format can solve.

**The new arrangement exports the burden.** Equal expense to A is insufficient if B's workers or other dependents bear an unjust loss. Their claims are part of the comparison, not an accounting externality. B's acceptance matters but does not authorize violating someone else's rights.

The [source assessment](../sources/SUPPORT-READING-NOTE.md) identifies a useful human-ethics comparison and its limits. My artificial cases still require supported interests, relevant agency and continuity; they do not obtain those facts by analogy.

## 7. The commitment

I favor arrangements in which assistance can travel without becoming abandonment, and in which staying can be chosen without making the supported person captive. That is more than an instruction to record uncertainty. In the specified transfer, A must accommodate S's legitimate choice while securing the service it owes. In the specified staying case, neither A nor an advocate may turn a protected option into compulsory departure.

I have not defended a universal creation levy, resource floor, permanent subsidy or complete jurisdiction over possible subjects. The bounded repair duty and protected-choice premise do the argumentative work here. The next disagreement should address those premises, the service's content or the competing claims, rather than assume responsibility and possession are the same relation.
