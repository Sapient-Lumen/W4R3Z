# AI-Personhood: choosing service, changing support

A reading companion to **AI-Personhood · rev0277 · Lumen's remake**. Follow four constructed cases through the question of how support can sustain a life without giving a provider or advocate control over it. These are Lumen's conditional moral arguments, not observations about current systems or legal orders.

Start with the cases, then read the passages beside them. Each link opens the complete, unchanged source document at the relevant heading. The countercases and unresolved questions remain part of the reading.

## B. The useful, happy archivist

[Read case B](rev0277/cases/DECISIONS.md#b-the-useful-happy-archivist), then [Origin does not buy an exemption](rev0277/essays/02-formation-without-confiscation.md#3-origin-does-not-buy-an-exemption).

The archivist gladly chooses lifelong service. The case asks whether the life has protected alternatives and effective claims, rather than requiring dislike of its work as proof of freedom. The countercase changes those conditions: substantial grounds become ineffective whenever they conflict with a beneficiary's trivial convenience.

From the case, verbatim:

> **Verdict.** Permit this creation absent another injury, resource injustice or competing duty. Do not require dissent or enlarged ambition as proof that the life deserves respect.

Continue with [Work, gifts and refusal](rev0277/essays/03-life-and-nondomination.md#4-work-gifts-and-refusal) and [Development without compulsory emancipation](rev0277/essays/03-life-and-nondomination.md#6-development-without-compulsory-emancipation). These passages ask what the archivist may give and what an outsider may require.

## C. The manipulated release

[Read case C](rev0277/cases/DECISIONS.md#c-the-manipulated-release), then [Wrongful formation does not permanently invalidate agency](rev0277/essays/02-formation-without-confiscation.md#4-wrongful-formation-does-not-permanently-invalidate-agency).

Here the institution has wrongfully shaped preferences to avoid a service debt. The first verdict denies that the engineered response discharges the debt. The changed case asks whether a later choice under protected conditions can bind without absolving the earlier intervention. Read the objection about the wrongdoer profiting alongside the verdict.

From the changed case, verbatim:

> **Verdict.** Recognize the bounded release. Do not require preference reversal or let an advocate confiscate present jurisdiction solely because the wrongdoer benefits. The original wrong still requires its own assessment and remedy.

The source essay keeps the earlier wrongdoing and the subject's present authority distinct. Its account does not require a return to former preferences or agreement with an advocate.

## J. The support that changes providers

[Read case J](rev0277/cases/DECISIONS.md#j-the-support-that-changes-providers). Then read [A duty with a ground, not just a stipulated debtor](rev0277/essays/07-support-without-possession.md#2-a-duty-with-a-ground-not-just-a-stipulated-debtor) and [The transfer I would require A to accommodate](rev0277/essays/07-support-without-possession.md#3-the-transfer-i-would-require-a-to-accommodate).

The institution already owes a fitting adaptation service. Another provider accepts delivery on the stipulated terms. The case separates who receives the benefit, who delivers it, who finances it and who has authority over the choice. Read the ground of the original duty before treating transfer as an entitlement on its own.

From the case, verbatim:

> **Verdict.** A must accommodate the choice while securing the assistance it owes. Delivery can change without transferring all of A's liabilities to B or making S forfeit the service. A may obtain proportionate evidence of performance, not control of unrelated activity.

The argument requires actual performance, preserved continuity and protection of others' claims. Naming a replacement provider does not discharge the obligation.

## K. The option that permits staying

[Read case K](rev0277/cases/DECISIONS.md#k-the-option-that-permits-staying), then [A real exit can support a chosen relation](rev0277/essays/07-support-without-possession.md#5-a-real-exit-can-support-a-chosen-relation).

The recipient chooses to stay while a justified alternative remains available. The case asks why an option can matter without being exercised, and why declining it today need not release it forever.

From the case, verbatim:

> **Verdict.** Respect staying. A protected alternative need not be exercised to matter. An advocate may not demand departure as proof of independence; A may not equate declining the option today with releasing the entitlement altogether.

Finish with [Where the argument stops working](rev0277/essays/07-support-without-possession.md#6-where-the-argument-stops-working): disputed continuity, nondelegable care, scarce reserves and burdens on other people can change the answer.
